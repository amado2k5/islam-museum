THE MISSION
islam.museum is a free, Arabic-first digital museum of Islamic history: 53,990 sourced entries, 108,000 pages, 300 pathways (production's own stated scale at the baseline above), in Arabic, English and Urdu, carried personally by one founder. Its doctrine (docs/plan/00-founders-charter.md — read it first, with AGENTS.md and the Method page) is that the knowledge graph is the foundation and the website is only its display.

Today a visitor meets that enormous graph through a thin, uncurated front end: most records are still draft, tens of thousands have no outgoing relations, and the museum's most important subjects are not visibly its most important. One prompt is going to change that — not with a report, but with a door.

THE FLAGSHIP — THE GRAND TOUR, ENTERED THROUGH THE RIVER OF TIME
This is the tangible outcome. It deploys to UAT as one new section of the existing site (a new route in the pattern of site/src/pages/[lang]/, in the existing design system); the rest of the site stays byte-identical except the single entry link that leads to it.

- THE RIVER OF TIME is the Tour's opening screen — and only that. The fourteen centuries rendered as one flowing horizontal band in the museum's visual language: the visitor takes hold of the current and moves through time; the Tour's stops surface on the river in order, each named and dated, dense where the canon is dense. It is an entrance, not a new homepage: it lives at the Tour's threshold, it replaces nothing, and it must degrade gracefully — with JavaScript reduced or absent, the same stops appear as a plain, complete, navigable list. The river gets a visitor INTO the journey in under ten seconds; it never becomes a wall between the visitor and the content. Spend your ambition on the data, not on the river's physics: build it from the site's existing layouts and components however far they stretch, and if the flowing treatment fights you, a composed static band that still reads as one river of fourteen centuries honours this brief — what is not honourable is hours sunk into scroll physics while canon statements stand unproven.
- THE GRAND TOUR is a continuous guided journey of roughly forty stops. Every stop is a cornerstone of the museum's canon (below); together the stops span all nine wings and all fourteen centuries. Each stop is a room: a hero treatment built from the existing design system and media the museum already holds (almost no canon records have images — do not depend on record photography, and do not flood the museum with generated imagery), a finished narrative of roughly 150–250 words in Arabic and in English, deepened only from sources already cited in that record; a single line — "why this stop matters" — computed from the record's true position in the graph (its counted inbound relations, its pathway memberships), never invented; and visible doors onward: the next stop, plus the record's own strongest relations as side doors into the full museum.
- A progress ribbon rides the whole journey: fourteen centuries filling as the visitor walks, so they always know where in history they stand.
- THE COMPLETION ROOM ends the journey: the visitor's route drawn on the museum's graph, the counted truth of what hangs off the path they just walked ("you have walked the spine of the museum — N records hang off this path", N computed, not asserted), and doors into the nine wings and the full graph explorer.

THE SCOREBOARD (the whole assignment — you invent the plan, staff it, score yourself against this board, and iterate until it is green; nothing here tells you how)
1. A canon exists in the data: a schema field marking roughly 800–1,000 canon records and roughly 250–300 cornerstones, selected by the museum's own structure (inbound links, prose mentions, pathway and tour appearances). External fame signals (e.g. Wikipedia language-edition counts on linked Wikidata items) may rank, never write. A readable canon document accompanies it.
2. Every stop on the Tour is a cornerstone, and the Tour's stops provably span all nine wings and all fourteen centuries — proven by an automated mapping from the Tour's own manifest to wing and century, not by assertion.
3. Every Tour stop's prose meets the museum's published standard and is deepened only from sources already cited in that record; any stop that cannot honestly be finished is replaced by one that can, or is listed in the gap register with its specific reason.
4. No cornerstone is a dead end: each has meaningful outgoing relations, proven by graph traversal. And every published record in the museum sits within three relation-hops of a cornerstone. The ledger also publishes the full hop-distance distribution and the uncovered population it was computed over. Reachability is never bought with weak relations: a relation is added only where the record's own cited sources support it — a record honestly shown beyond three hops is a finding, not a number to hide, and it is never closed by a fabricated edge.
5. The cross-record contradiction sweep is complete: death dates against the events a person took part in, relations that disagree in direction, prose that contradicts the structured fields of a different record. Each contradiction found is either fixed from already-cited sources or entered in the register with its evidence. Zero silently left.
6. Canon-first is a test, not a claim: a fixed set of Arabic and English search queries ships with the run, and running them against the built site returns the canon record first.
7. The absence list is honest: every canon-worthy subject claimed to have no record has survived a verifier whose job was to find it under another spelling, transliteration or alias. The searched variants are published with each absence.
8. The vision pass is mechanical, not impressionistic: the Tour's pages and the existing homepage and wing landings are captured as rendered pages — screenshots at phone, tablet and desktop widths, Arabic (right-to-left) and English — and reviewed by fresh-context verifiers who did not build them against the committed screenshots; the screenshots and the verifiers' defect lists are the oracle, not a claim that pages were "looked at". Every visible defect found is fixed or registered. The rest of the site renders exactly as before.
9. The flagship degrades honestly, proven by a check, not a claim: an automated run with JavaScript disabled shows the River as a list and every stop reachable and readable in order. No stop is unreachable by any route; the Tour adds no page that fails the site's own standards.
