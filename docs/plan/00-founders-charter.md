# The Founder's Charter — islam.museum

*Stated by the founder, Ahmed Abdel Aal, in his own words on 8 November 2026 — first written
to explain the project to a friend who had reviewed the site, then directed to stand as the
museum's vision, strategy and doctrine. The message itself is preserved verbatim beside this
document (`THE-MESSAGE-TO-HALA-verbatim.md`). Where this charter and an older document
disagree, this charter governs, and the older document is corrected.*

---

## The vision in one paragraph

The museum exists to build **one knowledge graph of the whole Islamic umma** — every person,
place, event, saying and story the tradition's mother books contain, connected to everything
around it. The graph is the work. The website, and everything in it, are applications and
ways of displaying that graph — they serve it; they are not the foundation. Everything else in
this charter is machinery for building that graph honestly, at the scale the books demand.

## Article 0 — The overall goal

*(Added the same day, in the founder's second statement — preserved verbatim in
`founders-goal-2026-11-08.md`.)*

The overall goal, God willing: that we make **Islam — with all its contents, its figures,
its events, its places, and everything about it — available to everyone**, in an easy,
simplified form, readable by the old and the young, by Arab and non-Arab, by Muslim and
non-Muslim. And that anyone may reshape the information — and the knowledge graph itself —
**in any way, in any form, in any application that suits their own users, above all schools,
universities and study circles (ḥalaqāt al-ʿilm)**.

Because there is knowledge no one could reach except by reading many books and spending
great time and great effort — and that has become nearly impossible for the new generations.
So the knowledge graph, God willing, is **the bridge**: the one that performs a revival
(iḥyāʾ) of the religious sciences for this age, and for the future.

Everything in Articles 1–6 is in service of this Article. A use of the graph by a school, a
university, a study circle or any builder, in any shape that suits their people, is not a
side effect of the project — it is the goal itself.

## Article 1 — The corpus: the mother books

The raw material is the content of **one to two thousand of the mother books of Islam — the
most famous and foundational of them** (ummahāt al-kutub). Not summaries of them, not
commentary about them: their contents, read in full. The programme works through this corpus
book by book, and its coverage is measured against the corpus, not against the site's page
count.

## Article 2 — The pipeline: read page by page, resolve, then judge

Artificial intelligence reads the corpus **page by page**. For every page, in this order:

1. **Mentions.** Is a person mentioned here? Who is he?
2. **Identity resolution.** Do we already have him — from another book, perhaps? If yes, the
   existing object is **enriched**; if this is the first time we see him, a **new entry** is
   created. One person, one object, however many books name him.
3. **Situation.** Where was he? Who was he with? What does he say — the exact quotes are
   harvested as quotes. What does he do? What are the events? What is the context? Under
   which theme does this fall?
4. **Rules.** Only then do the rules apply — among them the frame question: is this person,
   this account, this book, Sunni or not Sunni?

Extraction first, judgement last. No classification is made before the identity and the
context are established.

## Article 3 — The graph is the foundation; the site is an application

The aim at the end of the pipeline is a **knowledge graph for the entire Islamic umma**. The
website itself, and everything in it — the wings, the timelines, the maps, the explorers, the
children's wing — are **applications and display media for that graph, not the foundation**.
Consequences that already hold as engineering rules and are restated here as doctrine:

- Every feature is built on the one graph — never a side table, never a second graph.
- The graph is published openly (structured data CC0, a public API, downloadable in full), so
  any application — ours or anyone's — can be built on it.
- A display may be redesigned or retired; the graph is what endures, and work is judged by
  what it adds to the graph.

## Article 4 — Scale, stated honestly

So far, mining and harvesting have covered **barely 1–1.5% of the content of the first
thousand books** *(founder's estimate, 8 November 2026; to be verified against the harvest
records and corrected here when the count is made)*. That one percent alone yielded **more
than 160,000 pages containing persons, places, events and exact quotes**. The museum's
published size — its entries and pages — is therefore a fraction of a fraction of the corpus,
and every public statement of scale must keep that proportion visible: the graph is a beginning
on the books, not a completion of them. Gaps are shown, never hidden.

## Article 5 — What AI-generated images are

The AI images are **an application**, built like this: the AI is told *"now that you know
from the knowledge graph that there is a person, a place, or a story such-and-such — draw me
your imagining of what it looks like."* The results are therefore **not claimed to resemble
reality**. Their value is diagnostic: they show the extent of the AI's understanding of the
knowledge graph, and how far that understanding stands from reality or how close to it.
Consequences:

- An AI image is the model's reading of the graph, never a depiction of the thing itself —
  and it is presented that way wherever it appears.
- The standing prohibitions are untouched: no figurative depiction of prophets or
  Companions, and no AI-generated portrait presented as a person's likeness.

## Article 6 — The invitation

The founder needs help **in every direction**, and anyone who wishes to contribute may —
validation first among the named needs, and any other contribution a person offers. Helpers,
partners, sponsors, assistants and volunteers are named and thanked on the site under the
appropriate sections. This is the museum's standing posture, not a campaign: the graph
belongs to the umma it describes, and the door is open.

## What this charter confirms, and what it corrects

**Confirmed (already doctrine; restated so the charter is complete):** the publication
standard (a record publishes when it cites defined sources and records its provenance; one
defined, cited source is enough; no review stands between a record that meets the bar and
its publication); the faith discipline (a person's faith settles only on two independent
sources attesting it for the person himself — never from office, headship, family, descent,
party, an infobox or a database value; doubt stays *unknown* and rival readings are
preserved); the Sunni frame, stated openly, with every other view labelled before it is
stated and a perspectives block with no verdict on contested topics; claim statuses
(scriptural, tradition, historical, debated, legend) with the source's own doubt markers kept
as data; no honorifics typed (the interface renders them); the Qur'an's text never typed,
only referenced; Arabic first, English and Urdu in the same commit; prose CC BY-SA 4.0, data
CC0, code MIT; no AI at runtime; corrections in the open, in the public repository.

**Corrected by this charter:**

1. **The order of description is inverted.** Older texts describe a museum with a graph
   inside it ("a knowledge graph with a museum's soul"). The charter states the reverse:
   the graph is the foundation and the museum — site, wings, every feature — is among its
   applications. Texts that imply the site is the achievement are to be reworded.
2. **Coverage is measured against the corpus.** Progress is the share of the mother books
   mined (today: an estimated 1–1.5% of the first thousand), not the count of entries or
   pages the display happens to hold.
3. **AI images carry their true description.** Any text presenting an AI-generated image as
   an illustration *of* its subject, rather than as the model's imagining *from the graph*,
   is to be corrected; the diagnostic purpose (measuring the model's distance from reality)
   is part of the description.
