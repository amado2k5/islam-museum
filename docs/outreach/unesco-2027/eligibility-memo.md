# Who may nominate or endorse for the 22nd edition: eligibility memorandum

_Draft for the founder, 15 November 2026. Every quotation below was read first-hand that day, at the address given. Nothing here is legal advice, and on three points only the Prize Secretariat can give the answer; they are listed in section 6 and asked in letter L1 ([`letters.md`](letters.md))._

## 1. The answer

1. **The Statutes in force are those of Annex I to document 224 EX/9.II.** The Executive Board approved them at its 224th session (April 2026). This was unverified in the first package and is now confirmed.
2. **Article 6.2 names six kinds of nominator, not one.** Governments of Member States; non-governmental organizations and foundations in official partnership with UNESCO; former laureates of the Prize; eminent personalities; any person working in the field; any civil society organization working in the field.
3. **The model letters' reading stands on all four points**: partner organisations and foundations (a), former laureates (b), civil society organisations in the field (c), and an individual as nominator (d). Each carries a condition, set out in section 3.
4. **The Secretariat's guidance of 2 November does not narrow this.** Its own words include "other organizations eligible under the Statutes", and the Help Card it attached speaks of "an eligible nominator", not of an organisation.
5. **One point is not settled in writing:** whether the letter that lets a candidate file his own form may come from a *person* as well as from an organisation. The call page and the Help Card say "an eligible nominator"; the Secretariat's email describes the mechanism for "an eligible organization". Ask (L1). A person can also avoid the question entirely by filing the short form himself or herself.

## 2. The texts

### 2.1 Which Statutes

Document **224 EX/Decisions** (Paris, 22 May 2026), decision **9.II**, page 15:

> "4. Decides to renew the UNESCO-Sharjah Prize for Arab Culture for a period of six years;
> 5. Approves the amended Statutes of the UNESCO-Sharjah Prize for Arab Culture as set out in Annex I to document 224 EX/9.II, including the specific provisions previously approved by means of 195 EX/Decision 11.III.C, takes note of the deviations from the standard model text for the statutes of UNESCO prizes [...]"

Read at `https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000398080`, reached from UNESCO's list of Executive Board documents. UNESCO's own prize page (`https://www.unesco.org/en/prizes/sharjah`) links Annex I as "Statutes": `https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000397499`.

The Director-General's report in the same document (224 EX/9.II, paragraph 9) explains why this Prize's list of nominators is wider than usual:

> "The present Statutes maintain specific provisions that deviate from the model statutes for UNESCO prizes, notably with regard to the nomination process as set out in Article 6.2 and the distribution of the Prize among laureates. These deviations were previously approved by the Executive Board at its 195th session (195 EX/Decision 11.III.C)."

So the wide list is deliberate, and the Board has approved it twice.

### 2.2 Article 6, in full

> "6.1 When UNESCO has received the funding of the Prize as indicated in Article 2 above, the Director-General of UNESCO shall officially invite the submission of nominations to the Secretariat of the Prize, by March of the following year, from the entities referred to in Article 6.2 below.
>
> 6.2 Nominations shall be submitted to the Director-General by the governments of Member States, in consultation with their National Commissions, and by non-governmental organizations and foundations maintaining official partnerships with UNESCO and active in a field covered by the Prize; as well as former laureates of the Prize; eminent personalities qualified in the opinion of the Director-General, and by any person or civil society organization working in the field of Arab Culture and for the advancement of peace and intercultural dialogue. A self-nomination cannot be considered.
>
> 6.3 Each nomination shall be accompanied by a written recommendation, which shall include, in English or French, inter alia:
> (a) a description of the candidate's background and achievements;
> (b) a summary of the work or the results of the work, publications and other supporting documents of major importance, submitted for consideration;
> (c) a definition of the candidate's contribution to the Prize's objectives."

### 2.3 The call for nominations (13 November 2026)

> "All nominations must be submitted or formally endorsed by an eligible nominator in accordance with the Statutes of the UNESCO–Sharjah Prize for Arab Culture."
>
> "Self-nominations are accepted only if accompanied by a letter of endorsement from an eligible nominator. The endorsement must clearly support the application and meet the Prize requirements."

`https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/2026-unesco-sharjah-prize-arab-culture-call-nominations`. Deadline as printed: "30 November 2026 (midnight, UTC+1)".

### 2.4 The Secretariat's reply to the founder (2 November 2026) and its Help Card

The email:

> "NGOs in official partnership with UNESCO, as well as other organizations eligible under the Statutes, may submit nominations.
>
> If an eligible organization prefers not to manage the nomination process directly, it may provide you with an official endorsement letter authorizing you to prepare and submit the application on its behalf, subject to the applicable nomination requirements."

The attached "Nomination Help Card, 22nd Edition":

> "All nominations must be submitted or formally endorsed by an eligible nominator in accordance with the Statutes of the UNESCO–Sharjah Prize for Arab Culture"
>
> "Self-nominations are permitted ONLY when accompanied by an endorsement letter from an eligible nominator. The endorsement must clearly support the candidature and meet the Prize requirements"

The founder's question had been about NGOs, which is why the email answers in terms of organisations.

### 2.5 The nomination form

"I am submitting this nomination as": Government of a UNESCO Member State; National Commission for UNESCO; UNESCO Official Partner NGO; UNESCO Official Partner NGO/Foundation; Former Laureate of the UNESCO-Sharjah Prize; Eminent Personality; Civil Society Organization; Candidate on behalf of an eligible nominating entity. The form adds that self-nominations are not eligible and that UNESCO may verify the nominator's eligibility. The nominator's "organisation" field is marked "if applicable". ## 3. The six kinds of nominator and the condition on each

| # | Nominator (Article 6.2) | Condition in the text | Form option |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governments of Member States | "in consultation with their National Commissions" | Government; National Commission |
| 2 | Non-governmental organizations and foundations | "maintaining official partnerships with UNESCO **and** active in a field covered by the Prize": both | Official Partner NGO; Official Partner NGO/Foundation |
| 3 | Former laureates of the Prize | None stated. A laureate may be a person or an institution | Former Laureate |
| 4 | Eminent personalities | "qualified in the opinion of the Director-General". The text gives no test; UNESCO decides, and it cannot be known in advance | Eminent Personality |
| 5 | Any person | "working in the field of Arab Culture **and** for the advancement of peace and intercultural dialogue". No partnership and no eminence is asked | None of its own. A person would tick "Eminent Personality" |
| 6 | Any civil society organization | The same two-part field condition. No partnership with UNESCO is asked | Civil Society Organization |

Two remarks on reading it.

- **The sentence is loosely built**, and the closing words "working in the field of Arab Culture and for the advancement of peace and intercultural dialogue" can be read as qualifying kinds 5 and 6 only, or more of the list. On either reading kinds 5 and 6 exist.
- **"And", not "or".** Read strictly, a person or organisation of kinds 5 and 6 works both in Arab culture and for peace and intercultural dialogue. A historian of the Arab world meets the first half plainly and the second by the nature of the work; a humanitarian or youth organisation meets the second half more plainly than the first. How strictly UNESCO reads it is not written anywhere I could find.

## 4. The model letters' four readings, one by one

| Reading in the models | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Organisations and foundations in official partnership with UNESCO | **Stands** | Add the second condition when writing to one: "active in a field covered by the Prize" |
| (b) Former laureates of the Prize | **Stands**, with no further condition | The laureate's standing is a fact UNESCO holds; the letter should give the year |
| (c) Civil society organisations working in the field of Arab culture and for peace and intercultural dialogue | **Stands** | The organisation's field must fit, and UNESCO "may verify the eligibility of the nominator" |
| (d) An individual may recommend and endorse | **Stands on the Statutes**: as an eminent personality (if the Director-General so considers) or as "any person" working in the field | Two cautions: the form has no option named for "any person"; and the endorsement-letter mechanism was described to the founder for organisations. See section 6 |

The first model for a person said only "under Article 6.2". It now asks the signer to state the capacity in which he or she writes, in the Statutes' own words. Corrected wording, applied in [`form-texts.md`](form-texts.md), section 5a:

> I write as `[title and institution; one line on my own work]`, and in the capacity of `[choose one, in the words of Article 6.2: a person working in the field of Arab culture and for the advancement of peace and intercultural dialogue / a former laureate of the Prize (year) / an eminent personality in the field, should the Director-General so consider]`, to recommend Ahmed Abdel Aal for the UNESCO–Sharjah Prize for Arab Culture.

And the closing sentence now offers both ways of filing:

> `[Either:]` As I prefer not to manage the nomination process myself, I authorise Mr Abdel Aal to prepare and submit the nomination application on my behalf. `[Or:]` I am submitting this nomination myself through the Prize's online form.

## 5. Why the campaign's working basis was narrower

- The Canadian Commission's advice of 19 October, as the founder's letters of 21 October report it, was that candidates are nominated through NGOs in official partnership with UNESCO. That describes one of the six doors. (The Commission's own message was read on the morning of 15 November; it is closed and nothing may suggest its support.)
- The Secretariat's reply of 2 November confirmed that door and added "other organizations eligible under the Statutes". It was read as confirming the narrow list.
- The audit of 5 November that the follow-up brief mentions was not among the papers I was given. As the brief describes it, it asked which partner NGOs publish an intake route for people outside their membership. That is a question about who will take a request, not about who may nominate.

None of these contradicts Article 6.2. Together they left four of the six doors untried.

## 6. What only the Secretariat can settle

| # | Question | Why it is open | Where it is asked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | May the endorsement letter for a candidate who files "on behalf of an eligible nominating entity" come from a person (kinds 3, 4 and 5), not only from an organisation? | The Help Card and the call say "eligible nominator"; the email says "eligible organization"; the form's option says "entity" | L1, point 1 |
| 2 | How is "working in the field of Arab Culture" read for an organisation whose work is Islamic education, youth or humanitarian service? | The text gives no definition | L1, point 1(c) |
| 3 | Which nationality and which region does a citizen of Canada and of Egypt enter? | Article 1 rewards "a national of an Arab country and a national of any other country"; the form allows one nationality | L1, point 2 |
| 4 | Does "midnight, UTC+1" on 30 November close that day or open it? | The call does not say | L1, point 3 |

Until the first answer arrives, the safe course with a person is to offer both routes: sign the letter, or file the form directly (about ten minutes, with the dossier and the letter attached).

**Addendum, 18 November 2026.** L1 was sent by the founder on the evening of 15 November, and the Prize Secretariat replied on 18 November. It clarified the nominator rules: Article 6.2 names six kinds of nominator — Member State governments; partner NGOs and foundations; former laureates; eminent personalities; any person working in the field of Arab culture and for peace and intercultural dialogue; and any civil-society organisation in the field — and a self-nomination is permitted only with an eligible nominator's endorsement letter. It also signalled that the 30 November 2026 deadline is expected to be extended. That is a signal, not a confirmed new date: the deadline stands on paper until UNESCO announces otherwise.

## 7. What the Statutes do not say, and what they add

- **No residence or nationality rule for the nominator.** The founder asked on 21 October whether the nominator must be based in the candidate's country; nothing in the text requires it.
- **No rule on several letters.** One eligible nominator is what the text requires. Whether a second letter helps or confuses is a question of presentation, not of eligibility.
- **Article 3, on the candidate.** Candidates "should have acquired an international reputation in this field and distinguished themselves by meritorious actions extending over several years. Also, they should have contributed to promoting cultural dialogue and revitalization of the Arab culture." This, not the choice of nominator, is the weak point of a museum opened on 11 October 2026, and every letter must let the endorser see it.
- **Article 5.3.** A jury member "shall not take part in a vote concerning a nomination from his or her country."
- **Article 9.** "Proposals received for the award of a Prize may not be divulged." Whether that binds a candidate as well as UNESCO is not stated. The museum's own rule already forbids "nominated" or "candidate for" before a nomination is filed. If one is filed, ask the Secretariat before saying so in public.
