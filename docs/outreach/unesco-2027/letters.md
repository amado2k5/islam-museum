# UNESCO–Sharjah Prize, 22nd edition: draft letters

_Drafts for the founder, 15 November 2026, revised that evening. **He sends; no agent sends.** Who and why: [`endorsers.md`](endorsers.md). Dates and order: [`state-of-play.md`](state-of-play.md). The letter each one asks for: [`form-texts.md`](form-texts.md), section 5. The statutory basis: [`eligibility-memo.md`](eligibility-memo.md)._

## Before any letter goes

1. **Search Sent for the recipient first**, whatever any summary says, this one included.
2. **Check the To line before sending.** The follow-ups of 2 November to IFLA, ICOMOS and IRCICA were replies to the founder's own sent messages and went to his own address alone. A reply to one's own message must have the organisation's address put back in "To".
3. **Re-read the figures on the day.** These drafts say "some 63,000 records": the public figure was 63,078 on 15 November 2026, drafts included (`api.islam.museum/api/v1/meta/counts.json`). The site's home page calls them entries; these letters say records so that nobody takes the figure for a count of finished articles.
4. **Attach the recommendation model as PDF and DOCX.** The earlier model went out as a `.md` file, which few offices can open and some mail filters drop.
5. **One link, the site.** No dossier is attached to a first message; it is offered. The dossier of 21 October that went to IFLA, ICOMOS and IRCICA is out of date and is not sent again.
6. **A first message to a university address the founder has never written to carries no link and no attachment** (L11b). The museum's address is written as plain words, and the model is offered on reply. This follows the lesson he drew from an earlier university approach, where links from a personal mailbox were flagged.
7. **Every ask tells the endorser how the museum is made and how young it is.** Someone who signs without knowing has been misled, and will find out.
8. **No letter offers anything in return.** No seat, no say, no listing, no partnership. An endorsement is recognition and nothing else.
9. **Signature block for all:** Ahmed Abdel Aal · Founder, islam.museum (a personal, non-profit project) · Oakville, Ontario, Canada · `[email]` · https://islam.museum/en/

## L1. The Prize Secretariat (Monday 16 November; reply in the existing thread)

Thread: "Nomination rules for the 22nd edition — are NGO nominations accepted?" To: the Secretariat's address in that thread.

> Dear Secretariat,
>
> Thank you for your reply of 2 November and for the Help Card. Before I ask anyone for an endorsement letter, I would be grateful for your guidance on three points, so that the letter I bring you is one you can accept.
>
> **1. Who may issue the endorsement letter.** Your Help Card says a self-nomination needs "an endorsement letter from an eligible nominator". Article 6.2 of the Statutes lists, beside Member States and UNESCO's partner organisations and foundations, former laureates of the Prize, eminent personalities, and "any person or civil society organization working in the field of Arab Culture and for the advancement of peace and intercultural dialogue." May the letter for a candidate who files "on behalf of an eligible nominating entity" come from any of these, including an individual? In particular: (a) a university professor of Arab and Islamic history, writing as a person working in the field; (b) a former laureate of the Prize; (c) a civil society organisation whose work is education or humanitarian service among Muslim youth rather than Arab culture as such. And if such a person prefers to file the online form directly, which option should he or she choose?
>
> **2. Nationality and region.** I am a citizen of Canada and of Egypt and I live in Canada. The form asks for one nationality and for "Arab Region" or "Non-Arab Region". Which should I enter?
>
> **3. The deadline.** The call gives 30 November 2026, midnight UTC+1. Does the form close at the end of that day in Paris?
>
> So that you have the whole picture: the candidate would be myself, as an individual, for one work, islam.museum, a free Arabic-first digital museum of Islamic history that opened to the public on 11 October 2026. Its records are drafted and translated by artificial-intelligence tools from cited sources under rules I publish, and the site says so.
>
> With thanks for your help,
> Ahmed Abdel Aal
