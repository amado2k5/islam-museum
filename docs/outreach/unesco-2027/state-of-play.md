## Five findings that change the plan

1. **The eligible nominators are a much wider group than the campaign assumed.** Article 6.2 of the Statutes (224 EX/9.II, Annex I, read first-hand) admits nominations from governments of Member States in consultation with their National Commissions; NGOs *and foundations* in official partnership with UNESCO and active in a field the Prize covers; **former laureates of the Prize**; **eminent personalities** qualified in the Director-General's opinion; and **"any person or civil society organization working in the field of Arab Culture and for the advancement of peace and intercultural dialogue."** UNESCO's nomination form offers the same choices: Government of a Member State, National Commission, Official Partner NGO, Official Partner NGO/Foundation, Former Laureate, Eminent Personality, Civil Society Organization, and "Candidate on behalf of an eligible nominating entity" (the endorsement-letter route). The form's nominator section marks the organisation field "if applicable", so a nominator may be a person. Every approach so far went to UNESCO-partner NGOs or to bodies that are not eligible at all.
2. **IFLA and ICOMOS never received the endorsement-letter request.** The follow-ups of 2 November, the ones that carried the small ask and the model letter, were addressed to the founder's own Gmail address and to nobody else. Confirmed in the message header for IFLA (from the founder, to the founder); the ICOMOS and IRCICA threads show the same pattern. Each organisation has had one message, on 21 October, asking a different and larger question. What goes to them this week is the first delivery of the ask, not a third touch.
3. **IRCICA answered on 3 November and declined.** It wrote that it does not nominate candidates for UNESCO prizes, this one included, and that as far as it knows States nominate. IRCICA is an organ of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, so it fits none of the nominator categories either. This door is closed. No nudge.
4. **The International Islamic Youth League wrote again this morning (15 November, 10:05) and the message was unread.** It welcomes the pilot the founder proposed, offers an online meeting, and asks for his one-page concept note first. It is the warmest institutional thread the museum has.
5. **The candidature's weak point is Article 3, not paperwork.** Candidates "should have acquired an international reputation in this field and distinguished themselves by meritorious actions extending over several years." The museum opened to the public on 11 October 2026. No draft may blur that. Against it: Article 1 rewards "a national of an Arab country and a national of any other country", and UNESCO's own evaluation of the Prize (same document, paragraph 8) recommends reflecting "digital innovation, diaspora engagement and youth contributions". The latest edition drew more than 110 applications.

## Decisions that are the founder's

1. **Go this year, or build for next year?** Recommended: go only through the warm routes (Hoyland, An-Na'im, IIYL) and the two organisations that never received the ask. An endorsement from someone who has used the museum is worth more to a jury than one won from a stranger in ten days, and it costs no goodwill to ask.
2. **The cold routes** (the Arab Thought Foundation and the three former laureates): send or hold. Each is one email. Each also asks a stranger to vouch for a five-week-old project.
3. **Nationality and region on the form.** The form asks for one nationality and for "Arab Region" or "Non-Arab Region". Recommended: put the question to the Secretariat (L1) and state both citizenships in the text either way.
4. **Whether a government may nominate** (Egypt), given the standing practice of holding ministries back.
5. **The Muslim World League follow-up**, and whether an endorsement from a religious organisation sits well with the independence rule. An endorsement is not oversight, but it is an association a juror will see.

## Honest assessment

A nomination can still be filed this year if one of three people or one organisation who already know the museum writes a one-page letter within ten days and the Secretariat accepts the category. That is a real chance and worth the week. Winning is a different matter: a jury applying Article 3 as written will look for years of record that the museum does not have, among more than a hundred candidates. The case for filing anyway is that it costs nothing, the Prize's own evaluation asks for digital and diaspora work, and a careful first file makes the second one stronger. The case against is that an endorser's name is spent once. That is why the letters tell each endorser exactly what the museum is and how young it is.
