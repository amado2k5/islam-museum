The letters are in [`letters.md`](letters.md), the models to attach in [`form-texts.md`](form-texts.md) section 5, the dates in [`state-of-play.md`](state-of-play.md). Contact details of organisations were read on their own sites by a research sub-agent (**agent-read**): look at the page once more on the day of sending._

## How the list is ranked

Four questions, in this order.

1. **Does the name stand under Article 6.2 without a ruling from UNESCO?** A former laureate does. An organisation whose field is not plainly Arab culture needs one.
2. **Does the person or body know the museum?** A letter from a reader is worth more to a jury than one won from a stranger in ten days, and costs less goodwill to ask for.
3. **Can the ask be answered by Wednesday 25 November?** A professor can sign a page in a week. A secretariat with a board usually cannot.
4. **What is lost if it goes wrong?** Some threads here are worth more than a prize letter. They are held back for that reason, and the reason is given.

Every approach was also read against the founder's sovereignty rule. An endorsement is recognition and nothing else: no letter below offers or implies a seat, a say, shared management, a merger or anything in return.

## 1. Professor Robert Hoyland, Institute for the Study of the Ancient World, New York University

- **Basis.** "Any person [...] working in the field of Arab Culture", and an eminent personality if the Director-General so considers. He is a historian of early Islam and of Arabia.
- **Fit.** The strongest there is: a scholar of exactly the period the museum's Arabic sources cover, who has already put a judgement in writing.
- **Thread.** He replied on 29 October: "Your digital museum looks very impressive, and I am sure it will be a wonderful resource for interested lay persons and students alike (which you may quote me on)". No reply from the founder is in that thread, seventeen days on. L2 begins by thanking him.
- **Smallest ask.** One page, from a model he may change or discard, and the five details the form asks of a nominator. Or, if he prefers, ten minutes on the online form himself.
- **Route.** Reply in his thread. Check that his address is in "To".
- **Risk.** US Thanksgiving is Thursday 26 November. He wrote one kind sentence; a prize letter is a larger thing, and L2 says so.
- **Sovereignty check.** Passes: a recommendation only.

## 2. Professor Abdullahi Ahmed An-Na'im, Emory University School of Law

- **Basis.** The same two capacities. His field is Islamic law, human rights and dialogue within and between cultures, which meets the second half of the condition squarely.
- **Fit.** High. He approached the museum warmly and his name carries weight on the Prize's "dialogue" side.
- **Thread.** The founder wrote on 2 November; he replied on **3 November** (the first package said 2 November: corrected) that he was "so eager to explore ways of benefiting from this tremendous resource" and asked whether he could share it with his collaborators. He asked for a one-page note, which the founder sent that day, and he is the first entry on the museum's Network page since 4 November. Nothing stands open; the last message is the founder's.
- **What he has not said.** That he has read the museum's records. He gave no permission to be quoted. A letter of 13 November to another recipient quotes his phrase; that should not be repeated without asking him ([`claims-corrections.md`](claims-corrections.md), C10).
- **A correction he is owed.** The founder's first letter told him "every fact carries its citation" and gave "44,000+ sourced entries". L3 puts that right before it asks for anything.
- **Smallest ask.** As for Professor Hoyland.
- **Route.** Reply in his thread.
- **Sovereignty check.** Passes. The Network page lists his site as a courtesy between two personal projects; L3 asks for nothing that would tie them.

## 3. The Arab British Centre, London

- **Basis.** Former laureate: UNESCO's laureates page gives 2012 (read on the morning of 15 November; the page returned an error when reopened that evening). Its own site announces the 2012 prize, presented in Paris on 25 April 2013 (agent-read). It is also a civil society organisation whose stated work is "to further understanding of the Arab world in the United Kingdom" (agent-read). It stands on two of the six doors at once.
- **Fit.** The best statutory fit of any body that does not know the museum.
- **Thread.** None.
- **Smallest ask.** One page on its letterhead; the founder files everything.
- **Route.** The general address printed on its contact page, to the attention of its Executive Director, who is named on its staff page (agent-read: confirm the name on the day).
- **Risk.** It owes the museum nothing, and a small charity may not turn a letter round in a week.
- **Sovereignty check.** Passes.

## 4. IFLA

- **Basis.** An NGO in official partnership with UNESCO, associate status, on UNESCO's list of August 2025 (agent-read; UNESCO's partnership pages returned an error when I tried to re-read them on 15 November). The second condition, "active in a field covered by the Prize", is arguable for a federation of libraries.
- **Fit.** Moderate: open access to knowledge, preservation, multilingual cataloguing.
- **Thread.** One message reached it, on 21 October, asking whether IFLA nominates. The follow-up of 2 November, which carried the small ask and the model letter, was a reply to the founder's own sent message and went to his own address only. IFLA has never seen the endorsement request.
- **Smallest ask.** One page; a one-line answer either way.
- **Route.** Reply to the message of 21 October with IFLA's address put back in "To". The name of its Secretary General could not be confirmed on IFLA's own site (the site refused the sub-agent); do not address a person by name unless the founder confirms it.
- **Sovereignty check.** Passes. L5 says plainly that he is not a member; it does not offer to join in exchange.

## 5. Professor Bahia Shehab, The American University in Cairo

- **Basis.** Former laureate: UNESCO's page gives 2016 (morning reading); her faculty page lists the Prize under 2016 (agent-read). Also a person working in the field: an artist and historian of Arabic script.
- **Fit.** High on the Arab side of the Prize, and she is a compatriot.
- **Thread.** None.
- **Smallest ask.** One page, or the form itself.
- **Route.** The address printed on her faculty page at the university (agent-read). It is not reproduced in this public file.
- **Risk.** A cold letter to a university address from a personal mailbox. Following the lesson the founder drew from an earlier university approach, L11b carries **no link and no attachment**: the museum's address is written as plain words and the model is offered on reply.
- **Sovereignty check.** Passes.

## 7. Arab Thought Foundation, Beirut

- **Basis.** A foundation in official relations with UNESCO, on the list of September 2026 as a sub-agent read it. I could not re-read the list on 15 November. If it stands, both conditions of kind 2 are met: the Foundation's field is Arab thought and culture.

## 8. Biblioteca Islámica "Félix María Pareja", AECID, Madrid

- **Basis.** Former laureate, 2015 (UNESCO's page, morning reading; AECID's own site says the same, agent-read).
- **Fit.** Good: a research library for the Arab and Islamic world that will judge an Arabic-source reference work quickly.
- **Thread.** None.
- **Route.** AECID prints only its general information address; no address for the library was found (agent-read). A name for the library's head appears in a 2019 article and may be out of date.
- **Risk.** The library is part of a government agency. A letter may need clearance it cannot get in two weeks, and the French model (5c) may travel better than the English.
- **Sovereignty check.** Passes. An agency of a foreign ministry writing one page is recognition, not oversight; if it ever asked for more, the answer is no.

## 9. The Mauritanian institute for heritage research and training (IMRFPC)

- **Basis.** The institute is described elsewhere as a public body created by decree (search result only, not opened). A public body is none of the six kinds of nominator; only Mauritania's government could nominate, "in consultation with" its National Commission, and that is out of reach in two weeks. Its deputy director, a university professor, could write as a person working in the field.
- **Fit.** Good in substance: Arabic manuscript heritage in an Arab country.
- **Thread.** Opened on 14 November. By the mailbox's own summary and the newest line of the thread (I did not open the messages), the deputy director wrote back on 15 November with interest of his own and on the institute's behalf, will put the proposal to his Director General on the latter's return from abroad, and a first joint piece of work on four historic towns has been floated.
- **Verdict.** Do not ask first. The thread is two days old and its subject is cooperation, which is worth more than a letter. If he asks what else he can do, or the founder judges the moment right, L12 is one optional paragraph in French.
- **Sovereignty check.** **Needs care.** The summary speaks of formalising an agreement. Any agreement must be about content only: the institute's scholars advise and correct, the museum keeps editorial responsibility and ownership, and nothing is signed that gives either side a say in the other.

## 10. International Islamic Youth League (IIYL), Freetown

- **Basis.** A civil society organisation. Its own description, in its letters, is "an NGO with experience in humanitarian and development work since 1991". Whether that is "working in the field of Arab Culture" is doubtful and is UNESCO's call (L1, point 1c).
- **Fit.** As a partner for the education pilot, the warmest thread the museum has. As a nominator for a prize in Arab culture, weak.
- **Thread.** Moving fast. 14 November: two replies confirming interest and curriculum mapping. 15 November, 10:05: asks for a one-page concept note. 12:29: the founder sends his own note and offers three dates. 13:33: the League confirms **Tuesday 24 November, 13:00 Toronto time**, and will send the meeting details. 13:49: in a separate message the League asks the museum to sponsor, fund or help raise funds for a distribution of 100,000 copies of the Qur'an. 15:25: the founder declines, saying the museum is a personal project with no funds, and keeps the pilot offer open.
- **Verdict.** **Do not ask in writing this week.** A request for a favour hours after declining theirs invites a trade, and an endorsement that could look exchanged for support would cut against the museum's own rule that a gift buys nothing. If the founder still wishes to ask, the moment is the end of the meeting on 24 November, by voice, in the words of L4, with the pilot and every question of funding kept apart. A letter would then be needed by Friday 27 November.
- **Sovereignty check.** **Needs care**, for the reason above, and because the League writes of "partnership". The pilot is cooperation on lessons; the museum keeps editorial responsibility.

## 11. Muslim World League

- **Thread.** Two messages, 17 October and 8 November. The second, with the request and the model letter, **is in the Sent folder** (the follow-up brief had it as unconfirmed). No reply.
- **Verdict.** One optional follow-up on Monday 23 November, because the 8 November letter needs its basis corrected (L9). No more than that.
- **Sovereignty check.** The founder's to weigh. An endorsement is not oversight, but his independence rule names religious institutions, and a juror will see the association.

## 12. ICOM

## 13. Arabic Language International Council, Beirut

- **Basis.** Its own site says it is accredited among UNESCO's partner organisations (agent-read).

- **Verdict.** **No further message** to the same inbox. Its Secretary-General is named on its site (agent-read), but the only address printed is the one already tried twice.
