## 1. Recommendation

Send the request first to the **International Council of Museums (ICOM)**. It is an NGO with associate status in official partnership with UNESCO, based in Paris, and it is the body whose members run the museums the prize would be judged against. Its address and form are below.

The **International Council for Open and Distance Education (ICDE)**, in Norway, replied on 21 October 2026 that it nominates only from its membership network and invited the museum to join (EUR 200 a year for an individual). Membership is not pursued unless the founder wants ICDE's education network for its own sake.

The **Arabic Language International Council** (Beirut, consultative status on UNESCO's September 2026 list) fits the Arabic-first design; its own site gives info@alarabiah.org. Other sources describe it as intergovernmental, so the email asks it to confirm its UNESCO status (section 7b).

## 3. The UNESCO-Sharjah Prize form

The prize page (https://www.unesco.org/en/prizes/sharjah) says "Nominations are open" and links to an online form, "Submit your nomination", which is hosted on KoboToolbox. The page does not say who may nominate or whether self-nomination is accepted. Earlier editions accepted nominations only from governments and from NGOs in official partnership, and the secretariat (prix.sharjah@unesco.org) is the authority on the current rule. Ask the secretariat before filling in the form. Do not submit the form as an individual unless the secretariat says it is allowed.

Dear colleagues,

I am writing to ask whether the International Council of Museums, which holds associate status in official partnership with UNESCO, would provide a nomination letter for islam.museum for the UNESCO-Sharjah Prize for Arab Culture, 22nd edition.

islam.museum is a free, open, Arabic-first digital museum of Islamic civilization. Its collection is made of records about people, places, events, texts, objects and ideas, and each record cites its sources, states its date precision and shows its review status. Structured data is dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0, prose is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, and the software is MIT-licensed. Photographs of objects are never copied: they are displayed from the holding institutions' own servers, under their own terms, with credit.

The Canadian Commission for UNESCO has told me that it is unable to support applications for the UNESCO prizes and that Canadian applicants should approach an NGO in official partnership with UNESCO. I am therefore writing to ICOM. I do not ask ICOM to take any position beyond its own judgement of the museum's work.

I would be grateful for answers to three questions:

1. Does ICOM nominate candidates for the UNESCO-Sharjah Prize, and if so, what must a nomination letter contain?
2. What is the deadline for the 22nd edition, and does ICOM set an earlier deadline for requests?
3. Would ICOM be willing to discuss the project with me, by video call or in writing?

The enclosed dossier sets out the mission, the technical architecture, the open-data framework, the sustainable development goals the project serves, and its limits. It states that the records are still in draft review, that the legal entity is in formation, and that the public site opened on 11 October 2026 after more than a year of planning. Its figures are dated 17 October 2026; current figures are on https://islam.museum/en/coverage/.

Thank you for your time, and for ICOM's work for museums everywhere.
