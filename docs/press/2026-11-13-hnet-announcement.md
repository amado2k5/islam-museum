# islam.museum Officially Announced on H-Net Academic Consortium

islam.museum has been vetted and published on **H-Mideast-Medieval**, a network of
H-Net (Humanities and Social Sciences Online) — the global consortium of scholars,
teachers and academic societies for the humanities and social sciences. The
announcement, reviewed and approved by the network's editors, introduces the museum
to the scholarly community for medieval and Islamic history:

https://networks.h-net.org/group/announcements/20169387/islammuseum-free-sourced-digital-museum-and-cc0-dataset-islamic

## What the announcement highlighted

The broadcast presented the museum's verified scale and its open-science
commitments:

- **More than 54,000 sourced entries across some 108,000 pages** — every entry a
  structured record compiled from verified published books, with its facts cited,
  organised as a knowledge graph across nine wings and 300 guided pathways, in
  Arabic first with English and Urdu editions.
- **The CC0 dataset on Zenodo** — the museum's full knowledge graph is released
  into the public domain under CC0 (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23692511), free for anyone
  to download, reuse and build on, with a free public API alongside it.
- **Preservation by Library and Archives Canada** — the museum has been accepted
  into Library and Archives Canada's web preservation programme, so the resource
  is being archived by a national institution for the long term.
- **The Education Explorer** — a curriculum layer mapping the museum's content to
  **65 Ontario curriculum courses** for Grades 1–12, in 360 lessons addressing 536
  official curriculum expectations, ready for classroom use.

islam.museum is a non-profit project, not a business: it is free for everyone —
free to visit, free to download and run locally, free to use as a template and
free to query through its API — and it is personally funded by its founder.

## Why this milestone matters

Publication on H-Net marks a step change in what islam.museum is. The museum began
as one person's independent project; an editor-vetted announcement on a principal
scholarly consortium establishes it as something more — reliable, citable Digital
Humanities infrastructure, recognised by the academic establishment and ready for
educational adoption by the schools, universities and teachers the network serves.
