# A door into the museum, and what was found behind it

_A Grand Tour of forty rooms, and 130 contradictions found behind it. islam.museum, 11 November 2026. The instrument was an AI model, Claude Fable 5.2, working from one written brief. None of this is live yet: the founder decides what is accepted and when it opens._

## The museum

islam.museum is a free, Arabic-first digital museum of Islam and its civilization: 54,135 records and 300 guided pathways, in Arabic, English and Urdu, every record tied to its sources.
One person founded it, built it and carries it.

## The Grand Tour, as a visitor meets it

On the homepage there is one new button. It opens on a river: fourteen centuries as a single band across the screen, with forty stops standing on it in order, from the Kaaba to the cloth still woven for it every year. Each stop is a room: a name, a date, an account in Arabic and in English with a footnote on every sentence, one line counted from the museum's own records saying why this stop matters, and doors onward. A ribbon of centuries fills as the visitor walks. The last room draws the route and counts the records that hang directly off it: 5,871. With scripts switched off the river becomes a plain list, and every room still opens.

## What was asked

1. Mark the museum's most important records, by how much the museum itself leans on them.
2. Make every stop one of them, across nine wings and fourteen centuries.
3. Finish every stop's account, saying nothing its sources do not say.
4. Leave no such record a dead end, and every published record within three steps of one.
5. Check the records against each other: correct each contradiction from the sources, or list it with its evidence.
6. Make a search for a famous subject return that subject first.
7. Hunt for everything said to be missing, under other spellings.
8. Photograph the pages, have strangers to them inspect the pictures, and leave the rest of the site as it was.
9. Make the Tour work with scripts off.
10. Give every claim a check anyone can run again.
11. Save the work for the founder, phase by phase.
12. Fix the starting point, and hold it while others change the museum.
13. Let every "done" come from running the check.

## How it came out

1. **Done.** 824 records marked, 271 of them cornerstones. One century had none and 48 sat in no wing; both gaps are closed. Of twelve looked for on the live wing pages, 3 are listed; on the new pages, all 12. A caution: for 489 of the 824, the subject's fame helped decide whether it qualified.
2. **Done.** Forty stops, nine wings, fourteen centuries. Two of the nine wings are each reached by one stop, placed there by a judgement of this work that the founder may overrule.
3. **Done, within a limit.** All 80 accounts (forty stops, two languages) are full length and footnoted throughout, with no source added. Where a cited edition was not at hand its footnote was carried over unread, and the notes say where.
4. **Not done.** 12 of 29 dead ends remain: records too thin to connect honestly. 7,114 of 18,699 published records are within three steps; most of the rest are book entries, tied to records by citation.
5. **Done. 130 contradictions found; 38 corrected from sources the records already cited; 84 listed with their evidence; 8 found consistent after all.**
6. **Not done.** On the live site, 2 of 50 test searches return the right record first. The repair sits in an older page this work was told to leave alone.
7. **Done.** Of 31 subjects listed as missing, 24 were there all along under another name.
8. **Done.** Ninety photographs, five reviewers, three rounds, 135 visible faults. 23 are fixed, each confirmed gone by a later reviewer; 26 on the Tour's pages remain, small or partly fixed; 77 are on older pages, listed for the founder. The older pages rebuilt for comparison came out identical byte for byte, the one new button apart.
9. **Done.** All forty rooms were walked with scripts off, in both languages.
10. **Done.** Each claim has its check and its recorded result.
11. **Done.** Seven phases saved. None is accepted into the museum yet.
12. **Done.** Other work landed in the museum 24 times meanwhile; the checks were run again on top of it.
13. **Done.** A program writes the results table from the checks' own last lines; another check fails if a line was typed.

## The most surprising finding

The museum's record of the Qur'an could not be given an account. A visitor would expect it to be the fullest record in the building, and it cites exactly one source: the text itself. A writer asked to finish it reached the length by describing how the museum stores the text. A reviewer showed that those sentences claimed what the source cannot support, and that one was false. The account was withdrawn, and the Qur'an has no room on the Tour. The museum's rule is that nothing is written without a source that says it. Held to that rule on its most important record, the honest result was an empty room, and a founder who now knows what that record needs first.

## What its own reviewers and checks caught

Every piece of work went to a fresh reviewer told only to prove it wrong.

- **Accounts.** In 730 sentences: 5 errors, 56 weaker problems, 10 places where Arabic and English disagreed. Two phrases echoed verses of the Qur'an, which the museum never types. One sentence praised a scholar in the museum's own voice. One number was off by a night.
- **Links.** Of 82 added, 17 said more than the record did. All were removed: dead ends fell from 29 to 12, not to 7 as first reported.
- **Corrections.** Two were wrong and 3 rested on nothing the record cited. All 5 were undone, and a sixth proved hidden, not fixed: 38, not the 44 first reported.
- **The missing list.** 24 of its 31 entries were wrong.
- **Numbers.** Two "before" figures had been measured after the work began, and the last room's total counted links it should not have.
- **Pages.** A repair reported as done was still visible, and the Tour's arrows pointed backwards in Arabic. The next repair dimmed a number it should not have. A stray space had been added to the Urdu homepage, which the brief said not to touch.
- **Tests.** The first test of "works with scripts off" passed for the wrong reason: its pages had no styling.
- **The Tour.** It began with 42 stops. Two could not be finished honestly and left.
