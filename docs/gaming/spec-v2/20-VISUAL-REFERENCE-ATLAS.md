# Supplement of 25 November 2026: twelve high-resolution concept images

_Added to the repository copy of this atlas on 25 November 2026 (the package text above is unchanged). The founder attached twelve images to the launch instruction: three for The Silk Road and one for each of the other nine games. They replace the seven low-resolution crops as the working references; the crops and the SVG boards above stay as they were. The copies here are 1280 × 720 (the supplied files are 1672 × 941)._

**What these images are.** AI-generated concept art. They show an ambition for composition, materials and atmosphere. They are not gameplay, not historical evidence and not production assets. Every number, distance, date, price, label, border and building in them is invented until a museum record says otherwise, and nothing from them enters a game's data without that check (`AGENTS.md`, "Truth in every public claim"; master mandate, outcome 6).

**One fault they all share.** All twelve use the same interface kit: dark teal panels, thin gold rules, ornate serif titles, a resource bar across the top and inspector panels down the sides. The mandate asks for ten distinct identities and warns against "a common ornate template" (every GDD, "Interface, controls and localization"). The production interfaces take the information hierarchy from these images and nothing of the shared skin. All text is baked in English: the real interfaces are live text in the museum's seven languages, with right-to-left layout, and no label is ever part of an image.

## The Silk Road — Merchant's Legacy (three images, one game)

The three images are three takes on one screen: the route decision on the road to Samarkand.

![Silk Road 1: Route to Samarkand](assets/v2s-silk-road-1-route-to-samarkand.webp)

**1. Route to Samarkand — the environment reference.** What to take: the layered composition (caravan and rock in the foreground, river valley and walled city in the middle distance, snow range behind, each layer lighter and bluer than the one before it); the river as a line that leads the eye to the destination; cloud lying below the peaks; cargo that reads as cargo (bales, rugs, jars) on the animals. What is wrong: the interface covers both lower corners and the right third, about 45% of the frame, so the landscape the image is selling is mostly hidden; the route is described three times (top bar, route panel, map).

![Silk Road 2: Caravan route decision](assets/v2s-silk-road-2-caravan-route-decision.webp)

**2. Caravan route decision — the inventory and control reference.** What to take: cargo listed with quantity and unit per good (bales, crates, bundles, jars, sacks) and a mark on a good that the contract wants; one bottom rail of five places (Caravan, Trade, Contracts, Ledger, Map); weather, date and season together at the top right; a contract card that states deadline, reward and reputation in three lines. What is wrong: the two route cards carry photographs that crowd out the facts; "Condition impact −10% to −30%" names a consequence but not its cause or what the player can do about it.

![Silk Road 3: Mountain route decision](assets/v2s-silk-road-3-mountain-route-decision.webp)

**3. Mountain route decision — the primary reference for the route-selection interface.** What to take: the comparison table (distance, time, risk, cost, trade opportunity, one row each, two routes side by side) is the clearest statement of the decision in any of the three; both routes are drawn on the map in the colours of their cards; the choice is two buttons of equal weight, so neither is the "right" answer. What is wrong: the same facts appear in the cards, in the table and in the map legend; risk is a word (High, Low) with no odds, no consequence and no countermeasure, where the GDD asks for risks to be previewed with what the player can do about them.

**Combined direction.** Image 1's landscape with nothing over it but a thin top bar; image 3's comparison table as the one place the two routes are compared, opened over the lower third when a decision is due and gone when it is made; image 2's cargo list and bottom rail, off screen until asked for. The map is the real route graph, never a drawing of one.

**Not to be copied into data.** The three images disagree with each other and with geography. The distance to Samarkand is 640 km in the first, about 740 km in the second and about 780 km in the third. Kashgar and Samarkand are about 775 km apart in a straight line by their published coordinates (39.47° N 75.99° E and 39.65° N 66.96° E), so 640 km is impossible and any route through the mountains is longer than all three figures; the game's distances come from the route graph built on museum place records. The maps do not agree on where Samarkand lies relative to the caravan (west in the first, north-east in the second and third). The second image is dated 393 AH, about 1003 CE, while the city it shows has the tiled domes of the Timurid buildings of four centuries later; the first and third show a city wall with square crenellated towers of a European kind. Prices, rewards and "dinars" are invented; the GDD requires simulated prices to be labelled as fiction. The animals look like one-humped camels; the species and the pack gear for a Central Asian route are to be checked with a museum record before any model is made. Any banner or sign must carry real, readable text or none.

## The Golden Age — Rise of Civilizations

![Golden Age: Baghdad](assets/v2s-golden-age-baghdad.webp)

Take: the river as the spine of the city; a building under construction with its ground marked out, as the centre of attention; markets, a water wheel, gardens and boats all visibly at work; the construction bar's row of building types. Wrong: it names a real city and year ("Baghdad, Capital of the Abbasid Caliphate, Year 320 AH") and fills it with invented architecture, including tiled domes and portals of a much later period and a lifting crane of a European medieval kind; the GDD's city is fictional and says so. The top bar carries six resources with seven-figure totals and monthly changes, which is the "wall of numbers" the GDD lists as a pitfall. The construction bar has a "Military" tab in a game whose promise is economy, welfare and learning. "Translation & Preservation, 18 months remaining" is waiting presented as progress.

## The House of Wisdom — Masters of Invention

![House of Wisdom: water-lifting challenge](assets/v2s-house-of-wisdom-water-lifting.webp)

Take: one machine fills the frame and every part of it moves; the goal is one sentence with a number ("lift water to the upper basin using 18 components or fewer"); target and measured values sit next to each other; the parts tray is six kinds of part. Wrong: glass pipes, flanged and bolted pipe joints and a gate valve with a red handwheel are modern plumbing; nine floating callouts cover the machine they describe; the performance charts repeat the numbers panel; kilowatts, litres per minute and revolutions per minute are fine as a modern measuring overlay but must be labelled as such. A twin-cylinder pump driven by a water wheel is close to a documented design, so it is named and credited only from the museum's own record of it, never from this image.

## Lost Manuscripts — The Archivist's Mystery

![Lost Manuscripts: the traveling treatise](assets/v2s-lost-manuscripts-traveling-treatise.webp)

Take: the desk as the playing surface; two documents joined by a visible link with the problem named between them; a notebook that fills in as the player works; a timeline with a question mark where the gap is. Wrong: the case as drawn has no contradiction (a work completed in 642 after a journey of 639 to 641 is simply in order), so the puzzle would have to be rewritten; hypothesis A is already labelled "most plausible" and each hypothesis carries a percentage, which both solves the case for the player and claims a precision no archive supports; the GDD forbids turning scholarly disagreement into a single right answer. The "manuscripts" are English text in a manuscript style: production documents are real, readable text with a transcription and a source, or clearly fictional documents marked as such, and never imitation script.

## ChronoQuest — Guardians of History

![ChronoQuest: the missing observation](assets/v2s-chronoquest-missing-observation.webp)

Take: a place the player wants to walk into; a quest stated as three plain steps; interaction prompts attached to the thing they act on. Wrong: "Lv 12, 5,280 / 7,200 XP" is the grind the GDD rules out; the hero has a specific, realistic face, and the museum's rule is no AI-generated portraits, so characters are plainly fictional and stylised; a giant armillary sphere on a plinth in a public garden is decoration, and the instruments and buildings of the Samarkand observatory are taken from museum records and labelled as reconstruction where they are reconstruction; the label "Samarkand, Timurid Era, 15th Century" promises a real place that the scene only resembles.

## Atlas — The Historical World Challenge

![Atlas: daily challenge](assets/v2s-atlas-daily-challenge.webp)

Take: the cleanest of the twelve, and the closest to its GDD (modern cartography, navy and ivory, no parchment); image, clues, globe and timeline are each one clear region; round progress as five dots; one primary button. Wrong: the three clues together all but name the answer, and the pin already sits on it; the photograph carries no credit, and every Atlas image needs a licence and an attribution that does not give the answer away; the globe shows modern city lights and offers a "Political Borders" layer, both of which put the present day into a historical question and can leak the answer; the overlay "al-Andalus (c. 750–1492)" states a date range that must come from a museum record.

## Dynasty — The Age of Kingdoms

![Dynasty: the Ayyubid council](assets/v2s-dynasty-ayyubid-council.webp)

Take: relief cartography with soft political colour over it; a council decision as four cards, each with the same six effects so they can be compared line by line; objectives that are stability, knowledge and trade and not conquest. Wrong: it shows a portrait of a named, real ruler, which the museum does not generate (the GDD allows only fictional council portraits); borders are drawn as exact lines, the GDD's first named pitfall; several labels need checking against the museum's records for the year shown (1225 CE), among them an undivided "Byzantine Empire" two decades after 1204, and dynasties of the Maghrib that general references date to the 1230s; "Council Recommendation" answers the question the player is being asked; "End Turn" and "Confirm Policy" are two primary buttons.

## Relics — The Museum Curator

![Relics: Across the Mediterranean](assets/v2s-relics-across-the-mediterranean.webp)

Take: a believable modern gallery with daylight; placing an object shows its snapping, viewing height and conservation range at once; the visitor path drawn on the floor; an exhibition has a title and a thesis. Wrong: chrome on all four sides (eight-item left rail, top statistics, right inspector, two bottom toolbars); money in US dollars and "daily visitors" make it a tycoon game about revenue, where the GDD's subject is curation, interpretation and care; the astrolabe's label ("Islamic world, c. 13th–14th century") is a placeholder, and every object shown in the game is a real museum record with a licensed image or model.

## The Knowledge Web — Six Degrees of History

![Knowledge Web: Maragha to Copernicus](assets/v2s-knowledge-web-maragha-to-copernicus.webp)

Take: start and target are unmistakable; every edge carries a typed relation in words; the current path is restated as a row at the bottom; a "forbidden shortcut" explains why two things cannot be joined directly. Wrong: "Maragha Observatory" and "Al-Tusi's Observatory" are two nodes for one place; the edge into the target ("developed mathematical models used by") states as fact a transmission that scholars debate, so in the game it is a debated relation shown with its positions and sources, and a daily challenge never depends on it as its only solution; each node is a 3D model on a starfield with a city behind it, which costs legibility and nothing else.

## Architects of History — Wonders of the World

![Architects: courtyard arcade](assets/v2s-architects-courtyard-arcade.webp)

Take: the piece being placed is drawn in a different material with its dimensions on it; snapping and the grid are visible; sun through arches onto water is the mood to match; build, blueprint, walkthrough, day and night, photo and share are one row. Wrong: the style set mixes regions and periods in one courtyard; "Moorish Window" and "Corinthian–Andalusian" are loose labels, and the museum's own terms are used; open shelves of books stand in an arcade exposed to the weather; "Structural Stability 100%" and "Daylight 92%" claim an engineering precision the simulation will not have, and the GDD asks for its limits to be stated.
