# Briefs for the six reviewers

_Written 26 November 2026 at the founder's request ([`../spec-production/owner-decisions-2026-11-26-b.md`](../spec-production/owner-decisions-2026-11-26-b.md), O1), for him to use when he looks for reviewers. **No reviewer has been named.** These briefs do not close that: the ledger stays "historical review pending" until a real person is named for each role and has decided each entry. They were drafted by an AI agent and should be read by the founder before they are sent to anyone._

## What every reviewer is told

- **What this is.** islam.museum is a free, open, digital museum of Islam. It is building three browser games. They are prototypes, marked as such, on a test address that search engines do not index. Nothing here is published as verified history.
- **What you would review.** Entries in the [historical-review ledger](ledger.md): each is one thing a game says or shows about the past, with where it appears, its source, and what is uncertain about it. You see the game itself at the same time.
- **How these were made.** Every entry was prepared by an AI agent. The museum's own records, which most entries cite, were written with AI agents from cited sources and are published **without requiring a specialist's read**. They are treated here as source material that itself needs review, not as approved fact.
- **What you are asked to decide**, entry by entry: _approved_; _approved with changes_ (you say which); or _rejected_ (it comes out of the game). A short reason is enough. "I cannot judge this" is a useful answer and is recorded as such.
- **What you are not asked.** To approve the game, its design, or its release. That is the founder's, and his approval is never recorded as a judgement of history. You are also not asked to vouch for anything you did not review.
- **Your name.** It is recorded against each decision, with the date, only with your consent to that. If you prefer, your role is recorded without your name in public pages and your name is kept by the founder.
- **Sensitive ground.** Where an entry touches disease, famine, conflict or unrest, the games' rule is mitigation and recovery, never spectacle. Tell us where that rule is not met.
- **Not yet settled, and the founder's to settle with you:** how much time is asked, whether it is paid, and how you wish to be credited.

## R1. Nasrid Granada and al-Andalus

- **Sought:** a historian or archaeologist of al-Andalus with knowledge of the emirate of Granada in the fifteenth century: its countryside and irrigation, its towns, its economy and its relations with its neighbours.
- **Entries today:** HR-GR-01 to HR-GR-10. The game "Granada" is set in the emirate in 1475 (the design's year) and plays in an invented valley, labelled as invented. It names eleven real places and links the museum's records of the Alhambra, the Generalife, the madrasa of Granada and three events.
- **Asked in particular:** whether the title "The Last Kingdom of al-Andalus" and the setting are fairly put; whether an invented valley beside real names risks being taken for a real place; what a steward of such a district could plausibly do and not do.
- **Coming, and to be entered before it is built:** scenarios and a campaign of several hours; trade with Málaga and Almería; a strategic treatment of defence and diplomacy with no battle on screen (walls, readiness, supply, warnings, tribute and negotiation); events such as drought, flood, crop disease and border tension.

## R2. Abbasid Baghdad and Islamic urban history

- **Sought:** a historian of the Abbasid period or of the Islamic city: its institutions, water, markets, endowments and administration.
- **Entries today:** HR-GA-01 to HR-GA-07. "The Golden Age" is a fictional district and says so. It is to be described as _a fictional city simulation inspired by records of Abbasid Baghdad and other historical institutions of the Islamic world_, and never as a reconstruction unless evidence supports that.
- **Asked in particular:** whether "inspired by" is honest for what the game shows; whether the game's form of the endowment (a sum set aside whose yield pays a building's upkeep) is a fair simplification; whether droughts, silting and fevers are treated with respect.
- **Coming:** a campaign of several hours in which the district grows to a city; a category of "civic resilience" in place of anything military (public safety, fire, flood, walls and gates where relevant, emergency food and water, response to disease, the safety of roads, negotiated security); real institutions only where each links to a reviewed record.

## R3. Islamic architecture, material culture and clothing

- **Sought:** an architectural historian or curator of Islamic art with knowledge of the western Mediterranean in the fifteenth century and of Abbasid Iraq.
- **Entries today:** HR-GR-06 and HR-GA-07, the two "reconstructions": the look of the valley (lime-washed houses with clay-tile roofs, a mosque with a square minaret, a watermill, a stone bridge of three arches, ox-carts, the dress of people walking) and the look of the district (houses, domes, a bath, a library, a hospital, a water wheel). **No source stands behind any of these shapes.** They are the developer's general picture and are stated nowhere as fact.
- **Asked in particular:** what is wrong, what is anachronistic, what would mislead; and what sources a modeller should work from. This review comes before any building is made in number: the first "proof frame" of each game is where your corrections cost least.
- **Rule already in force:** no figurative depiction of prophets or Companions, and no generated portraits.

## R4. History of science, medicine and education

- **Sought:** a historian of science, technology or medicine in the Islamic world.
- **Entries today:** HR-GR-05, HR-GA-02 to HR-GA-04 and HR-GA-06. The games link tools, buildings and research projects to the museum's records of real things: covered water conduits, the surveying of qanats, water-lifting wheels, the paper mill, the licensing of physicians in Baghdad, hospitals, the madrasa, market inspection, the matching of soil and water.
- **Asked in particular:** whether each link is to the right thing and fairly labelled; where a game mechanic borrows a real name for something that did not work that way.
- **Note:** the linked records are the museum's and are reviewed as source material; a doubt about a record is passed to the museum's own data, not patched in the game.

## R5. Historical geography, borders and cartography

- **Sought:** a historical geographer or cartographer of the Islamic world.
- **Entries today:** HR-AT-01 to HR-AT-04, and HR-GR-03. "Atlas" asks where a person, place or event of the museum's records was: 2,125 rounds at 568 places, each from one record. 263 events named for one place and scored at another are held out, listed for the museum to look at.
- **Asked in particular:** how to sample 2,125 rounds for review, since nobody can read them all; whether the regions used for a hint are sound; and, before anything is built, the rules for layers: no modern border in an unanswered round; historical boundaries only where reviewed, tied to a period, and marked approximate or disputed; modern borders only after the answer is shown.
- **Coming:** overlays of historical regions and routes, each needing your review before it is drawn; a globe with relief, and with night lights that are of today and must not be read as history.

## R6. Arabic and Urdu museum editing

- **Sought:** an editor who is a native reader of Arabic, and one of Urdu (one person only if truly at home in both), with experience of museum or scholarly text.
- **What there is to read:** the games' interface in each language, written by an AI agent and read by no native speaker: Granada 196 strings, The Golden Age 290, Atlas 58, the music control 12. The same again in the five other languages, for other readers.
- **Asked in particular:** mistakes; wording a museum would not use; honorifics (the games type none; the museum's pages add them); numerals, dates and the direction of mixed text; whether uncertainty and fiction are as clearly marked in Arabic and Urdu as in English.
- **Also the museum's editor:** for every entry a specialist has decided, the editor confirms wording, uncertainty and tone before it stands as approved.

## What happens to a decision

Each decision is written into [`ledger.json`](ledger.json) with the reviewer's roster id, name (if consented) and date; the generator refuses a decision without them. An entry approved with changes is changed in the game in the same pull request. An entry rejected is taken out in the same pull request. Until then every entry reads "historical review pending", and historical approval of any production release is blocked.
