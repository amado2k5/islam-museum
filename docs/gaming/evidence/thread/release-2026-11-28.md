# The Thread of Time: release evidence, 28 November 2026

**Decision.** On 28 November 2026 the founder asked to finish one game at a time, the next closest to finishing, and to mark each one complete once it is done. The Thread of Time came after Kalima and Five Questions. Its status is `production` in [`release-status.json`](../../spec-production/release-status.json) and `released` in `gaming/games/registry.json`, on his word.

**What was not done, and still is not.** The founder released the game without these:
- **A playtest.** No person outside the agents has played it.
- **Native readers.** No native speaker has read the Urdu, French, Spanish, Turkish or Indonesian strings or event names. They are machine work.
- **Real phones.** No real phone was used: the phone checks are an emulated 375×812 screen.

## Changed for this release

1. **A keyboard player no longer loses their place.**
   - After a move, focus went to the moved event's arrow in the same direction.
   - At the top or the bottom of the list that arrow is disabled, so focus was lost.
   - Now it falls back to the other arrow of the same event.
   - `tools/thread-play.ts` checks this after every single move.
2. **Arabic is set in Arabic type, in all four daily games.**
   - The shared font list ended with an Urdu Nastaliq font. On a machine without a Naskh font earlier in the list, Arabic text fell through to it: words climbed diagonally, and in the Thread's cards they ran into the line above.
   - Each script now has its own fonts, as the games' platform stylesheet already does:
     - Arabic in Naskh (SF Arabic, Geeza Pro, Noto Naskh Arabic);
     - Urdu in Nastaliq, with taller lines.
   - Kalima's and Five Questions' checks were run again after the change (56 of 56 each), and their Arabic captures were replaced.
3. **The label.** The prototype label is gone from the page and from the game's card on the museum's games page. The page's introduction and status box now name the three finished games, in all seven languages.

## Checks run

All checks ran on this machine against the local host (`tools/local-host.ts` over `dist/release`). None ran against UAT or production.

- **`tools/thread-play.ts` (new):** 56 runs, no problem found ([`thread-play-2026-11-28.json`](thread-play-2026-11-28.json)).
  - **How it plays:** whole days with the keyboard alone. Each event is raised to its place with Enter on its ▲ button, then the order is checked.
  - **Coverage:**
    - 7 languages;
    - a phone (375×812, touch) and a desktop (1440×900);
    - light and dark;
    - once in the right order and once reversed;
    - the thread of 1 December 2026.
  - **Each run checks:**
    - the page's direction;
    - that focus stays on an enabled button after every move;
    - the right-place marks;
    - that each event links to its museum record;
    - no sideways scroll at phone width;
    - that the finished day is kept after a reload;
    - no console error;
    - that every visible text has 4.5:1 contrast. WCAG exempts inactive controls, so this does not apply to the disabled ▲ of the first event or the ▼ of the last.
  - **Captures:** Arabic and English on a phone, light and dark, are kept here.
- **Unit tests:** `npm --prefix gaming test` covers 400 days of threads in every language. Each day's thread is the same for everyone and solvable, and every event it uses exists and carries a source.

## What a person should check next

1. **A playtest:** play a week of days on a real phone, in Arabic and in English.
2. **Native readers:** have native speakers read the Urdu, Turkish and Indonesian strings and event names.
3. **A screen reader:** run VoiceOver or TalkBack through one whole day. The moves are announced through a live region.
