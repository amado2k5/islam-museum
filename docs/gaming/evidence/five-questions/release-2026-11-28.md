# Five Questions: release evidence, 28 November 2026

**Decision.** On 28 November 2026 the founder asked to finish one game at a time, the next closest to finishing, and to mark each one complete once it is done. Five Questions was the game closest to finishing after Kalima. Its status is `production` in [`release-status.json`](../../spec-production/release-status.json) and `released` in `gaming/games/registry.json`, on his word.

**What was not done, and still is not.** The games' specification asks for these before a *validated* release. The founder released the game without them:
- **A playtest.** No person outside the agents has played it.
- **Native readers.** No native speaker has read the Urdu, French, Spanish, Turkish or Indonesian strings or record names. They are machine work.
- **Real phones.** No real phone was used: the phone checks are an emulated 375×812 screen.

## Changed for this release

1. **An answered question stays readable.** Once a question was answered, every answer button faded to 55% opacity, the right answer included. That took its text below readable contrast at the moment the player reads it.

   Now:
   - the chosen answer and the right answer keep full colour, with their ✓ and ✗ marks;
   - the other answers are shown in the muted text colour, not faded.

   The lowest text contrast in the game is now 4.5:1 or more in every language, size and colour scheme.
2. **The label.** The prototype label is gone from the page and from the game's card on the museum's games page. The page's introduction and status box now name Kalima and Five Questions as finished, in all seven languages.
3. **Kalima's fixes apply here too.** Five Questions shares Kalima's stylesheet, so the contrast fixes made for Kalima apply to it as well.

**Download size, recorded and left as it is.** The game needs the whole pack and every record's name, because each question's wrong answers are drawn from all of them. So it cannot load a small subset the way Kalima does. Compressed, a first visit transfers about 190 to 250 KB of data:
- the pack: 38 to 48 KB;
- one language's names: 116 to 204 KB.

That is about the size of one photograph.

## Checks run

All checks ran on this machine against the local host (`tools/local-host.ts` over `dist/release`). None ran against UAT or production.

- **`tools/five-questions-play.ts` (new):** 56 runs, no problem found ([`five-questions-play-2026-11-28.json`](five-questions-play-2026-11-28.json)).
  - **Coverage:** whole days played with the keyboard alone, the way a keyboard player does it: Tab to an answer, Enter to choose it, Enter on "Next".
    - in 7 languages;
    - on a phone (375×812, touch) and on a desktop (1440×900);
    - in light and in dark;
    - once with every answer right, and once with every answer wrong;
    - on the quiz of 1 December 2026.
  - **Each run checks:**
    - the page's direction;
    - the score;
    - that each of the five questions links to its museum record;
    - no sideways scroll at phone width;
    - that the finished day is kept after a reload;
    - no console error;
    - the contrast of every piece of visible text in the game, after every answer and on the summary.
  - **Captures:** Arabic and English on a phone, light and dark, are kept here.
- **`tools/daily-check.ts`:** all four daily games in 7 languages, on a phone and a desktop.
- **Unit tests:** `npm --prefix gaming test` covers 400 days of quizzes in every language. Each day's quiz is the same for everyone and can be answered, and every record it names exists and carries a source.

## What a person should check next

1. **A playtest:** play a week of days on a real phone, in Arabic and in English.
2. **Native readers:** have native speakers read the Urdu, Turkish and Indonesian strings and record names.
3. **A screen reader:** run VoiceOver or TalkBack through one whole day.
