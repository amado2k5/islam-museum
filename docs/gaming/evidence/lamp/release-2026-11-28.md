# The Lamp of the Ages: release evidence, 28 November 2026

**Decision.** On 28 November 2026 the founder chose the Lamp as the next game to finish: "finish it and leave me a prompt for chatgpt to create for you images and when these are ready you can update the game later". Its status is `production` in [`release-status.json`](../../spec-production/release-status.json) and `released` in `gaming/games/registry.json`, on his word.

**What the release is.** The game is released with its pictures still drawn by code: the map from Natural Earth's outline, and the lamp and its smoke as simple shapes. The page says so in "How to play". ChatGPT has the prompt for the painted art. When the founder accepts the look, the art comes in as an update.

**What was not done, and still is not:**
- **A playtest.** No person outside the agents has played it.
- **Native readers.** No native speaker has read the Urdu, French, Spanish, Turkish or Indonesian strings.
- **Real phones.** No real phone or minimum machine was used.
- **Historical review.** The review of its records (HR-LA-01, HR-LA-02) is still pending, and the page keeps its note saying so.

## Changed for this release

1. **Map names readable on a phone.** The map scales to the screen, and on a phone its city names came out near 7 pixels. They are now about 12 to 14 pixels.
2. **Record ids run left to right.** In Arabic and Urdu, a record id such as `work/kitab-al-manazir` is Latin text inside right-to-left text, and broke into jumbled pieces. Ids are now isolated as left-to-right text.
3. **The label.** The prototype label is gone from the page and from the game's card on the museum's games page. The page now names the Lamp among the finished games, in all seven languages.
4. **Shared with the daily games.** The fixes made for the daily games apply here too: the per-script fonts (Arabic in Naskh, Urdu in Nastaliq) and the contrast fixes. The Lamp uses their shared stylesheet.

## Checks run

All checks ran on this machine against the local host (`tools/local-host.ts` over `dist/release`). None ran against UAT or production.

- **`tools/lamp-play.ts` (new):** 28 whole games, no problem found ([`lamp-play-2026-11-28.json`](lamp-play-2026-11-28.json)).
  - **How it plays:** each game runs from a fresh save to the end with the keyboard alone. The century dial is moved with the arrow keys; every button is pressed with Enter. At each of the five places it meets the figure, answers wrongly once and then rightly, takes the record card and invites up to two companions.
  - **Coverage:** 7 languages × a phone (375×812, touch) and a desktop (1440×900) × light and dark.
  - **Each run checks:**
    - the page's direction;
    - that focus lands on the view's heading after every action, and stays on the dial while it moves;
    - that the dial stands at the intended century;
    - that a wrong answer keeps the question open;
    - all five cards in the satchel, each linking to the museum;
    - two companions;
    - the end message;
    - that the save survives a reload;
    - no console error;
    - no sideways scroll at phone width;
    - every button and control at least 24×24 CSS pixels (WCAG 2.2 target size);
    - every visible text at 4.5:1 contrast.
  - **A note on the dial:** in a right-to-left page the right arrow lowers a slider, as browsers intend, so the check moves the dial with the up arrow, which raises it in either direction.
  - **Captures:** the end screen in Arabic, English and Urdu on a phone, light and dark, is kept here and was looked at by eye.
- **Unit tests:** `npm --prefix gaming test` covers the Lamp's own tests:
  - every mission completes;
  - the gates and companions work;
  - every shown id is a museum record;
  - the record file equals a fresh build;
  - each answer is named in its record;
  - the save round-trips.
