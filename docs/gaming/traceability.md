## Decisions that still need the founder

- **O1.** Who the named historical reviewers are, and who gives the museum's editorial approval. Until then all 18 entries of the ledger stay pending and nothing new is stated as history. _'Already approved islam.museum records' is read as the records the museum has published: by the founder's ruling of 15 November 2026 the museum publishes on cited sources without a human read. If he means something narrower, the games' links to the museum must be cut back to it._
- **O2.** The two minimum machines. Neither is here: the games have been measured only on an Apple M4 Max. Who runs them on an M1 MacBook Air with 8 GB and on a Windows 11 laptop with a Core i5-1135G7? _The steps can be written so that anyone can run them and send back the report. A rented device service would be a paid service and his to approve._
- **O3.** How the art is made. The images show hundreds of distinct buildings, crowds and painted light; one building has been authored so far, by script in Blender. Whether that route reaches the targets' finish is unproven. _Proposed: a proof frame for each game set beside its target (package A0), for him to judge before the rest is built. If it falls short the choice is his: a human 3D artist, licensed model packs, or generative tools, each with a cost or a licence to weigh._
- **O4.** The testers: 5 to 8 for each game, then 12 to 20, three or more of them reading Arabic or Urdu. Who finds and approves them, and when. _The protocol is ready and asks for no name, face or voice in the repository._
- **O5.** Atlas's pictures. Four of the museum's pictures qualify; 52 are needed before a picture round can run weekly. The eight V&A images held for him; whether the museum's media work should gather licensed photographs of places; whether a picture round may begin in practice below 52.
- **O6.** Atlas's globe data: relief, cloud and night-light imagery in the public domain, kept as media on the games host. His approval of each set once its licence has been checked and written down. _Modern city lights can give an answer away; the night side may have to be drawn without them during a round._
- **O7.** The order after Granada: The Golden Age and then Atlas, one after the other, or beside Granada in sessions of their own. _One after the other is the plan, following his choice of Granada first and his preference for one agent at a time._
- **O8.** Granada's defence, in what may be shown. The plan is walls, towers, readiness and standing with neighbours, with no battle on screen. Say if a siege or a battle is ever to be depicted.
- **O9.** The Golden Age as Baghdad. The image names the real city and a year; the game is a fictional district and says so. Until reviewers are named it stays a fictional district, and the campaign of three hours is built as that district grown to a city. _Confirm, or say that the real Baghdad must be the subject from the start, which waits on O1._
- **O10.** A rollback on UAT has never been tried. It changes what he is looking at, so it needs his word.
- **O11.** Production. Deploying the games to production and taking any prototype label away remain his, after the evidence.
