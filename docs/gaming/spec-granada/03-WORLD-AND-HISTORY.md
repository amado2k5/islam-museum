# World, historical integrity and content pipeline
## Grounded starting point
The Nasrid dynasty is conventionally dated 1232–1492. The Alhambra was a palatine city, not just a fortress. Granada's irrigation infrastructure and surrounding fertile plain are suitable foundations for water/economic gameplay. The Albayzín and Generalife illustrate architectural and agricultural context. The Met and Archnet document Nasrid arts, including silk and ceramics and commercial ties. See [source register](11-SOURCES-AND-VALIDATION.md) for starting evidence. This does not establish every crop, route, building, population, price or date proposed here.

1475 CE is the primary concept scenario's start, selected for economic growth before the late-war crisis. Treat it as a fictional steward's story inside a historically sourced setting. The historical trajectory toward 1492 is presented in sourced chronology, not forced outcomes of the simulation. The Granada War 1482–1492 provides later authored scenario context; verify precise chapter events and political affiliations before publishing. Distinguish Crown of Castile and Crown of Aragon and their historically specific political actors; do not project modern nations or religious communities as enemies. No invented speech attributed to real historical figures.

## Spatial design and scale
Regional map: use verified coordinates for Granada, Málaga, Almería and candidate settlements. Build terrain from licensed elevation/land-cover sources, projection and bounds explicit. Distances for regional trade follow a graph of routes, not screen-space distance; travel time depends on route, weather, capacity and scenario assumptions. Show a distance scale, north marker, selected viewport and map-layer filters. Borders have uncertainty tags, period and evidence; if uncertain, use zones or dotted approximate boundaries rather than hard precision. Historical routes need sourced links, or be labeled plausible reconstruction or fictional player route.

Local settlement: Granada inland, Alhambra on Sabika hill, Darro/Genil and the Vega with plausible elevation; verify river alignment and local camera view. Do not place Mediterranean ports on the same neighboring hillside. Create separate local scene modules and regional transition screens. A stylized strategic view may compress distance but must be identified as schematic. Avoid pretending a regional map uses the same metric scale as a close-up camera. Maintain georeferenced anchors and local origin transforms (meters in local simulation; stable region IDs in strategic graph) with round-trip tests. Large world coordinates must not create visible floating-point jitter. Terrain authored for gameplay may deviate from reconstruction; tag it explicitly.

## Era-appropriate art and data
Use period-filtered architectural modules: walls, towers, gates, courtyards, plaster, timber, ceramics, roofs, irrigation canals, market stalls, houses and orchards. Verify which monuments existed at scenario date; avoid post-Nasrid structures such as later palace and church additions in the 1475 scene. Treat current-day photographs as reference, not direct historical evidence of past state. Model the actual historical Alhambra separately from player-created castles. No invented sacred inscriptions, pseudo-Arabic or generated heraldry presented as authentic. If unsure, use non-text geometric decoration and fictional neutral symbols. Characters are fictional and culturally informed; show diverse civilian roles with dignity. Use contextual historical entries for real figures, not generated likeness or dialogue where prohibited. Do not gamify worship or theological merit.

### Content record contract (proposed)
Each historical or reconstructed feature gets `recordId`, `canonicalMuseumId` if available, `kind`, `validFrom/To` (uncertainty-aware), `regionId`, `geometryConfidence`, `claimClass`, `sourceIds`, `assetRights`, `editorReview`, `localeKeys`, `gameplayProjection`. Claim classes: documented, approximate reconstruction, disputed, fictional scenario, simulated value. Never allow mods to change canonical claims. In-game inspector offers source link in active locale, date range and explicit class. Fictional prices, troop counts, contracts, characters, population and quests are always simulation, not museum records. If data missing use clearly fictional name or omit; do not backfill history via plausible hallucination.

## Data compilation
Inspect actual repository knowledge graph and existing museum IDs. Build a small versioned Granada pack containing verified sites, time ranges, public metadata, localized titles, claims, asset manifest and normalized geographic graph. Separate curated historical content from recipes, economic tuning and quests. Cache by graph digest and game schema version. Validate references, coordinate order, scenario dates, missing translations, duplicate IDs, source links and rights. Report actual dataset coverage and unresolved questions; keep source notes in approved engineering docs. No entire knowledge graph in the initial browser load. Avoid data-driven changes silently invalidating a running save: pin content version, migrate only with backup and explicit compatibility checks.

## Campaign expansion candidates (not launch obligations)
1232–38 establishment, 14th-century cultural/architectural development, 1475 economic/diplomatic growth, 1482 onset of war, 1487 territorial crisis, 1491–92 final negotiations, alternate history beyond 1492. Verify each scenario's territory, authority, population movement, economic opportunities and time boundaries. Do not claim historical victory or survival if the game produces it. A replay summary may contrast simulation choices with the documented historical outcome through source-linked museum cards.
