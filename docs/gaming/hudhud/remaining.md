# Hudhud: what remains before it is a whole game of many hours

_26 November 2026, brought up to date later that day (the third to twelfth trial quests, and what is public; the eleventh, the Alhambra and the dry fountain, and the twelfth, al-Razi's loose leaves, added 27 November 2026, and the thirteenth, Karima's copies, later that day, UTC). The founder asked, in chat: "what is remaining for this game to be fully completed with all its scenarios, game play, stories, features, graphics, with a gameplay for visitor that would last hours?" This page is the answer, kept current. The plan it measures against is [his instruction](owner-instruction-2026-11-26.md); the estimate is in [the design](design.md#how-big-this-is-said-plainly)._

## Rooms for every scene (27 November 2026)

The founder asked on 27 November 2026 for every scene to be a complete room with its own figure, not a page and a corridor. **Built**: the room engine (`scenes/room.ts`) and three rooms on it, Ibn al-Haytham's workshop, al-Jazari's workshop (the elephant clock) and al-Tusi's observatory (the two circles, added later on 27 November 2026 after the founder said to continue all the remaining work); the two new rooms are code-drawn trial art, a trial of the look and not final art. **Still to build: ten rooms**, one for each quest still played on a page (al-Kindi's secret page, al-Khwarizmi's locked box, al-Idrisi's scattered sheets, al-Jahiz's animal cards, the paper of Samarkand, Ibn Battuta's thread, the journey of coffee, the Alhambra and the dry fountain, al-Razi's loose leaves, Karima's copies). Each is about one session at trial quality (the al-Jazari room took one, and so did al-Tusi's: its room, figures, rules over the quest's own, words in seven languages, tests and browser checks); until a scene has its room, its page and its corridor stay so that the journey runs end to end.

## What must be built, in the order it unblocks the rest

**1. A decision on the look (the founder's).** Everything drawn waits on it. If the trial is not accepted, the routes are an illustrator and animator, the process that made the 80 thumbnails, or a blend with code for light and effects ([art bible](art-bible.md#how-the-art-can-actually-be-made)). Until then the agent builds rules, words and features with replaceable trial art, and draws nothing in number.

**2. The other systems of play.** Of the twelve, three are built in the flying scene, and two more (Mechanism, Cipher) have rules, Mechanism with three trial quests (the elephant clock, the paper of Samarkand and al-Razi's loose leaves) and Cipher with one; a rule set for equations was also built, outside the twelve, and others since (exchanging sheets, sorting cards, threading a route, setting a mechanism, turning channels, and comparing copies sign by sign for Karima's copies, which comes nearest to Observe and Match without being it). Still to build: Mechanism beyond the chain (gears, floats, valves), Cipher beyond one quest (neighbouring letters, pointing at the picture), Path (the design gave it to al-Khwarizmi, whose trial quest uses the equation rules instead), Route (the quest of Ibn Battuta's thread is a smaller idea, not this), Trace and Align, Observe and Match, Mend and Build, Network, Rhythm. Each needs: tested rules, a generator of challenge rooms from seeds, a Story form, a form in words. About one to two working sessions each for the rules; the room, art and words of its first quest are on top.

**3. The frame the quests hang in.**
- A playable prologue: the storm, meeting Hudhud, the first flight, the first page. (Built so far: the five still panels of the opening story, which tell the storm and the meeting. Still missing: anything to play in it, animation, voice, and a reading by a child.)
- The hub as a place: the Atlas's hall, with ten wings that fill as pages return and characters who visit. (Built so far: the Atlas page, with one code-drawn picture of the open book whose medallions show how many pages of each world are home and lead to its list. Still missing: a hub the bird can fly about in, wings that change as pages return, and visiting characters.)
- A world map and, for each world, a thread that ties its five quests and something that changes when they are done.
- An ending, and the post-game: the last page, the player's own.

**4. Thirty-seven quests** (thirteen of the fifty exist as first trials, counting the flying scene, and each of those still wants everything below). For each: read the museum's records; write the claims into the review ledger; design the room and the puzzle on one or two of the systems; write the three layers of words (essential lines, "ask more", the museum's note) in Arabic first and then six more languages; three goals and a secret; draw it; test it; capture it. Fifteen to twenty-five minutes of play each.

**5. The long game.** Mastery goals a quest; hidden things of five kinds; time trials and precision routes; daily and weekly challenge rooms from seeds with an archive that never expires; postcards made on the device; New Journey+; side stories for characters.

**6. Features not yet built.** A simple-language option and reading help; a real phone layout; loading a world at a time; texture atlases (the 2048-pixel limit itself is held since 26 November 2026, defect D15 in the [test plan](test-plan.md), and keyboard and gamepad remapping are built); a recording as well as stills.

**7. Evidence that it works for people and machines.** Both reference machines; Firefox, Safari, Edge, iOS and Android; a 60-minute profile and a four-hour soak (a ten-minute soak of UAT was made on 26 November 2026, see the evidence above); a screen-reader pass and a conformance-level accessibility audit (the machine scan above is not one); tests with children aged 6 to 9, 10 to 13 and 14 to 17, with parents, and with Arabic and Urdu readers, under the museum's consent process; fixes; a second round.

**8. The museum's side.** The Play page, the game's own page and the menu entry ([plan](website-plan.md)); a UAT release with rollback rehearsed (UAT and production both exist for the games now, and no rollback has been rehearsed with Hudhud in them); then the museum's own pages, which still do not mention the games, at the founder's word.

## How long

An agent can write the rules, the words and the features at roughly a system or a quest's rules in a session; that part is perhaps 110 to 170 sessions. It is the smaller part. With art at about a world a month, review keeping pace and playtests between worlds, **about a year from an accepted look**. With no accepted look, there is no date. These are estimates from one scene and have no playtest behind them.
