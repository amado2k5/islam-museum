# Hudhud: the player's explorers

_26 November 2026. The founder asked for this in chat while the first pull request was open, in three messages, in his words: "also allow the user to start using male and female characters, young discoverers and explorers", "of different colors and clothing and from different areas in muslim world from the past 1000 years"._

## What is built

On the first screen, before Play, the player chooses **a girl explorer or a boy explorer**. Each face on that screen is drawn by the same code as the figure in the scene, in the look that will be played. In Settings the explorer can be changed at any time without losing the quest: girl or boy, four skin tones, three sets of clothes, four kinds of hair or headwear (short curls, a headscarf, an embroidered cap, two braids). That is 96 looks from one set of parts.

In languages that mark it, the explorer's title and lines take the form that fits: «حافظة الحكايات» and «حافظ الحكايات», « Gardienne » and « Gardien », «Guardiana» and «Guardián», «رکھوالی» and «رکھوالا», and one Urdu line whose verb changes. A test checks that every such form exists in all seven languages.

The save keeps three small numbers and a 0 or 1. It asks for no name and no age.

## What is not built, and why it is not a quick addition

The clothes that exist are one generic tunic and vest in three colourings. They are **not** the dress of any region or century, and nothing on the page says they are.

Explorers "from different areas of the Muslim world over a thousand years" means children whose dress, hair and headwear are right for a named place and time. The founder's own instruction (section 3) says region- and time-specific clothing is used only after review, and warns against one costume for every culture and against caricature. A code-drawn guess at, say, a girl of fourteenth-century Timbuktu is exactly where that risk is highest. So this is set out as a roster to be researched, reviewed and then drawn by whichever art route the founder chooses ([art bible](art-bible.md)), not drawn now.

**What each needs before it is drawn:** what children of that place and time wore, from a source a reviewer accepts (reviewer R3, architecture, material culture and clothing: not named); a skin tone range chosen without stereotype; a name; an entry in the review ledger. Until then the game offers the 96 generic looks and says nothing about where they are from. Entered in the ledger as HR-HH-09.

**A design question for the founder.** Is the chosen explorer the same child in all ten worlds (a visitor from the museum, in the museum's own clothes), or does the player pick a child of a region and time and travel as them? The first is simpler and avoids putting a tenth-century child in a seventeenth-century city without comment; the second gives the roster more meaning. The frame story as written assumes the first.
