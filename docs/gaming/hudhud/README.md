_A 2D story game for the museum's gaming platform. Working title. **Prototype: one scene of a few minutes and nine short trial quests (al-Jazari's elephant clock: a row of parts put in order; al-Kindi's secret page: marks counted, ranked and given their signs; al-Khwarizmi's locked box: a problem said in words brought to one thing equal to a number by restoring, balancing and sharing; al-Idrisi's scattered sheets: the sheets of an imagined map exchanged until every seam matches; al-Jahiz's animal cards: eight animals put in the groups his passage gives them; Samarkand's paper: a papermaker's steps put in order; and Ibn Battuta's thread: the stops of a stage of his journeys threaded in the order the record gives; and al-Tusi's two circles: a small circle set turning inside a large one until the point on it traces a straight line; and the journey of coffee: cards about coffee put on three shelves by how sure the museum's record is of each (the record states it plainly, a tradition, a legend); all nine played with ordinary page controls and a simple code-drawn picture, no flying scene, and kept when left in the middle). Public at `games.islam.museum` since 26 November 2026, from an earlier build (commit `885c7d97a5a`, read on 26 November 2026) that has the flying scene, the elephant clock, the opening story, the secret page and the locked box, but not the scattered sheets, the animal cards, the paper, Ibn Battuta's thread, al-Tusi's two circles or the journey of coffee.** Started 26 November 2026 on the founder's instruction, which is filed here unedited and governs: [`owner-instruction-2026-11-26.md`](owner-instruction-2026-11-26.md) (SHA-256 `5942fd78824a788cadc656dba755730498b7d1749fb280a476df231006f20b66`; taken from the session it was pasted into, as that message's pasted text)._

## Found in passing in the museum's records, and not changed

While looking for names to show in the Atlas, the museum's own labels were read for the fifty subjects (26 November 2026). They were not used, and nothing in `data/` was touched; this is reported for whoever owns those records:

- 39 of the 50 first records, all of them `person` records, have no Turkish and no Indonesian `label`.
- Five have an Arabic `label` only: `person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi`, `person/ibn-al-shatir`, `person/ibn-al-baytar`, `person/wallada-bint-al-mustakfi`, `person/usama-ibn-munqidh`.
- Some Turkish labels read as mistranslations: `concept/pigeon-post` is «Mesaj Banyosu (kartlar)», which says "message bath"; `work/alf-layla-wa-layla` is «Bin Bir Gece (Efsanelerinin Hazarı)». Seen earlier the same day, outside the fifty: `work/kitab-al-manazir` (the Book of Optics) is «Tartışmalar Kitabı» in Turkish and «Buku Perdebatan» in Indonesian, both of which say "book of debates".

Found while writing the quests that use these records (26 November 2026, each read again in `data/` and `content/` before it was written here; nothing under `data/` or `content/` was touched, and the games' own tests pin the places where a game uses a different form):

- **Place labels that are plainly wrong (field `label`).** `place/fars` has Turkish «Şövalye» ("knight") and Indonesian «Ksatria» ("knight") for Fars. `place/zeila` has Turkish «Sil» and Indonesian «Hapus» ("wipe", "delete") for Zeila. `place/zaila`, a separate record, has Turkish «Üzgün» ("sad") and Indonesian «Kecewa» ("disappointed"). `place/tanja` has Urdu «تانگیر», while the museum's Urdu pages write «طنجہ» (106 times in 61 pages under `content/ur/`, against two uses of «تانگیر»). `place/maragha` has Urdu «مراگھا۔», with a stray full stop, while the Urdu pages write «مراغہ» (105 times, and «مراگھا» not once). `person/batlamus` has French «Ptolemy» and Spanish «Ptolemy», the English name left untranslated, while the French and Spanish pages write «Ptolémée» (352 times) and «Ptolomeo» (354 times); it has no Turkish or Indonesian label. `person/hulagu` has an Arabic `label` only («هولاكو خان»); the Urdu pages write «ہلاکو» (193 times) and «ہولاکو» (14 times), and the record's own Urdu page uses the rarer form.
- **Dates that two records give differently.** (1) The Nuzhat al-Mushtaq: `work/nuzhat-al-mushtaq` (field `composed`, Hijri `0548-10`) and `person/al-idrisi-muhammad-ibn-abd-allah` (summary) say Shawwal 548 AH; `event/nuzhat-al-mushtaq-completed-549` (field `date`, Hijri `0549`, 1154) says 549. (2) The finishing of the Rihla: `content/en/person/ibn-battuta-muhammad.md` says 3 Dhu al-Hijja 756, "which is 9 December 1357"; `work/rihla-ibn-battuta` (field `composed`, `1355-12`, Hijri `0756-12-03`) has December 1355 for the same Hijri day. (3) Ibn Battuta's age on leaving Tangier: `content/en/person/ibn-battuta-muhammad.md` says "aged twenty-two" (after the Rihla's opening); `event/ibn-battuta-sets-out-for-hajj-1325` (summary, after Britannica) says "aged 21". (4) The founding of the observatory at Maragha: `building/maragha-observatory` (field `built`) dates it 657/1259; `content/en/polity/ilkhanate.md` says it was built "about 656/1258"; `content/en/building/maragha-observatory.md` itself says its life was "55 or 60 years" in one paragraph, and the same record's summary (`data/building/maragha-observatory.yaml`) says "some 55 or 60 years", while the paragraph on al-Tusi that it cites, and `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md`, say it "lasted only about fifty years".
- **Four accounts of how paper came to Samarkand.** `invention/paper-mill-samarkand` (summary) says "transmitted accounts" tell of captured Chinese artisans after the battle near the Talas in 133 to 134/751, and keeps the doubt; `place/samarqand` (`content/en/place/samarqand.md`) says without any caution that paper was "brought in by the Chinese artisans captured at the battle of Talas in 133/751"; `content/en/job/papermaker.md` says some researchers put the start of Samarkand paper with the captives of Talas in 756 and that the Iranica assessment differs; `invention/baghdad-papermaking` reports, through Bloom's Iranica article, a Manichaean community producing paper for export in Samarkand in the Hudud al-Alam.

## To play it on this machine

```bash
npm --prefix gaming run dev
```

Then open `http://127.0.0.1:4330/games/hudhud/index.html?lang=ar` (or `en`, `ur`, `fr`, `es`, `tr`, `id`). Add `&q=high`, `&style=story` or `&motion=reduced` to try a preset. The music control appears only where the museum's tracks have been imported (`npm --prefix gaming run music:import`).

```bash
npm --prefix gaming run hudhud:play -- media/hudhud/captures/mine en high 1920 1080
```

plays it in real Chrome and writes pictures and a report.
