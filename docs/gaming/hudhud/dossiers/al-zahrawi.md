## What the records say they did

1. Abu al-Qasim Khalaf ibn Abbas al-Zahrawi, an Andalusian physician and surgeon, authored *al-Tasrif*, which includes detailed drawings and explanations of surgical instruments, such as the urethral-stone drill. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — Summary`; citation `{cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191} {cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp. 132–133} {cite:alzahrawi-stones-abdelhalim-2003 pp. 1286–1289}`; flag: **stated**.
2. Esin Kahya's biography warns that al-Zahrawi's fame does not correlate with extensive early biographical details, and his birthplace is uncertain, though most researchers believe it to be Madinat al-Zahra near Cordoba. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — A life with sparse testimony`; citation `{cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191} {cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191} {cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191}`; flag: **stated as uncertain**.
3. The biography notes that the death date of 404 AH/1013, advanced age, and service to al-Mansur are based on a later account by Leo Africanus and are recorded cautiously, differing from a 2003 study's confident claims. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — A life with sparse testimony`; citation `{cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191} {cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191} {cite:alzahrawi-stones-abdelhalim-2003 p. 1284} {cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191}`; flag: **stated**.
4. The 30-book *al-Tasrif* covers medical principles, diseases, symptoms, medicines, and food preparation, not just operations, and is described as a compilation of about fifty years of learning and experience. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — An encyclopaedia, not only surgery`; citation `{cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp. 132–133} {cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp. 132–133} {cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191} {cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp. 132–133}`; flag: **stated**.
5. The surgical book in *al-Tasrif* includes over 200 instrument illustrations and covers various surgical practices, including eye and dental work, fractures, and urinary disease, which are historical practices, not modern medical advice. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — An encyclopaedia, not only surgery`; citation `{cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp. 132–133} {cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191}`; flag: **stated**.
6. Abdel-Halim and colleagues translate surgical chapters 60–61, noting that the author describes his personal experience with surgical instruments without providing patient records or modern statistical evaluations. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — Instruments, experience and evidence limits`; citation `{cite:alzahrawi-stones-abdelhalim-2003 pp. 1283–1284} {cite:alzahrawi-stones-abdelhalim-2003 pp. 1288–1289} {cite:alzahrawi-stones-abdelhalim-2003 pp. 1283, 1287–1289}`; flag: **stated**.
7. The paper explicitly identifies al-Zahrawi as a Muslim surgeon, using Sunni as the default frame for this context, not quoting his personal legal-school allegiance. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — Instruments, experience and evidence limits`; citation `{cite:alzahrawi-stones-abdelhalim-2003 pp. 1283–1284} {cite:tdv-zehravi pp. 189–191}`; flag: **stated as uncertain**.
8. Surgical chapter 87 describes the use of animal-intestinal filaments for internal suturing, which Leclerc notes remains experimental, and Majeed credits this as one of al-Zahrawi's surgical developments. — source location `content/en/person/al-zahrawi.md — Suture material and the experimental qualification`; citation `{cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211} {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 pp.207–208} {cite:majeed-islam-medicine-2005 pp.1486–1487}`; flag: **stated**.
