## What the records say they did

1. The Encyclopaedia of Islam article on al-Biruni, written by D. J. Boilot, introduces Abu al-Rayhan Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Biruni as one of the greatest scholars of medieval Islam. The author adds his own judgement that he was also the most original and the most profound. He was at home in the mathematical, astronomical, physical and natural sciences, and distinguished himself as a geographer, historian,… — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
2. According to the article, al-Biruni was born of an Iranian family in 362/973, in the suburb of Kath, the capital of Khwarizm, a region in the delta of the Amu Darya. The Gregorian day given is 4 September, with the Islamic day cited from al-Ghadanfar as the third of Dhu al-Hijja. He spent his first twenty-five years in his homeland, where he was trained by masters such as the mathematician Abu Nasr Mansur ibn Ali… — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Birth and early training`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
3. The article says he seems to have visited the Samanid sultan Mansur II ibn Nuh, who reigned 387 to 389/997 to 999, and whom he praised as his first benefactor. He then stayed for a long time at Jurjan, south-east of the Caspian Sea, apparently from 388/998, when the Ziyarid ruler Qabus ibn Wushmgir, called Shams al-Ma'ali, returned from exile. From there he was able to travel as far as Rayy. At the court of… — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Birth and early training`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
4. The article notes that he was raised in the Iranian dialect of Khwarizm and spoke Persian, yet deliberately chose Arabic for his scientific writing. It adds that some later works may have been written in Persian, or in Arabic and Persian together. — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Language`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
5. The article reports that he returned to his own country before 399/1008 and was received by Prince Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ma'mun. He then served for seven years the prince's brother, the Khwarizmshah Abu al-Abbas Ma'mun ibn Ma'mun. Because of what the article calls his golden and silver tongue, he was entrusted with delicate political missions. — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Return to Khwarizm`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
6. The article also mentions two colleagues. Ibn Sina is said to have left the Khwarizmian capital for Jurjan of his own will in 398/1008, together with the Christian physician Abu Sahl Isa ibn Yahya al-Masihi al-Jurjani. This physician collaborated closely with al-Biruni, even to the point of writing a series of works in his name, as did Abu Nasr. — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Return to Khwarizm`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
7. In 407/1016 to 1017 the Khwarizmshah was killed by his rebellious troops, and the country was then conquered by the Ghaznavid sultan Mahmud ibn Sebuktegin. In the spring of 408/1017 many prisoners were taken to Ghazna, among them learned men, including al-Biruni, Abu Nasr and the physician Abu al-Khayr al-Husayn ibn Baba al-Khammar al-Baghdadi. — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Ghazna and India`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
8. From then on al-Biruni was kept at the court of Ghazna, possibly as official astrologer, and he accompanied Mahmud on several military expeditions to north-west India. The article says that he taught the Greek sciences there and received in exchange an initiation into Sanskrit and various dialects, and an immense body of knowledge. He put it into his description of India, Tahqiq ma li-l-Hind, completed in… — source location `content/en/person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad.md — Ghazna and India`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1300}`; flag: **stated**.
