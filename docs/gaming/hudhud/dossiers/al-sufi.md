## What the records say they did

1. Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Umar Abu l-Husayn al-Sufi al-Razi was an astronomer of Persian lands. The Biblissima authority record gives his years as 903 to 986 CE . He is known for his book on the fixed stars. — source location `content/en/person/abd-al-rahman-al-sufi.md — Summary`; citation `{cite:biblissima Q16843}`; flag: **stated**.
2. The museum's card records the nisba al-Razi as a sign of an association with Rayy, not as proof that he lived there. This page holds no detailed life story, because his entry in a biographical dictionary has not yet been read on the page . — source location `content/en/person/abd-al-rahman-al-sufi.md — In the sources`; citation `{cite:biblissima Q16843}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Rayy is an ancient city near present-day Tehran. It was among the greatest cities of Persia in the Abbasid era. The physician Abu Bakr al-Razi is attributed to it. — source location `content/en/place/rayy.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:geonames-gazetteer}`; flag: **stated**.
4. Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Rhaga(i)/Europos/Arsakia/Ray» (Pleiades no. 903104), classed as settlement, with activity attested from 750 BCE to 640 CE. Among its recorded names are «Rhagai», «Europos» and «Arsakia». Pleiades describes Rhagai, now the city of Rey in Iran, as an important settlement of the Medes that declined after the Mongol invasions. — source location `content/en/place/rayy.md — In the Pleiades gazetteer`; citation `{cite:pleiades 903104}`; flag: **stated**.
