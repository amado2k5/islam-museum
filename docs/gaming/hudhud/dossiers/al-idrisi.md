## What the records say they did

1. Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Idris, known as al-Idrisi, was a geographer who wrote the "Nuzhat al-Mushtaq fi ikhtiraq al-afaq" for King Roger II of Sicily. — source location `content/en/person/al-idrisi-muhammad-ibn-abd-allah.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.4}`; flag: **stated**.
2. Al-Idrisi opens the book by praising the great king Roger, "the victorious by God's might", whom he calls king of "Sicily and Italy, Ankabarda and Calabria". He says that the king conquered lands to east and west. — source location `content/en/person/al-idrisi-muhammad-ibn-abd-allah.md — The book and the king`; citation `{cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.4}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Al-Idrisi says that the book was finished in the first ten days of the month that corresponds to Shawwal, in the year 548 AH. — source location `content/en/person/al-idrisi-muhammad-ibn-abd-allah.md — The date of completion`; citation `{cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.7}`; flag: **stated**.
4. After describing the islands, al-Idrisi says that he will describe "Upper Sicily", which he calls "unique in its age for excellence and virtues". — source location `content/en/person/al-idrisi-muhammad-ibn-abd-allah.md — Sicily in the book`; citation `{cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.588}`; flag: **stated**.
5. The Norman king Roger II hosted al-Idrisi at Palermo in Sicily about 1138, commissioning a survey of the earth's knowledge and a silver celestial sphere . — source location `data/event/al-idrisi-at-roger-ii-court.yaml summary.en`; citation `{cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}`; flag: **only in a summary, not in the prose**.
6. Al-Idrisi completed for Roger II the Nuzhat al-Mushtaq geography in 549/1154 with the celebrated planisphere known as the Tabula Rogeriana . — source location `data/event/nuzhat-al-mushtaq-completed-549.yaml summary.en`; citation `{cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}`; flag: **only in a summary, not in the prose**.
7. The Lighthouse of Alexandria: a tall lighthouse on the coast of Alexandria, built of finely cut stone. Al-Idrisi reports that its height is about three hundred cubits, that a fire was lit in it night and day to guide ships, and that it was called a lantern . — source location `content/en/place/al-manara-iskandariyya.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.320}`; flag: **stated**.
8. Al-Maqrizi says that most people of Alexandria attribute it to Alexander son of Philip the Macedonian. Others say that the queen Dalukah built it, and others that the tenth of the pharaohs of Egypt did . — source location `content/en/place/al-manara-iskandariyya.md — According to al-Maqrizi`; citation `{cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.476}`; flag: **stated**.
