## What the records say they did

1. Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Kinani, Ibn Jubayr, an Andalusian traveller from Valencia and Granada. He performed the hajj in 579 AH and wrote an account of his journey, which describes the roads and the cities. — source location `content/en/person/ibn-jubayr-muhammad.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.491}`; flag: **stated**.
2. On his first journey he stayed in Damascus, Mosul and Baghdad, and he embarked from Acre with the Franks. His ship was damaged in the narrow gulf of Sicily, and he endured hardships until he reached al-Andalus in 581 AH. — source location `content/en/person/ibn-jubayr-muhammad.md — The journey`; citation `{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.491}`; flag: **stated**.
3. He set out again for the East, and died in Alexandria on Wednesday the twenty-seventh of Sha'ban 614 AH. Al-Maqqari relays from Ibn al-Raqiq that prayers made at his grave are answered. — source location `content/en/person/ibn-jubayr-muhammad.md — Later life`; citation `{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.491}`; flag: **stated**.
4. He transmitted the *Shifa'* (the Book of Healing) from Muhammad ibn Isa al-Tamimi, from the judge Iyad, and he afterwards held the judgeship of Ceuta. — source location `content/en/person/ibn-jubayr-muhammad.md — Later life`; citation `{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.491}`; flag: **stated**.
5. The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes Muhammad ibn Ahmad Ibn Jubayr (died 1217) as a Muslim traveller from Spain, best known for his Rihlat Ibn Jubayr. It counts this book among the earliest medieval travel accounts and says it is still an important source for the society of the Mediterranean shores and the Arabian peninsula in that age. — source location `content/en/person/ibn-jubayr-muhammad.md — In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam`; citation `{cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e946}`; flag: **stated**.
6. Ibn Jubayr stayed at Baghdad describing the Nizamiyya college, the quarter of Abd al-Qadir and its markets . — source location `data/event/ibn-jubayr-baghdad-580.yaml summary.en`; citation `{cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907}`; flag: **only in a summary, not in the prose**.

## Three candidate quest premises

These are proposals for Claude and the founder, not content.

1. A journey problem asks the player to apply Route and Journal to a documented leg or account, with uncertainty visible rather than hidden.

2. Hudhud helps compare what a traveller says, what a later record says and what the thumbnail invents, using Talk plus Route and Journal.

3. The quest returns a page to the Atlas, with optional context, a hidden collectible and replay goals; this structure is game design only, not historical content.

## Sensitivities

Travel accounts may touch war, slavery, illness, death, sect and living disputes. Do not turn real trauma into amusement; label literary or uncertain reportage; invented dialogue must be dramatized paraphrase.
