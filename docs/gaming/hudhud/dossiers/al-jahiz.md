## What the records say they did

1. Abu Uthman Amr ibn Bahr ibn Mahbub al-Kinani al-Laythi, known as al-Jahiz, a man of letters and scholar from Basra. He was born around 150 AH (767 CE) and died in 255 AH (869 CE). Al-Mas'udi says that he died in Basra in Muharram 255 AH, though it is also said 256 AH. — source location `content/en/person/al-jahiz.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.568}`; flag: **stated**.
2. The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, describes him as a famous Arab prose writer, the author of works of adab, of Mu'tazili theology and of politico-religious polemics. It gives his full name as Abu Uthman Amr ibn Bahr al-Fukaymi al-Basri. 3. According to the Encyclopaedia, he was born in Basra into an obscure family of clients from the Banu Kinana, probably of Abyssinian origin. His nickname, the article explains, comes from a malformation of the eyes, a projecting cornea. Little is known of his childhood. He mixed with the groups that gathered in the mosque to discuss many questions, watched the philological discussions held at the Mirbad, and followed the lectures of the leading scholars of philology, lexicography and poetry, among them al-Asma'i, Abu Ubayda and Abu Zayd. The article says that he soon mastered the Arabic language together with the usual traditional learning. — source location `content/en/person/al-jahiz.md — Origins and the Basra years`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 410}`; flag: **stated**.
5. His quick mind won him a place in Mu'tazili circles and in the salons of the educated middle class. 6. The Encyclopaedia judges that Basra in its prime shaped his mind decisively, leaving on it a rationalist and realist stamp. In its view he could be seen as the most complete representative of the city, since what he later learned in Baghdad changed his cast of mind very little, and Basra is, in its words, the continuous thread through all his works. — source location `content/en/person/al-jahiz.md — Origins and the Basra years`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 410} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 412}`; flag: **stated**.
