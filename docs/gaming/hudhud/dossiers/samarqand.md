## What the records say they did

1. Samarkand, an ancient city on the Zarafshan river, was the largest city in the region during early Islamic times, despite Bukhara being the administrative capital under the Samanids. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1105}`; flag: **stated**.
2. The origin of the first part of Samarkand's name is unclear, but the second part, 'kand', means 'town'. Historical evidence shows it was known as Marakanda during Alexander's campaigns. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — Name and ancient history`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1108}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Chinese sources indicate that a dynasty related to the Yüe-či had ruled Samarkand since before the Christian era, and the city was visited by the Buddhist pilgrim Hiuen-tsang around 630. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — Name and ancient history`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1108}`; flag: **stated**.
4. The article doubts the tradition that the Prophet's cousin Qutham ibn al-Abbas was in Samarkand in 676, suggesting the cult of his tomb arose later under the Abbasids. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — Arab arrival and the rulers of Sughd`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1108}`; flag: **stated**.
5. Tarkhun, the Iranian ruler of Samarkand, paid tribute to the Arabs but was later deposed and possibly killed by an anti-Arab faction. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — Arab arrival and the rulers of Sughd`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1108}`; flag: **stated**.
6. Islamisation in Samarkand was slow beyond the Arab garrison, despite Qutayba building a mosque there. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — Arab arrival and the rulers of Sughd`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1108}`; flag: **stated**.
7. Samarkand was home to various religious groups, including Christians and Buddhists, with Buddhism almost disappearing by the eighth century. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — The faiths of the city`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1108}`; flag: **stated**.
8. The city was affected by the 'wearers of white' movement under the early Abbasids, and its outer wall was reportedly built by Abu Muslim and later repaired by Harun al-Rashid. — source location `content/en/place/samarqand.md — Abbasid rule and the Samanids`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1108}`; flag: **stated**.
