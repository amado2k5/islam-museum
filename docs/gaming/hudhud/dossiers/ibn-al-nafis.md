## What the records say they did

1. ʿAla al-Din ʿAli ibn Abi al-Hazm, known as Ibn al-Nafis, was the shaykh of medicine in Egypt, the one to whom knowledge of medicine ultimately led. He also took part in jurisprudence, the principles of law, hadith, Arabic and logic. Al-Subki said, "There was no one like him on the face of the earth in medicine." It was also said that after Ibn Sina no one like him had come, and that he was greater than him in… — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-nafis.md — The shaykh of medicine`; citation `{cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 702-703}`; flag: **stated**.
2. He wrote the medical book "al-Shamil," whose index suggests that it would run to three hundred volumes. He made fair copies of eighty volumes of it. He dictated his works from memory and did not need to consult sources, because of his mastery of the field. He died on the twenty-first of Dhu al-Qaʿda 687 AH, when he was nearly eighty. He endowed his properties and his books to the Mansuri hospital, and no one like… — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-nafis.md — "Al-Shamil" and the endowment`; citation `{cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 703}`; flag: **stated**.
