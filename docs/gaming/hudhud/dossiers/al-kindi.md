## What the records say they did

1. Abu Yusuf Ya'qub ibn Ishaq ibn al-Sabbah al-Kindi, called the "Philosopher of the Arabs". Ibn al-Nadim describes him as the most excellent man of his age and the unique one of his time in knowledge of the ancient sciences, and says his books were on logic, philosophy, geometry, arithmetic, music and the stars. — source location `content/en/person/al-kindi.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 357}`; flag: **stated**.
2. Al-Mas'udi counts him among those who engaged in the sciences of astronomy after the appearance of Islam, together with al-Khwarizmi, Abu Ma'shar, al-Farghani, al-Battani and others. — source location `content/en/person/al-kindi.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 519}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Al-Mas'udi says that he wrote a treatise in two parts refuting the claims of those who claim to make gold and silver without their ores. — source location `content/en/person/al-kindi.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 619-620}`; flag: **stated**.
4. The Encyclopaedia of Islam article on al-Kindi, whose account this page follows alongside the sources above, gives his full name as Abu Yusuf Ya'qub ibn Ishaq and notes that the bio-bibliographers supply a distinguished genealogy for him. It says he was born before the end of the second Islamic century and died around the middle of the third, perhaps roughly 185 to 252 AH, which is 801 to 866 CE. — source location `content/en/person/al-kindi.md — Life and times`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 140}`; flag: **stated**.
5. The article describes him as an eminent universal scholar and philosopher who lived through a period of intellectual ferment in the sciences and in kalam, the age of the translations and of the controversies over Mu'tazilism. — source location `content/en/person/al-kindi.md — Life and times`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 140}`; flag: **stated**.
6. He was a companion of the caliphs al-Ma'mun and al-Mu'tasim. He dedicated his work On first philosophy to the latter, and he dedicated a number of other treatises to al-Mu'tasim's son Ahmad, who was his pupil. The article adds that he fell into disfavour under al-Mutawakkil and was even deprived for a time of his large library. — source location `content/en/person/al-kindi.md — Life and times`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 140}`; flag: **stated**.
