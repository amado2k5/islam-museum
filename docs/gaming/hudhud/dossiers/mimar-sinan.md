## What the records say they did

1. Sinan, born in 1490, was the chief court architect of the Ottoman Empire from 1538 until his death in 1588, according to B. O'Kane. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 644}`; flag: **stated**.
2. Sinan, originally a Christian Greek, was taken in the devshirme levy and served in various military roles before becoming an architect, learning his craft through military construction projects. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — From the levy to the court`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 644}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Three manuscript sources of the late sixteenth century set out his work: the Tadhkirat al-bunyan and Tadhkirat al-abniya of Mustafa Sa'i, and the anonymous but partly autobiographical Tuhfat al-mi'marin. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — How many buildings`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 644}`; flag: **stated**.
4. Sinan's three largest complexes, Shehzade, Suleymaniye, and Selimiye, each represent a step in his architectural maturity. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — The Shehzade`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 644}`; flag: **stated**.
5. Sinan's work introduced more windows and a more intricate roofline compared to earlier Ottoman mosques. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — The Shehzade`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 644}`; flag: **stated**.
6. The Suleymaniye in Istanbul, built in 957-64/1550-7, was the most ambitious single Ottoman complex, with some fourteen buildings of different uses fitted onto the sloping ground around the mosque. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — The Suleymaniye`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 644}`; flag: **stated**.
7. The Selimiye complex in Edirne features a dome similar in size to Hagia Sophia's but with a different plan and more light. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — The Selimiye`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 644} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 645}`; flag: **stated**.
8. Sinan's smaller projects include the Rustem Pasha mosque, known for its Iznik tiles, and the Mihrimah Sultan mosque, which had a teacher appointed to it. — source location `content/en/person/mimar-sinan.md — Smaller works`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 645}`; flag: **stated**.
