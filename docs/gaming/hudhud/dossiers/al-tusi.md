## What the records say they did

1. Nasir al-Din Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Tusi died in 1274 CE, known as 'the learned verifier'. — source location `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.268} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 798}`; flag: **stated**.
2. Al-Tusi was born in 1201 in Tus, receiving his education there before moving to Baghdad where he died on June 25, 1274. — source location `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md — Life`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 797} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 802}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Al-Tusi worked for the Isma'ili courts in Persia from the early 1230s until 1256, with a debate over his religious conversion. — source location `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md — Years with the Isma'ili courts`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 802}`; flag: **stated**.
4. For his patron Muhtasham, al-Tusi translated a treatise on child behavior and wrote a book on practical ethics. — source location `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md — Years with the Isma'ili courts`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 798}`; flag: **stated**.
5. Al-Tusi wrote 'Akhlaq-i Nasiri' for his patron in Quhistan, and later wrote another ethical treatise under the Mongols. — source location `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md — Akhlaq-i Nasiri`; citation `{cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.268} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 798} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 799}`; flag: **stated**.
6. After the fall of Alamut, al-Tusi joined Hulagu's court, becoming an astrologer and director of religious endowments, and later founded an observatory in Maragha. — source location `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md — With the Mongols and the Maragha observatory`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 802} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 798}`; flag: **stated**.
7. The Maragha observatory was a significant scientific institution, attracting scholars and students, including Chinese astronomers. — source location `content/en/person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi.md — With the Mongols and the Maragha observatory`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 802} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 798}`; flag: **stated as uncertain**.
