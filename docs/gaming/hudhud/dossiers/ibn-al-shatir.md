## What the records say they did

1. Ala' al-Din Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Ansari, known as Ibn al-Shatir, a timekeeping engineer (muwaqqit) in Damascus. — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 570-571}`; flag: **stated**.
2. Haji Khalifa says of him that he studied the astronomical instruments closely and found that they did not serve every task at every latitude. He judged that the error entered them because of difficulty in setting them or because some parts moved against others and their lines crowded together. He therefore devised an instrument he called *al-Rub' al-Tamm* (The Complete Quadrant), which yields every task in every… — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 570-571}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Ibn Hajar gives a short entry for Ali ibn Ibrahim ibn Muhammad of Damascus, Abu al-Hasan Ibn al-Shatir: born in Rabi' al-Awwal 704, he excelled in astronomy. The entry gives no date of death. — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina`; citation `{cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/8}`; flag: **stated**.
4. David A. King explicitly identifies Ibn al-Shatir as Muslim and describes his head timekeeping position at Damascus's Umayyad Mosque. This supplies individual Muslim identity; the record's Sunni frame is the museum's declared default, not testimony to a particular personal legal school. Earlier fields, prose and attribution remain intact; identity is not inferred from a name or city . — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — Timekeeping and the lunar model: documented extension`; citation `{cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea opening}`; flag: **stated**.
5. Muammer Dizer describes childhood bereavement, learning ivory inlay from a relative, study in Cairo and Alexandria, and return to Damascus after time in Aleppo. This account explains the combination of craft and scholarship, but establishes neither a lunar discovery night nor a particular instrument used to determine a parameter . — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — Timekeeping and the lunar model: documented extension`; citation `{cite:tdv-ibn-shatir-dizer biography}`; flag: **stated**.
6. Dates are not silently reconciled: the earlier text reports Ibn Hajar's Rabi al-Awwal704 AH, Dizer gives Shaban705 AH/March1306, and King circa 1305. Attributed alternatives remain; we neither change the original nor make a new calendar conversion. King places death circa 1375, while Roberts's opening gives 1375/6 within his older chronology . — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — Timekeeping and the lunar model: documented extension`; citation `{cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/8} {cite:tdv-ibn-shatir-dizer biography} {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea heading} {cite:roberts-shatir-1957 p428}`; flag: **stated**.
7. The double-epicycle lunar model is distinct from the complete quadrant: a mathematical construction reducing exaggerated Ptolemaic distance variation while remaining Earth-centred. Roberts studied it in Nihayat al-sul using Marsh139, copied 768 AH/1366. The museum read his article, not the manuscript; copying does not date the invention . — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — Timekeeping and the lunar model: documented extension`; citation `{cite:roberts-shatir-1957 pp428,431–432}`; flag: **stated**.
8. King distinguishes the lost early handbook and observational work with no known copy from the surviving theoretical treatise and new handbook. He also describes a monumental sundial, portable devices and quadrant/astrolabe writings, distinguishing original sundial fragments from a later copy. Every book title is not a new instrument, nor does an instrument's survival make the missing observational text available . — source location `content/en/person/ibn-al-shatir.md — Timekeeping and the lunar model: documented extension`; citation `{cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea planetary-works-and-instruments}`; flag: **stated**.
