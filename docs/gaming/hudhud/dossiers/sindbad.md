## What the records say they did

1. Al-Mas'udi noted in 332 AH that 'Hazar Afsana', meaning 'A Thousand Fables', was translated from Persian and referred to as 'The Thousand and One Nights'. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — In al-Mas'udi`; citation `{cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.550}`; flag: **stated**.
2. The book includes tales of a king, his vizier, and the vizier's daughter and maid, named Shirzad and Dinazad, alongside stories of Farzah and Simas, and Sindbad. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — In al-Mas'udi`; citation `{cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.550}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Ibn al-Nadim reports that Muhammad ibn Abdus al-Jahshiyari, the author of "Kitab al-Wuzara", began to compose a book in which he chose a thousand tales from the tales of the Arabs, the Persians and the Byzantines. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — In the Fihrist`; citation `{cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.423}`; flag: **stated**.
4. Ibn al-Nadim saw parts of al-Jahshiyari's work in Abu al-Tayyib's possession. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — In the Fihrist`; citation `{cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.423}`; flag: **stated**.
5. The Encyclopaedia of Islam describes 'Alf layla wa-layla' as the most famous collection of Arabian fairy-tales, noting that much of its material was borrowed from Persia. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — Encyclopaedia of Islam account: origins and Galland`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 380-386}`; flag: **stated**.
6. Antoine Galland discovered 'The Nights' and translated parts of it, receiving four volumes of the oldest known manuscript from Syria. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — Encyclopaedia of Islam account: origins and Galland`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 380-386}`; flag: **stated**.
7. In 1709, Galland met Hanna of Aleppo, who provided oral tales and transcripts that filled the last four volumes of Galland's translation. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — Encyclopaedia of Islam account: origins and Galland`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 380-386}`; flag: **stated**.
8. Other manuscripts were used for supplements to Galland's translation, including tales by Denis Chavis and William Beloe. — source location `content/en/work/alf-layla-wa-layla.md — Later supplements`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 380-386}`; flag: **stated**.

## Three candidate quest premises

These are proposals for Claude and the founder, not content.

1. Players can sail to the lands of the tales, collecting and translating stories from various cultures, aiming to complete 480 nights of tales as al-Jahshiyari did [3, 4].

2. Children mend the lost transcripts of Hanna's tales, using the Mend system to piece together the stories that filled Galland's last volumes, distinguishing between historical and invented tales [7].

3. Using the Route system, players navigate through the lands of the Nights, discovering new tales and connecting them to the existing stories, distinguishing between Galland's translations and the invented tales [6, 8].
