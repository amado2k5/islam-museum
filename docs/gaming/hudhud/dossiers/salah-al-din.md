## What the records say they did

1. Salah al-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub, known in Europe as Saladin, was sultan of Egypt and Syria and founder of the Ayyubid dynasty. He was born at Tikrit in 1137 or 1138 into a prominent Kurdish family, grew up in Baalbek and Damascus, and died at Damascus on 4 March 1193. — source location `content/en/person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub.md — Summary`; citation `{cite:britannica-saladin}`; flag: **stated**.
2. He began his career on the staff of his uncle Asad al-Din Shirkuh during the campaigns in Egypt. After Shirkuh's death he became, in 1169, commander of the Syrian troops in Egypt and vizier of the Fatimid caliph. In 1171 he ended the Fatimid caliphate and restored the Abbasid khutba. After Nur al-Din Mahmud died in 1174 he moved into Syria, and between 1174 and 1186 worked to unite Syria, northern Mesopotamia,… — source location `content/en/person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub.md — From vizier to sultan`; citation `{cite:britannica-saladin} {cite:britannica-saladin}`; flag: **stated**.
3. He defeated the army of the kingdom of Jerusalem at Hattin and recovered Jerusalem on 2 October 1187, ending nearly nine decades of Frankish rule there. He then faced the Third Crusade at the siege of Acre and the battles of Arsuf and Jaffa. — source location `content/en/person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub.md — Hattin and Jerusalem`; citation `{cite:britannica-saladin} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}`; flag: **stated**.
