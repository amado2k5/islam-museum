## What the records say they did

1. Zakariya ibn Muhammad al-Qazwini, author of "The Wonders of Creation" and "The Monuments of the Lands and the Reports of the Servants", which Haji Khalifa says he composed in 674 AH. — source location `content/en/person/al-qazwini.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 179-180}`; flag: **stated**.
2. His pupil Ibn al-Fuwati relates that he died in Muharram 682 AH. The Gregorian date recorded in the data (about 1283 CE) is derived from the Hijri date. The data describe him as a man of learning and writing in Persia and Central Asia. — source location `content/en/person/al-qazwini.md — His death`; citation `{cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 179-180}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Qazwin is a city in the mountains of al-Daylam. Al-Baladhuri mentions that the Asawira residing opposite al-Daylam embraced Islam when the Muslims came upon them at Qazwin, in the same way as the Asawira of Basra did. — source location `content/en/place/qazwin.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.394}`; flag: **stated**.
4. Yaqut describes Qazwin as a famous city twenty-seven farsakhs from Rayy and twelve from Abhar, in the fourth climate. Citing Ibn al-Faqih, he says it was first built by Shapur Dhu al-Aktaf, that its fortress was called Kashrin in Persian, and that between it and the Daylam lay a mountain where the kings of the earth kept a frontier guard of asawira against the Daylam.
