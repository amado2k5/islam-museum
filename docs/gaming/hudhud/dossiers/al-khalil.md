## What the records say they did

1. Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Khalil ibn Ahmad ibn Amr ibn Tamim al-Farahidi, one of the greatest scholars of Arabic. He is of the Azd of Oman and grew up in Basra. His birth is estimated around 100 AH (718 CE), and he died in 175 AH (791 CE), according to the "Encyclopaedia of Notable Scholars and Men of Letters". — source location `content/en/person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi.md — Overview`; citation `{cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}`; flag: **stated**.
2. It is considered likely that he originated many of the scientific concepts of grammar and linguistic analysis. Al-Mas'udi, citing al-Jahiz's Hashimite Epistle, says that he wrote a book on rhythm and the structure of sounds, and a book on speech. — source location `content/en/person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi.md — His learning and works`; citation `{cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 677-678}`; flag: **stated**.
3. Ibn al-Nadim says that the principles and the questions in the book of Sibawayh are his, that he was the teacher of Sibawayh who took grammar from him, and that he himself took from Isa ibn Umar al-Thaqafi. — source location `content/en/person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi.md — His learning and works`; citation `{cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 110,142-143}`; flag: **stated**.
4. After setting out the disagreement over who first introduced vowel signs and dots, al-Suyuti states that the first to introduce the signs for hamza, shadda, rawm and ishmam was al-Khalil. — source location `content/en/person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi.md — On the signs of the vowel marking`; citation `{cite:itqan-suyuti-calcutta-1852 p. 868}`; flag: **contested**.
5. Basra, in Arabic al-Basra, is a city of lower Mesopotamia on the Shatt al-Arab, about 420 km south-east of Baghdad. The Encyclopaedia of Islam, whose account this page follows, distinguishes two sites: Old Basra, marked today by the village of Zubayr, and New Basra, a later settlement near ancient al-Ubulla from which the modern town grew. The article says the Muslim town can be treated as a new construction, although it probably stood on the site of ancient Teredon and more certainly on that of a Persian settlement called Vahishtabadh Ardashir. The Companion Utba ibn Ghazwan camped in 14/635 on the ruins of an old Persian post that the Arabs called al-Khurayba, "the little ruin". 7. The camp lay about fifteen km from the Shatt al-Arab. The article gives it three purposes: to control the route from the Persian Gulf, Iraq and Persia; to serve as a base for later expeditions east of the Euphrates and Tigris; and to help settle the Bedouin. — source location `content/en/place/basra.md — Foundation as a military camp`; citation `{cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1145}`; flag: **stated**.
8. The first dwellings were simple huts of rushes gathered from the nearby marshes. They were later strengthened with low walls and, after a fire, rebuilt in crude brick. Only under Ziyad ibn Abi Sufyan were baked bricks introduced and the place given the look of a real town, with a new Great Mosque and a residence for the governor.
