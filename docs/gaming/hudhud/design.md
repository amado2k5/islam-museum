# Hudhud: The Atlas of Wonders — game design

_26 November 2026. Phase 0. Written by an AI agent from [the founder's instruction](owner-instruction-2026-11-26.md), which governs wherever this page and it differ. Nothing here has been played by a person other than the author's script. Everything about history stands as "historical review pending"._

## The player's promise (one page)

You are a young Storykeeper of the museum, and your guide is Hudhud, a small orange hoopoe with a fan of a crest and a great deal to say. A storm of loose pages has scattered the Atlas of Stories across ten worlds. In each world you meet people, and a few animals, whose stories the museum keeps: someone who watched light, someone who built a clock, someone who walked across the known world and wrote it down. You fly, you look, you turn and tune and mend things, you ask questions, and a page of the Atlas comes home.

- **You can fly in the first minute.** No manual, no long film.
- **You learn by doing.** A puzzle works the way the idea works. Reading the museum's note afterwards is always optional.
- **Nobody is hurt and nobody is the enemy.** What you face is forgetting: wind, lost ink, ink moths, a muddle to set straight.
- **It is honest about what is real.** The Atlas, the storm, the rooms and every word spoken are invented and say so. The people were real, or are marked as story tradition, and the museum's record is one link away.
- **Three ways to play:** Story (gentle, with hints), Explorer (as meant), Challenge (tighter, never grindy).
- **Free, private, yours.** No account, no adverts, no purchases, no timers that punish you for leaving, nothing that leaves your device.
- **Seven languages**, Arabic and Urdu right to left.

## The frame (fiction, and said to be)

The Atlas of Stories is a great book in the museum's keeping in which every page is a story of how something was found, made, healed, built or told. A storm of loose pages, lost ink and mischievous ink moths has scattered it. Where a page lands, its ink goes pale and the story around it starts to come apart: a clock stops, a map loses its coasts, a poem loses its metre. Hudhud saw where the pages fell. The Storykeeper carries the Atlas's empty cover.

The force behind the storm is **forgetting**. It has no face, no people and no country. Its creatures are ink moths, which are nuisances, not villains: they nibble ink, tangle threads, blow out lamps. They are shooed, outwitted or befriended, never harmed. The larger mystery, which the ten worlds answer piece by piece, is how a story survives at all: somebody writes it down, somebody carries it, somebody translates it, somebody teaches it, somebody checks it. The last world gathers these into one answer, and the post-game asks the player to add a page of their own (kept on the device).

## The loop

| Span | What the player does | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Seconds | run, hop, flap, glide; look; touch; turn, tune, connect, carry | immediate picture and sound; the goal line updates |
| A quest (15 to 25 minutes) | arrive, meet, try, understand, restore the page; optional secret and three goals | a page returns; a museum note opens; three goals (Curiosity, Ingenuity, Kindness) and hidden things are recorded |
| A world (2 to 3 hours) | five quests in any order after the first; a world thread ties them | a wing of the Atlas hub is restored: a shelf, a constellation, a garden bed, a working machine |
| The game | ten worlds from the hub; the mystery of the storm | the hub fills with visitors and working exhibits; the last page is the player's own |
| After | mastery goals, secrets, time trials, daily and weekly challenges made from seeds, New Journey+ | nothing expires; an archive keeps every past challenge |

**The golden scene is this loop at the scale of minutes**: fly (seconds), meet Ibn al-Haytham and the cat, turn mirrors until the beam reaches the page (the quest's heart), find the dark room and a feather (secrets), read the note (after).

## The journey and the door (as built, 27 November 2026)

Asked for by the founder on 27 November 2026: several scenes, each with a different place and a different figure, and when a scene's mission is done a door is highlighted, and reaching the door moves the player to the next scene. What was built keeps the loop above and the Atlas as the hub and the free choice, and adds one more way through the same quests; no quest's puzzle changed.

- **The journey** (`content/journey.ts`) plays the built quests one after another: the flying scene (Ibn al-Haytham's workshop) first, then the twelve quests played on a page in the Atlas's own order, by world and then by quest code (al-Tusi, al-Jazari, al-Khwarizmi, al-Kindi, al-Idrisi, al-Razi, Karima al-Marwaziyya, Ibn Battuta, coffee, the Alhambra, al-Jahiz, Samarkand). A test holds the list to every quest that is built, so a quest added later must be added to it. Above the worlds, the Atlas has a button that begins the journey, continues it ("Continue the journey:" and the next figure as the Atlas heading names it), or begins it again when every scene is done. The quest buttons under the worlds still open any quest on its own ("free play"), and the Atlas's main button is still the workshop's.
- **Where the journey has got to** is kept in the save slot (`journey: { at, round? }`, optional: an old save loads with no journey; a wrong type is read as no journey; two tabs keep the further, a later round being further than the end of an earlier one). Finishing a scene in the journey moves it on at once, so that leaving before the door, or loading the page again, goes on with the next scene and does not ask for the finished one again. Inside a scene, the quest's own place in the middle is kept as before.
- **The door in the flying scene** stands on the floor at the garden's far end (centre x 2342 of the 2400-pixel world, 110 pixels wide, clear of the fountain and the lantern): a tall arched wooden door in a stone frame, drawn in code with no inscription. It is dark until the page is home, then lit: a warm glow behind it, a light line round its leaf and under it, and a gentle pulse unless movement is reduced (shape and light, not colour alone). The page's touch that ends the quest still ends it; since this change no sheet opens over the game: the goals, the museum note, its links and "Historical review pending" sit in a panel beside (under) the stage, Hudhud says "The door is open! Let us go through it.", the goal line says to walk to the door, and the bird stays free. Reaching the door (the same nearness test as touching a thing: within 110 pixels of a point 70 pixels above the floor, measured from 30 pixels above the bird's feet, so from about x 2240 on the floor) goes on: in a journey to the next scene, in free play back to the Atlas.
- **The corridor for a quest played on a page** (`ui/corridor.ts`, `art/corridor.ts`, rules in `sim/door.ts`): once the page reports the quest done, a strip appears above its results (which stay): a plain wall and floor in one colour scheme for its world, taken from the kit's own colours (it shows no real place), Hudhud at the near end and the lit door at the far end. Hudhud walks with the flying scene's own controls and its own fixed-step mover, on one floor with no perches (the jump is ignored); at full speed the walk takes 289 steps of 1/120 s, about 2.4 seconds. On a right-to-left page it is mirrored: the bird starts on the right and the door is on the left, and Left still moves left.
- **A button, "Go through the door"**, beside each door (in the workshop's panel, under the corridor, and in the scene in words) does what reaching the door does, for a player who cannot use the walking controls.
- **Moving on** is one function: it closes the scene that is open (its picture, a page's animation, the corridor's loop) and opens the next, then shows Hudhud's line "On to the next page of the Atlas:" with the figure's name; after the last scene the Atlas comes back with "Every page built so far is home. More pages are on their way." The "flight" between scenes is a change of page and a line from Hudhud, not a played level.

## Rooms: the shape of every scene (27 November 2026)

The founder, 27 November 2026, in chat: "why are you stuck with hudhud leaving from the door then going just into a corrdior he should go to another complete room with other character...etc". He was answered that every scene can become a complete walkable room like Ibn al-Haytham's workshop, with its own figure, and its puzzle played in the room by flying to things and touching them; that a shared room engine would be pulled out of the workshop first; and that one new room would be built to show him, al-Jazari's workshop with the elephant clock, while the other rooms wait for his word on the look (his instruction holds back art in number until he accepts the look in words).

- **The shape of a scene is now a room**: one long walkable place the height of the workshop (720 game pixels, the floor at 624, about 2,400 wide), with its own figure or figures, the player's explorer following Hudhud, the puzzle's things standing in it to be flown to and touched, a list in words under the stage that plays the whole room without the picture, and the same door at the far end, lit when the mission is done.
- **Which scenes have rooms** (27 November 2026, later that day): seven. Ibn al-Haytham's workshop (the light and the mirrors), **al-Jazari's workshop** (the elephant clock), **al-Tusi's observatory** (the two circles), **al-Khwarizmi's room** (the locked box), **al-Kindi's room** (the secret page), **al-Idrisi's hall** (the scattered sheets) and **al-Razi's study** (the loose leaves). The other six built quests are still played on their page, with the corridor to the door as the stand-in until each has its room.
- **No person stands over another where a room begins** (27 November 2026): the player's explorer began at 170 game pixels in every room, where Hudhud's tail (the bird begins at 250) was drawn over it; it now begins at 106, still near enough to the bird that it does not walk at the start, and a unit test (`tests/people.gtest.ts`) checks in every room that the drawn boxes of Hudhud, the explorer, the room's figure and its helper do not overlap at the start, from the painters' own sizes and the scenes' own placements.
- **Al-Jazari's workshop, as built.** A workshop of an engineer at a court: a window of evening light, a shelf of gears, a water basin (decoration only), a long workbench where the spare parts lie, a high shelf with a box that holds the feather (reached only by flying), and on a low platform the frame of five places that holds the clock's parts in the order the motion passes through them (each plinth numbered by dots), with a lever beside it. Al-Jazari stands by the window and the apprentice by the workbench; each is touched to talk. The frame is the game's own and no elephant is drawn; the room's note says so (`jr_note_room`).
- **Playing the clock in the room.** Touch a part (on the clock or on the workbench) to pick it up: it shows in Hudhud's beak and the list says "Carrying: the falcon". Touch a place on the clock to put it there: the part that stood there goes to where the carried one came from (the other place, or the workbench). This is the plainer of the two choices for a child, because nothing is left in the beak that the child did not choose, and it is exactly the clock's own `put` (the page quest's), so every placement is one counted move. Touch the place it came from to put it back (no move); touch another spare part to put the carried one back and take that one. Touch the lever to set the clock going: the run is shown a place at a time (each place lights, the ball appears once the falcon lets it go, the gong sounds at the vessel), or at once when movement is kept still. The moves, the fewest, the three ways of playing, the goals and every sentence of the run are the page quest's.
- **Talk in the room.** Touching al-Jazari asks him about the float and the balls (the clock quest's own lines, said by the museum's record), with one new greeting and one new farewell; touching the apprentice gives the kind or the blunt answer; the feather is in the box on the high shelf (the clock quest's own "A feather, under the spare parts!"). When the clock tells the half hour, Hudhud says the page has come loose, the door lights, and the panel beside the stage shows the four goals, the clock's notes, the room's note and the links; walking to the door moves on as in the workshop.
- **Kept**: the clock's own saved place with what Hudhud carries added; the page quest and the room each take up the other's.
- **Without WebGL** (or with `room=off` in the address, for the checks of the page quest) al-Jazari's scene is played on the clock's page as before, with its corridor.
- **Al-Tusi's observatory, as built** (the third room; the founder said on 27 November 2026 to continue all the remaining work). A hall at night: a tall opening on the stars by the door the bird comes in at, shelves of books (coloured spines, no writing: the record names a large library), and at the far end a long table and a high shelf of plain instruments (rings on small feet, rods, boxes: no named historical instrument). In the middle the **two circles stand as a large upright model on a stand**: the large circle, the small circle inside it and the marked point (a star in a ring, so marked by shape), drawn each frame from the quest's own arithmetic, with the path the point traces **drawn in the air beside it** at the same size. On the stand's base stand **plain levers**, one for each choice the way of playing offers (Story the direction; Explorer the direction and the speed; Challenge the direction, the speed and the size; each lever's knob has its own shape), and a **crank** on its side. Al-Tusi (a cut-out in night colours, not a likeness) stands by the books and the girl who cleans the instruments by the table; each is touched to talk. The room's note says the observatory, the model on its stand, the levers and the crank are invented and are not a picture of the Maragha observatory or its instruments (`tsr_note_room`).
- **Playing the two circles in the room.** Touching a lever moves it to its next setting (after the last the first again) and says the setting in words ("Moved the speed lever: Twice as fast as the large circle."), with the page's own words for each setting; it is exactly one `set` of the quest's rules, the same command as choosing that radio button on the page, and like it clears the path. Touching the crank is the page's "Turn the circles" (`turn`): the turning is shown for as long as on the page (the small circle goes round, the crank turns, the path grows in the air), can be skipped from the list, and is drawn whole at once when movement is kept still (the shared `calm` helper). What it says afterwards is the page's, by the way of playing (Story names the shape and says which way to try, Explorer names the shape, Challenge says only that it is not a line). For a child the rule is: three handles each with a few positions, try one, turn the wheel, look at the drawing in the air. The counts, the goals, the three ways of playing and the shapes are the page quest's to the letter.
- **Talk in the room.** Touching al-Tusi greets the player and offers the page's two questions (why he looked for a model, and the observatory: the museum record's lines, said by the narrator), with one new greeting, one new thank-you and one new farewell; the girl gives the kind or the blunt answer; the feather is among the instruments on the high shelf, reached only by flying (the page's own "A feather, fallen between two instruments!"). When the line is found, Hudhud says the page can rest, the door lights, and the panel beside the stage shows the four goals, the quest's four notes (the one that states the model, `ts_note_2`, shown only now, as on the page), the room's note and the links; walking to the door moves on.
- **Kept**: the two circles' own saved place (the page quest and the room each take up the other's), by the page's own rule of when it is kept.
- **In the journey**, whose order puts al-Tusi second (after the workshop) and al-Jazari third, the workshop's lit door now leads into the observatory and the observatory's into al-Jazari's workshop: the first three scenes are rooms in a row, with no page and no corridor between them.

- **Al-Khwarizmi's room, as built** (the fourth room, 27 November 2026, continuing the founder's word to build every remaining scene as a room). A quiet scholar's room by day: a window with a lattice and a palm far off by the door the bird comes in at, shelves of books and scrolls (coloured spines and plain rolls, no writing), a low writing desk with a plain inkpot and reed pens, and in the middle a **long table on which two large trays stand side by side with an equals sign between them**. The trays hold the problem exactly as the page's picture shows it: a thing is a small closed box, a unit is a coin, and what is taken away is the same shape drawn hollow with a bar through it, in a strip along the bottom of its tray (hollow boxes in one part, hollow coins in the other, so each taken-away group has a place of its own). No numeral is drawn anywhere. On a page read right to left the first side is on the right tray, as on the page's picture. Beside the table stand **two plain brass weights** on a low stand, one marked with a box shape and one with a coin shape (no numeral), a **scoop** lies on the near end of the table, and at the far end the **locked box** stands on a stone plinth by the door. Al-Khwarizmi (a cut-out in day colours, not a likeness) stands by the desk and the child who carries the box by the plinth; each is touched to talk. The room's note says the room, the trays, the weights, the scoop and the box are invented and are not a picture of where he worked (`akr_note_room`); the quest's own note already says nothing read for the game says he used trays or scales.
- **Playing the locked box in the room.** Each move is a thing to fly to and touch, and each touch is exactly one of the page's own commands. A **hollow group** on a tray, touched, is the page's restore for that side and kind (al-jabr): it is there only while something of its kind is taken away on its side, as the page shows a restore button only then. A **weight** touched is the page's balance of its kind (al-muqabala): the one marked with a box balances the things, the one marked with a coin the numbers. The **scoop** is the page's share (the game's own plain step). A weight or the scoop that does not apply is a slip, counted, with the page's own words for the way of playing (Story explains and names the moves that would work now, Explorer says which kind of move does not apply, Challenge says only that nothing changed). After every move the problem is said again in words (the page's own sentence, with its singular, dual and plural forms) in a polite live region and in the list under the stage, which also says what each tray holds. For a child the rule is: touch the hollow ones to put them back on both sides, touch a weight to take the same from both sides, and touch the scoop when only things stand on one side and only coins on the other.
- **Talk in the room.** Touching al-Khwarizmi greets the player and offers the page's two questions (how he wrote problems, and the two operations: the museum record's lines, said by the narrator), with one new greeting, one new thank-you and one new farewell of this room's own; the child gives the kind or the blunt answer; the feather is under the locked box on its plinth, reached only by flying (the page's own "A feather, under the box!"). When one thing equals a number the box opens, the page of the Atlas rises out of it, Hudhud says the page is home, the door lights, and the panel beside the stage shows the four goals, the quest's four notes, the room's note and the three links; walking to the door moves on.

- **Al-Kindi's room, as built** (the fifth room, 27 November 2026, continuing the founder's word to build every remaining scene as a room). A scholar's study by lamplight: a window on a night sky with stars and a thin crescent by the door the bird comes in at, a bookcase (coloured spines, no writing) with a torn page lying on its top, a reading desk with a closed book and a small oil lamp whose light breathes on the wall, two plain **count boards** on stands, and on the wall a **large board that holds the secret page**. Along the board's top runs a strip of the message's marks in order, with a red line under the first two as on the page's picture; under it hangs a plain tile for each mark the message uses (from the commonest, as the page's lists are), and under each tile an empty **place** for the sign given to that mark. On a long shelf below the board stand the **sign tokens**, the quest's own drawn signs (as many as the way of playing has: five, seven or eight), and beside the board a **bell** hangs on a low stand. The count boards are the page's two tables drawn as bars of dots, one dot for each time a sign or a mark occurs, the longest bar the commonest, with a small sun or mark at the head of each to say which table it is: no digit, no word. **No letter of any script and no writing is drawn anywhere in the room**, and the quest's own rule stands (drawn signs and marks, never letters; the opening is the feather, then the sun). On a page read right to left the strip, the tiles and the tokens run from the right, and the two count boards change places. Al-Kindi (a cut-out in lamplit colours, not a likeness) stands by the desk and the young messenger (a boy, the quest's invented person, «حامل الرسالة الصغير» in Arabic, never «الرسول») by the door; each is touched to talk. The room's note says the room, the large board, the marks hung on it and the bell are invented and are not a picture of where al-Kindi worked (`kdr_note_room`).
- **Playing the secret page in the room.** What a bird can do with its beak: carry one sign. **Touch a token** on the shelf to pick its sign up: it shows in Hudhud's beak and the list says "Carrying: the sun"; touch it again to put it back, or another to take that one instead. **Touch a mark's place** on the board to give that mark the sign carried: this is exactly the page's choice of that sign in that mark's list (`assign`), so a sign already given to another mark moves to this one and the other is left empty, as the page frees it, and the words say so ("That sign was taken from: the zigzag."). A sign given to a mark stays on the shelf, dimmed, as the page's lists always offer every sign. **Touch a place with nothing carried** to clear it (the page's "not yet"). **Touch the bell** to read the page (the page's "Read the page"): a reading with a mark still empty is refused and not counted; a wrong one is counted and says, by the way of playing, which marks are wrong (Story), how many are right (Explorer) or only that it does not read (Challenge), in the page's own words. For a child the rule is: fly to a sign, fly to a mark, and when every mark has a sign, ring the bell.
- **Talk in the room.** Touching al-Kindi greets the player and offers the page's two questions (how to begin, and short messages: the museum record's lines, said by the narrator), with one new greeting, one new thank-you and one new farewell of this room's own; the messenger gives the kind or the blunt answer (the page's own lines); the feather is under the torn page on top of the bookcase, reached only by flying (the page's own "A feather, under the page!"). - **Al-Idrisi's hall, as built** (the sixth room, 27 November 2026, continuing the founder's word to build every remaining scene as a room). A bright hall by day: three tall arched windows on a pale sky with the sunlight falling from them across the wall and the floor, a tall shelf of plain rolled sheets and books (coloured spines, no writing) with a stack of blank loose sheets lying on its top, a basket of rolled sheets by the far end, and in the middle a **great table seen at an angle**: its top slopes from a high back edge down to a low front one and is wider at the front, and on it the map's sheets lie in their rows (two rows of three in Story, three of four in Explorer, three of five in Challenge), the rows further back drawn a little smaller. Each sheet is **the page quest's own drawing** (`drawSheet` in `art/sheets.ts`, shared and not copied): parchment, the lines of its edges made from the edge codes (a coast, a river, a road), and its number as dots in a corner; it is an imagined coast, never al-Idrisi's map or any real place. Along the front of each row runs a **low rail**, a perch the bird stands on to reach that row; no sheet can be touched from the floor. By the table a **plain lamp** stands on a low stand. **No globe, no disc, no inscription and no writing** is drawn anywhere in the hall. On a page read right to left the first column lies on the right and every sheet is its own mirror image, as on the page's picture, so that the lines still meet across every seam. Al-Idrisi (a cut-out in sea-green and white, not a likeness) stands before the second window and the young helper (the quest's invented person, in a headscarf, holding a loose sheet) by the door; each is touched to talk. The hall's note says the hall, the great table, the sheets on it and the lamp are invented for play, and that the drawing on the sheets is an imagined coast, not al-Idrisi's map (`isr_note_room`).
- **Playing the scattered sheets in the hall.** What a bird can do: lift one sheet. **Touch a sheet** to lift it: it rises with a shadow under it and a gold frame round it, its button in the list is pressed, and the list says "Lifted: sheet 3". **Touch another sheet** to exchange the two: this is exactly the page's second press (`exchange`), one move, and neither stays lifted. **Touch the lifted sheet again** to put it down, which is not a move (the page's "Let go"). In Story and Explorer the seams that do not match are marked on the table with the page's broken red line and said in the page's words (Story names them, Explorer counts them); the exchange that makes every seam match finishes the map at once, as on the page. ## Goals, replay and what is deliberately absent

- **Three goals a quest**: Curiosity (asked for more, or found the hidden thing), Ingenuity (solved it within a fair count), Kindness (did the small good thing: greeted the cat, returned what was dropped, waited for someone). They are about what the player did. They never grade a person of history.
- **Hidden things**: feathers, map fragments, seeds, gears, star marks. They are things that belong in an atlas, never objects taken from a place.
- **Made from seeds, kept for ever**: daily and weekly challenge rooms come from the shared seeded generator (the Beam generator exists and is tested over 3,000 seeds), every past one stays in an archive, and a challenge can be shared as a short text code. No online scores.
- **Absent on purpose**: accounts, adverts, purchases, loot boxes, energy timers, streak penalties, notifications, public chat, anything made by a model while the game runs, any score sent anywhere.

Mechanics studied as benchmarks only, with nothing copied: low-pressure exploration and collecting (*A Short Hike*), warm character talk (*Spiritfarer*), flow and secrets (*Rayman Legends*), legible visual puzzles (*Monument Valley*), assists and precise movement (*Celeste*), a place that restores (*Stardew Valley*), talk that reacts to what you did (*Hades*), variety (*Dave the Diver*).

## How big this is, said plainly

The instruction asks for 20 to 30 hours on the main path (fifty quests of 15 to 25 minutes, a prologue, a hub and ten world threads) and 40 to 60 for mastery. What exists is one scene of a few minutes (the script that plays it takes about one minute; no person has been timed) and twelve short trial quests played on a page (the elephant clock, the secret page, the locked box, the scattered sheets, the animal cards, the paper of Samarkand, Ibn Battuta's thread, the two circles, the journey of coffee and, added 27 November 2026, the Alhambra and the dry fountain, al-Razi's loose leaves and Karima's copies; no person has timed them either).

What one agent session produced, on 26 November 2026, in about an hour and a quarter of working time: three of the twelve systems (Flight, Beam, Talk; the rules of a fourth, Mechanism, followed the same day), saving, one room with a garden, four figures, 163 strings in seven languages, 85 tests of its own and a tool that plays the scene in Chrome. That rate does not scale linearly, for three reasons that are larger than code:

1. **Art.** The pictures in the scene are a code-drawn trial. If the founder judges them not good enough, as he judged Granada's, the art for fifty subjects is the work of an illustrator and an animator, not of this agent ([art bible](art-bible.md)). That decision sets the calendar more than anything else here.
2. **Review.** Fifty subjects need a named reviewer in history of science, medicine, architecture, geography and literature, and a native reader for six languages. None is named.
3. **People playing it.** The targets for age bands, playtime and "children begin without explanation" can only be met by watching children play. No playtest has been run.

An estimate, with its assumptions on the page:

| Piece | Agent work (sessions like this one) | Waits on |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1, this scene, to acceptance | done as a trial; 1 to 2 more to act on the founder's judgment | the founder's word on the look |
| Phase 2, the 60 to 90 minute slice (prologue, hub, four quests) | 12 to 20: four more systems, a hub, four rooms, remapping, a real phone layout, postcards | the art decision; one reviewer; first playtests |
| Phase 3, ten worlds | 8 to 12 a world once the systems exist, so 80 to 120 | art for each world; review of each world; playtests after each |
| Phase 4, the candidate | 15 to 25: soak, the two reference machines, all gates | both physical machines; beta players; the founder |

That is on the order of **110 to 170 agent sessions**, and it is the smaller part. With an illustrator and an animator at a pace of one world a month, review keeping up, and playtests between waves, the whole game is **about a year** from an accepted look. Without an art route the founder accepts, there is no date. These are an agent's estimates from one scene; they are not commitments and have no playtest behind them.
