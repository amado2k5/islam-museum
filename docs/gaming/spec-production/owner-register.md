## What still needs the founder's own approval

_Each is asked for by itself, in words, when its time comes. None follows from a merged pull request, a release to UAT, or a finished milestone._

1. Deploying any game to production, and carrying its music or models to production.
2. Pointing production traffic at the games, including a Play entry or page on the museum's site.
3. Removing a prototype or not-finished label from a game. This is a separate approval from deploying.
4. Announcing that a game is ready for production, or marking a game final.
5. Waiving a gate that failed, or counting a gate as passed on evidence other than the kind it asks for.
6. Each release to UAT. It is asked for each time, as until now, unless he says candidates built from main are approved as a class.
7. Any further rollback on UAT: one drill was approved and has been used. Any rollback in production.
8. Accepting a proof frame, and making assets in number after it.
9. Bringing any globe data set into the project, after seeing its line in the table.
10. Using the eight V&A pictures; running a picture round with fewer than 52 pictures; relaxing any of the ten requirements for a picture.
11. Tactical combat units, battlefield control or graphic violence in Granada; any military design in The Golden Age.
12. Accounts, sign-in, leaderboards or any comparison of scores between people.
13. Anything paid: an artist, licensed models, a device-testing service, a form service, paying reviewers or testers, any new infrastructure.
14. Calling The Golden Age, or anything in any game, a reconstruction; publishing any historical claim as verified.
15. Support for phones and tablets.
16. Producing The Golden Age or Atlas in full beside Granada.
17. Sending a game anywhere outside the museum's own hosts: a store, a portal, a competition, the press; any exclusivity or money.
18. Putting a reviewer's, operator's or tester's name on a public page.
