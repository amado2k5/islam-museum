## Cross-level comparison (item 5)

Compared in English, Arabic and Urdu. Identical across levels: Khwarizmi (b-1: only the closing question differs, intentionally), Baghdad (a-1 vs b-1 differ by design), calligraphy (closing question only), Arabiyya and script-origin (level2 and level3 c-1).

Differ in fact:

1. `practice/greeting-salam` (b-2). Level1 and level2 English say the greeting "is given on meeting and on parting, and that the first is no more deserving than the last"; level3 English says it "belongs to arriving and to leaving alike, and that the first is no more due than the last". The record has the level3 wording. Arabic and Urdu are identical in all three levels, so the English of level1 and level2 differs from the Arabic and Urdu they sit beside. Fix level1 and level2 English to the record's wording.
2. `practice/eid-al-fitr` (level1 c-1 vs level3 c-1). Level1, all three languages: unattributed ("The record on Eid al-Fitr says"), says the Muslims have two Eids "and no other", and has the takbir "on the two Eids". Level3, all three languages: attributed to "the Kuwaiti jurists' encyclopaedia", leaves out "no other". The level1 version states an exclusivity and a ruling in the museum's voice. Use the level3 text at level1.
3. `concept/gratitude-shukr` (level1 a-2 vs d-2). Two different cards from one record, a-2 on the definition, d-2 on the good word; neither attributes. Not a conflict, but they should name the same source.

## Patterns

1. **"The record says" and unattributed rulings.** Albisa, Eid, shukr, kunya, tasmiya, agriculture-in-islam, the two greeting cards. The rule names the person or body; here the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia of jurisprudence is behind most of them, and the card says "the record".
2. **Records that are lectures or television programmes.** hsc4m-d-1 (a preacher's talk, REMOVE), the greeting cards (Mansour's Ahalina, Abdelkafy), the Ramadan reasons (a lesson and a programme). Where the card names the speaker and the expectation is about what people say or do, KEEP-FIX; where the expectation asks for the subject, REMOVE.
3. **Adjacent: the record has the topic but not the noun.** Kiswa (garment), silk-rulings (process), census-837 (technological development), the two Cairo/Baghdad city cards (the language), loanwords (conventions), Yeomans's preface (Said's ideas), measures (a skill). The Briefs of these lessons already admit it ("your class brings ...").
4. **The "Judgment call" on the cities.** Baghdad and Cairo are offered as "communities where Arabic is spoken". If the coordinator accepts "a city of the Arabic-speaking world" without the record saying so, KEEP; I read the rule strictly. level1 a-1 stands on `language/arabic`.
5. **Unsuitable material behind a mild card.** Ghulamiyyat (slave girls, a caliph's passion), walid-bimaristan (lepers confined), the two famine records (cannibalism, children). The card sanitises; the Try-this sends students into the full record.
6. **Health and food.** Halal-haram, Ramadan and hoarding cards are attributed and none gives advice, but the Ramadan record holds a diabetic-fasting ruling and the Briefs do not say "not dietary or health guidance".
7. **Superlatives.** "Greatest phenomenon" (al-Biruni), "the first" (Nizamiyya), "the first thing of its kind" (green turban; attributed to al-Suyuti, acceptable), "most honoured of the Islamic arts" (calligraphy record; correctly left out of the cards).
8. **Brief and connections out of step.** hsp3u-d-2 promises a climate record that is not a connection.
9. **Arabic, Urdu and English numbers** agree in every card except hfa4u-c-1 (flagged lesson, skipped) and hnb4m weaver cards, where Arabic uses words (3,000, 1830s) instead of digits; no disagreement of fact.
