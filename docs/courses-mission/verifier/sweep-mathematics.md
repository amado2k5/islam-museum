## Patterns

1. concept/islamic-geometric-patterns is one sentence and was linked in eight lessons (Grades 1 to 4 and 8). It says a pattern exists and nothing else. REMOVE everywhere; concept/girih (tiles, stars, polygons) carries the weight in Grades 2 to 4 and 8. The same goes for symmetry and transformations: no record in the museum, as read here, supplies them, so G8 E1.1 is half a gap.
2. invention/algorism was offered at every grade from 1 to 8. It is a history of the Indian numerals, with place value to thousands. It suits place value in Grades 3 to 6; it does not suit counting to 50 (G1), coding (G1, G5), numbers to a billion (G7) or powers of ten and percents (G8). One record, five different fits.
3. Fiqh rulings stand in for the thing asked: kasb (G4 earning), riba (G8 interest), zakat as a "tax" (G5). The financial-literacy expectations ask for factors in a choice, interest calculation, levels of government; a ruling answers none of them.
4. The grade ladder is ignored in algebra: quadratics (al-durub-al-sitta, G6 and G7), binomial expansion (karaji, G6, G7), a negative "debt" (G5), continued fractions (G8). Linear one-step equations are what the grades teach, and the records hold quadratics. Cut the procedure; keep the check.
5. Unattributed claims: Cairo editors (concept/algebra, algorism "after" cards), Hasan Ibrahim Hasan (sakk, G6), the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia (sadaqa). The Arabic differs from the English in G2 f-1 ("silver" only in English) and G5 b-1 (a repeated numeral); card lengths differ by up to 50 words between languages in G3 d-1, G6 f-1 and G7 b-1, c-1, so the three versions do not say the same thing.
6. Inheritance (G2 b-2): the museum's mathematical use of shares is natural for fractions, but the context (a parent's or spouse's death, unequal shares for male and female) makes it unfit for Grade 2. A fractions lesson in Grade 6 to 8 could use the same passages with the expectation reaching the ruling; this sweep does not recommend it.
7. Honest cards exist: the sa' (G3), the elephant clock (G2), al-Maraghi's rates in zakat (G7), the circle rules in ilm-al-misaha (G7). They name a person, say what the record says and ask the class one question tied to the expectation.
