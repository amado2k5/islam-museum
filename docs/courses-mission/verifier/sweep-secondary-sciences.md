## Records that must not be linked

None found. Every linked record was scanned (English and Arabic) for hostile or hateful content about a group; the only matches were reported academic remarks on al-Razi's contested religious views ("hostile accounts", in `invention/al-razi-smallpox-measles` and `invention/razi-substance-classification`), which concern one man and are not hateful. Two records need cleaning but are not hateful:

- `person/ibn-al-nafis` carries, on the page a student opens from four lessons, television-programme claims: "the first to prescribe salt moderation showing its effect on blood pressure", the lesser circulation "wrongly attributed" to Harvey and "Western scientists" (Zaghloul El-Naggar), and a deathbed anecdote about wine. - Cards were mostly careful: hedges, "not an invention of X" disclaimers and attributions by name are present in nearly every inventions card. The failures are of a few kinds below.
- **Adjacent by shared word.** "Refraction" listed among three conditions (optics concept); "catapult" and "shot" for projectile motion; "sound" for a whistle and a string; "pollution" in one sentence for public health; "water" for a pump offered as renewable energy; "fertilizer" as a purity ruling. In each the record lacks the very noun of the expectation.
- **Rulings and legal summaries beside science expectations** (simad, agriculture-in-islam) are unattributed and answer no part of the expectation; none of the four expectations asks for a legal or ethical view.
- **Modern living persons sourced only to Wikipedia/Wikidata** (Mubarak Ahmad Khan, Rizwana Hasan) carry a worldwide-superlative, a product-performance claim and a court outcome in the museum's voice.
- **Priority and superlative wording** was mostly attributed or denied by the records ("no priority claim"). Exceptions: "first known account of all the blood" (pulmonary circulation, two cards, should be West's), "principal optical work of the Islamic world" (data summary only), the unnamed editor's "most valuable thing Arab scholars produced in physics" (four cards).
- **A television programme as the biography of a physician** (Suwaidan on Ibn al-Nafis): the museum's records disagree with it on birthplace, age at moving to Cairo and the teacher's date; three lessons give three different stories.
- **Card text drawn from a data summary when the page has no English or Urdu prose** (`concept/al-abad-al-falakiyya`); a reader cannot check the card against any page.
- **Reuse across courses**, judged use by use: the double-piston pump is honest in four lessons (motion conversion, simple machines, energy, hydraulics) and adjacent in the fifth (renewable energy); pulmonary circulation is honest in six uses (one lesson links it twice); Ibn al-Haytham's circular-reflection record is honest in Grade 10 academic but too abstract for applied; the Bira siege fits neither projectile motion (Grade 11) nor, without trimming, impact (Grade 12); the physician and Ibn al-Nafis cards are reused for different expectations (training, contribution) that they do not equally serve.
- Medical cards: no card reads as advice. al-Razi, Ibn Sina and al-Zahrawi cards all carry a "not a guide today" disclaimer. The only advice-flavoured claims (salt moderation; wine) are on the Ibn al-Nafis person page, not in cards.
- Rulings: besides simad and agriculture-in-islam, no fatwa is linked. The sabil card is a charitable endowment, not a ruling.
- Fit the grade: no card is unsuitable for its age except that the Bira siege card (killing, plunder) is trimmed rather than removed in Grade 12 because the expectation reaches the subject. The smallpox epidemic mortality figures suit Grade 12.

## The five worst things

1. Six lessons have no honest connection and go: pollution as a health threat (snc4m-e-3), sound in design (sph3u-e-1), an environmental challenge (svn3m-b-1), soil fertility (svn3m-d-2), water management (svn3m-d-3), renewable energy (svn3m-f-1). Four of them rest on single-author opinion, a jurists' disagreement or a pump that has nothing to do with the expectation.
2. Unattributed rulings sit in two Grade 11 environmental lessons (jurists' differences on manure, an idle-land rule) beside expectations that do not ask for a legal view.
3. The Ibn al-Nafis career card is built on a television programme that contradicts the museum's own records on teacher, age and birthplace, and the person page also holds the television priority claims (salt moderation, Harvey).
4. Wikipedia-only records of two living people carry a worldwide-leader superlative, a biodegradation claim ("three to four months") and a court outcome as plain fact.
5. Priority and data-summary-only claims leak into cards: "first known account of all the blood", "principal optical work of the Islamic world", and `al-abad-al-falakiyya` has no English or Urdu page at all; the Bira siege summary describes a human arm being thrown.
