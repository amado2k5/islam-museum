## Specific checks asked for

1. Sama' and Mevlevi whirling offered as "dance" (g1-arts-a-1): confirmed REMOVE. The record is one sentence calling it a "session of listening to remembrance with the whirling of the Mevlevi dervishes"; it never says dance. The lesson goes, because it is the only connection.
2. Music cards about a singer, a song, a festival: Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (g2, g5), Abida Parveen (g8), "Kun Faya Kun" (g5) and the Fez festival (g3) are about the music the expectation asks for and are suitable; KEEP. The Nusrat and Abida records are Wikipedia/Wikidata-sourced (names, dates, careers), and the cards restate only those. The al-Farabi cards (g6, g8) are not about music: REMOVE. The oud-origin card (g5) hides a graphic death: REMOVE.
3. Figurative painting for young children: Mughal painting (g2) is figurative but shows only an elephant and the card is fine after cutting jargon; the bowl with a lute player (g1) is fine. No Arts card shows or describes a depiction of a prophet or Companion. The waqwaq "heads on the vines" cards (g3, g4) are the only image-based cards I would remove for the grade.

## Patterns

1. Storytelling is offered as "drama and theatre" (g2, g4, g7 B3): minstrel and epic records for expectations that ask for theatre. Three of four such lessons go; g6 and g8 survive only through the one real dramatist.
2. Music expectations are met with a record about an instrument string or a scholar's book list, not about music. Ziryab appears in six lessons; it is right for "instruments and innovation" (g5, g7) and wrong for "forms and pieces" or "listening" (g3, g4).
3. Design records are mostly one-sentence Wikidata records (geometric patterns, carpets, oud). They can serve "notice shapes and repetition" but not "analyse" or "evaluate".
4. Cards for ages 6 to 9 reuse the upper-grade card with a hard clause left in: Yeomans/Owen Jones/"Graeco-Roman" in Grades 1 to 4, "Samarra style B/polylobes" in Grades 5 and 6, Deccani/Rajput in Grade 2, Rezavi in Grade 1.
5. Questions that ask pupils to "choose" a material (g6, g8 pigments) with no word on safety.
6. Outside my scope but seen: the skipped Grade 3 and Grade 4 "Writing as art" lessons carry `work/monumental-calligraphy-in-fatimid-egypt` (politics of a caliph and his vizier, "a nod to the real holder of power") and `work/islamic-calligraphy-blair` (a book offered to 8 and 9-year-olds, with no sign, symbol or style in it, only that a book names styles). I would apply the same (c) and (b) tests to them.

## The five worst things found

1. g5-arts-c-2, al-ud: the oud-origin record is built on a father hanging his dead son's body from a tree until the limbs came apart; a 10-year-old is sent to it.
2. g1-arts-a-1: Sama' and Mevlevi whirling offered as "dance" from a one-sentence Wikidata record with an unattributed "best-known Sufi ritual".
3. g1-arts-b-1: Kalila wa-Dimna offered as a tale for dramatic play; the record contains no tale, only chapter counts, translators and a dispute about authorship.
4. g3-arts-d-1 and g4-arts-d-1: the "heads on the vines" waqwaq card, a mythical tree hung with heads, offered to 8 and 9-year-olds with nothing about design principles.
5. g6/g8 pigments: a card inviting children to choose among historical pigments, including verdigris and lead white, with no safety word. (Close sixth: the Grade 4 ziryab card, a historian's critique of a medieval biography, offered for "listening to music".)
