## Records that must not be linked

1. **event/change-of-qibla** (linked from `ca-on-g11-hrt3m-e-1` and `ca-on-g11-hrf3o-c-2`). Its section "In The Sealed Nectar" says the change "exposed the weak Muslims and the Jewish hypocrites who had entered their ranks to stir up confusion", and that the ranks were "cleansed of people of treachery and betrayal". That is hostile content about a religious group, stated in the record's own voice. The card is clean, but the rule is that such a record is never linked. Both connections go. (The page also says the change "exposed the hypocrites among those who doubted", which is a second, separate use of the word.)

2. **Borderline, for the coordinator to decide; I have not marked these REMOVE.**
   - `passage/quran-3-97` (linked from `hrt3m-c-1` and `hrf3o-e-2`): its commentary section reports a weak-chained hadith that a person who can make the pilgrimage and does not "makes no difference to him whether he dies a Jew or a Christian". It is a reported opinion that uses Jews and Christians as the standard of failure; the editor calls the chain very weak. The verse itself and both cards are fine. If the coordinator applies the rule strictly, the Qur'an card has to go and `hrt3m-c-1` still stands on hajj and tawaf.
   - `concept/malaika` (linked from `hrt3m-d-1`): a preacher's lecture summary says the Companions responded "with hearing and obedience, unlike the Children of Israel". Mild, and a contrast drawn from the Qur'an's own account, but it is a comparison against a people. The card uses none of it.
   - `practice/ashura` (`hrf3o-c-2`): gives "differing from the Jews" as one scholarly reason for fasting the ninth. A neutral distinction, not hostile; the card does not use it. Recorded only so the reading is on file.

3. Scanned all 76 linked records for hateful or conspiratorial material about any religious or ethnic group (Jews, Christians, Shia, Magians, "pagan", "infidel", racial language, conspiracy). Nothing else found. No blocked organisation named in any linked record.

## Patterns

1. **Television and preacher records treated as the subject.** al-Farabi and Ibn Sina (Suwaidan's "Al-Mubdi'un"), and the soul record (Sha'rawi, Abdelkafy), supply six cards across four lessons, for expectations that ask for a philosopher's position or a philosophical question. The cards are attributed, but the rule is that such records are not offered beside an expectation that asks for the subject itself.
2. **Biography or list of titles offered for "positions" and "theories".** Miskawayh, Akhlaq-i Nasiri, Ibn Sina, Ash'ari, Tahafut al-Tahafut. Where the Brief has to say "the museum does not set out the positions", the link goes.
3. **Legal rulings for expectations about meaning or practice.** Burial law for afterlife ideas; nikah definitions for rites of passage; Eid prayer rulings for a festival's significance; tawaf kinds for a quest for meaning. In the Kuwaiti-encyclopaedia records the ruling is usually the record's main content and the lesson's expectation is not.
4. **"The record says" for rulings and views.** About 75 of the 107 cards open or carry "the record says/calls/adds". For rulings (dafn, nikah, hajj, adhan, Eid) and for attributed claims (Kaaba, Medina document) the named author or body is missing. Many cards need the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia, al-Mubarakpuri, al-Zirikli, Wikipedia or Wikidata named.
5. **Wikidata-only records (imam, muezzin, calligraphy) and Wikipedia-only (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan) carry facts the cards restate as findings**, including a "first" and a death toll.
6. **Superlatives and near-synonyms.** "Greatest" versus "largest" (Bayt al-Hikma, Arabic and English differ); "first historian" from an architecture guide; "the strongest causes" is Ibn al-Nadim's and attributed. A noun difference: "fifty books on him" (falsafa) versus fifty works in all fields (Ibn Rushd).
7. **Briefs that do their job on comparative religion.** Every Brief in hrt3m and hrf3o says the class brings the other traditions. The Briefs of the lessons that lose connections (hzb3m b-1, b-2, b-3, b-4, c-1; hrt3m e-1, e-2) must be rewritten to match what is left.
8. **Hajj and the Qur'an passage pages carry long commentary sections** that were not meant for a class. Worth a decision on whether the Qur'an connection should link the passage page at all.

## The five worst things

1. A record with hostile content about Jews ("Jewish hypocrites ... people of treachery and betrayal", event/change-of-qibla) is linked in two lessons (hrt3m-e-1, hrf3o-c-2); two further records carry milder reported comparisons that need a decision (quran-3-97, malaika).
2. Television and preacher records are offered as philosophy: al-Farabi in four cards, Ibn Sina, and the Sha'rawi soul record in two. Two whole lessons (hzt4u c-2 and d-1) have nothing left once the unfit links are removed.
3. Links whose record lacks the thing the expectation asks for: Athens for archaeological investigation, Miskawayh and Akhlaq-i Nasiri for ethical positions, Tahafut and the Ash'ariyya for arguments, burial and funeral law for afterlife ideas, nikah for a rite of passage, calligraphy for a sacred writing.
4. Nikah cards put "ownership of licit enjoyment of a woman" in the museum's voice with no named jurist or source, in two lessons for 16-year-olds.
5. Superlatives and numbers that the cited source cannot carry or that differ across the museum's own records: "first muezzin" (Wikidata), "first historian" (an architecture guide), "greatest" versus "largest" (House of Wisdom), "fifty books on him" versus fifty works in all fields, and "the Romans" for the Byzantine empire.
