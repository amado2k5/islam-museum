Courses: ca-on-g9-avi1o, ca-on-g10-avi2o, ca-on-g11-avi3m, ca-on-g11-avi3o, ca-on-g12-avi4m, ca-on-g12-avi4e, ca-on-g11-amu3m, ca-on-g12-amu4m, ca-on-g9-mth1w. Students 14 to 18. 51 lessons, 128 connections, all read against the linked record read with `--full`. None of these slugs was in fleet-flagged.md. Nothing was edited except this file.

## Records that must not be linked

No linked record carries hostile or hateful content about a group. I searched every `--full` brief for such vocabulary and read the full prose of the records the cards depend on. Three records are sensitive but not hateful, and need care:

- `concept/al-ud` (linked in AMU4M-C-1): the origin story hangs a dead son's body from a tree "until its limbs came apart" (English and Arabic prose both say it) and makes the instrument from it. It is a legend, and the record itself says it "needs a further source". The card leaves out the hanging, but the lesson sends the student to the page (see AMU4M-C-1 below). Verdict: REMOVE.
- `concept/zakat` (linked in MTH1W-B-3): the card uses al-Maraghi's book, which is sound. The same page carries preacher lectures with unsourced arithmetic ("the arithmetic example in the recording is confused"), a declared "rant" perspective, and statements that wealthy Muslims are "sinful" for camp dwellers. The lesson's Try-this sends students to the whole page. Keep the link only if the Try-this names the section.
- `concept/al-qiyan` (AMU3M-B-1, AMU4M-B-1): singing slave women, their sale, gifting and prices. See below.

## Answers to the seven special checks

1. **Sibling visual arts cards.** Every shared card is byte-identical in English, Arabic and Urdu between AVI3M and AVI3O, and between AVI2O and AVI4E (pigments, calligrapher x3, inlay, Damascus Room, Doha, kufic, coins, Fatimid inscriptions, glass, Mughal, architect, potter x3, girih, arabesque, zellij, pouncing, Dome of the Rock, tesserae, tiraz, illumination, calligraphy, Nishapur). No card differs in fact between siblings. AVI1O's cards for the same records differ from the later courses only in which sentences of the record they choose (for example the coin card names the Ghurid victory only in AVI3), never in a noun or number. The real sibling problem is the reverse: one identical card is judged under different expectations and does not serve all of them (AVI4E-B-1 asks for meaning and values, and gets a description of doors and gilding; AVI3M-A-2 asks about variation of display and gets a one-sentence record). Those are listed below.
2. **`concept/islamic-calligraphy`.** Two cards link it (AVI2O-C-2, AVI4E-C-2). Both state only the definition and the four script names, cite only `source/wikidata`, and contain no judgement of worth ("noblest" and "most honoured" in the record are not copied). They obey the Wikidata limit. They fail a different test: the record names four scripts and no convention, element or principle of design, and the card tells the student to learn the names "as you would names of lines or colours", which treats script names as elements of design. REMOVE (b).
3. **`concept/al-ud`.** One card links it, in AMU4M-C-1. The card says "a son he loved died" and the body-like-a-thigh image, and omits the hanging. The lesson's second section, "See how the instrument is likened to a body in both accounts", and its Try-this "Read the records [[concept/al-ud|The oud]]" send the student to the full story. The card does not stay clear of it; the lesson does not either. REMOVE.
4. **`concept/al-qiyan`.** Linked in AMU3M-B-1 and AMU4M-B-1. Attributed: yes ("Ahmad Amin describes"). Kept to what the expectation asks: no. The record has one sentence on the music (trained "in melodies and voices"); the card spends its words on who owned them, a broker displaying them "so that young men might listen", and the Arabic adds "and spending on them". Leading question: yes. "What does such an institution show about the society that kept it?" asks the student to pass judgement on a society. The lessons make it worse: AMU3M-B-1 and AMU4M-B-1 both ask "who paid for it or sponsored it", which leads straight to the prices in the record. Verdict FIX in both (secondary, and the expectation does reach music in a slave-owning court), with a strong case for REMOVE in AMU3M-B-1, where Kun Faya Kun alone would carry the lesson. Exact change in the table.
5. **MTH1W rulings and histories.** Zakat is a religious rate offered as the context for percentages: acceptable as a context only if framed as the jurists' rule and converted into percentages (FIX). Sakk is a description of an instrument, not a ruling: KEEP. No riba record is linked. A history of a method offered for a skill: `invention/karaji-polynomial-arithmetic` in B2.2 (exponent patterning) is a history plus one modern illustration the record itself says is "not a line from the book": REMOVE. The same record in C1.4 is acceptable because it names the operation the expectation asks (collecting like terms) and gives a checkable example. `concept/dhira-fiqh` in E1.3 is a fiqh page on the arm (ablution, retaliation for cutting the arm, four camels for a broken bone) with one sentence on the cubit and no value for it: REMOVE.
6. **Urdu of AVI3M/AVI3O against the Urdu prose of the record.** Seven compared. Differences found: (a) pigments card writes "گوند یا گوندِ عربی" (gum or gum arabic) where the Urdu record says "سریش یا گوند عربی" (glue or gum arabic): the glue is lost, a near-synonym swap; (b) inlay card adds "پیتل اور کانسی کے برتنوں پر" (on brass and bronze vessels), the Urdu record's sentence says only "پیتل اور کانسی میں"; (c) potter card calls the maker "چینی ساز" where the Urdu record says "کمہار" for the same word; (d) girih card asks about "تکرار" (repeats): the word is only in the uncited "For Explorers" paragraph, not in the cited summary; the English card has the same "repeats". The coin, Mughal and glass cards match the Urdu prose in every noun, name and number. No difference in fact that changes meaning. Separately, three plain parentheses around Arabic-script text in Urdu (AVI1O-C-2 calligrapher "(سانچا)", AVI4M-B-1 Dome of the Rock "(موزیک)", and AMU3M-C-1 expectation C2.2 "(ابوریجنل)") break the U+2066 rule.
7. **Hostile content.** None (see top).

## Patterns

- **The card admits the gap.** Eight cards say in their own words what the record lacks ("says nothing more about how the room is displayed", "not the music it treats", "no architectural description", "describes neither kilns nor guilds"). A card that confesses the missing noun is a REMOVE under the runbook; the Brief then repeats the confession.
- **Description offered for meaning.** Dome of the Rock, Cordoba and the tesserae cards give materials and counts under expectations that ask for purpose, meaning, values or society. Only the Qabbani sentence serves those.
- **A dig, a museum, a room.** The "where art is shown" lessons are served by Doha only; the two other records are about excavation and a moved room.
- **Career lessons need a pathway.** The potter record has none and says so; the architect and calligrapher do.
- **Cards that drop the unsettling part but keep the link.** al-Ud (hanging the body), Ishaq (the devil), qiyan (sale). The page the student opens still has it.
- **Three languages, one omission.** The tasweer card drops the third view in English, Arabic and Urdu together.
- **Superlatives and unnamed authors.** "The author", "she notes" (tesserae); an attributed "unequalled" (inlay, acceptable because named).
- **Sources outside the prose.** "Three shells" (summary field only); "repeats" (uncited audience paragraph).
- **History for skill (MTH1W).** B2.2 and (partly) C1.4, E1.4 use histories or modern illustrations in place of the mathematics; the research-a-story expectations (B1.1, C1.1, E1.1) are served well.
- **Grade fit in mathematics.** Completing the square and radicals offered to Grade 9.
- **Urdu.** Near-synonym swaps (glue becomes gum; potter becomes "chini saz"), an added noun (vessels), three unwrapped parentheses.
- **Expectation codes to the gap register if the REMOVE and FIX verdicts are taken:** MTH1W B2.2; AMU3M C2.1; AVI2O A3.2 (unless the signing card is kept).
