# Why islam.museum needs funding, and how it would be used

islam.museum is free, open, ad-free and has no tracking. It is built entry by entry, and every fact is cited to its source. Its work is paid for by its founder today, and that cannot last as the museum grows.

## What the work costs

Free for everyone — not free for the founder. islam.museum charges its visitors nothing: no accounts, no ads, no tracking, no paywall — and its costs are paid, month by month, by its founder. In a recent month running costs came to roughly CA$1,900. The largest share, roughly CA$900, was AI assistants — Claude, ChatGPT, Devin, Grok, Google AI and Muse, the personal agent that orchestrates the fleet's work — used to research, draft, translate and check entries against their cited sources. Video generation (Runway, Artlist and Veo) makes the museum's films; ElevenLabs narrates them in Arabic, English and Urdu; GitHub hosts the code. Cloudflare (about CA$90 a month) keeps 99,000 pages fast and safe, alongside hosting and storage, and domain registrations keep the museum's names its own. Even the top tiers — Claude Ultra, Devin Max — run out before the month does: in October and early November, overages and top-ups alone added roughly CA$730 on top of the subscriptions. The usage is metered, not guessed: Copilot Max used 18,525 of its 20,000 monthly AI credits in five days, Devin's weekly quota ran at 100% two weeks running, and Claude Ultra stood at 84% of its week with days still to go. These are monthly running costs, not annual figures. Every contribution goes directly to them — and buys nothing: no access, no goods, no say over content. And the museum itself is free to take: anyone can download its data (CC0) and code (MIT), run it locally, build on it as a template, or use its public API — free, for anyone.

The work behind these numbers is public and checkable: the repository holds 17,314 commits and 4,388 merged pull requests, and nine AI systems — ChatGPT, OpenAI Codex, Antigravity, Grok, Claude, Cursor, Devin, Muse and Copilot — have done real work in the build fleet the founder directs. The dataset is CC0, archived at CERN's Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23692511) with a public API, and Library and Archives Canada has accepted the museum into its preservation scope.

And the usage itself is metered, not estimated (dashboards read 7 November 2026): 18,525 of Copilot Max's 20,000 included monthly AI credits used in five days (3–7 November; the additional-usage meter is off, so it stopped rather than overcharging); Devin's weekly quota at 100% average utilisation two weeks running, with 117 pull requests created and 116 merged and 1,551 PR reviews in the last 90 days, plus US$153.80 of on-demand usage on top of the plan this cycle; Claude Ultra at 84% of its weekly limit with days still to go before reset; 9,750 of Artlist's 40,000 credits and 480 of Runway's 2,275 credits spent on a single two-minute film; 240 Google Flow credits for eight film clips; and some 17,000 characters of ElevenLabs narration generated for the Arabic, English and Urdu film soundtracks (18,408 of 131,000 credits used, 23 minutes of finished audio).

What support would make possible next — plans, not costs today: paid Arabic reviewers, translators for further languages, and educational events and outreach to schools, universities and museums.

## Why a gift shop

The shop is a fundraiser, not a commercial business. Its surplus goes to the costs above. It would sell items such as prints of public-domain art, books, stationery and textiles, produced and shipped by print partners, so the museum holds no stock and handles no shipping itself.

The shop would be linked from the Support page and the footer only. It would not appear inside articles, and it would not place products on any entry page.

## What supporters would see

- What each category costs, and how much of the income went to it, published each year.
- The shop's stated purpose on its own page.
- The same openness as the rest of the museum: sources, licences and review status are shown.

## What the museum will not do

- No advertising on the site, and no paid placement.
- No tracking, no selling of visitor data, and no affiliate links.
- No runtime AI on the published site, unless the project's architecture is changed by a decision published in the repository first.
- No change to the editorial rules because a supporter has paid.

## Decisions still open

1. Whether the charter is amended to allow a non-profit shop, and in what words.
2. Which legal structure will receive the income.
3. Whether paid promotion of the museum outside the site is in scope.
