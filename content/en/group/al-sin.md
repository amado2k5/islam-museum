## Summary
Al-Sin — China — in the books of hadith and geography is the farthest people of the east: the famous «seek knowledge even unto China» (weak though it is), and the travel books describing its land, kings, sea and customs.

## In the texts
The weak famous hadith names it in seeking knowledge; the geographers place it at the farthest east on the sea of China and the Silk Road; and the travellers like Sulayman the Merchant describe its cities, customs and crafts {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun}.

## In the Sira and history
The conquests reached China's marches under Qutayba ibn Muslim, who famously took Kashgar; Muslims entered it as traders and scholars until Islamic states and great cities stood in it — and China in the Arab imagination was the example of distance {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}.

## For younger visitors
China is the very far land of the east; the Arabs used it as the image of vast distance — seek knowledge even unto China.

## Notes for researchers
The China hadith is weak by the hadith scholars' agreement — cite it as such and do not argue from it; the travel books, not the hadith, are the source for the land.
