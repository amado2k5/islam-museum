## Summary
The Iraqi Academy of Sciences (al-Majma' al-'Ilmi al-'Iraqi) in Baghdad is, according to the research summary supplied to the museum, the third Arabic language academy, founded in 1947. {cite:wikipedia-iraqi-academy-of-sciences-en research summary} {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021 research summary} {cite:wikipedia-iraqi-academy-of-sciences-ar research summary} Its name is not "language academy": it is a scientific academy, and the summary describes it as working to develop and regulate Arabic in Iraq and the Arab world. This page reports what that summary says; the museum did not open the pages it rests on, with the founder's approval of 21 November 2026.

## Founding and founders
The summary gives 1947 as the founding year and makes its first president Shaykh Muhammad Rida al-Shabibi, 1948 to 1949. {cite:wikipedia-iraqi-academy-of-sciences-ar research summary} It does not say whether 1947 was the year of the law or of the start of work. It lists as members of the founding era Ahmad Sousa, Tawfiq al-Suwaydi, Mustafa Jawad and [[person/mohammed-bahjah-al-athari|Muhammad Bahjat al-Athari]]. {cite:wikipedia-iraqi-academy-of-sciences-ar research summary} The museum found no record matching this al-Shabibi, Ahmad Sousa, Tawfiq al-Suwaydi or Mustafa Jawad, so only their names, as the summary gives them, are stated here.

## Role and works
The summary describes the Academy's work as developing and regulating Arabic in Iraq and the Arab world, coining scientific terms and publishing a scholarly journal. {cite:wikipedia-iraqi-academy-of-sciences-en research summary} It does not name the journal or list other publications such as dictionaries or critical editions. What sets the Baghdad body apart from its sisters in Cairo and Damascus is that its name gathers the sciences under one title, although the summary counts it an Arabic language academy.

## Kurdish and Syriac
A particular feature of the Iraqi Academy, according to the summary, is that since 1963 it has had departments for the Kurdish language and for Syriac, which is Aramaic. {cite:wikipedia-iraqi-academy-of-sciences-en research summary} The museum has a record of [[language/kurdish|the Kurdish language]]. The summary does not detail what these two departments did or issued, which this page does not reach. It bears on the linguistic situation of Iraq, for which see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-iraq|Arabic in Iraq]].

## Arabisation, terminology and the Union
Scientific terminology is the Academy's visible work in the summary and the place of Arabisation in Iraq, that is, putting modern concepts into Arabic. {cite:wikipedia-iraqi-academy-of-sciences-en research summary} With the academies of Cairo and Damascus it was a founder of the [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|Union of Arab Language Academies]] in 1971. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} Its sisters are the [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-dimashq|Damascus academy]] and the [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-qahira|Cairo academy]]. For wider context see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab world]].

## What this page does not reach
The source is a research summary supplied to the museum that rests on English and Arabic Wikipedia; the museum did not open those pages, and the founder approved using the summary without re-verification on 21 November 2026. So whether 1947 is the date of the law or the opening, the journal's name, the work of the Kurdish and Syriac departments and the dictionaries the Academy issued are not known here. All of the above is attributed to the summary.
