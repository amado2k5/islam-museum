## Overview
Ikhwan al-Safa was a literary-philosophical circle in fourth-century Basra, authors of the fifty-one Epistles of the Brethren of Purity {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house}.

## In the sources
Al-Dhahabi's Siyar records Abu Hamid al-Ghazali's engagement with the epistles and his entanglement with the philosophers' doctrines, and describes the epistles elsewhere through their critics' words {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam, which also calls them the Brethren of Purity, describes the Ikhwan al-Safa as a clandestine fellowship of philosophers and scientists of the tenth and eleventh centuries who wrote the Epistles of the Brethren of Purity. Both Twelver Shiis and Ismailis claim the Epistles as part of their teachings, it says, even though the text criticises the teaching about the Hidden Imam. The authorship of the Epistles, the Dictionary adds, has been assigned in different accounts to Ali, to Jafar al-Sadiq and to some of the Hidden Imams. It says the brotherhood taught that religion strengthens group cohesion, or asabiyyah, and also sought to bring the pre-Islamic understanding of the cosmos into harmony with what the Quran contains. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e994}
