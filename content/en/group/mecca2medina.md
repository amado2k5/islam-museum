## Summary
Mecca2Medina is a British Islamic hip-hop and nasheed group from London, founded in 1996 by Rakin Fetuga after he left the rap group Cash Crew in 1995; its first EP, Life After Death, came out the same year. {cite:wikipedia-mecca2medina-en} {cite:wikidata-mecca2medina} It has released three nasheed albums, two hip-hop albums and a mix CD, and makes music for Muslims and non-Muslims alike. {cite:wikipedia-mecca2medina-en}

## Members and style
Ismael Lea South and Imran Kazi joined Fetuga first, then Daoud Saifallah and Abdul Kareem Talib; the article, citing Alison Pargeter's book, says all of them converted to Islam except Kazi, who was born Muslim. {cite:wikipedia-mecca2medina-en} Their songs express their Islamic views and comment on social issues of "ghetto life", rapped over African drum rhythms for conservative Muslim audiences and over electronic beats for hip-hop fans. {cite:wikipedia-mecca2medina-en} The members include youth workers, a social worker, a teacher and a civil servant, and they have run workshops around the country with disaffected young people and prisoners. {cite:wikipedia-mecca2medina-en}

## The Salam Project
As The Salam Project they have organised talks with scholars including Abdul Hakim Quick, Ahmed Babiker Abu Bakr al-Sudani and Michael Mumisa, comedy nights with Prince Abdi and Jeff Mirza, and tours for new Islamic rap artists, among them [[group/poetic-pilgrimage|Poetic Pilgrimage]]; they also worked on an album by the Malaysian group [[group/raihan-band|Raihan]]. {cite:wikipedia-mecca2medina-en} Saifallah, then Kazi, then Talib left, leaving Fetuga and South as a duo; Fetuga released a solo album as Rakin Niass. {cite:wikipedia-mecca2medina-en}
