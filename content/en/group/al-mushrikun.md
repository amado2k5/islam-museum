## Summary
The polytheists in the sources are the idol-worshipping Arabs of Makka and those who shared their religion around the Sanctuary: they worshipped idols and made partners with God until Islam came, fought them, argued with them and opened Makka upon them.

## In the texts
The Qur'an addresses them and argues with them and tells of their falsehood: names them with the unbelievers in al-Baqara, orders the bara'a from them in Bara'a, describes their shirk in al-An'am, al-Hajj and al-Rum, names their idols — al-Lat, al-'Uzza, Manat in al-Najm and Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq and Nasr in Nuh — then tells of the conquest of Makka and their entering Islam in crowds {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01}.

## In the Sira and history
The Sira keeps their stand against the Call: the persecution at Makka, then Badr, Uhud, the Trench, al-Hudaybiyya and the Conquest; their leaders Abu Jahl, Abu Lahab, 'Utba, Shayba, Umayya ibn Khalaf, al-Walid ibn al-Mughira and Abu Sufyan; their treaties and their breaking — until they entered Islam on the day of the Conquest and in the delegations of year nine {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For younger visitors
The polytheists were Makka's Arabs who worshipped idols before Islam; they fought the Prophet, then Makka was opened and they became Muslim.

## Notes for researchers
The browser keeps the Arab polytheists of the Jahiliyya in their own section apart from the People of the Book and the other religions; the Sira books name their idols, rites and Jahiliyya customs — do not conflate them.
