## Overview
The first Muslim community — from the vulnerable companions in Mecca to Medina's society — is the kernel from which "the Muslims" became an umma with borders and sovereignty.

## History
The early consensus was built on precedence: the first forerunners in the Sunni tradition are the Meccans first, then [[group/muhajirun|the Muhajirun]], then [[group/ansar|the Ansar]]. In the Meccan decade the community was a persecuted minority worshipping in secret — some fled [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|to Abyssinia]], and its first martyrs fell: [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya]] and her family {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

In Medina the community gained political form: whoever wished entered by faith and emigration; the Charter ordered it as a constitution with the independent tribes; and it stood firm at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] and the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Trench]] {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## Legacy
The very concept of "the Companions" — whoever saw the Prophet a believer and died so — seeded all the sciences of rijal, and made their collective uprightness the cornerstone of Sunni hadith doctrine; historians, for their part, read it as a term the later tradition built, with an isnad function {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.
