## Overview
The Shi'a is the name for those who followed Ali when Talha and al-Zubayr opposed him and insisted on seeking vengeance for the blood of Uthman, and Ali set out to fight them until they returned to God's command. So Ibn al-Nadim relates, and he is the only source cited here. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.249}

## Names
Ali used to say "my Shi'a". They were also called the chosen ones, the friends, the "shurta al-khamis" (the guards of the Thursday), and the companions. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.249}

Ibn al-Nadim explains "shurta al-khamis" by Ali's words to them that he made a bargain with them for Paradise, not for gold or silver. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.249}

## The doctrine of the imamate
Ibn al-Nadim says that the first to speak on the doctrine of the imamate was Ali ibn Isma'il ibn Maytham al-Tammar. He mentions Hisham ibn al-Hakam, and says that he opened up discussion of the imamate and refined the doctrine. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.249}
