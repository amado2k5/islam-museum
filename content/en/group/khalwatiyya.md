## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes the Khalwati order as a Sufi order noted for its custom of periodic retreat, from which the name comes. It says the order began in Central Asia, reached the Ottoman Empire in the 1400s and by the 1500s was the most widespread Sufi order there. It joined learning with devotional practice, and required the disciple's heart to be bound symbolically to the master's, a tie meant to be firmer than any between a father and his son. It also emphasised silence, vigil and taking part in dhikr, the collective prayer, as well as the shared recitation of the wird al-sattar, a work from the fifteenth century that stands at the centre of Khalwati ritual. Revived in Egypt in the seventeenth century, the order became the dominant one there in the eighteenth, a position it held for eighty years, and it made room for both leading scholars and ordinary people. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1265}
