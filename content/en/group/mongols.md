## Summary
The Mongols (Tatars) are the peoples of Mongolia and the far east who united under a single ruler, Genghis Khan, at the start of the seventh Hijri century, extended his rule into the Turkish kingdoms and beyond, and whose descendants completed the conquests until Hulagu took Baghdad in 656 AH and ended the Abbasid caliphate in Iraq {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.34-36}.

## In Ibn Khaldun's Ibar
Ibn Khaldun records in the *Ibar* that the Tatars and Mongols were many dispersed peoples whom Genghis Khan united under his rule, after which they expanded into the neighbouring kingdoms of the Turks, Khurasan, Khwarazm, Iraq and Syria {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.34-36}. The fall of Baghdad to Hulagu in 656 AH, in which the caliph al-Musta'sim was killed and the Ilkhanid states rose on its ruins, marks a turning point in Islamic history {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}.

## For young visitors
The Mongols were peoples of Asia who united under Genghis Khan, expanded until they entered Baghdad in 656 AH and ended the Abbasid caliphate in Iraq; many of them later became Muslim in the states they ruled.
