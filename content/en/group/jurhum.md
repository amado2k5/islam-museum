## Overview
Jurhum: the Yemeni tribe that settled Mecca by leave of Isma'il's mother, from which Isma'il married; it governed Mecca's affairs for some twenty centuries until Khuza'a expelled it.

## Life
A Yemeni tribe — Jurhum — came and settled Mecca by leave of Isma'il's mother {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. Charge of Mecca passed after Isma'il's sons to their grandfather Madad ibn Amr al-Jurhumi; leadership moved to Jurhum and stayed in their hands, Isma'il's sons keeping a respected place for their father's building of the House {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab}.

Later their rule soured: they wronged the pilgrims and made free with the Ka'ba's property, so Khuza'a fought them with Banu Bakr ibn Abd Manaf's aid until driving them out in the middle of the second century CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. They sealed Zamzam and buried in it the Ka'ba's two golden gazelles and the corner-stone, then departed for Yemen grieving at what they had left {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}.

## For young visitors
Jurhum were Isma'il's first neighbours in Mecca; they ruled it a long age until Khuza'a drove them out — and they buried treasures in the well.
