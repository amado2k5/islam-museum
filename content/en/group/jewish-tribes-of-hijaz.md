## Overview
The Jewish tribes who settled in Yathrib ([[place/medina|Medina]]), [[place/khaybar|Khaybar]] and [[place/tayma|Tayma]] after the Jews migrated from Palestine, and who built villages, fortified houses and castles there. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.151}

## The two migrations
The Sealed Nectar gives two phases of Jewish presence in the peninsula: a migration after [[person/bukhtanasar|Bukhtanasar]] ruined Palestine in 587 BCE to the north of the Hijaz, and a migration after the Romans occupied Palestine in 70 CE, when many tribes moved to the Hijaz. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} The details are in [[concept/judaism-in-arabia|Judaism in the Arabian Peninsula]].

## The well-known tribes
When Islam came, the best-known Jewish tribes were [[group/jews-of-khaybar|Khaybar]], [[group/banu-nadir|al-Nadir]], al-Mustaliq, [[group/banu-qurayza|Qurayza]] and [[group/banu-qaynuqa|Qaynuqa]]. Al-Samhudi in Wafa al-Wafa says that the number of Jewish tribes exceeds twenty. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} {cite:wafa-al-wafa p.116} Judaism also had a part in the political events that preceded the rise of Islam and occurred in its early days. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40}

## Notes for researchers
"Al-Mustaliq" appears in the book's list as it is written. The people of the biography tradition count Banu al-Mustaliq as a branch of Khuza'a, so the mention needs to be checked against another edition of the book.

## For young visitors
Before Islam many Jewish tribes lived in Medina, Khaybar and Tayma. The best known were Qurayza, al-Nadir and Qaynuqa.
