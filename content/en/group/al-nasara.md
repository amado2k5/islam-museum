## Summary
The Christians in the sources are the people of the Gospel who followed 'Isa ibn Maryam as their books record: the Christian Arabs of Ghassan, Lakhm, Taghlib and Najran, and the people of al-Sham, Egypt and Abyssinia who confessed Christianity before Islam and in its time.

## In the texts
The Qur'an names them al-Nasara in many places: «you will find the nearest of them in affection to the believers those who say 'we are Christians'» (5:82); gathers them with the Jews, the Sabians and the Magians in al-Hajj, al-Baqara and al-Ma'ida; and describes their monks, scholars, scriptures and the monasticism they invented {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01}.

## In the Sira and history
The Sira keeps the delegation of Najran who debated the Prophet in the mosque and the mubahala, the Prophet's letters to Heraclius of Rome, to al-Muqawqis of the Copts and to the Negus of Abyssinia, the Islam of Arab Christians like 'Adi ibn Hatim and Waraqa ibn Nawfal, and the treaties of the people of Najran, Ayla and Jerash on jizya {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. The historians record the Christians of al-Sham, Egypt and Iraq and their dhimma and aman under the conquests {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}.

## For younger visitors
The Christians are the people of the Gospel; among them were the Arabs of Najran, Ghassan and Lakhm, and the people of Abyssinia, Egypt and al-Sham — and the Prophet wrote to their kings and made treaties with some.

## Notes for researchers
The heresiographies detail the Christian sects — Jacobite, Nestorian and Melkite — by the names their own people gave them; the Arab Christians are described apart from the rest.
