## Overview
Tayy is a Yemeni tribe from the branches of [[group/kahlan|Kahlan]]. The Sealed Nectar counts it among the best-known branches of Kahlan, with Hamdan, Anmar, Madhhij, Kinda, Lakhm, Judham and al-Azd. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16} It left [[place/yemen|Yemen]] in the Kahlani migrations and settled the two mountains [[place/aja-and-salma|Aja and Salma]], which became known as the mountains of Tayy. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}

## The migration to the mountains
The author divides the emigrants from the branches of Kahlan into four groups, and makes Banu Tayy the third: they travelled north after [[group/al-azd|al-Azd]] had gone, until they settled at the two mountains Aja and Salma and remained there, until the two mountains became known as the mountains of Tayy. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.231-235} In describing the dwellings of the tribes, he says that the lands of Bahtar of Tayy lay between [[group/banu-asad-ibn-khuzayma|Banu Asad]] and [[place/tayma|Tayma]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/15-16}

## Religion
The author says that Christianity was adopted by Arabs among the Ghassanids, the tribes of Taghlib and Tayy and others, because of their neighbourhood with the Romans. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}

## In the history of al-Hira
Among Tayy was [[person/iyas-ibn-qabisa|Iyas ibn Qabisa al-Ta'i]], whom Khosrow appointed over al-Hira after he imprisoned [[person/al-numan-ibn-al-mundhir|al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir]]. In his days came [[event/battle-of-dhu-qar|the battle of Dhu Qar]]. The author says that the Prophet was born eight months into Iyas's rule over al-Hira. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}

## For young visitors
Tayy is a tribe that came from Yemen and settled two mountains in the north of the Peninsula, which took its name. Among them was Hatim al-Ta'i, famous for generosity.

## In Ibn Hisham's biography (volume 4)
[[event/delegation-of-tayy|The delegation of Tayy]] came to the Prophet, with their chief [[person/zayd-al-khayl|Zayd al-Khayl]] among them, and they embraced Islam and their Islam was excellent. The Prophet named him Zayd al-Khayr and gave him [[place/fayd|Fayd]] as a grant. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/224} The Prophet's horsemen took captives from Tayy, among them [[person/safana-bint-hatim|the daughter of Hatim]]; they were placed in an enclosure at the door of the mosque where the captives were held. See [[event/capture-and-release-of-hatims-daughter|the capture and release of Hatim's daughter]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/225} [[person/adi-ibn-hatim|Adi ibn Hatim]] used to go among his people taking the mirba', a quarter of the spoils, as their chief. He was a Christian, fled to Syria, then came back and embraced Islam; see [[event/islam-of-adi-ibn-hatim|the Islam of Adi ibn Hatim]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/225} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/227} The Prophet appointed Adi over Tayy and its alms, and over Banu Asad. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/247}
