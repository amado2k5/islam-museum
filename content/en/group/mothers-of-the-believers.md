## Summary
A Qur'anic title for the Prophet's wives, from His word "and his wives are their mothers"; he contracted thirteen women, consummated with eleven of them, and died leaving nine {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 473, 475}.

## Their story
The Prophet married [[person/khadija|Khadija bint Khuwaylid]] when he was twenty-five and she forty — the first woman he married; he took no other wife while she lived, and had sons and daughters by her {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 473}. The Prophet was distinguished from his umma by the licence to marry more than four wives for many purposes; the women he contracted numbered thirteen — he died leaving nine of them, two died in his lifetime (Khadija and Zaynab bint Khuzayma, Mother of the Poor), and two he did not consummate with {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 473, 475}. The eleven are: Khadija; [[person/sawda-bint-zama|Sawda bint Zam'a]] in Shawwal of year ten of the prophethood; [[person/aisha|A'isha bint Abi Bakr]] in Shawwal of year eleven; [[person/hafsa|Hafsa bint Umar]] in 3 AH; [[person/zaynab-bint-khuzayma|Zaynab bint Khuzayma]], Mother of the Poor, in 4 AH — who died two or three months after the marriage; [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama Hind bint Abi Umayya]] in Shawwal 4 AH; [[person/zaynab-bint-jahsh|Zaynab bint Jahsh]] in Dhu al-Qa'da 5 AH; [[person/juwayriya-bint-al-harith|Juwayriyya bint al-Harith]] in Sha'ban 6 AH; [[person/umm-habiba-bint-abi-sufyan|Umm Habiba Ramla bint Abi Sufyan]] in al-Muharram 7 AH; [[person/safiyya-bint-huyayy|Safiyya bint Huyayy ibn Akhtab]] after Khaybar's conquest in 7 AH; and [[person/maymuna-bint-al-harith|Maymuna bint al-Harith]] in Dhu al-Qa'da 7 AH during the umra of consummation {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 473-475}.

## The author's view of the marriages' purposes
Al-Mubarakpuri explains this number was not from lust: the Prophet spent some thirty years of his youth's prime confined to one wife; the purposes were kin-by-marriage to Abu Bakr, Umar, Ali and Uthman; breaking the tribes' enmity to Islam, as with Makhzum, Banu al-Mustaliq and Banu al-Nadir; educating the umma's women so they would carry the call to women where men do not mix with them; and breaking the Jahili rule of adoption by the marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 475-477}. They did great service transmitting his home life to people — especially the longest-lived of them like A'isha, who related much of his deeds and words {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 476}.

## For young visitors
The Qur'an called the Prophet's wives "Mothers of the Believers"; they were eleven ladies who transmitted the Prophet's household news and teachings in the finest details of daily life.
