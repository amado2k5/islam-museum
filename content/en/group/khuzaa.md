## Summary
A Tihama tribe that neighboured [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and intermarried with the house of prophethood. It entered the Prophet's pact and covenant on the day of the [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|Treaty of al-Hudaybiyya]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}. [[group/banu-bakr|Banu Bakr]], backed by Quraysh, attacked it at [[place/al-watir|al-Watir]]. It appealed for help to Medina, and that appeal was among what stirred the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|Conquest of Mecca]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31-37}.

## The old alliance
Khuza'a's alliance with the Prophet's house is old. Abd Manaf's mother (Abd Manaf being the father of Hashim) and Qusayy's mother, Fatima bint Sa'd ibn Sayl, were of Khuza'a, so lineage joins the two houses {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/36}. In the appeal verses of [[person/amr-ibn-salim|Amr ibn Salim]], the alliance is described as that of "our father and his father of old" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/36}.

## The attack on al-Watir
During the truce of al-Hudaybiyya, [[person/nawfal-ibn-muawiya|Nawfal ibn Mu'awiya]] of [[group/banu-al-dil|Banu al-Dil]] raided them by night at al-Watir. They struck a man of them and pursued them until they took refuge in the houses of [[person/budayl-ibn-warqa|Budayl ibn Warqa']] and [[person/rafi-mawla-khuzaa|Rafi']], their freedman {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32-33}. Quraysh supported the attackers with weapons {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}.

## The appeal
[[person/amr-ibn-salim|Amr ibn Salim]] went out to Medina, stood before the Prophet and appealed to him in his verses. Then Budayl came with a group of men to tell him that the covenant had been broken {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/36-37}. Ibn Ishaq says: "That was one of the things that stirred the conquest of Mecca" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/36}.

## For young visitors
A tribe that lived near Mecca and had an old alliance with the Prophet of the Muslims. It was attacked by night while at peace, and its men went to Medina seeking help. Its poet called out the most famous verses of appeal.

## In the introduction to al-Raheeq al-Makhtum
Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum makes Khuza'a one of [[group/al-azd|the Azd]] of Kahlan. Haritha ibn Amr, that is Khuza'a, and his sons moved through the Hijaz until they camped at [[place/marr-al-zahran|Marr al-Zahran]]. Then they took possession of the sanctuary, settled Mecca and expelled its inhabitants, [[group/jurhum|the Jurhum]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.231-235}. When Khuza'a camped at Marr al-Zahran and saw the Adnanites' aversion to the Jurhum, who had wronged visitors and made free with the Ka'ba's property, they fought Jurhum with the help of Banu Bakr ibn Abd Manaf ibn Kinana, until they drove them out of Mecca and took its rule around the middle of the second century CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.28} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.231}. Khuza'a took sole charge of Mecca's affairs, apart from three matters that remained with the Mudar tribes, and its rule over Mecca lasted three hundred years, until [[person/qusayy-ibn-kilab|Qusayy ibn Kilab]] wrested it from [[person/hulayl-ibn-hubshiyya|Hulayl ibn Hubshiyya]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.29} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut 5/181}. For details see [[event/expulsion-of-jurhum|the expulsion of Jurhum]] and [[event/qusayy-takes-mecca|Qusayy's seizure of Mecca]]. Their chief, [[person/amr-ibn-luhayy|Amr ibn Luhayy]], is in the author's account the one who brought the idols into Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35}.

## For Sprouts
Khuza'a was a Tihama tribe neighboring Mecca, in-law to the Prophet's house, and inside the Prophet's pact on the day of the Hudaybiyya treaty. Banu Bakr raided it at al-Watir with Quraysh's backing, and it appealed to Medina — the appeal that set off the opening of Mecca.

## For Explorers
Khuza'a was a Tihama tribe neighboring Mecca, in-law to the Prophet's house — the mother of Abd Manaf, Hashim's father, and Qusayy's mother Fatima bint Sa'd ibn Sayl were of Khuza'a — and inside the Prophet's pact and covenant on the day of the Hudaybiyya treaty. In that truce Nawfal ibn Mu'awiya of Banu al-Dil raided it by night at al-Watir, struck a man of it and chased it until it took shelter in the houses of Budayl ibn Warqa and their client Rafi'; Quraysh supplied the attackers with arms. Amr ibn Salim went out to Medina, stood before the Prophet and appealed in his famous lines 'the pact of our father and his father, the Atlida'; then Budayl came with a party reporting the broken covenant — and Ibn Ishaq says 'that was among what set off the opening of Mecca'.
