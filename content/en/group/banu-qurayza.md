## Overview
Banu Qurayza — the last of Yathrib's great Jews — whose sentencing after the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Trench]] siege is the most disputed event of Medina in narration and reading alike.

## History
Medina accused them of opening the channels to the polytheists during the Trench siege. Besieged for days, they surrendered to the arbitration of [[person/sad-ibn-muadh|Sa'd ibn Mu'adh]], who ruled that their fighting men be killed and their dependants taken captive — some six hundred men were executed, and the pits were dug in Medina's market {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

In the Sunni tradition it is a just ruling on treachery — Sa'd himself, one of their allies, was brought in ill, and his verdict was "by God's ruling", meaning the law of Moses — while in the academic reading it is a turning point from the plural society to the unitary state {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley} {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## Legacy
The Qurayza affair — the Muslims' heaviest case against critics, from medieval churchmen to today's writers — remains the one event in the Prophet's sira that defenders and critics alike prefer to consider on its own {cite:watt-1956-medina}.
