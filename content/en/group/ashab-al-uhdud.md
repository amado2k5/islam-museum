## Summary
The People of the Ditch in Surat al-Buruj are those who dug a trench, fed its fire and burned the believers in it while they sat over it watching; the commentators say they were the Himyarite king Dhu Nuwas and his court who burned the Christians of Najran.

## In the texts
Surat al-Buruj: «slain were the People of the Ditch, of the fuel-fed fire, when they sat by it, and were witnesses of what they did to the believers; they resented them only for believing in God, the Mighty, the Praiseworthy» — this is its whole text on them, and the commentators tell the story at length {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01} {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In the Sira and history
For al-Tabari and the Sira the king Dhu Nuwas — the last Jewish king of Himyar — persecuted the Christians of Najran, dug the trench for them, kindled the fire and cast them into it; this was two generations or so before the Call and the cause of Abyssinia's taking of Yemen {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## For younger visitors
The People of the Ditch burned the believers in fire; Surat al-Buruj names them, and they are said to be the king of Himyar and his court who burned Najran's Christians.

## Notes for researchers
The commentators carry three reports on where the Ditch was — Najran, al-Sham and Iraq — Najran under Dhu Nuwas being the famous one; the Qur'an names neither place nor time.
