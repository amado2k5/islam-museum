## Summary
The Magians in Arab speech are the people of Fars, the fire-worshippers: the Sasanian kings and their commons on the religion of Zardusht, and the name came to cover the whole Persian kingdom until Islam conquered it.

## In the texts
The Qur'an names them in Surat al-Hajj: «the believers, the Jews, the Sabians, the Christians, the Magians and the polytheists — God will judge between them on the Day of Rising»; the hadith treats them in «treat them as you treat the People of the Book» and in marriage, slaughtered animals and jizya {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the Sira and history
The Magians' king when the Prophet was sent was Khosrow Parviz: he tore the Prophet's letter when it was read to him, and the Prophet's saying on the fall of his rule is famous; Khosrow was killed in his son Shirawayh's revolt, then the conquests took Ctesiphon and Fars under 'Umar, and the Magians remained on dhimma and jizya, whom the jurists discuss among the People of the Book and those whose slaughtered animals are eaten {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For younger visitors
The Magians were Persia's fire-worshippers in the Prophet's time; their king Khosrow tore his letter, then Islam conquered their land and they remained on jizya.

## Notes for researchers
The historians distinguish official Sasanian Zoroastrianism from the other faiths inside the kingdom — Iraqi Christianity and Judaism, Khurasani Zardushtism and Manichaeism — and the heresiographies list their sects.
