## Summary
Banu Waqif were a clan of al-Aws ibn Haritha, counted among "Aws Allah" together with Banu Umayya ibn Zayd, Khatma and Wa'il. They settled with Banu al-Salm by the Fadikh mosque, and from them came Hilal ibn Umayya al-Waqifi, one of the three who stayed behind from Tabuk. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 207-208}

## Details
Ibn Ishaq lists Banu Waqif within the dar of Banu Umayya ibn Zayd, Khatma, Wa'il and Waqif — "those are Aws Allah, of al-Aws ibn Haritha." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa j.4 p.162} Al-Samhudi records that Banu Waqif and Banu al-Salm, the two sons of Imru' al-Qays ibn Malik al-Awsi, settled by the Fadikh mosque; Banu Waqif built a fortress (utm) called al-Zaydan, most of which lay south of the Fadikh mosque, until a quarrel between al-Salm and Waqif scattered them. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 207-208}

With their brothers of Aws Allah they were the last of the Ansar to accept Islam: Abu Qays ibn al-Aslat held them back until Badr, Uhud and the Trench had passed, after which they all embraced Islam. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 239-240} From them was Hilal ibn Umayya al-Waqifi, named by Ibn Ishaq as a brother of Banu Waqif and one of the three who remained behind from the Tabuk expedition and were granted repentance. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa j.4 p.178}
