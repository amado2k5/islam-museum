## Overview
The Muhajirun — those who left home and wealth in Mecca, emigrating to God and His messenger — are the state's first emirs, its first four caliphs, and its army commanders in the conquests.

## History
The emigrations began small — [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|Abyssinia]] first — then turned after Aqaba into a collective move to Yathrib {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. In Medina the Prophet made brotherhood between the Muhajirun and [[group/ansar|the Ansar]], until the kinless emigrant became a brother in portion and creed {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

They led its succession after his death — Abu Bakr, then Umar, then Uthman — and in the Sunni tradition they alone hold the class "the first forerunners of the Muhajirun" attested by the Qur'an; among them the conquests' commanders Khalid, Amr, al-Zubayr and Sa'd {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
"Hijra" became the creed's own term — forsaking the homeland for the religion — and the knot of every later brotherhood between stranger and native, from the Ansar to modern jihad movements {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## For Sprouts
The Muhajirun are the Muslims who left their homes in Mecca and emigrated to Medina. They left their money and houses behind, and the people of Medina — the Ansar — received them, made them brothers and shared their wealth and homes.

## For Explorers
The Muhajirun in Islamic terminology are the Muslims who left Mecca and emigrated to Yathrib before the conquest of Mecca; the hadith books record that emigration ended with its conquest. Some emigrated secretly leaving wealth and family behind, some with family and money; among the first were Umar, Uthman, Ali, al-Abbas and others. The people of Yathrib — the Ansar — received them, and the Prophet made a famous brotherhood between them and the Ansar, so they shared houses, palm groves and wealth. In Sunni standing they precede the Ansar by precedence, and the Qur'an mentions their faith, emigration and struggle.

## For Scholars
The Muhajirun are the community that emigrated from Mecca to Yathrib before the conquest of Mecca, the first rank in the Sunni ordering of the Companions' degrees, superior by precedence. They emigrated in secret and open waves between the two pledges of Aqaba and the Prophet's entry into Medina; some left family and wealth, and the Prophet established a famous brotherhood between them and the Ansar dividing houses, palms and property. The Qur'an mentions their emigration alongside faith and struggle in several verses, and the hadith books record that emigration ended with the conquest of Mecca. Among them was the early elite — Abu Bakr, Umar, Ali, Uthman, al-Zubayr, Talha, Sa'd, Sa'id, Bilal, Ammar and others — and among them men who witnessed Badr, Uhud and every event. Jurists and historians detail them: the people of the two pledges, those who emigrated before the great emigration, and questions like the ransom of the emigrant captive. Their formation is the nucleus of the Medinan society that carried the call into the era of the state.
