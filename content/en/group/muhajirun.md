## Overview
The Muhajirun — those who left home and wealth in Mecca, emigrating to God and His messenger — are the state's first emirs, its first four caliphs, and its army commanders in the conquests.

## History
The emigrations began small — [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|Abyssinia]] first — then turned after Aqaba into a collective move to Yathrib {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. In Medina the Prophet made brotherhood between the Muhajirun and [[group/ansar|the Ansar]], until the kinless emigrant became a brother in portion and creed {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

They led its succession after his death — Abu Bakr, then Umar, then Uthman — and in the Sunni tradition they alone hold the class "the first forerunners of the Muhajirun" attested by the Qur'an; among them the conquests' commanders Khalid, Amr, al-Zubayr and Sa'd {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
"Hijra" became the creed's own term — forsaking the homeland for the religion — and the knot of every later brotherhood between stranger and native, from the Ansar to modern jihad movements {cite:watt-1956-medina}.
