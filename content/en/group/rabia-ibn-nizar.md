## Overview
Rabi'a is one of the four great tribes descended from the sons of Nizar ibn Ma'add ibn [[person/adnan|Adnan]]: Iyad, Anmar, Rabi'a and Mudar. Mudar and Rabi'a are the two whose branches multiplied and whose clans widened. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/14-15}

## Branches of Rabi'a
From Rabi'a came Asad ibn Rabi'a, Anaza, Abd al-Qays, the two sons of Wa'il [[group/bakr-ibn-wail|Bakr]] and [[group/banu-taghlib|Taghlib]], and [[group/banu-hanifa|Hanifa]] and others. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20}

## Dwellings
Abd al-Qays and branches of Bakr ibn Wa'il migrated to [[place/bahrayn|Bahrain]], and Banu Hanifa went out to [[place/al-yamama|al-Yamama]]. The rest of Bakr settled from al-Yamama to Bahrain, to Kazima and then the edges of the Iraqi plain, and Taghlib settled in the Euphrates region of the Jazira. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/15-16} The book also says that in the accounts of al-Hira, the rule of Judhayma al-Wadah in the days of Ardashir was over al-Hira and all who were in the Iraqi and Jazira desert from Rabi'a and Mudar. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}

## For young visitors
Rabi'a is a large branch of the Adnanite tribes. Most of its branches lived in the east of the Peninsula, as far as Iraq.
