## Overview
Banu Hashim is the clan of [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] within [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]]. Its small quarter defended him at the height of the enmity, and it later became the name under which the Hashimite, Alid and Fatimid states claimed legitimacy.

## History
The clan includes the household of his grandfather [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]], and his uncles [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]], [[person/hamza|Hamza]] and [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]], his paternal uncles, Muslim and non-Muslim alike. Its guardianship grew great when the tribe took refuge in it during the boycott of [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|the Shi'b of Abu Talib]], an ordeal in which the alliance bore hardship together with the members who had not become Muslim {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

After the death of Abu Talib, the roles followed one another: the diplomatic leadership of al-Abbas, the martyrdom of Hamza at Uhud, and, in Shia belief, the inheritance of the imamate through [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and [[person/fatima|Fatima]] {cite:watt-1953-mecca}.

## Legacy
From this clan emerged the Abbasids, then the Hashemites of Jordan and the 'Alawis of Morocco. In Sunni heritage the Hashimi lineage became the most honoured of the state's lineages; in Imami heritage it is the very title of authority {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## In the introduction to The Sealed Nectar
The Prophet's family is known as the Hashimite family, after his grandfather [[person/hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim ibn Abd Manaf]]. The author therefore discusses the circumstances of Hashim, then of [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]], and then of [[person/abdullah-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|Abdullah]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49}. The house of Hashim is one of the four branches of Abd Manaf, along with Abd Shams, Nawfal and al-Muttalib. It is from this house that God chose the Prophet, according to Muslim's hadith {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} {cite:sahih-muslim 2/245}. When [[person/nawfal-ibn-abd-manaf|Nawfal]] returned Abd al-Muttalib's arkah, Nawfal allied with Banu Abd Shams against Banu Hashim. [[group/khuzaa|Khuza'a]] entered the Dar al-Nadwa and allied with Banu Hashim against Banu Abd Shams and Nawfal. The author says this alliance became the cause of the conquest of Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.50} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul-al-najdi p.41-42}. The ten sons of Abd al-Muttalib and his six daughters are in the entry on Abd al-Muttalib {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52}.
