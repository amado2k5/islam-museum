## Overview
Banu Hashim, [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]]'s clan in [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]] — its smallest quarter, which sheltered him at the height of enmity, and later became the name of the right on which Hashimite, Alid and Fatimid states stood.

## History
The clan comprises his grandfather [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]]'s house and his uncles [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]], [[person/hamza|Hamza]] and [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]] — his blood uncles, Muslim and non-Muslim alike. Its charge grew great when the tribe took shelter in it during the boycott of [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|Shi'b Abi Talib]] — an ordeal in which the bond held even with members who had not become Muslim {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

After Abu Talib's death the roles followed: al-Abbas's diplomatic leadership, Hamza's martyrdom at Uhud, and — in Shia belief — the inheritance of the imamate through [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and [[person/fatima|Fatima]] {cite:watt-1953-mecca}.

## Legacy
From this clan sprang the Abbasids, then Jordan's Hashemites and Morocco's Alawis; Hashimi lineage became in Sunni heritage the state's noblest pedigree, and in Imami heritage the very title of authority {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.
