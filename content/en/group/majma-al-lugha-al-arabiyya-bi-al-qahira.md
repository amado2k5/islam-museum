## Summary
The Academy of the Arabic Language in Cairo was established by royal decree on 31 December 1932 and inaugurated on 30 January 1934, according to the research summary supplied to the museum. {cite:wikipedia-academy-of-the-arabic-language-in-cairo-en research summary} {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021 research summary} {cite:arabicacademy-gov-eg research summary} The summary says its work is to adapt Arabic to modern scientific and cultural needs, to coin scientific terms and to publish major lexicons. This page reports what that summary says; the museum did not open the pages it rests on, with the founder's approval of 21 November 2026.

## Founding and founders
The summary says the decree was issued on 31 December 1932 and the inauguration followed more than a year later, on 30 January 1934. {cite:wikipedia-academy-of-the-arabic-language-in-cairo-en research summary} {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021 research summary} It ascribes the founding to King [[person/fuad-i-of-egypt|Fuad I]] of Egypt and names with him Prime Minister Ismail Sidky, Education Minister Muhammad Hilmi Isa, [[person/ahmed-lutfi-al-sayyid|Ahmed Lutfi el-Sayed]] and the first permanent secretary, Mansur Fahmi. {cite:wikipedia-academy-of-the-arabic-language-in-cairo-en research summary} The summary does not detail what each did, so this page gives the names and posts as they came; the museum has no records yet for Ismail Sidky, Muhammad Hilmi Isa or Mansur Fahmi.

## Role
The summary describes the Academy's work as adapting Arabic to the scientific and cultural needs of the age, including the coining of scientific terms in Arabic. {cite:wikipedia-academy-of-the-arabic-language-in-cairo-en research summary} It says it led early debate on reforming the Arabic script, including defining the letters پ and ڤ for the sounds p and v, which have no letter in the Arabic alphabet. {cite:wikipedia-academy-of-the-arabic-language-in-cairo-en research summary} The mention is brief; the summary does not say what the Academy finally decided or what was adopted, so no more should be read from it than that the Academy was a party to the debate.

## Lexicons and publications
The summary names among the Academy's major lexicons al-Mu'jam al-Kabir and al-Mu'jam al-Wasit. {cite:wikipedia-academy-of-the-arabic-language-in-cairo-en research summary} The museum has a record of each: [[work/al-mujam-al-kabir-majma-al-qahira|al-Mu'jam al-Kabir]] and [[work/al-mujam-al-wasit|al-Mu'jam al-Wasit]]. The summary lists the Academy's official website among its references, and it does not name the Academy's journals or conferences or the number of volumes of al-Mu'jam al-Kabir. {cite:arabicacademy-gov-eg research summary} The dictionaries as a whole have their own browser in the museum.

## Arabisation, terminology and the Union
Scientific terminology is the Academy's most visible fruit in the summary, the practical side of Arabisation, making Arabic able to carry modern scientific meanings. {cite:wikipedia-academy-of-the-arabic-language-in-cairo-en research summary} In 1971 the Academy, with those of Baghdad and Damascus, was a founder of the [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|Union of Arab Language Academies]], whose first president was [[person/taha-hussein|Taha Hussein]]. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} Older than Cairo, as the summary describes it, is the [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-dimashq|Damascus academy]], and after them the [[group/al-majma-al-ilmi-al-iraqi|Iraqi Academy of Sciences]]. For wider context see [[concept/tarikh-al-lugha-al-arabiyya|the history of Arabic]] and [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab world]].

## What this page does not reach
The source is a research summary supplied to the museum that rests on English Wikipedia and the Academy's official website; the museum did not open those pages, and the founder approved using the summary without re-verification on 21 November 2026. So what the Academy decided about the two letters, its members, its presidents' works and the dates of the lexicons are not known here. All of the above is attributed to the summary.
