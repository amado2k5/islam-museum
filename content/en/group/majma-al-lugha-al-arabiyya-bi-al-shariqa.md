## Summary
This page presents the Arabic Language Academy in Sharjah, in the United Arab Emirates, as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it among the Arabic-language institutions of the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula. The museum opened neither the pages cited nor the Academy's site, and what is here is ascribed to the summary.

## What it produces
The summary lists its outputs: support for and oversight of major lexicographical work, including a historical dictionary project presented in multiple volumes; the journal Majallat Majma' al-Lugha al-'Arabiyya bi-l-Shariqa; a library of core linguistic and literary works; conferences, linguistic consultations and terminology work; facilitation of cooperation among Arab language academies, including hosting aspects of [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|the Union of Arab Language Academies]]; and a comprehensive Arabic encyclopedia project {cite:alashj-ae}.
## The historical dictionary
The summary says Sharjah's Academy and [[concept/mashru-mujam-al-doha-al-tarikhi|the Doha Historical Dictionary]] project have had some historical interaction around historical lexicography {cite:alashj-ae} {cite:dohadictionary-org-home}. It does not make clear which historical dictionary it means in Sharjah or the nature of the interaction, so the museum adds no detail. For comparison with the earlier Cairo academy see [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-qahira|the Academy of the Arabic Language in Cairo]].
## Its place among the institutions
It is the most recent of the Emirati academies in the summary, and its form rests on an Amiri decree and not a ministerial decision, that is, a body with an emirate-level government standing. That differs from [[group/king-salman-global-academy-for-the-arabic-language|the King Salman Global Academy]], created by Council of Ministers decree, and from [[group/markaz-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-dubai|the Arabic Language Centre in Dubai]], a private teaching institute.
## What the summary does not say
The summary does not name the Academy's president or structure, the number of volumes of the historical dictionary or when they appeared, the title of the encyclopedia or its scope, or which conferences it held. It describes outputs under general headings, so detail is left to the Academy's site. The museum ascribes nothing to it beyond what the summary says.
## The five bodies at a glance
The summary places these bodies together as the principal contemporary official or major institutional efforts centred in the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula, plus the pan-Arab layer of ALECSO. The table gives in brief what it says of each, and separates what is official, what is commercial and what is a research project.

| Body | Year | Seat | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| King Salman Global Academy for the Arabic Language | 2020 | Riyadh | A government academy by Council of Ministers decree |
| Arabic Language Academy in Sharjah | 2016 | Sharjah | A government academic body by Amiri decree |
| The Arabic Language Centre | 1980 | Dubai | A private commercial teaching institute |
| ALECSO and its Coordination Bureau | 1961 or 1962 for the Bureau | Rabat for the Bureau | A pan-Arab organisation |
| Doha Historical Dictionary | 2013 | Doha | A lexicographic research project |
## An academy that brings academies together
Among the most notable things in the summary is that the Academy facilitates cooperation among Arabic language academies, hosts aspects of the Union of academies, and supports Arab and Islamic language academies together. Its role in the summary is one of coordination and patronage beside lexical production, which distinguishes it from a teaching centre such as the one in Dubai.
