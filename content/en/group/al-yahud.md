## Summary
The Jews in the sources are the people of the Torah among Banu Isra'il and those who joined their faith: the tribes of Yathrib — Qaynuqa', al-Nadir and Qurayza — the Jews of Khaybar and Najran, and those who came after them in the cities of Islam. The Qur'an calls them "those who are Jews" and al-Yahud; the Sunna and the Sira keep their covenants, their affairs and their rabbis.

## In the texts
The Qur'an names the Jews in many places: it gathers them with the Christians, the Sabians, the Magians and the polytheists in Surat al-Hajj; describes their disputes, questions, scripture and its bearers; and tells of their markets and partners, while a party among them is rebuked for distortion and breaking. Surat al-Baqara carries the longest Banu Isra'il narratives, and al-Ma'ida and al-Nisa' their rulings and positions {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01}.

## In the Sira and history
The Prophet made with the Jews of Medina the covenant the historians call 'the Sheet': it made them one ummah with the believers, theirs their religion and the Muslims theirs, with succour against whoever fought the people of the Sheet, and loyalty without sin. Then came the affairs of Qaynuqa', al-Nadir and Qurayza; Khaybar and Medina's villages were taken; and the Jews of Yathrib, Khaybar, Najran and Wadi al-Qura remained on the dhimma and jizya under the caliphs {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. The historians also record the Jews of Egypt, al-Sham, Yemen and Khurasan and their dhimma rules in Islam {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## For younger visitors
The Jews are the people of the Torah; many of them lived in Yathrib, Khaybar and Najran, so the Prophet made the Covenant of Medina with them, and then the known events followed.

## Notes for researchers
The sources distinguish 'Banu Isra'il' — the first community of Musa — from 'the Jews' of Medina and Khaybar, and name their rabbis — Huyayy ibn Akhtab, Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, Sallam ibn Mishkam and Kinana — in the Sira and the Tabaqat.
