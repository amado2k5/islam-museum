## Summary
The people of Nuh in the Qur'an are the first nation to deny its messenger among the recounted peoples: Nuh called them nine hundred and fifty years and few believed, so the Flood destroyed them and God saved Nuh and the people of the Ark.

## In the texts
God tells their account in many suras — Hud, al-Mu'minun, al-Shu'ara', al-'Ankabut, al-Saffat, al-Qamar and Nuh: Nuh's call, his people's denial, the Ark, the Flood, the drowning of his son and the Ark resting on al-Judi {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01}.

## In the Sira and history
The commentators and chroniclers place them in the first human generations and the beginning of idol-worship with the idols of Nuh's people — Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq and Nasr, the first idols on the famous account {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## For younger visitors
Nuh's people denied him so the Flood drowned them, and he and the believers were saved on the Ark.

## Notes for researchers
The Qur'an names no land for Nuh's people; the commentators put them in Iraq and the Jazira — some name Babylon — so check their reports before assigning a place.
