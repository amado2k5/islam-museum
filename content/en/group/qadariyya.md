## Overview
The Qadariyya, as Ibn Badran describes them in al-Madkhal, claim that capacity, will and power belong to them, that they own their deeds, good and evil, and that servants know their deeds without God's prior knowledge of them {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.96}. Ibn Badran holds that their view resembles that of the Magians and the Christians, and that it is the root of heresy {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.96}. According to al-Balkhi, as Ibn al-Nadim reports in al-Fihrist, the first to speak of predestination and of the Mu'tazili position was Abu Yunus al-Aswari, known as Sinsawayh, one of the Asawira, followed by Ma'bad al-Juhani; it is also said that Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik spoke of it {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 557}. Most of what follows comes from the chapters on predestination in [[work/sharh-usul-itiqad-lalakai|Sharh Usul I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna]] by [[person/al-lalakai|al-Lalaka'i]], and describes them in the words of their opponents among the people of hadith. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 563}

## When the question of predestination arose
Al-Lalaka'i has a chapter on when the question of predestination arose and spread in Islam. He relates that Ata' came to Ibn Abbas while he was drawing water from Zamzam and told him that people had spoken about predestination; Ibn Abbas said, "Have they done it?", and held that verses of Surat al-Qamar were revealed about them. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 656} He relates from Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani that he knew people whose only speech was "if it is decreed, if it is ordained". {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 656} He relates from al-Hasan ibn Muhammad that it was first spoken of when the Ka'ba burned: one man said this was of God's decree, another that it was not; the editor dates the burning to some nights into Rabi' I of the year 64, before the news of Yazid ibn Mu'awiya's death arrived. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 657} He relates from Ibn Awn that people spoke only of Ali and Uthman until a man called Sinsawayh the grocer appeared; from Yunus ibn Ubayd that he knew Basra when its only Qadaris were Sinsawayh and [[person/mabad-al-juhani|Ma'bad al-Juhani]]; and from al-Awza'i that the first to speak of predestination was a man of Iraq called Sawsan, a Christian who became Muslim and then reverted, from whom Ma'bad took it, and from Ma'bad [[person/ghaylan-al-dimashqi|Ghaylan]]. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 658} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 659} The editor comments that the report naming Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali as the first is not sound, and that the first is known to be Ma'bad al-Juhani. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 657}

## "The Magians of this community"
Al-Lalaka'i gives the hadiths "The Qadariyya are the Magians of this community; if they fall ill, do not visit them, and if they die, do not attend their funerals", through chains from Ibn Umar and Sahl ibn Sa'd. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 563} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 564} The editor, Nash'at al-Misri, judges their chains weak, cites Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn al-Qayyim as weakening the hadith and al-Albani as judging it hasan through supporting reports – which, he says, is open to question – and quotes al-Bayhaqi: they were called Magians because their view resembles the Magian doctrine of two principles, light and darkness, since they ascribe good to God and evil to another. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 563} In another chapter the editor quotes Ibn Abi al-Izz: the Jabriyya went to excess in affirming predestination and denied the servant's own action, while the Qadariyya, deniers of predestination, made servants creators alongside God. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 612}

## Judgements on them in the reports
Al-Lalaka'i gathers differing reports: from Ibn Abbas that the speech of the Qadariyya is unbelief; from Ibn Umar that he disowned them; and from Ali that he told a man who had spoken about God's will and then affirmed it, "Had you said otherwise I would have struck the place where your eyes are." {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 623} He reports from Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, Malik and al-Awza'i that they are to be asked to repent and, if they do not, put to death; from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, "The Qadariyya are Jews", and from al-Sha'bi, "The Qadariyya are Christians"; from al-Shafi'i, in al-Muzani's report, that he declared them unbelievers; and from Ahmad the like of Malik's view. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 623} In the chapter on punishments carried out on them he relates that Tawus confronted Ma'bad during the circumambulation, and that [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] summoned Ghaylan, had him recite the opening of Surat Ya Sin, and Ghaylan declared his repentance; Umar prayed, "O God, if he is truthful, accept his repentance; if he is lying, make him a sign for the believers"; and he relates from Ibn Awn that he later saw Ghaylan crucified. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 628} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 629}

## Praying behind them and dealing with them
In the chapter on the ban on praying behind the Qadariyya, intermarrying with them, eating their slaughter and accepting their testimony, he relates that [[person/wathila-ibn-al-asqa|Wathila ibn al-Asqa']] ordered a prayer behind a Qadari to be repeated, and Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn likewise; that Malik said one does not pray behind them, naming also Sufyan al-Thawri, Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Abu Yusuf and Ahmad; and that Ibn Sirin disliked their slaughtered meat. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 642} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 643}

## "The disgraces of the Qadari shaykhs"
Al-Lalaka'i has a chapter titled "What has been mentioned of the disgraces of the Qadari shaykhs and the scandals of the Mu'tazila", in which he transmits reports disparaging [[person/amr-ibn-ubayd|Amr ibn Ubayd]] and his father, among them a saying attributed to Amr about the verse on Abu Lahab and the Preserved Tablet; these are polemical reports and are given here attributed to their transmitters. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 648}

:::perspectives
**Sunni: al-Lalaka'i in Sharh Usul al-I'tiqad.** Denying predestination is an innovation that arose late in the Companions' age; the reports on its holders range from declaring them unbelievers to asking them to repent and refusing to pray behind them. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 623} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 642}

**Sunni (Hanbali): Ibn Badran in al-Madkhal.** Their view resembles that of the Magians and the Christians and is the root of heresy. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.96}
