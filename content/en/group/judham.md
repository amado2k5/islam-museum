## Overview
Judham is a Yemeni tribe that The Sealed Nectar counts among the best-known branches of [[group/kahlan|Kahlan]], with Hamdan, Anmar, Tayy, Madhhij, Kinda, Lakhm and al-Azd. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16} The author makes Lakhm and Judham the second group of emigrants from the branches of Kahlan, and notes that among the Lakhmids was Nasr ibn Rabi'a, father of the kings of the Munadhira in al-Hira. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.231-235}

## Not covered here
The Sealed Nectar does not detail Judham's dwellings or their history in the pre-Islamic period. Other sources mention them in the alliance of Heraclius at the battle of Mu'ta (see [[event/battle-of-mutah|Mu'ta]]), among them [[person/farwa-ibn-amr-al-judhami|Farwa ibn Amr al-Judhami]].

## For young visitors
Judham is a tribe that left Yemen with many other tribes and settled near the lands of Syria.

## In Ibn Hisham's biography
Ibn Ishaq relates [[event/raid-of-zayd-on-judham|the expedition of Zayd ibn Haritha against Judham]], after [[person/rifaa-ibn-zayd-al-judhami|Rifa'a ibn Zayd]] embraced Islam and came to his people with the Prophet's letter. Al-Hunayd ibn Aws of Banu al-Dubayb raided Dihya al-Kalbi, and some of the Dubayb rescued what had been taken. Then Zayd came and raided the people at al-Fadafid, killing and taking captives. A delegation of them rode to the Prophet with Rifa'a's letter, and he sent Ali to return what had been taken. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/261} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/264} In the land of Judham is the water of [[place/al-salsal|al-Salsal]], after which [[event/expedition-of-dhat-al-salasil|the expedition of Dhat al-Salasil]] was named. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/272}
