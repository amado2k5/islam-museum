## Summary
The Nabataeans in the historians are the people of Petra and Mada'in Salih who dwelt in the northern Hijaz and southern al-Sham before Islam; their remains stand at al-Hijr and Petra, and the commentators and geographers name them in the account of Thamud.

## In the texts
The historians and commentators name them among the kings of al-Hira and al-Sham and among those who dwelt in Thamud's homes after them: the Nabataean kings of Petra and al-Hijr and their dealings with Rome and Persia, then their account in the Jahiliyya {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01}.

## In the Sira and history
The Nabataeans were an Arab state before Islam whose kingdom ran from the Hijaz to Damascus: their best-known kings al-Harith IV and Malichus II; then Rome took them, and their cities — Petra and al-Hijr — remain among earth's most famous remains {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## For younger visitors
The Nabataeans are the people of Petra and Mada'in Salih before Islam; their rock-carved houses still stand today.

## Notes for researchers
'Anbat' for the chroniclers and geographers names two peoples: the Arabs of al-Hijr and Petra, and the older Nabataeans of Iraq's Sawad — keep them apart, do not merge.
