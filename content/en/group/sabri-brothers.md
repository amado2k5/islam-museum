# The Sabri Brothers

## Summary

The Sabri Brothers were a Pakistani qawwali party fronted by the brothers Ghulam Farid Sabri and Maqbool Ahmed Sabri, closely connected with the Chishti order and counted among the greatest Sufi qawwals; they were called 'Shahanshah-e-Qawwali' and 'the roving ambassadors of Pakistan'. {cite:wikipedia-sabri-brothers-en} {cite:wikidata-sabri-brothers}

## Beginnings

The brothers learned from their father, Inayat Hussain Sabri; the family claimed descent from Mian Tansen of Akbar's court, and they studied poetry with Hairat Ali Shah Warsi. Ghulam Farid first sang in public at the urs of Mubarak Shah in Kalyana in 1946, and the family moved to Karachi after Partition in 1947. {cite:wikipedia-sabri-brothers-en}

Maqbool Ahmed Sabri, aged eleven, formed the 'Bacha Qawwal Party', which first performed in 1956 at an urs at Jameel Amrohi's home; Ghulam Farid joined at their father's insistence and became its leader, and on their 1975 American tour their promoter Beate Gordon suggested the name 'The Sabri Brothers'. {cite:wikipedia-sabri-brothers-en}

## Career

Their first record, 'Mera Koi Nahi Hai Tera Siwa', came out on EMI Pakistan in 1958; in the 1970s 'Tajdar-e-Haram', 'O Sharabi Chhod De Peena' and 'Khwaja Ki Deewani' became their great hits, and their qawwalis featured in Pakistani and Indian films, among them 'Bhar Do Jholi Meri Ya Muhammad' (1975). They were the first qawwals to perform in the United States and the West and the first Asian artists at New York's Carnegie Hall, in 1975; they sang at the Royal Albert Hall at the World of Islam Festival on 20 June 1976, recorded for the UNESCO Collection of Traditional Music in 1977, and made 'Ya Habib' (1990) for Real World. {cite:wikipedia-sabri-brothers-en}

Ghulam Farid Sabri died of a heart attack on 5 April 1994; Maqbool Ahmed Sabri then led the party until his death in South Africa on 21 September 2011, and the youngest brother, Mehmood Ghaznavi Sabri, until his own death on 21 June 2021, when the group came to an end. Ghulam Farid's son Amjad Sabri led his own party until his assassination on 22 June 2016. {cite:wikipedia-sabri-brothers-en}

## Influence

The article records [[person/abida-parvin|Abida Parveen]] saying she learned 'Man Kunto Maula' from them, and [[person/sami-yusuf|Sami Yusuf]] covering 'Ya Mustafa' on his debut album 'Al-Mu'allim' (2003). An underpass at Liaquatabad, Karachi, was named after Ghulam Farid Sabri in 2008, and Atif Aslam sang 'Tajdar-e-Haram' in tribute on Coke Studio (2015). {cite:wikipedia-sabri-brothers-en}

## Honours

The whole group received the [[award/pride-of-performance|Pride of Performance]] from the President of Pakistan in 1978, and Maqbool Ahmed Sabri the Tansen Samman of the Madhya Pradesh government in 2005. {cite:wikipedia-sabri-brothers-en}
