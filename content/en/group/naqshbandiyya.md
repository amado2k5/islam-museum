## Overview
The Naqshbandi order is among the most widespread Sufi orders in Islamic history; it is named after Baha al-Din Naqshband of Bukhara (d. 791 AH/1389 CE) and was marked from its Central Asian beginnings by silent remembrance (dhikr khafi) rather than audible dhikr, and by outward sobriety {cite:trimingham-sufi-orders-1998 p. 31}.

## Spread and branches
The order extended from Khorasan into the Ottoman lands and then into India, where its best-known branch — the Mujaddidi — arose under Ahmad Sirhindi; recent scholarship documents its Mujaddidi networks running through Yarkand, Kabul and Sindh in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries {cite:ziad-naqshbandi-mujaddidi-2018}. Kashf al-Zunun attests Naqshbandi affiliations of Ottoman-era scholars {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan}.
