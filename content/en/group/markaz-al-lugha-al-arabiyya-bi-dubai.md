## Summary
This page presents the Arabic Language Centre in Dubai as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it among the Arabic-language institutions of the Gulf. The summary notes that it is a commercial teaching institute and not a governmental language academy or research body, so it is placed here with that difference kept. The museum opened neither the pages cited nor the Centre's site, and what is here is ascribed to the summary.

## What it offers
By the summary the Centre teaches Arabic as a foreign language to adults, children and corporate clients, using the proprietary Maliha Method, which focuses on practical spoken Arabic, often Levantine-influenced with elements of Modern Standard Arabic. Its courses run from beginner to advanced and A-Level, and it provides corporate training {cite:arabiclanguagecentre-com}.
## Why it appears in the browser
The research file lists this Centre among the bodies working on Arabic in the Gulf today, and it represents a different face from the academies: teaching Arabic to foreigners and residents in a multilingual city, which connects with what the museum says of Arabic in the Emirates, see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-united-arab-emirates|Arabic in the Emirates]]. It should not be taken to be an official body or its curricula to express a state language policy.
## Contrast with the academies
The Centre's task differs from that of [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-shariqa|the Sharjah Academy]], which rests on an Amiri decree and supports language and lexicography, and from [[group/king-salman-global-academy-for-the-arabic-language|the King Salman Academy]]. The Centre charges fees and sells teaching, the academies are public bodies, and the summary confirms this by calling it a commercial institute.
## What the summary does not say
The summary gives no number of learners, no results, no detail of the Maliha Method, no word on whether the Centre still works under the same name, and no date for its accreditation. The museum therefore claims nothing of its quality and does not assess it.

## Four decades of teaching
By its founding year the Centre's work runs from 1980 to today, which makes it older than the academies of Sharjah and Riyadh. That does not make it an official language body, but it shows that teaching Arabic to foreign residents in Dubai precedes the modern academies. This is a reading of the numbers in the summary and not a claim about its influence.
