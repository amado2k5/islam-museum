## Overview
The Jahmiyya split into three groups over the question of the Qur'an. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.54} According to the book's description, the people of this view say that the Qur'an is created, that God did not speak to Musa, and that God is not a speaker. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.96} The book reports from the scholars that they agreed that whoever holds this view is asked to repent; if he does not repent, he is put to death, and he may not marry nor is his judgement accepted. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.54}

## In the Book of the Sunna of Abu Dawud
In the Book of the Sunna of the [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan]], [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] has "a chapter on the Jahmiyya" followed by "a chapter refuting the Jahmiyya". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 61} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 67}
- **The annotator's definition**: quoting Muhammad Muhyi al-Din 'Abd al-Hamid, the annotator says the Jahmiyya are named after [[person/jahm-ibn-safwan|Jahm ibn Safwan]] of Samarqand, al-Rasibi, whom he calls "a pure determinist" who agreed with the Mu'tazila in denying the eternal attributes and went further; that al-Tabari records him as secretary to [[person/al-harith-ibn-surayj|al-Harith ibn Surayj]], who rose in Khurasan at the end of the Umayyad state; that his doctrine appeared in [[place/tirmidh|Tirmidh]]; and that [[person/salm-ibn-ahwaz|Salm ibn Ahwaz]] (the text as read has «Salama») killed him at [[place/marw|Marw]] at the end of Umayyad rule. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 61-62}
- **Hearing and sight**: Abu Dawud relates from Abu Yunus, client of Abu Hurayra, that [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] recited [[passage/quran-4-58|al-Nisa' 58]] up to "All-Hearing, All-Seeing", put his thumb on his ear and the next finger on his eye, and said he saw the Prophet do so; Abu Dawud says, "This is a refutation of the Jahmiyya." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 65} [[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|Al-Khattabi]] explains it as affirming the attributes of hearing and sight for God, not ears and eyes, which are organs; God "has no organs, parts or portions". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 65}
- **The reports "refuting the Jahmiyya"**: Ibn 'Umar's report of the heavens being folded on the Day of Resurrection and the words "I am the King; where are the tyrants?", and Abu Hurayra's report of the Lord's descent each night to the lowest heaven when the last third of the night remains. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 67-68}

:::perspectives
**Sunni: al-Khattabi in Ma'alim al-Sunan.** For him, the way of the early scholars and the masters of law is to let reports on the attributes, such as the report of the descent, run on their apparent wording, without seeking out meanings for them or interpreting them; he relates from al-Awza'i that Makhul and al-Zuhri said, "Pass the reports on as they came," and counts them among the ambiguous verses whose apparent sense is believed while their inner meaning is left to God. He criticises a hadith scholar who said of the descent, "He moves if He wills and does not if He wills", calling it a gross error, since motion and rest are qualities of created things. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 67-68} On the report of the mountain goats and the creaking of the Throne, he holds that taken at face value it implies a manner (kayfiyya), which is denied of God, so it is "speech of approximation" meant to convey God's majesty to a Bedouin. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 64}

**Sunni: the annotator's pointers to other views.** The annotator notes that Ibn al-Qayyim wrote some twenty pages on the creaking report, on the meaning of *istiwa'* and the scholars' views, in his edition of al-Mundhiri's abridgement, and that [[person/muhammad-zahid-al-kawthari|Muhammad Zahid al-Kawthari]] wrote an essay, "The Legend of the Mountain Goats", quoting Ibn al-'Arabi that these were things picked up from the People of the Book with no sound basis; these texts have not yet been read in their sources. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 63-64}
:::
