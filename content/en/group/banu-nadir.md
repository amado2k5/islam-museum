## Overview
Banu al-Nadir — Yathrib's farming Jews, the grandest of them in houses and wealth — were expelled with choice of what they could carry in 625 CE after an accusation of a murder plot; their fate ended at [[place/khaybar|Khaybar]], then in its undoing.

## History
In the years after Uhud Medina accused their chief [[person/huyayy-ibn-akhtab|Huyayy ibn Akhtab]] of arranging the Prophet's assassination by inviting him to his gathering; the armies besieged them until they left with six hundred loads and whatever the camels carried — save weapons {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. Many settled at Khaybar, and Huyayy became the leader of the Jewish front against the state until he was killed after it fell {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

Their land — Fadak's orchards and the Nadir's estates — became in the tradition a fay' belonging to the Prophet, and from it came his endowment {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
The Nadir story is a knot in Jewish–Islamic classification: in the Sira a just punishment for treachery; in academic study a prime example of "pre-emptive expulsions"; and in Jewish heritage a chapter of the peninsula's lost laments {cite:watt-1956-medina}.
