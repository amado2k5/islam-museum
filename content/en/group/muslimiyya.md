## Overview
The Muslimiyya were a belief that arose in Khurasan after the coming of Islam, according to Ibn al-Nadim. Its followers are the followers of Abu Muslim: they believe in his imamate and say that he is alive and provided for. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.483}

## After the killing of Abu Muslim
Ibn al-Nadim says that his missionaries fled to the regions of the land when al-Mansur killed him. A man known as Ishaq reached the Turks in Transoxiana, set up a missionary for Abu Muslim, and claimed that he was imprisoned in the mountains of al-Ray and would come out at a time known to them, as the Kaysaniyya claim of Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.483}

He says that some people said Ishaq was of the Alawids, of the descendants of Yahya ibn Zayd ibn Ali. He transmits from al-Balkhi that some people call the Muslimiyya the Harmadiniyya. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.483-484}
