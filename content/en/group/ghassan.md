## Summary
In Ka'b's account, "the king of Ghassan" sent him a letter with a Nabataean of Syria's Nabataeans, inviting him to join him {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/179}.

## In this batch
The letter, its text and Ka'b's reply to it are in [[event/ghassan-kings-letter-to-kaab|the event of the letter]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/179}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- The text does not name the king and attributes to him only the title "king of Ghassan". The page is about a tribe or kingdom according to the text, not a historical investigation (needs_source).

## In the introduction to al-Raheeq al-Makhtum
Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum counts the sons of Jafna, the kings of Syria, among the clans of [[group/kahlan|Kahlan]] of Qahtan. [[person/jafna-ibn-amr|Jafna ibn Amr]] went from [[group/al-azd|the Azd]] to Syria, where he and his sons settled. He is the father of the Ghassanid kings, named after a water in the Hijaz called Ghassan, where they first settled {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.231-235}. Sulayman al-Nadwi's preferred view, which the book reports, is that the kings of the house of Ghassan came from the house of [[person/nabit|Nabit ibn Isma'il]], not from Qahtan {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran 2/78-86}.

The house of Ghassan overcame [[group/dajaima|al-Dajaima]], and the Romans made them kings over the Arabs of Syria. Their base was [[place/dumat-al-jandal|Dumat al-Jandal]]. The Ghassanids remained agents of the Roman kings until the battle of al-Yarmuk in 13 AH. Their last king, [[person/jabala-ibn-al-ayham|Jabala ibn al-Ayham]], embraced Islam in the reign of Umar ibn al-Khattab {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/34} {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran 2/80-82}. The Ghassanids embraced Christianity because of their proximity to the Romans {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}. See [[polity/ghassanid-kingdom|the Ghassanid kingdom]].
