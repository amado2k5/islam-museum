## Summary
The Persians in the sources are the people of Fars and its kings: the Sasanian kingdom that ruled Iraq, Persia and parts of Arabia and al-Sham until Islam conquered it — their Khosrow, their Magianism and their 'ajam speech. Al-Mas'udi recounts their kings, cities and chronicles in Muruj al-Dhahab.

## In the texts
The Qur'an mentions Persia in 'the Romans were defeated' on the famous reading without naming it — the Romans defeated by Persia, then victorious in a few years — and the hadith names them: «were faith hung on the Pleiades, men of Persian stock would take it», and among the Prophet's companions of them was Salman al-Farisi {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the Sira and history
Persia's king when the Prophet was sent was Khosrow Parviz, who tore his letter; then came the conquests of Qadisiyya, Ctesiphon, Jalula and Nahawand under 'Umar and Sasan fell after four centuries, and the Persians entered Islam — many of Islam's notables came of them {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}.

## For younger visitors
The Persians are the people of Fars and its Sasanian kings; their king Khosrow tore the Prophet's letter, then Islam conquered their land and they entered it.

## Notes for researchers
'Fars' to the Arabs names the province and the kingdom, 'al-Furs' its people, 'al-'Ajam' the tongue — and Magianism was their official faith, with Christians, Jews and Zardushtis among them before the conquest.
