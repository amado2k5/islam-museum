## Overview
Taghlib is one of the two sons of Wa'il together with [[group/bakr-ibn-wail|Bakr]], of [[group/rabia-ibn-nizar|Rabi'a ibn Nizar]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} Taghlib settled in the Euphrates region of the Jazira, and some of its branches lived alongside Bakr. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/15-16}

## Religion
The Sealed Nectar notes that Christianity was adopted by Arabs among the Ghassanids, the tribes of Taghlib and Tayy and others, because of their neighbourhood with the Romans. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}

## The wars of Bakr and Taghlib
The author gives the wars of Bakr and Taghlib as an example of how rivalry for honour and leadership often led to wars between tribes that share one father. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.45}

## For young visitors
Taghlib were a tribe who lived in the north of Iraq on the Euphrates, and most of them were Christian before Islam.

## For Sprouts
Taghlib, a tribe of Rabi'a ibn Nizar that settled the Euphrates Jazira, mostly Christian before Islam.

## For Explorers
Taghlib, one of Wa'il's two sons with Bakr, of Rabi'a ibn Nizar; it settled the Euphrates Jazira, with clans that neighbored Bakr. The Raheeq records that Christianity was embraced among the Arabs by the Ghassanids and the tribes of Taghlib, Tayy and others for living beside the Romans. The author takes the wars of Bakr and Taghlib as his example that rivalry in honor and supremacy often ended in wars between tribes sharing one father.
