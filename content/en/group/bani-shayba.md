## Overview
## Who they are
Bani Shayba are a clan of Banu Abd al-Dar ibn Qusay of Quraysh — descendants of Uthman ibn Talha — who have carried the key of the Kaaba from the pre-Islamic period to this day. Their key-bearer is called the sadin, the custodian, and the family carries the nisba al-Shaibi.

## The line of custody
The tradition in their telling runs back to Ibrahim entrusting the Kaaba's custody to his son Ismail and his sons; it passed to Jurhum, then Khuza'a, then Qusayy ibn Kilab and his son Abd Manaf, who before his death gave it to his eldest son Abd al-Dar in his honour. It moved within the house of Abd al-Dar until it reached Uthman ibn Talha; on his death without heirs it went to his cousin Shayba ibn Uthman ibn Abi Talha, in whose line it remains.

## The covenant of the conquest
On the day Mecca was conquered in 8 AH the Prophet took the key from Uthman ibn Talha, entered the Kaaba and prayed inside it, then came out and returned the key saying: "Take it, O Bani Abi Talha, eternally, until the Day of Resurrection; none shall take it from you but an unjust, oppressive tyrant." That covenant is the ground on which the Al al-Shaibi carry the key to this day.

## What they do now
Bani Shayba greet visitors at the Kaaba's washing ceremony and wash its interior alongside them. The eldest of the family holds the key: Shaykh Abdul-Aziz al-Shaibi held it eighteen years until his death in November 2010; his brother Abdul-Qadir — the 108th successor of Uthman ibn Talha — until 2014; then Dr Saleh ibn Zayn al-Abidin al-Shaibi until his death on 22 June 2024, and now Abdul-Wahab ibn Zayn al-Abidin al-Shaibi.

## Bab Bani Shayba
A free-standing arch beside their houses near the Kaaba bore their name — Bab Bani Shayba — removed around 1950 in the mataf expansion; the name survives as one of the gates of the Masjid al-Haram through which pilgrims enter to the tawaf. {cite:wikipedia-banu-shaybah-en}
