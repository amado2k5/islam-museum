## Summary
This page presents the King Salman Global Academy for the Arabic Language in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it among the Arabic-language institutions of the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula today. The museum opened neither the pages cited nor the Academy's site, so everything here is ascribed to the summary.

## What it produces
The summary lists its outputs: computational linguistics tools and corpora, among them the Falak platform; the Riyadh Dictionary of Contemporary Arabic; a digital library and publications; educational programmes and Arabic competency tests, including for non-native speakers; cultural initiatives and heritage revival; and partnerships, among them with ALECSO on unified terminology dictionaries and language-policy systems {cite:ksaa-gov-sa} {cite:alecso-org}.
## The unified terminology platform
The summary's entry on ALECSO mentions a Unified Arabic Terminology Dictionary platform launched in 2026 with the Academy, aggregating terms from dozens of dictionaries; see [[group/alecso|ALECSO]]. It is a partnership joining a Saudi national body with a pan-Arab organisation.
## Its place among the institutions
The summary says the Academy has become, since 2020, a highly active national and international hub with a strong digital and policy focus. Historically it is later than [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-qahira|the Academy of the Arabic Language in Cairo]], founded in 1932, and than [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|the Union of Arab Language Academies]]. It works alongside [[concept/mashru-mujam-al-doha-al-tarikhi|the Doha Historical Dictionary project]].
## What the summary does not say
The summary does not give the Academy's administrative structure, its head or budget, does not say when the Riyadh Dictionary was launched or how many entries it has, and does not detail the competency tests it runs. It does not distinguish what is done from what is under way. That is left to a visit to the Academy's site, and the record will be updated as things are established. It also does not set out the Academy's relation to the older academies, so the museum claims no relation beyond the partnership with ALECSO.
## The five bodies at a glance
The summary places these bodies together as the principal contemporary official or major institutional efforts centred in the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula, plus the pan-Arab layer of ALECSO. The table gives in brief what it says of each, and separates what is official, what is commercial and what is a research project.

## Digital and policy work
The Academy's work in the summary joins computational linguistics with language planning: corpora and platforms on one side, a reference role in policy on another, and educational programmes on a third. That puts it at the meeting of technology, policy and education, and not at the dictionary alone as in the older academies.
