## Overview
The Jarajima are a community from the town of Jurjuma ([[place/jurjuma|Jurjuma]]) on the Lukkam mountain between Bayas and Buqa. In the Roman period they were subject to the patriarch of Antioch. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The treaty
The Muslims made peace with them on condition that they be helpers, informants and garrison guards, without poll tax, and that they be given a share of the spoils of those they killed. Merchants, hired workers and the Nabateans entered into their treaty, and so they were called the Rawadif. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} See [[event/treaty-with-jarajima|the agreement with the Jarajima]].

## In the time of al-Walid
Al-Baladhuri reports that Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan secured their loyalty after the strife of Ibn al-Zubayr. Then al-Walid opened their city for them in the year 88 AH, on condition that they might settle wherever they chose in Syria, that each man receive eight dinars and food for his dependants of wheat and oil. They were not forced to leave Christianity, they wore the clothing of the Muslims, and there was no poll tax on them or on their children and women. They fought with the Muslims, and what was taken from their trade was what was taken from the Muslims. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Al-Walid destroyed their town and settled them on the mountain of al-Mawar, the Sanh al-Lulun and the depths of Tizin, and some of them went to Homs. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The poll tax afterwards
Some of the agents in Antioch imposed the head tax on them. They appealed to al-Wathiq, who waived it for them. Then al-Mutawakkil ordered it collected from them again, with their allowances continued, because they were among those used for garrison duty. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
