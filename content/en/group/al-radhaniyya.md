## Overview
Ibn Khurradadhbih describes them as Jewish merchants who speak Arabic, Persian, Roman, Frankish, Andalusian and Slavic. They travel by land and sea from the East to the West and from the West to the East. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp. 153-155}

## Their goods
From the West they bring slaves, concubines, brocade, sable and squirrel furs, fox pelts, and swords. From China they carry musk, aloes wood, camphor and cinnamon. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp. 153-155}

## Their routes
By sea, they board ships from the Franks in the western sea and land at al-Farama, then carry their goods on the back of beasts to al-Qulzum, and sail the eastern sea to Sind, India and China. There is also an overland route from al-Andalus to Tangier, then Ifriqiya, Egypt, Syria, Iraq, Persia, Sind, India and China. Sometimes they pass through the lands of the Saqaliba, the Khamlij city of the Khazars and the sea of Jurjan, to Transoxiana and China. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp. 153-155}
