## Overview
The Almohads were the call of the Masmuda, established by Muhammad ibn Tumart, known as al-Mahdi, in the cause of monotheism and in fighting those he named the anthropomorphists. The state of Abd al-Mu'min ibn Ali then arose on this call and ruled both Maghribs. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1668-1669}

## The name
Ibn Khaldun says that Ibn Tumart called his followers the Almohads, as a pointed allusion to the Almoravids of Lamtuna. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1668-1669}

See [[group/masmuda|the Masmuda]] and [[group/lamtuna|the Lamtuna]].

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam defines the Almohads, also called al-Muwahhidun or Unitarians, as a movement begun in southern Morocco by [[person/ibn-tumart|Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Tumart]], and gives its period of rule as 1130 to 1269. It says the movement preached moral reform, the supremacy of the Qur'an and hadith, and tawhid, and rejected the authority of the law schools, anthropomorphism, pagan Berber customs, wine, music and luxurious clothing, wanting to restore the early Islamic community. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e134}

It adds that Islam was used to give legitimacy to new political elites and to unite tribes, that the regime was a leader with a ten-member council of disciples, itself advised by an assembly of fifty delegates of the tribes, and that religious offices covered a moral overseer, muezzins and Qur'an teachers. The Dictionary credits the movement with taking Morocco, reaching into part of Spain, and attacking Algeria and Tunisia. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e134} This is a summary of a short entry; Ibn Khaldun's account above remains this record's first source, and the Dictionary is not offered as the only witness for anything stated here.
