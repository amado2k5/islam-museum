## Summary
The Tabi'un (Successors) are the generation that learned from [[group/sahaba|the Companions]]. [[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] calls knowledge of them, together with knowledge of the Companions, "two great bases" by which a *mursal* report is told from a connected one; this is type forty of al-Taqrib, which [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] explains in [[work/tadrib-al-rawi|Tadrib al-Rawi]]. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 239} A single one of them is called *tabi'i* or *tabi'*. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 239}

## Who counts as a Successor?
Hadith scholars disagreed on the definition. [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|Al-Khatib]] said: one who accompanied a Companion; for him mere meeting is not enough, unlike a Companion with the Prophet, which al-Suyuti explains by the Prophet's rank. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 239} Others said: one who met a Companion even without accompanying him; this is [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]]'s view, which [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] called "closer" and al-Nawawi "more evident". {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 239} The editor Muhammad Awwama notes that al-Hakim's own wording implies both meeting and hearing, and that al-Nawawi's phrasing here falls short of it. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 240}

[[person/abd-al-rahim-al-iraqi|Al-Iraqi]] said most hadith scholars act on the second view: [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] and [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]] placed [[person/al-amash-sulayman-ibn-mihran|al-A'mash]] among the Successors because he saw [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], even though no hearing from him is soundly established, and Abd al-Ghani ibn Sa'id counted Yahya ibn Abi Kathir among them because he met Anas. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 240} Ibn Hibban required that the Companion be seen at an age when one retains what one hears, so he counted Khalaf ibn Khalifa among the Followers of the Successors although he had seen Amr ibn Hurayth, because he was then small; al-Iraqi said this choice has a point, just as discernment is required of one who saw the Prophet. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 241} Al-Iraqi argues that seeing suffices from the hadith "Blessed is he who saw me and believed in me, and blessed is he who saw one who saw me"; the editor lists its routes and judges that together they support one another. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 241} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 242}

Al-Suyuti quotes Ibn al-Salah that the word *tabi'i*, used without qualification, means one who followed "in excellence" (*bi-ihsan*); al-Iraqi objected that if excellence means Islam the point is obvious, but if it means perfection and probity he knew of no one who required it, since the authors of the generation-books included the reliable and the unreliable alike. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 242} The editor suggests that Ibn al-Salah was speaking of transmission and had in mind excluding figures such as Ibn Muljam and al-Hajjaj, so his remark has some ground. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 242}

## Tiers
Muslim divided them into three tiers, [[person/muhammad-ibn-sad|Ibn Sa'd]] into four, and al-Hakim said they were fifteen. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 242} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 243} His first tier are those who met the Ten, such as [[person/qays-ibn-abi-hazim|Qays ibn Abi Hazim]], [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]], [[person/abu-uthman-al-nahdi|Abu Uthman al-Nahdi]], Abu Wa'il and [[person/abu-raja-al-utaridi|Abu Raja' al-Utaridi]]. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 243} Al-Hakim was faulted over Ibn al-Musayyab, who was born in the caliphate of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] and did not hear most of the Ten; some said his hearing is soundly established only from [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd]], and al-Iraqi traces this to [[person/qatada-ibn-diama|Qatada]]'s words in the preface of Muslim's Sahih: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab never told us anything face to face from a veteran of Badr except from Sa'd ibn Malik. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 243} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 244} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 245} Yet [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] affirmed that he heard from Umar, [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]] said he saw Umar as a child, [[person/abu-hatim-muhammad-al-razi|Abu Hatim]] said he saw him on the pulpit announcing the death of al-Nu'man ibn Muqarrin, and al-Iraqi cites a report in the Musnad showing he heard from Uthman. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 245} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 246} Qays, for his part, heard the Ten and narrated from them, the only one to do so, though some said he did not hear [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]]. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 246}

Ibn al-Salah and al-Nawawi put next those children of Companions born in the Prophet's lifetime, such as Abd Allah ibn Abi Talha, Abu Umama As'ad ibn Sahl and Abu Idris al-Khawlani; al-Bulqini objected that this holds neither in meaning nor in transmission, since one born in the Prophet's lifetime should come first, and al-Hakim did not say so. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 247} Al-Hakim's second tier: [[person/al-aswad-ibn-yazid-al-nakhai|al-Aswad ibn Yazid]], [[person/alqama-ibn-qays-al-nakhai|Alqama]], [[person/masruq-ibn-al-ajda|Masruq]], [[person/abu-salama-ibn-abd-al-rahman|Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman]] and [[person/kharija-ibn-zayd-ibn-thabit|Kharija ibn Zayd]]; his third: [[person/amir-al-shabi|al-Sha'bi]], [[person/shurayh-al-qadi|Shurayh]] and [[person/ubaydallah-ibn-abdallah-ibn-utba|Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Utba]]. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 248} The last are those who met Anas in Basra, Ibn Abi Awfa in Kufa, [[person/al-saib-ibn-yazid|al-Sa'ib ibn Yazid]] in Medina, Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn Jaz' in the Hijaz and [[person/abu-umama-al-bahili|Abu Umama al-Bahili]] in Syria; al-Hakim in fact described only the first three tiers and the last. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 248}

## The mukhadramun and the Seven Jurists
Among the Successors are the [[concept/mukhadram|mukhadramun]], who lived in the Jahiliyya and in the Prophet's time and accepted Islam without seeing him; Muslim counted twenty of them, and there are more. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 248} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 252} Among the greatest Successors are [[group/al-fuqaha-al-saba|the Seven Jurists]] of Medina. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 255}

## The best of them, and the leading women
Who was the best Successor is debated: the people of Medina said Ibn al-Musayyab, the people of Kufa [[person/uwais-al-qarni|Uways al-Qarani]], and the people of Basra [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan]]; the question has its own card, [[question/afdal-al-tabiin|Who was the best of the Successors?]] {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 258} Ahmad said that no Successor gave more fatwas than al-Hasan and [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata']], Ata' being the mufti of Mecca and al-Hasan the mufti of Basra. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260} [[person/abd-allah-ibn-abi-dawud-al-sijistani|Ibn Abi Dawud]] said the two leading women Successors were [[person/hafsa-bint-sirin|Hafsa bint Sirin]] and Amra bint Abd al-Rahman, followed by [[person/umm-al-darda-al-sughra|Umm al-Darda' the Younger]]; and [[person/iyas-ibn-muawiya|Iyas ibn Mu'awiya]] said he had met no one he would rank above Hafsa — not al-Hasan, not Ibn Sirin. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 261} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 262}

## Misclassifications
The book warns that some were counted as Successors who never met a Companion, such as Ibrahim ibn Suwayd al-Nakha'i and Bukayr ibn Abi al-Samit, while some Successors were placed among the next generation because most of their narration came from it, such as [[person/abu-al-zinad|Abu al-Zinad]], who met Ibn Umar and Anas. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 262} Some Companions were counted as Successors, either in error, like al-Nu'man and Suwayd, the sons of Muqarrin, in al-Hakim, or because they were junior Companions who narrated mostly from other Companions, like Yusuf ibn Abd Allah ibn Salam and Mahmud ibn Labid in Muslim; and the reverse happened too, as when Muhammad ibn al-Rabi' al-Jizi listed Abd al-Rahman ibn Ghanm al-Ash'ari among the Companions who entered Egypt, though on the sounder view he was not one. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 263}

## The first and the last
[[person/siraj-al-din-al-bulqini|Al-Bulqini]] said the first Successor to die was Abu Zayd Ma'did ibn Yazid, killed in Khurasan — or Azerbaijan — in the year 30, and the last was Khalaf ibn Khalifa in 180. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 264} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 265} In Ma'rifat Ulum al-Hadith al-Hakim gave the Followers of the Successors a type of their own. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 265}
