## Overview
A branch of [[group/hawazin|Hawazin]], the people of Halima al-Sa'diyya, who nursed the Prophet. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.56}

## In Ibn Hisham's biography
Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr sent to the Prophet a man of theirs called [[person/dimam-ibn-thalaba|Dimam ibn Tha'laba]] as a delegate. See [[event/delegation-of-dimam-ibn-thalaba|the arrival of Dimam]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/219} When Dimam returned to them and spoke to them, the men and women of his settlement embraced Islam before that day was over. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/221} The tribal index of the 1936 edition gives other places for the branch in the accounts of Hunayn. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/342}

## In the birth section of The Sealed Nectar (pp. 55-57)
[[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] had the Prophet nursed by a woman of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr, [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima bint Abi Dhu'ayb]]. Her husband, [[person/al-harith-ibn-abd-al-uzza|al-Harith ibn Abd al-Uzza]], was of the same tribe, and his paternal uncle [[person/hamza|Hamza]] was also nursed among them. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55} {cite:zad-al-maad 1/19} Halima describes their land ([[place/bilad-banu-sad|the lands of Banu Sa'd]]) as among the most barren, and their women used to go out to Mecca seeking nurslings in the years of drought. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55-56} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/162-164} The Prophet remained with them until his fourth or fifth year. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.56} {cite:talqih-fuhum-ahl-al-athr p.7}
