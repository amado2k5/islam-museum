## Overview
Kinda is a Yemeni tribe from the branches of [[group/kahlan|Kahlan]]. The Sealed Nectar counts them as the fourth group of emigrants from Kahlan. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16} They settled in [[place/bahrayn|Bahrain]], were then forced to leave it and settled in [[place/hadramawt|Hadramawt]], where they met what they had met in Bahrain. Then they settled in Najd and formed a government of great standing, which soon perished and whose traces vanished. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.231-235}

## In the history of al-Hira
When Qubad ibn Firuz called on [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-ma-al-sama|al-Mundhir ibn Ma' al-Sama']], king of al-Hira, to adopt the Mazdakite doctrine and he refused, Qubad deposed him and put in his place [[person/al-harith-ibn-amr-al-kindi|al-Harith ibn Amr ibn Hujr al-Kindi]], who had accepted his call to this doctrine. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32} Later Khosrow Anushirvan restored al-Mundhir to his rule and sought al-Harith, who escaped to the lands of Kalb and remained among them until he died. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}

## In Ibn Hisham's biography (volume 4)
[[person/al-ashath-ibn-qays|Al-Ash'ath ibn Qays]] came with [[event/delegation-of-kinda|the delegation of Kinda]], eighty riders, to al-Zuhri. They wore robes of al-Hira trimmed with silk, and they embraced Islam. Al-Ash'ath traced his descent to [[person/akil-al-murar|Akil al-Murar]], and said to the Prophet: "And you are the son of Akil al-Murar." The Prophet said: "Rather, we are the sons of al-Nadr ibn Kinana." Ibn Ishaq says that "Kinda were kings". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/232} Ibn Hisham attributes Akil al-Murar to "Mu'awiya ibn Kindi, or it is said Kinda". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/233} [[person/farwa-ibn-musayk|Farwa ibn Musayk]] came to the Prophet, separating himself from the kings of Kinda and keeping away from them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/228}

## Not covered here
The Sealed Nectar does not detail the kings of Kinda in Najd or the story of Imru' al-Qays. The apostasy of Kinda with al-Ash'ath is covered in the entry on al-Ash'ath.

## For young visitors
Kinda is a tribe from Yemen that moved a great deal: from Bahrain to Hadramawt and then to Najd, where it set up a kingdom that did not last long.

## For Sprouts
Kinda, a Yemenite tribe of Kahlan's clans; they settled Bahrayn then Hadramawt then Najd and built a great government that soon perished — Ibn Ishaq says 'Kinda were kings'.

## For Explorers
Kinda, a Yemenite tribe of Kahlan's clans, the fourth of Kahlan's migrating sections in the Raheeq: they settled Bahrayn, were forced to leave, settled Hadramawt and suffered as they had there, then settled Najd and built a great government that soon perished leaving no trace. When Qubadh ibn Fayruz, king of Hira, called al-Mundhir ibn Ma' al-Sama' to the Mazdaki creed and he refused, he deposed him and installed al-Harith ibn Amr al-Kindi; then Kisra restored al-Mundhir and sought al-Harith, who escaped to the lands of Kalb and died among them. Al-Ash'ath ibn Qays came in Kinda's delegation — eighty riders in Hira cloaks edged with silk, per al-Zuhri — and they entered Islam; al-Ash'ath claimed descent from Akil al-Murar and the Prophet answered 'rather we are Banu al-Nadr ibn Kinana'; Ibn Ishaq says 'Kinda were kings'. And Farwa ibn Musayk came to the Prophet parting from Kinda's kings.

## For Scholars
Kinda, a Yemenite tribe of Kahlan's clans, the fourth of Kahlan's migrating sections in the Raheeq: they settled Bahrayn, were forced to leave, settled Hadramawt and suffered as they had in Bahrayn, then settled Najd and built a great government that soon perished leaving no trace. When Qubadh ibn Fayruz, king of Hira, called al-Mundhir ibn Ma' al-Sama' to the Mazdaki creed and he refused, he deposed him and installed al-Harith ibn Amr ibn Hajar al-Kindi after he answered the call; then Kisra Anushirwan restored al-Mundhir to his governorship and sought al-Harith, who escaped to the lands of Kalb and stayed among them until he died. Al-Ash'ath ibn Qays came in Kinda's delegation — eighty riders per al-Zuhri, in Hira cloaks edged with silk — and they entered Islam; al-Ash'ath claimed descent from Akil al-Murar saying 'and you are a son of Akil al-Murar', and the Prophet said 'rather we are Banu al-Nadr ibn Kinana'; Ibn Ishaq says 'Kinda were kings'. Ibn Hisham gives Akil al-Murar's line as 'Mu'awiya ibn Kindi, it is also said Kinda'. And Farwa ibn Musayk came to the Prophet parting from Kinda's kings and keeping his distance.
