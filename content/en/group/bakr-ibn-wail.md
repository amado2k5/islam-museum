## Overview
Bakr ibn Wa'il is a tribe of [[group/rabia-ibn-nizar|Rabi'a ibn Nizar]]. The Sealed Nectar counts from Rabi'a: Asad ibn Rabi'a, Anaza, Abd al-Qays, and the two sons of Wa'il, Bakr and [[group/banu-taghlib|Taghlib]] and [[group/banu-hanifa|Hanifa]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/14-15}

## Dwellings
When the sons of Adnan scattered, branches of Bakr ibn Wa'il migrated with Abd al-Qays, together with branches of Tamim, to [[place/bahrayn|Bahrain]]. [[group/banu-hanifa|Banu Hanifa ibn Sa'b ibn Ali ibn Bakr]] went to [[place/al-yamama|al-Yamama]] and settled at Hajr, its chief town. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} The rest of Bakr ibn Wa'il stayed along the length of the land from al-Yamama to Bahrain, to the shore of the sea at the Gulf of [[place/kazima|Kazima]], then the edges of the Iraqi plain, al-Ubulla and Hit. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/15-16} Taghlib settled in the Euphrates region of the Jazira, and some of its branches lived alongside Bakr. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22}

## The day of Dhu Qar
From Bakr ibn Wa'il came [[group/banu-shayban|Banu Shayban]], who were given refuge by al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir, who entrusted them with his family and wealth. When Kisra demanded them back, [[person/hani-ibn-masud|Hani ibn Mas'ud]] refused to hand them over, and so came [[event/battle-of-dhu-qar|the battle of Dhu Qar]], in which Banu Shayban were victorious. The author counts it as the first day on which the Arabs won against the Persians. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}

## Wars of Bakr and Taghlib
The author gives the wars of Bakr and Taghlib, along with those of the Aws and al-Khazraj, and of Abs and Dhubyan, as examples of how rivalry for honour often led to wars between tribes that share one father. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.45}

## For young visitors
Bakr ibn Wa'il is a large tribe that lived in the east of the Arabian Peninsula, up to the edges of Iraq. Among its branches were Banu Shayban, who defeated the Persians at the day of Dhu Qar.
