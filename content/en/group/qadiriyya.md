## Overview
The Qadiri order is among the oldest and most widespread Sufi orders of the Islamic world; traced to Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani (d. 561 AH/1166 CE), it expanded from Baghdad east and west {cite:trimingham-sufi-orders-1998}.

## In the sources
Academic study describes the Qadiriyya as the first broad organization of the Sufi path, extending from Iraq to India and North Africa {cite:trimingham-sufi-orders-1998}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam says the Qadiri order was founded after the death of Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani by his followers, in fourteenth-century Damascus, and that its legal orientation is Hanbali. It describes the order as spreading across the Arab world and Africa south of the Sahara, especially prominent in Egypt and Syria by the end of the fourteenth century and, by the nineteenth century, a presence stretching between West Africa and Southeast Asia. It was also prominent in the Malay peninsula, India and North Africa, and the Dictionary adds that leaders of the order took a significant part in the nineteenth-century resistance to European imperialism in Africa. Its account of a fourteenth-century founding in Damascus differs from the record above, which has the order expand from Baghdad; both are reported and neither is settled. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1900}
