## Summary
The Murji'a, as Ibn Badran describes them in al-Madkhal, claim that faith is speech without deed, that it neither increases nor decreases, and that people do not differ in it {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.96}. He reports from their view that whoever believes with his tongue and does no deed is truly a believer, and judges this false {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.96}. The fullest material on this page comes from [[work/ibana-al-kubra-ibn-batta|al-Ibana al-Kubra]] of [[person/ibn-batta-al-ukbari-ubayd-allah|Ibn Batta al-Ukbari]], who closed his Book of Faith with them; everything that follows describes them in the words of their opponents among the people of hadith. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 884} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 906}

## The chapter on the Murji'a in al-Ibana
Ibn Batta's chapter is titled "On the Murji'a, what has been transmitted about them, and the scholars' rejection of their evil doctrines". He opens it with a hadith from Abu Hurayra that every prophet's community had Murji'a and Qadariyya, and one from Anas on two groups who will not receive intercession, the Murji'a and the Qadariyya; the editor, Rida Mu'ti, judges the first weak, citing al-Albani. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 884} He then transmits reports from the Successors, among them Ibrahim al-Nakha'i's saying that their trial was more to be feared for the community than that of the Azariqa, and al-Zuhri's that no innovation more harmful to the people of Islam than irja' had been introduced. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 885} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 893}

## Their doctrine as Ibn Batta describes it
Ibn Batta sums up their doctrine as follows: faith is word without deed; God imposed the obligations without wanting His servants to perform them, and forbade the forbidden things while they remain believers if they commit them; knowledge of God is a faith that dispenses with obedience, so whoever knows God in his heart is a believer of complete faith, like the faith of Gabriel; and faith does not vary, increase or decrease, the obedient and the disobedient being equal in it. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 893} He reports from Yahya ibn Sulaym al-Ta'ifi that they say circling the House is not part of faith. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 899}

## Ibn Batta's judgement on them
Ibn Batta judges these doctrines unbelief and misguidance that take their holders out of the law of Islam, and warns against sitting with those who hold them. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 893} He argues that whoever calls knowledge faith must make Iblis, who knew God, equal in faith to Abraham and Moses, and must rank Abu Jahl, who knew in his heart that Muhammad was God's Messenger, with the people of Badr and the Pledge of Ridwan. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 893} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 896} He reports from al-Zuhri and al-Dahhak that the hadith "Whoever says there is no god but God enters Paradise" came before the obligations and penalties were revealed. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 896} He warns against anyone who says "I am a believer in God's sight", "I am a believer of complete faith" or "my faith is like the faith of Gabriel and Michael", and transmits from al-Awza'i that all three are innovations. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 899} For him this is bound up with [[question/may-one-say-i-am-a-believer-god-willing|the question of saying "God willing" about one's faith]] and with [[question/does-faith-increase-and-decrease|whether faith increases and decreases]].

## The return of Awn ibn Abd Allah
Ibn Batta ends the chapter with the report of Awn ibn Abd Allah, whom he describes as among the most cultivated and learned men of Medina, who had been a Murji'i and turned away from it, and quotes verses of his on leaving the Murji'a's doctrine. He then says that this is the end of the refutation of the Murji'a and of the seventh part of the Usul. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 906}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Hanbali): Ibn Batta in al-Ibana al-Kubra.** Faith is word and deed and increases and decreases; irja' is among the most harmful innovations; "faith is word without deed" and "knowledge is faith" are misguidance in his view. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 893}

**Sunni (Hanbali): Ibn Badran in al-Madkhal.** He describes their view as faith being word without deed. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.96}
