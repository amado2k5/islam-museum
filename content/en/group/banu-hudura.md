## Overview
A great people with great force who overcame much of the land, according to al-Mas'udi. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 464-465}

## Their lineage and dwellings
People disagree about their lineage: some joined them to the perished Arabs, and others saw them as descendants of Yafith. Their dwellings are also disputed: some placed them in the land of al-Samawa between Iraq and Syria, and others in the lands of the district of Qinnasrin, from Tall Masih to Khunasira. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 464-465}

## Their story in al-Mas'udi
Al-Mas'udi reports that God sent them a prophet, Shu'ayb ibn Mahdam, and that he was not the Shu'ayb of Midian, and that there were centuries between them. They killed him. He then says that God revealed to Barkhiya ibn Akhbiya of the tribe of Judah that he should order Nebuchadnezzar to invade the Arabs. He marched against them, their armies scattered, and the sword took them. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 464-465}

This account is transmitted by al-Mas'udi alone among the sources on this page, and needs a second source.
