## Summary
Lakhm is a branch of [[group/kahlan|Kahlan]], of the Qahtanite lineage {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16}. Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum pairs it with Judham in the second group of Kahlan's emigrants, and among the Lakhmis was Nasr ibn Rabi'a, father of the Manadhira kings at al-Hira {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}. From them came the kings of the [[polity/lakhmid-kingdom|kingdom of al-Hira]], who served the Persians until 632 CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27}.

## Their first king
After the death of [[person/jadhima-al-abrash|Jadhima al-Abrash]], around 268 CE, the Persians made [[person/amr-ibn-adi|Amr ibn Adi ibn Nasr al-Lakhmi]], the first of the Lakhmid kings, governor of al-Hira in the reign of Shapur ibn Ardashir. From then on, kings of the Lakhmis succeeded one another over [[place/al-hira|al-Hira]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}.

## Al-Mundhir and al-Nu'man
In the reign of Qubadh ibn Firuz, [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-ma-al-sama|al-Mundhir ibn Ma al-Sama]] refused to adopt the Mazdakite doctrine, so Qubadh deposed him and appointed al-Harith ibn Amr al-Kindi in his place. Then Khosrow Anushirvan restored him, and the kingship stayed in his line until [[person/al-numan-ibn-al-mundhir|al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir]], whom Khosrow imprisoned until he died {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}. After Iyas ibn Qabisa, Khosrow appointed a Persian governor. In 632 CE the kingship returned to the house of Lakhm, and [[person/al-mundhir-al-marur|al-Mundhir, known as al-Maghrur,]] was appointed from among them. His rule did not exceed eight months before Khalid ibn al-Walid arrived against him with the armies of the Muslims {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}.

## Religion
The author notes that some of the kings of al-Hira embraced Christianity {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}.

## For young visitors
Lakhm was a tribe that ruled al-Hira for the Persians. Its last king fell in 632 CE before the armies of Islam.
