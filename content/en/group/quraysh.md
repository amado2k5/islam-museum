## Overview
Quraysh, the tribe of Mecca in [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]]'s mission-time — keepers of the Ka'ba and owners of its trade — whose leadership was the call's fiercest enemy in its early years, then became, after the conquest, the new state's twin.

## History
Quraysh trace themselves to al-Nadr ibn Kinana and branch into clans — Hashim, Umayya, Makhzum, Zuhra and others — sharing the honour of the House's charge and the pilgrims' watering {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. The Sira has preserved the picture of the class that monopolised Meccan decision: its chiefs — like Abu Jahl and Abu Lahab — opposed the call of their kinsman Muhammad, and boycotted [[group/banu-hashim|Banu Hashim]] in the shi'b for three years {cite:watt-1953-mecca}.

After the battles of [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] and the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Trench]] and the knot of [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|al-Hudaybiyya]], Mecca was entered in 630 CE with hardly a fight, and its leaders became Muslim — from [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] to [[person/ikrima-ibn-abi-jahl|Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl]] — and the relation changed: Quraysh became the pillar of army and administration in the Rashidun and Umayyad eras {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
"The caliphate belongs to Quraysh" — a saying the tradition transmits from the [[event/saqifa|Saqifa]] context — consecrated the tribe's centrality in Islamic political imagination; historians outside the tradition see in it a later legitimisation of a social superiority already existing {cite:watt-1956-medina}.
