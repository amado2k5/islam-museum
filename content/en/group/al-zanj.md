## Summary
The Zanj in the books of geography and history are the people of the East African coasts and Zanjibar: their slave-and-gold trade from the earliest reports, and their rising against the Abbasids — 'the Revolt of the Zanj' — the greatest affair al-Tabari and Ibn al-Athir recount.

## In the texts
The geographers — al-Mas'udi, al-Jahiz and Ibn Khurradadhbih — describe their land, tongue and trade; the historians recount their rising which stood at Basra and fell after fifteen years, and the story of their chosen city {cite:muruj-al-dhahab} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}.

## In the Sira and history
The Zanj in Arab usage were the people of Barbara, Zanj and Sofala, brought in the slave trade even before Islam; then their rising against the Abbasid caliphate was the greatest turmoil of its age, and afterwards they settled in the lands of Islam and entered it {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}.

## For younger visitors
The Zanj are East Africa's people; their famous rising against the Abbasids is told at length by the historians.

## Notes for researchers
'Zanj' for the early writers is a geographic, not an exact ethnic name — it covered the East African coast and those brought from it in the slave trade — so read the geographies before applying modern categories.
