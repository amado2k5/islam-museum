## Summary
Kahlan is one of the two great tribes of [[group/qahtan|Qahtan]], alongside [[group/himyar|Himyar]]. Its best-known clans are [[group/banu-hamdan|Hamdan]], Anmar, [[group/tayy|Tayy']], [[group/madhhij|Madhhij]], [[group/kinda|Kinda]], [[group/banu-lakhm|Lakhm]], [[group/judham|Judham]], [[group/al-azd|Azd]], the Aws and the Khazraj, and the sons of Jafna, kings of Syria {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/11-13}.

## Their departure from Yemen
Kahlan's clans left Yemen and spread through the peninsula. Most of them emigrated shortly before [[event/flood-of-al-aram|the Flood of al-Arim]], when their trade failed {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16}. The author holds that the rivalry between the clans of Kahlan and the clans of Himyar led to Kahlan's departure, as shown by Himyar's remaining in Yemen while Kahlan left {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}.

## The four groups of emigrants
The author divides the emigrating clans of Kahlan into four groups {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.231-235}:
- [[group/al-azd|Azd]]: they emigrated on the opinion of their chief [[person/imran-ibn-amr|Imran ibn Amr Muzayqiya']], and scattered after travelling. Some went to the Hijaz and Medina (Tha'laba and his sons, the Aws and the Khazraj), some to Mecca (Harith ibn Amr, who is Khuza'a), some to Oman (Imran), some to Tihama (Azd Shanu'a), and some to Syria (Jafna, father of the Ghassanids) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}.
- Lakhm and Judham: among the Lakhmis was Nasr ibn Rabi'a, father of the Manadhira kings of al-Hira {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}.
- Banu Tayy': after the Azd they went north and settled the two mountains [[place/aja-and-salma|Aja and Salma]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}.
- Kinda: they settled Bahrain, then Hadramawt, then Najd, and there they formed a government of great standing that soon perished {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}.

He adds that a tribe of Himyar, though its descent from him is disputed, namely [[group/qudaa|Qudaa]], left Yemen and settled the desert of al-Samawa at the edge of Iraq {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}. He notes the wide disagreement among the sources on the dates and causes of these migrations {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}.

## For young visitors
Kahlan is a great tribe of Yemen. Its sons went out as emigrants, and from them came many tribes in Medina, Mecca and Syria.
