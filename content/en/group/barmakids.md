## Overview
The Barmakids were an old Persian family who traced their line to their ancestor Barmak, a title given to the keeper of the Nawbahar temple in the city of Balkh. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.96-100}

## At the start of the Abbasid state
Among them came out Khalid ibn Barmak. Al-Saffah entrusted him with the land-tax office and the army office, and he took the office of executive vizier after the death of Abu Salama al-Khallal. He served as adviser to al-Mansur, and kept his standing under al-Mahdi. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.96-100}

## Yahya, al-Fadl and Ja'far
His son Yahya came next. Harun al-Rashid appointed him vizier of delegation and gave him absolute powers, so that all the registers were in his hand. Yahya, with his two sons al-Fadl and Ja'far, ran the caliphate for seventeen years (170-187 AH), which was their golden age. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.96-100}

## The end
Al-Rashid had Ja'far killed on the first night of Safar in the year 187 AH, imprisoned Yahya and the rest of his sons, and confiscated their property. Yahya died in the year 190 AH and al-Fadl in 193 AH. This account is linked to [[event/killing-of-jafar-and-the-barmakids|the killing of Ja'far and the fall of the Barmakids]]. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.96-100}

## The historian's interpretation
Muhammad Suhayl Taqqush holds that the idea of avenging the killing of Abu Muslim took a new course: taking control of the caliphate from within, after attempts to harm it from outside had failed. He mentions that the Barmakids showed political leanings towards the Talibids, and their history was tied to their sympathy with Yahya ibn Abd Allah al-Alawi and Ahmad ibn Isa ibn Zayd. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.96-100} {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.100}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "A Story and an Idea"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the seventeenth episode of his programme [[work/kissa-wa-fikra-program|"A Story and an Idea"]] to the Barmakids and [[person/harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]], under the idea of "the corrupt inner circle": if a ruler's inner circle is sound all is sound, and if it is corrupt all is corrupt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:01:30} What follows is his account.

The ancestor Barmak was a custodian of a fire temple, and after the conquest of Persia the family became Muslim; [[person/khalid-ibn-barmak|Khalid]] was a missionary for the Abbasid cause and held office under al-Saffah and al-Mansur, and [[person/yahya-ibn-khalid-al-barmaki|Yahya ibn Khalid]], known for his skill in administration, raised Harun, who called him "my father", while Yahya's wife nursed Harun with her own son al-Fadl. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:03:00} On becoming caliph Harun made Yahya his chief minister and handed him his seal ring; Yahya ruled with his sons [[person/al-fadl-ibn-yahya-al-barmaki|al-Fadl]], [[person/jafar-ibn-yahya-al-barmaki|Ja'far]], Musa and Muhammad, who lived with Harun in the Palace of Eternity and went in to him without leave. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:05:30} Al-Fadl oversaw the upbringing of al-Amin and governed the East; Ja'far oversaw that of al-Ma'mun, governed the West from Baghdad and handled complaints against officials, the post and the coinage, and historians say his name appeared on some coins. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:08:30} Al-Suwaidan cites reports of lavish gifts from state money, such as three million dirhams given by al-Fadl to poets on his return from Daylam. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:15:00}

He places the first responsibility on Harun, who trusted them too much. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:09:30} He sets out several accounts of what turned Harun against them: the historian Mustafa Jawad's view, which he reports, linking it to the fate of [[person/musa-ibn-jafar-al-kazim|Musa al-Kazim]]; Ja'far's beheading of an Alid prisoner whom Harun had ordered held in comfort, the head sent to Harun on a covered tray on the day of Nawruz; harsh tax collection; the rivalry over the succession, in which [[person/zubayda-bint-jafar|Zubayda]] backed her son al-Amin and the Barmakids backed al-Ma'mun; and al-Fadl's raising of a private army of fifty thousand in Khurasan. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:17:30}

He says the Barmakids were ascendant for sixteen years. In 186 AH Harun went on Hajj with Yahya and his sons, and at the Ka'ba Yahya prayed that any punishment for his sins come in this world rather than the next. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:20:00} On the way back, at al-Anbar, Harun sent Masrur, head of his guard; Ja'far was beheaded and his body hung on the bridges of Baghdad. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:22:00} All the Barmakids were seized except Muhammad ibn Yahya, found free of abuses, and their property was confiscated; Yahya chose prison with his sons over house arrest at al-Raqqa, and they died in prison. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:24:00}

The true cause, he says, puzzled [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]], al-Ya'qubi and [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]], and historians agree that the fall fed anti-Arab feeling, which produced the picture of Harun as dissolute found in the *Aghani*, while reliable histories show him going on Hajj one year and campaigning the next. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:24:30} He rejects the claims that the Barmakids were secret Magians and the story of a marriage between al-Abbasa and Ja'far, calling it baseless on the authority of al-Tabari and [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]], and warns against taking history from the *Thousand and One Nights* or historical novels. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:26:00} His lesson: a rightly guided ruler chooses capable and clean men and carries the burden of those he appoints. He closes with Yahya in prison, asked by his son how they came to this, answering that it was the prayer of a wronged man that travelled by night while they were heedless. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep17 00:28:30}
