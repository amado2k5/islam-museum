## Summary
The Sabians in the Qur'an are a community named with the believers, the Jews, the Christians and the Magians in three verses — al-Baqara, al-Ma'ida and al-Hajj. The commentators differ over them: said to be a party of the People of the Book, or star-worshippers, or the people of Harran.

## In the texts
The three verses — «the believers, the Jews, the Christians and the Sabians, whoever believes in God and the Last Day» in al-Baqara and its like in al-Ma'ida, then «God will judge between them on the Day of Rising» in al-Hajj — are all that the revelation states, and on them the commentators built and differed {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01}.

## In the Sira and history
The chroniclers record the Sabians of Harran in the Jazira, who kept their creed into Islamic times — Thabit ibn Qurra the scientist among them — and some say the Sabians were a people between the Magians and the Jews, in neither's religion {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun}.

## For younger visitors
The Sabians are a people the Qur'an names three times with the Jews, Christians and Magians; scholars differ over who they were — people of the Book, or the star-worshippers of Harran.

## Notes for researchers
The dispute over identifying the Sabians is old and gathered in al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir; go back to the three verses and the commentators' reports — never attribute to the Qur'an more than it states.
