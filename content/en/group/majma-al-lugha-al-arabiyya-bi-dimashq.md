## Summary
The Arab Academy of Damascus (Majma' al-Lugha al-'Arabiyya bi-Dimashq) is, according to the research summary supplied to the museum, the oldest modern Arabic language academy. It began in 1918-1919 under [[person/faisal-i-of-iraq|Faisal I]] as ruler of Syria, and [[person/muhammad-kurd-ali|Muhammad Kurd Ali]] was its first president, from 1919 to 1953. {cite:wikipedia-arab-academy-of-damascus-en research summary} {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021 research summary} {cite:wikipedia-arab-academy-of-damascus-ar research summary} This page reports what that summary says; the museum did not open the pages it rests on, with the founder's approval of 21 November 2026.

## Founding and founders
The summary says the Academy arose in 1918 and 1919 in the reign of Faisal I, and gives no particular day for the formal founding. {cite:wikipedia-arab-academy-of-damascus-en research summary} {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021 research summary} It names Muhammad Kurd Ali as founding president from 1919 to 1953, which is until his death as his own record states. With him it lists as founding scholars Sa'id ibn Ali al-Karmi, Mitri Qandalaft and Abd al-Qadir al-Mubarak; the museum has no records of these three yet, so only their names, as the summary gives them, are stated here. {cite:wikipedia-arab-academy-of-damascus-ar research summary} The summary says the Academy was modelled on the Académie française. {cite:encyclopedia-com-arab-academy-damascus research summary}

## Role and works
The summary says the Academy led the Arabisation movement after Ottoman rule, that is, the work of making Arabic the language of administration, teaching and science in place of the former state language. {cite:encyclopedia-com-arab-academy-damascus research summary} It researched Arab culture and history and formal Arabic (al-fusha), issued a scholarly journal from 1921 and published critical editions of books. {cite:encyclopedia-com-arab-academy-damascus research summary} Among the dictionaries it commissioned was Ahmad Rida's Matn al-Lugha, the [[work/mujam-matn-al-lugha|Mu'jam Matn al-Lugha]] by [[person/ahmad-rida|Ahmad Rida]]. {cite:encyclopedia-com-arab-academy-damascus research summary} The summary does not give the journal's name or the number of its volumes.

## Arabisation and terminology
The summary puts Arabisation first among the Academy's tasks after the Ottoman period, the work of finding Arabic words for modern meanings. {cite:encyclopedia-com-arab-academy-damascus research summary} With the academies of Cairo and Baghdad it became one of the founders of the [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|Union of Arab Language Academies]] in 1971, a body that coordinates the academies' work on scientific terminology. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} Its sister in Egypt is the [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-qahira|Academy of the Arabic Language in Cairo]], and in Iraq a third is the [[group/al-majma-al-ilmi-al-iraqi|Iraqi Academy of Sciences]]. For the wider picture see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab world]] and [[concept/tarikh-al-lugha-al-arabiyya|the history of Arabic]].

## What this page does not reach
The source is a research summary supplied to the museum that rests on English and Arabic Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com; the museum did not open those pages, and the founder approved using the summary without re-verification on 21 November 2026. So the exact day of the formal founding, the journal's name, which critical editions were published, what became of the Academy after Kurd Ali's death and its other members are not known here. All of the above is attributed to the summary.
