## Summary
The Union of Arab Language Academies (Ittihad al-Majami' al-Lughawiyya al-'Ilmiyya al-'Arabiyya) is a body that brings together the Arabic language academies and coordinates their work; it was founded on 13 March 1971 after a recommendation of an Arab League conference in 1956, according to the research summary supplied to the museum. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} {cite:unionacademies-org research summary} Its seat is 6th of October City in Egypt and it works under the umbrella of the Arab League. This page reports what that summary says; the museum did not open the pages it rests on, with the founder's approval of 21 November 2026.

## Founding and founders
The summary says the academies of Cairo, Baghdad and Damascus founded the Union together, so it began as a union of the three oldest academies. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} Its first president was [[person/taha-hussein|Taha Hussein]], Ibrahim Madkour was elected secretary-general, and [[person/ahmad-abd-al-sattar-al-jawari|Ahmad Abd al-Sattar al-Jawari]] and Adnan al-Khatib were elected assistant secretaries-general. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} The museum has no record yet of Ibrahim Madkour or Adnan al-Khatib, so only what the summary says of them is given. The summary ties the founding to the recommendation of an Arab League conference in 1956, fifteen years before, and does not explain the interval.

## Role
The summary describes the Union as coordinating the member academies across the Arab world and standardising scientific and technical terms through periodic symposia. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} It is the body that spearheaded the Historical Dictionary of the Arabic Language, a dictionary that shows the history of words. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} The summary gives neither the number of members nor their names beyond the founding academies, nor the dates of the symposia, nor the state of the historical project, all of which this page does not reach. {cite:unionacademies-org research summary}

## Arabisation and terminology
Standardising terms is the heart of the Union's work in the summary, the collective side of Arabisation: each academy coins its own terms, and there remains the need for the countries to agree on one word for one concept. {cite:wikipedia-union-of-arab-language-academies-ar research summary} Its founding members are the [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-qahira|Cairo academy]], the [[group/al-majma-al-ilmi-al-iraqi|Iraqi Academy of Sciences]] and the [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-dimashq|Damascus academy]]; among other academies is the [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-al-urduni|Jordan Academy of Arabic]], and the summary does not say when it joined. The Historical Dictionary bears on [[concept/tarikh-al-lugha-al-arabiyya|the history of Arabic]] and sits in the wider setting of [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab world]].

## What this page does not reach
The source is a research summary supplied to the museum that rests on Arabic Wikipedia and the Union's official website; the museum did not open those pages, and the founder approved using the summary without re-verification on 21 November 2026. So what exactly happened at the 1956 conference, the list of members today, the fate of the Historical Dictionary and the terminology recommendations published are not known here. All of the above is attributed to the summary.
