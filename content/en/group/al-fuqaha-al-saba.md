## Summary
[[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] states in al-Taqrib that among the greatest [[group/tabiun|Successors]] are the Seven Jurists, and [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] specifies in [[work/tadrib-al-rawi|Tadrib al-Rawi]] that they were of [[place/medina|Medina]]. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 255}

## The usual list
As most scholars of the Hijaz count them, they are [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]], [[person/al-qasim-ibn-muhammad-ibn-abi-bakr|al-Qasim ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq]], [[person/urwa-ibn-al-zubayr|Urwa ibn al-Zubayr]], [[person/kharija-ibn-zayd-ibn-thabit|Kharija ibn Zayd ibn Thabit]], [[person/abu-salama-ibn-abd-al-rahman|Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], [[person/ubaydallah-ibn-abdallah-ibn-utba|Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Utba ibn Mas'ud]] and [[person/sulayman-ibn-yasar|Sulayman ibn Yasar al-Hilali, Abu Ayyub]]. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 256}

## The disputed seventh
[[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak]] put [[person/salim-ibn-abd-allah-ibn-umar|Salim ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar]] in place of Abu Salama, while [[person/abu-al-zinad|Abu al-Zinad]] put [[person/abu-bakr-ibn-abd-al-rahman-ibn-al-harith|Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Rahman]] in place of both Salim and Abu Salama. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 256} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 257} [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ali ibn al-Madini]] counted twelve: Ibn al-Musayyab, Abu Salama, al-Qasim, Kharija and his brother Isma'il, Salim, [[person/hamza-ibn-abd-allah-ibn-umar|Hamza]], [[person/zayd-ibn-abd-allah-ibn-umar|Zayd]], Ubayd Allah and Bilal, the sons of Abd Allah ibn Umar, [[person/aban-ibn-uthman|Aban ibn Uthman]] and [[person/qabisa-ibn-dhuayb|Qabisa ibn Dhu'ayb]]. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 257}

## Their standing
A note printed on the page quotes [[person/badr-al-din-al-zarkashi|al-Zarkashi]]'s al-Bahr that, for those who hold this view, when the Seven Jurists agreed on a question a consensus was formed by them and no one else could depart from it, and [[person/ibn-khallikan|Ibn Khallikan]]'s history that they lived in a single age and that from them knowledge and fatwas spread through the world. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 255} The note gives two verses listing their names; the editor Muhammad Awwama concludes that the verses are by the hafiz Ibn al-Mufaddal al-Maqdisi (544–611 AH) and were ascribed to his student Ibn al-Abyad al-Halabi because he used to quote them. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 255}
