## Summary
Thaqif is the tribe of [[place/taif|Ta'if]], an ally of [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]] in the sacred land. The Islamic state besieged it until it surrendered in 630 CE on special terms. It was the last of the great tribes to enter Islam.

## History
Ta'if's mistreatment of the Prophet there was known before Yathrib's: he was stoned by slaves and fools. Later, Thaqif fought at Hunayn with its ally Hawazin. During the siege of Ta'if, they were asked to drop al-Lat and break its sanctuary {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. That same year saw a famous delegation in which Thaqif bargained over a prayer without prostration, which was refused, and over keeping al-Lat for three years. They were answered with refusal, and surrendered on special terms {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

In the Rashidun era, Thaqafis of Ta'if became officials and conquest leaders, among them al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba and the family of al-Hajjaj {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## Legacy
For historians, Thaqif is the model of "negotiated Islam". It became Muslim under siege, on conditions. This is evidence that the late joining of some tribes was not always pure defeat, but a bargain that ended in shared interest {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## In the introduction to al-Raheeq al-Makhtum
In the distribution of the Adnanite tribes, al-Raheeq al-Makhtum says that Thaqif lived in [[place/taif|Ta'if]], and Hawazin to the east of Mecca, around Awtas {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/15-16}. On the idols, it says the Arabs took [[object/al-lat|al-Lat]] in Ta'if after [[object/manat|Manat]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35}.

## For Sprouts
Thaqif were the tribe of Ta'if and allies of Quraysh in the Sacred Town. Ta'if was remembered for its insult to the Prophet before the Hijra; the Muslims then besieged it until it surrendered in 630 CE on its own terms — the last great tribe to enter Islam.

## For Scholars
Thaqif were the tribe of Ta'if, allies of Quraysh in the Sacred Town — the last great tribe to enter Islam. Ta'if was remembered for the Prophet's mistreatment there before Yathrib — stoning by slaves and fools — then they fought at Hunayn beside Hawazin, and in the siege of Ta'if they were asked to drop al-Lat and break her sanctuary per Ibn Ishaq. The same year saw their famous embassy: Thaqif negotiated over the prayer 'no prostration' and over keeping al-Lat three years — refused, they surrendered on special terms per Ibn Sa'd. Under the Rashidun their men became viziers and conquest commanders — al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba and the house of al-Hajjaj. Historians read them as the model of 'negotiated Islam': proof that the late joiners did not always enter by sheer conquest but by a bargain ending in shared benefit. In the Adnanite distribution al-Raheeq places Thaqif in Ta'if, and the Arabs' al-Lat there after Manat.
