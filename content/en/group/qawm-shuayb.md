## Summary
The people of Shu'ayb in the Qur'an are the dwellers of Madyan and the Companions of the Ayka: Shu'ayb was sent to them forbidding short measure and corruption in the land; they denied him, so the earthquake and the torment of the Day of Shadow took them.

## In the texts
God tells them in al-A'raf, Hud, al-Shu'ara', al-Hijr, al-'Ankabut, Qaf and al-Dhariyat: their shortchanging of measure and weight, their highway robbery, their saying «Shu'ayb, does your prayer command you that we leave what our fathers worshipped?», and the torment of the Day of Shadow {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01}.

## In the Sira and history
For the commentators Madyan is a town on the Sham side near the sea towards Tabuk, where Musa fled from Egypt and saw Shu'ayb's daughters and watered for them; the Ayka is a tangle of trees — some say a grove — and the sounder view makes its people and Madyan one nation {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## For younger visitors
Shu'ayb's people cheated the scales; he called them to fairness, they denied him, and the earthquake destroyed them.

## Notes for researchers
Some commentators make the Companions of the Ayka other than the people of Madyan, though Shu'ayb was sent to both — the verses pair them here, so follow the Qur'an first then their reports.
