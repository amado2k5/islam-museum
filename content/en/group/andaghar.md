## Summary
The Andaghar were a people from the region of Kirman bordering [[place/sijistan|Sijistan]], counted among the captives the Persians had settled from the land of [[place/sind|Sind]] in their armies. They embraced Islam in the wake of the Asawira, and [[person/abu-musa-al-ashari|Abu Musa al-Ash'ari]] settled them in [[place/basra|Basra]] together with the Siyasija and the Zutt. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 522}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri relates that the Siyasija, the Zutt and the Andaghar had served in the Persian host as levies drawn from Sind and from earlier captives; when they heard what had become of the Asawira they accepted Islam and came to Abu Musa, who settled them in Basra — a body of men so equipped with arrows that not one of them missed his shot {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 522}. Their homeland he places on the side of Kirman adjoining Sijistan {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 524}. Having joined the Asawira, they were coveted by the Banu Tamim, so that the Asawira came to lodge with the Banu Sa'd and the Zutt and Siyasija with the Banu Hanzala, fighting alongside them against the polytheists {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 520}. In the affairs of Kirman, Abu Musa sent al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad who conquered the district of al-Shirajan and made peace with the people of Bamm and the Andaghar; when they apostatised and broke the pact, Mujashi' ibn Mas'ud took the town and conquered Jiruft by force {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 551}.
