## Overview
The Mudéjares were the Muslims of al-Andalus who stayed under Christian rule after the fall of the peninsula's Muslim cities in the thirteenth to fifteenth centuries {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari}.

## In the sources
Al-Maqqari's Nafh al-Tib mentions the renegades and the Mudéjares in its reports of the final years of al-Andalus and the fall of its cities {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari}.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives episodes 8 and 9 of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]] to the Muslims of al-Andalus who remained under Spanish rule after [[event/fall-of-granada-897|the surrender of Granada]], and calls those of them forced into Christianity the Moriscos {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:02:00}.

### Emigration and forced conversion
Al-Suwaidan relates that after Abu Abdallah left, the Christians did not keep their pledges but humiliated the Muslims and confiscated their property {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:02:30}. Nine years after the fall of Granada, in 1501 CE, he says, Ferdinand and Isabella issued an order to the effect that they had been sent to rid al-Andalus of those they called unbelievers, meaning the Muslims; the presence of Muslims was banned and offenders were punished with death {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:03:30}. He says 450,000 Muslims chose to emigrate from al-Andalus to Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and other lands {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:04:00}. A decree then ordered the conversion of those who stayed: some truly converted, others outwardly took Christianity while hiding their faith {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:04:00}. The converts, he says, were called "Moriscos" in contempt and belittlement; a Morisco was never a first-class Christian in their eyes {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:04:30}.

### Step-by-step restriction
Al-Suwaidan relates that the pressure came gradually: at first they were allowed to pray in mosques, then this was stopped; they still spoke Arabic and wore Arab dress, and every so often a new law tightened things in some direction {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:06:30}. He describes a harsh law, announced on 1 January, aimed at what remained of the Andalusian Muslims' distinctive culture: it banned the use of Arabic in private and in public, required them to master Castilian within three years, after which anyone speaking Arabic would be brought before the Inquisition, with its prisons, torture and executions {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:08:00}. The decree banned ritual ablution, he relates; people replaced it with bathing, so a further decree barred them from public baths {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:08:30}. It required marriages, births and burials to follow Catholic rites, banned circumcision outright, required the dead to be buried in closed coffins, a Christian Castilian midwife to attend births, and doors to be kept open {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:10:00}. It banned Arab dress in every form, standing long facing the qibla for fear of secret prayer, polygamy, and the use of henna {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:10:30}. Decisions were also issued, he says, banning Arabic names {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:11:00}. He notes that the Jews of al-Andalus were also subjected to the Inquisition, and that he has read in their own books that they never knew such honour and tolerance as under Muslim rule in al-Andalus {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:09:30}.

### Negotiation and petitions
Al-Suwaidan relates that the Andalusians first tried to soften their lot by negotiation, taking their protest to the president Deza and asking him to repeal the law or delay it; he received their delegation with arrogance and asked for the law to be enforced strictly {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:11:30}. He says the governor of Granada sent the king a petition warning that the Moriscos' despair could lead to revolt and to intervention by the Ottomans, who then ruled Algiers; neither the petition nor the appeals had any effect, and the Muslims despaired {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:12:30}.

### Between negotiation and revolt
Al-Suwaidan relates that under this pressure the Muslims split in two: one side sought a remedy through negotiation, the other through revolt and armed resistance, and the latter, finding no help, fled to the mountains {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:14:30}. Among the fruits of negotiation he mentions a decision allowing a death sentence for leaving Christianity to be commuted to a heavy fine, which he sees as one concession after another {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:15:00}. Matters came to a head, he says, on 1 January 1568 CE with a decree obliging the Muslims to hand their children to the church authorities to be taught Christianity and Castilian, which drove their leaders in Granada to prepare for revolt {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:15:30}.

### Preparing the revolt
Al-Suwaidan says the Muslim leaders met in secret at the end of 1567 and the beginning of 1568 CE and agreed on two conditions: that at least all the Muslims of the kingdom of Granada take part, and that they obtain help in arms, men and money from Muslims outside the country {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:17:00}. They wrote, he relates, to [[polity/ottoman-empire|the Ottomans]] in [[place/algiers|Algiers]], whose sultan was then [[person/selim-ii-uthmani|Selim II]], and to [[polity/saadi-dynasty|the Saadians]] in Morocco {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:19:00}. Al-Suwaidan regrets the poor relations between the Saadians and the Ottomans, even in the face of a common danger {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:04:00}. Both answers came with promises, he relates: the sultan of Morocco promised help once the revolt was declared, the governor of Algiers promised to land troops on the Andalusian coast, the Ottomans sent money and weapons back with the envoys, and volunteers set out from Algeria and Morocco {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:06:30}.

### The secret organisation
Al-Suwaidan relates that the organisers contacted the towns and villages of the kingdom of Granada and of the kingdom of [[place/valencia|Valencia]] in strict secrecy, using as cover a charity licensed to raise funds for a hospital for the poor, and under its name sent a delegation of three of their leaders, with a licence from the president Deza {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:08:00}. The delegation, he says, estimated that 45,000 men aged between twenty and forty-five could join the revolt, and found every district of the old kingdom of Granada in favour, while the Muslims of [[place/murcia|Murcia]] and Valencia refused to take part, doubting it could succeed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:08:30}. Rumours spread, he relates, but neither Deza nor the army commander believed them, and the organisers sent a man to deny them and to offer hostages as a sign of good faith {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:09:30}. A traitor, he says, told Deza of the revolt ten days before its date; Deza arrested a number of Muslim notables and cancelled arms permits, and the organisers had to postpone it; al-Suwaidan counts treachery among the things that make revolts fail {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:13:30}. He relates that they decided the revolt needed a political symbol and pledged allegiance to a young man of twenty-two descended from the Umayyad caliphs of Córdoba, who took the name Muhammad ibn Umayya, and set 1 January 1569 CE as the new date; what followed belongs to [[event/alpujarras-rebellion-1568|the Alpujarras revolt]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep09 00:16:30}.

### The comparison with Palestine
Al-Suwaidan repeatedly compares the Moriscos' condition with that of the Muslims in Palestine, holding that the existence of resistance is part of the steadfastness the community needs, and that resistance inside an occupied country cannot win without outside help {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:17:00}. This is his view in his programme and is given here as his {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:07:00}.
