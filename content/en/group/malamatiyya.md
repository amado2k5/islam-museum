## Summary
The "People of Blame" or Malamatiyya were a Sufi group which, the translator of [[work/kashf-al-mahjub|Kashf al-Mahjub]] says, appeared in Nishapur in the second half of the third century AH; al-Hujwiri calls them the "Qassariyya" or "Hamduniyya" after [[person/hamdun-al-qassar|Hamdun al-Qassar]] (d. 271 AH). {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 262} Al-Qushayri says in his notice of Hamdun that "the way of the Malamatiyya spread from him in Nishapur". {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 152} What follows presents a Sufi group and the words of its members and critics without choosing between them.

## What blame meant for them
The translator explains that blame means the Malamati blaming himself and people blaming him: he sees no share for his self and does not trust it, and is anxious to hide his dealings with his Lord, so much that they deliberately did what drew people's displeasure for fear that pride would creep in; she quotes Abu Hafs al-Haddad describing them as showing people the bad in what they did and hiding their virtues. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 262} She notes that the Malamatiyya wrote no books, and that al-Sulami collected their sayings in an epistle named after them. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 262}

## The critical position
The translator notes that the Malamatiyya were criticised by [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] in Talbis Iblis, who described them as having thrown away their standing with people. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 262}

## The Sufi position: al-Hujwiri
[[person/al-hujwiri|Al-Hujwiri]] opens the chapter by saying that some masters of the path took the way of blame, and that blame has a great effect on the purity of love; God, he holds, set people upon His friends to blame them and so guarded them from self-admiration, since no affliction on this path is harder than becoming pleased with oneself. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 262} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 263} He then divides blame into three kinds: {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 264}
- **The blame of right conduct:** a man does his work well and keeps the religion, and people blame him while he is indifferent to them.
- **Deliberate blame:** a man gains standing among people and takes on the way of blame "in something that does no harm under the law" so that they turn away from him and his heart is free for God.
- **The blame of abandonment:** unbelief and error take hold of a man until he professes abandoning the law and claims that what he does is blame; this al-Hujwiri condemns.

As an example of deliberate blame he relates that 'Uthman ibn 'Affan, while caliph, carried a bundle of firewood on his head to test himself, and tells of Abu Yazid eating a loaf in the market in Ramadan when people came out to greet him, so that they left him; these are stories al-Hujwiri relates, not affirmed by the museum. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 265} Of his own time he says that blame once required some objectionable act, but now anyone who wants to be blamed need only pray long. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 266}

## Other Sufi positions
The translator reports that al-Sulami praised them, Ibn 'Arabi placed them at the highest degree of the travellers, and al-Suhrawardi took a middle position, describing the Malamati as sincere and truthful but ranking the Sufi above him. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 262}
