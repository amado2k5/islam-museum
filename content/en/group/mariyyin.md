## Summary
The Mariyyin were a sect whose master was Mari the bishop; they held dualist doctrines and did not forbid sacrifices, and Dashti was of his followers before breaking with him, according to the Fihrist. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 474}

## Details
Ibn al-Nadim records in the Fihrist, among the sects and creeds, that the Mariyyin and the Dashtiyan had as their master Mari the bishop, held the doctrines of the dualists, and did not prohibit sacrifices; Dashti had been of Mari's followers and then split from him. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 474} They appear among the sects he collects in this section alongside the People of the Sky's Cover, whose master Aridi dwelt at Ctesiphon and Bihrasir and invented for himself a religion to which he called the people, the Asuriyyin whose head was a man called Ibn Saqtri ibn Aswadi, and other holders of creeds he assembled in his book. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 474} The Mariyyin thus stand, in his account, among the Christian sects that broke away in the wake of Mari the bishop. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 474}
