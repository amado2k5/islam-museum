## Summary
The Turks in the sources are the people of Transoxiana and the Central Asian steppes: a people of the far lands whom the hadith names among the signs of the Hour, and whom history houses in the conquests, the Seljuks and the Ottomans.

## In the texts
The hadith names them: «the Hour will not come until you fight the Turks, small of eye» and «until you fight a people whose faces are like hammered shields» in al-Bukhari; the geographers place them in Transoxiana, Farghana, Khwarizm and Bulghar {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}.

## In the Sira and history
The Muslims conquered the lands of the Turks under Qutayba ibn Muslim and al-Walid; they entered Islam gradually until their states arose — the Tahirids and Samanids, then the Seljuks and the Ottomans who ruled six centuries — a nation the sources first met as a far people, then a nation of Islam ruling its caliphate {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun}.

## For younger visitors
The Turks are Central Asia's people; the hadith names them, then they entered Islam and ruled with the Seljuks and Ottomans.

## Notes for researchers
For the early geographers 'the Turks' were many nations, not one people — Ghuzz, Qirghiz, Uyghur and Khazar, whose kings became Jewish — so distinguish the hadith's mention from the historians' and geographers'.
