## Summary
A large Arab tribe descended from Tamim ibn Murr. Ibn Hisham, in part four, traces its clans to Zayd Manat ibn Tamim: [[group/banu-darim|Darim]], Bahdala and [[group/banu-minqar|Minqar]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/206} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/207}.

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
Ibn Ishaq reports that Bishr ibn al-Harith al-Sahmi had a maternal half-brother from Banu Tamim, Sa'id ibn Amr {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/8}. The best-known report about this tribe in this volume is [[event/delegation-of-banu-tamim|the arrival of its delegation]] in [[event/year-of-delegations|the Year of Delegations]]. Utarid ibn Hajib was among its nobles, and with them came al-Aqra' ibn Habis, al-Zibriqan ibn Badr, Amr ibn al-Ahtam, al-Hattat ibn Yazid, Na'im ibn Yazid, Qays ibn al-Harith and Qays ibn Asim. They boasted before the Prophet with their orator and their poet, and then they embraced Islam {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/206} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/212}. Ibn Ishaq says that the verse [[passage/quran-49-4|al-Hujurat 4]] was revealed about them {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/213}. In the tribal index of the 1936 edition, its clans are listed as Banu Darim ibn Malik, Banu Bahdala, Banu Minqar, Banu Abd Allah ibn Darim and Banu Mujashi' ibn Darim {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/342}.

## What is not covered here
Tamim ibn Murr's lineage to Mudar, its homelands in Najd, and its role in the Ridda and the conquests will be added from other sources.

## In the introduction to al-Raheeq al-Makhtum
Tamim ibn Murra is among the clans of [[person/ilyas-ibn-mudar|Ilyas ibn Mudar]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/14-15}. Some clans of Tamim emigrated with Abd al-Qays and Bakr to Bahrain, and Banu Tamim settled in the desert around Basra {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22}. When [[group/sufa|Sufa]] died out, Banu Sa'd ibn Zayd Manat of Tamim inherited from them the ifada, the duty of sending the people out from Mina {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.29} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/119-122}. The author counts the story of Hajib ibn Zurara of Tamim among the examples of keeping faith among the people of the pre-Islamic era {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.47}.

## Banu al-Anbar in Ibn Hisham's Sira
Among the expeditions, Ibn Ishaq mentions the raid of Uyayna ibn Hisn against [[group/banu-al-anbar|Banu al-Anbar]] of Banu Tamim, and the arrival of a Tamimi delegation, including al-Aqra' ibn Habis and Qays ibn Asim, who spoke with the Prophet about the captives. See [[event/sariyya-of-uyayna-to-banu-tamim|the raid on Banu al-Anbar]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/270}.
