## Summary
Hamdan is the first of the most famous clans of [[group/kahlan|Kahlan]], of the Qahtanite line in Yemen, that *The Sealed Nectar* counts {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/11-13}.

## Rivalry with Himyar
In the period of disturbance that the author dates from 300 CE, the Romans entered Aden. With their help, the Abyssinians occupied Yemen for the first time in 340 CE, exploiting the rivalry between the tribes of Hamdan and [[group/himyar|Himyar]]. Their occupation continued until 378 CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}. See [[event/first-abyssinian-occupation-of-yemen|the first Abyssinian occupation of Yemen]].

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira (volume four)
Shortly before Islam, Hamdan struck [[group/murad|Murad]] at [[event/battle-of-al-radm|the Day of al-Radm]]. They were led by al-Ajda' ibn Malik according to Ibn Ishaq, or by Malik ibn Harim according to Ibn Hisham {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/228}. Their [[event/delegation-of-hamdan|delegation]] came to the Prophet on his return from Tabuk: [[person/malik-ibn-al-namt|Malik ibn al-Namt]], known as Dhu al-Mish'ar, Malik ibn Ayfa', Dimam ibn Malik al-Salmani, and 'Umayra ibn Malik al-Kharifi, from the districts of Kharif, Yam and Shakir. The Prophet wrote for them a [[work/letter-to-hamdan|letter]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/244} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/245}. It also mentions the letter of the kings of Himyar: al-Nu'man, qayl of Dhu Ru'ayn, Ma'afir and Hamdan {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/235}.

## What is not covered here
*The Sealed Nectar* adds nothing more to this on the accounts of Hamdan. Its conversion at the hands of Ali is recorded in *The Sealed Nectar*.

## For young visitors
Hamdan is a large Yemeni tribe. Its rivalry with Himyar helped the Abyssinians occupy Yemen for the first time.

## For Sprouts
Hamdan, the best-known of the Qahtani tribe Kahlan's branches in Yemen; its delegation came to the Prophet on his return from Tabuk and he wrote them a letter.

## For Explorers
Hamdan, first of the famous Qahtani Kahlan branches in Yemen named by al-Raheeq. In the era of troubles the author opens at 300 CE the Romans entered Aden and, with their help, the Abyssinians occupied Yemen in 340 CE for the first time, exploiting the rivalry between Hamdan and Himyar — the occupation lasting to 378 CE. Before Islam Hamdan routed Murad on the Day of al-Radm, led by al-Ajda' ibn Malik per Ibn Ishaq or Malik ibn Huraym per Ibn Hisham. Its delegation came to the Prophet on his return from Tabuk — Malik ibn al-Namt of the marks, Malik ibn Ayfa', Dimam ibn Malik al-Salmani and Umayra ibn Malik al-Kharifi of the Kharif, Yam and Shakir districts — and the Prophet wrote them a letter; the kings of Himyar numbered al-Nu'man Qayl Dhu Ra'in, Ma'afir and Hamdan.
