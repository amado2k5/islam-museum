## Summary
A clan of [[group/banu-bakr|Banu Bakr]] ibn Abd Manaf ibn Kinana; Khuza'a had killed their chiefs — Banu al-Aswad ibn Razn — before Islam, so they seized the al-Hudaybiyya truce and ambushed Khuza'a by night at [[place/al-watir|al-Watir]] under [[person/nawfal-ibn-muawiya|Nawfal ibn Mu'awiya]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31-32}.

## The old feud
Before Islam, Khuza'a killed Banu al-Aswad ibn Razn — Salama, Kulthum and Dhu'ayb — at Arafa by the Sanctuary's boundary stones; Banu al-Aswad used to pay double blood-money for their Jahiliyya honour {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31}. That feud stayed pending until the al-Hudaybiyya truce came, Khuza'a entering the Prophet's pact and Banu Bakr entering Quraysh's {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}.

## The night ambush at al-Watir
Nawfal ibn Mu'awiya went out among Banu al-Dil — not all Banu Bakr followed him — and ambushed Khuza'a by night at al-Watir {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}. At the Sanctuary's bounds he said to whoever appealed to him: "take your revenge" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}.

## Their poet
[[person/al-akhzar-ibn-lut|al-Akhzar ibn Lu't al-Dili]] recited verses boasting of driving Banu Ka'b from al-Watir, and [[person/budayl-ibn-warqa|Budayl ibn Umm Asram]] answered him with a poem containing the detailed reply {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/34}.

## For young visitors
A small clan of Banu Bakr with an old feud against Khuza'a. When a peace pact bound the tribes, they took advantage of the quiet and attacked their neighbours at night — their misstep was the beginning of the end of the whole war.
