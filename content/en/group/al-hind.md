## Summary
The Hind in the sources are the people of the Indian subcontinent: the hadith names them in the 'raid on India', the conquests under al-Hajjaj and Muhammad ibn al-Qasim, and the geographies and travel books in their land, kings and religions.

## In the texts
The hadith names them in the famous report of 'Isa ibn Maryam leading Muslim armies on the raid on India (weak though it is); history names them in the conquest of Sind, Multan and India under al-Walid and Muhammad ibn al-Qasim; and the geographers describe their kings, cities, Brahmins and Buddhas {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}.

## In the Sira and history
The Arabs knew India by trade before Islam — its sea, ports, Sind and Kirman — then Muhammad ibn al-Qasim took Sind and India lay on that border for centuries until its Islamic sultanates arose — Ghaznavid, Delhi and Mughal — and it became one of Islam's great nations {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun}.

## For younger visitors
The Hind are the people of the great land east of Arabia; the Muslims took Sind long ago and Hindus and Muslims lived there together.

## Notes for researchers
The people of India in the early sources are many — Hindus, Buddhists and others — and the name 'al-Hind' covers them all in Arab usage; separate the name of the land from the names of the religions.
