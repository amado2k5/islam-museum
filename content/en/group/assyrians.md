## Summary
The Assyrians in the chronicles are the people of ancient Assur and its state that ruled Mesopotamia and al-Sham before Babylon; the historians name them among the kings of the old nations and in the succession of Iraq's kingdoms before Persia and Rome.

## In the texts
The Qur'an and hadith do not name them; history names them among the pre-Islamic kingdoms — Assur, Babylon, Media and Persia — and in the reading of Nineveh, the town of Yunus's people, on one view {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun}.

## In the Sira and history
Assur is an old state that stood on the Tigris two thousand years before Islam: its famous kings Tiglath-Pileser, Sennacherib and Ashurbanipal; the Babylonians and Medes ruined their kingdom, and the name Assur remained on the land and the people — of whom today are the Christians of Iraq and al-Sham {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## For younger visitors
The Assyrians are the people of ancient Assur in Iraq; they had a great kingdom two thousand years before Islam.

## Notes for researchers
Applying 'Assyrian' to today's Syriac Christians is a late usage for the early writers — record the ancient kingdom in its own time, not by today's names.
