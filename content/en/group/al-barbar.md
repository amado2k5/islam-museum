## Summary
The Berbers in the books of conquest and history are the people of the farthest and middle Maghrib: its great tribes — Zanata, Sanhaja and Masmuda — whose faith before Islam was mixed among idol-worship, Christianity and Judaism, conquered by Musa ibn Nusayr and Tariq ibn Ziyad.

## In the texts
Ibn Khaldun treats them at length in the History of the Berbers, and the conquest books in the taking of Ifriqiya, the Maghrib and al-Andalus: their stand against the conquerors — Kusayla and al-Kahina — then their entry into Islam and their leading of the armies of the Maghrib and al-Andalus {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999}.

## In the Sira and history
'Uqba ibn Nafi', Musa ibn Nusayr and Tariq ibn Ziyad conquered them under the Umayyads; they became Muslim and led al-Andalus's armies, then their states arose — Almoravids, Almohads, Marinids and Zayyanids — and they became a nation of Islam. Among them before the conquest were Roman Christians, the Jews of Jerawa and polytheists {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun}.

## For younger visitors
The Berbers are the Maghrib's people before and within Islam; 'Uqba and Musa ibn Nusayr conquered them, then they led al-Andalus's armies and the Maghrib's states.

## Notes for researchers
'Berbers' for the historians are tribes, not one religion — among them Muslims, Christians, Jews and polytheists — so this section files them among peoples, not faiths; Ibn Khaldun's History of the Berbers is their reference.
