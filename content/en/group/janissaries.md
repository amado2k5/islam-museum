## Summary
The Janissary corps (Yeniçeri) — the standing 'new army' that al-Sallabi says Orhan founded on his brother Alaeddin's proposal: captives and prisoners reared on Islamic training and sent to the army's van {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 54-55}. The book describes Orhan organizing the force in tens, hundreds and thousands, reserving a fifth of spoils for it, and building dedicated training centres {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 53-55}.

The author insists that the familiar devşirme picture — Christian boys forcibly levied and converted — is 'a lie planted in Orhan's and Murad's history', and that Orhan never took a single Christian boy from his father's house; this is the author's stated defence, not a consensus of historians {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 53-55}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes the Janissaries as an elite infantry of slaves set up in the Ottoman Empire of the fourteenth century, and it explains the name as the Turkish for "new troops". It says young boys from conquered regions, above all the Christian Balkans, were taken in a levy held every four years, the devshirme; they were thoroughly trained as soldiers, learned Turkish and adopted Muslim names and identities. Their loyalty was something the sultan could rely on, since disobedience brought summary execution while obedience brought great personal power and exemption from tax. As Ottoman government weakened around the middle of the seventeenth century, the outlying regions increasingly came under Janissary control. Sultan Mahmud II drew on his newly created army and on public contempt for the tyranny of the Janissaries, and in 1826 massacred the greater part of the corps and drove the survivors out of the provinces. This account of the devshirme differs from the card above, which reports al-Sallabi's rejection of that picture; both are shown here and neither is settled. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1189}
