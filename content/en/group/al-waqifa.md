## Summary
"The Waqifa" ("those who stop") is a name given by third-century hadith imams to those who suspended judgement in the question of the [[concept/khalq-al-quran|createdness of the Qur'an]], saying "the Qur'an is God's speech" but neither "created" nor "uncreated". It is a name by which their opponents described a position, not the name of an organised group that called itself so. They appear in the chapter "Abu Abd Allah's view of the Waqifa" in Abd Allah ibn Ahmad's [[work/al-sunnah-ibn-hanbal|Kitab al-Sunna]]. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 117} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 118}

## As their opponents defined them
The editor Adil Al Hamdan quotes Harb al-Kirmani's *Sunna*: the Waqifa are those who say "the Qur'an is God's speech" but do not say "uncreated". {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 117} He quotes from al-Khallal [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]]'s saying that the [[group/jahmiyya|Jahmiyya]] divided into three branches, one being "those who doubted", meaning the Waqifa. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 92} In al-Hasan ibn Thawab's report Ahmad called them "a kind of Jahmiyya who hid behind suspending judgement". {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 117}

## Those associated with the view
In Ibrahim ibn al-Harith's report, as the editor quotes it from Harb al-Kirmani's *Sunna*, Ahmad was asked whether one who says "I do not say the Qur'an is created, nor that it is not created" can be among Ahl al-Sunna; he said no, and that he had heard that "Ibn Mu'adhdhal" held this view and that many people of Basra had been led astray by it. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 117}

## Ahmad's judgement
Abd Allah relates that his father, asked about the Waqifa, said: whoever of them disputes and is known for theology is a Jahmi; whoever is not known for theology is to be shunned until he returns; whoever has no knowledge is to ask and learn. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 118} The editor quotes Abu Dawud's report that Ahmad was asked whether a man may say "God's speech" and stop there; he said silence would have been allowed had people not fallen into the dispute, but since they have spoken he should speak. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 117} And from Ibn Abi Hatim: one who suspends judgement out of ignorance is taught and declared an innovator, not an unbeliever, while one who suspends judgement in doubt is a Jahmi. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 118}
