## Brief
This lesson asks you to describe the basic processes in textile production, from fibre to thread to yarn to fabric. The museum offers glimpses of these stages in cotton and woven writing; it does not describe knitting or the dyeing, printing and finishing processes in full, and your class completes that. {cite:ontario-curriculum-social-sciences-humanities}

## At which step does the material become cloth?
Put what you read on a ladder: fibre, then thread or yarn, then weaving, then dyeing. Mark the steps the records leave out.

## Go deeper
Follow cotton from fibre to cloth as the record on the [[job/weaver|Ottoman weaver]] gives it. Cotton was the main fibre, and the entry names Isparta, Denizli, Konya, Ankara and Manisa among the cotton regions. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84} The weaver bought yarn ready-spun or spun raw cotton himself, and worked to orders from the cloth merchants. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84} Looms differed in size, and the comb and shuttle were measured for the cloth. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84} Men of experience checked the length and quality of the cloth. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84} After dyeing, the merchants sold the cloth in the covered market, and cloth was stamped for a fee. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84} The weaver could not sell his own cloth in the market. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84}

## Often misunderstood
It is easy to read every establishment called a "dar" as a place where cloth was woven. The TDV encyclopaedia, in its account of later Fatimid institutions, distinguishes dar al-tiraz, where cloth was woven, from dar al-kiswa, where it was cut and sewn, and khizanat al-kiswa, where completed clothing was stored. {cite:tdv-tiraz pp. 112–114} The [[invention/tiraz|tiraz record]] says this is not an established arrangement for Abd al-Malik's workshops: the later example separates weaving, making clothing and storage. {cite:tdv-tiraz pp. 112–114} Iranian workshop terms included tirazkhana and kargah, while cloth carrying tiraz could be called mutarraz. {cite:tdv-tiraz pp. 112–114}

## How do we know?
The ladder is built from different records. The steps of yarn, loom and market come from the TDV entry on the cullah, which concerns the Ottoman weaver, {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84} the letters woven in gold or coloured thread come from Ibn Khaldun's description of tiraz, {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 256} and the later house names come from the TDV article on tiraz. {cite:tdv-tiraz pp. 112–114} The record on the weaver says that it did not read the entry's bibliography or the entries on tiraz and on the carpet, and that silk weaving and carpet weaving are not yet written. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84} It does not describe how cloth was dyed, only that the merchants sold it after dyeing. {cite:tdv-cullah p.83-84}

## Think about it
Draw the ladder from fibre to cloth using only the records. Which step do they say least about, and why does that gap matter for what you can say about how cloth was made?
