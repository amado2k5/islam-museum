## Brief
This lesson asks you to see that day and night, and the four seasons, repeat in cycles, and to describe how the light and heat from the Sun change through the day and the year. The museum offers one example of people dividing daylight and darkness into hours; the class observes its own sky and weather. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## What repeats, and what changes each time?
Notice when it gets light and when it gets dark where you live. Ask what is the same from one day to the next, and what is different from one season to the next.

## Go deeper
Al-Jazari's castle clock opens doors and drops balls from the beaks of two falcons {cite:mfa-castle-clock-14533 catalogue}. It divides daylight into twelve parts and darkness into twelve. Their lengths change with the seasons, so a seasonal hour is not always sixty minutes {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.17–18,editor-notes-I.1}. At night, lit roundels show the parts going by {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.39–41}. A person has to return the water and reset the parts for the next cycle {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.39–41}.

## Think about it
Daylight is cut into twelve parts, and so is darkness. Think about where you live. In which season would a daytime part be longer than a night part, and why?
