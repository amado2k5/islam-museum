## Brief
This lesson asks you to translate between words, algebraic expressions and pictures that describe equal relationships, and to solve equations with whole numbers up to 100 and check the answers. The museum offers one record on al-Khwarizmi's algebra: finding an unknown number through an equation, his drawings of squares and rectangles, and the fact that his algebra was written in words without symbols. The algebraic expressions, the equations and the checking are for your class to bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## How can one relationship be said in words and in symbols?
Take a sentence about an unknown number and write it as an equation. Then draw it, for example as a balance, and check that all three say the same thing.

## How do you know a solution is right?
Put your answer back into the equation. Say what it means when both sides match, and what to do when they do not.

## Try this
Read about [[invention/algebra|algebra in al-Khwarizmi's work]]. Write your own problem about a number you do not know, and set it up as an equation.
