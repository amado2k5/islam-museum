## Brief
This lesson asks you to find the areas of trapezoids, rhombuses, kites and composite polygons by splitting them into shapes you know, and the surface areas of prisms and pyramids by adding the areas of their faces. The museum offers area rules from an early algebra book and a method for adding up the surfaces of a ceiling. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## How do you split a shape so that you know every part?
Draw the shape on a grid and cut it into rectangles and triangles. Add the areas of the parts, then check by cutting it another way.

## Try this
Read the records on mensuration and on al-Kashi's muqarnas. Pick the rhombus rule, test it on a drawing, and then list the faces of a prism you can hold and add their areas.
