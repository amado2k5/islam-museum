## Brief
Biodiversity shrinks when habitats are lost, species are moved where they do not belong, or climates shift. The strand ends with action: what actually protects variety.

## Invasive species
Purple loosestrife and zebra mussels arrived in Ontario without their predators and spread fast. Restoration means replanting natives, reintroducing predators, and changing the habits that caused the damage.

## Go deeper
Some records in the museum describe land set aside for animals to graze, called a *hima*. When the people of Jurash embraced Islam, the Prophet reserved for them a *hima* around their village, marked by known signs, for the horse, the riding camel and the plough-ox; whoever grazed his livestock there would have gained unlawfully {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/234}. The Kuwaiti Encyclopaedia of Fiqh later lists conditions: a *hima* should serve the Muslims, not be private property, and be small, and the ruler may not reserve it for himself {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7286} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7287}. It adds that the ruler should appoint an agent to admit the herds of the weak and bar those of the strong {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7287}. These records speak of grazing land and herds. They do not mention wild species or biodiversity.

## Think about it
The Kuwaiti Encyclopaedia of Fiqh says the ruler should appoint an agent to admit the herds of the weak and bar those of the strong {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7287}. In your biodiversity plan for the school grounds, who or what plays the part of the weak herds that need protecting from the strong, and what rule would you set for them?
