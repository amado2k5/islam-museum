## Brief
This lesson asks you to pose and solve problems about rates, percentages and proportions in various contexts. The museum offers one context, the fixed portions in zakat, and no worked problems, and it gives the portions as one author lists a rule, not as guidance; the other contexts are for your class to bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-mathematics}

## How do you turn "a quarter-tenth" and "a tenth" into percentages?
Start from the fraction each name states, then write it as a percentage and as a decimal. Compare the portions: which is largest, and by how many times?

## Go deeper
Read the record's definition slowly. The jurists define [[concept/zakat|zakat]] as the transfer of a fixed portion of a defined wealth to a defined recipient {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 43} {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 45}: the portion, the wealth and the recipient are each fixed. The record then names the book that sets the thresholds down. Abu'l-Wafa' al-Maraghi lists them in his *Qadaya*, beginning with five camels, thirty cattle and forty sheep {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 46}. The word itself has its own account. In his commentary on [[passage/quran-2-43|Qur'an 2:43]], al-Tabari says the root of zakat is growth and increase of wealth, and it is so called because God increases what remains with its owner when he gives it out. He gives a second possible sense, that it purifies what is left of the wealth, and says he prefers this second sense {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 573} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 574}.

## How do we know?
The record gives the thresholds and rates as one author lists them, in one book, and cites its pages {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 46} {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 47}. The definition rests on the same book, which reports what the jurists define {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 43} {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 45}. Al-Tabari is a different kind of witness: a commentary on the meaning of a word, not a list of amounts {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 573}. Ask of each: what can one author's list tell you about what the schools held, and what can it not tell you?

## Think about it
The record gives its portions as one author lists them. Take a position and answer the strongest objection to it: may a student treat Abu'l-Wafa' al-Maraghi's list as "the rate of zakat", or only as "the rate in this author's list"?

## Into the museum

- [[practice/sadaqa|Sadaqa]]: how the jurists use a word that covers zakat and voluntary alms.

## Try this
Read the Overview section of [[concept/zakat|Zakat (alms tax)]], where Abu al-Wafa al-Maraghi lists the thresholds and rates, and no further. Write three problems of your own, each using a different portion the record names and an imagined amount, and solve them with percentages.
