## Brief
This lesson asks you to describe how a variable is used and to use one in problems. The museum offers a record on algebra, the branch of mathematics that solves problems by positing an unknown. Choosing a letter or symbol and using it in your own problems is the class's work. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What can a letter stand for?
Make up a problem with a number you do not know yet. Give the unknown a letter. What does the letter stand for in your problem?

## Go deeper
[[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]] wrote a book about finding a number you do not know. He used words, not symbols, for everything: the unknown was called a “thing”, and its square a *mal* {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.31}. The science of [[concept/algebra|algebra]] takes its name from the word *al-jabr*, which he used as the title of [[work/al-jabr-wal-muqabala|his book]] {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.25}.

## Think about it
Al-Khwarizmi wrote in words, not symbols, and called the unknown number a “thing”. In your problem about marbles, which would help a friend more, the word “thing” or a letter, and why?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/algebra|Algebra (al-jabr)]]: where the name of the science comes from.
- [[work/al-jabr-wal-muqabala|The Book of al-Jabr and al-Muqabala]]: the book, and how it writes everything in words.
- [[person/al-khwarizmi|al-Khwarizmi]]: the scholar who wrote it.

## Try this
Read the record on algebra. Write a short problem about an unknown number of marbles, give the unknown a letter, and explain to a partner what it stands for.
