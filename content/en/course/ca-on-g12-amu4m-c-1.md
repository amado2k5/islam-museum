## Brief
This lesson asks you to analyse, from in-depth research, the characteristics of and ideas in forms of traditional music. The museum offers only one account of one instrument, the oud, as its source reports it: an added string. Canadian, Indigenous and other musics of the world come from your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-arts}

## What idea lies behind this account?
See how the account likens the instrument to a body. Ask what this idea says about how the narrator thought of music, and mark what in your answer is your own opinion.

## What is established and what is only attributed?
In the record, separate what the source reports from what it attributes to a person.

## Go deeper
Maqqari begins with a four-string oud corresponding to four bodily temperaments {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}. The [[invention/ziryab-fifth-string|fifth-string record]] gives the strings by colour and thickness: a yellow zir, a red mathna, a white mathlath and a black bamm, with the additional red string between the middle ones {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}. The account associates the added string with the soul in an instrument-as-body analogy {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}. The record presents the comparison as part of the historical explanation, useful for understanding that thinking, not for describing human physiology today {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}. The [[concept/al-ud|record on the oud]] holds a second writer who likens the instrument to a body: al-Mas'udi transmits from Ibn Khurradadhbih an account in which the body of the instrument is made like a thigh, its neck like a leg, its head like a foot and its strings like veins {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.588}.

## Often misunderstood
Do the colours and the temperaments give a way to calculate the sound of Ziryab's oud? The record says no. The colours are not a modern key from which frequency can be calculated, and they take part in an analogy of humours and soul, not medical evidence or an acoustic law {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}. It adds that symbolic colour cannot supply missing acoustic values {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.126–127}.

## How do we know?
Sort the reports by who makes them. Maqqari reports the four strings, the red fifth string, its place between the middle ones and the analogy {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}. The TDV encyclopaedia, reporting Henry George Farmer, gives a separate account of tuning, which the record calls the encyclopaedia's account of the historian, not an independent reading of Farmer {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465}. The oud pictured with the record is a later one, and the record says no surviving original supports the attribution: the evidence is a text {cite:cleveland-oud-1918-350 inspected photograph}. The account of the oud's origin in al-Mas'udi is attributed to Ibn Khurradadhbih, and its record says it needs a further source.

## Think about it
Maqqari ties the four strings to four bodily temperaments and the added string to the soul. Take a position and answer the strongest objection to it: does an idea like this belong in a report on the characteristics of the oud's music, or only in a report on how its narrator thought about music?
