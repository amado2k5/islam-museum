## Brief
This lesson asks you to analyse grassroots initiatives that aim to reduce the impact of environmental factors on human health. The museum offers one association's court campaign over shipbreaking yards; whether it counts as grassroots, and what it achieved, are for your class to decide from research. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## What was the initiative trying to change?
Separate the health reason, the legal step and the result. Ask who started it, who it was meant to protect, and what other evidence you would need to judge its effect.

## Go deeper
Look at one case as English Wikipedia gives it in the record of [[person/rizwana-hasan|Rizwana Hasan]]. She became involved in the shipbreaking industry by suing the breaking yards in Chittagong in 2003 for, among other reasons, health hazards to workers, poor working conditions and improper waste disposal; in March 2003 the court declared shipbreaking without an environmental clearance illegal {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en}. She has continued to press for labour rights and a safer working environment in the industry {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en}. Under her leadership BELA won the United Nations Environment Programme's Global 500 Roll of Honour in 2003 {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en}. Since 2003 she has led court campaigns on the shipbreaking industry and on lake-filling, polythene, hill cutting and deforestation {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en} {cite:wikidata-rizwana-hasan}.

## Why they did it
The record quotes no words of Hasan's on why she brought this case, and it supplies no motive for her. What it reports are the grounds named for the 2003 suit: health hazards to workers, poor working conditions and improper waste disposal, among other reasons {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en}. It also gives the reason the article quotes for her 2012 Ramon Magsaysay Award: "for her uncompromising courage and impassioned leadership in a campaign of judicial activism in Bangladesh that affirms the people's right to a good environment as nothing less than their right to dignity and life" {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en}. Those are the award's words about the campaign, not Hasan's words about her reasons.

## How do we know?
The record rests on the English Wikipedia article and on Wikidata {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en} {cite:wikidata-rizwana-hasan}. It says revisions of the article must be dated, and must name the source that changed each fact. It gives the March 2003 decision as the article tells it; it names no court, no case title and nothing about what happened afterwards in the industry's practice. A decision reported this way tells of a legal step, and does not measure its effect on workers' health.

## Think about it
The article separates a health reason, hazards to workers, from a legal step, suing the yards, and from a result, the court's declaration that shipbreaking without an environmental clearance was illegal {cite:wikipedia-rizwana-hasan-en}. Does that declaration alone let you say that workers' health was protected? Take a position and answer the strongest objection to it, that a court ruling is a concrete result for an initiative: why does your position still stand?
