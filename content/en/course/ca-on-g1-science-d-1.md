## Brief
This lesson asks you to find structures made to carry a load, to name the materials they are made of, and to say which properties of a material help it do its job. The museum offers one building element; the class looks at structures near school and home. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## What is holding this up?
Pick a roof, a bridge or a shelf and trace the path from the thing it carries down to the ground. Ask what each material along the way must be able to do.

## Go deeper
Picture a big dome in Cairo. At [[building/al-azhar|al-Azhar]], the dome rests on keel arches over old columns, and wooden beams brace the columns {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.53}. A keel arch is a pointed arch shaped like an upturned boat {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.53} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.54}. The historian Richard Yeomans says this is much lighter than the solid brick piers of an earlier building {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.53}.

## Think about it
Yeomans says the arches and wooden beams are much lighter than solid brick piers. If you were building a roof that must stay up, which would you choose, and why?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/keel-arch|Keel arch]]: the arch shaped like an upturned boat, and where it appears in Cairo's buildings.
- [[building/al-azhar|al-Azhar]]: the building whose dome rests on keel arches.
- [[building/al-aqmar-mosque|al-Aqmar mosque]]: a front with three keel-arched niches.

## Try this
Build a small arch or bridge from blocks or paper and load it with a book. Compare how it carries the load with the keel arch on this page.
