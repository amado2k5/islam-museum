## Brief
Eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids and comets orbit one ordinary star. Scale is the hard part: if the Sun were a beach ball, Earth would be a peppercorn a few classrooms away.

## Earth–Moon–Sun
Earth spins daily (day and night), orbits yearly (seasons with the axial tilt), and the Moon orbits monthly (phases and tides). Most of what the sky does is geometry {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}.

## Go deeper
[[person/al-battani|Al-Battani]] spent most of his life in al-Raqqa and observed the sky there for many years to set the elements of his tables for that city's meridian and latitude {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 26}. He measured the Sun's course over successive years, fixed the solstices and equinoxes above all in 882 and 883 CE, and observed solar and lunar eclipses in 883 and 891 {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 26}. He later stayed for some time in Antioch, where he observed the solar eclipse of 23 January and the lunar eclipse of 2 August 901 {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 26}. Among his corrections, he found that the Sun's apparent diameter varies and so allows annular eclipses {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 51}.

## Why they did it
Al-Battani modestly says he follows Ptolemy's path while changing some of his elements on the strength of new observations, and that he wished to smooth the way for students of astronomy {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 50}. He urges the observer to perseverance and care, saying that truth in celestial matters is reached only through the labours of many successive generations {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 55}. Nallino says the preface of the Zij cites many Qur'anic verses, and counts this the surest witness to his Islam {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 24} {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 25}; the record does not say what those verses say or how the preface uses them.

## Often misunderstood
A story current among some Europeans made al-Battani a prince or king of Syria. Nallino rejects it, saying he found nothing of the kind in the Arabic authors {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 26}. He infers al-Battani's devotion to study and indifference to praise from the advice he gives to observers and from his great modesty {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 26}. Almost everything known about his life goes back to Ibn al-Nadim's Fihrist and al-Qifti's History of the Sages {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 17} {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 18}.

## Think about it
Al-Battani measured the Sun's course over successive years, not on a single night. Why would repeated observations over years give a better picture of the Sun's course than one good observation, and what could still remain in doubt?
