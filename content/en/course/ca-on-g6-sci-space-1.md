## Brief
This lesson asks you to describe effects of the relative positions and motions of Earth, the Moon and the Sun. The museum offers what its records say about eclipses, the Sun's course through the years and the Moon's changing distance from Earth; the class brings the other effects, such as the seasons and the phases of the Moon. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## Which positions make the effect?
For each effect you read about, write which bodies are involved, where each one stands, and which motion changes the picture from one day or year to the next.

## Go deeper
[[person/al-battani|Al-Battani]] later stayed for some time in Antioch, where he observed the solar eclipse of 23 January and the lunar eclipse of 2 August 901 {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 26}. In his [[work/al-zij-al-sabi|Zij]] he describes the instruments he used: a tall gnomon divided into twelve parts; a celestial globe with five rings, apparently his own invention; a mural quadrant with a side of no less than a metre; and horizontal and vertical sundials to measure the seasonal hours {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 55}.

## Why they did it
In the [[work/al-zij-al-sabi|Zij]], al-Battani modestly says he follows Ptolemy's path while changing some of his elements on the strength of new observations, and that he wished to smooth the way for students of astronomy {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 50}. He urges the observer to perseverance and care, saying that truth in celestial matters is reached only through the labours of many successive generations {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 55}.

## Often misunderstood
A new model of the Moon can be mistaken for a step towards a Sun-centred sky. The record on [[invention/ibn-al-shatir-lunar-model|Ibn al-Shatir's lunar model]] says the achievement concerns a particular mathematical relationship, not invention of the Moon, all astronomy or heliocentrism {cite:roberts-shatir-1957 pp428,431–432}, and that Earth remains central to the overall description {cite:roberts-shatir-1957 pp428,431}.

## Think about it
Al-Battani measured the Sun's course over successive years, and al-Farghani explains an eclipse of the sun by the Moon's passing between the Sun and the Earth at conjunction. Why must someone who wants to say when eclipses fall follow the places of the Sun and the Moon for a long time, and not only on one day?
