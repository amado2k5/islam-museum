## Brief
This lesson asks you to distinguish between reference systems, inertial and non-inertial, with respect to the real and apparent forces acting within them. The museum offers one historical debate in which a ship under way served as the example for asking what moves relative to what; the forces, the equations and the definitions are for your class. The medieval authors argue about whether the Earth could move without our noticing; they do not distinguish inertial from non-inertial frames or speak of apparent forces. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## Moving relative to what?
In each description ask who is moving and who is at rest, and what each observer sees when a stone is dropped or thrown. Then ask which descriptions would change if the ship sped up or turned.

Read about [[concept/earth-rotation-ship-analogy|the ship analogy in Islamic astronomy]]. This exercise is your class's own physics, not something the record contains: describe a stone thrown upward from the point of view of a passenger on the ship and of a person on the shore.
