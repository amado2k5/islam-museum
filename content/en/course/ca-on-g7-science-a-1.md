## Brief
This lesson asks you to describe how ideas from science and technology are used in occupations, including skilled trades, to address real-world problems, and to investigate how science and technology can work with other school subjects. The museum offers three records from the Islamic world, the trade of the architect, a manual for surveying a water tunnel and a method for measuring a muqarnas ceiling; your class must bring occupations and subject combinations from its own surroundings. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## Which subjects meet in the job?
These records pair mathematics and building knowledge with a job on a building or on water. As you read, list the school subjects involved and the person who would use them.

## Go deeper
The TDV encyclopaedia, in the record on [[job/architect|the architect]], reports an order from Sinan's time that no one should build without the chief architect's knowledge. It followed buildings that fell or burned after being put up by men from Rumeli and elsewhere, who worked "with a yard-stick in hand" without knowing carpentry or masonry {cite:tdv-mimari p.91-95}. From the sixteenth century, an architect, a journeyman or a master working outside the office needed a licence from the chief architect attesting his competence {cite:tdv-mimari p.91-95}. The architect was expected to know geometry, building technique and the measurement of land {cite:tdv-mimari p.91-95}.

## Why they did it
The museum's records preserve no sentence in which al-Karaji or al-Kashi says why he took up his work. They show the problem each work answers. [[invention/karaji-qanat-surveying|Al-Karaji's]] manual follows water from exploration to maintenance and handover {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.763–768}, and a later manuscript introduces levels the author says he devised {cite:karaji-inbat-ljs399 f.26r}. For [[invention/kashi-muqarnas-mensuration|al-Kashi]], MacTutor describes the book's intended mathematics for astronomy, surveying, architecture, accounting and commerce {cite:kashi-mactutor Key}, and Dold-Samplonius connects area with possible estimates of the paint or gilding a building needs {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 pp193-194}.

## Often misunderstood
These two records are easy to read as inventions of the thing itself. The records say otherwise. The 2020 study places qanats in traditions far older than al-Karaji, and it does not credit him with creating the system {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.762–763}. Al-Kashi's method is a calculation method within an older architectural practice, not an attribution of muqarnas itself to one inventor {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 pp193-194-and202-205}.

## Think about it
The record on al-Karaji says a straight tunnel can climb, while one with the intended fall can deviate sideways {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.766–768}. If a crew could measure only one of the two, height or direction, which would you choose, and what would still go wrong without the other?
