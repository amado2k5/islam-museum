## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify, from texts you hear, social conventions of language, among them greetings, expressions of courtesy and ways of expressing gratitude. The museum offers two: how a greeting is returned, and what thanks is. The Arabic expressions themselves, formal and informal address, and body language, your class brings. {cite:ontario-curriculum-classical-studies-intl-languages}

## How does the speaker answer?
Listen to a short dialogue and notice who begins and who answers, and the words that acknowledge a kindness. What do they tell you about the social setting?

## Go deeper
Two records give the meaning of a word, both from the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia of jurisprudence. In the record on [[practice/greeting-salam|greeting with peace]], among the senses of taslim in the language is conveying, taslim is the salam, and the jurists' usage does not depart from these senses {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5084}. In the record on [[concept/gratitude-shukr|shukr]], shukr in language is the acknowledgement of a kindness received, its spreading and praise of its doer, and it is said only in return for a kindness or favour {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10145} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10146}. Praise stands beside it: praise (madh) is good speech about a person, and may come before or after a kindness, while gratitude comes only after a kindness {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10146}.

## How do we know?
The encyclopaedia explains the words as the language uses them and as the jurists use them {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5084}. It is not a recording of what people say when they greet and thank one another in a conversation, so the dialogues your class hears are the evidence for that.

## Think about it
The encyclopaedia says gratitude comes only after a kindness, while praise may come before or after one {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10146}. Give a reason from a dialogue you hear: when a speaker thanks someone, how can you tell that a kindness has already been done?

## Try this
Read the records on [[practice/greeting-salam|greeting with peace]] and on [[concept/gratitude-shukr|shukr]]. Note the greetings and thanks you hear in a dialogue, and compare them with what the two records say.
