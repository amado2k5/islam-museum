## Brief
This lesson asks you to understand and recall commonly used percents, fractions and decimal equivalents. The museum offers one context, the fixed portions in zakat, and no worked problems; the other contexts are for your class to bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## Which fraction does each portion name?
Start from the fraction each portion names, then write it as a decimal number and as a percent. Compare the portions the record names: which is largest, and by how many times?

## Go deeper
The jurists define [[concept/zakat|zakat]] as the transfer of a fixed portion of a defined wealth to a defined recipient {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 43} {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 45}. The portions themselves are set down in a book. Abu'l-Wafa' al-Maraghi lists the thresholds in his *Qadaya*: twenty mithqals of gold and two hundred dirhams of silver, at a quarter-tenth {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 46}; and a fifth on minerals and buried treasure {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 47}. The museum restates one author's list and nothing more. Turning each portion into a decimal and a percent is your class's work.

## Think about it
The list names a kind of wealth first and a portion second: gold and silver at one portion, minerals and buried treasure at another. Using one example from the record, why does a list of portions need to name the kind of wealth before it names the portion?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/zakat|Zakat (alms tax)]]: the definition, the thresholds and the rates, as the record gives them.
- [[practice/sadaqa|Sadaqa]]: a word that, in the jurists' usage, covers zakat and also voluntary alms.
- [[concept/idkhar-fiqh|Saving (idkhar)]]: how the jurists treat wealth whose zakat is unpaid.

## Try this
Read [[concept/zakat|Zakat (alms tax)]]. Make a table with the fraction, the decimal and the percent for each portion the record names, and learn those equivalents.
