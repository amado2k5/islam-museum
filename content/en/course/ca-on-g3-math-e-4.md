## Brief
This lesson asks you to measure area with non-standard units and to explain how gaps and overlaps change the result. The museum offers a text that defines an area as a cubit by a cubit; the record has nothing on gaps and overlaps. Covering real surfaces with tiles, and the gaps and overlaps that appear, are the class's own work. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What does a surface need before you can count its area?
Cover a sheet of paper with square tiles of the same size. Leave no gaps and no overlaps. Then leave a gap and count again. What changes?

## Go deeper
[[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]] gave measuring a chapter of his [[work/al-jabr-wal-muqabala|Book of al-Jabr and al-Muqabala]]. The chapter opens by saying that "one by one" means an area, that is, a cubit by a cubit {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.70}. In the preface he lists the measurement of land among the needs he wrote the book for {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.30}. So the record on [[concept/ilm-al-misaha|mensuration]] shows what is measured (an area), in what unit (a cubit by a cubit), and a need it served (land).

## Think about it
Al-Khwarizmi counts an area as a cubit by a cubit and lists the measurement of land among his book's needs. Why might someone who owns land want its area told as a number of squares of one size, and not as "about this big"?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/ilm-al-misaha|Mensuration]]: what is measured, and in what unit, in al-Khwarizmi's chapter.
- [[person/al-khwarizmi|Al-Khwarizmi]]: the author of the book in which the chapter appears.

## Try this
Read the record on mensuration. Cover a sheet of paper with square tiles and count them. Then try tiles with half the side and compare the counts.
