## Brief
This lesson asks you to measure area with non-standard units and to explain how gaps and overlaps change the result. The museum offers a text that defines an area as a cubit by a cubit; the record has nothing on gaps and overlaps. Covering real surfaces with tiles, and the gaps and overlaps that appear, are the class's own work. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What does a surface need before you can count its area?
Cover a sheet of paper with square tiles of the same size. Leave no gaps and no overlaps. Then leave a gap and count again. What changes?

## Try this
Read the record on mensuration. Cover a sheet of paper with square tiles and count them. Then try tiles with half the side and compare the counts.
