## Brief
This lesson asks you to take information about daily life in an early society from what people used and built, and to describe some of that daily life, such as how water was kept and drunk. The curriculum asks for a few societies, including a First Nation and an Inuit society; the museum offers one medieval Islamic example, an Iranian city's clay jars and the public fountains called sabils, so your class must bring the other societies and their visual evidence. {cite:ontario-curriculum-sshg-2023}

## What can a thing tell us that a book does not?
Look at the expectations: they ask you to read daily life from visual evidence and to describe it. For each object or building, ask what it was for, who used it every day, and what it cannot show.

## Try this
Read the records on [[concept/unglazed-earthenware|unglazed earthenware]] and on [[concept/sabil|the sabil]]. Draw one day in a household that has to store and fetch its water, and label each thing the household uses.
