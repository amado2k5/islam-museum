## Brief
Electrical energy comes from transformed sources: chemical cells, falling water, steam spun by fossil fuels or uranium, wind, sunlight. Energy is never created — only converted.

## Transformations
A battery converts chemical energy to electrical; a lamp converts electrical to light and heat. Every conversion pays a tax of lost heat, which is why efficiency matters {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}.

## Go deeper
[[person/al-jazari-engineer|Al-Jazari]]'s book describes a pool in which a wooden cow seems to turn a water-lifting wheel. [[invention/jazari-hidden-drive-water-lifter|The record]] says the cow is hollow wood, carried by a lever with its hooves clear of the platform, and that water drives the machine {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.182–183}. Beneath the floor, discharged water passes over a wheel in a chamber, and gears carry the rotation up a vertical shaft to a wheel of jars {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.182–183}. The loop of jars carries vessels into the water to fill, then raises them to discharge into a channel {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.183}. The shaft turns the cow, not the reverse {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.182}. The source is Donald Hill's translation. The record asks two questions of the scene: what moves before the eye, and what supplies the energy? {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.182–183}

## Often misunderstood
A moving figure is not necessarily what makes a machine go. In this machine the shaft turns the cow, not the reverse {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.182}. The record says the familiar appearance of an animal drive does not produce power here, and that the most conspicuous component can be a load carried by the drive {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.182}. It adds that the arrangement needs a supply of water, a way to drain it and a downward path that lets the water do work, and that it is not a closed pool creating its own power {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.182–183}.

## Think about it
Your lesson says energy is never created, only converted. The record says the lifter needs a supply of water, a drain and a downward path {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.182–183}. Following that idea, where does the energy that raises the jars come from, and what would you expect to happen to the jars if the supply of water stopped, and why?
