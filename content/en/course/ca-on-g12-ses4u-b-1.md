## Brief
This lesson asks you to analyse why and how one technology for astronomical research was developed and how it was improved over time. The museum offers one such technology, the astrolabe, in two redesigns from the Islamic world. Telescopes and the other instruments the curriculum names are for your class to bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## What problem was each redesign trying to solve?
Name the limit of the earlier instrument, then ask what the new design changed to remove it. Note that a change is not always an improvement in every respect.

## How do you tell an improvement from a trade-off?
List what each design gained and what it lost, using the records' own statements, and decide which gains matter for which kind of user.

## Go deeper
Read one report about [[invention/universal-astrolabe-al-zarqali|the universal plate]] and notice the route by which it reaches us. Roser Puig describes [[person/al-zarqali|al-Zarqali]] as a Toledan instrument maker who taught himself astronomy and worked with Said al-Andalusi under the city's ruler al-Mamun {cite:zarqali-puig-bea pp. 1258–1260}. This was the 11th-century ruler of Toledo, not the Abbasid caliph who lived centuries earlier {cite:zarqali-puig-bea pp. 1258–1260}. Puig reports a 14th-century Egyptian witness to a lost work by Said: al-Zarqali constructed an instrument called al-zarqala for al-Mamun in 1048/1049 and wrote 100 chapters on its use {cite:zarqali-puig-bea pp. 1258–1260}. This is a report transmitted through a later witness, not a foundation document independently read by this museum {cite:zarqali-puig-bea pp. 1258–1260}.

## Why they did it
The museum's records hold no statement in which al-Zarqali says why he designed the plate, and they do not supply a motive for him. What they show is the problem his design addressed: an ordinary astrolabe's horizon was drawn on a plate associated with a particular latitude {cite:zarqaliyya-bir-2020 pp. 40, 49}. For [[invention/tusi-linear-astrolabe|al-Tusi's staff]], Ozel describes simplicity, low cost and fewer defects as intentions behind the instrument, and the record calls these design aims, not measured superiority {cite:tdv-sharaf-tusi-ozel astrolabe} {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 pp498-514}. The translated uses include altitude and time, and relate the afternoon prayer-time problem to shadows and altitude {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 pp509-514}.

## Often misunderstood
"Universal" means applicable across latitudes, not a machine that discovers location and time without knowledge or interpretation {cite:zarqaliyya-bir-2020 pp. 47–53}. A second confusion concerns al-Tusi's name. This is Sharaf al-Din al-Muzaffar al-Tusi, not Nasir al-Din al-Tusi of the Maragha observatory, and the 1895 editor explicitly corrects an earlier addition of Nasir's name {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 p465} {cite:sharaf-tusi-mactutor biography}. A shared geographical surname is not an identity record {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 p465} {cite:sharaf-tusi-mactutor biography}.

## How do we know?
The detailed witness to al-Tusi's staff is later: Carra de Vaux's 1895 edition of passages from al-Marrakushi, with French translation, whose editor reports a missing ending in his manuscript {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 pp465-467}. The record read selected pages of that publication, not Sharaf's autograph, and did not collate its Arabic text against manuscripts {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 pp465-467}. The 1895 editor notes that al-Marrakushi sometimes depends on an unnamed author and does not confidently identify that author; the museum does not fill the gap with a guess {cite:tdv-sharaf-tusi-ozel astrolabe} {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 p467}. For a physical object, the record on al-Zarqali lists a brass saphea at Louvre Abu Dhabi, ca. 1224, and says its catalogue does not establish that al-Zarqali made it or that its manufacture dates the invention {cite:louvre-abu-dhabi-saphea catalogue fields}. Ask which witness, the printed text or the displayed object, stands closer to the maker's hand.

## Think about it
The translated witness explicitly calls the staff's azimuth method incomplete and discusses difficulties near the equinoxes and in nighttime meridian determination {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 pp467-468} {cite:carra-vaux-linear-astrolabe-1895 p514}. Ozel describes simplicity, low cost and fewer defects as intentions behind the instrument {cite:tdv-sharaf-tusi-ozel astrolabe}. Take a position on whether such a design is an improvement, and answer the strongest objection to it: is a tool that cannot do some of what its predecessor did not a substitute rather than an improvement?
