## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify conventions of greeting, courtesy and address, and to use them appropriately when you speak. The museum offers two: how a greeting is given on meeting and on parting, and how a person is honoured by a kunya. The phrases you practise, regional variants, everyday formality and body language, your class brings. {cite:ontario-curriculum-classical-studies-intl-languages}

## When do you greet, and how do you address someone?
Notice the occasions for the greeting in the record: on meeting and on parting. Then compare how you would address an older person, a friend and a stranger, and what changes between them.

## Go deeper
Many people in the records are known by a kunya. The Kuwaiti encyclopaedia of jurisprudence says that a [[concept/kunya-fiqh|kunya]] is a name that begins with ab, father, or umm, mother, such as Abu Abd Allah or Umm al-Khayr {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13538}. It is a name besides the given name and the laqab, used with them or alone to honour its bearer {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13538}. A laqab is a byname that praises or blames, while the kunya is what begins with ab or umm {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13538} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13539}. The encyclopaedia adds that among the Arabs kunyas were so common that they sometimes overcame the proper names {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13538}.

## How do we know?
This is the definition of one jurists' encyclopaedia, and it adds that al-Jurjani counts ibn and bint as well {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13538}. The record does not say how kunyas are used in speech today, so listen for them in the dialogues your class hears.

## Think about it
The encyclopaedia says a kunya is used with the given name or alone to honour its bearer {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13538}. Think of a way of addressing someone in your own language that shows respect: why does it show respect, and would a stranger understand it?
