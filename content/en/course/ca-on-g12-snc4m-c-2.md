## Brief
This lesson asks you to evaluate how individual choices affect the control of disease and to describe non-medical ways of protecting oneself. The museum offers two records on contagion and on what a person is told to do when plague is in a land, with the views of the scholars attributed. These are scholars' views on causation, not an account of how infection spreads; medical explanations come from your class's sources. What these choices achieved, and how health authorities advise people today, are for your class to bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## Whose choice, and who is affected?
For each instruction in the records, name the person who must choose and the people who are affected by the choice. Then ask in which situations a single choice protects only the chooser and in which it protects others.

## Try this
Read [[concept/adwa|the record on contagion]] and set the two views side by side in a chart, naming who holds each.
