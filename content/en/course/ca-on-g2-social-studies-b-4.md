## Brief
This lesson asks you to name basic human needs and to describe ways people in communities around the world meet them. The museum offers two historical ways of bringing water to people: the sabil and the underground tunnel called a qanat. Your class adds the other needs and how communities meet them today. {cite:ontario-curriculum-sshg-2023}

## Which need is it, and how is it met?
Choose a need such as water. List the ways you know of meeting it: a tap, a well, a river, a public fountain.

## Go deeper
The record on the [[concept/sabil|sabil]] says that sabils received great care in their siting, building and decoration, and in the many endowments that kept them working {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.9}. A qanat is a gently sloping underground tunnel that brings water out by gravity {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.762–763}. The record on [[invention/karaji-qanat-surveying|al-Karaji's methods]] says his manual follows water from exploration to maintenance and handover {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.763–768}. It links measurement to making the route, maintenance, clearing blockages and handing over the work {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.763–764,767–768}.

## Think about it
Think of the water you use at home or at school. Who keeps it flowing, and what would go wrong for the people who use it if that work stopped, and why would that matter to them?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/sabil|The sabil]]: a water building with many endowments to keep it working.
- [[invention/karaji-qanat-surveying|Al-Karaji's levelling and tunnel-alignment methods]]: a manual that follows water from searching for it to handing over the work.

Read [[concept/sabil|Sabil]] and [[invention/karaji-qanat-surveying|al-Karaji's levelling and tunnel-alignment methods]]. Draw how water travels in each case, from where it starts to where people use it.
