## Brief
This lesson asks you to read, build and break apart whole numbers up to 200 and to say how numbers are used in everyday life. The museum offers one account of how numbers are written in places, with units, tens and hundreds; the objects, the drawings and all the practice your class brings. Adding and subtracting with objects, diagrams and equations is not covered here. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## How can one number be taken apart?
Pick a number between 100 and 200. The expectations ask you to compose and decompose it with tools: try bundles of ten, a hundred chart or place-value blocks, and compare the ways you found.

## Try this
Read the record on algorism. Choose a number under 200, write it in its places, and say how many hundreds, tens and ones it has.
