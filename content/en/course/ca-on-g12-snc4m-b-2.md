## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify alternative therapies used to treat human health conditions and to assess how well one of them works. The museum offers two therapies, cupping and honey, as the jurists' encyclopaedia records them: what they are and how the jurists rule on them. The encyclopaedia gives no measurement of effect, so assessing effectiveness is the class's own inquiry. The museum gives no medical advice: cupping involves cutting the skin, and what works is a matter for medical research and professionals. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## What would count as evidence?
Write what you would measure, in whom, and compared with what, before saying that a remedy works. Then check which of these the record supplies.

## Try this
Read [[concept/hijama|the record on cupping]] and [[concept/asal|the record on honey]]. For each, list what the entry states and what it leaves unmeasured.
