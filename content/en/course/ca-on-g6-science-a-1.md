## Brief
This lesson asks you to describe how science and technology concepts are used in various occupations and how those uses address real problems, and to analyse what various communities have contributed to science and technology. The museum offers the work of an astronomer, an engineer and a timekeeper in medieval Muslim societies; the class brings the occupations of today, including the skilled trades, and the contributions of other communities, which the museum does not hold. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## Which problem did the work answer?
The expectations link a skill to a problem and a contribution to a community. For each person you read about, note who needed the work, what problem it answered and what the record says of how it was done.

## Go deeper
The TDV entry on the astronomer's science holds that astronomy in its proper sense began in Islam with translation {cite:tdv-ilm-i-felek p.126-129}. Under the caliph al-Mansur an Indian Sanskrit work by Brahmagupta, written around 770 CE, was translated under the title al-Sindhind, and from Indian writings Muslims learned the first concepts of trigonometry {cite:tdv-ilm-i-felek p.126-129}. Ptolemy's Almagest was translated into Arabic toward the end of the eighth century, and many scholars studied it, wrote commentaries and summaries {cite:tdv-ilm-i-felek p.126-129}. This is the record on [[job/astronomer|the astronomer]].

## Why they did it
The sources used for this lesson contain no sentence in which [[person/al-jazari-engineer|al-Jazari]] or [[person/ibn-al-shatir|Ibn al-Shatir]] says why he chose his work. For al-Jazari TDV shows a patron: a book commissioned by Nasir al-Din Mahmud {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. For Ibn al-Shatir, Haji Khalifa says he studied the astronomical instruments closely and found that they did not serve every task at every latitude {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 570-571}. These are reports by named writers about the work, not statements by the men themselves.

## Often misunderstood
A marble dial on a minaret can sound like the start of timekeeping. The record on [[invention/shatir-polar-sundial|Ibn al-Shatir's sundial]] says this is not invention of shadows or the world's first clock, but a specific refinement within a long history of astronomical measurement and timekeeping manufacture {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea sundial} {cite:tdv-gunes-saati-cam historical-survey}.

## Think about it
The astronomer's tables, the engineer's pump and the timekeeper's dial each answered a need. For which of the three do the records show most clearly who needed the work and what problem it answered, and what is missing for the others?
