## Brief
This lesson asks you to compare and contrast music from the past with music of the present. The museum links records of a reported change to an instrument and of a singer of today; the other side of each comparison your class brings. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-arts}

## What stays the same, and what changes?
Choose one thing to compare: an instrument or a singer's way of performing. Ask what you can say about each side from the record, and what you would still need to find out.

## Go deeper
The TDV biography connects [[person/ziryab|Ziryab]] with Baghdad, Kairouan and Cordoba. {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465} Maqqari recounts a meeting with Harun al-Rashid, his refusal to use his teacher's oud, his preference for an instrument made by his own hands, his departure westward and the generous patronage he found in al-Andalus. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.122–125} In Cordoba, the biography says, he gave organized singing instruction, and schools on its model followed in other Andalusi cities. {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465} It describes stages that move from rhythm and words to melody and then to the subtleties of performance. {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465}

## Often misunderstood
"Ziryab changed the oud" sounds like one change. The record separates three questions: string count, tuning and the plectrum. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.126–127} Count concerns the additional string and its position, tuning concerns relationships between sounds, and the plectrum changes the means of exciting vibration. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.126–127} The TDV account, reporting Henry George Farmer, gives a tuning story distinct from string count. {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465} Attributing all three to one person does not make them one change. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.126–127}

## Think about it
The record on [[person/abida-parvin|Abida Parveen]] says that she trained with her father, Ustad Ghulam Haider, who ran a music school, and that Ustad Salamat Ali Khan also taught her. {cite:wikipedia-abida-parvin-en} The TDV biography describes Ziryab's teaching as moving from rhythm and words to melody. {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465} What would you still need to find out about each before saying how learning music then differs from learning it now, and why?

## Into the museum
- [[invention/ziryab-fifth-string|Ziryab's reported fifth oud string]]: the account, the three changes and the limits of the sources.
- [[person/ziryab|Ziryab]]: his training and travel, his teaching and his family, as the records report them.
- [[person/abida-parvin|Abida Parveen]]: her teachers, the languages she sings in and the songs the record names.
