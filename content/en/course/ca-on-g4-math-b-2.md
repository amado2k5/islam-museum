## Brief
This lesson asks you to read, compare and order decimal tenths, and to show how tenths and fractions match. The museum offers one record on early work with decimal fractions; your class brings the number lines, models and practice. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## How can one number be written in two ways?
Look for a fraction and a decimal tenth that name the same amount. Explain how you know they are equal.

## Go deeper
Picture a strip of paper cut into ten equal pieces: five of them make a half. Cut it into a hundred pieces and twenty-five make a quarter. Today we write 0.5 and 0.25, but our signs are modern, not a manuscript imitation {cite:uqlidisi-saidan-dsb decimal-fraction discussion}. [[person/al-uqlidisi-ahmad-ibn-ibrahim|Al-Uqlidisi]] wrote a book at Damascus with an early, direct treatment of such fractions {cite:tdv-oklidisi pp. 25–26} {cite:uqlidisi-saidan-dsb decimal-fraction discussion}. The idea that matters is that a digit's position tells us which part we count, and a mark for the units place protects that reading {cite:uqlidisi-saidan-dsb decimal-fraction discussion}.

## Think about it
On a strip cut into ten pieces, a half is five tenths. Why can the same strip not show a quarter as a whole number of tenths, and what would you do to show it?
