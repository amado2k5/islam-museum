## Brief
This lesson asks you to locate features of the night sky with star charts, computer models or direct observation, and to record their position in astronomical terms and systems. The museum cannot do the locating for you. It offers records in which earlier observers wrote down what the sky showed, so you can compare their way of recording with yours. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## What does a useful record of the sky contain?
Ask what a later reader would need in order to find the same feature again: where, when, how long, and from which place.

## What can a record not tell you?
Compare the words of each record with the terms of the expectation, such as the celestial equator and the ecliptic, and list what is missing before you could plot the feature on a chart.

## Try this
Read the record of [[event/comet-905|the comet of 905 AH]]. List each place it names, then write how you would record the same sighting with an altitude and an azimuth.
