## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify sources of water in natural and built environments. The museum links ways people in the past brought water to where it was needed: a public fountain, a tunnel and a well machine. Your class will name the natural sources, such as lakes and rivers, and look at the water around your own school. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## Where does the water here come from?
Trace the water you use at school or at home back to where it starts. Say which part of the way is natural and which part people built.

## What do people build to reach water?
Look for wells, pipes, tunnels, tanks or machines. For each one, say what source of water it reaches.

## Go deeper
A qanat is a gently sloping tunnel that brings water out by gravity, with vertical shafts for working, access and maintenance {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.762–763}. [[person/al-karaji|Abu Bakr al-Karaji]] describes levels and surveying procedures in a manual that follows water from the search for it to maintenance and handover {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.763–768}. A later copy shows a level: a rectangular frame, two raised sight fittings, a triangle and a string ending in a weight {cite:karaji-inbat-ljs399 f.26r}.

## Think about it
A qanat slopes gently down so that water comes out by gravity. What would happen to the water if the tunnel climbed uphill instead, and why?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/sabil|The sabil]]: the public water fountain, cared for in its siting and building and kept working by many endowments.
- [[invention/karaji-qanat-surveying|Al-Karaji's levelling and tunnel-alignment methods]]: how a route for water is chosen and checked.
