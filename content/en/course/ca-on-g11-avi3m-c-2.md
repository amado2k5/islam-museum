## Brief
This lesson asks you to explain terms for a variety of techniques, materials and tools and to use them correctly. The museum offers terms from three Islamic crafts, pouncing, tile firing and calligraphic measure; the terms of your own studio come from your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-arts}

## Can you explain the word to someone who has never seen the step?
Pick a term from each record and explain it in two sentences without using the term itself. Then add a small diagram.

## Which word do you already use under another name?
Match each term to a step in your own studio. Where there is no match, say what the missing step would be.

## Go deeper
One method has two names in two crafts. [[practice/pouncing-in-persian-painting|The record on pouncing]] gives Norah Titley's description: transparent deerskin was laid over the original and its outline traced, the line closely pricked with a fine needle, then the pricked skin laid on the paper and charcoal dabbed on it from a mesh bag {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 215}. [[job/calligrapher|The calligrapher's record]] describes large jali work. The master made a template, the kalip: the outline of the letters was pricked with a needle through several sheets, and the surface was dusted with charcoal or chalk so the design passed to the sheet below {cite:tdv-hat p.427-437}. The first record calls the method pouncing; the second calls what the calligrapher made a template.

## How do we know?
Titley's example is an object she names: a horseman and lion traced on skin in an album in the Topkapı Palace Museum Library (Hazine 2153) {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 215}. For the calligrapher's template the record cites the description in an encyclopaedia entry, and names no template that survives. So the word pouncing rests on a named object, and the word template on a description.

## Think about it
In Titley's description the pricked skin is laid on the paper and charcoal is dabbed through it {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 215}. In the calligrapher's record the outline is pricked through several sheets and dusted with charcoal or chalk {cite:tdv-hat p.427-437}. Take a position and answer the strongest objection to it: are these one technique with two names, or two techniques?
