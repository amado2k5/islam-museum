## Brief
This lesson asks you to represent and solve problems that add, subtract, multiply and divide whole numbers and decimal tenths, using tools and strategies that include algorithms and arrays. The museum offers the history of how such written steps reached the Islamic world and where the word "algorithm" comes from; the problems, the arrays and the practice are your class's. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## Which operation is the problem asking for, and in what steps?
Read each problem and decide whether it joins, separates, joins equal groups or shares equally. Then write your steps in order so that someone else could follow them.

## Go deeper
[[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] counts four jobs in arithmetic. Joining one by one is adding, he says, and joining by multiples is multiplying; separating one by one is taking away, and separating into equal parts is dividing {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 494}. The record on [[invention/algorism|algorism]] says that with the Indian numerals these four operations become steps repeated place by place {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 494}. From al-Khwarizmi's name comes the word "algorithm", for an ordered way of solving problems {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p. 25}.

## Think about it
Ibn Khaldun describes multiplying as joining by multiples and dividing as separating into equal parts. Why might it help to see multiplying as a kind of joining and dividing as a kind of separating when you decide which operation a problem asks for?
