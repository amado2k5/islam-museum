## Brief
Biodiversity is the variety of living things in a place — different species, different genes, different habitats. Scientists measure it by counting species and seeing how evenly they are spread.

## Species, habitats, ecosystems
A species is a group of organisms that can reproduce together. A habitat is where something lives; an ecosystem is the habitat plus all the living and non-living things interacting there. Ontario alone holds tens of thousands of species.

## Why variety matters
Diverse ecosystems are harder to knock down: if one species drops out, others can fill its role. That is the curriculum's core question — what do we lose when variety is lost? {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## Go deeper
In [[work/kitab-al-hayawan|Kitab al-Hayawan]], [[person/al-jahiz|al-Jahiz]] says that he does not dwell on the great-bodied for their size, and that wisdom may show more plainly in the fly, small as it is, than in the splendid horse {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 82} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 83}. He then announces how he will arrange what follows: he will begin with the small, short chapters so that the reader leaves each one eager for the next, starting with the sparrow, then the mouse and the scorpion and their enmity {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 85} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 86}. The record reads the book in the Beirut edition of 2003, with notes by Muhammad Basil 'Uyun al-Sud {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v1 p. 2}.

## Why they did it
Al-Jahiz says what he looks for: "what is most marvellous, most telling in wisdom, and most indicative to the common people of the Lord's wisdom" {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 82} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 83}. He defends a long chapter on fire, in a book about animals, as bearing on it in noble ways, useful to mention and prompting reflection {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 82}. The Encyclopaedia of Islam adds that, without stepping outside the bounds of the faith, he claimed the right to examine accepted attitudes towards natural phenomena {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 412}. The book itself opens with a prayer for an unnamed addressee and then a reproach to him {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v1 p. 7}.

## Often misunderstood
A book called "Animals" sounds like a list of animals. Its editor, Muhammad Basil 'Uyun al-Sud, says the title suggests a book confined to animals, while it contains larger sciences and learning: verses of the Qur'an and hadith, a picture of the Abbasid age with its culture and customs, the theological questions for which the Mu'tazila were known, the features of many lands and some matters of history, all laced with humour. In his words it is "a collection of books gathered in one book" {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v1 p. 4} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v1 p. 5}.

## Think about it
Al-Jahiz says that wisdom may show more plainly in the fly than in the splendid horse {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 82}. Use your schoolyard list: does a creature's size tell you how much it matters to the variety of life in a place, and why?
