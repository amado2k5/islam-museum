## Brief
This lesson asks you to show awareness of the use of music and musical instruments in various traditions, from early times to today. The museum offers one instrument, the oud, with an account of a string added to it in al-Andalus. Other instruments and other traditions your class must bring from elsewhere. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-arts}

## Who tells the story of an instrument, and when?
In the example notice who attributes what to whom, what the source says and what a later scholar says about it. Then write one sentence about the account: this is what it says, and this is what it does not say.

## What was the instrument before the story?
Look for what already existed before the attributed addition.

## Go deeper
Al-Maqqari's account starts with an oud that already has four strings. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126} The strings have colours: a yellow zir, a red mathna, a white mathlath and a black bamm. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126} Then he adds one more, a red string between the middle ones. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126} The record says this is an extra string within the oud's arrangement, not a separate new instrument. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126} Maqqari wrote much later, so this is his account of Ziryab. {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 pp.155–158}

## Think about it
The record's photograph shows an oud made long after Ziryab. Can that photograph show what Ziryab's oud was like, and why or why not?
