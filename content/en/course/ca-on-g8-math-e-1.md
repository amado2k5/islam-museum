## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify the geometric properties of shapes that tessellate and the transformations that occur in tessellations. The museum offers one short card, on girih; the shapes themselves, the measuring of their angles and the transformations are for your class to bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What lets a shape fit beside its copies with no gap?
Look at a picture of a pattern of stars and polygons. Choose one shape, count its sides, and think about the angles that meet at each vertex. Then describe how one copy moves to the next: by sliding, by reflecting or by turning?

## Go deeper
Yeomans sees the genesis of [[concept/arabesque|the Islamic arabesque]] in one style of Samarra stucco, where leaves and palmettes become abstract, lose their stems and are organised into the surface geometry of polygons and polylobes {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.37}. The style survives in the arch soffits of the [[building/masjid-ibn-tulun|Mosque of Ibn Tulun]], where vine leaf, acanthus, palmettes and rosettes fill networks of interlacing circles, triangles and squares {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.38} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.39}. In Yeomans's view geometric forms in Islamic art are dynamic and open-ended, rarely coming to rest {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.39}. The Encyclopaedia of Islam says two aesthetic rules are observed in the arabesque, and one is the wish to fill the whole surface with ornament {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 583}. The records read name no designer of these patterns.

## Think about it
Both writers describe patterns that cover a whole surface. What is the difference between a shape that tessellates with no gaps and a pattern an artist chooses to spread over a whole wall, and why does it matter which of the two you are looking at?

- [[concept/girih|Girih]]: geometric patterns of stars and polygons.

Read about [[concept/girih|girih]]. On squared paper, draw a polygon that tessellates with no gaps and name the transformation that carries one copy to its neighbour.
