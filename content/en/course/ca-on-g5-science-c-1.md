## Brief
This lesson asks you to assess what the way a common product is made does to society and to the environment. The museum offers paper: how its fibres become a sheet, a workshop set up in Baghdad and what the spread of paper meant for records and books. What the process does to the environment, and other products, are for your class to investigate. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## What are the steps from raw material to finished product?
Choose a product from your classroom. Write its steps in order and mark each step that uses water, heat or energy. The expectation asks you to assess impacts, so the steps come first.

## Who is affected when a product becomes common?
Think of the people who make, sell and use the product, and of what is left over. Which impacts can you support with evidence?

## Go deeper
Think about an old rope. While it remains a rope, you cannot write across its threads as a single page {cite:iranica-paper-i opening definition}. Papermaking separates the fibres in water, gathers them into a new surface and dries it, and the fibres may come from plants, rags or old ropes {cite:iranica-paper-i opening definition}. [[invention/baghdad-papermaking|The paper workshop in Baghdad]] was founded, says the encyclopedia writer Osman Ersoy, at the suggestion of al-Fadl ibn Yahya {cite:tdv-kagit Baghdad paragraph}. The craft itself came from China, centuries before Islam {cite:iranica-paper-i early history}.

## Think about it
Paper can be made from old ropes and rags as well as from plants. Is a product that can use old materials better for a town than one that needs new plants only, and why?

## Into the museum

- [[person/al-fadl-ibn-yahya-al-barmaki|Al-Fadl ibn Yahya]]: the administrator to whom the encyclopedia credits the proposal.
