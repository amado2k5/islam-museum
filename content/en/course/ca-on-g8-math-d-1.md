## Brief
This lesson asks you to tell when data involve one variable and when two. The museum offers one account of a letter-counting method and of the limits of what counting proves. Letter counts are one-variable data; the record has no scatter plot or second variable. Collecting data and displaying it in graphs are for your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What can a small number of observations say?
Choose one short paragraph and one long one, and count how often each letter appears in each. Compare the results and say which paragraph is closer to the general pattern, and why. Decide whether your data involve one variable, and what you would need to make them involve two.

## Try this
Read about [[invention/frequency-analysis-al-kindi|al-Kindi's letter-frequency cryptanalysis]]. Write a short sentence, replace each letter with a symbol, then ask a partner to guess the letters by counting symbols, and record what was right and what was wrong.
