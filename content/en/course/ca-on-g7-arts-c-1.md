## Brief
This lesson asks you to analyse some historical, cultural and technological influences on style, genre and innovation in music. The museum offers two cases from the Islamic world: a reported change to the strings of the oud and a boat that plays by water power. The styles, genres and the music itself your class must bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-arts}

## What counts as an influence on music?
An influence may come from the past, from a culture, or from a machine. Before you read, list what you think could change how music sounds or how it is made. Then sort the museum's two cases under your list.

## Go deeper
A small boat carries a courtly scene, then breaks its silence with moving hands and a whistle: this is [[person/al-jazari-engineer|al-Jazari's]] account of [[invention/jazari-musical-boat|the musical boat]]. {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.107–109} An upper reservoir supplies a small tipping bucket, which empties onto a scoop-wheel, and the turning axle moves rods connected to the performers' hands. {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.108–109} The detailed section describes four performers: two tambourine players, a harpist and a wind-instrument player. {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.108–109} According to the text itself, the harpist's fingers move above the strings without touching them. {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.109} A separate path admits water to a vessel, displacing air into a whistle. {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.109}

## Why they did it
The author begins with a commission from someone he could not disobey, then explains that he found no way to supply water to or discharge it from the boat, choosing an internal arrangement. {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.107} The record does not identify the commissioner by conjecture, though it gives al-Jazari's general context as Artuqid service at Amid. {cite:tdv-cezeri pp.505–506} The record of his life says that his book was commissioned by Nasir al-Din Mahmud. {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506} The records preserve no other statement of why he wanted a musical boat.

## Often misunderstood
The account of Ziryab's fifth string is told by al-Maqqari, who wrote much later. {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 pp.155–158} Dwight Reynolds shows how Maqqari assembled older voices into an idealized portrait of one artist. {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 pp.158–166} The record therefore gives a broad ninth-century setting, not a year, day or court session for the change. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126} Its photograph shows a much later Cleveland instrument, not an oud surviving from his court. {cite:cleveland-oud-1918-350 catalogue}

## Think about it
One case is an extra string on an old oud, the other a boat whose moving hands play without touching the strings. Which of the two do the records show changing what a listener hears, and which what a listener sees, and how can you tell?
