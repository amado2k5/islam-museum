## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify properties of triangles and build different kinds, and to use area relationships to develop the area formula for a triangle. The museum offers one chapter that names three kinds of triangle and gives the area of a triangle with a worked example. Building triangles from measurements, parallelograms and your own development of the formulas are for your class to bring. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## How do the three kinds of triangle differ?
Draw a triangle of each kind and look at its angles. Say what you see that sets each kind apart.

## Why half of the base times the height?
Cut or fold a rectangle or parallelogram to make a triangle. Explain how the two areas are related.

## Go deeper
The chapter on measuring in [[person/al-khwarizmi|al-Khwarizmi's]] book says triangles are of three kinds, right, acute and obtuse. It works one example, an acute triangle of unequal sides of 13, 14 and 15 cubits: the height is found by al-jabr and al-muqabala, giving a height of twelve and an area of eighty-four {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.78} {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.79}. The record on [[concept/ilm-al-misaha|mensuration]] gives the chapter's rule: the area of a triangle is its height times half its base {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.71}.

## Think about it
In al-Khwarizmi's example the height is twelve, the area is eighty-four, and the sides are 13, 14 and 15. Which side do you think was the base, and why?
