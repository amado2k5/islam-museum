## Brief
This lesson asks you to show an understanding of some drama and theatre themes and traditions from a variety of times, communities and places. The museum links one record, on a nineteenth-century dramatist of the Caucasus; your class will add other traditions, including those the curriculum names. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-arts}

## What does a theatre tradition carry?
Look at the expectation: it asks for themes and traditions from different times and places. For one play or playwright, note who wrote it, in which language, for which audience, and what it argues for.

## Go deeper
[[person/mirza-fatali-akhundov|Mirza Fath 'Ali Akhund-zada]] wrote six comedies between 1850 and 1857 in Adhari Turkish, the Azerbaijani Turkish of the Caucasus. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 353} Several of his plays were published in Russian in the official journal Kavkaz and performed in Russian at Tiflis and St Petersburg. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 353-354} Pupils of the Azerbaijani state schools gave the first performances in the original language at the end of the 1870s. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 353-354}

## Why they did it
The Encyclopaedia of Islam says that the advance of the theatre at Tiflis under the military governor, Prince Vorontsov, gave him his push towards drama; he worked in Vorontsov's chancellery as an oriental interpreter. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 353} It also says that he urged loyalty to the Russian authorities, to ease the passage of the Transcaucasian Muslims into modern life. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 353-354}

## Think about it
Several of the plays were seen in Russian before pupils performed them in their original language. What does a play gain or lose when it is performed in a language other than the one it was written in, and why?

## Into the museum
- [[person/mirza-fatali-akhundov|Mirza Fath 'Ali Akhund-zada]]: his schooling, his plays and the languages they appeared in.
- [[place/tiflis|Tiflis]]: the town where he worked, as the museum's older records describe it.

## Try this
Read [[person/mirza-fatali-akhundov|Mirza Fath 'Ali Akhund-zada]] and make a card that says where and when he wrote, what his plays argued against, and who first performed them in the original language. Then make a second card for a tradition from another community.
