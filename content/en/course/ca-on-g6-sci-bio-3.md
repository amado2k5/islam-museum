## Brief
Energy moves through an ecosystem in one direction: sun to producer to consumer to decomposer. Food webs map those paths; break a link and the effects ripple.

## Producers, consumers, decomposers
Plants capture sunlight; herbivores eat plants; carnivores eat herbivores; decomposers return the remains to the soil. Remove one group and every other group feels it.

## Go deeper
Several chapters of [[work/kitab-al-hayawan|Kitab al-Hayawan]] name creatures together, or name a relation between them. When [[person/al-jahiz|al-Jahiz]] announces the order of what remains, he lists the sparrow, then the mouse and the scorpion and their enmity, then the scorpion and the beetle {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 85} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 86}. The editor's list of the fifth part includes opposites, differences and agreements, water, the birds that live in people's houses, and scorpions and mice {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 597} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 598}. These are chapter titles, not what al-Jahiz concluded in each chapter, so the record cannot tell us whether he described what we now draw as a food web.

## Why they did it
Two headings in the editor's list show how al-Jahiz framed his subject. The third part opens with the chapter on pigeons "and the kinds of knowledge God placed in them", and the seventh has a chapter on "what is taken from animals as evidence of the beauty of God's making" {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v2 p. 3} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v2 p. 509} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v4 p. 156}. About the order of the book, he says he will begin with the small, short chapters so that the reader leaves each one eager for the next {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 85}.

## Think about it
A chapter title tells you the subject of a chapter, not what its author found. From the titles the record lists, what could you reasonably say about how al-Jahiz saw animals affecting one another, what could you not say, and what would you have to read in the book itself to find out?
