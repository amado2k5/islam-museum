## Brief
This lesson asks you to research the historical development of a hydraulic or pneumatic system, to ask why it was developed and how it was improved, and to describe the common components of such systems and what each does. The museum offers two pumps from two medieval books, one with two pistons and one with six, both with pistons and valves; modern systems, their social effects and the full history of pumps are for your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## What changed from one design to the next?
Compare the two pumps. List the parts they share and what each adds. What does each record say the design was meant to do?

## What does a valve do?
Find where the valves sit in the pump you read about. What would happen to the water without them?

## Go deeper
Look inside one cylinder of [[invention/jazari-double-piston-pump|al-Jazari's double-piston pump]]. Each copper cylinder has two clack valves allowing upward movement only, one at its intake and another at its delivery pipe {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.187}. An iron piston rod ends in two discs with hemp cord packed between them, sealing the fit inside the smooth cylinder {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.187}. Both delivery pipes join one vertical riser {cite:jazari-hill-1974 p.188}. Now ask what the water would do on the downstroke if the valve at the delivery pipe were missing.

## Why they did it
The records say what each design was meant to do, not why its maker took it up. For al-Jazari, TDV describes an engineer improving earlier work {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. For [[invention/taqi-six-cylinder-pump|Taqi al-Din's pump]], the record reads the intention as successive delivery rather than only one piston's pulse {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp47–50}. Al-Hasan compares al-Jazari's pump and identifies earlier cam arrangements in Heron and China {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp49–50}.

## Often misunderstood
More tubes are not a rule. Taqi al-Din's text allows one tube, prefers at least three for smoother delivery, and says two can achieve the purpose {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp48–50}. A preference is not a physical limit prohibiting all pumping with fewer than three {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp48–50} {cite:cabeza-pump-2021 section4.1}.

## How do we know?
The six-cylinder pump is read in Ahmad Yusuf al-Hasan's edited text, not in an original machine {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp47–50}. The modern study of it explicitly lacks evidence of historical manufacture and assumes many model dimensions {cite:cabeza-pump-2021 sections2–4}. The museum's model of the double-piston pump is a reconstruction {cite:smg-jazari-double-pump catalogue}.

## Think about it
Taqi al-Din's text allows one tube, prefers three and says two can do the job. Why might a designer prefer more tubes when fewer can work, and what would you measure to test that preference?
