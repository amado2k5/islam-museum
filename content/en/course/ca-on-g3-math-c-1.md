## Brief
This lesson asks you to find and describe the elements and operations that repeat in patterns, and to create patterns and show them in different ways: with shapes, numbers and tables. The museum offers one record on girih, which builds stars and polygons from interlacing bands and rests on girih tiles; the rules, tables and extensions are the class's own work. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What repeats, and what changes?
Look at a pattern of stars and polygons. Point to the part that comes back. Does it come back in the same place, or somewhere else?

## How can you show the same pattern another way?
Draw the pattern with shapes, then write it with numbers or in a table. Does each way show the same repeat?

## Go deeper
See where one kind of ornament turns up. The Encyclopaedia of Islam's article on [[concept/arabesque|the arabesque]] is illustrated with a motif from the [[building/masjid-amr-fustat|mosque of Amr at Fustat]], a Qur'an manuscript made in Granada, Egyptian wood carving, a faience mosaic in a tomb at Konya, and a Persian stucco tile {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 584} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 585}. The article says the ornament is not tied to one material {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 586}.

## Think about it
The same kind of ornament appears in wood, in stucco, in tile and in a book. If you wanted to find out what stays the same when it moves from one material to another, what would you look for, and why?

## Try this
Read the record on girih. Choose a star you can draw, draw three repeats of it, and make a small table that counts the points at each step.
