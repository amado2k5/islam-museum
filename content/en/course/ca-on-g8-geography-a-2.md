## Brief
This lesson asks you to describe various ways in which human settlement has affected the environment. The museum links one historical case of people working with water and farmland; the effects of settlement on air, wildlife and climate are for your class to bring from other sources. {cite:ontario-curriculum-sshg-2023}

## What changes when people bring water to land?
Look at what the expectation asks: ways settlement has affected the environment. For the case, note what changed (the land, the water, the plants), who changed it, and what the people hoped to gain.

## Go deeper
Al-Baladhuri reports that al-Hajjaj dug the river al-Nil and the river al-Zabi. He named it al-Zabi because he took it from the old al-Zabi, revived the lands on both of them, and founded the town known as al-Nil, which he made a garrison city {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.408}. That is all [[place/al-nil|the record]] preserves: one report, with a page reference. It gives no motive for what al-Hajjaj did, and it says nothing about what changed in the land, the water or the plants. Those are questions the lesson leaves to the class, and a class that answers them should say which part comes from the report and which part it is guessing.

## Think about it
Al-Baladhuri says that al-Hajjaj revived lands on both rivers and founded a garrison city. Which effects of digging a canal could you check on a map, which only from other sources, and why can the report alone not settle them?

## Into the museum
- [[place/al-nil|Al-Nil]]: the town and the two canals in al-Baladhuri's report.
- [[event/digging-of-nahr-al-ubulla|The digging of the Ubulla canal]]: another canal in al-Baladhuri, dug for the people of Basra.
- [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|Al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]]: the governor who dug the canals.

## Try this
Read the record [[place/al-nil|al-Nil]]. Make a two-column chart: in one, what people did to land and water; in the other, what you think changed as a result, marking what the record does not say.
