## Brief
Read the date on a calendar and use a calendar to find days, weeks, months, holidays and seasons. The museum offers one other calendar, counted by the moon; the calendar on your classroom wall is the one you will read first. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What does a calendar count, and what does it help you find?
Look at a month on your classroom calendar: count the days, find the weeks, and look for a holiday. Then ask what a calendar counted by the moon would do differently.

## Go deeper
A calendar can be counted by the moon. The record on the [[concept/hijri-calendar|Hijri calendar]] says it is a lunar calendar whose years are counted from the Prophet's migration from Mecca to Medina {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}. The Kuwaiti Encyclopaedia of Fiqh, in the record on the [[concept/hilal-fiqh|hilal]], says the hilal is technically the first sighting of the lit part of the moon on the first night {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16552}. It also says the size of the crescent is no guide to the start of the month; what counts is the night of sighting {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16554}.

## Think about it
The encyclopaedia says what counts for the start of the month is the night of sighting. Why might a family look up at the sky before they say that a new month has begun?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/hijri-calendar|The Hijri calendar]]: the lunar calendar whose years are counted from the Hijra.
- [[concept/hilal-fiqh|The hilal]]: what the Kuwaiti Encyclopaedia of Fiqh says about the crescent and the night of sighting.
