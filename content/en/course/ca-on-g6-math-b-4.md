## Brief
This lesson asks you to understand the divisibility rules and use them with 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, and to write composite numbers as products of prime factors, for example with factor trees. The museum offers one record about numbers and their divisors; the rules and the trees are for your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## How can you tell, without dividing, what divides a number?
Look at the expectations: they ask for rules, not for long division. Test each rule on numbers you can check, and use a factor tree to see which prime factors you cannot split further.

## Try this
Read the record on al-Farisi's method for amicable numbers. List the divisors of 220 and add the ones that are smaller than 220, then try the same with 284.
