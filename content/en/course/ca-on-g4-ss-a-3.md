## Brief
This lesson asks you to describe natural events such as river floods and how they affected a society, and to identify major scientific and technological developments of the medieval world. The curriculum asks for a few societies, including a First Nation and an Inuit society; the museum links only medieval Islamic examples, so your class will add the others. {cite:ontario-curriculum-sshg-2023}

## What did the river or the new tool change?
The expectations ask how natural events such as floods affected a society, and what major developments it made. Pick one event and one development from the linked records and say what depended on each.

## Go deeper
Picture a stone-lined pit beside the Nile. Twenty-four steps lead down into it, and at its centre stands a marble column divided into nineteen cubits; the sixteenth-cubit mark stands for the ideal flood level {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.30}. Yeomans writes that [[building/nilometer-of-rawda|the Nilometer]] measured the height of the Nile waters during the annual flood, and that its readings were essential in setting irrigation policy {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.29}.

## Think about it
The column has a mark for the ideal flood level. If a reading showed the water well below that mark, what would you want to know or decide as a farmer or an official, and why?

## Into the museum
- [[building/nilometer-of-rawda|The Nilometer of Rawda Island]]: the pit, the column and its cubit marks.
- [[invention/baghdad-papermaking|Papermaking in Baghdad]]: the story of moving a craft to a city, not inventing paper again.
- [[building/bayt-al-hikma|The House of Wisdom]]: a library in Baghdad that Ahmad Amin describes in Duha al-Islam.

## Try this
Open [[building/nilometer-of-rawda|the Nilometer of Rawda Island]] and make a labelled sketch of the column and its cubit marks, then write what a farmer or an official could decide from a reading.
