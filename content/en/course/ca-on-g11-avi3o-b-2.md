## Brief
This lesson asks you to explain, from research, how knowing the cultural and historical context of an art work has clarified its meaning, and how works respond to and reflect the society that made them. The museum offers three cases: glass, painting and tile. The research and the comparison with other works come from your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-arts}

## What did the maker have, and who paid?
List the materials, techniques and patrons each record names. Say which of them you think explains the work best, and what else you would need to find out to be sure.

## How does the work reflect its society?
Choose one feature of a work and show what it tells you about the people who made or ordered it. Mark what the record says and what you are inferring.

## Go deeper
Follow the glassworkers. Nadine Schibille ties the changes in the glass industry to new capitals. Umayyad Damascus imported large consignments of mosaic tesserae for the [[building/umayyad-mosque|Great Umayyad Mosque]] and its palaces {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 233}. At al-Raqqa, after it became the caliphal residence in 796, two plant-ash glass groups were produced side by side, probably by glassworkers brought from Mesopotamia {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 233} {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 234}. Al-Ya'qubi relates that [[person/al-mutasim|al-Mu'tasim]] gathered craftsmen, glassworkers among them, from Basra and sent them to [[place/samarra|Samarra]] {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 145}, where glass was used for prestigious decoration such as mosaics and glass inlays {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 20}.

## How do we know?
Two kinds of evidence meet here. The first is a written report: al-Ya'qubi's account of what al-Mu'tasim did {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 145}. The second is Schibille's account of the glass groups made at al-Raqqa, where she says the workers were probably brought from Mesopotamia {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 234}. Glass weights are among the most precisely dated witnesses to Egyptian glassmaking {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 23}. The record does not say what the workers were paid or who ordered each building.

## Think about it
The records say rulers brought glassworkers to new capitals. Give a reason, and answer the best argument against you: was it the ruler's order or the workers' skill that made a capital's glass?
