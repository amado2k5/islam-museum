## Brief
This lesson asks you to analyse the effect of trends in agriculture, such as genetic engineering, on food supply and production. The museum holds one resolution that defines such foods, names the aims of the modification and sets conditions for eating them; it does not measure any effect on supply, which your class's sources must do. {cite:ontario-curriculum-social-sciences-humanities}

## Which aims could change supply, and how would you test that?
Read the aims the resolution lists. Choose the ones that could change how much food is produced, and write down what data your class would need to see whether they do.

## Go deeper
Read the resolution of the [[work/fatwa-iifa-266-genetically-modified-animal-foods|International Islamic Fiqh Academy]] slowly, and look at the aims it names and the conditions it sets. The Academy noted that the aims of modifying animals are to make the animal more resistant to disease, improve its nutritional value, speed its growth or adapt it to particular environments {cite:iifa resolution 266}. It permitted consuming these foods on conditions: the modification is done by safe methods that neither contravene the sharia nor harm health; it takes place between two animals that are lawful to eat; and the necessary information about the modified food and how it was produced is disclosed {cite:iifa resolution 266}. The first condition concerns the method, the second the animals, the third what the public is told. This is a collective resolution of an organ of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, adopted as resolution 266 at its 26th session in Doha {cite:iifa resolution 266}.

## How do we know?
The resolution is a text issued by a collective body, so it witnesses what the Academy resolved {cite:iifa resolution 266}; the museum records it as a historical document attributed to the body that issued it, and issues no ruling {cite:iifa resolution 266}. The record says the Academy's council considered the research and deliberations on these foods {cite:iifa resolution 266}, and it neither prints that research nor names who prepared it. The resolution names no animal, no product and no figure for production or supply. It witnesses what was permitted and on what conditions, not what the modification does to food or to markets.

## Think about it
Resolution 266 requires safe methods that neither contravene the sharia nor harm health, and names no body that judges them safe {cite:iifa resolution 266}. [[work/fatwa-iifa-265-cultivated-meat|Resolution 265]] requires that the whole process of cultivated meat run under a trusted specialist authority, and that it supplement animal meat rather than replace it {cite:iifa resolution 265}. What does each text suggest about who decides, what in its wording shows this, and why does it matter to someone studying how such foods affect supply?
