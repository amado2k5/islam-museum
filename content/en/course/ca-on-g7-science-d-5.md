## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify the factors that decide whether a material suits a product or a structure. The museum offers one record on glazed ceramics, an opaque white glaze made with tin oxide in ninth-century Iraq. It shows what makers chose; the tests of strength, cost and weather are for your class. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## Why this material?
List what the maker wanted it to do and what the material had to do in use. Then say which of your points the record supports.

## Go deeper
Arthur Lane holds, as the record on [[work/lane-early-islamic-pottery|his book]] reports, that the Baghdad potters devised the opaque white tin glaze to imitate white Chinese ware and then painted on it {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 28} {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 29}. The record on [[concept/abbasid-tin-glazed-ware|Abbasid tin-glazed ware]] says the surface of Chinese porcelain could not be reproduced with a clear lead glaze over white slip, so the potters of Iraq used a device known earlier to the Egyptians: they mixed tin oxide into a modified lead glaze, making it opaque and white {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 28}. They then painted on the white surface with cobalt blue, copper green, manganese purple and sometimes antimony yellow, paint and glaze being fixed in one firing {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 28}.

## Often misunderstood
On a well-purified yellow or pinkish clay, the tin glaze looked deceptively like porcelain {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 28}. The record nevertheless calls what was made in Iraq an imitation: imitations of the white porcelain were found at Samarra alongside the Chinese originals {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 28}.

## Think about it
The record says the tin glaze made a clay body look deceptively like porcelain {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 28}. For a bowl used every day, is that appearance enough to judge the material, and what else would you want to test, and why?

## Into the museum
- [[concept/abbasid-tin-glazed-ware|Abbasid tin-glazed ware]]: how the white glaze was made and which colours were painted on it.
- [[concept/islamic-lustreware|Islamic lustreware]]: pottery with a metallic sheen, and how Lane describes its making.
- [[work/lane-early-islamic-pottery|Early Islamic Pottery]]: Lane's book and what it says about the Baghdad potters.
- [[person/arthur-lane|Arthur Lane]]: the historian on whom this record rests.
