## Brief
Space exploration runs on satellites (weather, GPS, communication), robotic probes, and crewed stations. Canada's Canadarm built the shuttles' and stations' reach.

## Why go at all
Exploration pays off in technology, in understanding our own planet, and in the older human habit of going to see. It is also expensive and risky — the curriculum asks you to weigh both {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}.

## Go deeper
From 17 to 24 June 1985, [[person/sultan-bin-salman-al-saud|Sultan bin Salman Al Saud]] flew as a payload specialist on the Discovery mission STS-51-G, one of a seven-member international crew that also included American and French astronauts, representing ARABSAT in deploying its satellite ARABSAT-1B {cite:wikipedia-sultan-bin-salman-al-saud-en}. At the time of his flight he had over 1,000 cumulative flight hours {cite:wikipedia-sultan-bin-salman-al-saud-en}. The record rests on two Wikipedia articles, so it supports names, dates and places, not an account of what the flight cost, risked or achieved {cite:wikipedia-sultan-bin-salman-al-saud-en} {cite:wikipedia-sultan-bin-salman-al-saud-ar}.

## Think about it
The record says the mission deployed a communications satellite, but it gives neither cost nor risk. Which cost or risk would you want to look up, and why would it matter for judging whether a mission like this is worth it?
