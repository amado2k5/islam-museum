## Brief
This lesson asks you to analyse the impact that conditions in space have on the people who explore it and how they meet their needs there, to assess the role of space-exploration technology in observing and understanding environmental changes on Earth, and to identify technologies used in space exploration and describe how innovations have contributed to our understanding of space. The museum offers an astrolabe plate of earlier sky-watchers, a scientist who worked with photographs of the Moon and satellite images of the Earth, and one astronaut's days on the International Space Station; the class brings today's telescopes, probes, satellites and stations and what each has taught us, which the museum does not hold. {cite:ontario-science-tech-2022}

## What did the technology make possible?
For each technology or person you read about, write what it let someone do or see that was harder before, what the record says about the needs of the people involved, and what it does not say.

## Go deeper
The difficulty was not absence of earlier astronomical instruments: an ordinary astrolabe's horizon was drawn on a plate associated with a particular latitude, and moving north or south changes the horizon's relationship to the celestial sphere, requiring a suitable plate {cite:zarqaliyya-bir-2020 pp. 40, 49} {cite:zarqaliyya-bir-2020 pp. 49–53}. The universal plate addresses a design question: could a different representation allow one disc to serve multiple latitudes? {cite:zarqaliyya-bir-2020 pp. 47–53} The record on [[invention/universal-astrolabe-al-zarqali|al-Zarqali's plate]] adds that a person still chooses the inputs and reads the marks {cite:zarqaliyya-bir-2020 pp. 47–53}.

## Why they did it
Roser Puig describes al-Zarqali as a Toledan instrument maker who taught himself astronomy and worked with Said al-Andalusi under the city's ruler al-Mamun {cite:zarqali-puig-bea pp. 1258–1260}. Puig reports a 14th-century Egyptian witness to a lost work by Said: al-Zarqali constructed an instrument called al-zarqala for al-Mamun in 1048/1049 and wrote 100 chapters on its use {cite:zarqali-puig-bea pp. 1258–1260}. The record gives a patron and a use; it preserves no sentence in which al-Zarqali states his own reason.

## Often misunderstood
The word "universal" can sound like an instrument that finds your place and the hour by itself. The record says universal means applicable across latitudes, not a machine that discovers location and time without knowledge or interpretation {cite:zarqaliyya-bir-2020 pp. 47–53}.

## Think about it
Al-Zarqali's plate lets one disc serve several latitudes, and El-Baz used images taken from satellites to study deserts. What does each technology let a person work out that was harder before, and why?
