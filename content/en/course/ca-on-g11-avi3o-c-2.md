## Brief
This lesson asks you to explain terms for a variety of techniques, materials and tools and to use them correctly. The museum offers terms from three Islamic crafts, pouncing, tile firing and calligraphic measure; the terms of your own studio come from your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-arts}

## Can you explain the word to someone who has never seen the step?
Pick a term from each record and explain it in two sentences without using the term itself. Then add a small diagram.

## Which word do you already use under another name?
Match each term to a step in your own studio. Where there is no match, say what the missing step would be.

## Go deeper
Tile has its own words. [[job/potter|The potter record]] says the Ottoman word cini means Chinese work, from Cin, China {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}. A coloured glaze is fired over a tile body and gives a permanent colour {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}. At Kubadabad, near Beysehir (1226–1236), the design was painted on a matt white, purple or turquoise glaze with a silver or copper oxide mixture and fired again at low heat {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}. The mina'i technique, with seven colours painted over a fired glaze and fired again, is found in Anatolian Seljuk art, in the entry's account, only at the Alaeddin kiosk in Konya {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}.

## How do we know?
All of this comes from one entry in the TDV encyclopaedia {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}. The entry is about architectural tile, not vessels, and does not describe a kiln, quartz frit, cobalt by name, a guild or an apprenticeship {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}. The museum has no person record for Merendli, Muhammad al-Majnun or Ali ibn Ilyas Ali, the makers the entry names {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}. So the terms a glossary can take from the record are glaze and technique words, not kiln words.

## Think about it
The entry says lustre was painted with a silver or copper oxide mixture and fired again at low heat, and that mina'i was painted over a fired glaze and fired again {cite:tdv-cini p.329-335}. Take a position and answer the strongest objection to it: is a second firing a technique of its own, or only a step of the first?
