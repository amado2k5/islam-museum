## Brief
This lesson asks you to explain processes used to separate mixtures, including solutions, into their components, and to identify some applications. ## What goes in, what comes out?
For any separating process, name the mixture you start with, the parts you want, and what each part of the apparatus does.

## Go deeper
The record on [[concept/anbik|the anbik]] describes the complete distilling apparatus as three parts: the cucurbit (qar'a), the cap or head (anbik), and the receiver (qabila) {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 509}. Illustrations of Arabic apparatus survive in al-Dimashqi's cosmography, where the cucurbit is placed in front of the cap rather than under it, and the cap then resembles a cupping-glass {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 509}. Modern retorts combine the cap and the cucurbit into one {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 509}. Often the name refers only to the faucet-pipe that fits onto the cap, and special kinds include the blind anbik, without a faucet, and the anbik with a beak {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 509}. Writers often describe the anbik as one of the apparatus used for distilling rose-water {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 509}.

## Think about it
The record says the complete apparatus had three parts, and that modern retorts combine the cap and the cucurbit into one {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 509}. Which design would you expect to be easier to take apart and clean, and which easier to keep sealed, and why?
