## Brief
This lesson asks you to identify and describe careers related to physics and the education and training they need. The museum offers one person who worked as an engineer in the Islamic world and what his record says about that work. It holds nothing on the education these careers require today, so your class will bring that. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-science}

## What does a career look like in a record?
Look at the expectation: it asks for a career and for the training behind it. Ask what a record tells you about the work, and what it leaves unsaid about how the person came to it.

## Go deeper
Read the plan of a book as the plan of a job. TDV records that [[person/al-jazari-engineer|al-Jazari]] served the Artuqids at [[place/amid|Amid]] in 1181–1206 {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. It gives the book two titles, *Kitab fi marifat al-hiyal al-handasiyya* and *al-Jami bayn al-ilm wa-l-amal al-nafi fi sinaat al-hiyal*, and six divisions: clocks, vessels for drinking and banquets, handwashing and bloodletting vessels, fountains and automatic musical instruments, water-raising machines and miscellaneous devices {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. The first four have ten chapters each and the last two five each: fifty devices in the account {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. The encyclopedia describes coloured drawings, lettered parts and instructions moving from the general form to making components, assembly and operation {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}.

## Why they did it
The museum's records hold no passage in which al-Jazari says why he wrote his book, and they do not report what the book's own introduction says. What they show is who asked for it: TDV connects the book to Artuqid patronage, and the Met's catalogue likewise identifies Nasir al-Din Mahmud, ruler of Amid, as its commissioner {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506} {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Catalogue Entry}. TDV says little is known of his life beyond the book's introduction {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. This is work and patronage. The records read give no schooling or training for him, and the museum does not fill the gap by guessing.

## How do we know?
The record's account of al-Jazari draws mostly on one reference entry, Sadettin Ökten's in the TDV encyclopaedia {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. The picture it cites for the elephant clock is not a machine either: the clock survives in a later manuscript folio at the Met, dated 715/1315, not an original working machine {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Catalogue Entry}. Ask which parts of his career you know from his own book and which from a later summary of it.

## Think about it
TDV says little is known of al-Jazari's life beyond his book's introduction. If you could read only one thing to write about his career, the introduction itself or an encyclopaedia's summary of it, which would you choose, and what would you lose by leaving the other unread?
