## Brief
This lesson asks you to understand the variety of ways in which art works, including applied and commercial ones, can be presented to reach different audiences and for different purposes. The museum offers one case from the Islamic world: the forms and places in which a calligrapher's work reached the people who saw it. Choosing how to present your own work is for your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-secondary-arts}

## Who is the audience, and where do they meet the work?
List the forms and places the record names. For each, ask who would see the work there, and for what purpose, and what you would add to or leave out of a piece meant for it.

## What changes when the place changes?
Compare a piece kept in a book or a case with one that runs round a wall or a dome. Which is seen by more people, and how might that affect what is written?

## Try this
Read the record of [[job/calligrapher|Calligrapher]]. Draw a table with two columns: the form or place, and who sees the work there. Add a row for a piece of your own.
