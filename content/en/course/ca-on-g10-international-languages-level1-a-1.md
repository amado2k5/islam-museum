## Go deeper
In the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia of jurisprudence, as the record on [[concept/arabiyya|Arabic in jurisprudence]] gives it, Arabic in the lexicon is eloquence and purity of speech, and the Arabic language is what the Arabs spoke, and the Qur'an came down in it; the jurists' usage stays within this meaning {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11542}. The encyclopaedia adds that 'ajamiyya is the opposite of Arabic, and that lugha, language, is wider, since it covers any speech by which a people express their aims {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11542}. The record on [[language/arabic|Arabic]] adds a short history: the language of the Arabian Peninsula that spread with the Islamic conquests until it became the language of learning, religion and literature from al-Andalus to Persia {cite:wikidata}.

## How do we know?
The meaning of the word comes from the jurists' encyclopaedia, which says the jurists' usage stays within it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11542}. The short history of the language is cited in the record to Wikidata {cite:wikidata}. Neither says where Arabic is spoken today or by how many people, so your class finds that in other sources and says where each fact came from.

## Think about it
The lexicon says Arabic is what the Arabs spoke {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11542}, and the record on Arabic says it spread from the Arabian Peninsula to lands from al-Andalus to Persia {cite:wikidata}.
