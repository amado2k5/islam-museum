## Brief
This lesson asks you to read and represent integers, including on horizontal and vertical number lines, The museum offers one historian's example in which a quantity called a debt appears as a middle step of a multiplication; the multiplication itself is not for this grade, and the properties of operations are for your class. {cite:ontario-curriculum-elementary-mathematics}

## What does a number below zero stand for?
Think of a situation where you owe something, or a level below a starting point. Mark it on a vertical number line and on a horizontal one, and explain which direction means more and which means less.

## Go deeper
[[person/abu-al-wafa-al-buzjani|Abu al-Wafa al-Buzjani]] wrote [[work/hisab-kuttab-abu-wafa|a practical arithmetic book]] for scribes and officials {cite:abu-wafa-mactutor arithmetic} {cite:iranica-abu-wafa-works work1}. The historian Youschkevitch gives one example from its multiplication: three times five. The complements of the two numbers to ten are seven and five; subtracting five from three yields a debt of two {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}. Multiplying that debt by ten and adding thirty-five gives fifteen {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}. Both factors and the final product are positive; only the middle quantity is called a debt {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}. The record adds that modern symbols are only a way to make the calculation legible, not the author's own notation {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2} {cite:abu-wafa-mactutor arithmetic}.

## Why they did it
The records read here preserve no sentence in which Abu al-Wafa says why he wrote this book. They show who it was for: scribal, administrative and commercial arithmetic, with sections on taxation, measurement and exchange {cite:tdv-ebul-vefa-buzcani works2} {cite:abu-wafa-mactutor arithmetic}. MacTutor says the author himself knew Indian numerals but chose a presentation suited to business users {cite:abu-wafa-mactutor arithmetic}. The record adds that this context does not turn the example into a real loan or a documented treasury sum {cite:tdv-ebul-vefa-buzcani works2} {cite:abu-wafa-mactutor arithmetic}.

## Often misunderstood
A debt inside a multiplication is easy to read as the invention of negative numbers. The record says otherwise: this is not about inventing debt or a first negative number, but about a specific rule and evidence that a negative intermediate was accepted within practical arithmetic {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}. Youschkevitch proposes Indian influence on the debt language, rather than demonstrating a specific Sanskrit book in Abu al-Wafa's hands {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}.

## Think about it
A single worked example shows a debt as a middle step. What more would you want to see in a book before you said its author understood amounts below zero, and why?
