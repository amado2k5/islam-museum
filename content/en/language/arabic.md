## Overview
The language of the Qur'an and the prayer; the language of the Arabian Peninsula that spread with the Islamic conquests until it became the language of learning, religion and literature from al-Andalus to Persia. {cite:wikidata}

## For Sprouts
The Arabic language is a beautiful and rich language in which the Qur'an was revealed, spoken by millions of people across many lands.

## For Explorers
Arabic is a classical Semitic language intimately connected with the revelation of the Qur'an and daily Islamic worship across the globe. Developing comprehensive grammar, rhetoric, and vast lexicons, it became the international language of science, philosophy, and medicine for centuries, leaving an enduring mark on world civilization.

## For Scholars
A major Semitic language characterized by a sophisticated triconsonantal root system and rich morphology. Its historical trajectory and global status were shaped by its selection as the linguistic medium of the Qur'anic revelation, making it the liturgical language for Muslims worldwide. Classical scholarship formalized pioneering grammatical and phonetic disciplines through master philologists like Sibawayh and al-Khalil ibn Ahmad. Serving as the primary lingua franca of global science, philosophy, and medicine throughout the medieval era, Arabic profoundly enriched Persian, Turkish, Urdu, and Romance tongues.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)

The Sheikh distinguishes between najah and falah in Arabic: najah is succeeding in one situation, such as a pupil passing a term, the end of the year or a qualification, while falah is succeeding in all situations, and a person who has najah may still fail in character, conduct and dealings with others {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:08:30}.

He adds that someone who succeeds in one respect may fail in dutifulness to his parents and in his treatment of his wife, and that when the muezzin calls «hayya ala al-falah» it is as if he invites us to till our hearts as the farmer tills the land, splitting the soil, turning it over, exposing it to the sun, sowing and watering {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:09:00}.

He says the kafir is so named because he covers the seed with soil after sowing it, and then asks why we do not repeat «hayya ala al-salah, hayya ala al-falah» when the muezzin says it, as we repeat his other words {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:09:30}.

He explains the root sense of concealment in the root j-n-n, saying that the night covers, that a madman's mind is covered and veiled, that the Prophet disliked this word and that people used to say «afflicted» instead, and he cites the common saying to bear with a madman until one madder comes {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:09:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 3)

The Sheikh explains that «the day» in the Arabs' language means the daytime, unlike what we mean today when we say «three days», namely twenty-four hours for each day {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:10:00}.

He illustrates this with a devout worshipper who fasted his day and prayed his night, that is, fasted the daytime and prayed through the night {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:10:30}.

He says that «bard» in the language means sleep, that the Arabs say the intensity of the cold kept me from sleep, and he notes that the verse describing those in torment denies them the taste of sleep and drink, since sleep is a relief for a person {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:57:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)

The Sheikh recalls what he said earlier: that «bard» is sleep, that «hamim» is boiling water and that «ghassaq» is the pus and discharge flowing from those in torment {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:49:30}.
