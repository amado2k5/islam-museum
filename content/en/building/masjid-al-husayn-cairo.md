## Overview
The shrine of al-Husayn in Cairo, opposite which stood Dar al-Fitra {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1178}.

## The teaching circle
Al-Maqrizi says that when al-Malik al-Nasir ruled, he set up a teaching circle and jurists in the shrine, and gave it to the jurist al-Baha' al-Dimashqi, who taught at the mihrab behind the tomb {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1181}.

## The iwan and the fire
Al-Maqrizi says that the vizier Mu'in al-Din Husayn ibn Shaykh al-Shuyukh ibn Hamuya took charge of the shrine. From its endowments he built the teaching iwan and the houses of the Alawid jurists. The shrine burned in the Salihi years, in one of the years of the 640s AH {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1181}.

## The head of al-Husayn and its shrine in Yeomans
Yeomans relates the tradition that after Karbala in 680 al-Husayn's head was buried at Ascalon and later discovered by Badr al-Jamali or his son al-Afdal; according to Ibn Muyassar it was al-Afdal, after taking Jerusalem in 1098, who found it and built a shrine for it. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.66} When the Crusaders under Baldwin III besieged Ascalon in 1153 the relic was taken to Cairo before the city fell and buried in the palace; a shrine was built for it in the palace precincts after al-Salih Tala'i's request to move it to his mosque was refused. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.66} The shrine stayed sacred to Shi'is and Sunnis alike even under Salah al-Din; Ibn Jubayr, in Cairo between 1182 and 1185, described people kissing the tomb, circling it, weeping and praying. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.66} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.67} The present nineteenth-century mosque stands on the palace site of the original shrine. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.67}
