## Overview
The Great Mosque (Jamaa el-Kebir) of [[place/algiers|Algiers]] is traditionally said to be the oldest mosque in the city. Its minbar bears a date showing that it existed in 1018, and its minaret was built by [[person/abu-tashfin-i-zayyanid|Abu Tashfin]], sultan of Tlemcen, in 1324. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p.27}

## Among the mosques of Algiers
[[person/rexford-newcomb|Newcomb]] names with it the New Mosque (Jamaa el-Jedid) of the seventeenth century, and judges neither as interesting for its tilework as the old Moorish houses of the city, such as the Kasbah and the palaces of the governor-general and the archbishop, or the [[building/sidi-abderrahman-mosque-algiers|mosque of Sidi Abd al-Rahman]] with its abundant tiles. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p.27}
