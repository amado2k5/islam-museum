## Summary
The imperial külliye of Yıldırım Beyazit Cami was built in 1390–6 by Beyazid I on a hilltop east of Bursa's centre {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.56}. Badly damaged in the 1855 earthquake and repaired in 1878 and 1948, it lost both minarets — one in 1855 and one in 1949 — whose bases remain at the north-east and north-west corners {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.56}. Of the original külliye only the mosque, one of the two medreses and the türbe survive {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.56}.

## Architecture
The façade is handsome marble and cut stone; a five-domed porch leads through a domed vestibule to a cross-axial interior flanked by zaviyes with flat-topped cross-vaults, fireplaces and storage niches {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.56}. The main eyvan is framed by a 'Bursa arch' — a shallow bow-flattened arch like a drawn Turkish bow — the first appearance of this form in Ottoman architecture after Seljuk precedents {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.56}. Below the mosque stand the medrese with its twenty cells and the domed-cube türbe; Beyazit was buried there only in 1406, when his son Çelebi Mehmet recovered his remains {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.57}.

## For young visitors
The mosque of Sultan 'Thunderbolt' lost its minarets in an earthquake and a fire — and it introduced a new arch named after its city, Bursa.
