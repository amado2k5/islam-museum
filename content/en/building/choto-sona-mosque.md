## Overview
The Small Golden Mosque, which Ravenshaw called "the gem of Gaur", stands in the southernmost quarter of the city, on the east side of the Nawabganj road in the area known as Firuzpur, a little over a mile south of the Kotwali Gate. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 160} Its name comes from the gilding of its ornament, of which Creighton and Cunningham saw remains on the mihrab wall. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 160} It is also called the Khwaja's mosque, and on the strength of an undated inscription it is ascribed to Wali Muhammad in the reign of Husain Shah. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 160}

## The building
It is an oblong 82 by 52½ feet outside and 70 feet 4 inches by 40 feet 9 inches inside, 20 feet high, planned like the earlier [[building/darasbari-mosque|Darasbari mosque]]: a central nave 14 feet 5 inches wide divides it into northern and southern halls. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 160} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 161} Three four-sided Bengali *chau-chala* roofs, their decoration copied from bamboo framework, cover the nave, and each side hall has six hemispherical domes. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 161} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 162} It is entered by five cusped pointed arches on the east and three doors on each side; at the north-west a two-storeyed porch leads to the women's gallery, carried on slender black basalt pillars. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 162}

## Later history
The qibla wall with its six carved mihrabs fell in the earthquake of 1897 and has been rebuilt by the Archaeological Department since 1900. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 162} A mihrab from the mosque is in the Royal Scottish Museum in Edinburgh, the subject of an appendix to the book. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 contents}
