## Overview
The Old Mosque (Hamza Bey Mosque, "Cami-i Atik") stands in the centre of Stara Zagora, Bulgaria; built by Hamza Bey in AH 811 (1408-09), it is today the only surviving place of worship among the seventeen mosques and masjids Evliya Çelebi saw in the city in 1652 {cite:yilmaz-turkish-monuments-churches-2023 pp.79-80}.

## Architecture
Its near-square prayer hall (19.32 × 20.50 m) behind a three-section narthex is covered by a high-drummed dome 17.50 m in diameter, exceptional among Ottoman mosques for its dome size; the slender minaret rises over the body wall and the narthex corner with its stair inside the wall thickness, and the dome's transition from square to drum is made by wide squinches {cite:yilmaz-turkish-monuments-churches-2023 pp.80-81}.

## Fate
The Bulgarians destroyed its minaret in the war of 1877-78 and the mosque served briefly as a church and then as an arms depot until Ottoman pressure returned it to the Islamic community on 21 April 1881; long closed and even used as a tavern, it is now proposed for restoration as a Museum of Religions {cite:yilmaz-turkish-monuments-churches-2023 pp.79-81}.
