## Overview
The King's Mosque (Fâtih Sultan Mehmet Mosque) of Pristina, Kosovo, was built by Mehmet the Conqueror in AH 865 (1461); it stands in the city centre surrounded by a double bath, a clock tower, the bazaar and a Kadiri lodge, and Ekrem Hakkı Ayverdi counts it among the finest mosques of Rumelia {cite:yilmaz-turkish-monuments-churches-2023 pp.355-356}.

## Architecture
A classical-style mosque of smooth-cut stone: a square-planned prayer hall under a large dome, a three-section narthex, and a stone minaret with a single balcony at the north-east corner {cite:yilmaz-turkish-monuments-churches-2023 pp.355-356}.

## Fate
It was converted into a Catholic church during the Austrian occupation of 1689-90, its interior decoration renewed in that period, then returned to a mosque when the Ottomans retook the city; the Germans restored the interior between 1950 and 1961, and in 2010 TİKA repaired the mosque and landscaped its surroundings {cite:yilmaz-turkish-monuments-churches-2023 pp.356-357}.
