## Summary
Hüdavendigâr Camii, on the west bank of the Tunca, is the earliest of Edirne's imperial mosques; formerly attributed to Yıldırım Beyazit, Aptullah Kuran has argued convincingly it was most probably built by Murat I Hüdavendigâr soon after his capture of Adrianople in 1369 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.79-80}.

## Form
The mosque is thought to have been built on and from the ruins of a Greek church — the foundations and lower wall courses appear Byzantine; an eighteenth-century restoration obscured the original layout {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.80}. An eastern courtyard with an octagonal şadırvan precedes it; of a former five-bay portico only column bases survive, and the left-corner minaret is an eighteenth-century replacement standing to its balcony {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.80}. Inside, the cross-axial eyvan plan opens a barrel-vaulted vestibule into a domed central hall with three barrel-vaulted eyvans; the mimber and mihrab stand in the corners of the southern eyvan {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.80}.

## For young visitors
Edirne's oldest sultanic mosque was built by Murat I on the remains of an old church in 1369.
