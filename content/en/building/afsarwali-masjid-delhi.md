## Overview
The Afsarwali Masjid is a three-arched mosque on the same platform as the [[building/afsarwala-tomb|Afsarwala tomb]], south-west of [[building/humayun-tomb|Humayun's tomb]] and north-west of the Arab Sarai. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 234} It has no inscription; Nath dates it on style to about 1530, earlier than the adjoining tomb. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 197} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 234}

## The building
The prayer hall has three bays and three broad arches on the facade, each in its own frame — a device Nath finds more effective than recessed planes — with red painting on the spandrels and two oblong openings at the ends holding stairways to the roof. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 234} It is built of plastered rubble; inside are stucco decoration and incised medallions, the finest being the painted incised stucco on the soffit of the central bay. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 234} A single dome on an octagonal drum, square in plan inside to strengthen the walls that take its load, roofs the central bay; there is no other dome or chhatri. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 234}

## Its place
For Nath it follows the conventional forms of the three-arched (trimukhi) type and adds little to them, unlike the [[building/isa-khan-mosque-delhi|mosque of Isa Khan]] after it. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 234} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 235}
