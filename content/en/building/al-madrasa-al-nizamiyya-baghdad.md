## Overview
The Nizamiyya madrasa in Baghdad was the first of the madrasas founded by the vizier [[person/nizam-al-mulk|Nizam al-Mulk al-Tusi]], and the most famous. Construction began in 457 AH. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.25} {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.285}

## Branches
Similar madrasas were set up after it in Nishapur, Isfahan, Herat, Basra, Mosul and Amid. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.285}

## Teaching and standing
Teaching there was entrusted first to the Imam of the Two Sanctuaries, al-Juwayni, and then to Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi. It became one of the chief Sunni institutes, and later madrasas in Baghdad and elsewhere followed its model. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.285}

Caliphs prayed in it, and Nizam al-Mulk himself visited it to inspect its library. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.285}

See also [[building/al-madrasa-al-bayhaqiyya|the Bayhaqiyya madrasa]], which al-Subki and al-Suyuti considered older than Nizam al-Mulk's.
