## Overview
Osijek lies in the Slavonia region of Croatia on the road to Buda; its castle, taken in 1526 on the way to Mohács, stayed under Ottoman rule for 170 years and became an important trading centre. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 312} Gazi Kasım Pasha, superintendent of the castle's building, raised a mosque bearing his name in its middle fortress (today Križanić Square), with a fountain and his tomb beside it. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 312} Evliya Çelebi describes it as unmatched in Buda, Eger, Kanizsa, Belgrade or Timişoara: lead-roofed, a hundred feet long and wide, with a portico of seven domes on six white marble columns and a tall, finely made minaret; the chronogram ending its inscription gives AH 966. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 312}

## Its fate
Kasım Pasha also built a mosque at Pécs in Hungary on the same plan — a square single-domed prayer hall with a seven-domed portico — and both were turned into churches by the Jesuits. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 312} A plan of 1688 gives the Osijek prayer hall as 25 by 25 metres. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 313} It became a church after 1687 and in 1734 was entirely pulled down for the baroque Catholic church of St Michael, completed in 1768; excavations in the square before it found the foundations of Kasım Pasha's octagonal tomb west of the mosque. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 313}
