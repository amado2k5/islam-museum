## Summary
Darülhadis Camii belongs to a külliye Murat II founded in 1434; the sultan first meant the main building to be a darülhadis, a school of sacred tradition, then had his architect Koçu Ahmet convert it into a mosque {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.96}. The külliye also had a large medrese, imaret, hamam and hospice; the hospice, minaret and porch were destroyed in the 1912 Balkan Wars, leaving the cross-vaulted canopy and six ancient marble columns {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.96}.

## Building and tombs
The mosque is a cut-stone rectangle 15.40 by 9.85 metres: a deep three-bay vestibule under ellipsoidal vaults, then a square domed prayer room — Kuran notes the main dome 'is not a hemisphere but a domical vault with rounded corners like a hassock' {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.96}. Behind stand two tombs: the open hexagonal Açık Türbe dated 1434 and the octagonal Şehzadeler Türbesi of 1449, holding Murat II's sons Hasan and Orhan plus children of Mustafa II and Ahmet III {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.96-97}. The külliye's bath, Tahtakale Hamamı, also dated 1434, still functions — Edirne's oldest working hamam {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.97}.

## For young visitors
The sultan meant a hadith school and got a mosque — and its bathhouse has been running since 1434.
