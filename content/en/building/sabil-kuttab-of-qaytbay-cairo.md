## Overview
[[person/qaytbay|Qaytbay]] built Cairo's first free-standing [[concept/sabil-kuttab|sabil-kuttab]] in 1479 on Saliba street between the [[building/masjid-ibn-tulun|Mosque of Ibn Tulun]] and the [[building/citadel-of-cairo|Citadel]]; such buildings stayed popular into the Ottoman period and shaped much of the city's character. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.224} With its tall trilobed portal it loosely resembles the northern part of his [[building/qaytbay-complex-cairo|funerary complex]], but its decoration is far richer. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.224}

## The façades
The façade is covered with ablaq masonry, polychrome marble and ceramic patterns, joggled voussoirs, medallions, interlacing arabesques, geometric shapes and inscriptions. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.224} Two large bronze sabil grilles sit at the base, each topped by nine panels: a lintel of arabesque in white, terracotta red and dark blue, a lintel of black and white joggled voussoirs shaped like branches with trefoil leaves giving an illusion of concavity, and three roundels, the largest round a bronze oculus. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.224} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.225} A band of calligraphy wraps the building between sabil and kuttab, and Yeomans cites Blair and Bloom that this format of arabesque, geometry and calligraphy derives from contemporary book illumination. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.225}

## The kuttab
The restored wooden kuttab stands higher than the one at the funerary complex, with cantilevered roofs and balconies on wooden brackets, slender columns, fretted woodwork and mashrabiyya echoing the bronze grilles below in a lighter key. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.224}
