## Summary
The Tankiziyya is a Mamluk madrasa in [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]], founded 728–729 AH (1328–1329) by the amir Sayf al-Din Tankiz al-Nasiri, governor of Damascus, above Bab al-Nazir on the Haram's western wall. Its building combined a madrasa, law court, hadith house and library; the tunnel dug along the Western Wall now passes beneath it, as the UNESCO mission described in 1986. {cite:meoc-mamluk-emblems} {cite:unesco-lemaire-jerusalem-1986}

## Fabric and foundation inscription
The building stands over a stone arch spanning the street to Bab al-Nazir and bears a foundation inscription naming Tankiz al-Nasiri dated 729 AH. It ranks among Jerusalem's finest Mamluk colleges in plan and ornament, and for a time served as a sharia court alongside its teaching function. {cite:meoc-mamluk-emblems} {cite:tei-epigraphie}
