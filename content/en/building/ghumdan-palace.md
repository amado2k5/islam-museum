## Overview
Ghumdan was the most famous of Yemen's ancient palaces, at Sanaa; Hisham ibn Muhammad al-Kalbi relates that Yashrah ibn Yahsub wished to build a palace between Sanaa and Tiwa, and when a kite snatched the builders' measuring cord and dropped it at the site of Ghumdan, the palace was built there with four faces: white, red, yellow and green {cite:mujam-al-buldan v4}.

## In the sources
Al-Kalbi's description in Yaqut gives an inner palace of seven storeys with forty cubits between each pair of floors, an upper hall of coloured marble roofed with a single slab, lion statues at each corner that roared when the wind passed through them, and lamps lit at night until the whole palace gleamed like lightning; others said Solomon son of David ordered the jinn to build Bilqis three palaces at Sanaa — Ghumdan, Salhin and Baynun {cite:mujam-al-buldan v4}.

## Demolition
Ghumdan was demolished in the days of Uthman ibn Affan; told that Yemen's soothsayers claimed its destroyer would be killed, he ordered it rebuilt, but hearing that the earth's treasure would not restore it as it was, he left it — and a tablet was said to be found in the ruins inscribed in cast lead: "Ghumdan is safe; your destroyer is slain" {cite:mujam-al-buldan v4}.
