## Overview
The Gâzi Kasım Paşa Mosque stands on Széchenyi Square in central Pécs, Hungary; it was built between 1548 and 1552 by Gâzi Kasım Pasha, governor of Buda, and is the largest surviving Ottoman mosque in Hungary, crowned by a great dome 16 metres in diameter {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 pp.106-107}.

## Fate
After Ottoman rule in Pécs ended, the Austrians demolished its minaret and narthex in 1776 and converted it into a church; the original fabric was entirely altered in the eighteenth century, then restorations in 1938 and later years returned its exterior to its original appearance {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p.106}.

## In the sources
Its oldest description is Evliya Çelebi's, who praised domes "like the dome of heaven" a hundred feet long and wide, a minber, mihrab, müezzin gallery and a kürsü like inlaid work, a lofty round dome rivalled only by that of the Sultan Selim Mosque in Istanbul, six tall columns bearing seven ornamented domes over the porch beside the door, and a tall single-balcony minaret {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p.106}. The mosque still stands with its walls and dome as an important monument of Turkish architecture in Central Europe; it remains in use as a church, with a museum of local history in its basement {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 pp.106-107}.
