## Overview
The Souk al-Ghazal Mosque stands beside the historic Bey's palace in [[place/constantine|Constantine]], which the author says was conquered in 1519. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 230} According to its inscription it was built by Kelyani Hüseyin Bey in 1731. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 230}

## Its inscription and conversion
After the French occupation of 13 October 1837 the mosque became the church of Notre-Dame, and its foundation inscription was taken down and set into the wall of a room in the Bey's palace. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 230} The author counts it among the oldest and finest inscriptions made by pouring lead into marble, and the oldest stone inscription in the city. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 230} After Algerian independence it became a mosque again and is open for prayer today. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 230}
