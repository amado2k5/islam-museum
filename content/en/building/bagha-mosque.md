## Overview
The Bagha mosque lies about 35 miles south-east of Rajshahi, in a walled compound in a neighbourhood associated with the saint Mawlana Shah Mu'azzam Dawla, on the bank of a large tank. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 136} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 137} Carstairs traced its inscription over the central door: three lines in black basalt recording the building of this congregational mosque by Sultan Nasir al-Dunya wa al-Din Abu al-Muzaffar Nusrat Shah, son of Husain Shah, in AH 930 (1523-24), opening with the prophetic saying on building mosques. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 136} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 137}

## The building
Bloch found it like the [[building/tantipara-mosque-gaur|Tantipara mosque]] in every detail. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 137} Two old gateways lead into the compound from north and south; the mosque is entered from the east by five decorated doorways, and four carved black basalt pillars divide it into two aisles and five bays under ten small brick domes, all of which collapsed in the earthquake of 1897. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 137} Octagonal corner towers in several stages buttress it, and the qibla wall has three ornate mihrabs in the three southern bays, a panel in the fourth and a small niche in the upper storey of the fifth. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 137} Reported measurements differ: 54 by 45 feet for Carstairs, 75 feet 8 inches by 42 feet 2 inches for Dani. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 137}
