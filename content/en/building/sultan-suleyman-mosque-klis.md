## Overview
Klis fortress, five kilometres east of Split, is one of the castles closest to the Adriatic; Gazi Hüsrev Bey and Gazi Murad Bey besieged and took it in 1537 after defeating a Christian army at Solin. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 300} A mosque was built at its highest point and served through the 111 years of Ottoman rule, until the fortress was lost in 1648. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 300}

## Conversion
After the Ottoman withdrawal its minaret was demolished and it became a church dedicated to St Vitus; Evliya Çelebi noted that the minaret's cap had been pulled down and a bell set in its balcony. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 300} An engraving of 1645 shows mosque and minaret clearly; one of 1704 labels the mosque *il duomo* and the minaret's place *campanil*; under Austrian rule after 1797 it was an arms store. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 300}

## The building
It is a plain rubble building, square, 7.90 by 8.25 metres inside, under a single dome, with a room on the east and the first steps of the vanished minaret in the north-east corner. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 300} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 301} A door was opened in the qibla wall in place of the mihrab, with a Latin inscription of 1743 above it, probably from a Venetian repair. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 300} Well kept today, it is used as a tourist site. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 301}
