## Overview
On the inner side of the southern gate of the inner citadel (Acropolis) of Thessaloniki stood a sanctuary known as the Eski Cami (Old Mosque, Cami-i Atik) or Papora Baba Mosque, which the book's entry ascribes to Çelebi Hüseyin Bey after 1429. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1296} S. Ünver reports it registered as Cami-i Atik in the Suluca quarter in 1685; it was also used as a time-keeper's house, and has records of repair in 1861 and 1863. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1296}

## Form and conversion
Photographs of 1916 show a square building under a tiled roof with an L-shaped wooden-posted portico on the north and east, like the mosque of Süleyman in Szigetvár castle, and two rows of windows on the west front. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1296} It was turned into a church in 1922 and its architecture entirely changed; Semavi Eyice reported in 1953 that its minaret had been cut down to its base and a projecting apse added to its side wall, that it bore the name of the Holy Anargyroi, and that its portico and old fountain partly survived. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1296} It was thoroughly rebuilt on 6 September 1959. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1296}
