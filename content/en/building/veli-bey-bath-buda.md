## Overview
This bath was built by Sokollu Mustafa Pasha, governor of Buda, in 1569, and is known as the Veli Bey Bath or the Császár ("Emperor's") Bath. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 33} Its main hall is laid out with four iwans and four private rooms round an octagonal pool; its cold room was removed after Ottoman rule ended and it was enlarged with other additions. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 33}

## In the sources
Ekrem Hakkı Ayverdi wrote that it was architecturally more advanced than the "Green Column" (Rudas) Bath, flawless in its arrangement of rooms and iwans, and fell behind only because it lay further from the centre. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 33} It is said that the dead were washed with its water in Ottoman times, and Evliya Çelebi reports that Gül Baba asked to be washed with it. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 33} It is still a working thermal bath, surrounded by hospitals, hotels and pools. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 33}
