## Overview
Çinili Cami, the "Tiled Mosque", is in Üsküdar about 500 metres east of Atik Valide Camii; a small mosque complex built in 1640 by the valide sultan Kösem, mother of the sultans Murat IV and Ibrahim {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.344}.

## Architecture
The precinct opens into a delightful garden of flowers and varied trees with a quaint şadırvan; the small mosque is of the simple type — a square room under a dome — ringed on three sides by a later picturesque porch with a steep penthouse roof on small marble columns, and its minaret is baroque, its şerefe corbelled with folded-back acanthus leaves {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.344-345}.

## Tilework
Tiles cover the west façade and the whole interior — hence the name (çinili, tiled); though later than the best Iznik period, which ended by 1620, they remain beautiful in design, limited to pale blue and turquoise on a white ground or white lettering on dark blue, while the white-marble mimber has its carved decoration picked out in gold, red and green {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.345}. At the apex of the külliye's triangular plot stands the darülhadis, and there is a handsome large mektep with a çeşme {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.345-346}.
