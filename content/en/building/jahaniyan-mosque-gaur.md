## Overview
The Jahaniyan mosque stands a little south of the tomb of Akhi Siraj al-Din at Gaur and is popularly called the Jan-Jan or Jhan-Jhania mosque; the author takes the name to be a corruption of the saint for whom it was built, Makhdum Jahaniyan Jahangasht, said to have brought from Arabia the footprint over which the [[building/qadam-rasul-mosque-gaur|Qadam Rasul]] was raised. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 141} It is one of the last buildings of the Husain Shahi period at Gaur. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 141}

## Inscription and patron
Over its middle doorway a tughra inscription opens with the hadith on building mosques and records the building of this congregational mosque in the reign of Sultan Ghiyath al-Dunya wa al-Din Abu al-Muzaffar Mahmud Shah, son of Husain Shah, by "Bibi Malti", in AH 941 (1535). {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 142} Abid Ali relates that Malati or Malti was a maidservant of Sultan Nusrat Shah called in the harem "Bua Malti", elder sister Malti, that the suburb name Guamalti may derive from her, and that she probably held a high position in the royal family and also provided a drinking-water shed at Chalispara, Old Malda, in AH 938 (1532). {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 142} The author notes how rare buildings by women of the court are. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 120}

## The building
It is an oblong brick building 56 by 42 feet, 40 feet by 26 feet 3 inches inside, buttressed by octagonal corner towers topped by small cupolas on lotus-petal bases, and entered by three arches on the east. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 143} Brick mouldings divide its facade into four panels with ornamental niches bearing the bell-and-chain motif — "verging on flamboyancy", in Dani's words; inside, two aisles lie under six domes on stone pillars, their lotus finials showing, for the author, Mughal influence, with three carved mihrabs. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 143}
