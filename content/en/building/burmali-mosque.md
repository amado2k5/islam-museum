## Summary
Burmalı Camii is a small mosque just north of the Şehzade complex on the Third Hill, built about 1550 by the kadı of Egypt, Emin Nurettin Osman Efendi, and restored in the early 1970s {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.256-257}. Though of the simplest kind — a square room with a flat wooden ceiling — it has peculiarities all its own {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.257}.

## The spiral minaret
Most notable is the brick minaret with spiral ribs from which the mosque takes its name (burmalı = spiral): unique in Istanbul and a late survival of an older tradition found at Amasya, Antalya and elsewhere {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.257}. The pitched (not domed) porch rests on four columns with Byzantine Corinthian capitals — reused ancient capitals being very rare in Istanbul; restorer Bayan Cahide Tamer found four matching capitals in the Archaeological Museum to replace the decayed originals {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.257}. The entrance portal sits on the right rather than in the middle, for no evident reason {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.257-258}.

## For young visitors
This little mosque's minaret twists like a screw — the only one of its kind in all Istanbul.
