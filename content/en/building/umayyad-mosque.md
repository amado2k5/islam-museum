## Overview
[[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|Al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]] began building the Umayyad Mosque in [[place/damascus|Damascus]] in 87 AH. Al-Suyuti counts it among the greatest things built in his reign. He reports from Ibn Abi Abla that al-Walid conquered India and al-Andalus and built the mosque of Damascus. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.367}

## Construction
Al-Sallabi says that al-Walid converted the church that stood on the site after negotiating with the Christians, spent large sums on the building, and that the construction was completed in 96 AH. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.131-132}

## The Gate of the Clocks and the talismans
Al-Umari reports that the Gate of the Clocks was so named because a clock compass was made there, by which every passing hour was known. On it were copper birds, a snake and a crow. When an hour was complete, the snake came out, the birds whistled, the crow called out, and a pebble fell into a basin. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.197-198}

He also reports that before the mosque was burned, talismans were hung from its ceiling against insects, mice, snakes, scorpions and spiders, and nothing was found in it until it burned. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.197-198}

The start date is derived from the Hijri date, and is approximate in the museum's calendar.
