## Overview
Omar Khalidi opens his essay on American mosque design with a comparison: Islam's first mosque, at Medina in 622, was a simple rectangular structure of palm logs and adobe brick, and the first purpose-built mosque in the United States, completed at Cedar Rapids, Iowa, in 1934, was a simple rectangular building of white clapboard on a cinder-block foundation, with a dome over the front door. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 4-5}

## In context
Khalidi notes that the United States has over 2,000 mosques, mostly in buildings first built for other purposes, and that of nearly 1,000 mosques and Islamic centres surveyed in the mid-1990s fewer than a hundred had been designed as mosques, the older ones not by architects. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 4-5} Such simple buildings also served as community centres, with classrooms and social halls for meetings and weddings beside a prayer room. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 4-5}
