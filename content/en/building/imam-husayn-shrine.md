## Overview
The shrine of Husayn ibn Ali at Karbala over the place of his killing in 61 AH on Ashura; a major pilgrimage centre for the Twelver Shi'a {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya} {cite:momen-1985}.

## Perspectives
- **Sunni:** the site of Husayn's martyrdom is held in esteem as Ibn Kathir records, while rulings on grave visitation differ {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya}.
- **Academic:** the shrine's growth made Karbala a Twelver pilgrimage and learning city {cite:momen-1985}.
- **Twelver:** the noblest of shrines and the destination of Ashura and Arba'in pilgrimage {cite:momen-1985}.

## Building history
The Mawsu'at al-Atabat records the fabric's chronology: Adud al-Dawla the Buyid built houses and a madrasa over the site, the Seljuk Malik Shah visited Karbala and ordered the enclosure's restoration in 479, and the standing structure is essentially Sultan Uways the Jalayirid's work of 767, completed by his sons Sultans Husayn and Ahmad in 786. Ismail the Safavid gilded the grave's surrounds and gifted twelve gold lamps in 914; Murad IV ordered the dome built; and Fath Ali Shah renewed the shrine, built the haram, replated the grave's casing in gold in 1227 and raised the Husayn and Abbas domes in 1250 — the dome rising 27 metres, clad in gold {cite:musawat-atabat}.
