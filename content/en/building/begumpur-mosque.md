## Overview
The Begumpur mosque stands off Sri Aurobindo Marg in the village of Begumpur in south Delhi, close to the building known as Bijay Mandal. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 85} Contemporary sources do not mention it and it carries no inscription, so its date is disputed: some give 1370, others 1387, and many count it among the seven mosques of [[person/khan-i-jahan-junan-shah|Khan-i Jahan Junan Shah]], vizier of [[person/firuz-shah-tughluq|Firuz Shah Tughluq]]. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 85} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 89} Agha Mehdi Hasan held that [[person/muhammad-ibn-tughluq|Muhammad ibn Tughluq]] built it, since it lies at the heart of Jahanpanah near his Hazar Sutun palace, and Ibn Battuta and Badr-i Chach say that sultan built a mosque there; the author notes that the attribution to Khan-i Jahan Tilangani is popular but undocumented, and that its features are nonetheless closer to the Firuzian mosques. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 89} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 90}

## Architecture
The mosque is a single storey on a heavy masonry plinth, about 308 by 289 feet outside, with an open central court of about 247 by 223 feet (75.29 by 67.97 metres). {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 85} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 86} Cloisters with a gateway in the middle of each run round three sides of the court; the eastern gateway, the largest, is reached by fifteen steps, and seven cells flank each gateway, forty-five rooms in all. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 86} The prayer hall façade is dominated by a broad, high pylon framing a formal iwan, flanked by tapering minarets in the manner of the Qutb Minar, which masks the dome over the square central chamber (thirty feet a side); in its west wall is a stone mihrab set in a cusped double arch between carved pilasters. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 87} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 88} The façade has 24 arched openings, the central one the highest, and the prayer hall is three bays deep under low domes. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 88} The plaster of the pylon, turrets and domes seems once to have been glazed-tiled, and traces survive, while some arch apexes carry diamond patterns of blue glazed tile. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 87} On the central chamber an inscription bears the name of God and the phrase "God is sufficient for me", one of the few religious inscriptions in Firuz Shah's mosques. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 57}

## Its place in architecture
Following R. Nath, the author holds Begumpur to be the first complete Indian mosque into which the iwan was successfully introduced, perhaps inspired by the iwan mosques of Iran, the earliest example in India of the four-iwan plan, and the start of the class of iwan (*brahatmukhi*) mosques that culminated in the Sharqi mosques of Jaunpur between about 1400 and 1480. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 87} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 88} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 89} The wings are now ruined; only the central chamber is used for prayer, and villagers have occupied the rest. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 85} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 88}
