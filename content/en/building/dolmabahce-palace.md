## Overview
Dolmabahçe Sarayı stands on the European shore of the Bosphorus three kilometres from the Golden Horn; it was the principal imperial residence during the last two-thirds of the Ottoman Empire's last century {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.411}.

## The site before the palace
Fatih laid out a royal garden here soon after the conquest and Selim I built a seaside kiosk; early in the seventeenth century Ahmet I extended the gardens by filling in the seashore, a work completed by his son Osman II, whence the name Dolmabahçe, the "filled-in garden". By the start of the nineteenth century a large imperial summer residence stood here, which Mahmut II seems to have preferred to old Topkapı {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.410-411}.

## Construction
Abdül Mecit decided to leave Topkapı altogether, and in 1844 commissioned Garabet Balyan and his son Nikoğos to replace the existing structures with a new palace in a neo-classical style; completed in 1855, it became home to the sultan and household, abandoning the four-century-old palace on the First Hill. All later sultans lived here except Abdül Hamit II, who preferred the more secluded Yıldız {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.411}.

## After the empire
With the Republic, Dolmabahçe became Atatürk's official Istanbul residence; he died there on 10 November 1938 in a seaside bedroom still furnished as it was, and the palace was then restored and opened as a museum and venue for state functions {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.411-412}.
