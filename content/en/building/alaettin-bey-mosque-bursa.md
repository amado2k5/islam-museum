## Summary
Alaettin Bey Camii is the oldest dated mosque in Bursa; a plate above the entrance records that it was erected in 1335 and restored in 1892 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.52-53}. It was built in honour of Alaettin Bey, Orhan Gazi's younger brother, who died in 1331 after serving as both first vezir and commander of the army {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.53}.

## Form
It is of the simple type Aptullah Kuran calls a single-unit mosque: a domed square structure with an added porch and minaret {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.53}. A three-bay porch on four columns with Byzantine capitals precedes a square room 8.2 metres on each side, covered by a hemispherical dome on a sixteen-sided belt of large triangles {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.53}. The minaret rises from the left of the porch and is original up to the şerefe; Kuran reckons it the earliest minaret in Ottoman architecture {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.53}.

## For young visitors
Bursa's oldest mosque was built in 1335 for Sultan Orhan's brother — and its minaret may be the oldest Ottoman minaret anywhere.
