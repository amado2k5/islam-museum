## Overview
Bursa's Ulu Cami, the Great Mosque, was built by Bayezid I in 1396-9, financed with the spoil of his victory at Nicopolis on 24 September 1396; it is the grandest of all the mosques raised in Anatolia in the two centuries before the conquest of Constantinople, demonstrating Ottoman ascendancy among the Turcoman emirates {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.42}.

## Architecture
The mosque is a rectangle 60 by 56 metres, its façade of honey-coloured limestone from Mount Olympus; its two minarets rise at the north-west and north-east corners — an inscription on the first records Bayezid I as its builder, while the second, standing free, was probably erected by Mehmet I, and the impressive main gateway too seems not to belong to the original structure {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.42}.

## Interior
Twelve great piers divide the vast interior into twenty domed bays, five in each of the four rows between entrance and mihrab; the domes rest on pendentives and rise on octagonal drums outside. The dome over the central bay of the second row originally stood open to the sky until its oculus was glazed in a modern restoration; below it is a circular şadırvan with a cascade fountain for ablutions, and the mimber is a superb work of carved walnut, among the finest in Turkey {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.42-43}.
