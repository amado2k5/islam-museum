## Summary
Hacı Özbek Camii in Iznik is the oldest surviving Ottoman mosque that still preserves most of its original fabric, dated by a building inscription to 1333 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.71}.

## Description
Its brick dome is covered with terra-cotta tiles — many original — carved to fit the spherical surface, a hallmark of early Ottoman mosques, and the mosque never had a minaret; its three-bayed porch on two marble columns with Byzantine capitals was demolished in 1959 when the street was widened {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.71}. It is of the single-unit type, a hemispherical dome on a belt of Turkish triangles; three niches pierce the qibla wall, the central one holding the plain modern mihrab {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.71}.

## For young visitors
In the Turkish town of Iznik stands the oldest Ottoman mosque still mostly original — built in 1333, its clay-tiled dome still on top.
