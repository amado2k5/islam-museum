## Summary
Murat Pasha Camii is the second-oldest of Istanbul's veziral mosques, on the Seventh Hill's eastern slope in the Lycus valley; it was built by Hass Murat Pasha — of the Byzantine imperial Palaeologues, a convert who became a vezir and favourite of the Conqueror — and an intricate Arabic inscription over the door dates it 1469 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.142}.

## Bursa style
Smaller and less elaborate than Mahmut Pasha Camii, it resembles it in general plan: a long rectangular prayer-hall divided by an arch into two domed squares, with two small side chambers on each side serving as hostels for itinerant dervishes {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.142-143}. Its five domed porch bays rest on six handsome ancient columns — two of Synetic marble, four of verd antique — and the walls alternate brick and stone {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.143}. Hass Murat Pasha died in battle in 1473 on campaign against Uzun Hasan of the White Sheep Turcomans; the külliye's medrese and large double hamam perished when the adjacent avenues were widened {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.142-143}.

## For young visitors
A minister from Byzantium's imperial family became Muslim and built this mosque in 1469 in the old Bursa style — then died in war four years later.
