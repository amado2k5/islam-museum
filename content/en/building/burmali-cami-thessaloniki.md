## Overview
The mosque known as Burmalı ("with the twisted minaret") stood in the quarter named after it at the Vardar Gate of Thessaloniki, at today's 4 Egnatia Street. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1292} A document of 1784 names its builder as Hajji Ali, son of Sultan Murad's comb-maker; the foundations archive registers it as "Burmalı Cami – Hüsrev Kethüda Camii", as does Ayverdi. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1292} Old photographs show a square building of alternating stone and brick under a single tiled dome — for the author a typical fifteenth-century mosque, on which ground he rejects the claim that it was converted from the church of St Kyriaki. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1292}

## Seizure and demolition
In a report of 22 September 1914 to the Ottoman Ministry of the Interior on the Greek government's seizure of Muslim schools and endowments, the Mufti of Thessaloniki, Ahmed bin Ali, wrote that the Burmalı mosque, though never a church, had been confiscated on a grocer's testimony and turned into one in breach of the Treaty of Athens. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1292} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1293} It survived the great fire of 1917 but was afterwards demolished and replaced by a hotel. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1292}
