## Summary
The Prophet's chamber (al-Hujra) is the house of [[person/aisha|'A'isha]] in which the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] died and was buried, and which holds the graves of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|'Umar]]. This page gathers what the books of hadith and history report on the shape of the graves, on 'A'isha's dream, and on the pulling down of the houses of the Prophet's wives and the taking of 'A'isha's chamber into the mosque. The burial itself has its own record: [[event/burial-of-the-prophet|the burial of the Prophet]]; the mosque has [[building/prophets-mosque|the Prophet's Mosque]], and the spot known as the Rawda has [[place/al-rawda-al-sharifa|al-Rawda al-Sharifa]].

## The houses of the Prophet's wives
Al-Salihi says his houses were nine, some of palm fronds plastered with mud and some of piled stones, that the rooms of Sawda and 'A'isha were built on the pattern of the mosque, and that he built the rest of the rooms as needed. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2005} He quotes al-Waqidi that 'Abd Allah ibn Yazid al-Hudhali saw the houses of the Prophet's wives when 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz pulled them down on al-Walid's order: they were of mudbrick with rooms of palm frond plastered with mud, nine houses with their rooms, from 'A'isha's house to the door next to the Prophet's door and on to the house of Asma' bint Hasan. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2005} Al-Hasan al-Basri says that in 'Uthman's caliphate, as a youth, he used to go into these houses and touch the ceilings with his hand. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2007}

## The wall around the chamber
Ibn Sa'd, as al-Salihi quotes him from 'Amr ibn Dinar and 'Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Marthad, says there was no wall around the Prophet's house in his time; the first to build one was 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, whose wall was low, and 'Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr later built it up. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2007}

## 'A'isha's dream
Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab reports that 'A'isha told her father, who was among the best at interpreting, that she had seen three moons fall into her lap; he said that if her dream was true, three of the best people on earth would be buried in her house, and when the Prophet died he said: 'A'isha, this is the best of your moons. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3389} Al-Dhahabi gives it in the Siyar with one wording that differs, falling in my room where the other has in my lap. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 140}

## The three graves
Abu Dawud reports from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: I went in to 'A'isha and said, mother, uncover for me the grave of the Messenger of God and his two companions; she uncovered for me three graves neither raised nor flat, spread with the red gravel of the courtyard. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300} {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3390} In the version of al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi, as Ibn Kathir has it, the Prophet is in front, Abu Bakr's head is between the Prophet's shoulders and 'Umar's head is at the Prophet's feet. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300} Al-Bayhaqi infers from the gravel that the graves are flat; Ibn Kathir objects that the report does not mention gravel and that a grave could be humped with gravel set in mud. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300} In al-Bukhari's report from Sufyan al-Tammar he saw the Prophet's grave humped, while al-Waqidi reports from Ja'far ibn Muhammad, from his father, that the Prophet's grave was made flat. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300}

## Pulling down the rooms and taking the chamber into the mosque
Ibn Kathir, in the events of the year 88, says al-Walid ibn 'Abd al-Malik wrote to 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz about what the ten jurists had agreed, ordering the demolition, the rebuilding of the mosque and the raising of its roofs. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 87} When they began to pull down, the nobles and notables of Banu Hashim and others cried out and wept as on the day the Prophet died; 'Umar bought the properties next to the mosque from those who agreed to sell, and took into the mosque the Prophet's chamber, 'A'isha's chamber, so that the grave came inside the mosque, its eastern side being the limit and the other rooms of the Mothers of the Believers as al-Walid ordered. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 87} Ibn Kathir reports that when they dug into the eastern wall of 'A'isha's chamber a foot appeared and they feared it was the Prophet's, until they established it was 'Umar ibn al-Khattab's. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 87} Al-Bukhari reports from 'Urwa that when the wall fell on them in al-Walid's time and they began rebuilding, a foot appeared, they were frightened and thought it the Prophet's, until 'Urwa told them it was only 'Umar's. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300} Ibn Kathir adds that Ibn 'Asakir reports from Zadhan, client of al-Furafisa, who built the mosque in the time of 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, much as al-Bukhari does, and describes the graves as Abu Dawud's report does. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300}

## The weeping and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab's objection
'Ata' al-Khurasani says he was present when al-Walid's letter was read ordering the wives' rooms pulled down, and that he never saw a day with more weeping; he heard Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab say he wished they had left them as they were, so that a young person of Medina would grow up and visitors come from the lands and see what the Messenger of God was content with in his life, which would make people less keen on boasting. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2006} Ibn Kathir reports that Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab disapproved of 'A'isha's chamber being taken into the mosque, as if he feared the grave would be made a place of prayer, and adds God knows best. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 87} 'Imran ibn Abi Anas says there were four rooms of mudbrick with frond rooms, and five of plastered fronds without rooms, with hair curtains on their doors. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2006}

## 'A'isha's instruction
Al-Bukhari reports from Hisham, from his father, from 'A'isha, that she instructed 'Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr not to bury her with them but with her fellow wives at al-Baqi', so that she would never be praised on account of it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300}

## What remains
These reports fix the three graves and their shape in the first century and tell of their inclusion in the mosque in al-Walid's time; they do not cover what happened to the chamber afterwards, which belongs to [[building/al-qubba-al-khadra|the Green Dome]] and the other records of the mosque.
