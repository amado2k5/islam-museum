## Overview
The mausoleum of Sultan [[person/moulay-ismail|Moulay Ismail]] stands at [[place/meknes|Meknes]]. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p.24}

## Its tiles
[[person/rexford-newcomb|Newcomb]] quotes Jean Gallotti: the mausoleum is wainscoted with tiles whose form and colour, especially the use of red, confirm the local tradition that Dutch captives made them; they are, in short, Delft faience tiles decorated in the Muslim style, and elsewhere in Meknes many tiles imported from Delft can be seen. Gallotti concludes that Moroccan painted tilework was influenced at its height by the Dutch school and owed its success to the many Christian captives of Moulay Ismail's reign (late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries); the manner did not outlast his reign, and mosaic remained in favour. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p.24}
