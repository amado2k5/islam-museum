## Overview
On the site of this sabil stood a small room beside the house of [[building/bir-zamzam|Zamzam]] with a pool filled from the well; in 807 AH it was turned into a vaulted pool with spouts in its wall towards al-Safa for ablution. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.41} [[person/muhammad-hamza-ismail-al-haddad|Al-Haddad]] cites al-Fasi that the place was demolished in the first ten days of Dhu al-Hijja 817 AH, because it was said some ignorant people used it to wash after relieving themselves, and a drinking sabil was built in its place, completed in Rajab 818 AH / 1415 by order of Sultan [[person/al-muayyad-shaykh|al-Mu'ayyad Shaykh]]. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.20} {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.41}

## Al-Fasi's description
A square house with three large iron grilles, each under a finely made wooden panel, one facing the Kaaba and two facing al-Safa, a basin under each window, a pool, a painted ceiling, a door towards al-Safa, a painted wooden canopy outside topped by carved stone merlons, its interior whitewashed and its exterior faced with coloured stone. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.41}

## Its importance
The author sees it as modelled on the Mamluk sabils of Egypt and, since it predates the sabil of Fayruz al-Saqi (830 AH / 1426), which Nuwaysir held the earliest three-window sabil, as pushing the beginnings of the type back before Barsbay and perhaps before al-Mu'ayyad himself; it is the earliest known three-window sabil so far. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.41} {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.42} {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.50} Its coloured, striped stonework is another of its features. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.42}
