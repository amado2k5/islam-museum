## Overview
Nuruosmaniye Camii stands on the Second Hill just east of the Covered Bazaar in Istanbul; it was begun by Mahmut I in 1748 and finished in 1755 by his brother and successor Osman, from whom it takes its name, the Mosque of the Sacred Light of Osman {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.369-370}.

## Foundation
The precinct has two entrances, one opposite the main gate of the Covered Bazaar, and its attractive outer courtyard of plane trees and horse-chestnuts is scattered with the mosque's dependencies — a medrese, a library, a türbe and a sebil {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.370}.

## Style
Freely counts it the first large Ottoman building to exemplify the new baroque style imported from Europe, and says its architect was apparently a Greek named Simeon who may have studied in France: a square room under a large dome on four wall-arches, a semicircular mihrab apse, and a curious semicircular seven-sided courtyard of nine domed bays, with a number of semicircular rather than pointed arches {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.370}. Judging its proportions awkward and ungainly, Freely pronounces the mosque "a failure, but a charming one" {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.370-371}.
