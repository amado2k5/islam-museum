## Overview
The Dar al-Hikma in [[place/cairo|Cairo]] was founded by the Fatimid caliph [[person/al-hakim-bi-amr-allah|al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah]] in 395 AH. It was the most famous of the centres of wisdom through which the Ismaili mission spread its teachings among its followers. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p. 405}

## Fatimid centres of wisdom
Centres of the mission began at al-Mahdiyya under Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi, then spread at al-Mansuriyya, and then in Cairo from the time of al-Mu'izz. They were not limited to Cairo, but extended to the main provinces and islands of the mission. They were among the institutions of the [[polity/fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid state]]. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p. 409}

## The Dar al-Ilm library
It was linked to the library of Dar al-Ilm, which held many books for reading and copying. The vizier al-Afdal closed it, and the caliph al-Amir later restored it. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim}
