## Summary
The Great Mosque of [[place/jenin|Jenin]] (Fatima Khatun Mosque) is the city's main congregational mosque, in the heart of the old town. Local tradition places it over an early Islamic mosque; the documented complex was endowed by Fatima Khatun, daughter of the Mamluk sultan Qansuh al-Ghuri and wife of Lala Mustafa Pasha, governor of Damascus, with property across twenty-six towns and villages. {cite:travel-palestine} {cite:marom-lajjun-2023}

## Architecture and history
The mosque has a prayer hall crowned by a large green dome — described as the second largest in Palestine after the [[building/dome-of-the-rock|Dome of the Rock]] — a northern portico under six small domes, a minaret and a marble-columned courtyard. The original endowment was a full civic complex with bathhouse, bakery, hospice, fountain, khan and twenty shops. Its eastern side was damaged by French bombardment in Napoleon's 1799 campaign, cracked in the 1927 earthquake, then expanded by the municipality in the 1950s; the Awqaf added a subterranean hall in 1998–1999. {cite:travel-palestine} {cite:wafa-news}
