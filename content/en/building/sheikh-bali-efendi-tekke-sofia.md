## Overview
At Salihiye (Knyazhevo) on the Pernik road out of Sofia stood the zawiya of Shaykh Bali Efendi — mosque, lodge, *imaret* and tomb — built in the sixteenth century by the Sofia qadi Alikuşçuzade Abd al-Rahman Efendi; only the tomb survives. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 156} Evliya Çelebi calls the shaykh a Bayrami, born at Sofia of the Yörük people, with seventy thousand disciples, and describes his tomb under a masonry dome in his grove, with the date 960/1553 in a verse inscription over its door. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 156}

## The shaykh and the sultan
The author notes that Bali Efendi, one of the great saints of Rumelia, joined some of Süleyman the Magnificent's campaigns to lend the army spiritual support. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 156} Evliya relates that on the Szigetvár campaign the sultan camped in the shaykh's grove hoping to visit him; the soldiers damaged the grove and the shaykh withdrew, and the sultan sent Ebussuud Efendi, to whom the shaykh foretold the fall of Szigetvár and showed signs of the sultan's coming death. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 156} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 157}

## Its fate
The *imaret*, lodge and mosque were demolished in 1879, and in 1893 the church of St Elijah was built on the mosque's site. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 157} The domed tomb Evliya described and its inscription are gone; the present tomb is a plain building of stone walls under a roof, repaired in 1897 and 1901, restored in 1943 by Abdülkadir Efendi, son of Sultan Abdülhamid, burnt in 1994 and restored again. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 157} The author says it is held sacred and visited by Christians as well as Muslims. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 157}
