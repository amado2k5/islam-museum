## Summary
The Üsküdar mosque of Mihrimah Sultan — called Iskele Camii after the ferry landing-stage it overlooks — is the first of the two mosques Sinan built for Süleyman the Magnificent's only daughter, completed early in 1548 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.246}.

## Site and plan
It rises on a terrace above the square on land reclaimed from the sea — Pierre Gilles watched the harbour being filled to make its site {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.246-247}. Its plan is unusual: the central dome is carried by three semidomes — south, east and west, none to the north — giving the mosque a trefoil form; Freely reads Sinan's career as a steady shedding of semidomes, from four at the Şehzade to two at the Süleymaniye to none at the Selimiye, and he never repeated this arrangement {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.248}. The külliye originally included a medrese, a primary school and an imaret, of which only the imaret has vanished {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.248}. The interior is dark — the reason, Freely suggests, that Mihrimah demanded floods of light in the mosque she commissioned from Sinan on the Sixth Hill in 1562 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.248}.

## For young visitors
An Ottoman princess named Mihrimah asked the greatest Ottoman architect to build her two mosques: this one above the ferry landing in Üsküdar in 1548, and a brighter one years later on a hill across the water.
