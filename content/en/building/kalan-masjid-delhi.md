## Overview
The Kalan ("great") Masjid stands near Turkman Gate in Old Delhi; it is also called the Kali Masjid, which has caused confusion with the [[building/kali-masjid-nizamuddin|Kali Masjid at Nizamuddin]]. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 80} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 81} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 82} It is one of the seven mosques attributed to [[person/khan-i-jahan-junan-shah|Khan-i Jahan Junan Shah]], vizier of [[person/firuz-shah-tughluq|Firuz Shah Tughluq]], and the inscription on its east gate, published by Zafar Hasan, names it the Kalan Masjid and dates its completion to 10 Jumada II 789, which the author gives as 28 June 1387. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 80} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 81} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 82} It seems to have served the northern population of Firuzabad and was later taken inside Shah Jahan's city of Shahjahanabad. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 82}

## Architecture
The mosque has an open court surrounded by arcades, the single-court plan R. Nath calls *ekangana*. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 81} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 83} It stands on a full basement storey, its east entrance reached by twenty-eight steps; tapering turrets flank the gate, conical bastions hold the corners, and the battered outer walls give it the air of a fortress towering over its surroundings. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 83} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 84} Single-aisle cloisters of square bays roofed with cupolas run round three sides of the court, with four arches on the north and south and five on the east. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 83} The prayer hall on the west has fifteen bays, three deep and five wide, behind a five-arched façade Nath calls *panchmukhi*; each bay carries a cupola on the Iranian *chahar taq* system, with no larger dome above, and stone *chhajjas* on brackets protect the arches. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 83} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 84} The pulpit has six steps instead of the usual three and has been recently faced with white marble, and chambers attached to the qibla wall, which the author thinks unique, may have served as stores or staff quarters. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 84} No minarets were added. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 82}

## Its state
The mosque has been renovated, whitewashed inside and redecorated, and is still in use; its interior was probably once painted stucco, and its cupolas may have been glazed-tiled. {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 57} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 58} {cite:seema-khan-mosque-architecture-firuzshah-tughlaq-2011 p. 84}
