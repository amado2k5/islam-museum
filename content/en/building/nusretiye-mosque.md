## Summary
Nusretiye Camii stands on the European shore of the lower Bosphorus about 200 metres beyond Kılıç Ali Pasha Camii; it was built for Mahmut II in 1822–26 by Kirkor Balyan, founder of the Armenian architectural family that served the Ottoman sultans through much of the nineteenth century {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.413}.

## 'Divine Victory'
The mosque's name means Divine Victory, dedicated by Mahmut II just after his 1826 annihilation of the janissaries — the 'Auspicious Event' of Ottoman history that freed him to create a new army and reform the empire {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.413}. Paris-trained Balyan blended baroque and Empire motifs — highly un-Turkish, as Eyice remarks, but not without charm and bravura {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.413-414}. The mosque abandons the traditional monumental courtyard: a series of palace-like apartments in two stories forms its western façade, a signature of later Balyan mosques, and its minarets proved so slender they collapsed soon after construction and had to be re-erected {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.414}.

## For young visitors
After abolishing the janissaries in 1826, Sultan Mahmut II built a "Victory" mosque on the Bosphorus — it looks more like a European palace than an old mosque.
