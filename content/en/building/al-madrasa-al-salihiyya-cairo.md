## Overview
The Salihiyya madrasas between the two palaces in Cairo were built by al-Malik al-Salih Najm al-Din Ayyub, who set four teaching posts there for the Shafi'i, Hanafi, Maliki and Hanbali schools {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1912}.

## Where they stood
Al-Maqrizi names one Salihiyya madrasa for the Hanafis and Hanbalis, and another for the Shafi'is and Malikis. The dome of al-Salih stood beside the second {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1061} {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1062}.

## The madrasa in Yeomans
Yeomans dates the Salihiyya to 1242 and counts it the first madrasa in Egypt to teach all four schools of law, and the first tomb in Cairo, after al-Shafi'i's, to form part of a madrasa complex. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.115} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} Its remains stand on al-Mu'izz street opposite the Qalawun complex; al-Maqrizi says [[person/al-malik-al-salih-ayyub|al-Salih]] pulled down part of the great eastern palace to build it. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} It was really two wings of one institution divided by a public passage, set at an angle behind the street front to face Mecca; each wing had a courtyard overlooked on two sides by two storeys of student rooms and on the other two by [[concept/iwan|iwans]]; the northern wing served the Malikis and Shafi'is, the southern the Hanafis and Hanbalis. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} Yeomans says it was not the first madrasa to bring the iwan courtyard to Egypt, but it is the earliest surviving one and its composition shaped Cairo's later architecture. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116}

## Minaret and façade
The minaret, of the mabkhara type, stands right over the entrance passage: a rectangular shaft with three tall keel-arched recesses with ribbed hoods on each side, an octagonal shaft pierced with lobed windows, and two bands of muqarnas under a ribbed helmet. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} The door has a lintel of joggled voussoirs, and above it a keel-arched panel of radiating muqarnas frames the Naskhi foundation inscription, a composition Yeomans ranks with the al-Aqmar façade. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.117} Of the southern wing only the façade survives, and of the northern only the north-west iwan with its original vault; the qibla iwan had three mihrabs. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.117}

## The tomb of al-Salih
His widow [[person/shajar-al-durr|Shajar al-Durr]] added his mausoleum in 1249, creating a funerary madrasa and setting the practice of burying the founder in his or her religious foundation. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.118} It is a domed cube projecting from the street front, its dome profile like al-Shafi'i's on a stepped octagonal zone of transition; inside survives much original Ayyubid woodwork in the cenotaph, doors and panels, and the mihrab is flanked by green columns. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.118}
