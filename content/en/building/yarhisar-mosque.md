## Summary
"Yarhisar Camii, on the slope of the Fourth Hill toward the Golden Horn, is probably the second-oldest mosque in the city, apparently predated only by Sağrıcılar Camii. According to the Register of Pious Foundations it was built in 1461; its founder Musliheddin Efendi was Judge of Istanbul in Fatih's reign." {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.151}

'It was once a handsome edifice built entirely of ashlar, its square chamber domed on pendentives and preceded by a porch of two domes and three columns. Badly damaged in the great fire of 1917 which consumed most of the district, it was restored in 1954–6 with a thin brick-and-stone veneer, à la Byzantine, covering the original structure — an unfortunate restoration that obscures what was still attractive and is untrue to the spirit of the building.' {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.151}
