## Overview
Mihrimah Sultan Camii stands on the summit of the Sixth Hill just inside the Edirne Gate, dominating the view for miles; Sinan built it between 1562 and 1565 for Princess Mihrimah, daughter of Süleyman the Magnificent {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.269}.

## The külliye and its fate
Besides the mosque the külliye included a medrese, a mektep, a türbe, a hamam and a long row of shops in the terrace substructure. It was severely damaged by the earthquakes of 1766 and 1894 — each time the mosque was restored while the other buildings were mostly neglected — and was damaged again in 1999, then repaired {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.269-270}.

## Architecture
The courtyard is ringed on three sides by the medrese's porticoes and cells with a şadırvan at its centre, and the mosque is preceded by an imposing porch of seven domed bays on eight marble and granite columns, before which stood a second, now vanished porch {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.270-271}. The square prayer hall is crowned by a dome 20 metres in diameter and 37 metres high, with triple arcades on granite columns east and west; its 204 windows flood it with light, unlike Mihrimah's dim Üsküdar mosque. It has a single minaret and no sultan's loge, curious for an imperial foundation {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.271-272}.

## Tomb and hamam
South-west of the mosque a small graveyard holds the türbe of the grand vezir Semiz Ali Pasha attached to a mektep — Mihrimah herself is buried with her father at the Süleymaniye and her husband Rüstem Pasha at Şehzade Camii — while a restored double hamam, with identical baths for men and women, stands south-east of the mosque {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.272}.
