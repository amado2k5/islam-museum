## Overview
The Vezir Mosque in the centre of Candia (Heraklion) in Crete, which passed to the Ottomans in 1669, was built in 1678 by the conqueror of its fortress, the grand vizier Köprülü Fazıl Ahmed Pasha; a two-balcony minaret was added in 1685. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1042} Around it were a fountain, an ablution fountain, a madrasa, a graveyard, a kitchen and storehouses; some of the fine tombstones of its graveyard are now shown in the Historical Museum of Crete. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1042}

## Rebuilding and conversion
It was entirely destroyed by the earthquake of 1856 and rebuilt through the efforts of the grand vizier Ali Pasha by the architect Athanasios Mousis from 1869, opening in 1871. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1042} It had no foundation inscription, but a repair inscription in thuluth by the calligrapher Mustafa İrfani Efendi, with the dates 1288 and 1311, is known; whether it survives is not known. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1042} In 1924 it was turned into the church of St Titus. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1042}
