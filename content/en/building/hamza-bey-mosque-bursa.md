## Summary
Hamza Bey Camii was founded in the mid-fifteenth century by Hamza Bey, who held important posts under both Murat II and Mehmet II and died in 1461 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.66}. A five-bayed porch precedes it, the dome of the central bay larger than the rest, and the minaret rises from the north-east corner {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.66}.

## The two tombs and the plan
At the south-east corner a domed square türbe holds the sarcophagi of Hamza Bey's wife and two daughters, while the octagonal structure in front is Hamza Bey's own tomb {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.66}. The mosque is a cross-axial eyvan type of four units: central hall and main eyvan both domed at 8.5 metres — the first on stalactited squinches, the second on a belt of Turkish triangles — with side eyvans opening through Bursa arches {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.66-67}.

## For young visitors
A 15th-century vezir's mosque keeps him, his wife and his daughters resting in two tombs beside its walls.
