## Overview
The Tomb of Gül Baba stands on a hill in Buda (Budapest), Hungary; it was built by Yahyâ Pashazâde Mehmed Pasha, governor of Buda in 1543-48, in the classical Ottoman style — an octagonal domed structure {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 pp.26-27}.

## The man
Gül Baba was a Bektashi dervish of the Veli Baba Lodge in Isparta who joined the Buda campaign at Süleyman the Magnificent's invitation and died on 2 September 1541 after the first Friday prayer in Buda; Süleyman carried his coffin, the Rumelian chief judge Ebussuud Efendi led the funeral prayer, and he was buried on the hill where the tomb now stands {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 pp.26-27}.

## Fate
A large Bektashi lodge erected beside the tomb was demolished after Ottoman rule in Buda ended, while the tomb itself, converted into a chapel, thereby survived; it became a tomb again after Sultan Abdülaziz's 1867 visit to Europe, was renovated in 1996 and 2018 by joint Turkish-Hungarian expert teams, and remains a symbol of Turkish-Hungarian friendship {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p.27}.
