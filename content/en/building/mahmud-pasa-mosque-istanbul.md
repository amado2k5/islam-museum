## Summary
Mahmut Pasha Camii is the earliest of Istanbul's three veziral mosques from Mehmet the Conqueror's time, in the market quarter on the Second Hill's shore; the grand vezir Mahmut Pasha built it in 1462, nine years after the Conquest, and it is one of only two 'Bursa style' mosques in the city {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.138-139}.

## The Bursa style
A five-bayed porch — its original columns replaced by ungainly baroque octagonal piers in restoration — leads through a portal into a narthex, a rare feature found only here and in Beyazit II's mosque {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.139}. The plan closely resembles Bursa's Hüdavendigâr: a rectangular hall divided by an arch into two square chambers under equal domes, flanked by barrel-vaulted passages giving access to three small dervish-hospice rooms on each side {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.139}. Behind the mosque in a small graveyard stands the founder's octagonal türbe, dated 1474 — the year Mahmut Pasha was executed — its exterior encased in blue and turquoise tile mosaic {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.140}. The Conqueror put his vezir to death in 1474, holding him responsible for the death of his son Prince Mustafa; Freely quotes the contemporary historian Kritovoulos's glowing portrait of the man {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.138-139}.

## For young visitors
Just nine years after conquering Istanbul, the sultan's chief minister built a mosque in the old Bursa style — it still stands in the market quarter today.
