## Overview
The Öziceli Hacı İbrahim mosque is built on the town wall at the Small Gate on the Danube, in the same street as the Mahkeme mosque; it is a rectangle with a stone wall, no dome and a wooden ceiling, and a minaret of cut stone at its north-east corner of which only the base remains. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 87} Evliya Çelebi describes it as a beautiful new mosque with a lead roof, painted ceiling and large congregation and a tall masonry minaret, with a bath, school, madrasa and lodge beside it, none of which survive. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 87} Nothing is known of its founder; Özice (Užice) is a town in western Serbia on the Bosnian border. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 87}

## History
Built in the seventeenth century, it served as a warehouse in the mid-eighteenth, then as a dwelling, and after its latest restoration in 2007 became a local history museum with old engravings of Esztergom and Turkish weapons; its seventeenth-century muqarnas mihrab survives. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 87}
