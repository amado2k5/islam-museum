## Overview
Laleli Cami, the "Lily Mosque", stands on the western side of the Third Hill in Istanbul; the second of the city's larger baroque buildings, it was founded by Mustafa III and built between 1759 and 1763 by Mehmet Tahir Ağa, whom Freely calls the greatest and most original of the Turkish baroque architects {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.371-372}.

## Architecture
The mosque rises on a high terrace above a labyrinth of winding passages and vaulted shops; at the centre of the substructure, directly under the mosque, a great hall is roofed on eight enormous piers with a fountain ringed by shops and cafés — a tour de force, Freely says, by which Mehmet Tahir showed he could support his mosque virtually on nothing {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.372}. The mosque is of brick and stone below and stone only in the superstructure {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.372}.

## Interior
The plan is an octagon inscribed in a rectangle, all but the north pair of supporting columns engaged in the walls; the walls are heavily revetted in variegated yellow, red and blue marbles, the north gallery wall carries opus sectile medallions of rare marbles and semi-precious stones — onyx, jasper, lapis lazuli — while a rectangular apse holds a sumptuous marble mihrab, a mimber of the same materials, and a Kuran kürsü of wood inlaid with mother-of-pearl {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.372-373}.
