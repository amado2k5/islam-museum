## Summary
The Medrese of Rüstem Pasha was built by grand vezir Rüstem Pasha on the slope of the Second Hill leading down to the Golden Horn; Sinan designed it and its historic inscription records completion in A.H. 957 (1550–1) {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.256}.

## A unique plan
Its plan is unique for a medrese: the courtyard is octagonal, ringed by a columned portico of twenty-four domes with a şadırvan, and the cells behind are octagonally arranged too — but the exterior is made square by filling the corners with auxiliary rooms including baths and lavatories; the lecture hall is a large domed room projecting from the square like a great apse {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.256}.

## For young visitors
Sinan's only octagonal-courtyard college — a square box hiding a perfect octagon inside.
