## Overview
The Khair al-Manazil stands on the west side of the [[building/purana-qila-delhi|Purana Qila]] opposite its Bara Darwaza, on the northern edge of Delhi's great necropolis, beside the Lal Darwaza also called Sher Shah's Gate. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 237} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 140} It is a fully developed mosque: a large courtyard at the centre, a three-bay prayer hall on the west, two-storeyed cloisters on the sides that once housed a madrasa, and a monumental red sandstone gateway on the east, on the single-court plan. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 237} Though the prayer hall is technically divided into five bays, its facade has only three arches, so it belongs to the three-arched type. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 237}

## Architecture and ornament
The prayer hall is of rubble and plaster; stucco medallions in its spandrels bear the words *Allah*, *Ya Allah*, *Muhammad*, *Ya Fattah*, *al-Mulk lillah* and the shahada in fine naskh. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 237} The frame of the central arch has a band of Qur'anic inscriptions in stucco inlaid with coloured tile, of which only the blue border survives; the spandrels still carry yellow, green and turquoise tiles that once covered the whole facade, and the mihrabs were finished in stucco, paint and tile. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 238} A dome covers the central bay, with nothing else on the roof. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 238}

## Patron and date
The Persian inscription above the central mihrab records its construction by Maham Anaga, Akbar's wet-nurse and mother of [[person/adham-khan|Adham Khan]], in AH 969/1561-62 under the superintendence of Shihab al-Din Ahmad Khan; the chronogram lies in the words "Khair al-Manazil", the best of houses. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 238} Nath notes that Shah Jahan chose this three-arched type, reduced and refined, for his private white marble mosques: the Mina and Nagina mosques in the Agra fort and the Moti Masjid in the Red Fort at Delhi. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 238}
