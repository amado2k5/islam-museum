## Overview
Sokollu Mustafa Pasha, governor of Buda for twelve years, restored the ruinous mosque of İbrahim Çavuş in the tanners' quarter (today Tabán), and it took his name after the repair. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 41} Evliya Çelebi visited it and noted its large congregation, its stone building, lead roof and tall minaret; engravings of the 1686 siege show it within courtyard walls, domed, with a single-balcony minaret. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 41}

## Conversion
After Ottoman rule ended it was rebuilt in 1728-36 by the architect Keresztély Obergruber as the church of St Catherine of Alexandria; nothing of the mosque remains, but the church entrance faces the qibla and a plate at the door records its former use, while the bell tower was added by Máté Nepauer in 1753. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 41} A Qur'an that Sokollu Mustafa Pasha endowed to the mosque is in the manuscripts department of Leipzig University Library (B.or.007), its last page bearing his endowment note of 1574. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 41}
