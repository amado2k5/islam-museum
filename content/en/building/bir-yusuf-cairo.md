## Overview
Joseph's Well, in the western enclosure of the [[building/citadel-of-cairo|Cairo Citadel]], is attributed to [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din]], though Yeomans thinks it probably existed in Fatimid times and was enlarged by him; it is in any case the only possible work of his on the western side of the Citadel. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.107} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.108}

## The structure
The double-shafted well is cut 87 metres through the limestone to the water table, a remarkable piece of hydraulic engineering in Yeomans's view. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.108} Ibn Abd al-Zahir (1223-1292) described cattle turning at the top to raise water from a reservoir halfway down, where other cattle raised it from the depths, with a path for the cattle down to the spring, all cut in the rock with no masonry. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.108} The spiral ramp down which the cattle went was lit from the inner shaft by pairs of windows. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.108}

## The Citadel's water
It was not the only source: there were other wells and cisterns on the Citadel, and Behrens-Abouseif notes that Salah al-Din built an aqueduct that carried and raised Nile water with waterwheels. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.108}
