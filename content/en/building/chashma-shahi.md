## Overview
The Chashma Shahi ("Royal Spring") is a small garden on the mountainside above Lake Dal, built round a renowned spring; [[person/john-brookes|Brookes]]'s plan dates it to 1632 and attributes it to [[person/shah-jahan-mughal|Shah Jahan]]. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.146} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.147}

## Water and terraces
The spring rose through a marble lotus basin in a pavilion at the top of the garden, the private zenana garden, flowed to a regular tank with a single jet, then into a central pavilion (now removed) to drop at once down a steep narrow chadar into a fountained pool in the lowest garden, the water descending over three levels in all. Brookes judges that without the central pavilion the garden has lost its point. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.146} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.147} Above it on the mountain stands the [[building/pari-mahal-srinagar|Pari Mahal]]. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.157}
