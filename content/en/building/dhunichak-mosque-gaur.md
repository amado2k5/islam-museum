## Overview
The Dhunichak mosque stands in the Mahdipur area of Gaur and takes its name from the quarter inhabited by the *dhunis*, cotton carders. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 126} Lambourne ascribed it to Dhanpat Saudagar, brother of Chand Saudagar, under Husain Shah; the author rejects this, since the site does not appear in Cunningham's city plan and the mosque's style belongs to the restored Ilyas Shahi dynasty. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 126}

## Description and place
Ilahi Bakhsh says it had three domes and that he saw a small ruined building north of it, perhaps the tomb of the builder and his family; the List of Ancient Monuments of 1887 records an old front with 31 columns and an interior of carved and coloured bricks. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 126} For the author it is the first example of the single-aisled oblong type, and probably the prototype of the three-domed Mughal mosques of Sherpur in Bogra, such as the Kherua mosque (AH 989/1582), and of the seventeenth-century mosques of Dacca. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 126} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 127}
