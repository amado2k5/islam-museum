## Overview
Cafer Ağa Medresesi stands near Haghia Sophia in Istanbul; Sinan built it for Cafer Ağa, chief white eunuch of Topkapı Sarayı under Süleyman the Magnificent, and it was completed by his younger brother Gazanfer Ağa, who succeeded him in office after his death in 1557 — its inscription dates completion to AH 967 (1559-60) {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.299}.

## The founder
Cafer Ağa and Gazanfer Ağa were Italians from Chioggia, south of Venice, captured by pirates in their youth, sold in the Istanbul slave market and castrated to serve as eunuchs in the Saray {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.299}.

## Architecture
The building is interesting for being raised on high vaulted substructures used as shops along Alemdar Caddesi, necessary because the hill slopes steeply up to the level of Haghia Sophia and the substructures provide enough level area for the courtyard; the medrese cells open off three sides of the porticoed court, with the large dershane in the middle of the fourth {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.299}.
