## Overview
The Ram Bagh, originally the Aram Bagh or "Garden of Rest", lies across the Yamuna from the Red Fort at [[place/agra|Agra]]. A photo caption in [[person/john-brookes|Brookes]] dates it to 1526, and he thinks it almost certainly the earliest Mughal garden to survive in recognisable form, though much altered. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.128} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.129}

## Babur's first garden at Agra
[[person/babur-mughal|Babur]] wrote that a great defect of Hindustan was its lack of running water, and that he meant to make water flow by wheels wherever he settled and to lay out grounds in an orderly, symmetrical way. The site of his first Agra garden is uncertain: tradition gives the name Chahar Bagh to a site at a bend of the river, but Brookes thinks the real site more probably the Ram Bagh. Its pattern is one of geometric walks with raised platforms for viewing the garden, and it may be here that Babur received the descendants of Timur and Chinggis Khan in 1528. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.128} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.129} It was well kept in the reign of [[person/jahangir-mughal|Jahangir]], who praised it. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.130}

## Its names
[[person/syed-ali-nadeem-rezavi|Rezavi]] adds that it is also known as the Bagh-i Gul Afshan, and that [[person/nur-jahan|Nur Jahan]] renovated it, after which it was called the Bagh-i Nur Afshan. {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-indian-architecture-2002 p.130}
