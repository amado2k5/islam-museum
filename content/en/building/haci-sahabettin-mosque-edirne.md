## Summary
Hacı Şahabettin Camii — Kirazlı Cami, 'the Cherry Mosque' — is inscription-dated 1436; its founder Hacı Şahabettin Pasha served as vezir and beylerbeyi of Rumeli under Murat II and Mehmet II, commanding armies at Varna in 1444 and the siege of Constantinople in 1453 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.97}.

## Form
A single-unit mosque under an eight-metre dome on pendentives carried on an octagonal drum; its two-bayed porch has vanished and the minaret rises at the north-east {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.97}. Its curious feature is a double mihrab: one inside, and a second on the porch set in a moulded stalactite niche {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.97}.

## For young visitors
A commander who fought at Varna and the conquest of Constantinople built it in 1436 — with two mihrabs, one indoors and one out.
