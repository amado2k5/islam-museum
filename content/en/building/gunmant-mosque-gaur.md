## Overview
Ilahi Bakhsh's *Khurshid-i Jahan Numa* describes this mosque as the "Gunmat Mosque"; it lies between the Kotwali Gate and the village of Mahdipur on the east bank of the Bhagirathi at Gaur, and later historians call it the Gunmant Masjid. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 98} The author finds it unmistakably like the [[building/adina-mosque-pandua|Adina mosque]] at Hazrat Pandua, built about a century earlier. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 98}

## The building
It is a rectangle on a north-south axis, 158 by 99 feet outside, with a vaulted central nave 51 feet long and 16 feet 10 inches wide leading from the courtyard to the central mihrab; on each side is a prayer hall whose brick arches, on square stone pillars, carry twelve small domes over brick stalactite pendentives. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 98} It is entered from the east by nine arches; the qibla wall of each wing has four mihrabs, there is no buttress behind the central mihrab as at Adina, and Cunningham noted traces of a corridor along the whole front. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 99}

## Date
Its date is uncertain: Ilahi Bakhsh names neither builder nor date, and King in 1875 called it a large stone building without inscription; Cunningham, however, assigned to it an inscription dated AH 889, which the author finds more refined than the other Mahdipur inscriptions, and which opens with the basmala, very rare in Bengal epigraphy. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 99} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 103}
