## Summary
The Marib Dam is the dam whose construction began in the time of the Mukarribs of [[polity/kingdom-of-saba|Saba]], with their capital at [[place/sirwah|Sirwah]], in the centuries before 650 BCE. It holds a great place in the history of [[place/yemen|Yemen]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}.

## The collapse
After Yemen gained its independence from the [[event/first-abyssinian-occupation-of-yemen|first Abyssinian occupation]], breaches began to appear in the Marib Dam. Then came the great flood, which the Qur'an refers to as the flood of al-Aram, in 450 or 451 CE. It was a major event that led to the ruin of settlements and the scattering of peoples {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}. See [[event/flood-of-al-aram|the flood of al-Aram]] and [[passage/quran-34-16|Saba 16]].

## Notes for researchers
The dates are the author's estimates, and he notes that the sources differ in fixing the years {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}.

## For young visitors
The Marib Dam was a great water barrier in ancient Yemen. One day it broke, and a great torrent flowed out that scattered many tribes.
