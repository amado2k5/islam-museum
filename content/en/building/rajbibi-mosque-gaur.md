## Overview
The Rajbibi mosque is a small, elegant building at Gaur that follows the [[building/chamkatti-mosque-gaur|Chamkatti mosque]] in plan and detail; according to Ilahi Bakhsh it lies south-east of the Kotwali Gate, east of the high road between two tanks. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 117} Its name means "Royal Lady", and the author thinks it was built by a prominent woman of the sultans' harem. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 118} Abid Ali confused it with the Dhunichak mosque and Dani described it as the Khania Dighi mosque, a name the author finds no basis for. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 118}

## Building and date
Abid Ali and Dani give its size as 62 by 42 feet, with a central chamber 28 feet square; its pointed dome is still visible though ruined, the qibla wall has three mihrabs, and it is entered by three arches on the east and a door on each side. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 118} The southern cornice keeps three rows of decoration — a chain below and niches with flowers above — in the scheme first seen at the Eklakhi tomb. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 118} It has no inscription; since it cannot be earlier than its model Chamkatti, the author places it in the restored Ilyas Shahi period, AH 841-893 (1437-87). {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 118} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 119}
