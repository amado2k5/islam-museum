## Summary
Eski Cami, "the Old Mosque," is the earliest of the three imperial foundations in central Edirne; it was begun in 1403 by Çelebi Süleyman, son of Beyazit I, and completed by his brother Mehmet I after the war of succession, as its inscriptions record along with the builder Ömer ibn Ibrahim and the architect Hacı Alaettin of Konya {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.80}.

## Nine domes
The mosque is a perfect square 49.5 metres on each side, divided into nine equal bays roofed by nine domes carried on four massive square piers; the three central-axis domes use different transitions — squinches, stalactites and Turkish triangles — placing it in the early Turkish "Great Mosque" type whose first Ottoman example was Beyazit I's Ulu Cami in Bursa {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.81}. Damaged by fire and by the earthquake of 1752, it was repaired by Mahmut I in 1753–4 and restored again in 1924–34 and 1995; beside it stands the bedesten that Mehmet I built immediately after the mosque as its endowed market {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.80-82}.

## For young visitors
The Old Mosque of Edirne — the city's oldest imperial mosque — has nine domes over four great piers, and was begun by one brother and finished by another after they had fought over the throne.
