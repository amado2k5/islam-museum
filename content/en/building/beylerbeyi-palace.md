## Overview
Beylerbey Sarayı stands on the Asian shore of the Bosphorus just above the first Bosphorus Bridge; it was built for Abdül Aziz in 1861-5 by Sarkis Balyan, brother of Nikoğos Balyan, the architect of Dolmabahçe {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.414-415}.

## History
The village was named after Beylerbey Mehmet Pasha, governor of Rumelia under Murat III, who had built a yalı here that was demolished for the palace; Beylerbey served mainly as a summer residence and a house for visiting royalty — beginning with the Empress Eugénie of France, then Franz Joseph of Austria, Shah Nasireddin of Persia and Edward VIII of England. Abdül Hamit II lived here after returning from exile and died in the palace in 1918; it has been splendidly restored and is now a museum {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.415}.

## Structure
The three-storey palace divides into the usual selamlık and harem: kitchens and storage on the ground floor, staterooms and imperial apartments on the two floors above — 26 rooms with six grand salons, the grandest the Yellow Pavilion and the Marble Pavilion centred on a pool with a cascade fountain. Its furnishing rivals Dolmabahçe: Hereke carpets, Bohemian crystal chandeliers, French clocks, vases from China, Japan, France and the Yıldız factory, and paintings by European artists, notably Aivazovski {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.415}.
