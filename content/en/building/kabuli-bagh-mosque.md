## Overview
The Kabuli Bagh Mosque at Panipat was built by Babur around 1527. [[person/syed-ali-nadeem-rezavi|Syed Ali Nadeem Rezavi]] treats it as the earliest Mughal example of the [[concept/chahartaq|chahartaq]], which covers its central nave. {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-indian-architecture-2002 p.134}

## Features
The mosque shows the "arch-net" worked as pseudo-structural plaster relief on the pendentives of the small domes of the side bays, a feature found again in Humayun's Kachhpura Mosque at Agra and later in the central dome of [[building/humayun-tomb|Humayun's tomb]]. {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-indian-architecture-2002 p.133}

## Sharqi or Timurid?
Some scholars have compared the facades of Babur's mosques, Kabuli Bagh among them, with those of the Atala Masjid and the Jami' Masjid at Jaunpur and traced them to Sharqi architecture. Rezavi argues from the plans that the Kabuli Bagh and Kachhpura mosques closely resemble the Namazgah Mosque at Qarshi, southwest of Samarqand: a high pishtaq, a chahartaq nave and lower side wings with four domed bays each. He recalls that Babur knew Samarqand and Bukhara before coming to India and had brought with him master masons trained in Timurid traditions. {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-indian-architecture-2002 p.135} {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-indian-architecture-2002 p.136}
