## Overview
The Ka'ba: the House whose foundations Abraham and Isma'il raised; its guardianship passed to Isma'il's descendants, then Jurhum, then Khuza'a, then Quraysh.

## The story
Abraham settled Hagar and her child in a valley without cultivation at God's Sacred House — which then was but a rise in the ground like a hillock, the floods coming to it and washing past its right and left {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. On his third journey he and Isma'il built the Ka'ba and raised its foundations {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

Isma'il held Mecca's leadership and the House's charge all his life; then it passed to Jurhum for some twenty centuries, then to Khuza'a for three hundred years, then to Qusayy and Quraysh from about 440 CE {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab}. The polytheists filled the Sacred Mosque with idols; when the Prophet conquered Mecca he found three hundred and sixty idols around the House and ordered them brought out and burned {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
The Ka'ba is God's House at Mecca; Abraham and his son Isma'il built it, and people kept making pilgrimage to it across the ages.
