## Overview
The mashhad of Sayyida Ruqayya in Cairo is the most impressive of the Alid shrines built or restored in the Fatimid period; Yeomans dates it 1133-1153. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.65} Ruqayya, he writes, was a daughter of Ali ibn Abi Talib not by Fatima, who according to one tradition came to Egypt with her sister Sayyida Zaynab. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.65}

## Tomb or memorial?
Inscriptions on the cenotaph identify it as her tomb, but other traditions place her burial in Damascus, which would make the building a memorial shrine raised after a vision rather than a tomb. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.65} Yeomans favours this explanation, citing Caroline Williams's observation that dreams, visions and miracles often provided the pretext for building mashhads. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.65}

## The setting of Fatimid shrines
Yeomans notes that the prestige of the Fatimid lineage grew in the first half of the twelfth century through popular devotion at the tombs of the Prophet's descendants in the Southern Cemetery, many of them shrines held by visitors to carry special sanctity and blessing. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.64} He reports Jonathan Bloom's suggestion that the cults of saints descended from Ali may have been fostered by Isma'ili missionaries before the Fatimid conquest, and that women's presence in the cemeteries helped the veneration of female saints. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.64} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.65}

## The building
The mashhad is a rectangular sanctuary in three bays, with a ribbed dome over an octagonal zone of transition at the centre. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.65} Its most distinctive features are three stucco mihrabs in the qibla wall; the principal one is a masterpiece of stucco design recalling the fluted hood of the [[building/al-aqmar-mosque|al-Aqmar]] façade, but here a bold crystalline arrangement of [[concept/muqarnas|muqarnas]] frames a hood hollowed by sixteen flutes radiating from a medallion with the names of Ali and Muhammad in interlocking Kufic. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.65}
