## Overview
Kilia castle came under Ottoman rule under Bayezid II in 1484; it stood on a trade route linking the Black Sea and the Balkans with the Baltic, and Evliya Çelebi recorded in it the mosque of Sultan Bayezid Wali and seven masjids. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 824} A map of 1770 gives the mosque as 18 by 18 metres, its dome 15 metres high and its minaret 21; a map of 1794 shows it square at the centre of the castle with its minaret, and the bath of Hajji Salih on its qibla side. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 824} The earliest document the author found on it is dated 1079/1669, and the Ottoman archives hold more than twenty papers on the appointment of its imam, preacher and muezzin. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 824}

## Conversion
After the Russian occupation of 1806 it was turned into a church; a report of the Metropolitanate of Bessarabia dated 15 April 1810 records permission to make it an Orthodox church dedicated to the Virgin under the Russian Patriarchate. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 824} It was used unchanged until 1823, then remodelled in 1823-25; the drawings for that project reveal its original form: a central dome on four piers, as at the Fethiye mosque in Athens and the Sultan Ahmed mosques of Navarino and Nauplion, a single-balcony minaret at the north-west corner, a portico on wooden posts and a projecting mihrab. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 824} The remodelling removed the portico, added four-columned pedimented porches on three sides, changed the windows and put a second roof over the dome. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 824}
