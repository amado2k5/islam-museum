## Overview
Bab Zuwayla is the southern gate of Fatimid Cairo, built in 1091-1092 in [[person/badr-al-jamali|Badr al-Jamali]]'s rebuilding of the walls; the city's north-south axis, al-Mu'izz street, ends at it and at [[building/bab-al-futuh|Bab al-Futuh]]. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.45} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.49} Its name was carried over from a gate of al-Mansuriyya in Tunisia, named after the African troops. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.44}

## The structure
Like Bab al-Futuh it has large semicircular towers, solid masonry to two-thirds of their height; their smooth curved stone fronts are relieved only by arrow slits and a moulding that defines a shallow vertical panel crowned by a pointed arch. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.49} The inner flanks of the towers hold recessed panels with lobed arches in the Moorish style, which Yeomans counts the earliest of the type in Cairo. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.49} Between the towers a semicircular gate carries a gallery, and above it an open semicircular arch forms a thick barrel vault; the gallery is thought to have held musicians accompanying ceremonial processions through the gate. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.49}

The inner vestibule was once flanked by two semicircular niches, the eastern one altered by al-Mu'ayyad, and the whole is covered by a shallow dome on pendentives in the Byzantine manner. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.49}

## The minarets
The towers carry two of the finest Mamluk minarets in Cairo (1420), serving the adjacent mosque of Sultan [[person/al-muayyad-shaykh|al-Mu'ayyad Shaykh]]. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.49}
