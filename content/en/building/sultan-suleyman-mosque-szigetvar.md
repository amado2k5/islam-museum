## Overview
After the conquest of Szigetvár, [[person/sokollu-mehmed-pasha|Sokollu Mehmed Pasha]] built this mosque in 1566 in the name of Süleyman the Magnificent, who had died during the campaign; Evliya Çelebi called it the Small Mosque of Sultan Süleyman. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 137} Unlike imperial mosques it is a modest rectangle with a wooden roof and no dome: a rectangular prayer hall, an L-shaped portico on the east and north, and a brick minaret at the north-east corner. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 137}

## History
After Szigetvár surrendered to the Austrians in 1689, General de Vecchi had its interior dug up in a search and then lived in it, and the minaret was cut down to its base; after his death in 1702 it became a military hospital, a granary in the eighteenth century, a church from 1760 to 1770, then a stable and a storehouse, until a 1963 restoration cleared its additions. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 137} It is now a local history museum, notable for the sixteenth-century wooden steps of its minaret and protected Ottoman inscriptions on its walls. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 137}
