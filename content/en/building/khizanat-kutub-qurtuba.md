## Overview
The library of sciences and books in the House of the Umayyads in [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]]. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari pp. 421–422}

## In the time of al-Hakam al-Mustansir
In his days it reached a size no king's library before him had gathered. Its catalogue was forty-four volumes, each with twenty leaves, listing nothing but the titles of the books. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari pp. 421–422}

He sent men from among the merchants with money to the provinces to buy books, until he brought in more than the Andalusians had ever known. In his house he gathered the skilled copyists, proof-readers and binders. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari pp. 421–422}

## Its fate
Most of the books were sold during the Berber siege, by order of Wadih, a client of al-Mansur, and what remained was plundered when they stormed Cordoba. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari pp. 421–422}

## For Sprouts
The grand library of the Cordoba palace gathered thousands of rare books and manuscripts to help students and scholars study.

## For Explorers
The palace library of Cordoba in al-Andalus was one of the largest libraries of the medieval world, nurtured and expanded by Caliph al-Hakam II. The caliph dispatched agents to Baghdad, Cairo, and Damascus to purchase and copy rare manuscripts, amassing around four hundred thousand volumes across all fields of learning, cataloged in extensive indexes.

## For Scholars
The royal library of the Umayyad caliphal palace in Cordoba, reaching its pinnacle under Caliph al-Hakam II (350–366 AH / 961–976 CE). Unrivaled across Western Europe and the Mediterranean, the repository housed an estimated 400,000 volumes encompassing religious sciences, philology, history, philosophy, medicine, astronomy, and mathematics. Al-Hakam II funded a dedicated corps of copyists, binders, translators, and catalogers who scoured eastern markets for rare treatises. Its catalogs alone occupied forty-four multi-page registers, establishing Cordoba as the premier intellectual capital of Europe.
