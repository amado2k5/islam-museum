## Overview
[[person/qaytbay|Al-Ashraf Qaytbay]]'s funerary complex (1472-1474) in the south of the [[place/eastern-cemetery-cairo|Eastern Cemetery]], today a dense residential quarter, is in Yeomans's view a masterpiece and one of the best proportioned and crafted buildings of its age; its composition and plan inspired the later [[building/emir-qurqumas-complex|Qurqumas complex]]. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.219}

## The façade
The portal on the north-east is flanked by the minaret on the right and the [[concept/sabil-kuttab|sabil-kuttab]] on the left; reached by steps, it has a trilobed muqarnas hood and a groined vault filled with kaleidoscopic ablaq inlay in terracotta and biscuit-coloured stone. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.219} The minaret rises from a square base in three shafts: an octagon with keel-arched niches, a shaft with stellar strapwork, and an eight-columned pavilion with a bulbous finial. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.219} The sabil-kuttab balances solid and void: heavy bronze grilles below, fragile mashrabiyya in the kuttab arcades above, under cantilevered eaves gilded and painted inside. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.221} Yeomans calls the fenestration from sabil-kuttab to mausoleum a masterly piece of proportioning, finished by fleur-de-lis crenellation. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.221}

## The dome
The dome rises on rippling scrolls at the corners of the zone of transition and is covered with stellar strapwork interlaced with arabesque, an innovation, Yeomans notes, since earlier domes carried only geometric ornament. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.221} Behrens-Abouseif analyses the design as drawn from a bird's-eye view: a sixteen-pointed star centred on the apex covers the upper half, its lines continued into a row of irregular seventeen-pointed stars towards the base, with arabesque filling the spaces to hide the irregularities. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.221} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.222}

## The madrasa
The madrasa interior is cruciform on the [[concept/qaa|qa'a]] model: a sunken durqa'a under an octagonal lantern, two unequal iwans framed by horseshoe arches, the larger the prayer hall, and lateral iwans as tall narrow recesses. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.222} The gilded, painted roofing is magnificent: the lantern holds a stellar pattern of fourteen-sided figures with eight arabesque roundels and a central calligraphic medallion, over a polychrome marble floor of interlacing circles and chevrons. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.222} The mihrab has lost its marble, but the original minbar survives, one of the finest of the period, equal to Qaytbay's other minbar in the Victoria and Albert Museum. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.223}

## A multi-purpose complex
Besides mosque, mausoleum and madrasa, the complex included a maq'ad (a second-storey reception loggia drawn from domestic architecture), lodgings for Sufis, and a rab' built over the storerooms of a wakala. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.224}
