## Overview
Rumeli Hisarı stands on the European shore of the Bosphorus at the strait's narrowest point, about 700 metres from the fortress of Anadolu Hisarı that Bayezid I built on the Asian shore in 1394; Mehmet the Conqueror built it in 1452 to cut the Byzantines off from the Black Sea in preparation for the siege of Constantinople — his earliest construction in Istanbul and the largest fortress the Ottomans ever built {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.104}.

## Construction
Fatih began preparations in the winter of 1451-2, gathering stone, timber and iron, and construction started on 15 April 1452 after he had staked out the site himself. The fortress spans a deep valley: two tall towers on opposite hills and a third at the water's edge, joined by curtain walls defended by smaller towers in an irregular figure some 250 metres long and at most 125 metres broad {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.104-105}. He supervised the 1,000 skilled and 2,000 unskilled workmen gathered from the provinces, entrusting each main tower to a vezir — the north to Saruca Pasha, the sea tower to the grand vezir Halil Pasha and the south to Zaganos Pasha — who raced one another to finish {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.105}.

## Site and purpose
The Byzantine emperor offered money to turn the sultan from building it, but Fatih insisted; when complete it rose 82 metres, al-Sallabi says, and only 660 metres separated the two fortresses, whose guns could stop any ship from reaching Constantinople {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 92}.
