## Summary
The Muradiye of Edirne is an imperial mosque on an isolated hilltop north-east of the town centre; Murat II founded it in 1435 as a zaviye, a dervish hostel — reportedly inspired by a dream in which Celalettin Rumi, founder of the Mevlevis, asked the sultan to build shelter for his brotherhood — and later converted it into a mosque, housing the dervishes in a now-vanished tekke in the garden {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.82-83}.

## Description
A five-bay porch precedes it — the central bay domed high, the others transverse-vaulted — and the single minaret is a nineteenth-century replacement; the mosque is of the cross-axial eyvan type with domed eyvans opening off the central hall to east, west and south {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.83}. The main eyvan is richly decorated with ceramic tiles and frescoes, and the mihrab carries some of the finest extant early fifteenth-century Iznik tilework {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.83}.

## For young visitors
An Ottoman sultan dreamed of sheltering wandering dervishes and built it in Edirne in 1435 — it became a mosque whose mihrab holds some of the world's most beautiful early tiles.
