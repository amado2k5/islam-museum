## Summary
Kılıç Ali Pasha Camii stands at Tophane on the European shore of the lower Bosphorus just outside the walls of Galata; Sinan built it for the chief admiral Kılıç Ali Pasha in 1580–1, when — by Freely's account — the architect was in his ninety-first year {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.308}.

## Founder and building
The founder, born Occhiali in Calabria, was captured by Algerian pirates and spent fourteen years as a galley slave before entering Süleyman's service as a captain, becoming a Muslim named Uluç Ali; the only Turkish officer to distinguish himself at Lepanto in 1571, he was made chief admiral by Selim II and renamed Kılıç Ali {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.308}. After recapturing Tunis from the Spaniards in 1573 he retired to Istanbul; when he asked Murat III's leave to build a mosque, the story goes, the sultan told him to build it on the sea since that was his domain — so he filled in a stretch of shoreline outside Galata's walls and raised his mosque on it {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.310}. Freely notes that Sinan, who had always avoided directly imitating Haghia Sophia, here designed what is practically a small replica of it — one of his least successful buildings, in Freely's judgement {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.310-311}. Ali Pasha died in 1587, reputedly the richest of the sultan's subjects {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.310}.

## For young visitors
A captured Italian sailor became the greatest admiral of the Ottoman fleet; when he asked the sultan where to build his mosque, the sultan joked "on the sea" — so he filled in part of the shore and did exactly that.
