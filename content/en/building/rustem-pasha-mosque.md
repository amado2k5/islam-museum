## Overview
Rüstem Pasha Camii is among the most beautiful of Sinan's vezir mosques, standing in the old market quarter by the Golden Horn above the Galata Bridge; its charter of foundation records that it was built in AH 968 (1561), the year its founder died {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.285}.

## History
Rüstem rose from governor of Diyarbakır to imperial son-in-law by marrying Mihrimah, daughter of Süleyman and Roxelana, in 1539, then served as grand vezir from 1544 to 1553 and again from 1555 to 1561, becoming the wealthiest and most powerful of the sultan's subjects {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.286}.

## Architecture
The mosque stands on a high terrace over vaulted shops whose rents maintain the foundation; stairs rise from the platform's corners to a spacious courtyard, and the mosque is preceded by a double porch — five domed bays then a low penthouse roof on a row of columns {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.286}. The prayer hall is an octagon inscribed in a rectangle: the dome rests on four semidomes set on the diagonals, with galleries on small marble columns to east and west {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.286-287}.

## Tilework
The mosque is famous for the superb Iznik tiles that nearly cover its walls inside and on the porch façade, from the third and greatest period of the Iznik kilns (about 1555-1620); Arthur Lane counts it the first mosque where tile decoration is truly extensive, covering walls, columns, mihrab and mimber, its red reaching on some tiles a true scarlet {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.287}.
