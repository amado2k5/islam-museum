## Overview
This mosque in the old Imaret quarter of Sofia was built by the Bosnian vizier Derviş Mehmed Pasha, called Sufi or Sofu, who after serving as beylerbey of Rumelia became second vizier in 1547 and then beylerbey of Baghdad, Bosnia and Buda. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 134} The lists of the architect Sinan's works name it the mosque of Bosnevi Mehmed Pasha; according to the author it is Sinan's only mosque in Bulgaria. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 134} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 135} Its endowment deed, confirmed in mid-Dhu al-Hijja 954 (late January 1548), made it the centre of a complex with a madrasa, library, *imaret* (public kitchen), hospital, bath and caravanserai. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 134} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 135}

## In Evliya Çelebi
Visiting Sofia in 1652, Evliya Çelebi calls it the mosque of Koca Derviş Mehmed Pasha or the Imaret mosque, a luminous sanctuary with a lofty dome like an imperial mosque, a broad courtyard whose lead-covered domes stand on columns, and a tall minaret, and names Sinan as its architect. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 134}

## Conversion
After Sofia left Ottoman rule in 1878 the mosque served for a time as a storehouse and arsenal; the author holds that its name Kara Cami ("Black Mosque") came from the Ottoman quarter of Kara Danişment rather than from that use. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 135} In 1901 its exterior was entirely remade, as if wrapped in a new casing, and it opened on 27 July 1903 as the Church of the Seven Saints; the Bulgarian inscription at its door credits its design to the Russian architect Pomerantsev. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 135}
