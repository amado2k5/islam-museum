## Overview
The formative phase of Muslim architecture in Bengal began in the late thirteenth century in the newly conquered areas of Tribeni, Chhoto Pandua and Satgaon in Hooghly district. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 127} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 128} Tribeni, a centre of Hindu culture, was conquered by Zafar Khan, military commander of the region under the governor Rukn al-Din Kaykaus (1292-1302), according to an inscription of AH 698/1298. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 128} The author takes his mosque as the earliest known example of mosque architecture in Bengal, far older than any building at Gaur or Hazrat Pandua. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 127} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 128}

## The building
The prayer hall is an oblong 76 feet 9 inches by 34 feet 7 inches outside, divided into two aisles by an arcade on four squat black basalt piers, from which longitudinal and transverse arches carry ten domes built of diminishing rings of brick. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 129} The qibla wall has five mihrabs, the finest the central one, its multifoil brick arch on slender pillars from a Hindu temple, its terracotta ornament a twining creeper. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 129} It is entered by five true pointed arches on the east and doors on north and south, and has no courtyard, fountain, minaret or corner towers. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 129} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 130}

## The debate
Fragments of non-Muslim buildings are built into it, as in the Quwwat al-Islam mosque at Delhi, and R. D. Banerji thought it had been a Vaishnava temple. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 128} The author, following Dani, answers that its plan is that of an oblong multi-domed mosque with all the elements of a congregational mosque, that a restorer's hand shows in the high dome and the assembled mihrab pieces but not in the original plan, and that reuse was a matter of expediency. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 128} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 129} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 131} Saraswati dated it to the late fifteenth century and Brown to the early sixteenth on the evidence of its plan, a view the author does not accept. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 128}
