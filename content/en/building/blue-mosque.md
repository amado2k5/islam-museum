## Overview
Sultan Ahmet I Camii — the Blue Mosque — is, with Haghia Sophia, one of the two landmarks of the First Hill for anyone approaching Istanbul from the sea; Ahmet I founded it in 1609, when the architect Mehmet Ağa began construction on the eastern side of the ancient Hippodrome, whose last remnants were demolished for it, and it was completed with all its foundations in 1616 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.329-330}.

## History
Tradition has it that the young sultan was so enthusiastic that he often pitched in himself to hurry the work, and that he appeared at the dedication wearing a turban shaped like the Prophet's foot in token of humility; but Ahmet died the year after his mosque's completion {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.330}.

## Architecture
A monumental courtyard of exactly the prayer hall's dimensions precedes the mosque, its peristyle of 26 columns roofed by 30 small domes around a hexagonal şadırvan, now decorative since ablutions are done at taps on the mosque's west side {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.330}. The central dome is flanked by semidomes on four sides with smaller semidomes and corner domes in a harmonious pyramidal cascade; of the six minarets, the four at the building's corners carry three şerefes each and the pair at the courtyard's far corners two each {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.330-331}.

## In Evliya Çelebi's travels
Joseph von Hammer notes that [[person/evliya-celebi|Evliya Çelebi]] records in his travels the building of the Sultan Ahmed Mosque, begun when Evliya was six years old, and that its gate was made under the supervision of his father, Dervish Mehmed, chief of the goldsmiths of Constantinople. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.9}
