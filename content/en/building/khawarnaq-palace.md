## Overview
The palace of al-Khawarnaq was among the famed palaces of al-Hira that the Arabs took as bywords; the transmitters differ over its builder — al-Haytham ibn Adi says it was ordered by al-Nu'man ibn Imri' al-Qays, who reigned eighty years and built it in sixty, its architect a Byzantine named Sinmar {cite:mujam-al-buldan v4}.

## In the sources
Yaqut also records other senses of the name: a country in the Maghrib, a village half a farsakh from Balkh called Khabnak, and a place at Kufa which Abu Mansur called a river; but the Khawarnaq of Arabic verse and proverb is, in the view of the traditionists, the palace on the outskirts of al-Hira {cite:mujam-al-buldan v4}.
