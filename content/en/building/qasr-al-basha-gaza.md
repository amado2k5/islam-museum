## Summary
Qasr al-Basha in the al-Daraj quarter of [[place/gaza|Gaza]]'s old town is a palace of two-storey buildings around a courtyard, its ground floor Mamluk and its upper floor largely Ottoman. It is commonly credited to Sultan al-Zahir Baybars (r. 1260–1277) on the strength of the relief of two facing lions — his blazon — above the entrance, though the founder's identity remains debated for want of a foundation plaque. {cite:aldohdar-pasha-palace-2020} {cite:cnrs-gaza-histoire}

## Through the centuries
It was the seat of Gaza's Mamluk governor (Qasr al-Na'ib, Dar al-Sa'ada), then the seat of the Radwan dynasty and Ottoman pashas (Qasr Al Radwan); Napoleon lodged in it in 1799, earning it the name "Napoleon's Fort"; it became a Mandate police station (al-Dabuya) and an Egyptian-era girls' school. Restored, it opened in 2010 as Gaza's national archaeological museum, holding some 17,000 objects and drawing about 70,000 visitors a year. {cite:aldohdar-pasha-palace-2020} {cite:cnrs-gaza-histoire} {cite:wmf-gaza-watch-2025}

## In the 2023–2025 war
The palace was struck on 11 December 2023 and again in January 2024, destroying more than seventy percent of its buildings; UNESCO verified the damage by satellite among Gaza's damaged sites. After the October 2025 ceasefire, Palestinian crews began clearing rubble by hand, sorting stones and salvaging what remained of the museum's collection. {cite:unesco-gaza-assessment} {cite:wmf-gaza-watch-2025}
