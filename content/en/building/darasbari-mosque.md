## Overview
The Darasbari mosque and madrasa lie half a mile south-west of the Kotwali Gate at Umarpur, between Mahdipur and Firuzpur in Gaur. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 155} *Daras bari* means "house of lessons", a madrasa; the adjoining madrasa long disappeared and has since been excavated, and the author compares the pair with the mosque and madrasa of Bibi Khanum at Samarqand and Gawhar Shad's complex at Mashhad. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 155} He sees the restoration of the Ilyas Shahis by Mahmud Shah in AH 841/1437 as opening a Persian cultural revival in Bengal. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 155}

## The building
It is an oblong 98 by 57 feet outside, of the curvilinear-roofed type begun by the Saith Gumbad at Bagerhat: a central nave, 51 feet by 25 feet 6 inches, leads to the main mihrab between two square side halls of three transverse aisles each. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 155} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 156} The roofing of the nave is disputed; the author, following Dani, holds that it carried three parallel four-sided Bengali *chau-chala* roofs, the earliest known in Gaur. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 156} A women's gallery 18 by 11 feet stood in the north-west corner, and a vestibule in front, entered by seven arches, had a *chau-chala* roof at its centre and three domes on each side. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 156} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 157}

## Inscription
Its tughra inscription, 11 feet 3 inches long, opens with the verse on mosques and the hadith and records that the just Sultan Shams al-Dunya wa al-Din Abu al-Muzaffar Yusuf Shah, son of Barbak Shah son of Mahmud Shah, built this congregational mosque in AH 884; it is now in the Calcutta museum. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 158} The author counts it the finest achievement of stucco ornament among these mosques, close to Chamkatti and Tantipara in its surface work. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 158}
