## Overview
The tomb stands in Giannitsa (Yenice-i Vardar), in the south-east of the town near Egnatia Street, beside the ruins of the mosque popularly called the [[building/iskender-bey-mosque-giannitsa|İskender Bey Mosque]]; it is the best-preserved Ottoman monument in the town. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1340} According to Heath Lowry it belongs to Ali Bey "the Two-Hearted" or Yakub Bey of the Evrenos family, the most famous of the Ottoman *akıncı* (frontier raider) houses. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1340} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1341}

## Building and conversion
It is a square building with a dome on an octagonal drum under Turkish tiles; restoration replaced its octagonal conical cover so that the inner dome now shows outside, and its front originally had two windows. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1340} In 1948 it became a Greek Orthodox church dedicated to St Paraskevi: the windows were closed, a semicircular apse added, and the interior walls, dome and pendentives renewed and painted with Christian images. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1340} The local historian Eleni Mavrokefalidou describes it as originally an eight-sided mosque or tomb with a minaret on its left. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1340} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1341} Its owner, Archpriest Nikandros Papaioannou, gave it to the municipality in 1951, and a bell tower was added to its west wing in 1990-92. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-3-2023 p. 1340}
