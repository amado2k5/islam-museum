## Overview
At [[place/mansura-tlemcen|al-Mansura]], about a mile and a half west of [[place/tlemcen|Tlemcen]], stand the ruins of a mosque whose minaret many consider one of the noblest the Maghreb produced. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p.28} {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p.30}

## The minaret
The minaret stands on the axis of the building and served as its main entrance, with a finely carved doorway. It is 125 feet high, largely of hewn stone, and an inscription records that it was erected by order of [[person/abu-yaqub-yusuf-an-nasr|Abu Ya'qub Yusuf]]. [[person/rexford-newcomb|Newcomb]] quotes Saladin: like the Hassan tower at Rabat, its upper part is dressed with a trellis of interlacing bands with blind arcades above, and green and brown faience tiles, now mostly lost, once glinted in the hollows of the carving. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p.30}
