## Overview
The Lattan mosque stands four miles north of Gaur police station across the Bhagirathi, on the right of the Nawabganj road between the [[building/tantipara-mosque-gaur|Tantipara mosque]] and the Five-Arched Bridge. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 119} Europeans called it the "China" or "Painted" mosque because its bricks were coloured. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 119} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 120}

## Its name
A local story, repeated by Francklin and Cunningham, holds that a dancing girl of the sultan's harem built it to atone for her former life, "Lattan" being a corruption of *natin*, dancing girl. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 119} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 120} The author prefers Bloch's view: that the name came from the mosque's gaudy tiled appearance, which reminded peasants of a dancing girl in bright clothes and jewels — "the mosque that resembles a dancing girl", not "the dancing girl's mosque". {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 120} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 121}

## The building
It follows the square plan begun by the Eklakhi tomb: 72½ by 51 feet overall, its central chamber 34 feet square under a hemispherical dome on squinches whose interior rises in three stages with eight ribs, bell-and-chain motifs between them and a lotus pendant at the crown. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 121} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 122} It is about 50 feet high inside and 70 outside; the qibla wall has three tiled mihrabs, and an eastern vestibule 34 by 11 feet has a four-sided Bengali *chau-chala* roof over its central bay; there are four fluted corner towers and two more at the corners of the vestibule. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 122} Against Cunningham's dismissal, the author judges its tilework, some of it now in the Victoria and Albert Museum, a gem of Muslim ornamental art in Bengal. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 123}
