## Overview
The three sabils of [[person/ibn-saud|King Abd al-Aziz]] on the road between Mecca and Jeddah, at Bi'r Umm al-Qurun, Hadda and Bi'r al-Maqtala, unlike older sabils still stand, which makes it possible to study their type and compare them with Ottoman sabils. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.37} {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.47}

## Description
Each is a rectangular trough with drinking openings under a vault, with a foundation inscription dated 1361 AH / 1942 over its face, a simple design suited to the climate and local materials: rough basalt, brick, lime, pottery and charcoal dust. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.47}

## Link with Ottoman sabils
[[person/muhammad-hamza-ismail-al-haddad|Al-Haddad]] reports Ghabbashi's view that their design follows the Ottoman sabils on the same road, such as those of Bi'r al-Hudaybiyya and Bi'r Umm al-Jud, perhaps by builders who had worked in the Ottoman period; the author nevertheless sees a new pattern in their roofing and inscriptions that gives them their own character despite their simplicity. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.47} {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.52} He adds that the sabil of al-Ma'abida has a large arched gateway crowning its face and five drinking openings. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.47}
