## Overview
Şehzade Camii, the "Mosque of the Prince", is a great complex on Istanbul's Third Hill that Süleyman the Magnificent ordered in memory of his eldest son Mehmet, who died of smallpox in 1543 aged twenty-one; Sinan was commissioned and built it between 1544 and 1548 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.222}.

## The külliye
An outer precinct wall encloses the mosque together with a medrese, an imaret, a primary school, a tabhane (hostel) and the türbe of Prince Mehmet, around which later tombs were built; Sinan himself called the mosque his "apprentice work", yet it was his first imperial mosque on a truly monumental scale {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.222-223}.

## Architecture
The mosque is preceded by a courtyard equal in area to the building itself, bordered by porticoes of five domed bays on each side, with an octagonal şadırvan at its centre that Evliya Çelebi says was a gift of Murat IV; the two minarets at the courtyard's inner corners carry geometrical relief sculpture and terra-cotta inlay {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.224}. Inside, Sinan expanded the prayer hall with four semidomes rather than two, so the dome rests on four great piers in a space without columns or galleries — an experiment he never repeated {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.225}.

## The tombs
Behind the mosque a walled graveyard garden holds türbes of such varied dates that their tilework surveys the two best ages of the Iznik kilns together with some Tekfursaray ware; the largest and grandest is the octagonal türbe of Şehzade Mehmet himself, built by Sinan {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.225-226}.
