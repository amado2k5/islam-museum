## Summary
Rum Mehmet Pasha Camii is the third of Istanbul's three veziral mosques from the Conqueror's time, near the shore at the Bosphorus's southern end in Üsküdar; it was built in 1471 by Rum Mehmet Pasha, a Greek convert who became grand vezir — and whom Fatih executed in 1472 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.143}.

## Byzantine appearance
It is the most Byzantine-looking of the city's early mosques: most notably the high cylindrical drum under the dome, the exterior cornice following the curve of the round-arched windows, and the square dome base broken by the projecting dome arches {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.143}. Inside, a central dome on smooth pendentives with a southern semidome, and side chambers completely cut off from the centre; behind stands the founder's gaunt türbe — of whom the historian Joseph von Hammer wrote that 'he left in Ottoman history no other memories than those of his crimes' {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.143-144}.

## For young visitors
The most Byzantine-looking of Istanbul's old mosques was built by a Greek minister who became Muslim — then was executed by Sultan Mehmet the Conqueror one year after his mosque.
