## Summary
Gazi Mihal Camii, just left of the road beyond the founder's bridge, is dated by inscription to 1422; it is of the axial-eyvan type, built by the converted Greek nobleman Gazi Mihal {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.95}.

## Form
A five-bayed porch precedes it, a large oval dome over the taller central bay and flat-topped cross-vaults over the rest; the square central hall carries a hemispherical dome on pendentives, while the two ribbed-domed side chambers — once dervish hospices — now open only from outside; the raised prayer eyvan has a shallow barrel-vault under a pitched exterior roof {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.95}.

## For young visitors
A 1422 mosque built by a nobleman who became Muslim — its side rooms once lodged travelling dervishes.
