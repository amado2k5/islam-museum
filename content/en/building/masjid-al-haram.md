## Overview
The Masjid al-Haram is a mosque in [[place/mecca|Mecca]]. {cite:wikidata}

## Al-Maqdisi's description
Al-Maqdisi, around 375 AH, described it as built by al-Mahdi. Its marble columns were carried by sea from Alexandria to Jeddah. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp. 81-82}

The walls of its three colonnades are covered in mosaic made by craftsmen of Syria and Egypt, and their names are on them. It has nineteen gates, among them the Gate of Banu Shayba, the Gate of al-Safa and the Gate of the Assembly House. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp. 81-82}

Around the circuit are miles of brass with hanging lamps, on which candles are lit for the kings of Egypt and Yemen and the ruler of Gharjistan. In Mecca there are three pools that are filled from a channel which Zubayda built from the garden of Banu Amir. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp. 81-82}

## Al-Umari's description
Some four centuries later, Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari recorded the measurements of the Ka'ba in cubits. The distance between the door and the Black Stone is four cubits, and this is the Multazam. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 pp. 137, 141-142}

On the back of the Ka'ba is a closed door called the Mustajar of Sins. Its gilded door was renewed in the Nasirid period, after its plates had been removed, and its cost was estimated at eighteen thousand dinars. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 pp. 137, 141-142}

## For Sprouts
The Sacred Mosque is the mosque of Mecca, holding the Ka'ba. Al-Muqaddasi described it about 375 AH with marble columns carried by sea from Alexandria and mosaic made by craftsmen of Syria and Egypt, and nineteen doors including the Banu Shayba and Safa gates. After the conquest of Mecca it became a school of learning: Mu'adh taught in it and Ibn Abbas held fatwa and lessons there.

## For Explorers
The Sacred Mosque is the mosque of Mecca holding the Ka'ba. Al-Muqaddasi described it about 375 AH as al-Mahdi's building: marble columns carried by sea from Alexandria to Jeddah, the walls of its three porticoes clad in mosaic by craftsmen of Syria and Egypt whose names remain on it, nineteen doors including Banu Shayba, Safa and Nadwa gates, and around the tawaf miles of brass with candles burning on them. Four centuries later al-Umari recorded the Ka'ba's measures: four cubits between door and Black Stone — the Multazam — and on its back the sealed 'Refuge from sins' door; its gilded door was renewed in the Nasiri days. After the conquest it became a school of learning: the book records the Prophet leaving Mu'adh to teach and Ibn Abbas holding fatwa and lessons, training Mujahid, Ata and Tawus; Hajj season was students' chance to meet scholars, and Ramadan held the Qur'an-finishing nights with the imams of the four schools.

## For Scholars
The Sacred Mosque is the mosque of Mecca holding the Ka'ba. Al-Muqaddasi describes it c. 375 AH as al-Mahdi's building: marble columns carried by sea from Alexandria to Jeddah; the walls of its three porticoes clad in mosaic made by craftsmen of Syria and Egypt whose names survive on it; nineteen doors including the Banu Shayba, Safa and Nadwa gates; and around the tawaf miles of brass bearing candles lit for the kings of Egypt, Yemen and the lord of Ghurjistan — Mecca also holding three pools filled from the channel Zubayda edged from Banu Amir's garden. Four centuries on, Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari recorded the Ka'ba's cubit measures: four cubits between the door and the Black Stone — the Multazam — the sealed 'Refuge from sins' door on its back, and its gilded door renewed in the Nasiri days at eighteen thousand dinars. After the conquest it became a school of learning: the book records the Prophet leaving Mu'adh to teach (a report needing verification), Ibn Abbas holding fatwa and lessons with Mujahid, Ata and Tawus graduating at his hands; the Hajj season as the meeting of students with scholars; the Ramadan nights of Qur'an completion; and the imams of the four schools, per Ibn Jubayr's journey.
