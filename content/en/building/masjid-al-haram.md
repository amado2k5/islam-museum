## Overview
The Masjid al-Haram is a mosque in [[place/mecca|Mecca]]. {cite:wikidata}

## Al-Maqdisi's description
Al-Maqdisi, around 375 AH, described it as built by al-Mahdi. Its marble columns were carried by sea from Alexandria to Jeddah. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp. 81-82}

The walls of its three colonnades are covered in mosaic made by craftsmen of Syria and Egypt, and their names are on them. It has nineteen gates, among them the Gate of Banu Shayba, the Gate of al-Safa and the Gate of the Assembly House. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp. 81-82}

Around the circuit are miles of brass with hanging lamps, on which candles are lit for the kings of Egypt and Yemen and the ruler of Gharjistan. In Mecca there are three pools that are filled from a channel which Zubayda built from the garden of Banu Amir. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp. 81-82}

## Al-Umari's description
Some four centuries later, Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari recorded the measurements of the Ka'ba in cubits. The distance between the door and the Black Stone is four cubits, and this is the Multazam. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 pp. 137, 141-142}

On the back of the Ka'ba is a closed door called the Mustajar of Sins. Its gilded door was renewed in the Nasirid period, after its plates had been removed, and its cost was estimated at eighteen thousand dinars. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 pp. 137, 141-142}
