## Overview
D. G. Crawford wrote that Pandua, after Satgaon the oldest place in Hooghly district, was once a Hindu raja's capital and is famous for a great victory won by the Muslims under Shah Safi around 1340. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 144} Besides the mosque and tomb of Shah Safi al-Din, its largest architectural work is the Great Mosque west of the minar — like [[building/zafar-khan-mosque-tribeni|Zafar Khan's mosque]] at Tribeni an oblong mosque, but with three aisles. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 144}

## The building
It is 231 feet long and 42 wide, roughly five times as long as it is broad, divided into three aisles by two arcades of twenty-one arches each; the massive brick arches spring from slender basalt shafts six feet high, without pilaster or impost. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 144} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 145} Its sixty-three domes fell long ago; Chakravarti thought them built of diminishing rings of brick, Dani of an arcuate type on corbelled pendentives. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 145} The qibla wall has twenty-one mihrabs, the central one with a cinquefoil arch; a canopied stone pulpit anticipates that of the [[building/adina-mosque-pandua|Adina mosque]]; and a raised platform in the north-west corner, said to be Shah Safi's retreat, was in the author's view a women's gallery. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 145}

## Debate
Cunningham held that the mosque stands on the mound of a Hindu temple whose pillars it reuses, and Blochmann that its bricks and pillars came from Buddhist buildings. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 144} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 145} The author finds it far-fetched to claim that it was built on the temple's own foundation. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 145} He takes it as the prototype of the three-aisled oblong mosques of the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, such as those at Shahzadpur, Masjidkur and Qasba. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 146}
