## Summary
Hüdavendigâr Camii in Bursa was built by Murat I in 1365–85; its long completion reflects a reign spent campaigning in the Balkans, and the name is the imperial title Murat took on becoming sultan, inscribed in the dedication of this and his other mosques {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.54}.

## A two-storeyed building
The design is without parallel in Ottoman architecture: a mosque-hospice on the ground floor and a medrese above. Badly damaged in the 1855 earthquake, when the original upper structure collapsed, its interior was altered by later restorations {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.54}. Kuran classifies it as cross-axial eyvan: a two-storeyed central hall surmounted by a dome 11 metres across and 22 metres high with an open oculus over a hexagonal marble pool, four steps rising to the prayer-hall eyvan, and an upper medrese with eight cells on either side {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.54-56}. Byzantine columns, capitals and acanthus-carved marble door pieces were reused throughout {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.54}.

## For young visitors
The strangest mosque in Bursa has two floors: a mosque and travellers' hospice below and a school above — built by Sultan Murat I in the pauses of endless wars.
