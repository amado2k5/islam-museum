## Overview
The khanqah, mausoleum and sabil-kuttab of Sultan [[person/qansuh-al-ghawri|Qansuh al-Ghuri]] (1503-1505) stand on the east side of al-Mu'izz street where it crosses Sharia al-Azhar, facing [[building/madrasa-of-sultan-al-ghuri|his madrasa and mosque]]; together they form a visual climax to this stretch of the street, painted by David Roberts and John Frederick Lewis. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.227} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.229}

## The building
At its northern corner a [[concept/sabil-kuttab|sabil-kuttab]] projects into the street on three sides; beside it steps lead to a trilobed portal and a square vestibule lit by a wooden lantern, with the mausoleum to the right and the khanqah to the left. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.229} The mausoleum once had a huge dome faced with green tiles, too ambitious to stand; it was rebuilt twice, and the last, of brick, fell early in the twentieth century, as Behrens-Abouseif notes, so the tomb now has a wooden roof. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.229}

## Burials
The tomb holds al-Ghuri's son, daughters and concubine and his successor [[person/tuman-bay-ii|Tuman Bay II]], but not al-Ghuri, who died on the battlefield in Syria; Yeomans quotes Ibn Iyas marvelling that al-Ghuri spent 100,000 dinars on his madrasa expecting a magnificent tomb and ended in the wilderness. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.229}

## The khanqah
The khanqah is a large assembly hall with a mihrab for Sufi meetings, without lodgings; it now hosts cultural events of "Sufi" music and dance. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.229}
