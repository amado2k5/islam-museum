## Summary
Yedikule, the Castle of the Seven Towers, is a curious part-Byzantine, part-Turkish structure in the land walls near the Sea of Marmara: four towers belong to the Theodosian walls and three were added inside by Fatih soon after the Conquest, joined by four heavy curtain-walls into a five-sided enclosure {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.121}. The two central Theodosian towers are marble pylons flanking the Golden Gate, the Roman triumphal arch erected about 390 by Theodosius I {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.121}.

## State prison and a sultan's death
Yedikule was never used as a castle in the usual sense: two towers served as Ottoman prisons while others stored part of the state treasure {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.122}. The tower by the entrance is called the Tower of Ambassadors because foreign envoys were often imprisoned there; some carved their names and tales of woe in half a dozen languages {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.122}. The pylon left of the Golden Gate was a prison and place of execution — here Sultan Osman II was put to death on 22 May 1622 aged seventeen; the book quotes Evliya Çelebi's grim account {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.122-123}. The fortress has been restored and is open as a museum and performance venue {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.122}.

## For young visitors
A Roman golden gateway became part of a castle where the Ottomans imprisoned foreign ambassadors — and where a teenage sultan died.
