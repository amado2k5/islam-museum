## Summary
Selimiye Cami in Üsküdar stands directly behind the huge Selimiye Barracks on the Asian Marmara shore; Selim III built it in 1803–4 for the troops quartered in the barracks bearing his name — the last and one of the handsomest of the pure baroque mosques {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.408-409}.

## Description
The mosque sits in an exceptionally lovely garden with three of the city's finest plane trees; the western gallery, mihrab and mimber are of highly polished grey marble, and the sultan's loge takes kiosk form — a feature later imperial mosques adopted {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.409}. The külliye also included a mektep, çeşme, sebil and a muvakkithane for the mosque astronomer {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.409}.

## For young visitors
