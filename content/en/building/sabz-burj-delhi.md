## Overview
The Sabz Burj ("Green Tower") is a tomb at the crossing of the Mathura Road and the Lodi Road in Delhi, with [[building/humayun-tomb|Humayun's tomb]] to its east and the [[building/dargah-nizamuddin|dargah of Nizam al-Din Awliya]] to its south-west. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 198} Its plan is an "octagonalised square" — a square whose angles are chamfered until each angle side exceeds half the larger side — all its sides are open, three corner alcoves are semi-octagonal and the fourth rectangular, and there is no mihrab. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 198} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 199} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 201}

## Ornament and dome
The semi-vaults of the alcoves carry incised stucco in geometric designs in deep red and white inlaid with black slate, which Nath calls an unprecedented inlay in stucco. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 199} Squinches carry the high bulbous double dome; what looks like a tall cylindrical drum is in fact the lower part of the dome itself, hollow between the inner shell and an outer shell built on eight ribs. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 199} The dome is entirely covered with blue and green tiles, which gave the building its name. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 199} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 200}

## Its place in the evolution of the style
Nath holds that this is the first fully developed example in medieval Indian art both of the octagonalised-square plan and of the high bulbous dome, and that the similar tomb of Nawab Amir Khalil Khan at Thatta in Sind, about 1575, followed it closely. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 200} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 201} He ascribes the bulbous double dome to Persian inspiration arriving after Humayun's return in 1555 — there is no earlier example at Delhi — and compares its dome with that of the Gur-i Amir at Samarqand. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 210}
