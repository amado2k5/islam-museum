## Overview
The complex of Sultan al-Ashraf Inal (1451-1456) at the northern end of the [[place/eastern-cemetery-cairo|Eastern Cemetery]] joins that of the [[building/emir-qurqumas-complex|amir Qurqumas]], forming a long free-standing ensemble seen from afar: the cubic bulk of two madrasas linked by lower residential units, broken by slender minarets and domes patterned with lozenges and chevrons. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.213} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.214} Inal began it as an amir and finished it as sultan; the minaret and mausoleum flanking the tall madrasa rise from lower bases, suggesting they were completed earlier. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.214}

## Minaret and dome
The mausoleum is a separate structure at the eastern corner, and the north-east front keeps the remains of a [[concept/sabil-kuttab|sabil-kuttab]] and the ruins of a large khanqah built in 1454. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.215} The minaret stands almost free, on a square base with three carved shafts, two balconies and a bulbous finial, its second shaft carved with vertical chevrons, echoed horizontally on the dome. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.215}

## A madrasa with a roofed court
The muqarnas portal leads to a cruciform hall dominated by the qibla axis, the side iwans subordinate, a growing trait of late Burji architecture. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.215} The central court, now ruined, was roofed in timber, a feature of late Burji madrasas whose source Yeomans finds in the domestic [[concept/qaa|qa'a]], part of a closer integration of religious foundation and residence. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.215} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.216} Two-storey rab'-type lodgings for Sufis and khanqah staff link Inal's complex with Qurqumas's. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.217}
