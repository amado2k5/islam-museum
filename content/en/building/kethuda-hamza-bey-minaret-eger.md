## Overview
In the heart of Eger stood the Kethüda Hamza Bey mosque, a church for about 150 years after Ottoman rule ended; it was demolished in 1841 as a danger, leaving only its minaret. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 167} Old engravings show it had stone walls and a wooden roof; the minaret is 35 metres high with 97 steps. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 167}

## History
Ayverdi, examining it in 1976, marvelled at how well it was kept, saying that this kindness made him forget its deformed pointed shaft and castellated balcony and even made it look charming. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 167} Today it serves visitors with a view over the whole town, and it is one of the three Turkish minarets surviving in Hungary, with those of Yakovalı Hasan Pasha at Pécs and Hamza Bey at Érd. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 167} {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 189}
