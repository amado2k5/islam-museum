## Overview
The tomb of İdris Baba stands outside the walls of Pécs in a small wood on Nyár Street north-west of the city, on the site of a former Muslim cemetery; it is octagonal, domed and of brick, one of the two Bektashi tombs surviving in Hungary along with that of [[person/gul-baba|Gül Baba]]. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 121} The historian İbrahim Peçevi wrote of a conversation with İdris Baba in 1591, and Evliya Çelebi mentions him in his account of Pécs in 1663. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 121}

## History
After 1693 the tomb became a chapel, an apse being added in place of one of its windows; restored in the 1960s as a tomb, it is open to visitors, and the Gül Baba Museum holds a symbolic figure of İdris Baba. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 121}
