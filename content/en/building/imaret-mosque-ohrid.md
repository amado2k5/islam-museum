## Overview
On Imaret Hill above Ohrid, beside the tomb of Sinan Çelebi, stood a fifteenth-century mosque built by Sinan Çelebi, in use as a mosque until 1912 and known as the Imaret, Hünkâr or Fatih Sultan Mehmed Mosque, with an *imaret*, school and madrasa around it. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 386} Its endowment deed was written in AH 896/1491, ten years after Mehmed II's death. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 386} It was built over the ruins of the church and monastery of St Panteleimon of 893 without reusing any of their walls, which an excavation under its floor uncovered in 1943. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 386}

## Building and fate
It was a square building, 16.90 by 16.95 metres outside, under a single dome, of cut stone and brick, with three rows of windows on every side but the north and a muqarnas mihrab niche, without minbar or gallery. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 386} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 387} In 1948 the hill was declared a national park and twenty-eight Turkish families living there were moved; in 1967 it became a protected archaeological site. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 386} The author relates that in October 1999 the Ohrid city council decided to demolish the mosque; after protests by local Muslims halted the work it was later completed, the dome, minaret and portico pulled down and only the walls left, and a church now rises on the site. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 386} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 387}
