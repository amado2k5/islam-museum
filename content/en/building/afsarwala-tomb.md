## Overview
The Afsarwala tomb stands inside the compound of [[building/humayun-tomb|Humayun's tomb]] on its south-west side near the Arab Sarai, on a broad platform that it shares with a three-arched mosque known as the [[building/afsarwali-masjid-delhi|Afsarwali Masjid]]. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 196} Nath notes that the names are unintelligible and that no documentary or epigraphic record throws light on the history of either monument. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 196}

## The building
The plan is square with angles chamfered to give an octagonal form; lintelled doorways on the sides each have an arched opening above, and the west side is open, without a mihrab. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 196} It is built of rubble and plaster with red and grey stone used widely on edges and angles; the red arches have white marble linings, and red stone rosettes in their spandrels are inlaid with pieces of white marble and black slate. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 196} A hemispherical double dome on a high octagonal drum takes up about half the building's height; there are no chhatris, pinnacles or eaves. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 196} {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 197}

## Date
Of three tombstones inside, one bears the figure 974, perhaps AH 974 (1566-67), to which Nath ascribes the tomb. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 197} He thinks the mosque on the same platform is earlier by about a quarter of a century, and that both may have been built by the person buried in the tomb. {cite:nath-history-of-mughal-architecture-1-1982 p. 197}
