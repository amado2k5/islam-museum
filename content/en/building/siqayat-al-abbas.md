## Overview
The Siqaya of al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib heads the list of Mecca's sabils; it stood between the well of Zamzam to the east and the Kaaba, was built under the Abbasid caliph [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] (158-169 AH), and was repaired and renewed in 200 AH by order of Husayn ibn Hasan al-Alawi, in 229 AH by al-Wathiq, in 706 AH by [[person/al-nasir-muhammad-ibn-qalawun|al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun]], and again in 807 AH. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.18} [[person/muhammad-hamza-ismail-al-haddad|Al-Haddad]] counts it a unique and rare type in the architecture of sabils generally and of the Hijaz in particular. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.52}

## First phase: al-Mahdi's building
The earliest description is al-Azraqi's, as the author quotes it: a rectangle of twenty-four by nineteen cubits, its walls with columns and teak panels between, eight cubits high, crowned by forty-six merlons, with two doors, one facing the Kaaba, and six basins fed by water running in a lead pipe from the Zamzam chamber. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.38} {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.39} The author thinks this plan has no known parallel among the siqayas of the Islamic lands. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.39} Its merlons were pulled down on al-Alawi's order in 200 AH, Mubarak al-Tabari restored the teak panels, and under al-Wathiq in 229 AH its form was changed, according to al-Azraqi. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.39}

## Second phase: the domed house
Al-Fasi described it in his day as a square house under a large brick dome set in lime, with iron grilles low in its walls overlooking the mosque, two marble basins on the north side flanking the door, and inside a large pool filled from Zamzam by a spout-like timber and an underground channel ending in a fountain at its centre; the dome was rebuilt in 807 AH / 1404 after termites ate the wooden dome and it fell. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.39} The author thinks the work of 807 AH changed nothing essential except replacing the wooden dome with a brick one, and that the domed-house type is rare among sabils, its best-known parallel being Qaytbay's sabil in Jerusalem (887 AH / 1482). {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.40} Ibn Jubayr saw and described this phase in 580 AH / 1184. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.52}
