## Summary
The beautiful Nişancı Mehmet Pasha mosque on the Fifth Hill is among the finest classical mosques; popularly ascribed to Sinan, it is absent from his Tezkere — it was built for the Lord Privy Seal (Nişancı) Mehmet Pasha between 1584 and 1588 by an unknown architect {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.335-336}.

## Description
From afar the upper structure shows masterly arrangement: the great dome ringed by eight little weight turrets, eight semidomes of two sizes, and the minaret unusually close to the dome base; one enters through an ogive-arched courtyard whose porch carries a tuğra of Mustafa III recording a 1766 restoration after that year's severe earthquake {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.336}. The plan varies the octagon-in-square: eight smaller semidomes serve as squinches, one covering a projecting mihrab apse, with galleries above three sides. Freely reads it as the unknown architect playing variations on Sinan's own themes — leading Kuran to suggest Davut Ağa as designer {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.336-337}. In the little graveyard is the founder's small unpretentious türbe, its inscription dating his death to A.H. 1001 (1592–3) {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.337}.

## For young visitors
A lovely mosque on Istanbul's Fifth Hill that everyone called Sinan's — but his own catalogue doesn't list it, so who built this masterpiece?
