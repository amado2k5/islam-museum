## Summary
Sinan Pasha Camii in Beşiktaş, on the European shore of the Bosphorus, was built by the architect Sinan for Sinan Pasha — brother of the grand vezir Rüstem Pasha and chief admiral of the Ottoman fleet under Süleyman the Magnificent; inscriptions on the şadırvan and portal date its completion to A.H. 963 (1555–6), two years after its founder's death {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.296}.

## Freely's account
Freely describes its plan as essentially a copy of Üç Şerefeli Cami in Edirne (1447): the central dome rests on six arches, one set in the south wall and the rest on four piers — two rectangular to the north, two hexagonal east and west — with two domed side bays beyond; here, though, Sinan added a five-bayed narthex-like porch {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.297}. Freely finds the proportions ungainly and the interior squat and heavy, judging that Sinan was less successful when directly copying an older building at a smaller scale {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.297}.

## For young visitors
The admiral Sinan Pasha wanted a mosque on the Bosphorus at Beşiktaş, and the most famous Ottoman architect built it for him — but finished it two years after the admiral had died.

- The Hijri date 963 is from the portal inscription as reported by Freely.
