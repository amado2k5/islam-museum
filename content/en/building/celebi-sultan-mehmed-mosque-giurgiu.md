## Overview
The Çelebi Sultan Mehmed Mosque stood in the old port quarter of Giurgiu, in today's Mircea cel Bătrân street; the book's entry ascribes it to Sultan Mehmed I (Çelebi) and dates it to the fifteenth century. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 674} A view of 1824 shows it with the castle. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 675}

## Conversion
After Giurgiu was ceded to the Russians following the Ottoman-Russian war of 1828-29 (in 1830), the mosque was dedicated to St Nicholas and made an Orthodox church, gaining a semicircular apse and a wooden bell tower. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 674} Damaged in the war of 1877, it remained a church, was repaired in 1905 with stones from the ruined castle, was badly damaged by German bombing in 1944 and was extensively restored by the architect Dobrescu in 1945. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-2-2023 p. 674}
