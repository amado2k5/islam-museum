## Overview
A great mosque in the city of [[place/al-fustat|Fustat]], built by [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] on the site of his tent and the land around it. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p. 209}

The site of his tent is where the mihrab and the minbar are. About eighty of the companions prayed in it. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p. 209}

## The mosque and the city in al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that when Amr ibn al-As camped beside the fortress he laid out the mosque known as the Old Mosque, also called the Mosque of Amr ibn al-As, and the Arab tribes settled around it, so that a city known as Fustat came into being. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.831}

After the conquest, the Nile's waters withdrew for some years from the land opposite the fortress and the Old Mosque. The Muslims tethered their animals there, and then laid out houses on it little by little. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.831}

In another place al-Maqrizi says that the Muslims laid out Fustat around the mosque of Amr ibn al-As and the Qasr al-Sham'. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.990}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Amr built the mosque on ground surrounded by gardens and vines; they stretched ropes until the line was true, and Amr stood until the qibla was set, Amr and Companions of the Prophet setting it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 133} He made a minbar in it, and Umar wrote: "Is it not enough for you to stand while the Muslims are beneath your heels?", and ordered him to break it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 133} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 134} Abu al-Khayr reports that Abu Muslim al-Ghafiqi, a Companion of the Prophet, was Amr's muezzin, and that he saw him perfuming the mosque with incense. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 134} Amr wrote to Umar that he had laid out a house for him by the mosque; Umar answered, "How should a man in the Hijaz have a house in Egypt?", and ordered it made a market for the Muslims. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 134}

## Enlargements of the mosque
[[person/maslama-ibn-mukhallad-al-ansari|Maslama ibn Mukhallad]] enlarged the congregational mosque after Amr's building; it was he who required the people of Egypt to build minarets for the mosques in the year 53, and a minaret was built with his name inscribed on it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 178} Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan then pulled the mosque down in the year 77 and rebuilt it, and al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik wrote to Qurra ibn Sharik, governor of Egypt from the year 90, who pulled it down and rebuilt it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 179}
