## Overview
Yıldız Sarayı, the "Palace of the Star", occupies an enormous strip of land running from the Bosphorus shore up into the hills; most of its surviving buildings date from the reign of Abdül Hamit II {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.413}.

## History of the site
The gardens here were originally known as Çırağan, first mentioned under Murat IV, who bestowed them on his daughter Kaya Sultan and her husband Melek Ahmet Pasha before they reverted to the sultan; Ahmet III gave them to his son-in-law, the grand vezir Nevşehirli Damat Ibrahim Pasha, who hosted the sultan's most extravagant spring fêtes of the Tulip Period here {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.413}. The present palace began to take form under Mahmut II (r. 1808-39), and its visible buildings date from his reign through Abdül Hamit II's (r. 1876-1909), mostly the latter; they spread over the upper part of a large park, the last extensive stretch of greenery on the European shore of the lower Bosphorus {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.413-414}.

## Buildings
Several kiosks and greenhouses have been restored as cafés — Malta Köşkü, Çadır Köşkü and the conservatories — while the grandest and most interesting structure is the Şale, named for its resemblance to a Swiss chalet {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 pp.413-414}.
