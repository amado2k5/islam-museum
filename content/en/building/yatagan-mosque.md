## Overview
Yatağan Camii stands on the Sixth Hill just inside the ancient Byzantine land walls of Istanbul; it was built in Mehmet the Conqueror's time by one of his artillery generals, Hacı Ilyas Ağa {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.137}.

## Architecture
It is of the simple rectangular type with a wooden porch, a wooden roof and a gallery. The Turkish architectural historian Ekrem Hakkı Ayverdi holds the woodwork to be contemporary with the mosque and therefore of great interest; Freely thinks this highly unlikely given the district's frequent fires, judging the woodwork a quite inferior product of very recent date {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.137}.
