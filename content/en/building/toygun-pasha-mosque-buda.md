## Overview
Toygun Pasha, governor of Buda from 1553 to 1558, built a mosque in the quarter later named after him; his son Ahmed Bey completed it after his death, and it stood beside a bath and a dervish lodge. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 37} Evliya Çelebi counted it among the four large lead-roofed domed mosques of the city's outskirts. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 37}

## Conversion and what survives
After the Ottomans left Buda it was given to the Capuchins as a church; after 83 years it was extensively rebuilt in 1770 in a new style, the Capuchin Church of St Elizabeth at 32 Fő utca rising on its main walls. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 37} The mosque's window openings still show in the church walls, and its mihrab and the window above it are exposed inside. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 37} Ayverdi surveyed it in 1976 and drew a restitution: a typical classical mosque of cut stone with walls 120 cm thick, a square single-domed prayer hall and a five-bay portico, like the Mihrimah Sultan mosque at Üsküdar or the Bali Pasha mosque in Istanbul. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 37}
