## Overview
Omar Khalidi takes the Islamic Center of Washington as the first example of "imported design", in which a traditional design is transplanted whole from Islamic lands, and calls it the first of the large traditionally designed structures in the United States and still architecturally one of the most significant buildings Muslims have built there. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 4-5} The essay dates it to 1957; the photo caption says it was built in 1950. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 4-5} {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 10-11}

## Architecture
It was designed by Mario Rossi, an Italian architect practising in Cairo, with the help of engineers from the Egyptian Ministry of Pious Foundations, which cares for mosques supported by religious endowments. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 4-5} Its design draws on the Mamluk architecture of Cairo with Ottoman and Andalusian decorative motifs, and its furnishings are a mix of gifts: wall tiles from Turkey, chandeliers from Egypt, carpets from the Shah of Iran. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 10-11}

## Standing
Financed by the diplomatic missions of Muslim countries and by wealthy Muslim sponsors, the project became a symbol of Muslim unity and identity in the United States; the building is listed, and so protected, in the Historic American Buildings Survey. {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 4-5} {cite:khalidi-donnellon-mosques-usa-canada-catalogue pp. 10-11}
