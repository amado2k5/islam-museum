## Summary
Al-Khadra Mosque in [[place/nablus|Nablus]] (Hizn Sidna Ya'qub, "Jacob's Grief") is a mosque and shrine in the al-Yasmina quarter of the old town on the slopes of Mount Gerizim. It stands over an earlier building variously identified as a Samaritan synagogue of Samaritan tradition and a pre-1187 Frankish church or structure, whose Crusader chevron mouldings survive in the portal. {cite:mwnf-explore} {cite:archnet}

## The Mamluk building
It was rebuilt as a mosque in the Mamluk period; a marble foundation plaque over the northern entrance names Sultan al-Mansur Qalawun and his son al-Salih Ali. The rectangular prayer hall is roofed by three cross-vaults, and to the north stands a tall square minaret (about 30 metres), a rare surviving example of the square Mamluk minaret in Palestine, carrying a reused Samaritan Aramaic inscription. A rock-cut cave on the west is linked in local memory to Jacob's grief. {cite:mwnf-explore} {cite:travel-palestine}

## Recent history
The mosque was severely damaged in the Battle of Nablus in 2002, losing its Mamluk mihrab and parts of its western wall and entrance; the Nablus municipality restored it, and it is again a functioning mosque. {cite:travel-palestine}
