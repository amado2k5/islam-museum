## Overview
At Guamalti, south-west of English Bazar in the centre of the old city, is a ruined mosque; Ilahi Bakhsh wrote that near the abandoned indigo factory, east of it, were a minar in good order and a ruined mosque, to which he attributed a brick inscription of AH 711 from the time of Sultan Bahadur Khan. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 135} The author holds that this inscription cannot belong to the mosque, and that the known Guamalti inscription is one of AH 894 (1489) from the reign of Firuz Shah II, published by Blochmann and Ravenshaw and said to come from a mosque whose ruins are still traced near the factory. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 135} {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 136}

## What is known of the building
As far as it can be reconstructed, the mosque followed the oblong type, with a two-aisled prayer hall under ten domes and probably five concave mihrabs; what Ilahi Bakhsh took for a minar was, in the author's view, its corner tower. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 135} In the store of the Victoria and Albert Museum the author found carved black basalt fragments — pillars and framed arches — given by Reginald Porch and dated about 1450, which he attributes to this mosque. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 135}
