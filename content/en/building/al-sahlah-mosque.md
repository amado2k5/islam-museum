## Summary
The al-Sahlah Mosque of Kufa — 'the surviving remnant of the mosques the tribes staked out after the Jami'' and the most renowned of the mosques the Shi'a hold blessed {cite:musawat-atabat}.

It stands today in Kufa's outskirts about two kilometres northwest of the Great Mosque; al-Majlisi's report counts it among the blessed mosques alongside those of Ghani, Bani Zafar, al-Hamra and Ja'fi {cite:musawat-atabat}.

## The fabric
The source surmises the mosque predates Ali's arrival at Kufa, and cites Ibn al-Faqih's report identifying Ali's 'masjid Zafar' — one of Kufa's sanctified plots — as al-Sahlah. It measures 125×140 m with walls some 22 m high, each of its four sides reinforced by equally-spaced semicircular towers, and the prayer hall is paved in 24×24 cm brick {cite:musawat-atabat}.
