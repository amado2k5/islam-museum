## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Zaytunah is described by The Oxford Dictionary of Islam as Tunis's mosque and seat of higher learning since the early eighth century. Under the Hafsids (1207 to 1534), who made the city their capital, it rose to rank among North Africa's foremost institutions of Islamic learning. The Spanish seizure of Tunis in 1534 saw its libraries and mosques plundered and many books and manuscripts burned or carried off; restoration came under the Muradid and Husaynid beys between 1631 and 1957. In 1965 it lost its standing as an independent school, absorbed into the University of Tunis as its faculty of theology and Islamic studies. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2573}
