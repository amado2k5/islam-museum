## Overview
The mosque of Sultan al-Mu'ayyad Shaykh stands inside Bab Zuwayla in Cairo. Al-Malik al-Mu'ayyad Abu al-Nasr Shaykh al-Mahmudi al-Zahiri built it on the site of Khizanat Shamail, where criminals were imprisoned, and of the qaysariyya of Sanqur al-Ashqar, the Darb al-Saghira, and the qaysariyya of Baha al-Din Arslan {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.2143}.

## The qaysariyya
The qaysariyya of Sanqur al-Ashqar was taken into the Mu'ayyadi mosque when it was demolished in Jumada I 818 AH {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1535}.

## The minaret
Al-Maqrizi says that Baha al-Din Muhammad was in charge of the mosque's building and its upkeep, and he gives verses that were said about the tilt of the minaret over Bab Zuwayla {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.2147}.

## In Yeomans
Yeomans relates that [[person/al-muayyad-shaykh|al-Mu'ayyad Shaykh]], in a topsy-turvy career, was imprisoned in the Khazanat al-Shama'il jail near [[building/bab-zuwayla|Bab Zuwayla]] and vowed, if released, to destroy the prison and build a mosque on its site; he did so with a complex of mosque, mausolea, khanqah-madrasa and baths (1416-1421), and also built a maristan near the Citadel (1418-1420). {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.208} Its twin minarets, built on the towers of the Fatimid Bab Zuwayla, are among the city's great landmarks. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.208}

The mosque fell into ruin in the nineteenth century; only the vestibule, mausolea and prayer hall survive, and the courtyard is now a garden. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.208} Its ablaq portal with a trilobed muqarnas hood has a pishtaq and carries Sultan Hasan's bronze doors, taken from his mosque in what Yeomans calls dubious legal circumstances; he quotes Rogers that in Islamic architecture the sincerest flattery was theft, not imitation. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.208} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.209} A Fatimid wooden frieze, now in the British Museum, probably from a palace of the Bab Zuwayla district, was also built in. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.209}

Yeomans counts it the last royal hypostyle mosque with mausolea flanking the prayer hall, like Faraj's khanqah. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.209} The three-aisled prayer hall has a painted, gilded wooden ceiling; its dado is of marble panels taken from old Alexandrian houses at a time of marble scarcity, topped by blind arches on turquoise-glass colonnettes; the mihrab stands between red granite columns under a hood of radiating joggled voussoirs, and the minbar is ebony inlaid with ivory and mother-of-pearl. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.209}
