## Overview
A mosque in [[place/quba|Quba]], a village that belonged to the territory of [[place/medina|Medina]]. Wikidata dates its construction to 622 CE. {cite:wikidata} The Encyclopaedia of Islam, in its article on the mosque, records the tradition that it was the first mosque founded by Muslims, ahead of the [[building/prophets-mosque|Prophet's Mosque]] in Medina, and notes that the sources disagree on who founded it. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 673} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 674}

## The Prophet's stay at Quba
The article says that on his [[event/hijra|hijra]] the Prophet stopped at Quba with the family of [[group/banu-amr-ibn-awf|Amr ibn Awf]]. Reports give the length of his stay variously as 3, 5, 8, 14 or 22 days. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 673} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 674}

## Two accounts of its founding
According to one tradition, the Prophet found a mosque already standing when he arrived, built by the first emigrants and the [[group/ansar|Ansar]], and he prayed in it with them. Another tradition says the Prophet founded it himself, on land belonging to his host Kulthum, used as a drying yard for dates, or, in other reports, to a woman named Labba, who tethered her donkey there. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 674}

A legend grew out of this second account, modelled on the story of the founding of the principal mosque in Medina. In it the Prophet has first [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] try to mount a camel without success, then [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]]; where the animal goes, the mosque is built from stone brought from the Harra. The Prophet lays the first stone, and Abu Bakr and [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] the next ones. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 674}

## The Prophet's visits
The tradition says the Prophet afterwards visited the mosque every Saturday, riding or on foot, and a pillar is still shown beside which he led the prayer. Another report has him praying there on a Saturday in Rabi I of the year 4/625, on his way to the Banu al-Nadir, an episode the museum treats under the [[event/expedition-of-banu-al-nadir|expedition against that tribe]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 674}

## Its standing
The article lists Quba among the mosques that are highly recommended, beside the three that hold an official special position. It reports that a prayer there is said to equal an umra, or two visits to the mosque in Jerusalem. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 686} The same article mentions that [[person/sad-al-qaraz|Sa'd al-Karaz]], successor to Bilal as the caller to prayer under Umar, is said to have given the call for the Prophet at Quba. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 704}

## What scholars question
The article judges that the later community's customs and ideas have shaped the legend of this mosque. It then asks whether the older tradition, that the mosque was founded by the Prophet or even by his followers before he arrived, is also a later invention. It describes Leone Caetani as inclined to deny that the Prophet founded or recognised any mosque other than the one in Medina, pointing to a later tendency to link mosques everywhere with the Prophet and to the Qur'anic condemnation of the opposition mosque, the [[building/masjid-dirar|Masjid al-Dirar]]. The article also notes that, in one tradition, the reference to a mosque founded on piety is to the mosque of Quba; the page is not fully legible there, so the transmitter is left out. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 674}

## Limits of this page
The article gives no description of the building, its architecture or its later history, and no date for its founding; the 622 CE date comes only from Wikidata.
