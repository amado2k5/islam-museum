## Overview
The tomb of Shajar al-Durr near the shrine of Sayyida Nafisa in Cairo. Its site was a garden that she had built {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1384} {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1657}.

## The garden
Al-Maqrizi says that Shajar al-Durr built a garden, a house and baths near the shrine of Sayyida Nafisa {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1384}.

## The tomb
Buildings extended to the tomb of Shajar al-Durr, where the garden known by her name had been, and from her tomb they extended to the Nafisa shrine {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1657}.

## The house
Al-Maqrizi says that a property in Cairo, built by Jahārkas, was bought for the mother of Khalil, called Shajar al-Durr al-Salihiyya, in 655 AH {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1536}.

## The tomb in Yeomans
[[person/shajar-al-durr|Shajar al-Durr]] built a madrasa in the cemetery of Sayyida Nafisa, where her own mausoleum (1250) stands opposite the shrine of [[building/mausoleum-sayyida-ruqayya-cairo|Sayyida Ruqayya]], in an area where many female saints are venerated. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.121} It is a free-standing domed cube of stucco-covered brick, its exterior altered when it was absorbed into a nineteenth-century mosque; the dome has a keel profile, the stepped zone of transition has three lights on each axial side, and the qibla wall bears keel-arched panels, rosettes and lozenges. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.121}

Its finest feature is the mihrab, whose mosaic hood in gold, green, red and black shows a tree with mother-of-pearl fruit, an allusion to her name, "Tree of Pearls", framed by muqarnas and resting on the ends of a Fatimid wooden frieze carrying Kufic around the walls. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.121} Her grave's exact place is uncertain; the present cenotaph is thought to stand over the grave of a later Abbasid caliph, al-Mutawakkil I (d. 1406) or al-Mutawakkil III (d. 1538). {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.121}
