## Overview
The complex of the amir Qurqumas (1506-1507) in the [[place/eastern-cemetery-cairo|Eastern Cemetery]] was built beside the [[building/funerary-complex-of-sultan-inal|Inal complex]] out of admiration for it; it is larger, its central plan echoes Qaytbay's, and two-storey Sufi lodgings link the two. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.217}

## The building
The trilobed muqarnas portal on the north-east is flanked by the minaret on the right and a [[concept/sabil-kuttab|sabil-kuttab]] on the left forming the eastern corner; the south-east front is finely proportioned, composed of the sabil-kuttab, the prayer hall and the projecting mausoleum. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.217} The minaret is taller than Inal's on a similar scheme, with lozenges on the second storey and an eight-pillared pavilion on the third; the dome sits on a scrolled zone of transition, with lozenges on its lower half and chevron bands above. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.217}

## Qurqumas's other tomb
Yeomans notes another tomb of the amir Qurqumas (1511), first near the mosque of al-Hakim, moved in 1983 beside the Barsbay complex in the Eastern Cemetery and rebuilt without dome decoration. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.210} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.211}
