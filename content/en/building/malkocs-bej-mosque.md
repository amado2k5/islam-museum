## Overview
The Malkoç Bey mosque is the only surviving mosque in Siklós castle, built in the early sixteenth century by Malkoç Bey, brother of Kara Ali Bey, regimental commander of Szeged, of an *akıncı* (raider) family. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 185} Evliya Çelebi describes it as domed with a tall minaret, and Ayverdi drew it domed in his restitution. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 184} {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 185}

## History
Abandoned after the Ottoman withdrawal, it served as a dwelling in the nineteenth century, had minor repairs in 1969 and 1988, and was thoroughly restored in 1993 by the architect Gyula Cséfalvay, who completed the originally domed prayer hall with an octagonal cone on the brick walls and gave the interior a domed form with a wooden grid in the ceiling. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 185} That year it won the Europa Nostra restoration award, and it is now a museum of Turkish-Islamic objects. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 185}
