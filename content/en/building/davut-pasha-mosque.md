## Summary
Davut Pasha Camii on the Seventh Hill is the earliest extant mosque of known date from Beyazit II's reign; grand vezir Davut Pasha built it, and an Arabic inscription over the door gives the date as A.H. 480 (1485) {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.210}.

## Description
The porch's five-domed roof fell but four of its six granite columns still stand and the domes and arches are well restored; the plan is simple — a square chamber under a large blind dome, the mihrab in a five-sided apse projecting from the south wall, and two pairs of small rooms east and west that sheltered itinerant dervishes {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.210-211}. Its distinction is the beautiful shallow dome — distinctly less than a hemisphere, like Yavuz Selim Camii — with magnificently bold stalactite pendentives carried far down the corners of the walls {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.211}. Behind, a topsy-turvy graveyard surrounds the founder's octagonal türbe with its odd eight-segment dome; across the street the külliye's medrese survives nearly ruinous among houses, while its imaret and mektep have disappeared {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.211}.

## For young visitors
Sultan Beyazit II's minister built this mosque in 1485 and wrote its date in Arabic over the door — its honeycombed dome still amazes visitors.
