## Overview
The Ali Pasha mosque on Zrínyi Square in central Szigetvár is the only survivor of a small complex with a tomb, fountain and courthouse; it was founded by Müezzinzade Ali Pasha, who joined the Szigetvár campaign as agha of the Janissaries, was charged with building a mosque in the castle in Süleyman's memory, became grand admiral in 1568 and fell at Lepanto in 1571. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 141} Evliya Çelebi visited it and recorded its inscription of AH 997/1589-90 over the entrance. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 141}

## History
Unharmed in the Austrian siege of 1689, it served for a time as a powder store, became the parish church in 1712, and lost its minaret to lightning in 1719. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 141} Its dome, 13 metres across, was hidden under a roof after the conversion and bears frescoes of the deaths of Süleyman and of Miklós Zrínyi; it is now St Roch's Church and, like other churches in the town, keeps an Ottoman bath basin used as a font. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 141}
