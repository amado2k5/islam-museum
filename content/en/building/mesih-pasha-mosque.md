## Summary
Mesih Pasha Camii stands on the Fourth Hill about 400 metres north-west of Fatih Camii; it was built in 1585 for the eunuch Mesih Mehmet Pasha — infamous for cruelty as governor of Egypt — who became grand vezir briefly at ninety under Murat III {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.332}.

## Architect and plan
Many authorities credit an unknown architect, but Aptullah Kuran argues convincingly that Davut Ağa built it: Mesih Pasha commissioned Sinan, who handed full responsibility to Davut Ağa, the creative force in Ottoman architecture of the 1580s {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.332-333}. In the courtyard the founder's picturesque open türbe takes the şadırvan's usual central place, so the ablution fountains sit unusually under the domed porticoes; the plan is an octagon inscribed in a square with semidome squinches, but the expected side aisles under the galleries are instead exterior porches opening through huge glazed arches {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.333}. Mihrab and mimber are fine marble works and the tiles are of the best Iznik period {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.333}.

## For young visitors
In this mosque's courtyard an open tomb takes the place of the usual fountain — and there's a mystery: was it designed by Sinan or by his pupil Davut Ağa?
