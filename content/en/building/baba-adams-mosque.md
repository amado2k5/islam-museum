## Overview
The mosque of Baba Adam, the patron saint of the locality, stands at the ancient Hindu site of Rampal in Dhaka district, where Raja Ballal Sena built his palace, half a mile north of the Ballalbari. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 138} Its two-line Arabic inscription in naskh opens with the verse "the mosques belong to God" and the hadith on building mosques, then records that the exalted Malik Kafur built this congregational mosque in the time of Sultan Jalal al-Dunya wa al-Din Abu al-Muzaffar Fath Shah, son of Mahmud Shah, in mid-Rajab 888 (August 1483). {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 138}

## The building
It is a simple, unassuming building, smaller than the ten-domed mosques, divided by a three-arched colonnade into two aisles and three bays and roofed by six hemispherical domes much renovated in recent times. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 138} It measures 43 by 36 feet outside and 34 by 22 inside and reuses carved stone pillars of Hindu workmanship, 20 inches across, octagonal at the base and sixteen-sided above, which Asutosh Gupta called the "clubs" of Ballal Sena. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 138} The qibla wall has three mihrabs, the central one the most ornate, with a multi-cusped arch on carved pillars and rosettes in its spandrels. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 140} The author sees it as anticipating the six-domed mosques at Sailkuppa in Jessore and Satgaon in Hooghly. {cite:hasan-mosque-architecture-pre-mughal-bengal-1979 p. 140}
