## Overview
In 1724 the Ottoman army retook Yerevan and rebuilt the city, and Recep Pasha, commander of Tbilisi, built a mosque in the citadel in 1725. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 238} Yerevan then had eight mosques, two in the citadel and six in the outer quarters. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 238} An engraving of 1796 shows the mosque with a dome and a single-balcony minaret. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 238}

## Conversion and demolition
After the Russians occupied Yerevan in 1827 the mosque became a church: the crescent on the dome was replaced by a cross and the minaret turned into a bell tower. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 238} A later extensive repair added triangular pediments and a colonnaded gallery to its facades in a classical manner, which the author sees as meant to erase its architectural character. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 238} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 239} The travellers Karl Koch and August von Haxthausen, in 1836 and 1843, reported the citadel near collapse, with one of its two mosques a church and the other an arms depot. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 238} {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 239} The mosque was entirely demolished in 1930. {cite:yilmaz-kiliseye-cevrilen-turk-eserleri-1-2023 p. 239}
