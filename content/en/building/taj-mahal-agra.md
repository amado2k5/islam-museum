## Summary
The Taj Mahal moves the focus of the chahar bagh from the centre to the riverfront, where the mausoleum stands, with octagonal bastions and a mosque and a mehmankhana at the corners {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.4} {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.5}.

## Plan
The plan is a single chamfered octagon set in the noni-partite scheme, which Rezavi reads as Iranian axiomorphics coupled with Indian idiom {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.14}. He calls it one of the best achievements of world architecture {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.15}.

## Decoration
Its facades carry the most representative Mughal calligraphy, and the exterior uses the arch-and-panel system modified by quoin shafts {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.7} {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.8}.
