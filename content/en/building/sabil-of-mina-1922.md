## Overview
Among the sabils most singular in form is the sabil of [[place/mina|Mina]], built in 1340 AH / 1921-1922 and known from two photographs published by the author of *Mir'at al-Haramayn*. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.46}

## Description
[[person/muhammad-hamza-ismail-al-haddad|Al-Haddad]] reads from the photographs the great care given to it: a free-standing form without precedent, a projecting entrance block, striped stonework, basins, arched recesses, arches and hexagonal piers with engaged corner columns, merlons crowning the façades, and inscriptions including the verse [[passage/quran-76-21|al-Insan 76:21]], which he notes Evliya Çelebi also saw on some sabils of Medina. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.46} {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.52}

## Its place
The author sees the Mina sabil as a splendid finale to Islamic sabil architecture in the Arab lands and the Hijaz, since it gathered many elements and details rarely found together in one sabil. {cite:haddad-asbila-makka-madina-2004 p.51}
