## Overview
Süleyman the Magnificent died in his tent on his thirteenth and last campaign, the siege of Szigetvár, a day before the fortress fell, on 8 September 1566; his death was kept secret to hold the army together, and his embalmed entrails were buried where his tent had stood. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 155} After the conquest a memorial tomb was raised over them, with a prayer hall beside it and lodges and barracks around, enclosed by a wooden wall and a water ditch as a redoubt. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 155}

## History
After 1683 the Austrians took the site, used the mosque as a church for a time and then demolished everything, and its place was forgotten for three centuries. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 155} In 2012 the Turkish Cooperation and Coordination Agency (TİKA) and the town of Szigetvár agreed to search for it, and Turkish and Hungarian teams excavating on Vineyard Hill in 2015-17 found the foundations of a mosque, tomb and lodge and many Ottoman stone, metal and ceramic finds; a museum to show them is planned. {cite:yilmaz-ottoman-architecture-hungary-2024 p. 155}
