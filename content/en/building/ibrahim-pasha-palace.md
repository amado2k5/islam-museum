## Summary
İbrahim Pasha Sarayı stands directly opposite the entrance of the Sultan Ahmet I mosque, on the site of the ancient Hippodrome; it is believed to have been built by Süleyman at the beginning of his reign in 1520, replacing an earlier wooden structure {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.230}. In 1524 it passed to grand vezir İbrahim Pasha — a Greek convert who had married Süleyman's sister Hatice — who served as grand vezir until Süleyman had him executed in 1536 {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.230-231}.

## The largest Ottoman palace
It is the largest palace the Ottomans ever built, far greater than any building in Topkapı Sarayı {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.231}. Its great hall overlooking the Hippodrome was the grand vezir's office and probably later the High Court of Justice; afterwards it became a janissary barracks, part of it a prison, and by the early twentieth century an abandoned ruin whose north-eastern wing was demolished in 1937 for the Adliye law courts {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.231}. Restoration began in 1965 and the palace now houses the Museum of Turkish and Islamic Art, formerly in the Süleymaniye imaret {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.231}.

## The museum
The restored south-east wing stands two storeys around three sides of a courtyard, over splendid vaulted substructures {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.231-232}. Collections include Umayyad, Abbasid, Mamluk, Seljuk, Beylik and Ottoman works — carpets, manuscripts, calligraphy, miniatures, woodwork, stonework, ceramics, glass and metalwork — the rarest in the great hall, including the carpets depicted by Holbein and other western painters {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.232}. Beneath the great hall an ethnological collection displays objects of the Yürük, the semi-nomadic Turcomans of Anatolia {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.232-233}.

## For young visitors
The biggest palace the Ottomans ever built belonged to a vezir who was executed — today it keeps carpets and manuscripts a thousand years old.
