## Overview
The mosque of Banu Salima in [[place/medina|Medina]], in the house of Banu Salima near al-Madhadh. It is called the Mosque of the Two Qiblas because the qibla was changed there. It is a place linked with the event of the [[event/change-of-qibla|change of the qibla]]. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 373-376}

## Why it is called that
Al-Samhudi reports from Ibn Zabala that a man from [[group/banu-salima|Banu Salima]] came to them while they were praying the dawn prayer, and told them that the qibla had been changed. They turned around after having prayed two rak'as, until they faced the Ka'ba. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 373-376}

## Differing reports
There is disagreement among the transmitters about the time of the change. Al-Zamakhshari and the writers of the biographies place it at the noon prayer, when the Prophet prayed two rak'as with his companions and then turned and faced the spout, and the men moved to the women's place and the women to the men's. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 373-376}

Ibn Hajar reconciled the reports. Al-Majd noted that according to the report of Ibn Zabala, the mosque of Quba was the more fitting to carry this name. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 373-376}

## Whose mosque it was
According to Ibn Zabala, the mosque belonged to Banu Sawad ibn Ghanm ibn Ka'b ibn Salima, in the area of al-Madhadh. Al-Mutarri returned it to Banu Haram. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 213}
