## Summary
Yeşil Türbe, the mausoleum of Mehmet I, crowns the hill across from the Green Mosque in Bursa — a tall octagon under a low lead-covered dome, commissioned by Murat II after his father's death in May 1421 and built by the architect Hacı Ivaz Pasha {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.63}.

## Decoration and külliye
Its exterior walls were originally revetted in the deep blue and green tiles that gave the türbe and mosque their names; destroyed in the 1855 earthquake, they were replaced by inferior modern Kütahya tiles. The interior rivals the mosque itself: carved doors, tile wall revetment, a decorated mihrab and the sultan's cenotaph in golden calligraphy on deep blue {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.63}. The Yeşil külliye also included a medrese, an imaret and a hamam; only the imaret survives, now housing the Museum of Turkish and Islamic Art {cite:freely-ottoman-architecture-2011 p.63-64}.

## For young visitors
Across from Bursa's Green Mosque stands a green tomb for an Ottoman sultan — its walls once glowed entirely with blue and green tiles until an earthquake destroyed them.
