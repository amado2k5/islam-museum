## Overview
The mosque that the Prophet founded in [[place/medina|Medina]] after the Hijra. Wikidata dates its construction to 622 CE; this date rests on one source and needs an additional one. {cite:wikidata}

## Its site
The Prophet's she-camel knelt at its site, and he said: "This is the dwelling, God willing." It was a drying ground for dates belonging to two orphan boys, Sahl and Suhayl, sons of Amr, in the care of As'ad ibn Zurara. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 334-349}

The Prophet refused to accept it as a gift until he had bought it. According to al-Waqidi, he bought it for ten dinars, which [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] paid. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 334-349}

In al-Bukhari's report, he told the Banu al-Najjar to name its price, and they said: "We ask for its price only from God." He ordered the graves of the polytheists to be dug up, the ruins levelled and the palms cut down, and the palms were set in rows facing the qibla. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1868}

## Its construction
The Companions built it of mud brick on a foundation of stone about three cubits high. Its area is reported in different ways: seventy cubits by sixty, or a hundred by a hundred, making it square, or less than a hundred. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 334-349}

Its qibla was at first toward Jerusalem. When the qibla was changed, the back door was closed and a door opened opposite it. It has three doors, one known as the Gate of Atika, which is the Gate of Mercy, and the Gate of the House of Uthman. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 334-349}

It was not roofed at first. When the heat grew fierce it was shaded with palm trunks and fronds, and then plastered with mud. The report says that the Prophet said he wished for a shelter like the shelter of Moses. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 334-349}
