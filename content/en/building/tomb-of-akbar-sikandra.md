## Overview
The tomb of [[person/jalal-al-din-akbar|Akbar]] at Sikandra near [[place/agra|Agra]] was begun by the emperor in his lifetime, following the Timurid custom of building one's own tomb, and finished by his son. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.136}

## Description
It is a monolithic red sandstone structure inlaid with marble, set four-square in a quartered garden ([[concept/chaharbagh|chaharbagh]]) proportioned to set off the great tomb. For [[person/john-brookes|Brookes]] its scale, like that of its predecessor [[building/humayun-tomb|Humayun's tomb]], foreshadows later Mughal building; the water channels linking its tanks are still narrow but now sit in a stronger axial plan. The garden today lacks its classic planting of cypress, pine and plane and the life of running water. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.136} The slender minarets of its gateway were a forerunner of those of the [[building/taj-mahal|Taj Mahal]]. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.154}
