## Summary
Saba was one of the oldest peoples known for [[place/yemen|Yemen]] among the original Arabs. Their name has been found in the excavations at [[place/ur|Ur]] twenty-five centuries BCE, and the flowering of their civilisation and the extension of their rule begin eleven centuries BCE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}.

## The kingdom's phases in the author's estimate
Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum divides Saba's phases by the following estimate {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran 1/133}:
- The centuries before 650 BCE: their kings bore the title "Mukarrib of Saba". Their capital was [[place/sirwah|Sirwah]], whose ruins lie a day's journey west of [[place/marib|Ma'rib]] and are known as Khuraybah. In their time the building of [[building/marib-dam|the Ma'rib Dam]] began. It is said that Saba extended their rule so far that they founded colonies inside and outside Arabia {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}.
- From 650 BCE to 115 BCE: they gave up the title "Mukarrib" and became known as the kings of Saba. They took Ma'rib as their capital in place of Sirwah, whose ruins lie sixty miles from Sana'a on its eastern side {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}.
- From 115 BCE to 300 CE: the tribe of [[group/himyar|Himyar]] overcame the kingdom of Saba and took Raydan as its capital, which was then called Zafar. See [[polity/himyarite-kingdom|the Himyarite kingdom]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}.
- From 300 CE until Islam reached Yemen: a period of unrest and occupations {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}.

## In the Qur'an
The author says that the great flood which destroyed the Ma'rib Dam is the one the Qur'an mentions as the flood of al-Arim ([[passage/quran-34-16|Saba 16]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}. See [[event/flood-of-al-aram|the flood of al-Arim]].

## Notes for researchers
The author warns that the sources differ widely on the dating, and reports that some writers called these details "the fables of the ancients" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}. The dates above are therefore the author's estimate, drawn from Tafhim al-Qur'an and Tarikh Ard al-Qur'an (History of the Land of the Qur'an). They have not yet been checked against the academic dating of the Sabaean inscriptions.

## For young visitors
Saba was a very ancient kingdom in Yemen, famous for the great Ma'rib Dam, which held back the rainwater to water the fields.
