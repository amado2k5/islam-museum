## Overview
The Hammudid state, of the Idrisids, descended from Hammud ibn Maymun, in Ceuta, Tangier and the land of Ghumara, and then in al-Andalus. It began with Ali ibn Hammud taking the caliphate in Cordoba in 407 AH (1016 CE), and ended with the killing of Sakut the Barghawati in Ceuta in 476 AH (1083 CE). {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## Ali ibn Hammud
When the Amirid state died out, the Berbers appointed Sulayman ibn al-Hakam, and Ali and al-Qasim, the sons of Hammud, became close to him. Sulayman appointed Ali over Tangier, but he broke away, called himself caliph, crossed to al-Andalus and took the caliphate. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## The family and the freedmen
Members of his family, al-Qasim, Yahya and Idris, succeeded one another in Cordoba, Malaga and Algeciras. Then Sakut the Barghawati, the chamberlain, a freedman of theirs, made himself independent in Ceuta and Tangier. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## The end
Yusuf ibn Tashfin attacked him in 476 AH and killed him, and his treasures were found, among them the ring of Yahya ibn Ali ibn Hammud. Their state died out. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}
