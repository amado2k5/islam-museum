## Summary
The Nabataean kingdom was the government of the sons of [[person/nabit|Nabit ibn Isma'il]] in northern Hijaz, as presented in al-Raheeq al-Makhtum. Their civilisation flourished, and they formed a strong government to which the peoples on its edges submitted. They took [[place/petra|Petra]] as their capital, and no one could oppose them until the Romans came and destroyed them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20}.

## Their effect on Yemen's trade
In his account of [[group/himyar|Himyar]], the author says that Yemen's trade largely failed, first because the Nabataeans extended their control over northern Hijaz, and then because the Romans dominated the sea trade routes {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}.

## A dispute over the lineage of some tribes
The author reports the view of Sulayman al-Nadawi that the kings of the house of Ghassan and the Ansar of the Aws and Khazraj descend from the sons of Nabit ibn Isma'il and their remnants in those lands, not from Qahtan {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran 2/78-86}.

## What is not covered here
al-Raheeq does not date the kingdom or name its kings. The academic history of the Nabataeans and their inscriptions has not yet been added.

## For young visitors
The Nabataeans were a people who built the city of Petra, carved into the rock, in the north of the Arabian Peninsula. They had a strong kingdom before the Romans defeated them.
