## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes the Afsharids as a dynasty of Turkic origin that governed Iran from 1736 to 1796 and, at its widest, reached from Iraq to northern India. Its founder, Nadir Shah Afshar, who reigned from 1736 to 1747, defeated the armies of the Shii Safavids with a force made up largely of Sunni Afghans. The Dictionary calls him a Shii in name only. It says he pressed Shii and Sunni religious leaders into a settlement so as to give weight to his claims upon Ottoman Iraq and Mughal India. The Shii scholars gave up practices openly hostile to Sunnis, and the Sunni scholars accepted certain principles of Shii law. His efforts, religious and political, to wipe out the Safavid Shii heritage did not succeed in the end, the Dictionary concludes. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e60}
