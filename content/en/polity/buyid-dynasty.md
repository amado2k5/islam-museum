## Overview
The Buyid dynasty, of the Daylam, founded by [[person/ali-ibn-buya|Ali ibn Buya]] and his brothers. Ibn Khaldun says it began in 322 AH (934 CE) and ended in 447 AH (1055 CE), when Tughril entered Baghdad. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## The rise
The Buyids were commanders of the Daylam. Ibn Khaldun says that they reached out to the territories of the Abbasid caliphs, since they saw no one to resist or defend them. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

They ruled Isfahan and al-Ray, then the land of Persia, then Shiraz and Arrajan. Mu'izz al-Dawla took al-Ahwaz in 325 AH, and then Wasit and Baghdad in 334 AH. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## The caliphs under their guardianship
The Abbasid caliphs came under their guardianship and tutelage, from the caliphate of al-Mustakfi until the end of their state. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## Three branches
- Fars: for Imad al-Dawla and his sons.
- The Jibal: for Rukn al-Dawla and his sons.
- Iraq: for Mu'izz al-Dawla and his sons. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## The end
Tughril moved to the seat of rule in Shawwal 447 AH, and his foothold in kingship and authority was secured. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}
