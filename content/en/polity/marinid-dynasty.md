## Overview
The Marinid dynasty, a ruling house founded in 1215 CE and ended in 1465 CE, recorded as the successor of [[polity/almohad-caliphate|the Almohad caliphate]]. {cite:wikidata}

## Not covered here
The two dates come from Wikidata, and that is a single source; a second source is needed. The data contain no sultans and no events so far.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives much of episodes 3 and 4 of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]] to the Marinids' part in defending [[polity/emirate-of-granada|the kingdom of Granada]], and presents them as an example that righteous leadership is part of the making of victory {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:02:30}.

### The rise of the state and its character
Al-Suwaidan says the Banu Marin rose against the Almohads inside the Maghreb at the time Seville was falling, and that their state was, in his words, a Sunni state founded on piety that helped Granada from time to time {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep02 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep02 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep02 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep02 00:19:00}. In his view the Andalusians were used, for more than two hundred years, to "importing victory" from outside whenever their land neared collapse: once from the Almoravids, once from the Almohads, and a third time from the Marinids {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep02 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:05:30}.

### Ya'qub ibn Mansur al-Marini
Al-Suwaidan says [[person/abu-yusuf-yaqub-marini|Ya'qub ibn Mansur al-Marini]] is called al-Marini to tell him apart from [[person/yaqub-al-mansur-al-muwahhidi|Ya'qub al-Mansur the Almohad]], and counts both among the great men and leading commanders of Muslim history {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:04:30}. He describes him as upright, generous, brave and eloquent, and says that when Muhammad ibn al-Ahmar I appealed to him he crossed to al-Andalus at the head of the Marinid army and repelled the Christian attack on Granada {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:05:00}.

### The battle of Don Nuño
Al-Suwaidan relates that under Muhammad al-Faqih ibn al-Ahmar, Castile attacked Granada with an army of 90,000; al-Faqih called on Ya'qub al-Marini, who was busy with revolts at home and the defence of his own state and so could raise only 5,000 men in 673 AH; Granada raised as many, making an army of 10,000 under Ya'qub's command {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:12:00}. He says the Castilian commander was called Don Nuño, that the battle is named after him, and that it took place in 674 AH, 1276 CE {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:13:00}. He relates that Ya'qub roused his army himself from the front rank, that his address spoke of paradise opening its gates and lying beneath the shade of swords and closed with the verse on patience and steadfastness [[passage/quran-3-200|Al Imran 200]], and that the men embraced, wept and made ready for martyrdom {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:14:00}. The Muslims won, he says: 6,000 of the Christians were killed and 8,000 captured, Don Nuño was killed and the rest fled; for him it was the first great victory since [[event/battle-of-alarcos-591|Alarcos]] in 591 AH, 1195 CE {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:14:30}.

### The campaigns after the battle
Al-Suwaidan relates that the army then split in two: one half under Ibn al-Ahmar went to [[place/jaen|Jaén]] and took it, the other under Ya'qub al-Marini went to [[place/seville|Seville]], whose people made peace on payment of jizya; Ya'qub then besieged [[place/cordoba|Córdoba]], which he did not take but which agreed to submit on payment of jizya {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:16:00}. He says Ya'qub gave all the spoils to the people of al-Andalus and went back to the Maghreb taking nothing, as [[person/yusuf-ibn-tashfin|Yusuf ibn Tashfin]] had done after [[event/zallaqa-504|al-Zallaqa]], and sees in this scrupulous piety and abstinence {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep03 00:16:30}.

### Almería and Tarifa
Al-Suwaidan relates that the governor of [[place/almeria|Almería]], one of the three provinces under Granada with near self-rule, died while Ya'qub was on his way home; his young son, who admired Ya'qub and his victories, offered to join Almería to the Maghreb for the sake of Muslim unity, and Ya'qub accepted and left a garrison of 3,000 men there {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:05:30}. A further 3,000 came from the Maghreb to help, and Ya'qub placed them at [[place/tarifa|Tarifa]], near [[place/gibraltar|Gibraltar]], as a reserve for al-Andalus, so that Almería and Tarifa came under the Marinids {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:06:30}.

### Muhammad al-Faqih's alliance with Castile
Al-Suwaidan relates that Muhammad al-Faqih ibn al-Ahmar feared his land would pass to the Marinids, recalling how al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad had called in Yusuf ibn Tashfin only for the Almoravids to take all al-Andalus, and so he asked the king of Castile to help drive Ya'qub out of Tarifa, and the two besieged it from two sides {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:08:30}. Ya'qub came back, raised his armies and defeated the Christians in a great battle in 677 AH, 1279 CE; Ibn al-Ahmar then showed remorse and apologised, and Ya'qub pardoned him and restored him to his throne, which al-Suwaidan calls perhaps "the mistake of history", since Ya'qub could have removed him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:10:00}.

### Ya'qub's death and the loss of Tarifa
Al-Suwaidan says Ya'qub ibn Mansur al-Marini died in 685 AH, 1286 CE, and was succeeded by his son Yusuf ibn al-Mansur, and that al-Faqih again turned to the Christians to drive the Marinids out of Tarifa {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:12:30}. He relates that Yusuf, new to power, could not save it; Tarifa fell, and although the Christians had agreed to give it to Ibn al-Ahmar they kept it, and because it overlooks the Strait of Gibraltar its loss cut the Maghreb's aid off from al-Andalus {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:13:30}.

### Ibn al-Hakim and the end of aid
Al-Suwaidan relates that Muhammad III ibn al-Ahmar, called al-A'mash ("the weak-sighted"), succeeded on al-Faqih's death in 701 AH, 1302 CE; being weak, he was ruled by his vizier Abu Abdallah ibn al-Hakim, who raised an army and sent it not north but to the Maghreb, where it took [[place/ceuta|Ceuta]] from the Marinids {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:15:30}. He says the vizier also wrote to a Marinid prince and armed him to rise against Yusuf ibn al-Mansur, stirring civil strife in the Maghreb, and that the result was the fall of [[place/gibraltar|Gibraltar]] to the Christians in 709 AH, 1309 CE and the complete isolation of al-Andalus from the Maghreb {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep04 00:17:00}. After these repeated betrayals, he says, the help Granada had relied on from the Maghreb for so long was cut off {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep05 00:04:30}.
