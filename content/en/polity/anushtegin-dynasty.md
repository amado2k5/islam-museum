## Summary
The Anushtegins were the last house to bear the title Khwarazmshah; under them the state reached its economic and political height and became a vast empire, as the quality and ornament of its coins show. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 11} Shohruxmirzo Ismoilov notes that no coins of the first two rulers of the house are known, and that the earliest known are those of Ala al-Din Atsiz, modelled on Seljuk coins. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 95} He infers that the Seljuks did not allow the rulers of Khwarazm to strike coins until about 1129. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 117}

## From vassalage to sultanate
- Atsiz (521–551/1127–1157): his first coins name a Seljuk sultan as overlord, Sanjar on some and Mas'ud, sultan of the Iraq Seljuks, on five (538, 539 and 543 AH); he struck in his own name alone from about 548/1154, and the title "sultan" first appears on Khwarazmian coins in his reign. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 95} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 96} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 97} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 98}
- Il-Arslan (551–567/1157–1172): struck at Jand after his father made him governor there, then independently, and took the title "the greatest sultan" after subduing Nasa, Dihistan and Nishapur. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 99} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 100} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 101}
- Sultanshah Mahmud (567–589/1172–1193): two types, one a gold coin with the title sultan. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 102}
- Ala al-Din Tekish (567–596/1172–1200): coins at Khwarazm and Nishapur in the names of the caliphs al-Mustadi and then al-Nasir. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 103}
- Ala al-Din Muhammad: coins were struck in his name in more than thirty cities according to Buniyatov, with titles including "the second Alexander" and "proof of the Commander of the Faithful". {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 107} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 109} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 110}
- Jalal al-Din Mingburnu: struck in widely separated regions, including Tbilisi after he took it in 1226, where coins of irregular weights and shapes, triangular, square, oval, rhombic and drop-shaped, were issued; the author reads them as evidence of his hard last years. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 116} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 117}

## Coinage
The author counts 135 Anushtegin types: 13 of Atsiz, 8 of Il-Arslan, 2 of Sultanshah, 22 of Tekish, 64 of Muhammad and 24 of Jalal al-Din. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 117} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 118} He notes that Qur'anic verses were dropped from most of them and that some carry images of animals and people, such as a horseman, a horse and a bull, because they followed the models of earlier rulers of the lands annexed to the state. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 11} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 12} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 95} He says the title "Khwarazmshah" appears on no ruler's coin after Il-Arslan: the rulers were styled sultan because the state had outgrown Khwarazm, and the title Khwarazmshah was given to princes governing provinces. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 117} Its mints in Transoxiana included Samarqand, Bukhara and Termez, and in Khurasan and the former Ghurid lands Herat, Balkh, Merv, Nishapur, Ghazna and Peshawar. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 164} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 173}
