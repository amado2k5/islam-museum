## Overview
The Tulunid state of Ahmad ibn Tulun, his sons and his clients, the Banu Tughj, in Egypt and Syria. [[person/ahmad-ibn-tulun|Ahmad ibn Tulun]] entered Egypt in Ramadan 254 AH (868 CE), and the state died out in 292 AH (905 CE). {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1092-1100}

## Founding
Ahmad entered Egypt as a deputy for Bakbak, then became independent there and added Syria to it. He built the Qata'i' east of Fustat. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1092-1100}

## The caliphs
Ahmad was succeeded by his son Khumarawayh, then al-Jaysh, then Harun. Their state then failed, and the Qaramita spread corruption in Syria. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1092-1100}

## The end
al-Muktafi sent Muhammad ibn Sulayman the secretary, who recovered Syria and entered Egypt. He put the Tulunids in chains and burned the Qata'i' in 292 AH, and their state died out. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1092-1100}

## The dynasty in Yeomans's account
Yeomans holds that [[person/ahmad-ibn-tulun|Ibn Tulun]] restored Egypt's independence, making it the centre of an empire with a status it had not known since Pharaonic times, and changed the face of its art and architecture with what he brought from Samarra. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.28} The state reached its widest extent with the settlement of 886 between al-Muwaffaq and [[person/khumarawayh|Khumarawayh]], which guaranteed the family Egypt, Syria, Cilicia and parts of northern Iraq for thirty years. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.40} After Khumarawayh's murder in 896 his fourteen-year-old son Abu al-Asakir Jaysh succeeded and was killed within months; decline followed, Syria was thrown into turmoil by the Qarmati revolt, the caliph intervened and crushed it, then sent his army against Egypt by land and sea; it entered Fustat in 905, [[place/al-qatai|al-Qata'i]] was destroyed except for the [[building/masjid-ibn-tulun|Mosque of Ibn Tulun]], and Egypt returned to Abbasid rule for thirty years under weak Turkish governors. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.41}
