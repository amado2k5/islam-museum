## Overview
The Hammudids were a dynasty that reigned over various towns in Muslim Spain from 407/1016 until 450/1058. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 157} They were genuine Idrisids: their great-grandfather Hammud was the great-grandson of Idris II. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 157}

## Ali b. Hammud and Cordoba
When Sulayman al-Musta'in took the caliphal throne a second time in Shawwal 403/May 1013 he allotted Ali b. Hammud the governorship of Ceuta, and his brother al-Qasim that of Algeciras, Tangier and Arzila. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 157} Ali declared independence on the pretext of freeing Hisham II, defeated and captured Sulayman in Muharram 407/July 1016, and, once it was proved Hisham had been murdered, executed Sulayman and was recognised as caliph, the first non-Marwanid on the Cordovan throne. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 157} His rule later turned harsh and he was murdered by three Slav palace slaves; his Zanata partisans proclaimed his brother al-Qasim. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 157}

## From al-Qasim to the end
Al-Qasim ruled moderately for three years; the Berbers then invited Yahya, Ali's eldest son, who was proclaimed caliph in the Alcazar of Cordoba on 22 Rabi II 412/5 August 1021 and held on a year and a half; al-Qasim returned for six months until the Cordovans rebelled on 21 Jumada II 414/10 September 1023. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 157} Yahya reigned at Malaga until 427/1035, with successors there until 449/1057, when Badis of Granada entered it and deposed the last, Muhammad II al-Mustali; at Algeciras al-Qasim's son Muhammad al-Mahdi reigned from 431/1039 to 440/1048 and his son al-Qasim al-Wathiq to 450/1058, when the town was taken by the Abbadids of Seville. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 157}
