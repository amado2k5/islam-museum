## Overview
The Banu al-Zurayi' of Aden, missionaries of the Fatimids in Yemen. They were of the clan of Jusham ibn Yam of Hamdan. The first of them to gather rule was the missionary al-Da'i ibn Abi al-Su'ud ibn al-Zurayi', who took Aden in 533 AH (1138 CE). {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1039-1040}

## Origin
They were among the closest clans of al-Sulayhi to him, and Ahmad al-Mukarram ibn Ali al-Sulayhi appointed them. Then they split, and the Banu al-Zurayi' ibn al-Abbas ibn al-Mukarram prevailed after great wars. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1039-1040}

## The kings
al-Da'i ibn Abi al-Su'ud died seven months after taking Aden, and his son al-Aghar succeeded him. Then their freedman Muhammad ibn Saba' became autocratic, was called al-Da'i al-Mu'azzam, and took the kunya Sayf Amir al-Mu'minin. He bought the fortress of Dhu Jibla, the seat of the rule of the Sulayhids. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1039-1040}

Their last king was Yasir ibn Bilal, who gave generously to poets, and Ibn Qalaqis praised him. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1039-1040}

## The end
Sayf al-Dawla, brother of Salah al-Din, took Aden and seized Yasir, and their state came to an end. They had founded the city of al-Hudayda near Aden. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1039-1040}
