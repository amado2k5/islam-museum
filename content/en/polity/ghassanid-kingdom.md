## Summary
The kingdom of the [[group/ghassan|Ghassanids]], the house of Jafna of the [[group/al-azd|Azd]], were kings of the Arabs and agents of the Romans in [[place/al-sham|Syria]]. The Sealed Nectar counts them with the kings of Yemen and al-Hira among the crowned kings who were not independent {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23}.

## Before them: the Dajaima
In the age when the Arabs were in turmoil through migrations, clans of [[group/qudaa|Qudaa]] reached the edges of Syria. They were from the Banu Sulayh ibn Hulwan, and among them were the Banu Dajam, known as the [[group/dajaima|Dajaima]]. The Romans made use of them to keep the desert Arabs from troubling them, and as a force against the Persians. They appointed one of them as king. Among their most famous kings was [[person/ziyad-ibn-al-habula|Ziyad ibn al-Habula]]. Their period is roughly estimated as running from the early second century CE to about its end {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/34} {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran 2/80-82}.

## The rise of the Ghassanids
The Dajaima's rule ended when the Ghassanids came and overcame them in what they held. The Romans then made the Ghassanids kings over the Arabs of Syria, with [[place/dumat-al-jandal|Dumat al-Jandal]] as their seat {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27}. The father of the Ghassanid kings was [[person/jafna-ibn-amr|Jafna ibn Amr]] al-Azdi. The name is traced to a water source in the Hijaz known as Ghassan {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}.

## The end
The Ghassanids continued to rule Syria as agents of the Roman kings until the Battle of Yarmouk in 13 AH. Their last king, [[person/jabala-ibn-al-ayham|Jabala ibn al-Ayham]], submitted to Islam in the time of Umar ibn al-Khattab {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/34}.

## Religion and dependency
The Ghassanids embraced Christianity because of their proximity to the Romans {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}. The tribes of the Syrian desert were subject to the Ghassanids in name only, not in fact {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32}.

## For young visitors
The Ghassanids were Arab kings in the land of Syria who served the Roman emperor. Their last king, Jabala, became a Muslim in the time of Umar.
