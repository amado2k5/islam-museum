## Overview
The Banu Hud, a family of Arab extraction, ruled Saragossa and were among the more important of the Party Kings of 5th/11th-century Spain. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 pp. 574-575} The article traces them to the tribe of Judham and notes that a legendary descent from the prophet Hud is mentioned by Ibn Khaldun. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 574}

## The founder and al-Muqtadir
Sulayman b. Hud al-Judhami, later styled al-Musta'in, occupied Saragossa in Muharram 431/September-October 1039 after the murder of the Tujibid Mundhir II, and ruled most of the Ebro valley from Saragossa and Lerida to Huesca, Tudela and Calatayud; he died in 438/1046 after dividing his possessions among his five sons. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 574} Ahmad I al-Muqtadir succeeded him, struggled with his brother Yusuf of Lerida, took Tortosa in 453/1061, expelled the Amirid Ali b. Mujahid from Denia in 468/1075-76, and built the Aljaferia of Saragossa, whose remains still stand. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 574} Abu l-Walid al-Baji and Ibn Ammar were at his court, and he died in 474/1081 or 475/1082. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 pp. 574-575}

## Al-Mu'taman and the fall of the line
Yusuf al-Mu'taman reigned until 478/1085 and was a student and author on mathematics. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 pp. 574-575} Ahmad II al-Musta'in was defeated near Huesca in 487/1094 (Alcoraz, 1096) and died fighting at Valtierra, near Tudela, on 1 Rajab 503/24 January 1110. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 575} The Almoravid commander Muhammad b. al-Hajj entered Saragossa on 10 Dhu l-Hijja 503/30 June 1110, Abd al-Malik Imad al-Dawla withdrew to Rueda, and the Almoravids held the city nine years until Alfonso I of Aragon took it on 3 Ramadan 512/18 December 1118. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 575}
