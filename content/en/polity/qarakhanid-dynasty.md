## Overview
The Turkish state in Kashgar and the lands of Turkestan. They were the kings of Turkestan, with Kashgar as their seat, and Sagun and Khimu reaching the borders of Shash. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1153-1154}

## Beginnings
The first of them to embrace Islam was Satuq Qarakhan, who called himself Abd al-Malik. They obeyed the Samanids and called them to arms in their wars. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1153-1154}

## Bukhara
Abu Ali ibn Simjur tempted them with the kingship of Bukhara when the Samanid state was unsettled in the 390s AH. Qarakhan went to their lands and took them, and died on his way from Bukhara. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1153-1154}

## Ibn Khaldun's description
Ibn Khaldun describes him as a just ruler of good conduct, a lover of scholars, a Sunni with Shi'i leanings. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1153-1154}

## Ilek Khan
His brother Ilek Khan Sulayman, called Shahir al-Dawla, succeeded him, and took Bukhara on the tenth of Dhu al-Qa'da in the year 389 AH. He seized Bakthrazun, Abd al-Malik and the deposed Abu al-Harith Mansur, and the Samanid state died out. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1153-1154}
