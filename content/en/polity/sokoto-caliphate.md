## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam credits Uthman Dan Fodio ([[person/usman-dan-fodio]]) with founding the Sokoto Caliphate in nineteenth-century Nigeria; he waged what it calls a holy war on the Hausa rulers, seeking the unity of the population under the caliphate. It describes the caliphate as a loose confederation of emirates, each recognizing its head as the commander of the faithful. Many of the founder's descendants, it says, belonged to the earliest generation of Nigeria's national leaders once independence came (1960). The sultan of Sokoto, it adds, is still seen as the Muslim community's spiritual leader, although lawmaking and executive power now rest with local, state and federal bodies. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2239}
