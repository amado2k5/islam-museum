## Summary
The kingdom of [[group/himyar|Himyar]] was the ruler of [[place/yemen|Yemen]] after [[polity/kingdom-of-saba|Saba]]. Himyar prevailed over the kingdom of Saba in the period that The Sealed Nectar dates from 115 BCE, and took Raydan as its capital instead of Marib. Raydan was then called Zafar, and its ruins lie on Mount Mudawwar near Yarim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}.

## The decline
In this period they began to fall and decline. Their trade largely failed: first because the Nabataeans extended their control over northern Hijaz; second because the Romans came to dominate the sea trade routes after extending their power over Egypt, Syria and northern Hijaz; and third because of rivalry between the tribes. These factors caused the dispersal and migration of the Qahtanites {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}.

## The age of upheaval (from 300 CE)
Upheavals, coups and civil wars followed one another in Yemen, leaving it open to foreigners until they ended its independence. The Romans entered [[place/aden|Aden]], and with their help the Abyssinians occupied Yemen for the first time in 340 CE, exploiting the rivalry between Hamdan and Himyar. Their occupation lasted until 378 CE ([[event/first-abyssinian-occupation-of-yemen|the first Abyssinian occupation]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}. Then Yemen regained its independence, but breaches began to appear in the Marib dam, until [[event/flood-of-al-aram|the Flood of al-Arim]] in 450 or 451 CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}. In 523 CE, [[person/dhu-nuwas|Dhu Nuwas]], the Jewish ruler, led his campaign against the Christians of Najran ([[event/massacre-of-najran|the Ditch]]). As a result, the Roman emperors incited the Abyssinians, who occupied Yemen again in 525 CE ([[event/abyssinian-occupation-of-yemen|the second Abyssinian occupation]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}. After the Battle of the Elephant, the Yemenis called on the Persians and gained independence in 575 CE, led by Ma'dikarib ibn [[person/sayf-ibn-dhi-yazan|Sayf Dhi Yazan]] al-Himyari. He was then assassinated, the kingship passed away from the house of Dhi Yazan, and Yemen became a Persian governorship ([[event/persian-rule-of-yemen|the Persian rule]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}.

## Religion
Judaism entered Yemen through [[person/tuban-asad-abu-karib|Tubban As'ad Abu Karib]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40}. Some men of Yemen adopted Zoroastrianism during the Persian occupation {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}.

## Notes for researchers
The dates are the author's estimates. He points out the wide disagreement among the sources over the years {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}.

## For young visitors
Himyar ruled Yemen after Saba. Then it weakened, the Abyssinians occupied its lands twice, and the Persians ruled it until Islam came.
