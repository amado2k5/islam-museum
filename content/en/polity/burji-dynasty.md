## Overview
The Circassian Mamluk sultanate, the Burji line, begins with al-Zahir Barquq's usurpation of the throne in 784/November 1382. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 pp. 343-344} The article regards the Circassian sultans as a dynasty by mamluk clientage rather than blood descent, since Qaytbay was a mamluk of Barsbay, himself a mamluk of Barquq. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 pp. 343-344}

## The pattern of succession
The sultanate follows an almost invariable pattern: a magnate usurps the throne, it passes to his son, and within a few years another usurper deposes him; but the usurpers emerged from the military households of earlier sultans, chiefly those of Barquq and Qaytbay, each of which produced five rulers. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 pp. 343-344} The article says that although the state kept the appearance of a great power through the 9th/15th century, it was in prolonged economic and military decline, which it traces to plague mortality more than to political disorder. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 344}

## Timur and the Ottomans
Barquq met the danger from Timur by sheltering Ahmad b. Uways the Jalayirid in 796/1394 and by a common front with the Ottomans and the Golden Horde; his son Faraj evacuated Syria in 803/1400-1 when Timur occupied it, but Timur did not invade Egypt. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 344} Under Barsbay a pepper monopoly was set up in 832/1428 and Cyprus conquered in 829/1426. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 344} The sultanate fought one major land war with the Ottomans, between 890/1485 and 896/1491 under Qaytbay, which maintained the status quo. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 344}
