## Overview
An Islamic state that ruled most of the Indian subcontinent from Zahir al-Din Babur in 1526, reaching its height under Akbar and Shah Jahan and dissolving gradually after Aurangzeb; Richards presents its imperial history to 1720.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes the Mughal Empire as a Muslim realm on the Indian subcontinent, established in 1526 by Babur (died 1530), who traced his descent to Timur through his father and to Genghis Khan through his mother. Its peak came under four great rulers, Akbar (reigned 1556-1605), Jahangir (1605-27), Shah Jahan (1627-57) and Aurangzeb (1658-1707), and Mughal rule extended over the entire subcontinent apart from the far south. When Aurangzeb died, the empire's strength faded and its former provinces broke away as independent states; in 1857 the British removed the last emperor. The Dictionary says Akbar set up the empire's administrative systems, drawing on Hindu Rajputs, Indian Muslims and Persians, bringing Hindus into the administration at every level and marrying Rajput princesses; his religious tolerance, it says, included ending the tax on non-Muslims. Along with the Safavids and the Ottomans, it says, the Mughals form one of three great empires in which Islamic culture reached some of its highest expressions, architecture above all. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1573}
