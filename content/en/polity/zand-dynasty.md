## Overview
The Encyclopaedia of Islam describes the Zand as an Iranian pastoral tribe of the eastern central Zagros, from which sprang a dynasty that ruled western Persia in 1164-1209/1751-94. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 460} They belonged to the Lakk group of Lurs, centred on the villages of Pari and Kamazan near Malayir; in 1144/1732 Nadir Shah deported thousands of Bakhtiyari and a number of Zand families to northern Khurasan, and after his assassination in 1160/1747 the Zand returned under Muhammad Karim Beg, later Karim Khan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 460}

## Karim Khan
Allied with the Bakhtiyari 'Ali Mardan Khan, Karim Khan occupied Isfahan in 1163/1750 in the name of a Safavid princeling styled Isma'il III; he later took the title wakil al-dawla, "regent", and by 1176/1762 had defeated three other contenders and pacified most of western Iran from the Caspian shore and Adharbaydjan to Kirman and Lar. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 460} He lived in Shiraz from 2 Safar 1179/21 July 1765 until his death on 13 Safar 1193/1 March 1779, did not take the title of shah, and reinterpreted wakil as "representative of the subjects". {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 460} The article credits him with reviving trade and agriculture in Fars, remodelling Shiraz with a covered bazaar and other buildings that still stand, and concluding commercial agreements with the East India Company at Bushehr; in 1190/1776 he occupied Basra from the weakened Ottomans, but his death forced a withdrawal. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 pp. 460-461} It says that he endorsed the majority Twelver Shi'a, which lies outside the Sunni frame this museum follows by default, without seeking the support of the ulama. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 461}

## Succession and fall
Karim Khan made no provision for his succession and his four sons became pawns in a power struggle among his brothers and cousins. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 pp. 460-461} Sadiq Khan seized Shiraz in 1195/1780; Ali Murad Khan took it the next year and killed Sadiq and his sons; and Zand control of northern Persia passed to the Qajars after Ali Murad's death in Rabi II 1199/February 1785. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 461} Luft Ali Khan, eldest son of Ja'far, secured Shiraz after a palace coup in 1203/1789 but was shut out of it by the kalantar Hajji Ibrahim; Agha Muhammad Khan Qajar entered Shiraz in 1207/1792, and Luft Ali was tortured and executed at Tehran in Rabi II 1209/November 1794. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 461} The article judges that the dynasty's reputation rests on its founder, and that his successors, none of whom aspired to the title of wakil, spent their energies on internecine warfare. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 461}
