## Overview
The Hafsid dynasty, a ruling house founded in 1229 CE and ended in 1574 CE in the Maghrib. The data record that it succeeded [[polity/almohad-caliphate|the Almohad state]]. {cite:wikidata}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes the Hafsids as a North African dynasty based in Tunis that reigned from 1229 to 1574. According to it, the family won independence and backing through the part it had played in establishing Almohadism. Under Hafsid rule Tunis became the intellectual capital of the Maghreb, an important system of madrasas was founded, and Maliki law and mysticism remained strong. Merchant communities from Andalusia and Christian merchant communities also prospered and held influence. The dynasty withstood a Crusade under the leadership of St. Louis, but in the end declined and passed under Ottoman rule, which the Dictionary puts down to the excesses of its rulers, plague, revolts and infighting. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e764}
