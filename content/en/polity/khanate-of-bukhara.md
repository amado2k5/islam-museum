## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam presents the Khanate of Bukhara as a Central Asian state that lasted from about 1500 to 1920, a longer span than the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries given in the museum's own summary; the two are shown side by side here and not reconciled. According to the Dictionary, the Uzbeks drove out the Timurids and set up a decentralised state with several centres, among them Bukhara, Samarqand and Tashkent. Toward the end of the 1500s Bukhara became the working capital of a more tightly governed state and kept that position while Central Asia declined politically and economically in the 1700s. In the late 1800s, trade and diplomacy with Russia gave way to armed conflict shortly after Russia took Tashkent in 1865. The former khanate was transformed in 1920 into the Bukharan People's Soviet Republic, which in 1924 was dissolved and became part of Uzbekistan, by then a Soviet republic. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e379}
