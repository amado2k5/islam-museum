## Overview
The Encyclopaedia of Islam has no article of its own for Venice; this page gathers what its articles on Italy, Alexandria, Iskender Beg and the Ottoman-Habsburg relations say about the republic. The article ITALIYA lists Venice among the towns, with Genoa, Pisa and Naples, to which Arabic geographers and travellers from the 3rd/9th to the 9th/15th century devoted much shorter descriptions than to Rome. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 292}

## Early encounters and trade
The earliest information the Arab chroniclers give about Saracen attacks on mainland Italy concerns the defeat of the Venetian fleet off Taranto, in 225/840 according to some Arab historians and a year later if the Latin sources are followed. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 293} In Mamluk Alexandria, the article on that city says, Venice as the leading commercial power gained a second funduk, the walled quarter in which its merchants lived under their own consul, in the course of the 13th century. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 148} It also says that by 837/1434, as the city declined and the number of its looms fell, imports of cheaper cloth from Flanders and England rose, from which the Venetians made considerable profit. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 148} Two Venetians, Josapha Barbaro and Ambrogio Contarini, visited Isfahan in 879/1474-5 when Uzun Hasan of the Ak-Koyunlu was there, and the former estimated the population at only 50,000. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 111}

## Venice and the Ottomans
The article on Iskender Beg shows Venice as a power on the Albanian coast: his father John Kastriota had acted as a buffer between the Venetians installed in Scutari and the Ottomans, ready to flee to Venetian protection and dependent on Venetian goodwill for the import of salt. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 151} A war between the Ottomans and Venice ran from 826/1423 to 833/1430, and when a new Ottoman-Venetian war broke out in the summer of 1463 Iskender Beg found in Venice a new patron and ally, concluding a pact with it on 20 August 1463. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 pp. 152-153} In the 18th century, the article NEMČE adds, the Habsburgs became involved in three further wars with the Ottomans because of their alliances with Venice and Russia and their ambitions in the Balkans. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 13}
