## Overview
The Alawis are the reigning dynasty of Morocco, sharifs of Hasanid descent who came from Arabia to Tafilalt at the end of the 13th century and for a long time played no part in politics. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 377} The article says they reached power amid the anarchy that marked the decline of the Saadid dynasty. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 377}

## Founding of the dynasty
As anarchy spread, the people of Tafilalt adopted Mawlay al-Sharif as leader; his son Mawlay Muhammad succeeded him in 1045/1635-6 and for twenty years tried to organise a small principality in eastern Morocco without lasting result. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 377} His brother Mawlay al-Rashid seized Fez in 1076/1666 and from then acted as sultan, conquered the north, defeated the Dila'ites, entered Marrakesh in 1079/1669, and died there in 1082/1672. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 377}

## From Ismail to Muhammad b. Abd Allah
His brother Mawlay Ismail reigned from 1672 to 1727; he faced two rival claimants and many revolts, moved his government to Meknes, relied on an army of the Black Bukhari slaves, recovered Mahdiyya and Larache, and saw the British evacuate Tangier. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 378} The article describes thirty years of anarchy after his death, in which seven rulers came and went. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 378} Muhammad b. Abd Allah, elected sultan in 1170/1757, gave a measure of peace, minted a sound currency, signed treaties with the European powers and began Mogador in 1179/1765. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 378}

## The nineteenth century and the Protectorate
Morocco clashed with France at the battle of the Isly on 28 Jumada II 1260/15 July 1844 under Abd al-Rahman b. Hisham, and fought Spain under his son Muhammad. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 379} The Convention of Madrid followed in 1297/1880 under Mawlay al-Hasan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 379} The Protectorate agreement was signed on 11 Rabi II 1330/30 March 1912, so the Alawid dynasty maintained its position under French protection; Abd al-Hafiz abdicated in 1913 and was replaced by Yusuf, succeeded in 1926 by his son Muhammad. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 379} This card goes only as far as the article read.
