## Overview
The Uqaylid state of the Banu Uqayl ibn Ka'b ibn Rabi'a ibn Amir ibn Sa'sa'a, in Mosul and al-Jazira. Ibn Khaldun places its founding in [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-musayyib|Muhammad ibn al-Musayyib]]. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1064-1065}

## The rise
Their affair grew strong when the Hamdanid state failed. Abu al-Dardaa Muhammad ibn al-Musayyib held it, then his brother al-Muqallad, known as Husam al-Dawla, under the guarantee of Baha al-Dawla the Buyid. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1064-1065}

Al-Muqallad had Mosul, Kufa, the palace and the two mosques. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1064-1065}

## The rulers
His sons Qiyrawash, Badran and their brother succeeded one another, until it passed to Muslim ibn Quraysh. Muslim extended its rule to Syria and Aleppo. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1064-1065}
