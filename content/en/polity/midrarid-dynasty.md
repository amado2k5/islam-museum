## Summary
The Banū Midrār were a Miknāsa Berber line that ruled Sijilmāsa at the end of the second Islamic century; Ibn Khaldun makes Midrār their founder, notes that he was a Ṣufrī Ibāḍī, and says their dominion in Sijilmāsa reached its height in his era {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1620}.

## Rule, building and intermarriage
Ibn Khaldun records that ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Rustam, master of Tāhart, managed the affairs of the young Midrār through his daughter Urwā, who became the grandmother of their princes — a marriage alliance joining the two Ibāḍī states — and that al-Muʿtazz b. Muḥammad b. Midrār later took sole power: "it was he who finished its building and erecting, and laid out its factories and palaces," dying in 221 {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1620}. He then lists his successors Abū l-Muntaṣir Muḥammad and Midrār, surnamed Abū l-ʿĀfiya, until the crisis of Abū Yazīd and what followed {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1621}.

## For young visitors
The Banu Midrar were a family that ruled the desert city of Sijilmasa in Morocco; they allied with their neighbours at Tahart and built up a great town.

## Research notes
- Text from the harvested Ibar of Ibn Khaldun (Heliopolis 1957): pp. 1619–1621 (the Miknāsa clans and the state of the Banū Midrār). The house is named "Banū Midrār" in the text; its kinship with the Banū Rustam is documented in the same section. The other source filed on the card (Yaʿqūt's Muʿjam al-Buldān) has not yielded an entry for the dynasty — the locally extracted Yaqut volumes do not reach letter sīn — so the source stays on the card without prose drawn from it.
