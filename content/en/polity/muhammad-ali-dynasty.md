## Overview
The article MUHAMMAD ALI PASHA in the Encyclopaedia of Islam says that Muhammad Ali was born in the late 1760s, the exact date being debated, in the Macedonian port of Kavala; his father was an Ottoman soldier of Albanian origin who commanded the local irregulars and traded in tobacco. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 475} In 1801 he went to Egypt as deputy commander of the Kavala contingent that joined the Ottoman forces fighting the French, and the article tells how, in collusion with the Cairene notables, he was proclaimed wali of Egypt and how the Porte, recognising his strength, endorsed the appointment reluctantly, by 1805. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 475}

## Consolidation of power
Between 1805 and 1811 he made a bid for hegemony by eliminating challenges to his authority, above all from the Mamluk beys; in March 1811 he treacherously massacred their leaders at a ceremony in the Citadel, an act the article says marks the end of Mamluk power in Egypt, though individual Mamluks were absorbed into the Ottoman-Egyptian elite. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 476} It describes his later reign in phases, among them the building of land and sea forces and of an administrative and economic base, with sons dispatched on campaigns in the Hijaz, the Sudan, Crete and the Morea. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 476}

## Hereditary rule
After the Ottoman defeat at Nezib, Sultan Abd ul-Mejid offered Muhammad Ali hereditary rule in Egypt, but he demanded the same for Syria and Adana; in July 1840 Britain, Austria, Prussia and Russia concluded the London convention, which offered him hereditary rule in Egypt and the province of Acre for life in return for withdrawing from Syria, and a sultanic edict of June 1841 confirmed him as hereditary governor of Egypt, limiting his army and applying Ottoman legislation. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 480} In 1847 the old pasha became mentally incapacitated; his son Ibrahim ran the government and was invested in July 1848, dying within months. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 481} Ibrahim was succeeded by Abbas Pasha (1848-54), who the article says consolidated the hereditary rule of Muhammad Ali's family in Egypt in a confrontation with the Porte; Sa'id followed in 1854-63 and then Ismail. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 480} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 482} Muhammad Ali died in Alexandria on 2 August 1849 and was buried in the Citadel mosque that Abbas completed. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 482}
