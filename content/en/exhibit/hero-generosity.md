## Summary
Generosity in this trail was never a passing gift: it was the conversion of a whole fortune into public good. Two merchants promised paradise, the Mother of the Believers, and a woman from Kairouan who turned her inheritance into a university for the world.

## Abd al-Rahman: caravans into Medina
[[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]] migrated from Mecca with no money; when his Ansari brother offered him half his wealth he said "God bless you in your family and your wealth — show me the market," and started from nothing until he became Medina's richest merchant {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}. He gave beyond counting: caravans loaded with dates and wheat entered Medina as charity, an endowment for the Mothers of the Believers and for the poor, until it was said he handed over all his wealth in God's path {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba}.

## Uthman: the Army of Hardship and the Muslims' well
[[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]] equipped the army of [[event/expedition-to-tabuk|Tabuk]] — the Army of Hardship — with what the seerah books record: nine hundred camels, a hundred horses and abundant money, until the Prophet said nothing Uthman did after that day could harm him {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}. He bought the well of Ruma when water was being sold to the people of Medina and endowed it to them, making it Islam's most famous water endowment {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym}.

## Khadija: the first to back the call
[[person/khadija|Khadija bint Khuwaylid]] believed before anyone believed, and spoke her famous words on the night of the first revelation: "No, by God — God will never disgrace you" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. A woman of wealth in Quraysh, she put her fortune in the service of the call and endured with the Prophet and the Muslims in the [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|Boycott of the Hashimites]] until her health failed in the siege {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Fatima al-Fihri: an inheritance that became a university
[[person/fatima-al-fihri|Fatima al-Fihri]] gave her entire inheritance and built in [[place/fes|Fez]] the mosque of [[building/qarawiyyin|al-Qarawiyyin]] around 859 CE — a mosque and school that became, over the centuries, among the oldest continuously working institutions of learning in the Islamic world {cite:wikidata}.

## For Sprouts
Abd al-Rahman started from zero and gave away whole caravans; Uthman equipped an entire army and bought a well for the people; Khadija gave the call all her wealth and endured the siege; and Fatima turned her inheritance into a school where people have learned for a thousand years.

## For Explorers
Four kinds of generosity: trade that gave, a water endowment, wealth that shielded a call, and a scholarly endowment still working. Walk the rooms and read what "waqf" means in each hero's record.

## For Scholars
Ibn Hisham, Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat for the first century; the Isaba and Abu Nu'aym's Ma'rifat al-Sahaba for the financial details; Wikipedia/Wikidata for Fatima al-Fihri where the museum still lacks a printed source — see the researcher notes.

## Notes for researchers
- The figure "nine hundred camels and a hundred horses" is the famous seerah-book account of the Army of Hardship, and its wording varies.
- Fatima al-Fihri's record currently rests on Wikidata; the Qarawiyyin's history is established in Maghrebi history but the record needs a printed source in a future verification round.
