## Summary
Steadfastness is not that you never shake: it is that the world shakes and you do not move. Four men who endured for decades, or in decisive moments, and changed history by their stillness rather than their noise.

## Bilal: "One, One" beneath the boulder
[[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal ibn Rabah]] was an Abyssinian bondsman whom Umayya ibn Khalaf tortured on Mecca's burning sand — his back on the hot gravel, a boulder weighing on his chest — and he only kept saying: One, One {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}. Abu Bakr bought and freed him, and after the Hijra he became Islam's first muezzin, his call rising over all the scars Mecca had left on him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## Salman: the longest road to truth
[[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman al-Farisi]] left a village in Persia following the faith from one Christian teacher to the next, until the last of them told him of a prophet appearing in the land of palms; he ended enslaved in Yathrib — and there found the one he had been seeking {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. At [[event/battle-of-the-trench|the Trench]] he showed the Muslims how to dig the ditch, and so became, by knowledge brought from afar, a cause of Medina's deliverance {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## Abu Bakr: the day the ummah shook
When the Prophet died and the Arabs apostatised and some withheld the zakat, [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]] stood on his word: "By God, I will fight whoever separates prayer from zakat" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}. Before that he had been the companion of [[event/hijra|the Cave]]; then he became the first caliph to settle the ummah's affairs in its hardest hours {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2}.

## Abd al-Muttalib: the unafraid word
[[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim]], chief of Quraysh, stood before Abraha on the day of the Elephant asking only for the return of his camels; asked why he did not plead for the House, he said: the House has a Lord who will protect it {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}. Earlier he had re-dug Zamzam and carried the service of God's House, and he kept his house's trust until the light came from his line {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For Sprouts
Bilal bore the hardest pain saying "One, One"; Salman travelled from land to land seeking the truth until he found it; Abu Bakr stood with the Prophet in the Cave and then held the people steady after him; and Abd al-Muttalib faced an army and was not afraid.

## For Explorers
Steadfastness of a voice, of a journey, of a decision, and of a word. Four moments from four different times — read the hero's record and the event's record together.

## For Scholars
Ibn Hisham in the Saqqa edition and Ibn Ishaq in Guillaume for the Meccan century; al-Raheeq al-Makhtum for the details of the torture, the adhan and the Trench; the Encyclopaedia of Islam for Abu Bakr. See the persons' records for the passages cited verbatim.

## Notes for researchers
- "By God, I will fight" appears in the seerah books and the Sahih collections; its context differs in detail between reports.
- Salman's search-story is narrated by him in Ibn Ishaq; its details are taken from that seerah context, not external verification.
