## Summary
Four members of Islam's first household paid the dearest price a person can pay — home, comfort and blood — for people they would never meet. These are their stories as the books of seerah and biography record them.

## Sumayya: the first soul on the path
[[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya bint Khayyat]] was a bondswoman of Abu Hudhayfa ibn al-Mughira; she, her son Ammar and her husband Yasir believed when faith in Mecca cost a daily price. The family of al-Mughira took Yasir's household out onto Mecca's burning sand at al-Abwah to torture them into recanting, and they only grew firmer {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume p.233}. Sumayya was beaten until she was killed — the first martyr of Islam — and her patience left no doubt that her faith was dearer to her than her body {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba}.

## Mus'ab: from Meccan luxury to the banner of Uhud
[[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]] was among the most pampered and best-dressed young men of Quraysh; when he embraced Islam his mother starved him and cast him out of her house {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.89}. The Prophet sent him to Yathrib with the pledgers as a teacher and reciter, and Islam entered the houses of the Ansar until they said no house remained without Muslims in it {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.144}. At [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] he carried the army's great banner and at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] the banner of the Emigrants; when the people scattered he stood with the flag until he was killed, and was shrouded in a cloak that could not cover both his head and his feet {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.282}.

## Nusayba: the woman who became a shield
[[person/nusayba-bint-kab|Nusayba bint Ka'b]], Umm Umara, went out on the day of Uhud to give the fighters water and tend the wounded; when she saw the battle closing on the Prophet she took up a sword and stood in front of the attackers until a deep wound struck her neck, and she held firm {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. Years later she returned at [[event/battle-of-yamama|al-Yamama]] to face Musaylima, suffered many wounds and lost her hand, and still did not retreat from answering the truth {cite:nisa-hawl-alrasul}.

## Zayd: the beloved who led to the spears
[[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] was the Prophet's freedman and beloved; when his father came to ransom him and he was given the choice between returning and staying with the Prophet, he chose the Prophet {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. When the Prophet equipped the army of [[event/battle-of-mutah|Mu'ta]] he made Zayd the first of its commanders, and he fought with the Muslims' banner before him until the enemy's spears brought him down, handing it on to Ja'far and then to Abdullah ibn Rawaha {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}.

## For Sprouts
Four heroes gave the most precious thing they had: Sumayya bore the pain and became the first martyr; Mus'ab left his rich home to teach people and then carried the flag; Nusayba came to give water and became a shield for the Prophet; and Zayd led the army out of his great love for the Prophet.

## For Explorers
Sacrifice here is not only death: Sumayya gave her life, Mus'ab gave his comfort and his years, Nusayba gave her strength and her hand, and Zayd gave a choice one day and his life another. Walk the four rooms and read each hero's record with its source.

## For Scholars
Every story here is backed by a dated source: Ibn Ishaq in Guillaume's translation for the Meccan seerah, Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat, Ibn Hajar's Isaba, al-Mubarakpuri's al-Raheeq al-Makhtum, and "Women Around the Messenger" for Nusayba. See the researcher notes in each person's record for what is confirmed and what still needs review.

## Notes for researchers
- The story of Sumayya and the torture of Yasir's family is established in Ibn Ishaq's seerah ⁦(Guillaume p.233)⁩; the details of her killing are narrated in varying words across the biographical books.
- Nusayba's presence at al-Yamama and the loss of her hand are in Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat and "Women Around the Messenger"; the number of her wounds at Uhud varies between reports.
