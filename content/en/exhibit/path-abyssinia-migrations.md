## The aim of this trail
To know the reason for the two migrations to Abyssinia and the names of the most prominent of those who took part in them, and to see how the accounts present their return.

## What you will know at the end
- Distinguish the first migration from the second, and state what marks each.
- Name those who sheltered them and those who spoke for them before the king.
- See how the different accounts of the emigrants' return are attributed to their narrators.

## How to read this trail
This lesson is smaller than "The Meccan Period" and narrower than it. It focuses on a single event and its people, and shows how the museum presents the different accounts of the same event.
Approximate duration: 25 minutes; 11 steps. This is an experimental trail within "Tour and Learn".

[[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far ibn Abi Talib]]: the one who spoke before the king on behalf of the emigrants. {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}

### 6. The emigrants of Abyssinia
[[concept/muhajirun-of-abyssinia|The emigrants of Abyssinia]]: how Ibn Ishaq classifies them according to their return.

### 7. A different case
[[event/ubaydullah-ibn-jahsh-leaves-islam-in-abyssinia|The conversion of Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh to Christianity in the land of Abyssinia]]: not everyone who emigrated remained upon Islam, as Ibn Ishaq relates. The book does not date the event. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/6}

### 8. Umm Habiba

### 9. Asma bint Umays
[[person/asma-bint-umays|Asma bint Umays]]: one of the women who emigrated with their husbands. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-abyari p.204-205}

### 10. The return

### 11. Another account of Ja'far's arrival
[[event/jafar-arrival-at-khaybar|Ja'far ibn Abi Talib's arrival to the Prophet at Khaybar]]: a report that Ibn Hisham transmits from a source other than Ibn Ishaq. Compare the two pages. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3}
