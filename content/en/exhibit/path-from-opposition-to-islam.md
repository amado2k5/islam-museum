## The aim of this trail
To see how the sources describe those who changed their position: when, and for what reason, without passing judgement on them. And to understand what is known as "winning hearts" (ta'lif al-qulub).

## What you will know at the end
- Order in time the conversions of the most prominent of these people.
- Distinguish those who became Muslim before the conquest from those who became Muslim at it.
- Know the meaning of "those whose hearts are to be reconciled" and an example of it.

## How to read this trail
Each step here describes what a person did in their own time, and attributes judgements to those who voiced them. The museum passes judgement on no one. These are the life stories of figures who stood on the other side at one stage, then crossed over; this is what makes the story complete.
Approximate duration: 40 minutes; 12 steps. This is an experimental trail within "Tour and Learn".

## The steps

### 1. Umar
[[event/islam-of-umar|The conversion of Umar ibn al-Khattab]]: he went out intending to kill the Prophet, and ended as a Muslim. {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}

### 2. Umayr ibn Wahb
[[event/conversion-of-umayr-ibn-wahb|The conversion of Umayr ibn Wahb]]: after Badr, what he had plotted and how it ended. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.233}

### 3. Khalid ibn al-Walid
[[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]]: he who led the cavalry of Mecca, then became Muslim before the conquest. {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}

### 4. The conversion of Abu Sufyan
[[event/abu-sufyan-conversion|The conversion of Abu Sufyan ibn Harb at Marr al-Zahran]]: the night of the conquest, at Marr al-Zahran. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/44-47}

### 5. Hind bint Utba
[[person/hind-bint-utba|Hind bint Utba]]: her history before the conquest and after it. {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}

### 6. Hakim ibn Hizam
[[person/hakim-ibn-hizam|Hakim ibn Hizam]]: the one of whom the Prophet said a word of safe-conduct. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.337}

### 7. Suhayl ibn Amr
[[person/suhayl-ibn-amr|Suhayl ibn Amr]]: the negotiator of al-Hudaybiyya, who became Muslim on the day of the conquest. {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}

### 8. Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl
[[person/ikrima-ibn-abi-jahl|Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl]]: one granted safe-conduct on the day of the conquest. {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}

### 9. Safwan ibn Umayya
[[person/safwan-ibn-umayya|Safwan ibn Umayya]]: he fled, then safe-conduct reached him. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.695}

### 10. Wahshi ibn Harb
[[person/wahshi-ibn-harb|Wahshi ibn Harb]]: the killer of Hamza, and what the narrative records after his conversion. {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}

### 11. Abdullah ibn al-Zibara
[[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zibara|Abdullah ibn al-Zibara]]: the poet of Quraysh, his return and his apology. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.875}

### 12. Those whose hearts are to be reconciled
[[concept/al-muallafa-qulubuhum|Those whose hearts are to be reconciled]]: what was prescribed to secure the Islam of these people and others like them.
