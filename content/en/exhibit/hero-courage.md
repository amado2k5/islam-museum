## Summary
Courage in these four was never recklessness: it was measured firmness when heart and foot advance together. Four men rode through four centuries — two Companions, a conqueror, and an old teacher who resisted an empire in his desert.

## Ali: the sleeper in the bed
On the night of the [[event/hijra|Hijra]] [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] slept in the Prophet's bed while the polytheists surrounded the house to kill him at dawn — the first open sacrifice, priced at a life and decided in a moment {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}. At [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] he was among those who answered the call to single combat, and at [[event/battle-of-khaybar|Khaybar]] he tore off the fortress gate when the army could not {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. Then he became the fourth caliph, and his reign was a further lesson that courage is hardest when it asks for patience rather than the sword.

## Khalid: the sword of the battlefields
[[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] embraced Islam shortly before the conquest of Mecca, and the Prophet named him what Muslims call him still: the Drawn Sword of God {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba}. At [[event/battle-of-mutah|Mu'ta]] he extricated the army by a withdrawal manoeuvre that pleased the Prophet, then led the armies of the Ridda, outmanoeuvred Persia at al-Qadisiyya, and at [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmuk]] devised a plan still studied today: he drew the Byzantines onto ground where his cavalry stood behind them and the ravine before them {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}. Dismissed late in life, he spoke his famous words about himself: no span of his body remained without a sword-blow, an arrow-wound or a spear-thrust {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba}.

## Saladin: the gracious knight
[[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din]] gathered Egypt and Syria after years of division, met the Crusaders at [[event/battle-of-hattin-583|Hattin]] in 583 AH in the defeat that opened the road to [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]] {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}. He entered it without killing a resident or demolishing a house, and the chroniclers of both sides recorded his succour, his word-keeping and his forbearance even toward his enemies {cite:britannica-saladin}.

## Omar Mukhtar: the sheikh of the desert
[[person/omar-mukhtar|Omar Mukhtar]] taught Qur'an in his Cyrenaican village for a lifetime; when Italy occupied Libya the seventy-year-old teacher became the resistance leader of the Green Mountain for some twenty years, on ageing horses with few rifles {cite:omar-mukhtar-sallabi}. Captured in 1931 CE, he chose the stand over the years left to him when they offered him surrender, and was hanged before the occupation's soldiers and his own people at Suluq {cite:tdv-bagy}.

## For Sprouts
Ali slept in the Prophet's place on a dangerous night; Khalid was never defeated in a hundred battles; Saladin took back Jerusalem and was merciful to its people; and Omar Mukhtar resisted the occupier as an old grandfather and never gave up.

## For Explorers
Each hero here was tested in a moment: the bed in Mecca, the manoeuvre at Mu'ta, the plateau of Hattin, the gallows at Suluq. Read the battle's record beside the hero's — in this museum the battle is its own sourced page.

## For Scholars
The Meccan seerah comes from Ibn Hisham and Ibn Ishaq; the first century from the books of Companion virtues and the Isaba; Ibn al-Athir's al-Kamil and Britannica for the Ayyubids; al-Sallabi's book and the Turkish TDV encyclopaedia for Mukhtar. Khalid's words about his body are taken from Ibn Hajar's biography in the context of his dismissal.

## Notes for researchers
- The title "the Drawn Sword of God" appears in Ibn Hajar's biographies and the Isaba; its attribution is agreed in the books of virtues.
- The details of Hattin and the entry into Jerusalem are in Ibn al-Athir's al-Kamil; Latin sources also record his clemency at Jerusalem in contrast to the Crusaders' earlier sack.
- Khalid's "about a hundred battles" is a seerah-book figure, not a documented count; his saying about his body is famous in the context of his dismissal.
