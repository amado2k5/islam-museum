## Overview
Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Waqid, a client of the Banu Hashim, author of the books of biography and campaigns. He was weakened as a transmitter of hadith. He died in 209 AH at the age of seventy-seven. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.426-427}

Ibn Khaldun counts him with al-Mas'udi among the great historians, while noting the errors in their books. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p.15} Ibn al-Nadim says that he shared with Abu Mikhnaf and al-Madaini in writing the "Conquests of Syria". {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.293}

Among his reports in al-Mas'udi is the story of the thousand dirhams that a Hashimite friend of his gave him in kindness; the news reached al-Ma'mun, who gave them seven thousand dinars. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.426-427}

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Waqid al-Aslami, their client, al-Waqidi of Medina, the judge, calling him "the author of the works, and one of the vessels of knowledge despite his weakness". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 662} The two sources differ on his father's name: "Umar" in al-Dhahabi, "Amr" in al-Mas'udi. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 662} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.426-427} Al-Dhahabi states that he was born in 130, met Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Ajlan, Ma'mar and Thawr ibn Yazid, and that his grandfather Waqid was a client of Abd Allah ibn Burayda ibn al-Husayb. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 664}

As evidence of his standing al-Dhahabi notes that [[person/ibn-majah|Ibn Maja]] narrated from Ibn Abi Shayba from "a shaykh of ours" a hadith on dress on Friday, that this shaykh is al-Waqidi, judge of Baghdad, and remarks: "It is enough for you that Ibn Maja does not dare to name him." {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 662} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 663} A footnote gives the view, from the margin of one manuscript, that the vagueness came from Ibn Abi Shayba, not Ibn Maja. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 663}

## The criticism of him
[[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] said: a liar who turns hadiths around, putting the hadith of al-Zuhri's nephew onto Ma'mar and the like. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 663} [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]] said: not trustworthy, and once: his hadith is not to be written. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 663} [[person/al-bukhari|Al-Bukhari]] and Abu Hatim said: abandoned; Abu Hatim also, and al-Nasa'i, said: he forges hadith; al-Daraqutni said: there is weakness in him; Ibn Adi said: his hadiths are not preserved and the fault is his. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 663} Abu Ghalib reported [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ibn al-Madini]] saying that al-Waqidi forged hadith, and al-Mughira ibn Muhammad reported him saying that al-Haytham ibn Adi was more trustworthy to him than al-Waqidi, whom he accepted neither in hadith, nor in genealogy, nor in anything. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 663} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 664} Al-Bukhari said: they were silent about him; Ibn Rahawayh said: to me he is one of those who forge hadith. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 665} He reports Ishaq ibn al-Tabba' saying he saw al-Waqidi on the Mecca road praying badly. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 664} Al-Dhahabi cites examples of the hadiths held against him, among them one forbidding the cursing of As'ad al-Himyari. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 664}

## The praise of his memory and learning
Al-Dhahabi reports Mujahid ibn Musa saying he had written from no one with a better memory than al-Waqidi, and comments: "He spoke truly; his memory was the utmost in reports, biography, campaigns, events, the 'days' of the people and law." {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 663} He quotes Sulayman al-Shadhakuni: either al-Waqidi is the most truthful of men or the greatest liar, for he wrote a book from him, later asked him about it, and he did not change a letter. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 664} Abu Dawud heard that Ibn al-Madini said al-Waqidi narrated thirty thousand rare hadiths. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 664}

Muhammad ibn Sallam al-Jumahi said: he was the scholar of his age; [[person/ibrahim-al-harbi|Ibrahim al-Harbi]] said: al-Waqidi is the most trustworthy of men over Islam, the most knowledgeable about Islam's affairs, but of the Jahiliyya he knew nothing. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 665} Mus'ab al-Zubayri said: by God, we never saw his like, and: trustworthy and reliable; and [[person/abd-al-aziz-al-darawardi|al-Darawardi]] is reported saying: al-Waqidi is the commander of the faithful in hadith. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 665} [[person/yazid-ibn-harun|Yazid ibn Harun]] and [[person/abu-ubayd-al-qasim-ibn-sallam|Abu Ubayd]] declared him trustworthy, and Muhammad ibn Ishaq al-Saghani said: had he not been trustworthy to me, I would not have narrated from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 665} Ibn Sa'd reports him saying: everyone's books exceed his memory, but my memory exceeds my books. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 665} Ya'qub ibn Shayba said that when he moved from the western side of Baghdad his books were carried on a hundred and twenty loads; it is also said he had six hundred chests of books. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 665}

## Judgeship in Baghdad and death
Al-Dhahabi quotes [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib]]'s *History of Baghdad*: al-Waqidi came to Baghdad and was made judge of its eastern side; his fame filled the east and west of the earth, and caravans carried his books on the campaigns, biography, the generations, the reports of the Prophet and the events of his time and after his death, law and people's differences in hadith; and he was generous, famed for liberality. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 665} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 666} Al-Dhahabi refers to his own "great history" for more of his reports and generosity. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 666} He died in office as judge in Dhu al-Hijja of the year 207 according to al-Dhahabi, and in 209 according to al-Mas'udi. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 666} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.426-427} Al-Dhahabi ends the entry: "and consensus settled on al-Waqidi's weakness". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 666}
