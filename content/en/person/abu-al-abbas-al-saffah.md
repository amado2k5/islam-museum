## Overview
He was given the allegiance to the caliphate in Rabi al-Akhir 132 AH, and died in 136 AH. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.420} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.470}

## Upbringing and qualities
According to al-Suyuti he was born in 108 AH, and it is said 104 AH, at al-Humayma in the district of al-Balqa', and his mother was Ra'ita al-Harithiyya. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 417-419}

Al-Mas'udi describes him as the most generous of people, who did not count a sum and then delay it, and who loved to converse with men. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 417-419} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 250-251}

## The judgement on him
Al-Mas'udi quotes al-Abdi that he was quick to shed blood and his governors followed him in that, yet he was also gracious and generous. This is a judgement attributed to al-Abdi, not the museum's. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 668}

## The allegiance and death
Abu Humayd al-Tusi gave him the allegiance on Friday, the twelfth night of Rabi al-Akhir 132 AH. His caliphate lasted four years, nine months and twenty days, and he died at al-Anbar in his city. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 238-242}

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.) entry describes Abu l-Abbas al-Saffah, the first 'Abbasid caliph, whose surname means either "the bloodthirsty" or "the generous". His uncle 'Abd Allah b. 'Ali defeated the Umayyads on the Upper Zab in Jumada II 132/January 750 and pursued Marwan II. The article records the reprisals that followed: about eighty Umayyad chiefs were killed at the Nahr Abi Futrus, Umayyad tombs were violated, and the 'Alid rising in Bukhara was crushed in 133/750-1. Al-Saffah then had his own commanders Abu Salama and Sulayman b. Kathir killed, and he died at al-Anbar in Dhu l-Hijja 136/June 754. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 122}

## His officials in Khalifa ibn Khayyat
[[person/khalifa-ibn-khayyat|Khalifa ibn Khayyat]] closes his account of this reign by naming its governors and office-holders. They are given here in his order; lines the editor restored from later authors are marked as such. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 412–415}

- **Basra:** Sufyan ibn Mu'awiya (dismissed); then [[person/umar-ibn-hafs-hazarmard|Umar ibn Hafs Hazarmard]] (dismissed 133 AH); then Sulayman ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas (until Abu al-Abbas died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 412}
- **Kufa:** [[person/dawud-ibn-ali|Dawud ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas]] (his uncle; then sent to lead the Hajj); then Isa ibn Musa ibn Muhammad ibn Ali (until Abu al-Abbas died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 412}
- **Mecca:** [[person/dawud-ibn-ali|Dawud ibn Ali]] (with Medina; died); then Musa ibn Dawud (his father's deputy, dismissed by Abu al-Abbas); then Ziyad ibn Ubayd Allah al-Harithi (his maternal uncle, with Medina and Ta'if); then Ali ibn al-Rabi' (appointed by his uncle Ziyad) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 412}
- **Medina:** Husayn ibn Ja'far ibn Tammam ibn al-Abbas (sent by Abd Allah ibn Ali from Damascus); then [[person/dawud-ibn-ali|Dawud ibn Ali]] (four months, then died); then Musa ibn Dawud (dismissed by Abu al-Abbas); then Ziyad ibn Ubayd Allah (arrived Jumada I 133 AH) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 413}
- **Yemen:** [[person/dawud-ibn-ali|Dawud ibn Ali]]; then Umar ibn Abd al-Hamid al-Khattabi; then Muhammad ibn Yazid ibn Ubayd Allah (his cousin; died; in (( )) in the edition: restored by the editor from later authors quoting Khalifa); then Ziyad ibn Ubayd Allah; then Ali ibn al-Rabi'; then Abd Allah ibn al-Rabi' (until Abu al-Abbas died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 413}
- **Khurasan:** [[person/abu-muslim-al-khurasani|Abu Muslim]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 413}
- **Sijistan:** [[person/malik-ibn-al-haytham|Malik ibn al-Haytham]] (sent by Abu Muslim); then Umar ibn al-Abbas ibn Umayr ibn Utarid; then Isma'il ibn Amr; then Muhammad ibn al-Ash'ath (some say, before Umar ibn al-Abbas) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 413}
- **Sind:** Mughallis (a man of Tamim, captured by Mansur ibn Jumhur); then Musa ibn Ka'b al-Mara'i (killed Mansur at Qandabil) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 413}
- **Bahrayn:** al-Masih ibn al-Hawari ibn Ziyad al-Ataki; then Abd Allah ibn Sulayman ibn al-Mundhir ibn al-Jarud; then [[person/umar-ibn-hafs-hazarmard|Umar ibn Hafs Hazarmard]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 413}
- **Yamama:** [[person/dawud-ibn-ali|Dawud ibn Ali]] (died); then al-Sari ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn al-Abbas {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 414}
- **Jazira:** [[person/abdallah-ibn-ali-ibn-abdallah|Abd Allah ibn Ali]] (after Marwan's defeat at the Zab); then Musa ibn Ka'b al-Mara'i (Abd Allah ibn Ali's deputy); then [[person/al-mansur|Abu Ja'far]] (his brother, with Armenia and Azerbaijan); then Muqatil ibn Hakim al-Akki (Abu Ja'far's deputy) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 414}
- **Summer campaign:** al-Nadr ibn Yarim; then [[person/salih-ibn-ali|Salih ibn Ali]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 414}
- **Judgeship of Basra:** [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-artat|al-Hajjaj ibn Artat]] (sent by Abu Ja'far; judged some months); then Abbad ibn Mansur (restored by Sulayman, then dismissed); then [[person/sawwar-ibn-abdallah-al-qadi|Sawwar ibn Abd Allah]] (jointly with Umar ibn Amir; resigned); then Umar ibn Amir al-Sulami (died); then Talha ibn Iyas al-Adawi (then dismissed); then Abbad ibn Mansur (until Abu al-Abbas died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 414}
- **Judgeship of Kufa:** [[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-rahman-ibn-abi-layla|Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 415}
- **Judgeship of Medina:** Ibn Abi Sabra (appointed by Ziyad ibn Ubayd Allah in 136 AH) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 415}
- **Police:** Abd al-Jabbar ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Azdi {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 415}
- **Seal and guard:** Asad ibn Abd Allah ibn Malik al-Khuza'i {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 415}
- **Chamberlain:** Abu Umara, his mawla {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 415}
