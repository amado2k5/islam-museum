## Summary
Nigâr Hanım (1856 – 1 April 1918) was an Ottoman poet, born at Istanbul and died there, the daughter of Macar Osman Pasha, a nobleman of Hungarian origin. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en} {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-tr} All three articles give the year 1856 for her birth, while Wikidata carries a second value, the year 1862 — the harvest queue's year — which no article supports; this entry follows the articles and states both readings. {cite:wikidata-nigar-hanim} Her collection Efsus was the first book of Western-style poetry published by a woman, and she is also known as Nigar Bint-i Osman. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en}

## Life and career
Nigâr Hanım was educated at the French school in Kadıköy and by private tutors, mastered eight languages and played the piano from childhood. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en} She was married at fourteen and divorced after unhappy years, and had a son, Feridun Bey. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en} She began in the classical divan style and moved towards a Western-influenced modernist stance under the influence of Recaizade Mahmut Ekrem, and her Istanbul salon drew literary society. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en} She received the Order of Charity (Şefkat Nişanı) for her humanitarian work and died at Istanbul on 1 April 1918, in isolation and pain. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en} {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-tr}

## Works
Her collections include Efsus (in two parts), Nîrân, Aks-i Sada, Safahat-ı Kalb and Elhan-ı Vatan; she also wrote the play Tesir-i Aşk and the memoir Hayatımın Hikâyesi, published posthumously in 1959. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section is read as a presentation of positions reported from the sources, not as a verdict of the museum.

The sources present her as a bridge between divan poetry and Western-style modernism, and as the first woman of her era to publish a book of poetry in the Western style. {cite:wikipedia-nigar-hanim-en} No source names a religious school for the woman herself: none of the three articles carries a religion field, her Hungarian-born father is described only as an Ottoman nobleman of Hungarian origin, and Wikidata gives only the generic value Islam, so her school is left unnamed in this entry. {cite:wikidata-nigar-hanim}
