## Summary
Mohsin Hamid (born 1971) is a Pakistani-British novelist born in Lahore. Educated at Princeton and Harvard Law School, he worked in management consulting before his first novel, Moth Smoke (2000); The Reluctant Fundamentalist (2007) made him internationally known, followed by How to Get Filthy Rich in Rising Asia (2013), Exit West (2017) and The Last White Man (2022). {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} He moved back to Lahore in 2009 and divides his time between Lahore, New York and London, holding dual Pakistani and British citizenship. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en}

## Life
Hamid was born in Lahore on 23 July 1971. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} {cite:wikidata-mohsin-hamid} He spent part of his childhood, from the age of three to nine, in the United States, where his father was completing a doctorate at Stanford, then returned to Lahore and attended the Lahore American School. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} At eighteen he went back to the United States, studying at Princeton and then at Harvard Law School, and worked as a management consultant in New York before turning to writing full-time; he later lived in London. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} In 2009 he moved to Lahore with his wife Zahra and their daughter, and he now divides his time between Lahore, New York and London. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} In a 2007 Religion News Service interview he described himself as a secular, progressive Muslim who had always thought of himself as a Muslim, and called his Lahore youth fairly quietly religious; the museum records no school or order for him. {cite:religion-news-service-mohsin-hamid}

## Work
Moth Smoke (2000), set in post-nuclear-test Lahore, tells of a marijuana-smoking ex-banker who falls for his best friend's wife and slides into heroin addiction; it became a cult hit in Pakistan and India and was a finalist for the PEN/Hemingway Award. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} The Reluctant Fundamentalist (2007) is the monologue of Changez, a Princeton-educated Pakistani whose American life curdles after the September 2001 attacks, addressed to an unnamed American in a Lahore bazaar. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} How to Get Filthy Rich in Rising Asia (2013) borrows the shape of a self-help book to follow a boy from poverty to tycoonhood; Exit West (2017) follows two lovers fleeing a city at war through doors that open onto other countries; The Last White Man (2022) opens with its protagonist waking to find his skin turned brown. {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en} His essays are collected in Discontent and Its Civilisations (2014). {cite:wikipedia-mohsin-hamid-en}
