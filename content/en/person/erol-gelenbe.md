## Summary
Sami Erol Gelenbe is a Turkish-French computer scientist, electronic engineer and applied mathematician who pioneered the mathematical modelling of computer systems and networks. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology; Wikipedia records his invention of the random neural network, his professorial chairs at Imperial College London and Duke University, and his current work in Poland. {cite:muslim500-erol-gelenbe} {cite:wikipedia-erol-gelenbe}

## Formation
Gelenbe was born in Istanbul in 1945, studied at Istanbul Technical University and took his doctorate at the Polytechnic Institute of New York University before settling into a career across France and the English-speaking world — posts at the Universities of Michigan, Paris, Liège, Nice and Duke, then a long chair at Imperial College London, where he held the Dennis Gabor Professorship. {cite:wikipedia-erol-gelenbe}

## The mathematics of networks
His research built the queueing-theoretic tools by which engineers predict how computers and communication networks perform under load: product-form networks, and the "G-networks" that extended queueing theory to systems with negative customers — models later applied to neural-like computation and even to simulating aspects of biology. In 1989 he introduced the random neural network, a recurrent network whose neurons exchange probabilistic spikes; it became a compact tool for optimisation, image processing and learning, and one of the early ancestors of energy-based neural models. {cite:wikipedia-erol-gelenbe} {cite:muslim500-erol-gelenbe}

## Breadth
Beyond theory, Gelenbe has worked on packet networks, ad-hoc and sensor networks, cognitive packet networks that route by learned goals, cyber-physical security, and recently on energy efficiency in computing. He is a member of the Polish Academy of Sciences, where he has worked at the Institute of Theoretical and Applied Informatics, and of European academies; honours include the ACM SIGMETRICS achievement award and honorary doctorates. {cite:wikipedia-erol-gelenbe}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Gelenbe stands for the generation that gave computer networks their mathematics: an Istanbul-born scientist of France, Britain, America and Poland whose models still underlie how engineers reason about the internet's congestion and whose random neural network anticipates, in miniature, today's machine learning. {cite:muslim500-erol-gelenbe} {cite:wikipedia-erol-gelenbe}

## Recognition across the academies
Gelenbe's career is measured in academies: fellowships of national science academies on three continents, the French Légion d'honneur, and — the record's landmark — election to the UK Royal Society in 2025, making him the first person of Turkish origin elected to Britain's oldest scientific academy. The sequence matters for the museum's record: the Turkish-British computer scientist who built his career in France and the UK, who became a national figure of science in both, and whose election made him a symbol for a diaspora watching Turkish names cross the oldest gates of Western science. {cite:wikipedia-erol-gelenbe} {cite:muslim500-erol-gelenbe}
