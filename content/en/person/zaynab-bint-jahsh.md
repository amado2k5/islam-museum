## Summary
[[person/zaynab-bint-jahsh|Zaynab bint Jahsh]] al-Asadiyya, "the Mother of the Believers, wife of the Prophet"; her mother [[person/umayma-bint-abd-al-muttalib|Umayma]], the Prophet's aunt, and her brother Abdullah {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1881}. Abu Umar relates that her name was Barra, and the Prophet named her Zaynab when she entered on him {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}.

## Her marriage
The Prophet married her in year 3 AH — or it is said year 5 — and the verse of the hijab descended on her account; before him she had been with his client [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1881}.

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: the account in al-Isaba (Ibn Hajar)**: [[passage/quran-33-37|al-Ahzab 37]] descended concerning her. Zayd used to be called ibn Muhammad; when [[passage/quran-33-5|al-Ahzab 5]] descended and the Prophet married his wife after him, what the Jahiliyya had held — that an adopted son becomes a son and the two inherit from each other — was nullified {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1881}.
:::

Ibn Hajar adds, in the report of her wedding, from Ibn Sa'd by way of al-Waqidi in a mursal chain: the Prophet was speaking at A'isha's when a covering came over him; then he was relieved, smiling, saying "who will go to Zaynab and give her the tidings?" — and he recited the verse. A'isha says that when she heard it, everything near and far stirred her at what had reached her of Zaynab's beauty, and that Zaynab "boasts over us with this" — that God married her from heaven {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1881}. Ibn Hajar says the account has a weak chain from Ibn Abbas that Zaynab prostrated when she was told {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}.

## Her description
A'isha describes Zaynab in the affair of the Ifk with the finest description — that God shielded her by piety — and says: she it was who rivalled me among the Prophet's wives, and she would boast over the Prophet's wives that she was his cousin and that God had married her to him while their own guardians married them {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1881}. She said to the Messenger of God: "I am not like any of your wives — there is no woman among your wives but her father or brother or family married her to you except me; God married me to you from heaven" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}. Umm Salama relates that she was "devout, fasting, standing in night prayer, a craftswoman who gave all of it in charity to the poor" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}.

## Length of the arm
In the two Sahihs, from A'isha: the Prophet said: "the quickest of you to catch up with me is the longest of you in arm" — she said: they would measure which of them had the longest arm, and the longest-armed of us was Zaynab, for she worked with her hands and gave charity {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}. A'isha says that when Zaynab died they knew the Prophet had meant length of arm in charity {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}.

## Her death
Al-Waqidi said she died in 20 AH {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}. Al-Tabarani relates from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abza that he prayed over Zaynab with Umar — she was the first of the Prophet's wives to die after him {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}. In Ibn Sa'd's account from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, she said when death came to her: "I have prepared my shroud, and Umar will send me a shroud — give one of the two in charity" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}. A'isha says: "she is gone, virtuous and devoted, the refuge of orphans and widows" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1882}.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the eleventh episode of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]] to Zaynab bint Jahsh. He says her mother was Umayma bint Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's aunt, and that she surpassed the women of Quraysh in beauty, rank and wealth, became Muslim and emigrated to Medina. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:01:30} When nobles of Quraysh sought her, she sent her sister Hamna to consult the Prophet, who proposed Zayd ibn Haritha, his freedman and adopted son. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:03:00} He tells Zayd's story at length: a boy of a noble Arab house, kidnapped and sold, given to the Prophet by Khadija, who chose to stay with the Prophet when his own father came to buy him back. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:06:00}

Zaynab and her family were angry at the match, and he says the Prophet meant to reorder values, so that religion counted for more than lineage, beginning with his own kin. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:08:30} After a verse came down on obeying God and His Messenger's decision, she accepted, but the marriage was unhappy, and Zayd asked to divorce her while the Prophet told him to be patient. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:11:00} He draws from it that affection is not in human hands, so divorce can be a mercy. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:17:00} Her marriage to the Prophet came by revelation to end the custom of adoption, and she would tell the other wives that their families had married them while God had married her from above the seven heavens. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:16:30}

He says the marriage was happy, and that Aisha said she had never seen a woman better in religion, and that Zaynab worked with her hands and gave what she earned in charity. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:19:30} The verses on guests who linger and on speaking to the Prophet's wives from behind a screen came down, he says, in her house on her wedding day. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:23:30} He gives her age at the marriage as thirty-five. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:24:00} When the wives asked who would follow the Prophet first, he said the one with the longest hand; she was the first of them to die after him, in 20 AH, and they understood that he had meant her generosity. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:25:00} He says she was called the mother of the poor and died at fifty. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep11 00:25:00}
