## Overview
Al-Husayn ibn Ahmad ibn Khalawayh, Abu Abd Allah, was a linguist among the great grammarians, originally of Hamadhan. He visited Yemen and stayed a while at Dhamar, then moved to Syria and settled at Aleppo, where his fame grew and the Banu Hamdan gave him high rank; he held sessions and disputations with al-Mutanabbi at Sayf al-Dawla's court, which charged him with the education of the ruler's sons. He died at Aleppo in 370 AH (980 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.230}

His books include a commentary on Ibn Durayd's Maqsura; an epitome on the irregular readings of the Qur'an; I'rab Thirty Suras; Laysa fi Kalam al-'Arab; al-Shajr; al-Al; al-Ishtiqaq; al-Jumal on grammar; al-Maqsur wa-l-Mamdud; and al-Badi'. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.230}
