## Overview
Abu Jafar al-Saymari, a vizier of [[person/muizz-al-dawla|Muizz al-Dawla]] ibn Buyah. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 365-366}

## His campaign against the Bataih
Muizz al-Dawla sent him to fight Imran ibn Shahin, lord of the Bataih marshes. He fought him again and again and took his family and dependants captive. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 365-366}

## The move to Shiraz
When Imad al-Dawla died in 338 AH, Muizz al-Dawla wrote to him to hasten to Shiraz to set its affairs in order, so he left Imran and went there. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 365-366}

## In Miskawayh's Tajarib al-umam
[[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] calls him Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Saymari and presents him as Mu'izz al-Dawla's vizier, in charge of the conduct of war and of state business. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.156}

### At the deposition of al-Mustakfi
Al-Saymari and Abu Ja'far ibn Shirzad were the first to enter [[person/al-mustakfi-billah|al-Mustakfi]]'s audience on the day he was deposed, taking their places before Mu'izz al-Dawla came in, as told in [[event/mustakfi-blinding-334|the account of the deposition]]. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.116}

### In the war with Nasir al-Dawla
In [[event/battle-of-baghdad-946|Nasir al-Dawla's war for Baghdad]] in 334 AH, al-Saymari handed over the household business of Mu'izz al-Dawla and his entourage to Abu Ali al-Hasan ibn Harun because he was occupied with the fighting. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.122} With the commander Isfahdost he routed Safi al-Tuzuni, who had crossed with a thousand men to surprise Mu'izz al-Dawla's camp. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.123} It was he who told Miskawayh that they were so exhausted that Mu'izz al-Dawla had resolved to withdraw to al-Ahwaz. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.124}

At dawn on Saturday, the last day of Dhu al-Hijja, al-Saymari and Isfahdost crossed to the east bank with a handful of men. Miskawayh writes: "Al-Saymari was the first to risk himself, because his men were afraid to cross; when he went ahead of them they were ashamed and followed him." {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125} When the soldiers would not stop looting, al-Saymari rode out, killed several of them, crucified some Daylamite soldiers and kept patrolling in person until he had calmed them. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.126} It was to him that Mu'izz al-Dawla handed the man who had tried to kill Nasir al-Dawla in his tent, and he put him to death. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.127}

### The marshes, Shiraz and his death
In 338 AH al-Saymari went down to fight Imran ibn Shahin, a man of al-Jamida who had fled into the marshes (al-Batiha), lived among the reeds and thickets, and gathered fishermen and bandits until he controlled the area. Al-Saymari defeated him again and again and captured his family and dependants. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.151} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.152} Then news came of Imad al-Dawla's death, and Mu'izz al-Dawla wrote telling him to hurry to Shiraz to set matters right; he left off pursuing Imran and hastened there. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.152} When Yanal Kusa and Kurkir killed their guards at the fortress of Ramhurmuz, Mu'izz al-Dawla wrote to al-Saymari, then at Shiraz, to hurry to the fortress and secure it. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.153}

Al-Saymari died in 339 AH "of an acute fever" after returning to fight Imran ibn Shahin in the district of al-Jamida. [[person/al-muhallabi-al-wazir|Abu Muhammad al-Muhallabi]] succeeded him as secretary; Miskawayh explains the choice by saying he had "deputised for al-Saymari in the vizierate, and so knew the hidden matters and secrets of the realm". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.156} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.157}
