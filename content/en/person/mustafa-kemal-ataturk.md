## Summary

Mustafa Kemal Atatürk (c. 1881 – 10 November 1938) was a Turkish field marshal and statesman, the founder of the Republic of Turkey and its first president from 1923 until his death; he led the Turkish War of Independence, then carried through a sweeping programme of secularising reforms, from the abolition of the sultanate in 1922 and of the caliphate in 1924 to the Latin alphabet, the Swiss civil code, the Hat Law and women's suffrage {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-ar}.

## Life

Mustafa Kemal was born in Salonica in the Ottoman Empire to Ali Rıza Efendi and Zübeyde Hanım; his exact birth day is not known, the English article giving only c. 1881, while Wikidata also carries the 19 May 1881 date he later adopted for himself, the anniversary of his 1919 landing at Samsun {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-en} {cite:wikidata-mustafa-kemal-ataturk}. He studied at the military school in Monastir and the War Academy in Istanbul, distinguished himself in the Balkan Wars and in the defence of Gallipoli in 1915, and after the Ottoman defeat led the national resistance in Anatolia; the Republic was proclaimed on 29 October 1923 and he became its first president {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-ar}. He married Latife Uşşaki in 1923 and divorced her in 1925, adopted several children, and died at Dolmabahçe Palace in Istanbul on 10 November 1938 {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-ar}.

## Work

His reforms remade state and society together: the sultanate and then the caliphate, the sharia courts and the ministry of religious endowments were abolished, education was unified, the Swiss civil code of 1926 improved women's legal standing, the Hat Law of 1925 regulated dress, the Latin alphabet replaced the Arabic script for Turkish in 1928, and women gained the vote in municipal elections in 1930 and national elections in 1934; he himself took the surname Atatürk, 'Father of the Turks', under the 1934 Surname Law {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-ar}. His personal religious beliefs remain widely debated, and this record settles none of it: the English article quotes his own description of Islam as 'the most reasonable and most natural' religion, and the Arabic article reports that his personal speeches and Turkish sources show a devout Muslim who called Islam 'our greatest religion' and the Qur'an 'the perfect book', while other sources have characterised him as an agnostic, a deist or an atheist, with the argument that his pro-Islam expressions were political calculation; both readings are preserved as they stand, pending the founder's ruling {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-kemal-ataturk-ar} {cite:wikidata-mustafa-kemal-ataturk}.
