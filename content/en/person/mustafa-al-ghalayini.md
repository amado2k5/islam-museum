## Overview
Mustafa ibn Muhammad Salim al-Ghalayini was a poet and one of the preaching littérateurs, a member of the Arab Academy of Damascus, born and died at Beirut in 1364 AH (1944 CE). He studied there and in Egypt, and in 1320 AH became a pupil of Shaykh Muhammad 'Abduh. {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.244}

Under the Ottoman constitution he published the magazine al-Nibras in Beirut for two years, taught Arabic at the Sultaniyya school for four, and was appointed preacher to the Ottoman Fourth Army in the First World War, marching with it from Damascus across the desert to the Suez Canal by Ismailiyya and witnessing the battle and the rout. After the war he volunteered in the Arab army at Damascus; arrested on a charge of complicity in the killing of As'ad Bey, the interior director, in 1922, he was released and went to Transjordan, where its amir Sharif Abd Allah charged him with teaching his two sons. He then returned to Beirut, was made head of its Islamic council and shari'a judge until his death. {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.244}

His books include Nazarat fi-l-Lugha wa-l-Adab; 'Idhat al-Nashi'in; Lubab al-Khiyar fi Sirat al-Nabi al-Mukhtar, abridged from his Khiyar al-Maqul fi Sirat al-Rasul; and al-Islam Ruh al-Madaniyya answering Cromer. {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.244}
