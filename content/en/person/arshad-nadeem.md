## Summary
Arshad Nadeem is a Pakistani javelin thrower, born in Mian Channu, Khanewal District, Punjab, on 2 January 1997. He is the reigning Olympic and Asian champion; his 92.97-metre throw at the Paris 2024 Olympics is an Olympic and Asian record and the sixth-longest throw in javelin history. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en} {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-ar}

He is the first Pakistani track and field athlete to earn direct Olympic qualification under modern entry standards, the first Pakistani to reach a World Championships final, and Pakistan's first Olympic medallist in athletics and first individual Olympic gold medallist. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

Raised in a poor family, he trained as a boy in courtyards and streets and has become a symbol of Pakistan's neglected non-cricket sports. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

## Career
Nadeem, the third-oldest of eight siblings, was a versatile school athlete who tried cricket, badminton, football and athletics; cricket was his passion until the coach Rasheed Ahmad Saqi spotted him at a school competition and began training him, and his father Muhammad Ashraf persuaded him to take up the javelin, which he first picked up in 2015. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

He began competing in 2015, received a World Athletics scholarship in 2016 to train at the IAAF High Performance Training Centre in Mauritius, and won bronze at the 2016 South Asian Games with a national record of 78.33 m. He added bronze at the 2017 Islamic Solidarity Games in Baku and bronze at the 2018 Asian Games in Jakarta with 80.75 m, Pakistan's first Asian Games athletics medal in 24 years. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

At the 2019 World Championships in Doha he set a national record of 81.52 m, then won gold at the 2019 South Asian Games with 86.29 m to book direct qualification for the Tokyo Olympics. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

At Tokyo 2020 he finished fifth in the final with 84.62 m, having trained beforehand in his home's courtyard and on the streets; his father said he had been given no proper training ground and no government financial assistance after qualifying. In 2022 he trained in South Africa under Terseus Liebenberg and finished fifth at the World Championships in Eugene with 86.16 m, the first Pakistani ever to reach a World Championships final. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

On 7 August 2022 he won gold at the Commonwealth Games in Birmingham with a games record of 90.18 m — the first South Asian to breach 90 metres, and Pakistan's first Commonwealth athletics gold since 1962 — and five days later won gold at the Islamic Solidarity Games with 88.55 m. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en} {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-ar}

He took silver at the 2023 World Championships in Budapest with 87.82 m, Pakistan's first ever medal at the World Athletics Championships. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

At Paris 2024 he won gold with 92.97 m, a new Olympic and Asian record and the sixth-longest throw ever; he was Pakistan's first Olympic athletics medallist, its first individual gold medallist, and the winner of its first Olympic medal since 1992 and first gold since 1984, and was honoured with a parade in Lahore. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

In 2025 he won the Asian Championships in Gumi with 86.40 m — the first Pakistani Asian gold since 1973 — and defended his Islamic Solidarity Games title in Riyadh with 83.05 m, after calf surgery in Cambridge in July 2025. In the 2026 season, as the article's current revision records, he failed to defend his Commonwealth title, finishing ninth. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

## Works
His leading marks and titles are the Paris 2024 Olympic gold (92.97 m, Olympic and Asian record), the 2023 World Championships silver, the 2022 Commonwealth Games gold (90.18 m), the Islamic Solidarity Games golds of 2022 and 2025, and the 2025 Asian Championships gold. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

He received the Pride of Performance in 2022 and the Hilal-e-Imtiaz in August 2024; a postage stamp was issued bearing his image, and a road in Islamabad was renamed after him. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section presents positions reported by the sources; it is not the museum's verdict on any of them.

Nadeem has spoken openly, as the article reports, about the extreme difficulties of non-cricket sportspeople in Pakistan, who are largely ignored by sports officials, and his father stated that he received no financial assistance from the government after qualifying for Tokyo. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

Before Paris he was widely considered an underdog; he answered with an Olympic-record gold. His coach Salman Iqbal Butt describes him as "Zen-like", quiet and focused, and Nadeem describes himself as an introvert. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en}

On his faith the sources used here give only general statements: the English article says he was born into a Punjabi Muslim Rajput family — a family-background description — and calls him "a devout Muslim" in its personal-life section, while his Wikidata item carries no religion statement; none of these names a school, so the museum records none. {cite:wikipedia-arshad-nadeem-en} {cite:wikidata-arshad-nadeem}
