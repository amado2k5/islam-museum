## Summary
Sherman Jackson is an American Muslim professor of Islamic studies and law, among the voices renewing jurisprudence in the West. He deploys both Azharite and academic training to approach questions of identity and citizenship for American Muslims. {cite:wikipedia-sherman-jackson}

## Life and upbringing
Born in the United States in 1956, he embraced Islam in his youth, studied at al-Azhar University in Cairo, then took a doctorate at the University of Pennsylvania, combining traditional learning with Western academic training. {cite:wikipedia-sherman-jackson}

## Career and works
He taught at several American universities and settled as professor at the University of Southern California, where he holds a chair in Islamic thought. His best-known books include Islam and the Blackamerican, on the experience of Black American Muslims, and Islamic Law and the State, on juristic constitutional theory. {cite:wikipedia-sherman-jackson}

## Method and contribution
He develops concepts such as Blackamerican fiqh and a “third source of authority” to describe Muslim communal authority in jurisprudence apart from the modern state. He is counted among the foremost theorists of American Islam, and his theses are debated in academic and religious circles alike, accepted and contested; the museum attributes the disagreement to its holders. {cite:wikipedia-sherman-jackson}
