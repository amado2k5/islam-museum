## Overview
Al-Mustarshid billah, Abu Mansur al-Fadl ibn al-Mustazhir, an Abbasid caliph who ruled between 512 and 529 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 663-665}

## His life
He was born in Rabi al-Awwal 485 AH, and was given the allegiance on the death of his father [[person/al-mustazhir-billah|al-Mustazhir billah]] in 512 AH. Al-Suyuti describes him as having high aspiration and a severe dignity; he kept the affairs of the caliphate in order, revived its customs, and took part in the wars himself. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 663-665}

He went out on one of his last campaigns, and his army was defeated near Hamadhan, and he was taken captive to Azerbaijan. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 663-665}

## Calligraphy and learning
He had beautiful handwriting. Al-Suyuti says that none of the caliphs before him had written like him, and that he used to correct and improve his books. Abu Bakr al-Shashi composed for him his book "al-Umda" on jurisprudence, which became known by its title "Support of the World and Religion". {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 663-665}

## His death
He was killed at Maragha on Thursday, the sixteenth of Dhu al-Qa'da 529 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 663-665}
