## Overview
Rukana ibn Abd Yazid ibn Hashim ibn al-Muttalib ibn Abd Manaf al-Muttalibi is known for the story of his wrestling bout with the Prophet. Ibn Hajar gives him entry 2766 in the letter ra in al-Isaba {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 481}, and Abu Nu'aym includes him in his Ma'rifat al-Sahaba, where he gives the wrestling report and the report of Rukana's divorce of his wife {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1113}.

## The wrestling story in Ibn Hajar
Ibn Hajar quotes al-Baladhuri, from Ibn Kharbudh and others, that Rukana came back from a journey and heard about the Prophet. He met him in a mountain near Mecca and said that if the Prophet threw him he would know he was truthful; they wrestled, the Prophet threw him, and Rukana became a Muslim at the conquest of Mecca, though some say he accepted Islam right after the bout {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 481}. He quotes al-Zubayr that Rukana was in Mecca before Islam and was the strongest of men; he said that if thrown he would believe, and after being thrown said the Prophet was a sorcerer, then accepted Islam later, and the Prophet gave him fifty wasaq of food {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 481}.

## The chain of the report
Ibn Hajar notes that Ibn Hibban said there is something to look into in the chain of the wrestling report. He explains that this points to the hadith recorded by Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi through Abu al-Hasan al-Asqalani, from Abu Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn Rukana, from his father, and that al-Tirmidhi called it gharib with a chain that is not firm {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 481}.

## Abu Nu'aym's long version
Abu Nu'aym narrates from Abu Umama that Rukana was a polytheist and one of the most formidable men, who grazed his sheep in the valley of Idam. The Prophet met him there alone, and Rukana proposed a wrestling match for ten of his sheep. The Prophet threw him three times, and each time Rukana said the God of Muhammad had helped him and that al-Lat and al-Uzza had failed him {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1114} {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1115}. The report goes on to say that the Prophet invited him to Islam and that Rukana asked for a sign; a tree split, came forward and returned to its place. Rukana said he had seen something great but did not want the women and boys of Medina to hear that he had given in out of fear, so he did not accept Islam on that occasion {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1115} {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1116}. It adds that Abu Bakr and Umar went looking for the Prophet, afraid that Rukana might meet him, and that the Prophet told them what had happened {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1116}.

## Other versions of the bout
Abu Nu'aym gives the version of Hammad ibn Salama, from Amr ibn Dinar, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, in which the Prophet passed Rukana at al-Abtah with some goats and they wrestled for a sheep, repeating the bout three times; Hammad says he thinks the report ends with Rukana accepting Islam and the Prophet returning his sheep {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1116}. He also gives Ma'mar's version, from Abd Allah ibn al-Harith, that the Prophet wrestled Rukana in the pre-Islamic period and threw him {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1117}.

## The report of his divorce
Abu Nu'aym narrates from Ibn Abbas that Rukana ibn Abd Yazid, of the Banu al-Muttalib, divorced his wife three times in one sitting and was very sad. The Prophet asked how many times he had divorced her, and when he said three in one sitting, the Prophet said it was only one, and that he could take her back if he wished {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1113}. A second chain, from Abd Allah ibn Yazid ibn Rukana, from his father, from his grandfather, says he divorced her by the word albatta; asked what he meant, he said one, and the Prophet had him swear to it {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1113}. Abu Nu'aym also gives al-Shafi'i's route, through Nafi' ibn Ujayr, naming the wife as Suhla al-Muzaniyya {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1114}. Ibn Hajar notes that the chain of this report is disputed among its transmitters {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 481} {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 482}.

## Those who narrated from him, and his death
Ibn Hajar says Nafi' ibn Ujayr and his grandson Ali ibn Yazid ibn Rukana narrated from him {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 482}. He quotes al-Zubayr that he died in Medina in Mu'awiya's caliphate, Abu Nu'aym that he died in Uthman's caliphate, and a third view that he lived until the year 41 {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 482}.

## Rukana Abu Muhammad
Entry 2767 in al-Isaba is Rukana Abu Muhammad. Ibn Hajar says Ibn Abi Dawud and al-Baladhuri separated him from Rukana ibn Abd Yazid al-Muttalibi, and that they gave, through Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Rukana from his father, the statement that he wrestled the Prophet and was thrown; Ibn Mandah thought he was the same man as the first {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 482}. Ibn Hajar says the sound view is that he is the same man, since the wrestling story is famous for Rukana ibn Abd Yazid and was recorded by al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Qani' and others {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 482}. Abu Nu'aym lists Rukana Abu Muhammad without a lineage, notes that some later scholars separate him from the first, and says he sees only the earlier man {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p. 1117}.
