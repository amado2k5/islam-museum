## Overview
Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam ibn A'yan ibn Layth, Abu al-Qasim, was known among his brothers as Ibn Abd al-Hakam. His editor reports that he was born about 187 AH, died at [[place/al-fustat|al-Fustat]] in 257 AH, and was buried beside his father next to the tomb of [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]]. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ج}

## Family
His father, [[person/abdallah-ibn-abd-al-hakam|Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam]], was a Maliki jurist and hadith scholar of Egypt who led the Malikis there after Ashhab and heard the Muwatta' from Malik himself. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ج} He was a friend of al-Shafi'i, who stayed with him on coming to Egypt and died in his house, as the editor quotes from Ibn Farhun's al-Dibaj al-Mudhhab. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ط} Among the brothers, [[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-allah-ibn-abd-al-hakam|Muhammad]] succeeded his father at the head of the Malikis, while Abd al-Rahman was a hadith scholar learned in history. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ط}

The editor relates two blows to the family: its property was confiscated and the sons jailed for a time when they could not pay a sum demanded of them, and later the sons were tried in the [[event/mihna-of-the-quran|Mihna]] under al-Wathiq, refused the doctrine of the created Qur'an, and one of them, Abd al-Hakam, died in prison. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ط} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ى}

## His book and method
He wrote [[work/futuh-misr|Futuh Misr wa-l-Maghrib]], giving chains of transmission in the manner of the hadith scholars, unlike al-Baladhuri, al-Tabari and al-Dinawari. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ى} He drew on the oral reports current in Egypt and on the written material of Yahya ibn Abd Allah ibn Bukayr, al-Waqidi and Ibn Lahi'a. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ى} Some scholars hold that most of his dates come from the writings of [[person/layth-ibn-sad|al-Layth ibn Sa'd]] and [[person/yazid-ibn-abi-habib|Yazid ibn Abi Habib]], and that he relied on Uthman ibn Salih for the dating of events. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ك} The editor observes that he gathered a large body of material, including popular tales and legends, without criticising the chains, and that he rarely gave chains in the chapter on the plots of al-Fustat, which he drew from what was common knowledge among its people and from what he had seen himself. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ك} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ل}
