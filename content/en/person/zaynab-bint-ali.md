## Brief
Zaynab was a granddaughter of the Prophet through [[person/fatima]] and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib]]. Taken captive with the women and children after Karbala, she is remembered in tradition as the family's fearless voice before its enemies.

## Life
Born in Medina about 626 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}, Zaynab was married to her cousin Abdullah ibn Ja'far and had a household of her own when her brother [[person/husayn-ibn-ali]] set out for Kufa in 680.

She accompanied him and was present at Karbala. When the killing was over, tradition tells that she spoke back to the governor Ibn Ziyad in Kufa and to Yazid in Damascus, refusing to let the family's death be treated as a rebel's end {cite:momen-1985}. Reports differ on her later life and on whether she died in Damascus, Cairo or Medina — Cairo keeps her shrine {cite:momen-1985}.

## Legacy
In both Sunni and Shia memory Zaynab carries the story of Karbala past the battlefield: the sister who buried the dead, guarded the orphans, and told the world what had happened. Her name is one of the most honored for Muslim girls.

## For young visitors
After a terrible battle, Zaynab was brought before the rulers as a prisoner. Instead of staying silent, she stood up and told them the truth about what they had done.
