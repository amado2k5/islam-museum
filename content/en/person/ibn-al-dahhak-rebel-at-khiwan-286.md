## Overview
The book gives this man no name beyond "Ibn al-Dahhak" and no lineage; it says he attacked [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] on the night at Khiwan and routed the commander with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144} [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] sought him and his companions at al-Ghubayb and found the people gone {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144}; when the rest came to him in obedience Ibn al-Dahhak, afraid of al-Hadi, stayed in the land of [[group/banu-mamar-hamdan|Banu Ma'mar]] with a small party of Khiwan's slaves. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145} The narrator calls him a weak boy with no sense, which is the narrator's view alone and not the museum's judgement. He is not linked to the narrator [[person/ahmad-ibn-al-dahhak-qadi-of-hamdan|Ahmad ibn al-Dahhak]] of p. 140, since the book does not join them. See [[event/al-hadi-at-hawth-and-al-ghubayb-and-the-return-to-khiwan-on-1-dhu-al-qada-286]].
