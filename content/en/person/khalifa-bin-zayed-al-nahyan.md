# Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan

Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan was the second president of the United Arab Emirates and the ruler of Abu Dhabi from 2004 until his death. He was born on 7 September 1948 at Qasr Al-Muwaiji in Al Ain, the eldest son of Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the federation's founding president, and of Hassa bint Mohammed Al Nahyan; his kunya was Abu Sultan, and he was named for his ancestor Khalifa bin Shakhbut. He died at Abu Dhabi on 13 May 2022 and was buried at Al Bateen Cemetery. {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-ur} {cite:wikidata-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan}

## Early life and education

Khalifa spent most of his childhood in Al Ain. His early education began with the traditional learning of the tenets of Islam from a religious teacher (mutawa), Sheikh Thani bin Ahmed; his father then arranged a qualified tutor from Muscat and a small school at Muwaiji, and Khalifa went on to the Al Nahyaneia Model School and Alfalah school, and later graduated from the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst. He regularly attended his father's majlis, where he gained experience in leadership. {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-ur}

## Offices before the presidency

In 1966, when his father became ruler of Abu Dhabi, Khalifa was appointed at eighteen as the ruler's representative in the Eastern Region and head of its courts. He was named crown prince of Abu Dhabi on 1 February 1969 and chaired its defence department; after the federation he became prime minister of Abu Dhabi in 1971, chaired its Executive Council from 1974, served as deputy prime minister of the UAE in the second federal cabinet (1973–1977), and became deputy supreme commander of the UAE armed forces in May 1976. He later chaired the Supreme Petroleum Council. From the late 1990s, as his father's health declined, he carried out some aspects of the presidency in a de facto capacity. {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-ur}

## President: 2004–2022

Khalifa succeeded his father as ruler of Abu Dhabi on 2 November 2004, and the Federal Supreme Council elected him president the following day. As ruler he attracted cultural and academic institutions to Abu Dhabi — the Louvre Abu Dhabi, New York University Abu Dhabi and Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi — and established Etihad Airways; under his presidency the UAE became a regional economic powerhouse and its non-oil economy grew. He was viewed as a pro-Western modernizer who forged closer ties with the United States and Israel. During the 2008 financial crisis he directed billions of dollars in emergency bailout funds into Dubai, and on 4 January 2010 the world's tallest structure, originally Burj Dubai, was renamed Burj Khalifa in his honour. {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-en}

## Later years and death

In January 2014 Khalifa suffered a stroke and underwent surgery, after which he was reported to be in stable condition. He then assumed a lower profile, retaining ceremonial presidential powers while his half-brother Mohamed bin Zayed carried out the public affairs of the state and the day-to-day decision-making of Abu Dhabi. Forbes named him in its 2018 list of the world's most powerful people. He died on 13 May 2022 and was succeeded by Mohamed bin Zayed. {cite:wikipedia-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan-en} {cite:wikidata-khalifa-bin-zayed-al-nahyan}
