## Overview
The vizier of the Abbasid caliph al-Musta'sim. The sources differ on his role in the fall of Baghdad, and this page gives what each source says in its own attribution.

## In al-Suyuti
Al-Suyuti says he was eager to remove the Abbasid state and transfer it to the Alids. He secretly sided with the Tatars, encouraged them to come to Iraq and take Baghdad. He kept their news from the caliph and advised him to cut down most of the army and to flatter the Tatars. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 710-711}

Al-Suyuti says that he did not achieve what he wanted, and he tasted humiliation from the Tatars until he died of grief. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 716,718}

## In Ibn Khaldun
Ibn Khaldun says he was a Rafidi, and that his letter reached Hulagu on the road, urging him to march on Baghdad. Then he went out to him when he camped at Baghdad, sought safe conduct for himself, and returned with the safe conduct to al-Musta'sim. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p. 900-901}

## In Ibn al-Imad
Ibn al-Imad says he wrote to the Tatars when the plunder fell on the people of his sect. He sent his brother and his slave to Hulagu and asked to be his deputy over Baghdad. He used to keep from the caliph the letters of the lord of Mosul. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 278}

The museum does not judge between these accounts. Comparing them needs an additional source.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives a modern Sunni account of the vizier Ibn al-Alqami's role in the episodes on the fall of Baghdad (fourteen to sixteen) of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]]; what follows is his account, under his name. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:02:30}

### The vizier at court
Al-Suwaidan says that Hulagu's political and diplomatic effort reached the chief minister of the Abbasid caliphate, the second man in the state after the caliph, the vizier Mu'ayyad al-Din Ibn al-Alqami. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:05:00} He says he "was not a Sunni", wonders that a Sunni state bearing the name of the caliphate gave him the premiership, and counts this as poor management by the caliph [[person/al-mustasim-billah|al-Musta'sim bi'llah]], who, he says, was taken up with amusement and left the whole running of the state to the vizier. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:05:30} He calls him one of the "evil inner circle" and says he stayed chief minister for fourteen years, from 642 AH until 656 AH, when Baghdad fell. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:07:00}

### The secret agreement
Al-Suwaidan relates that correspondence began between Hulagu and Ibn al-Alqami, and that the vizier reckoned that neither he nor the caliphate could hold out against the Tatars and their alliances, so he agreed to cooperate with them on two conditions: that the Tatars spare Baghdad and the caliphate and not kill the people, and that he himself sit on the governing council that would run Baghdad afterwards. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:08:00} He says the great historians, Ibn Kathir among them, point to this agreement and hold that without it Baghdad would not have fallen. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:08:00} Returning to it in episode fifteen, he says this "betrayal" is agreed on by the great historians, and that it passed from a secret agreement into fact and was a main cause of Baghdad's collapse. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:03:30}

### Counsel to weaken the army, and to appease
Al-Suwaidan says Ibn al-Alqami persuaded the caliph to cut the army's budget, reduce the number of soldiers, turn the state away from arms and put the army to civilian work in farming, manufacture and building, so as not to provoke the Tatars and to prove himself a man of peace. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:05:00} He reports that [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] in "al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya" lays full blame on Ibn al-Alqami for his advice to the caliph, but greater blame on the caliph himself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:07:00} When Hulagu's second letter came, Ibn al-Alqami, as he relates, advised the caliph to placate Hulagu with money and treasures, apologise to him, and put his name in the Friday sermon and on the coinage, and the Lesser Dawatdar, the army commander, accused him of treason and collusion with Hulagu. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:10:30} On 14 Muharram 656 AH, he says, Ibn al-Alqami went out with a body of his mamluks and followers telling people not to shoot arrows and saying that a peace was coming. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:16:00}

### Where the accounts meet and where they differ
Al-Suwaidan's account agrees with what this page gives from al-Suyuti, that the vizier advised the caliph to cut most of the army and to appease the Tatars. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:04:30} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 710-711} On motive the sources cited here differ: al-Suyuti ascribes to him the wish to move the caliphate to the Alids, Ibn al-Imad puts his letters to the Tatars down to the plundering suffered by those of his own school, while al-Suwaidan presents the agreement as a bargain in which the vizier sought safety for Baghdad and a seat in government afterwards. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 710-711} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 278} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:08:00} These accounts are set out in the record's perspectives block, each under its own name, with no verdict.
