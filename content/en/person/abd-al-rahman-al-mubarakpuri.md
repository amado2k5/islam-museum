## Summary
Indian hadith scholar (1865–1935), a luminary of the Ahl-i Hadith in the subcontinent. He is famed for Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi bi-Sharh Jami' al-Tirmidhi in ten volumes — still the widest commentary on Tirmidhi — and for Abkar al-Minan and other hadith studies. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-rahman-al-mubarakpuri}

## Life and career

Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd al-Rahim al-Mubarakpuri was born in 1865 in Mubarakpur, India, raised on religious study, then joined the Ahl-i Hadith school that prefers direct recourse to hadith texts, and kept company with its shaykhs until he excelled. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-rahman-al-mubarakpuri}

He served as a qadi in the princely state of Rampur, then taught and wrote, becoming known for rich editing and wide command of hadith commentaries and narrator biographies. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-rahman-al-mubarakpuri}

## Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi

He completed his commentary on Jami' al-Tirmidhi, Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi, in ten large volumes covering wording, narrators, derived law and madhhab disputes, until it became the leading printed reference for students of Tirmidhi. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-rahman-al-mubarakpuri}

Ahl-i Hadith sources relate that publishing houses with princely patronage commissioned it; hadith scholars acknowledge its abundance of material, while some note points where researchers need further refinement. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-rahman-al-mubarakpuri}

## Legacy

His other works include Abkar al-Minan fi al-Tanqih and treatises and editions in the hadith sciences; Indian muhaddiths who served the library of the Sunna studied under him. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-rahman-al-mubarakpuri}

He died in 1935; Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi remains the widest printed commentary on Tirmidhi and the most used in libraries and hadith studies. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-rahman-al-mubarakpuri}
