## Overview
Al-Fadl ibn Yahya ibn Khalid ibn Barmak, of the [[group/barmakids|Barmakids]], and the son of [[person/yahya-ibn-khalid-al-barmaki|Yahya ibn Khalid]]. He was a foster-brother of [[person/harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]]. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.98}

## In the state
Al-Rashid entrusted him with supervising the upbringing of his son al-Amin. He appointed him to administrative and military posts, especially in the East. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.98}

Taqqush says that in the East he brought about economic and building revival and secured the peace. He and his father and his brother Ja'far ran the caliphate for seventeen years (170–187 AH). {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.98}

## The fall
In the fall of the Barmakids he was imprisoned with his father and brothers, and their property was confiscated. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.100} See [[event/killing-of-jafar-and-the-barmakids|the killing of Ja'far and the fall of the Barmakids]].

He died in prison in 193 AH. {cite:tarikh-dawla-abbasiyya-taqqush p.100}

## Khurasan and return to Baghdad

Hakki Dursun Yildiz records his appointment over Jibal, Azerbaijan and Tabaristan in176 AH/792–793, followed by Khurasan the next year, with improvements and canals serving agriculture {cite:tdv-bermekiler al-Fadl career}. After returning to Baghdad he remained at the administrative center during Harun and Yahya's797 pilgrimage, then lost his responsibilities except al-Amin's education {cite:tdv-bermekiler al-Fadl career}.

## Transmitting papermaking

Osman Ersoy attributes the proposal for the Baghdad paper workshop in178 AH/794–795 to him: in the [[invention/baghdad-papermaking|papermaking record]] he is a transmitter and proposer, not paper's inventor or a substitute identity for unnamed workers {cite:tdv-kagit Baghdad paragraph}. The Chinese origin precedes this act by centuries; Bloom supports Baghdad manufacture by the late eighth century without independently verifying al-Fadl's name or the exact year {cite:iranica-paper-i early history}.

## Identity basis and reported allegations

**Museum Sunni frame:** Yildiz names al-Fadl in the802 pilgrimage party and associates him with Ramadan mosque lighting: personal biographical evidence for Muslim identity, not an inference from origin, name or office, nor a declaration of a particular legal school {cite:tdv-bermekiler pilgrimage and mosque lighting}.

**Yildiz's historical assessment:** He reports poetry implying the Barmakids had not accepted Islam, finding it improbable amid political rivalry; that is his account and assessment, not the museum's verdict or a personal fact about al-Fadl, and the original poems were not read here {cite:tdv-bermekiler allegations discussion}. He places al-Fadl's pilgrimage in the family's final journey before its fall and records his death in808; the inherited biography above remains with its original source {cite:tdv-bermekiler pilgrimage and fall}.
