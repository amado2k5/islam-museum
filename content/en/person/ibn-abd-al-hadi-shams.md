## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abd al-Hadi ibn Abd al-Hamid ibn Abd al-Hadi, Shams al-Din Abu Abd Allah, listed by al-Zirikli under Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi, was of Jamma'ili origin and later a Damascene of al-Salihiyya: a hafiz of hadith, learned in literature and one of the leading Hanbalis. He is called Ibn Abd al-Hadi after a remote ancestor. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 705 AH, 1305 CE, and his death as 744 AH, 1343 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 326}.

## Teachers and life
He studied under Ibn Taymiyya, al-Dhahabi and others, and wrote more than seventy books, of which the completed ones run to over a hundred volumes; he died before reaching the age of forty {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 326}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists among his books: al-Uqud al-durriyya fi manaqib Shaykh al-Islam Ahmad ibn Taymiyya, printed; al-Muharrar in hadith, a musnad, printed; Fada'il al-Sham, in manuscript; Qawa'id usul al-fiqh, in manuscript; al-Sarim al-munki fi l-radd ala Ibn al-Subki, printed; a commentary on al-Tashil; al-Ilal in hadith, arranged on the order of the fiqh books; al-Ihkam in Hanbali law; and Tarajim al-huffaz; he adds that there are others {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 326}.

## Death
He died on the outskirts of Damascus {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 326}.
