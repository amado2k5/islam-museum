## Overview
Hamdun ibn Ahmad ibn 'Imara al-Qassar al-Naysaburi, Abu Salih, was a Sufi, the shaykh of the People of Blame (ahl al-malama) at Nishapur from whom the Malamati way spread; he was also a scholar-jurist of the Thawri school and had a spiritual path of his own. He died in 271 AH (884 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.274}

Of his sayings: “Whichever of you can avoid being blind to his own shortcomings, let him do so.” {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.274}

## In al-Risala al-Qushayriyya
In [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]] al-Qushayri gives a notice of Abu Salih Hamdun ibn Ahmad ibn 'Umara al-Qassar: a man of Nishapur "from whom the way of the Malamatiyya spread in Nishapur", a companion of Salm al-Barusi and Abu Turab al-Nakhshabi, who died in 271 AH. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 152} Among the sayings he quotes: asked when a man may speak to people, he said, when it falls on him to discharge one of God's obligations in his knowledge, or he fears a person's ruin in an innovation from which he hopes God will save him; and: whoever thinks his own self better than Pharaoh's has displayed arrogance. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 152} He relates that a friend of Hamdun's died while he sat at his head, and Hamdun put out the lamp, saying: until now the oil was his; from now on it belongs to the heirs. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 153}
