## Summary
Yusuf Ali Kenadid (1837 – 14 August 1911) was a Somali sultan, founder and first Sultan of the Sultanate of Hobyo (reigned April 1878 – 28 September 1911), succeeded by his son [[person/ali-yusuf-kenadid|Ali Yusuf Kenadid]]. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en} Note for the reader: the dates given for him in the harvest list from which his name came (1845–1929) are supported by no source read; the article on which this page rests gives 1837–1911 with its citations, and those dates are followed here.

## Background
Yusuf Ali was born at Alula in the Majeerteen Sultanate, into the Bah Yaaqub branch of the Osman Mahamuud, Majeerteen Darod family. He was not a lineal descendant of the dynasties that had governed north-eastern Somalia before him: he independently amassed his own fortune and evolved into a skilled military leader. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en} "Kenadid" was not his surname but a title given to him by his rivals, meaning "he who refuses to be haltered". {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en} He was the father of Osman Yusuf Kenadid, who would later create the Osmanya writing script for the Somali language. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en}

## Founding of the Sultanate of Hobyo
The sultanate grew out of a rift within the Majeerteen Sultanate: Yusuf Ali first aimed to seize the neighbouring Majeerteen Sultanate from his cousin Boqor Osman Mahamud, failed, and was forced into exile in Yemen. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en} A decade later, in the 1870s, he returned from the Arabian Peninsula with a band of Hadramis, overpowered the local Habar Gidir clans in Mudug, and established the Sultanate of Hobyo in 1878. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en} {cite:wikipedia-sultanate-of-hobyo-en} The sultanate exercised strong centralised authority, with a functioning bureaucracy, a hereditary nobility, a state flag and a professional army, and kept written records of its activities, which still exist. {cite:wikipedia-sultanate-of-hobyo-en}

## The protectorate treaty and exile
To protect himself from further Zanzibari hostility, Yusuf Ali entered in late 1888 into a treaty with the Italians, making his realm an Italian protectorate in 1889; its terms specified that Italy was to steer clear of any interference in the sultanate's administration, in return for Italian arms, an annual subsidy and minimal oversight and economic concessions. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en} The relationship later soured when he refused the Italians' proposal to allow a British contingent to disembark in his sultanate to pursue the Dervish forces of the Somali religious and nationalist leader Mohammed Abdullah Hassan; viewed as too much of a threat, he was exiled by the Italians to Aden in Yemen and then to Italian Eritrea, as was his son Ali Yusuf, his heir apparent. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en}

## Death and succession
Yusuf Ali died at Hobyo in 1911, the article's lead giving the day as 14 August 1911, and was succeeded on the throne by his son Ali Yusuf, the second and last Sultan of Hobyo. {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-ali-kenadid-en}
