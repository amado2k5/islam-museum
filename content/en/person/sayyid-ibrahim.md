## Summary
Egyptian master calligrapher (1897–1994), counted among the great teachers of Arabic calligraphy in the twentieth century. He taught at Cairo's Royal School for the Improvement of Calligraphy, executed inscriptions for Egyptian mosques and buildings, and wrote many modern Qur'an manuscripts and books. {cite:wikipedia-sayyid-ibrahim}

## Life and formation

Sayyid Ibrahim was born in Egypt in 1897 and learned calligraphy under the masters of Cairo's Royal School for the Improvement of Calligraphy, the institution that trained a generation of leading Egyptian calligraphers in the first half of the twentieth century. {cite:wikipedia-sayyid-ibrahim}

He excelled in thuluth and naskh and transmitted his craft to many students who became masters in turn, so that his school of writing is described as a key link in the chain of modern Arabic calligraphy. {cite:wikipedia-sayyid-ibrahim}

## Works

His inscriptions were executed for a number of mosques and public buildings in Egypt, and he wrote Qur'an manuscripts and sections that were printed and circulated across the Muslim world, as well as instructional panels used to teach calligraphy to generations of learners. {cite:wikipedia-sayyid-ibrahim}

Sources record his contributions to the inscriptions of Cairo mosques, his penned Qur'an manuscripts printed in Egypt, and his artistic panels held in public and private collections. {cite:wikipedia-sayyid-ibrahim}

## Legacy

Sayyid Ibrahim is remembered among the great renewers of modern Egyptian calligraphy, combining the discipline of the Ottoman school tradition with a distinctive Egyptian sensibility in composition and letter ornamentation. {cite:wikipedia-sayyid-ibrahim}

He died in 1994 after a career spanning nearly a full century; his works survive in mosques, print and calligraphy collections, and his style is still cited in the teaching of calligraphy today. {cite:wikipedia-sayyid-ibrahim}
