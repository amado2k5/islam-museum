## Summary
Mehmed Fuad Pasha (born in Istanbul in 1814 – died at Nice in France in February 1869) was an Ottoman statesman, twice Grand Vizier (November 1861 to January 1863, and June 1863 to June 1866) and Foreign Minister for nearly ten years by the Turkish article's count; he led the Porte's mission to Syria during the 1860 Mount Lebanon civil war and stood with Mustafa Reşid Pasha and Mehmed Emin Âli Pasha as one of the three great men of the Tanzimat. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-tr} He was the son of the poet-scholar Keçecizade İzzet Molla and was famed for his sharp wit and his French. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-tr} {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en}

## Career
Fuad was born in 1814 in Constantinople to a prominent ulema family: his father İzzet Molla was a famous poet, his grandfather Mehmed Salih Efendi was Kazasker of Rumelia, and his mother descended from the seventeenth-century grand vizier Merzifonlu Kara Mustafa Pasha. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-tr} {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} He received a medrese education but had to leave it when his father was dismissed and banished; he then studied four years at the Tıbhane-i Amire medical school and served as an Admiralty doctor with no family support, while continuing as a littérateur and poet like his father. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-tr}

His fluent French took him to Tunis as scribe to the governor (1832–1836), then into Reşid Pasha's service; he was First Translator of the Porte from 1838 to 1852, negotiated in Bucharest and St Petersburg in 1848, where he persuaded Czar Nicholas I to drop his demand for the extradition of the 1848 revolutionaries, and worked in Egypt in 1852. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} He became Foreign Minister in 1852 on Âli Pasha's recommendation, then a member and in 1856 chairman of the Council of the Tanzimat, where his two years of study produced the Provincial Regulation of 1858 and financial reforms that ended tax farming in 1861. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en}

In 1860 he was sent to Syria with extraordinary powers after the Maronite–Druze massacres in Mount Lebanon: he executed hundreds of accused pillagers and Druze leaders after military trials, and chaired the international Beirut Committee whose March 1861 settlement made Mount Lebanon a mutasarrifate ruled by a non-local Christian governor under the Porte. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} Sultan Abdülaziz made him Grand Vizier in 1861 in place of Âli Pasha, and he served two terms, 22 November 1861 to January 1863 and June 1863 to June 1866, resigning over the Sultan's plan to marry the Khedive Isma'il's daughter. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} Reappointed Foreign Minister in 1867, he acted as Grand Vizier while Âli was in Crete and accompanied Abdülaziz on his European tour that summer; the double burden broke his health, he sought rest and treatment in France, and he died at Nice, his body being returned to Constantinople aboard a French naval aviso. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en}

## Works
Fuad was a maker of Ottomanism: the hope, in his words as the English article reports them, of finding "salvation for the empire by creating among its peoples the bond of equal citizenship based on Ottoman nationality", uniting Jews, Christians and Muslims so that equal rights through the millet order would dull nationalist and separatist tendencies. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} His Provincial Regulation of 1858 made each governor the chief authority and the central government's representative in his province, created cadastral departments registering every male resident — Muslim, non-Muslim or foreign — with a population tax certificate that served as an identity card, and introduced a new conscription system and annual budgets under treasury scrutiny. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} In his political testament he urged alliance with Britain and France against Russia, calling the English the empire's most important ally, to be held "to the last", even at the cost of provinces. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} He favoured a French-inspired civil code for the new civil courts, yet was a fervent supporter of absolute monarchy who rejected being bound by a constitution or legislature, and so clashed with liberal Young Ottoman intellectuals such as Namık Kemal, Ziya Pasha and İbrahim Şinasi. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum.

The English article records that his Syrian severity was called by some a "reign of terror", while Ottoman authorities framed the punitive measures as impartial justice aligned with Tanzimat ideals of equality before the law, and Lord Dufferin described him as "tall, handsome, well-versed in French, and of charming manners" and proposed him as governor-general of a semi-independent Syria. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en} His settled politics, as the article presents them, joined administrative modernisation to absolute monarchy, which made him the liberals' opponent. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en}

On his religion: no article read for this record names a school for him. The Turkish infobox religion field is empty, the Arabic infobox religion field is empty, the English infobox has no religion field, and Wikidata's religion value is the generic item Islam, naming no school. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-tr} {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-ar} {cite:wikidata-kececizade-fuad-pasha} His ulema family and medrese education are background and formation, which never count as attestation, and the English article's note that he was a freemason records a lodge membership, not a legal school; his school is therefore recorded as unknown, with the readings preserved in the record's faith note. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-en}

The documentary disagreements are three: the Turkish article gives his death year as 1868, in lead and infobox, against 1869 in the English and Arabic articles and Wikidata; the death day is 12 February in the English article and 13 February in Wikidata; and the days of his grand-vizier terms differ by a day or two between the Turkish infobox and the English succession boxes, whose dates this account follows. Wikidata also carries a day-precision birth, 17 January 1814, that no article supports; the record keeps the articles' year precision. {cite:wikipedia-kececizade-fuad-pasha-tr} {cite:wikidata-kececizade-fuad-pasha}
