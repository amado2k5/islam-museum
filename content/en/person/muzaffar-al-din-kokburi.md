## Overview
Muzaffar al-Din Kokburi ibn Zayn al-Din Ali, brother of [[person/zayn-al-din-yusuf-irbil|Zayn al-Din Yusuf]], lord of Irbil. He became lord of Irbil after his brother. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.199}

## After his brother's death
Zayn al-Din died on 18 Ramadan 586 AH while the two of them were with Salah al-Din in the army. Imad al-Katib describes in *al-Barq al-Shami* that people came to offer Kokburi condolences, and found him busy securing what his brother had left. He arrested a group of his emirs. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.199}

## Irbil
He asked Salah al-Din for Irbil in exchange for giving up Harran and Edessa, and Salah al-Din granted it to him, and added to it Shahrazur and its districts, Darbandh Qarababili and Bani Qafjaq. Ibn al-Athir says that it remained a grudge in the throat of the Atabeg house. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.199}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
In his entry on [[place/irbil|Irbil]], [[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] says that the amir Muzaffar al-Din Kökböri, son of Zayn al-Din Ali Küçük, built up the town in the suburb of its citadel, with its walls, markets and covered bazaars, settled there so that it gained a market, and "stood up to the kings and defied them by his boldness and long experience" until they feared him, so that strangers came and it became a great city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.177} Yaqut then says, as his own judgement, that this amir's nature was "contradictory": in his view he was very unjust, harsh to his subjects and eager to take money by improper means, yet generous to the poor, giving much alms to strangers and sending great sums to ransom captives from non-Muslim hands; he quotes a satirical verse on this. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.177}
