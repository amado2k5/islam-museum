## Overview
Najm al-Din Abu al-Jannab al-Khaywaqi Ahmad ibn 'Umar ibn Muhammad, the Sufi muhaddith, shaykh of Khwarazm, called al-Kubra: he travelled the regions riding and on foot and met innumerable shaykhs, receiving the Sufi cloak of the Nahrjuris from Shaykh Isma'il al-Qasri and that of the Suhrawardis, as a blessing, from Shaykh Abu Nasr 'Ammar ibn Yasir; he outstripped his peers in youth in grasping knotty obscurities so they dubbed him 'the Great Calamity', later shortened to al-Kubra. {cite:shadharat-dhahab v.7 p.140}

He heard at Hamadhan from the hafiz Abu al-'Ala' and at Alexandria from al-Salafi, devoted himself to the Shafi'i school and tafsir — his tafsir runs to twelve volumes — and Imam Fakhr al-Din al-Razi met him and acknowledged his merit. He settled in Khwarazm and became its shaykh, a refuge for strangers of great standing who feared no blame in God's cause, and was martyred at Khwarazm in the Tatar onslaught. {cite:shadharat-dhahab v.7 p.140}
