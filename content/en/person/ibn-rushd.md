## Overview
Abu al-Walid Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Rushd al-Andalusi, a philosopher from [[place/cordoba|Córdoba]], whom the Europeans call "Averroes". {cite:al-aalam p.849}

## His work
Al-Zirikli says that he devoted himself to the words of Aristotle, translated them into Arabic and added to them, and wrote about fifty books. Among them are [[work/fasl-al-maqal|Fasl al-Maqal]] (The Decisive Treatise), and [[work/tahafut-al-tahafut|Tahafut al-Tahafut]] (The Incoherence of the Incoherence), a reply to [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]]; [[work/bidayat-al-mujtahid|Bidayat al-Mujtahid]] (The Beginning of the Mujtahid), on jurisprudence; and *al-Kulliyyat* (Generalities), on medicine. {cite:al-aalam p.849}

## Trial and death
The Almohad caliph al-Mansur honoured him and brought him near. Then his opponents turned the caliph against him, and he exiled him to [[place/marrakesh|Marrakesh]] and some of his books were burned. Later the caliph relented and allowed him to return, and he died in Marrakesh in 595 AH, corresponding to 1198 CE, and his body was taken to Córdoba. {cite:al-aalam p.849}

The source gives his birth as 1126 CE. These dates come from one source and need an additional source. {cite:al-aalam p.849}
