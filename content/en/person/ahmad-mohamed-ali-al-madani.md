## Summary
Ahmad Mohamed Ali al-Madani founded and led the Islamic Development Bank in Jeddah for forty years, building it from a treaty idea into the central institution of Sharia-compliant development finance across the Muslim world. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-mohamed-ali-al-madani} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-mohamed-ali-al-madani}

## Formation
Born in 1934 in Medina, he studied law and economics at Cairo University, took a master's in public administration in the United States and completed a doctorate in public administration at the State University of New York at Albany, then entered Saudi public service as deputy minister of education. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-mohamed-ali-al-madani}

## Career
When OIC member states founded the Islamic Development Bank in 1975, al-Madani became its first president and remained until 2016 — an unusually long tenure. Under him the IDB financed infrastructure, education and poverty-alleviation projects in dozens of member countries, pioneered Islamic financial instruments, and promoted the 'Vision 1440H' development strategy. He also held board roles with the Muslim World League, the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation and King Abdullah University of Science and Technology. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-mohamed-ali-al-madani} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-mohamed-ali-al-madani}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him under Philanthropy, Charity and Development as the man most associated with institutional Islamic giving — the banker who gave the ummah a multilateral development institution. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-mohamed-ali-al-madani}
