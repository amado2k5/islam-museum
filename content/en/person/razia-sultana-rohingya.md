## Summary
Razia Sultana is a Rohingya lawyer and rights defender who devoted her professional life to documenting what was done to the women of her people in Myanmar and carrying it to the world's highest platforms: the first Rohingya to address the UN Security Council, founder of the Rohingya Women Welfare Society (RWWS) in the Cox's Bazar camps, and recipient of the US State Department's 2019 International Women of Courage Award. {cite:wikipedia-razia-sultana-rohingya}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1973 in Maungdaw, Rakhine State, Myanmar, to a Rohingya family, and grew up in Bangladesh where her father had migrated for work; she studied education then law — a rare path for a Rohingya woman of her generation — and worked as a lawyer and teacher before the escalation of the crisis turned her into a full-time activist in 2017. {cite:wikipedia-razia-sultana-rohingya}

## Documentation and fieldwork
Since 2014 she has worked with Rohingya women and girls in the Bangladesh camps; after the 2017 wave she conducted hundreds of documented interviews with survivors and published two reference reports — Witness to Horror and Rape by Command — documenting what she describes as systematic sexual violence against Rohingya women, and contributed to the Kaladan Press report The Killing Fields of Alethankyaw. In January 2018 she founded the Rohingya Women Welfare Society (RWWS), running anti-trafficking awareness, psychosocial and mobile medical support in the camps and shelter for widows and orphans. {cite:wikipedia-razia-sultana-rohingya} {cite:muslim500-razia-sultana-rohingya}

## International platforms
In April 2018 she became the first Rohingya to address the UN Security Council, at its open debate on sexual violence in conflict, telling the chamber she had documented the rapes of more than three hundred women and girls and describing rape as systematically deployed as a weapon — her documented description, not the museum's verdict. She also served as coordinator of the Free Rohingya Coalition (FRC) and director of the women's section of the Arakan Rohingya National Organisation (ARNO). {cite:wikipedia-razia-sultana-rohingya}

## Recognition
She received the US State Department's International Women of Courage Award in 2019, appears in rankings of influential women in Asian human rights, and is presented in minority-rights literature as the first legal voice of the Rohingya inside UN institutions. {cite:wikipedia-razia-sultana-rohingya}

## Legacy
Sultana transformed from local lawyer into the international witness of a stateless people: she gathered survivors' testimonies into files cited by international courts and the United Nations, and made herself — a Bangladeshi citizen of Rohingya origin — the only female voice to carry the Rohingya case directly to the highest international platform. {cite:wikipedia-razia-sultana-rohingya}
