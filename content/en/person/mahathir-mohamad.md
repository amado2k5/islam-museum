## Overview
Mahathir bin Mohamad (born 10 July 1925) is a Malaysian doctor and politician who served twice as Prime Minister of Malaysia: from 1981 to 2003 and from 2018 to 2020. He is the country's longest-serving prime minister and the first Malaysian prime minister to reach the age of 100. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar}

In his first tenure he led a sweeping economic modernisation that made Malaysia one of Asia's rising economies; in 2018 he returned to power at 92, the world's oldest serving head of government. His positions on the West, on Islamic questions and on his domestic opponents have been widely debated in Malaysia and abroad. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

## Early life and education
Mahathir was born at his parents' home in Lorong Kilang Ais, Alor Setar, Kedah. His father, Mohamad Iskandar — from Penang, of Malay and Indian descent, his own father from Kerala, India — was principal of an English-medium secondary school; his mother, Wan Tempawan Wan Hanapi, was a Kedah Malay. He had six half-siblings and two full siblings, and was the first Malaysian prime minister not born into the aristocracy or a prominent religious or political family; his childhood home, a single shared bedroom without electricity, is now a tourist attraction. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

He attended the Seberang Perak Malay Boys School from 1930 and the selective Government English School from 1933, becoming fluent in English, editing the English student newspaper and winning language awards. During the Japanese occupation, with schools closed, he ran a small stall at Pekan Rabu market selling coffee and snacks such as pisang goreng; after the war he graduated with the highest rank, then studied medicine at King Edward VII College of Medicine in Singapore (University of Malaya), taking his MBBS in 1953. From 1947 he wrote in *The Sunday Times* under the pen name "Che Det". {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar}

## Medical career
Mahathir did his housemanship at Penang General Hospital, was posted to Alor Setar General Hospital in 1954 and then to government clinics in Langkawi — the island's first stationed doctor, in 1955 — Jitra and Perlis, his salary rising from MYR 400 to MYR 770. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

He opened the private "Maha Klinik", the first privately owned clinic established by a Malay in Malaysia, building a reputation as a caring doctor who made house calls at any hour, crossed rice fields in the dark, accepted payment in instalments, and presided over the Kedah Tuberculosis Association. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

On 5 August 1956 he married Siti Hasmah Mohamad Ali, a fellow medical student who served 25 years as a government doctor; they had seven children, among them Marina, Mokhzani and Mukhriz. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

## Early politics, expulsion and return
Mahathir was active in UMNO from the 1940s, protesting the Malayan Union's citizenship policies for non-Malays, and chaired Kedah UMNO by the 1959 election. Elected MP for Kota Setar Selatan in 1964, he clashed with Singapore's People's Action Party and Lee Kuan Yew; Singapore was expelled from Malaysia in his first year in parliament. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

He lost his seat in 1969 to Yusuf Rawa of PAS after the 13 May 1969 racial riots, in which hundreds were killed, then wrote an open letter criticising Prime Minister Tunku Abdul Rahman for failing Malay interests and calling for his resignation; he was expelled from UMNO's supreme council and the party at the end of 1969. In those years he wrote *The Malay Dilemma* (1970), arguing for a balance between government support for Malays and enough competition; the book was banned in Malaysia until 1981 — under his own premiership — and scholars R. S. Milne and Diane K. Mauzy credit his criticism as the main cause of Tunku's resignation in 1970. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

Prime Minister Abdul Razak Hussein encouraged his return: senator for Kedah in 1973, MP for Kubang Pasu unopposed in 1974, Minister of Education (1974–1978) — among his first acts, greater government control over universities — Minister of Trade and Industry (1978–1981), and Deputy Prime Minister from 1976. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

## First premiership (1981–2003)
Mahathir became prime minister on 16 July 1981 and led Malaysia for over 22 years: rapid growth and modernisation, industry-wide privatisation, major infrastructure including the North–South Expressway and Kuala Lumpur City Centre, five consecutive general-election victories, continued pro-bumiputera policies, and a prominent role in the Muslim world as a proponent of "Asian values". {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar}

During the 1997 Asian financial crisis he imposed capital controls and stimulus that diverged from IMF prescriptions; Malaysia recovered relatively swiftly, which his supporters cite as proof of his economic skill. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

He resigned on 31 October 2003 and remained an influential public voice, critical of his successors. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

## Second premiership (2018–2020)
Mahathir quit UMNO in 2016 over the 1MDB scandal, co-founded the party BERSATU (chairman 2016–2020), chaired the Pakatan Harapan coalition (2017–2020) and led it to victory in the 2018 election, becoming prime minister again on 10 May 2018 — at 92, the world's oldest serving prime minister, a Guinness record noted in the Arabic article. He pledged investigations into 1MDB, anti-corruption measures and cuts to mega-project spending, and secured the pardon and release of Anwar Ibrahim. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar}

He resigned on 24 February 2020 amid a political crisis, serving as interim until 1 March 2020, then founded the party PEJUANG (chairman 2020–2022); he was MP for Langkawi (2018–2022), lost his seat in the 2022 general election, formed the Gerakan Tanah Air coalition in 2022, and has been associated with PUTRA since 2023. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

*Time* named him to the TIME 100 in 2019, and he continues to write on his blog, "Chedet". {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar}

## Positions and criticism
This section presents differing views as reported in the sources; it is not the museum's verdict on them.

**Judging the first tenure:** In 1987 he ordered Operation Lalang, detaining numerous activists; the 1988 constitutional crisis raised concerns about judicial independence; and his 1998 dismissal of his deputy Anwar Ibrahim sparked the Reformasi movement. Critics accuse him of authoritarianism — centralising power and suppressing dissent — while supporters argue his rule brought stability and development. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} He also supported the amendment stripping the royalty of criminal immunity, widely regarded as strengthening the rule of law. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

**His stated outlook:** The English article records that his views were "shaped by his Malay nationalism and Islamic religious beliefs"; the Arabic article presents him as respected in developing and Muslim countries and describes his rejection of multiculturalism in favour of his assimilative "Bangsa Malaysia" (Malaysian nation) concept. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar} He has been accused of antisemitism over remarks about Jews, and has claimed American involvement in the 9/11 attacks and a global elite seeking world control; these are claims and accusations that the sources report by attribution, and the museum adopts neither. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

**His faith:** Wikidata alone records his school as Sunni Islam; the English article names no school and the Arabic article gives his religion as Islam only. Under the two-source rule this record therefore registers his religion without a school. {cite:wikidata-mahathir-mohamad} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar}

## Awards
Mahathir received the King Faisal Prize for Service to Islam in 1997, Pakistan's Nishan-e-Pakistan in 2019, and Japan's Grand Cordon of the Order of the Paulownia Flowers in 2018, among other state honours. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}

## Personal life
Mahathir was married to Siti Hasmah for seventy years, until her death on 28 September 2026; they had seven children. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-ar} He turned 100 in July 2025 — the second-oldest living former state leader in the world and the first Malaysian prime minister to reach that age. {cite:wikipedia-mahathir-mohamad-en}
