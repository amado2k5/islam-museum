## Overview
Mansur ibn 'Ammar ibn Kathir, the eloquent, devout preacher Abu al-Sari al-Sulami of Khurasan — Basran by some accounts — who had no equal in admonition and exhortation. He narrated from al-Layth, Ibn Lahi'a, Ma'ruf al-Khayyat, Miql ibn Ziyad, al-Munkadir ibn Muhammad and others. He died around 200 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.3948}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 455 in the ninth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] and describes him as a man who described God's favours and drove people towards Him. The notice opens with a dream related by [[person/ahmad-ibn-abi-al-hawari|Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari]]: Mansur was seen after his death and said that his Lord had forgiven him in spite of much confusion in his deeds, because he used to gather people to God's remembrance. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/325-326}

**His answer to Bishr al-Marisi.** Mansur relates that [[person/bishr-ibn-ghiyath-al-marisi|Bishr al-Marisi]] wrote asking him whether the Qur'an is created or uncreated. He answered that debating the Qur'an is an innovation shared by the one who asks and the one who answers; that God is the Creator and everything other than God is created; and that the Qur'an is the speech of God, uncreated. He advised Bishr to stop at the names God Himself gave His book and not to invent a name for it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/326}

**With Sufyan ibn Uyayna.** He visited [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Sufyan ibn Uyayna]]; Sufyan related hadith to him and Mansur preached to Sufyan. When Sufyan's eyes filled, he raised his head and held the tears back. Mansur asked why he did not let them fall, and Sufyan answered that a tear kept in the eye keeps grief longer in the heart. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/327}

**Sayings.** He said that the self is safe when it is opposed and afflicted when it is followed; that whoever loses patience over the misfortunes of this world finds his misfortune moved into his religion; and that the best garment of a servant is humility and brokenness. He has a rhymed passage on how wisdom speaks in the hearts of knowers, ascetics, worshippers, seekers and scholars, each with a different tongue. Told "you speak like this, yet we see certain things in you", he said: count me a pearl you found on a rubbish heap. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/327}

**The young man of Kufa.** He tells that he went out one night into a lane of [[place/kufa|Kufa]] and heard a young man weeping and confessing his sin to his Lord. Mansur recited a verse that mentions the Fire; he heard a commotion, and then the voice fell silent. Returning the next day he found a funeral. The young man's mother, who did not know who he was, told him that a man had passed her son at prayer and recited a verse, and he died of fear. Abu Nu'aym gives the story by two close routes; in the second Mansur comments that this is what those who fear are like. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/328-329}

**Transmission.** Abu Nu'aym ends with hadiths that Mansur transmitted, related from him by his son Sulayman and others. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/329-331}
