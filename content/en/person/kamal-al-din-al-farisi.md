## Summary

Kamal al-Din Abu'l Hasan Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Farisi worked in optics and number theory. MacTutor explicitly identifies him as an Islamic mathematician, with birth about1260 and death about1320 in [[place/tabriz|Tabriz]]. These remain approximations, without an invented Hijri equivalent. Individual Muslim identity is documented; the separately declared Sunni museum frame is not a new personal denominational certificate {cite:farisi-mactutor summary-and-biography}.

## Study and revision

He studied with [[person/qutb-al-din-shirazi|Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi]]. Russell places his arrival in Tabriz possibly before1290. Questions about refraction prompted consultation; the teacher obtained a book by [[person/ibn-al-haytham|Ibn al-Haytham]] from a distant country. This is not a complete itinerary, nor evidence that al-Farisi visited every place from which a book arrived {cite:russell-faresi-iranica biography}.

The [[work/tanqih-al-manazir-farisi|Tanqih al-Manazir]] went beyond elementary commentary. It distinguished the predecessor's statements from the reviser's and could dispute a conclusion. Russell reports competing completion dates: before1290, or after1302 and before1311. These limits are retained rather than replaced by an exact date from an unread summary {cite:russell-faresi-iranica structure-and-dating}.

## Optics and limits

His [[invention/farisi-water-sphere-rainbow|water-sphere rainbow model]] represents a drop with a transparent water-filled vessel. Two refractions surround one or two internal reflections. The approximation involving glass remains explicit, as does the failure of his physical colour explanation. Russell also discusses shadows, eclipses, burning spheres, eye anatomy and reported experiments on a ram's eye. These are biographical interests, not instructions to reproduce those procedures {cite:russell-faresi-iranica optics} {cite:farisi-mactutor rainbow-and-colours}.

**Sunni — declared frame:** the documented Islamic identity is separate from priority attribution. **Academic account:** MacTutor reports Boyer's preference for the teacher's discovery and pupil's elaboration, and Rashed's objection. No final verdict is imposed. Russell finds no evidence of a Latin translation, while discussing the comparison with Theodoric {cite:farisi-mactutor attribution} {cite:russell-faresi-iranica reception}.

## Arithmetic and number theory

MacTutor describes a new proof of [[person/thabit-ibn-qurra|Thabit ibn Qurra]]'s amicable-number theorem in the Tadhkira al-ahbab fi bayan al-tahabb. It reports the pairs220/284 and17296/18416. Hogendijk argues that Thabit knew the latter pair earlier, so this is not an unqualified first-discovery claim for al-Farisi, nor an exclusive attribution to Euler because that name later became attached to the pair {cite:farisi-mactutor amicable-numbers}.

The account includes establishing the primality of1151 with lemmas including an application of Eratosthenes' sieve, and an approach involving factorisation and combinatorial ideas. Whether he proved, or attempted to prove, unique factorisation is a scholarly question reported through Rashed and another study, not something settled here by inspecting his original argument {cite:farisi-mactutor factorisation}.

MacTutor also describes connections between polygonal numbers and binomial coefficients, with early induction-style arguments linking repeated triangular-number sums to combinations. It records his assertion that a sum of two positive integer fourth powers cannot be a fourth power, without a proof. That does not become a proof of Fermat's general theorem, and every biographical observation is not automatically a separate invention {cite:farisi-mactutor number-theory}.

## Factorization and amicability

MacTutor describes the [[work/farisi-tadhkirat-al-ahbab|Memorandum for friends]] and its [[invention/farisi-amicable-factorization|factorization/combinatorial proof approach]] to Thabit ibn Qurra's amicable-number rule, with220/284 and17296/18416. It attributes the unique-factorization interpretation to Rashed and notes disagreement about proof completeness. Neither absolute proof priority nor first discovery of the larger pair is awarded {cite:farisi-mactutor number-theory}.

Bozhuyuk's TDV entry differs from the earlier name and approximate dates: Hasan ibn Ali ibn Hasan,1267 and1319, from a reported student's colophon.
