## Overview
Diya' al-Din Abdallah ibn Ahmad al-Malaqi al-Shashshab, known as Ibn al-Baytar, author of *al-Mufradat fi al-Adwiya* (On Simple Medicines). Ibn al-Imad in *Shadharat al-Dhahab* says that knowledge of plants, their properties, their uses and their habitats ended with him, and that he entered the service of al-Kamil and then of his son al-Salih, and was a chief in the lands of Egypt. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 234}

He died in Damascus in Sha'ban 646 AH, according to the same source. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 234}

For another biography of a scholar of this name, see [[person/ibn-al-bitar-abu-muhammad-abd-allah-ibn-ahmad|Ibn al-Bitar Abu Muhammad Abdallah ibn Ahmad]]. It needs an additional source to confirm whether the two are one person.

## Education, journeys and service

Mahmut Kaya describes Ibn al-Baytar's religious and transmitted-science education before botany and connects his epithet with his father's veterinary work. Several birth years are proposed; none is adopted here. He learned Andalusian plants, names and habitats with Abu al-Abbas al-Nabati, also called Ibn al-Rumiyya, then worked in Seville's environment and read Dioscorides and Galen. His own documented education supplies the Muslim basis, not his name or birthplace. The museum's default Sunni frame is separate and does not establish a personal legal school {cite:tdv-ibn-baytar-kaya education}.

Kaya describes North African and Anatolian travel, chief-herbalist service under al-Kamil and continued position under al-Salih. Some eastern itineraries remain Leclerc's inference from plant names, not an independently read diary. Ibn Abi Usaybia studied with him in Damascus in 633 AH; Ibn al-Suwaydi was another pupil. He died in Damascus. The older account's Sha'ban 646 date above is preserved, not newly checked against an unread source {cite:tdv-ibn-baytar-kaya travel teaching and death}.
