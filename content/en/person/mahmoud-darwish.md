## Summary

Mahmoud Darwish (1941–2008) was Palestine's greatest poet, born in the village of al-Birwa in the Galilee; his family was displaced in 1948 and his biography became his people's: exile, identity and land. He is counted among the greatest contemporary Arab poets.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-darwish}

## Life

After leaving Israel he lived in Lebanon, Egypt, Tunisia and France, headed the Palestinian Writers and Journalists Union, and documented Beirut 1982 in Memory for Forgetfulness. He died in Houston, USA, after heart surgery.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-darwish}

## Poetry and prose

His collections include Leaves of Olives (1964), Praise of the High Shadow, It's a Song, Like Almond Blossoms or Further and Mural (Jidariyya, 2000), the anthem of surviving death; his prose In the Presence of Absence (2006) is a self-elegy. He wrote the PLO's 1988 declaration of independence.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-darwish}

## Standing and debate

His poems were read worldwide, translated and set to music; his stance on the Oslo accords — which he resigned over — is debated. Palestinian identity in modern Arabic poetry is credited largely to his lyric voice.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-darwish}
