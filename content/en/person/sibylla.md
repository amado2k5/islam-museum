## Overview
Sibylla was a queen of Jerusalem in the Frankish kingdom and the wife of [[person/guy-de-lusignan|Guy de Lusignan]]. The Encyclopaedia of Islam was searched and has no passage on her, and Ibn al-Athir, the only source used, never names her: he speaks of the "queen" and of Guy as "Kay". The museum identifies his queen with Sibylla because she is the wife through whom Guy held the kingdom. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141}

## The heir's mother
Under the year 582/1186 Ibn al-Athir tells that the Frankish king of Syria, a leper, left the kingdom to a young nephew. When the boy died, the kingdom passed to his mother. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141} Since the chronicler calls the boy the king's nephew through a sister, the passage implies that the queen was the leper king's sister, which fits [[person/baldwin-iv|Baldwin IV]]; he does not say so. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141}

## Marriage to Guy
The chronicle says the queen fell in love with a man among the Franks who had lately come to Syria, called Kay, and married him. She transferred the kingdom to him and had the crown placed on his head. She summoned the patriarch, priests, monks, the Hospitallers, the Templars and the barons, told them she had given the kingdom back to him, and had them bear witness. They obeyed him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141} Ibn al-Athir counts the anger of [[person/raymond-iii-tripoli|Raymond of Tripoli]] at this arrangement among the causes of the Frankish split that helped Saladin. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 142} See [[event/frank-split-raymond-582|the Frankish schism]].

## After Hattin
Ibn al-Athir records that after the surrender of Jerusalem [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Saladin]] released the queen of Jerusalem, whose husband, taken at [[event/battle-of-hattin-583|Hattin]], had held the Frankish kingdom through her and in her name. Saladin returned her property and retinue and allowed her to join her husband, who was then held in the castle of Nablus. She went to him and stayed with him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 157}

## What is not known
The sources read give no birth or death date, no parentage, and no name for her. Nothing is known here of her life before the events above or after the release at Jerusalem. The museum has not read William of Tyre or other Frankish sources and does not supply details from them.
