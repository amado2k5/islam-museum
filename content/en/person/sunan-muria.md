## Summary
Sunan Muria, born Raden Umar Said, is according to the Babad Tanah Jawi manuscripts one of the nine Wali Songo who propagated Islam in Indonesia. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-en} A son of Sunan Kalijaga, he made his seat in the Kudus region beneath the mountain that bears his name, Mount Muria, and was buried at Colo on its slope. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-id} No source gives his birth year; the Indonesian tradition dates his death to 1560, the English article to 1551. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-id} {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-en}

## Among the common people
A big distinction of Sunan Muria, apart from the other sunans, was his very close relationship with the common people: he spread his teaching in rural and remote areas, and taught local people agriculture, fishery and other everyday livelihoods. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-en} The Indonesian article adds his dakwah through culture — wayang performances using his father's composed plays, tembang songs, and old traditions and religious practices Islamised — through which his preaching developed in Jepara, Tayu, Juwana and the country around Kudus, and it credits him in the tembang tradition with creating the short metres Sinom and Kinanthi. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-id}

## Family and teachers
The Indonesian article names his father as Sunan Kalijaga (Raden Sahid) and his mother as Dewi Saroh, and lists his teachers as his father together with Sunan Ngudung and Sunan Ngerang — the last also his father-in-law through his daughter Dewi Roroyono, whose abduction by the Mandalika chief Pathak Warak and rescue by the young Umar Said the tradition tells at length as a folk tale. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-id} The Arabic article likewise makes him a son of Sunan Kalijaga, of Majapahit princely descent, and places his death late in the sixteenth century at Colo in Kudus. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-ar} His tomb complex stands at Colo village, Dawe district, Kudus regency, over 1,600 metres up the mountain slope. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-id}

## Religion
The Indonesian article's infobox gives his denomination as Sunni, with the jurisprudence field blank; the Arabic article gives his creed as Sunni and his madhhab as Shafi'i, within the same Arabic wali tradition as the Bonang and Giri articles; Wikidata's religion value is Islam without qualification. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-id} {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-ar} {cite:wikidata-sunan-muria} No second independent source names a legal school for him, so this record preserves the Shafi'i attestation without adopting it; his documented work is that of a saint and teacher among the villages of the Muria region. {cite:wikipedia-sunan-muria-en}
