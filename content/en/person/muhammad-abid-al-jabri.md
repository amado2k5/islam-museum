## Summary

Mohamed Abed al-Jabri (1935–2010) was a Moroccan philosopher, author of the most famous project in contemporary Arab philosophy: an analysis of the structure of Arab reason across Islamic intellectual history in the four-volume 'Critique of Arab Reason'.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-abid-al-jabri}

## Life

Born in Figuig, Morocco, he studied philosophy at Mohammed V University and in Paris, taught philosophy and Islamic thought in Rabat for decades, and helped found the Arab Center for Research and Policy Studies in Doha.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-abid-al-jabri}

## Project

He distinguished three epistemic instruments in Arab reason — burhan (demonstration), bayan (linguistic explication) and 'irfan (gnosis) — analysing their structure in Bunyat al-'Aql al-'Arabi (1984), the categories of state, tribe, spoils and creed in Al-'Aql al-Siyasi al-'Arabi (1990), and the inherited ethical system in Al-'Aql al-Akhlaqi al-'Arabi (2001).{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-abid-al-jabri}

## Controversy and standing

He engaged in famous philosophical debates with Hasan Hanafi and George Tarabishi. He is honoured as author of the greatest contemporary Arab philosophical project and criticised for a Maghreb-centric reading and his methodological tools — the museum records both.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-abid-al-jabri}
