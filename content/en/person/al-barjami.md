## Summary
al-Barjami, chief of the ʿayyārūn (urban gangs) of [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]] in the years when the Buyid state was declining, to the point that he was almost given the khutba in the Rusafa mosque. He ended up drowned in the Tigris at the hands of the companions of Muʿtamid al-Dawla in 425 AH, one of the clearest scenes of the age of the ʿayyār that Ibn al-Jawzi portrays. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.414-422}

## Raids of the ʿayyārs
He raided the houses of Abu al-Rabiʿ and plundered their storehouses, and he made a stronghold for himself in a thicket extending five farsakhs, where al-Tall lay. It was said to the preacher: "If you do not give the khutba for al-Barjami, then do not give it for any caliph or king." The amirs received him with gifts. He took on the revenue contract of the upper Mosul army and had two boats and an appointment. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.415-418}

## The end
He plundered the Khan al-Qawarir at Bab al-Taq, until the companions of Muʿtamid al-Dawla drowned him at the mouth of the Dujayl on the sixteenth of Ramadan 425 AH, when he refused to vacate it for money. His brother was then killed in revenge for him. Ibn al-Jawzi relates his news on pages that could serve as the heading of an age in which the ʿayyār grew stronger than authority. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.419-422}
