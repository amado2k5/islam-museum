## Summary
Iraqi poet and educationist (1875–1945), a luminary of modern Arabic poetry. He studied in Baghdad under Mahmud Shukri al-Alusi, worked as a teacher and member of the Ottoman Chamber of Deputies, and was known for socially committed verse defending the poor, women's rights and education; he left the Diwan al-Rusafi and lectures on Arabic literature. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-al-rusafi}

## Life and career

Ma'ruf ibn Abd al-Ghani al-Rusafi was born in Baghdad in 1875 in the Rusafa quarter that gave him his nisba; he learned in its schools and in the circles of Mahmud Shukri al-Alusi, who oversaw his education and literary formation. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-al-rusafi}

He taught Arabic in Baghdad, Jerusalem and Istanbul, served as a deputy for Baghdad in the Ottoman Chamber of Deputies, then returned to teaching and translation in monarchical Iraq. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-al-rusafi}

## Poetry and works

His poetry was gathered in the Diwan al-Rusafi, marked by powerful language and courageous positions: poems on poverty, orphanhood, education, women's emancipation and social criticism, among them his address to the Muslim world and his poem on the orphan. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-al-rusafi}

His other works include literary lectures delivered at colleges, Al-Shakhsiyya al-Muhammadiyya on the Prophet's biography, a treatise on education, translations from Turkish and books on language. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-al-rusafi}

## Legacy

Al-Rusafi stands with al-Jawahiri and al-Zahawi in the trinity of modern Iraqi poetry, and was called 'the poet of society' for defending the poor classes and calling for social reform in his country. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-al-rusafi}

He died in Baghdad in 1945 after a tragic fall from a bridge; his poems are still read and taught in Iraq and the Arab world, and his statue stands in Baghdad. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-al-rusafi}
