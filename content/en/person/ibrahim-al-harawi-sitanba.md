## Summary
Abu Ishaq Ibrahim al-Harawi, known as Sitanba, was an ascetic of Herat. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 460 of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]]. He records that he kept company with [[person/ibn-adham-ibrahim|Ibrahim ibn Adham]], that he was a peer of [[person/bayazid-al-bistami|Abu Yazid al-Bistami]], that he was noted for trust in God and detachment, and that he died in [[place/qazvin|Qazvin]]. He is also one of the transmitters of Abu Yazid's sayings in the same book. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/43} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/34}

## His prayer on the pilgrimage
The people of [[place/herat|Herat]] held him in honour. The notice tells that he made the pilgrimage with no provisions, and that on that pilgrimage he prayed that God would cut his sustenance off from the wealth of the people of Herat and make them lose interest in him. After that he would go many days without eating anything, while people in the market of Herat said, as he passed, that he spent so many dirhams every day and night. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/43}

## The man in the desert
[[person/al-sulami|Al-Sulami]] relates, with his chain from Ibrahim ibn Shayban, that Ibrahim al-Harawi was in the desert and had gone some time without eating, drinking or wanting anything, and his self suggested to him that he had a rank with God. A man on his right then told him that he had been lying there, chronically ill, for eighty days without food or drink, and that he was ashamed before God to have such a thought. Ibrahim said that seeing him was a warning and brought him back to his earlier state. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/43}

## His counsels, from his son
His son Muhammad relates from him that whoever wants his prayer not to be veiled should watch five things in himself: that his food, his clothing, his sleep and his speech go no further than what he cannot do without, and that he be humble and guard his limbs. The road to Paradise rests on three things: a heart at rest in God's promise, contentment with His decree, and sincerity in works. Whoever wants every honour should choose seven things over seven: poverty over wealth, hunger over fullness, the low place over the high, abasement over rank, humility over pride, sorrow over joy, and death over life. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/43-44}
