# Berdi Kerbabayev

Berdi Kerbabayev (Turkmen: Berdi Myradowiç Kerbabaýew; 3 March 1894 – 3 March 1974) was a Turkmen Soviet writer, regarded as a founder of Turkmen Soviet literature. He was born at the village of Kowki-Zeren in the Tejen District of Transcaspia, in the Russian Empire, to a farmer's family; he studied at a village school (mekdep) until 1917 and then at a madrasa in Bukhara.{cite:wikipedia-berdi-kerbabayev-en}

During the civil war Kerbabayev served in a detachment of the Basmachi movement under Eziz-khan Chapyk and for a time worked at the headquarters of the Transcaspian front; in May 1919 he left the movement and joined the Bolsheviks. From 1924 to 1934 he edited the newspaper Turkmenistan and the magazine Tokmak, studied at the Leningrad Oriental Institute in 1927–1928, and headed the administration of science of the Turkmen SSR in 1934–1936; he became a member of the Communist Party in 1948.{cite:wikipedia-berdi-kerbabayev-en}

Kerbabayev's novel The Decisive Step, whose first part appeared in 1940 and whose complete version appeared in 1947, won the USSR State Prize in 1948; his later novels include Nebit Dag (1957). He was named a People's Writer of the Turkmen SSR and elected an academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Turkmen SSR. He died at Ashkhabad on 3 March 1974, his eightieth birthday.{cite:wikipedia-berdi-kerbabayev-en}
