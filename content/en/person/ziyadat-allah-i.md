## Overview
Ziyadat Allah ibn Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab ibn Salim, Abu Muhammad, was the fourth of the Aghlabids, the lords of Ifriqiya. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 172 AH, 788 CE, and his death as 223 AH, 838 CE, and says he died in Qayrawan {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 55}.

## Career
He took power after the death of his brother Abd Allah in 201 AH and received his diploma of investiture from the Abbasid al-Ma'mun; he kept up the invocation for al-Ma'mun during the days when Ibrahim ibn al-Mahdi seized the caliphate, and when the caliphate became al-Ma'mun's alone, the caliph thanked him for it {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 55}. The country then fell into disorder, revolts multiplied and his authority weakened, until in 209 AH only Gabes, the coast, Tripoli and the Nafzawa tribes of Ifriqiya remained loyal to him; his position then strengthened again and the Nafzawa came to his aid {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 55}. In 212 AH he fitted out a great fleet and sent it to Sicily, and it took most of the island's fortresses {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 55}.

## Character and works
Al-Zirikli describes him as eloquent and cultured, speaking correct Arabic without affectation {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 55}. He built the wall of Sousse, and he was the first of the Aghlabid governors to bear the name Ziyadat Allah {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 55}.
