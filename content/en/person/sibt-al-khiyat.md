## Overview
Sibt al-Khiyat, Abu Muhammad 'Abdallah b. 'Ali of Baghdad — Qur'an reader, Hanbali jurist and grammarian, shaykh of Iraq's readers and a prolific author; born 464, he read the Qur'an under his grandfather the ascetic Abu Mansur, excelled in Arabic under Ibn Fakhir, led prayer at the Harida mosque some fifty years, and was among the sweetest-voiced reciters of the Qur'an. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 609-610}

## His contemporaries' praise
Ibn al-Jawzi said: "I never heard a reader of sweeter voice or finer delivery, despite his age"; Ibn Shafi' said he was the shaykh of Iraq — a resort of religion, trustworthiness and integrity — and "the beauty of all Iraq." He died Monday morning, 12 Rabi' II; his coffin was lowered by ropes from his mosque's roof before the largest gathering Ibn al-Jawzi had seen, and he was buried at Imam Ahmad's dakka beside his grandfather. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 609-610}
