## Summary
Ibn al-Jamizi — Baha' al-Din 'Ali ibn Hibatallah al-Lakhmi of Egypt, Shafi'i, the musnid of the Egyptian lands — died in 649 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 257}.

## Details
Baha' al-Din Abu al-Hasan 'Ali ibn Hibatallah ibn Salama ibn Muslim al-Lakhmi al-Misri was the musnid, khatib and mudarris of Egypt. Born in Egypt on the day of al-Adha, 559, he memorized the Qur'an in 569; his father took him travelling so that he heard from Ibn 'Asakir at Damascus and from Shahda and others at Baghdad {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 257}. He read the readings under Abu al-Hasan al-Batayihi — the last person in the world to recite or transmit from him — and studied al-Muhadhdhab under the qadi Abu Sa'd ibn Abi 'Asrun, whose last living pupil he likewise was {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 257}. Peerless in his time, he taught and issued fatwas, students journeyed to him, and the headship of learning in Egypt devolved upon him: chief of the scholars of his day, honoured by elite and commoner alike, the pivot of fatwa in his country. Innumerable people transmitted from him; he died on 24 Dhu'l-Hijja {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 257}.
