## Summary
Al-Sayyida bint al-Mansur ibn Yusuf al-Sanhaji, known as Umm Malal, is described by [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']] as a woman of influence, authority, intelligence and judgement; she was born at the palace of al-Mansuriyya at Sabra, a mile from [[place/qayrawan|Kairouan]], and grew up in the care of her father [[person/al-mansur-ibn-buluggin|al-Mansur]], ruler of Ifriqiya, and her brother [[person/badis-ibn-al-mansur|Badis]], his heir, receiving a fine education in letters and learning. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.280}

## Her brother's partner
Kahhala says she surpassed her brother Nasir al-Dawla Badis, who made her a partner in government and took her advice on state policy, and that his preoccupation with putting down revolts across the realm allowed her to share in policy and in relations with neighbouring states. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.280}

## Regent for al-Mu'izz
In 406 AH Badis set out against rebels in the central Maghrib and died at al-Masila; the great men of the state concealed his death and agreed to proclaim his son [[person/al-muizz-ibn-badis|al-Mu'izz]], not yet nine, who took the throne in Dhu al-Hijja 406, and they made his aunt Umm Malal his guardian until he came of age. She governed the realm, rode out in procession among banners to celebrate his accession, and continued until he was ready to take power, al-Mu'izz acknowledging what he owed her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.280} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.281}

## Her death and funeral
She fell gravely ill and died in Rajab 414 AH; al-Mu'izz prayed over her. Kahhala relates from Abu al-Qasim al-Raqiq a coffin of Indian aloeswood set with jewels and gold plates, says she was carried to [[place/al-mahdiyya|al-Mahdiyya]] and buried there, that al-Mu'izz distributed ten thousand dinars among needy women at her mourning, and that more than a hundred poets lamented her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.281} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.282} He cites Hasan Husni Abd al-Wahhab that, on closer study, she was first buried at al-Mahdiyya and then moved to [[place/monastir|Monastir]], to the cemetery of the Sanhaja princes known after her as "the Sayyida's cemetery". {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.282}

## For young visitors
Umm Malal was a princess of old Tunisia who helped her brother rule the country, and when he died she looked after her young nephew until he grew up and became ruler.
