## Summary
Jeffrey Lang (born 30 January 1954) is an American mathematician and writer. Raised a Catholic at Bridgeport, Connecticut, and an atheist by eighteen, he embraced Islam in 1982, aged 28. He teaches mathematics at the University of Kansas and has written books on his path to Islam, among them *Even Angels Ask: A Journey to Islam in America*. {cite:islamreligion-jeffrey-lang-2006 profile} {cite:meq-pipes-review-lang-1998 review} {cite:wikidata-jeffrey-lang Wikidata}

## Background
He was born at Bridgeport into a Roman Catholic family and spent his first eighteen years in Catholic schools, which, the IslamReligion.com profile says, left him with many unanswered questions about God and Christianity. In the late 1960s and early 1970s he rebelled against social and religious institutions and was an atheist by eighteen. He took his master's and doctoral degrees at Purdue University and worked first as a young lecturer in mathematics before joining the University of Kansas, where he is a professor. {cite:islamreligion-jeffrey-lang-2006 profile} {cite:islamweb-jeffrey-lang-2008 piece} {cite:wikidata-jeffrey-lang Wikidata}

## The path to Islam
According to the IslamReligion.com profile, he began to reconsider his views through conversations with Muslim colleagues at the university, who answered his questions with care. A Saudi student, Mahmoud Qandeel, gave him a copy of the Qur'an and books on Islam. He read the Qur'an on his own, visited the students' prayer hall and accepted Islam, in the profile's words, with little struggle. He says that the mathematician's habit of valuing reasoned argument made Islam's appeal to reason important to him. Daniel Pipes's review dates the step to 1982, at the age of 28, and records that Lang felt alienated from American life, citing its "unrelenting greed and neglect", and came to Islam through close friendships. {cite:islamreligion-jeffrey-lang-2006 profile} {cite:islamweb-jeffrey-lang-2008 piece} {cite:meq-pipes-review-lang-1998 review}

## After embracing Islam
He is a faculty adviser to the Muslim Student Association at the University of Kansas, acting as a link between students and the university and arranging lectures. He married Raika, a Saudi Muslim woman, about twelve years before the profile was written. His best-known book is [[work/even-angels-ask|*Even Angels Ask: A Journey to Islam in America*]], which Pipes's review describes as an examination of the reliability of the hadith and the status of women, with the argument that Islam's distinctive strength is its rationality; the profile says his books are bestsellers among Muslims in the United States. {cite:islamreligion-jeffrey-lang-2006 profile} {cite:meq-pipes-review-lang-1998 review}

## Difficulties and reactions
The IslamReligion.com profile does not discuss the reaction of his family or any hardship. Pipes's review says his Muslim friends received him warmly, and that the stage after the conversion was harder than the conversion itself. {cite:islamreligion-jeffrey-lang-2006 profile} {cite:meq-pipes-review-lang-1998 review}

## Criticism and debate
No critical judgement of Lang appears in the sources read; Pipes's review gives no verdict in its own voice. The points of self-criticism it reports are Lang's own: he concedes that converts can be among the most rigid and least tolerant Muslims, and suggests that converts, without a traditional grounding, may bring ideas that seem alien and could renew Islam and appeal to Western audiences. {cite:meq-pipes-review-lang-1998 review}
