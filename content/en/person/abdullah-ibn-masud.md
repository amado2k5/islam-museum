## Brief
Abdullah ibn Mas'ud was a Meccan shepherd among the first half-dozen Muslims — the first man, tradition says, to recite the Qur'an aloud in public, and later the founding teacher of Kufa.

## Life
A slight-framed herdsman of the tribe of Hudhayl who pastured the flocks of Mecca's great men, Ibn Mas'ud embraced Islam among the very earliest converts, about 613 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. When the small community asked who would dare recite the Qur'an openly before Quraysh, tradition tells that he volunteered — and was beaten for it, and did it anyway {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He migrated twice — to Abyssinia, then to Medina — and fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. The Prophet is remembered saying that whoever wanted to hear the Qur'an "fresh as it came down" should hear it from Ibn Mas'ud. Under Umar he was sent to Kufa as its teacher in scripture and law, where his readings and judgments seeded the city's school {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He died in Medina about 653.

## Legacy
Kufa became the great early center of Qur'an study and law — and its founding figure is the shepherd who learned by listening. His personal reading of the Qur'anic text was much discussed when Uthman standardized the recension.

## For young visitors
Ibn Mas'ud was a poor shepherd — but he learned the whole Qur'an by heart directly from the Prophet, and became the teacher of a whole city.
