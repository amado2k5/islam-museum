## Summary
Abdullah ibn Mas'ud was a Meccan shepherd and one of the first six Muslims. According to tradition, he was the first to recite the Qur'an aloud before the people, and afterwards he was the first teacher of Kufa.

## Life
A slightly built man of the Hudhayl tribe, he herded the sheep of Mecca's nobles. He embraced Islam among the earliest Muslims, around 613 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. When the small community asked who would recite the Qur'an openly before Quraysh, the tradition relates that he volunteered. He was beaten for it, and went on his way {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He emigrated twice, to Abyssinia and then to Medina, and fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. It is narrated from the Prophet that whoever wishes to hear the Qur'an fresh should hear it from Ibn Mas'ud. Umar sent him to Kufa to teach the Book and rulings, and from his readings and judgments the city's school grew {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He died in Medina around 653 CE.

## Legacy
Kufa became the capital of early Qur'anic learning and jurisprudence, and its founding teacher was a shepherd who learned by hearing. His own reading was much discussed when Uthman unified the codex.

## For young visitors
Ibn Mas'ud was a poor shepherd. Yet he memorised the whole Qur'an from the Prophet's mouth, and became the teacher of an entire city.

## In the secret call (al-Raheeq al-Makhtum p.76)
The book counts him among those who followed the first vanguard among the earliest Muslims in the [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|secret call]]. Ibn Hisham counted more than forty men from all the clans of Quraysh among them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. The book calls him Abdullah ibn Mas'ud al-Hudhali {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## The killing of Abu Jahl (al-Raheeq al-Makhtum p.221)
He found Abu Jahl with a last breath left in him. He cut off his head and brought it to the Prophet ([[event/death-of-abu-jahl|the killing of Abu Jahl]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.221}.
