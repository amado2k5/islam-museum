## Overview
Jamal al-Din Ibn Nubata al-Misri, a well-known man of letters and a writer of chancery documents. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.403-404}

## From his chancery writing
Al-Maqqari quotes from his chancery writing a dispatch for the Marinid sultan Abu al-Hasan, in the three copies of the Qur'an endowed to three mosques. He also described the sultan as the one who extended his right hand with the sword and the pen. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.403-404}

His birth, death and other works need an additional source.

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Muhammad ibn Muhammad Ibn Nubata, of a family from Mayyafariqin, of Egypt, Abu Bakr: born in the Lane of Lamps in Cairo in Rabi' al-Awwal 676, he moved to Syria in 716 and lived in Damascus for nearly fifty years. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/216} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/217} Ibn Hajar quotes al-Dhahabi calling him a master of graceful verse. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/217} Al-Nasir Hasan summoned him to Cairo in 761, and he died on 7 Safar 768 in the hospital. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/218}
