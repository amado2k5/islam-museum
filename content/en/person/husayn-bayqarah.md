## Overview
Husayn Mirza Bayqara was the last of the well-known Timurid rulers of Khurasan. He was born at Herat in Muharram 842/June 1438 and died in 911/1506. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635} He ascended the throne at Herat on 10 Ramadan 873/25 March 1469 and, with one brief intermission, remained undisputed ruler of Khurasan until his death. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635}

## The road to power
At fourteen he entered the service of Abu l-Qasim Babur; after Babur made peace with Abu Said he served the latter but was imprisoned, released through his mother's intervention, and returned to Babur until his death in 861/1457. He then joined Muizz al-Din Sanjar, ruler of Marw, and married his daughter; their eldest son was Badi al-Zaman. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635} After the death of Abu Said he went to Herat and took the throne. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635}

## Rule and culture
The article's author judges his long reign more important culturally than politically: relatively peaceful conditions and prosperity returned to Khurasan, and Herat became a great cultural centre. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635} He and his close friend the Turkish poet Nawai patronised poets and scholars; Jami, last great classical Persian poet, the historian Mirkhwand, the miniature painter Bihzad and the calligrapher Sultan Ali Mashhadi were among the court's famous figures. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635} At the start of his reign he showed Shii inclinations, which he abandoned under Nawai's and others' influence; he was also a soldier who fought personally in many battles. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635}

## Poetry
He wrote poetry in Turkish and Persian under the pen-name Husayni, and his Turkish diwan has ghazals composed throughout in one variant of the ramal metre. The article judges the verse of no more than average quality despite Nawai's praise, attributing the translation of a selection into Persian by the Safavid Sultan Husayn to the poet's standing. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635} Sam Mirza ascribes the Majalis al-ushshaq to him, but Babur and Khwandamir state that it is by Kamal al-Din Husayn. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635}
