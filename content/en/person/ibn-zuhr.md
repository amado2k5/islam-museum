Abu Marwan Abd al-Malik ibn Zuhr, known as Ibn Zuhr and in Latin as Avenzoar, was a physician and poet of Seville from the Banu Zuhr of the Arab tribe of Iyad, a family that from the early tenth century produced six consecutive generations of physicians, jurists, poets, viziers and midwives.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en} The English article gives his birth at Seville in 1094, while the Arabic article, following the Arabic biographical tradition, gives 465 AH/1072 CE and Wikidata carries further conflicting values; the conflict is preserved in his record rather than hidden.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-ar}{cite:wikidata-ibn-zuhr} He began with religion and literature, as was the custom, then studied medicine with his father Abu al-Ala from an early age; by his own account his father introduced him to Galen and Hippocrates and had him swear the Hippocratic oath while still a youth.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en}

He began his career as a court physician of the Almoravids, then fell from the favour of the ruler Ali ibn Yusuf ibn Tashufin and fled Seville, only to be apprehended and jailed at Marrakesh in 1140 — the Arabic article puts his imprisonment at about ten years — an experience that left lasting resentment in his writings.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en}{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-ar} When the Almohads conquered Seville in 1147 he returned and devoted himself to medical practice, serving the Almohad caliphs as physician and vizier, and he was a friend of Ibn Rushd, at whose request he wrote al-Taysir as a practical companion to the Colliget.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-ar}{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en}

His major work, Kitab al-Taysir ("Book of Simplification Concerning Therapeutics and Diet"), runs to thirty chapters from the diseases of the head downward and contains accurate descriptions of oesophageal, stomach and mediastinal cancers, of pericarditis and otitis media, and the proposal of feeding enemas to keep stomach-cancer patients alive.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en} His earlier Kitab al-Iqtisad treats general therapy, and the Kitab al-Aghdhiya, written after his release for the Almohad leader Abd al-Mu'min, classifies foods and regimens by season.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en} His works were translated into Latin and Hebrew and printed together with Averroes' Colliget as a single book, remaining in use as late as the eighteenth century.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en}

His greatest contribution was method: he applied the experimental method to medicine, testing procedures on animals before humans — most famously incising a goat's trachea, washing the wound with water and honey and observing the animal recover and live long, to settle the controversy over performing a tracheotomy on a human patient.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en} He is also thought to have made the earliest description of bezoar stones as medicines.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-en} He died at Seville in 557 AH/1162 CE, on which both articles and Wikidata agree.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-zuhr-ar}{cite:wikidata-ibn-zuhr}
