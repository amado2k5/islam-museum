## Overview
Hammam ibn Ghalib al-Farazdaq, a poet of the Umayyad period. Al-Baladhuri reports that he praised the river al-Mubarak, which Khalid ibn Abd Allah al-Qasri dug. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Muruj al-Dhahab
Al-Mas'udi reports that the king of the Romans wrote to al-Walid when he demolished the church, and al-Farazdaq answered him with a verse of the Qur'an. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.157} He also reports verses of his in elegy for Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.184}

The data ascribes to him a work, [[work/farazdaq-verses-on-al-aqra-ibn-habis|his verses on al-Aqra ibn Habis]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy relates al-Farazdaq's poem in praise of Ali Zayn al-Abidin, when the crowd made way for him at the circumambulation and someone present asked who he was, and al-Farazdaq answered: 'This is he whose footsteps the valley of Mecca knows'. The Sheikh places the scene in the presence of the caliph Abu Ja'far al-Mansur. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:43:00} (needs_source: the literary sources usually place the story before Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik, while the Sheikh names al-Mansur; the difference is left as it stands.)

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
[[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] lists al-Farazdaq, Abu Firas the poet, in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], noting that he narrated from Companions. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 345} He reports that [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]] declared him weak, saying he slandered chaste women so his narration must be avoided, and comments: "he narrated little". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 345}
