## Overview

Taj al-Din Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ata Allah al-Iskandari, the ascetic preacher (d. 709 AH), pupil of Abu'l-Abbas al-Mursi of the Shadhili order. Al-Dhahabi describes him as 'one of the great opponents of Shaykh Taqi al-Din Ibn Taymiyya', whose funeral was widely attended and whose standing 'settled in souls' with a remarkable majesty {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house}.

He is famed for his Sufi and homiletic works, above all the *Hikam al-Ata'iyya*, which became one of the most commented books of the order.

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Abd al-Karim Ibn Ata' Allah, Taj al-Din of Alexandria, the Shadhili: a companion of Abu al-Abbas al-Mursi, he was the spokesman of the Sufis in his time and preached at al-Azhar. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/273} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/274} He died in mid-Jumada al-Akhira 709 at the Mansuriyya madrasa, with a very large funeral. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/274}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy explains Ibn Ata Allah's maxims in the series [[work/sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya|Sharh al-Hikam al-'Ata'iyya]], and says he chose them because the heart needs a charge of faith and a sound creed by which the servant rises through the stations of worship. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:03:30} He opens the first episode by saying that he is beginning 'a new maxim' of Ibn Ata Allah's, {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:05:00} and comments on his maxim about asking that it does not mean giving up supplication. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:11:00}
