## Summary
Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf in the Sira is a Jewish poet — his father al-Ashraf of Tayy and his mother of Banu al-Nadir: he lampooned the Prophet and stirred Makka against him after Badr, until the Prophet permitted his death and Muhammad ibn Maslama and his companions killed him by night.

## In the texts
Ibn Ishaq names him among the Jews' poets, in his lampoons of the Prophet, his elegies of the slain and his stirring of Quraysh; al-Bukhari has the story of his death: 'who is for Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, for he has hurt God and His Messenger?' — Muhammad ibn Maslama volunteered and killed him in his house at night with a party of his brothers {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the Sira and history
Ka'b was among Medina's most famous poets, rich and propertied; he lampooned the Prophet after Badr and stirred Quraysh to war on him, so the Prophet permitted him, and Muhammad ibn Maslama, Abu Na'ila and a party of the Ansar drew him from his fort on a pretext and killed him on a night of Safar in year three {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For younger visitors
Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf was a Jewish poet who lampooned the Prophet and stirred against him; the Prophet permitted his death and Muhammad ibn Maslama killed him by night.

## Notes for researchers
His father was of Tayy and his mother of Banu al-Nadir — he is Jewish through his mother, not his lineage on one view — and his poetry is in the Diwan and the Sira; the affair of his death is in al-Bukhari, the Sira and the Tabaqat.
