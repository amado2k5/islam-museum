## Summary

Mohammad Karim Khan Zand (c. 1705 – 1 March 1779) was the founder of the Zand dynasty, ruling all of Iran except Khorasan and Balochistan from 1751 to 1779 {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en} {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-ur}. Refusing the title of shah, he ruled from Shiraz as Vakil al-Ra'aya, representative of the people, and his years from 1765 to 1779 are remembered as the zenith of Zand rule, when a country devastated by forty years of war recovered tranquillity, security and prosperity {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}.

## Origins and service under Nader Shah

Karim was born about 1705 in the village of Pari in the Malayer district, eldest son of Inaq Khan Zand, of the Zand tribe of Laks concentrated on Pari and Kamazan {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}. When Nader subdued the Zagros tribes in 1732 he killed the Zand chief Mehdi Khan and four hundred of his kinsmen and force-migrated the survivors to Abivard and Dargaz, where the able men, Karim among them, were incorporated into Nader's army; Karim served as a cavalryman of no high status, and later used to tell against himself the story of a gold-embossed saddle he stole as a poor trooper and, conscience-smitten, secretly returned {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}.

## Rise to power

Nader Shah's murder in 1747 let the Zands return to western Iran {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}. In 1750 Karim joined Ali Mardan Khan Bakhtiari and Abu'l-Fath Khan in storming Isfahan and setting up the Safavid prince Abu Turab as Ismail III, taking command of the army himself; when Ali Mardan broke the compact, deposed and killed Abu'l-Fath and pillaged towards Shiraz, Karim defeated him in a war that ran through 1753, and in the end killed him and gained control of all Iran except Khorasan {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en} {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-ar}.

## Rule

Karim made Shiraz his capital and rebuilt it — his castle, gardens, mosques, a new city wall, baths, a caravanserai and a bazaar — and he had the tombs of Shah Shoja, Hafez and Saadi renovated {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}. Relations with Britain were restored and the East India Company was allowed a trading post in southern Iran {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}. Tales and anecdotes portray him as a compassionate ruler of modest tastes who sat on an inexpensive carpet rather than a throne, and the Oxford Dictionary of Islam, as the article reports it, calls him the most humane Iranian ruler of the Islamic era {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}.

## The Ottoman war and Basra

In 1775 Karim declared war on the Ottomans over Baban, Basra's trade and Omar Pasha's exactions on Iranian pilgrims to Najaf and Karbala and his seizure of the effects of pilgrims who had died in the 1773 plague {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}. His brother Sadeq Khan besieged Basra with 30,000 men from April 1775 until its commander Suleiman Agha surrendered on 16 April 1776 {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}. At his court Karim kept the young Qajar hostage Agha Mohammad Khan as a respected guest rather than a captive, calling him his Piran-e Viseh and asking his advice on affairs of state {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}.

## Death and succession

Karim Khan died on 1 March 1779, 13 Safar 1193, after six months of illness, most likely tuberculosis, and was buried three days later in the Nazar Garden at Shiraz {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en} {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-ar}. Civil war broke out at once among his descendants, none of whom ruled as effectively as he had, and the last of them, Lotf Ali Khan, was killed by Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}.

## Faith

No source read names a religion or a legal school for the man himself, and Wikidata carries no religion value for him {cite:wikidata-karim-khan-zand}. What the sources describe is the state he ruled: the Twelver Shia sanctuaries of Iraq mattered enough to his subjects that the pilgrim grievances stood among his declared grounds for war, while his own religious policy broke with the Safavid pattern — unlike the Safavids, he did not seek the approval of the ulama {cite:wikipedia-karim-khan-zand-en}. Rule over a Shia state is dynastic context, not a naming of the man's school, so his faith is recorded as unknown.
