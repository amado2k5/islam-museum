## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Sulayman is a man of [[group/hajur-tribe-yemen|Hajur]] whom the narrator describes as "with no fault in his religion and school". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.139} He said: "How many signs have we seen since al-Hadi came!" and told of a man among them whose bowels ran night and day; when a letter from [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] came he took its seal, went to the man and said: eat this, and he ate it and recovered. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.139} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.140} The book's index lists "Ibrahim al-Hajuri" at p. 441, pointing to p. 296, a page not yet read, so it is not asserted that he is the same man. The report is attributed. See [[event/reports-of-al-hadis-seal-and-two-cures-told-at-khiwan-286]].
