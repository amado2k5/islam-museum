Mustafa Busuladžić (1 April 1914 – 29 June 1945) was a Bosnian writer and translator from Gorica near Trebinje, and one of the prominent Islamic intellectuals of Bosnia between the two world wars.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-busuladzic-en} Educated at the Elči Ibrahim Pasha Madrasa in Travnik and the Gazi Husrev-beg Madrasa in Sarajevo, where he graduated in 1936, he published from his student days in journals such as Islamski glas, Novi Behar and El-Hidaja, and went on to the Islamic Sharia School in Sarajevo (graduated 1941) and two years of postgraduate Oriental studies in Rome, where he worked for the reviews Mondo Arabo and Oriente Moderno and as a radio news translator and announcer.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-busuladzic-en}{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-busuladzic-bs} Back in Sarajevo he taught at the Sharia Gymnasium and other schools, and as president of the Young Muslims in Sarajevo he stood at the head of the movement in the city.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-busuladzic-en}

His best-known work was the booklet Muslims in Soviet Russia, on the plight of Muslims under Bolshevik rule; his journalism also carried the antisemitic and pro-Ustaša advocacy that the Bosnian biography records plainly.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-busuladzic-en}{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-busuladzic-bs} Arrested in mid-April 1945 after the communist liberation of Sarajevo, he was tried by a military court on 22–23 May 1945 as a collaborator of the occupier — the charges resting on his cooperation with the Jerusalem mufti Amin al-Husseini, his teaching at the SS divisions' school and his writings — sentenced to death, and shot on the night of 29 June 1945 at Velešići in Sarajevo.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-busuladzic-en}{cite:wikidata-mustafa-busuladzic}
