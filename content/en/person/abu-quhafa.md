## Summary
Father of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]]; an elderly blind man who on the day of Mecca's conquest watched the army's arrival from Mount Abu Qubays with his daughter, until his son Abu Bakr led him to the Prophet at [[place/dhu-tuwa|Dhu Tuwa]] to declare his Islam {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/48}.

## His conversion
Yahya ibn Ubbad ibn Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr relates from his father from his grandmother Asma' bint Abi Bakr: when Abu Quhafa sensed the army spreading, he came down with his younger daughter; Abu Bakr took his hand and brought him to the Prophet, who said: "why did you not leave the shaykh in his house until we came to him?" Abu Bakr said: "he has more right to walk to you than you to walk to him" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/48}.

The Prophet seated him, stroked his chest, and he became Muslim; he ordered the whiteness of his hair to be changed {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/48}.

## For young visitors
A blind elderly shaykh, the father of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq. His son brought him to the Prophet to declare his Islam, and the Prophet welcomed him with every gentleness and warmth, honouring his great age.
