## Overview
Yazid ibn al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, was given allegiance to the caliphate after Hisham, and it is said that Marwan ibn Muhammad gave him the nickname "al-Naqis" (the Deficient). He died in the year 126 AH {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.262} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.298}. His kunya was Abu Khalid {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 212}.

## His lineage and nickname
According to al-Suyuti, his mother was Shahfarand, daughter of Firuz ibn Yazdegerd; the mother of Firuz was daughter of Shiruyeh ibn Khusraw; and the mother of Shiruyeh was daughter of Khaqan, king of the Turks. For this reason he said in boasting: "I am the son of Khusraw and of my father Marwan". al-Tha'alibi said that he was the most highly descended in kingship from both sides {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 408-409}.

al-Mas'udi reports that he was squint-eyed, and was given the nickname "al-Naqis", though he was not deficient in his body or his mind; rather, some of the soldiers had their allowances reduced {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 204}.

## His rising
According to al-Suyuti, he seized the caliphate and killed his cousin al-Walid. According to al-Mas'udi, his rising took place in Damascus, with a group of the Mu'tazila and others from the people of Darayya and al-Mazza, against [[person/al-walid-ibn-yazid|al-Walid ibn Yazid]], because of what had appeared of his wickedness among them {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 408-409} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 212}.

## Views and belief
al-Mas'udi attributes to him the adoption of the view of the Mu'tazila on the five principles, and reports that the Mu'tazila preferred him to Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz in religion {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 204}. al-Suyuti transmits from Ibn Abd al-Hakam, from al-Shafi'i, that when he came to power he called people to predestination and compelled them to it, and brought near the companions of Ghaylan {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 408-409}.
