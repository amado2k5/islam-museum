## Overview
Yazid ibn al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, was given allegiance to the caliphate after Hisham, and it is said that Marwan ibn Muhammad gave him the nickname "al-Naqis" (the Deficient). He died in the year 126 AH {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.262} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.298}. His kunya was Abu Khalid {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 212}.

## His lineage and nickname
According to al-Suyuti, his mother was Shahfarand, daughter of Firuz ibn Yazdegerd; the mother of Firuz was daughter of Shiruyeh ibn Khusraw; and the mother of Shiruyeh was daughter of Khaqan, king of the Turks. For this reason he said in boasting: "I am the son of Khusraw and of my father Marwan". al-Tha'alibi said that he was the most highly descended in kingship from both sides {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 408-409}.

al-Mas'udi reports that he was squint-eyed, and was given the nickname "al-Naqis", though he was not deficient in his body or his mind; rather, some of the soldiers had their allowances reduced {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 204}.

## His rising
According to al-Suyuti, he seized the caliphate and killed his cousin al-Walid. According to al-Mas'udi, his rising took place in Damascus, with a group of the Mu'tazila and others from the people of Darayya and al-Mazza, against [[person/al-walid-ibn-yazid|al-Walid ibn Yazid]], because of what had appeared of his wickedness among them {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 408-409} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 212}.

## Views and belief
al-Mas'udi attributes to him the adoption of the view of the Mu'tazila on the five principles, and reports that the Mu'tazila preferred him to Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz in religion {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 204}. al-Suyuti transmits from Ibn Abd al-Hakam, from al-Shafi'i, that when he came to power he called people to predestination and compelled them to it, and brought near the companions of Ghaylan {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 408-409}.

## His officials in Khalifa ibn Khayyat
[[person/khalifa-ibn-khayyat|Khalifa ibn Khayyat]] closes his account of this reign by naming its governors and office-holders. They are given here in his order; lines the editor restored from later authors are marked as such. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 369–371}

- **Iraq:** [[person/mansur-ibn-jawhur|Mansur ibn Jumhur al-Kalbi]] (some say he forged the appointment; about forty days; in (( )) in the edition: restored by the editor from later authors quoting Khalifa); then [[person/abdallah-ibn-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Abd Allah ibn Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 369} {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 370}
- **Mecca, Medina and Ta'if:** Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn Uthman ibn Affan (then dismissed); then Abd al-Aziz ibn Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 370}
- **Basra:** Muhammad ibn al-Qasim ibn Muhammad (fled); then Abd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Umayya, al-Afwah (chosen by the Basrans; led the prayer for a week); then Jarir ibn Yazid ibn Jarir ibn Abd Allah al-Bajali; then Abd Allah ibn Abi Uthman (Ibn Umar wrote to him and he led the prayer); then Sa'id ibn Amr ibn Ja'da ibn Hubayra al-Makhzumi (some say; expelled by the Basrans); then Amr ibn Suhayl ibn Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 370}
- **Kufa:** Ubayd Allah ibn al-Abbas (appointed by Mansur ibn Jumhur, dismissed by Ibn Umar); then Asim ibn Umar (brother of Abd Allah ibn Umar) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 370}
- **Sijistan:** Harb ibn Qatan; then Muhammad ibn Azzar (appointed by Mansur, dismissed by Ibn Umar); then Harb ibn Qatan (restored by Ibn Umar for a month); then Sawwar ibn al-Ash'ar al-Mazini (Harb's deputy); then Sa'id ibn Amr, of the family of Sa'id ibn al-As (sent by Ibn Umar) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 370}
- **Khurasan:** [[person/nasr-ibn-sayyar|Nasr ibn Sayyar]] (until the end of Umayyad rule) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 370}
- **Judgeship of Basra:** Amir ibn Ubayda (withdrew during the civil strife) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 371}
- **Judgeship of Kufa:** [[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-rahman-ibn-abi-layla|Ibn Abi Layla]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 371}
- **Judgeship of Medina:** Sa'd ibn Ibrahim (appointed by Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd Allah; dismissed by Abd al-Aziz ibn Umar); then Uthman ibn Umar al-Taymi {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 371}
- **Police:** Bukayr ibn Shammakh al-Lakhmi (until Yazid died; in (( )) in the edition: restored by the editor from later authors quoting Khalifa) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 371}
- **Chancery (letters):** Layth ibn Abi Sulayman ibn Sa'd (the editor suggests «Layth ibn Sulayman») {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 371}
- **Land tax, army and small seal:** al-Nadr ibn Amr (of Yemen; with the guard) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 371}
- **Caliphal seal:** Abd al-Rahman ibn Hanbal al-Kalbi; then Qatan, his mawla (some say) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 371}
