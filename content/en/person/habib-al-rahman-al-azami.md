## Summary
Indian hadith scholar (1901–1992), among the foremost editors of hadith compilations in the twentieth century. He is famed for his monumental critical editions of the Musannafs of Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn Abi Shayba, two of the widest sources of early hadith, besides other editions published in India. {cite:wikipedia-habib-al-rahman-al-azami}

## Life and education

Habib al-Rahman al-A'zami was born in 1901 in Azamgarh, India, and studied hadith and jurisprudence under the scholars of his land before joining the great teachers of Deoband and the Indian hadith schools, from which he graduated as an editor-scholar. {cite:wikipedia-habib-al-rahman-al-azami}

He was formed by his teachers' method of reviving manuscripts and devoted his life to publishing lost treasures of hadith — a continuation of the Indian editorial school that brought the books of the Sunna from library vaults to the printing press. {cite:wikipedia-habib-al-rahman-al-azami}

## Editions and scholarship

He supervised the critical edition of the Musannaf of Abd al-Razzaq al-San'ani in eleven volumes and that of the Musannaf of Ibn Abi Shayba, both issued with extensive documentation, commentary and indexes, becoming references for every student of the transmitted Companion and Successor reports. {cite:wikipedia-habib-al-rahman-al-azami}

Among his other editions are works in sira and fiqh; he contributed to producing Al-Hawi and served early hadith and juristic texts, working from the available manuscripts under the rules of academic editing. {cite:wikipedia-habib-al-rahman-al-azami}

## Legacy

His editions redrew the map of modern hadith studies: the two Musannafs opened early sources that had not been in circulation, and researchers in the history of legislation and hadith built many papers and theses upon them. {cite:wikipedia-habib-al-rahman-al-azami}

He died in 1992; his editions remain the standard in libraries across the Muslim world, and his name appears in the introduction of every serious work on the history of hadith before the canonical collections. {cite:wikipedia-habib-al-rahman-al-azami}
