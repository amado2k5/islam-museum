## Summary
Nasir-ud-Din Mahmud Shah Sharqi (d. 1457) was the fourth sultan of Jaunpur, the Sharqi kingdom of northern India, reigning from 1440 to 1457. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} He succeeded his father Ibrahim Shah Sharqi and inherited a large and stable kingdom, which he set out to enlarge at the expense of Bengal, Malwa, Orissa and Delhi. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} The one encyclopedia article on him is flagged as AI-generated, so this record keeps to the frame that the scholarly histories behind it — Habib & Nizami's Comprehensive History of India, Haig's Cambridge History of India, and Mian Muhammad Saeed's The Sharqi of Jaunpur — support, and flags the article's precise but uncorroborated dates as readings rather than facts. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} Wikidata independently carries his death year as 1457. {cite:wikidata-mahmud-shah-sharqi}

## Succession and expansion
Mahmud was the eldest son of Ibrahim Shah, and on his father's death in 844/1440 he succeeded at once. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} His first venture was an invasion of Bengal; through the 1440s he campaigned repeatedly — against the rebellious chiefs around the great fort of Chunar, against Kalpi in 1443 (a quarrel mediated by Mahmud Khalji of Malwa, with elephants exchanged as presents and Kalpi eventually restored to Nasir Khan), against the Ujjainiya chiefs of Bhojpur, and on a plundering expedition into Orissa reported by Nizam-ud Din and Firishta. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} At home his reign is remembered as prosperous: the article describes a ruler generous to nobles and scholars, who dedicated himself to strengthening his religion and his sultanate, commissioned mosques, and restored the Atala Mosque at Jaunpur built under his predecessors. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en}

## War with Delhi and death
Delhi's weakness drew both Jaunpur and Malwa into its territories, and after Bahlul Khan Lodi took the Delhi throne in 1451 the two sultanates came to open war. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} In 1452, while Bahlul was campaigning in the Punjab, Mahmud and Darya Khan Lodi besieged Delhi; Bahlul returned in time, captured the besiegers' pastured transport animals, and when Fath Khan's elephant was wounded by Qutb Khan Lodi's arrow the Jaunpur army broke — Fath Khan was beheaded and Mahmud retreated to Jaunpur with the loss of seven war elephants. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} In 1457 Mahmud marched on Shamsabad and defeated a Lodi force, taking Qutb Khan prisoner, but before the fighting could resume he died — the article's infobox gives 12 September 1457 — and his eldest son Bhikan succeeded him under the title Muhammad Shah, negotiating peace with the Lodis. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en} {cite:wikidata-mahmud-shah-sharqi} The article's infobox also gives his birth as 16 April 1429 at Jaunpur, a precision found in no other source consulted, and the museum's queue had carried 1420 for his birth and 1458 for his death, neither of them attested in the sources read. {cite:wikipedia-mahmud-shah-sharqi-en}
