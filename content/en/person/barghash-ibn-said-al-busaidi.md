## Overview
Barghash bin Said was sultan of Zanzibar; he succeeded his brother Majid on 7 October 1870 and reigned until his death on 27 March 1888. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1103}

## Coming to power
He tried to seize power on his father's death in 1856 and again in 1859, when British intervention defeated him and sent him to Bombay for two years. Britain supported his accession, but he at once resisted its efforts to suppress the slave trade, relying partly on the Ibadi Mlawa faction, hostile to European intervention. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1103}

## Policy at home and abroad
In 1873 he was obliged to close the slave markets and forbid slave exports, and was then invited to London; in 1876 the movement of slave caravans by land was forbidden. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1103} The British agent Kirk became the dominant personality in Zanzibar until 1886. In 1877 failed talks with Sir William Mackinnon over a concession between the coast and Victoria Nyanza ruined his best chance of enforcing authority in the interior; in 1884-85 the German agent Peters made treaties with chiefs over whom Barghash claimed suzerainty, Germany took them under protection, and a visit of five German warships and an ultimatum forced him to accept. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1103}

## Death and assessment
He died immediately on returning from a visit to Oman, his health broken. The article's author calls him an able and energetic ruler who supplied Zanzibar with pure water, organised the import of cheap grain and worked to restore the clove industry after the cyclone of 1872; contemporary Europeans often called him xenophobic, but the author says his position was extremely difficult. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1103}
