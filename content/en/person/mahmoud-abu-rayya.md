## Summary
Egyptian writer and researcher (1889–1970) known for his polemical book Adwa' ala al-Sunna al-Nabawiyya (1958), which questioned many hadiths of the two Sahihs and the method of transmission, provoking wide rejoinders from hadith scholars. He also wrote Shaykh al-Mudira and polemical treatises. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abu-rayya}

## Life and formation

Mahmoud Abu Rayya was born in Egypt in 1889, studied for years at al-Azhar, then worked in journalism and writing, absorbing the Egyptian reformist ideas of the early twentieth century. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abu-rayya}

He dealt with diverse scholars and schools and arrived at a critical stance toward the classical hadith literature that made him one of the most controversial writers of his age. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abu-rayya}

## Adwa' and the rejoinders

In 1958 he published Adwa' ala al-Sunna al-Nabawiyya, calling to distinguish between the revealed Qur'an and the reports beneath it, questioning the authenticity of many hadiths of the two Sahihs — especially the narrations of Abu Hurayra — and tracing part of them to old influences and political purposes, while seeing formative stages in hadith codification. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abu-rayya}

The book drew a wave of rejoinders from leading hadith scholars, including treatises by al-Albani, Muhammad Abd al-Razzaq Hamza, Ahmad Shakir, Muhammad Abu Zahra and others; Abu Rayya's name entered the history of the fiercest modern controversies over the Sunna. The museum presents the book and its rejoinders without ruling. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abu-rayya}

## Legacy

Adwa' ala al-Sunna stands as a landmark in the history of modern hadith criticism: sympathizers among Qur'anist and enlightened currents read it, while hadith scholars rebut it, and it remains a recurring reference in every debate on the authority of the Sunna. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abu-rayya}

Abu Rayya died in 1970, his name still contested: some see the raiser of a legitimate question about transmission, while hadith scholars see a renewer of old skepticism — both characterisations the museum record holds without preference. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abu-rayya}
