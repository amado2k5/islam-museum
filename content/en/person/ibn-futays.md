## Overview
Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn 'Isa ibn Futays ibn Asbagh, Abu al-Mutarrif, was a scholar of tafsir, hadith and the history of transmitters, of al-Andalus. Born at Cordoba, he held the mazalim jurisdiction there then the judgeship in 394 AH, retiring the next year, and died at Cordoba at the outset of the Berber fitna in 402 AH (1012 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.325}

He kept six copyists who always transcribed what he dictated of hadith and reports or what he chose to excerpt from others' books; he assembled a library such as no Andalusian of his age had gathered, and corresponded with the scholars of the East. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.325}

His works include al-Qisas wa-l-Asbab allati nazala min ajliha al-Qur'an in over a hundred parts; al-Masabih, biographies of the Companions in about a hundred parts; Fada'il al-Tabi'in in a hundred and fifty parts; al-Nasikh wa-l-Mansukh in thirty; al-Ikhwa min al-Muhaddithin in forty; and A'lam al-Nubuwwa wa-Dalalat al-Risala in ten. Ibn Nasir al-Din says his books sold after him for forty thousand dinars. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.325}
