## Overview
Abd Allah ibn Nafi' al-Sa'igh, a client of Banu Makhzum, is called by al-Dhahabi one of the great jurists of Medina {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2538}. He says the judge Iyad praised him lavishly and placed him at the head of his Madarik {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2538}. He gives his birth as some twenty years past the hundred {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. He is a different man from Abd Allah ibn Nafi' al-Zubayri; al-Dhahabi says the Sa'igh was older, earlier and more reliable on Malik because of his long companionship, and that he succeeded Malik in his teaching circle after Ibn Kinana {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2538}.

## Telling him from al-Zubayri
Al-Dhahabi quotes Iyad: Sahnun sent for Muhammad ibn Razin on hearing that he narrated from Abd Allah ibn Nafi'; Muhammad replied that it was al-Zubayri, not the Sa'igh, and Sahnun asked why he had concealed it, then said what scorpions would come out after him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2538}. Sahnun held that the two must be distinguished so their narrations do not mix, though both were reliable imams {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2538}. Al-Dhahabi quotes that al-Zubayri died in 216 AH and was a teacher of Ibn Habib and Sa'id ibn Hassan {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2538}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Hasan, who rose in Medina and was killed, Usama ibn Zayd al-Laythi, Malik ibn Anas, Ibn Abi Dhi'b, Sulayman ibn Yazid al-Ka'bi, a companion of Anas, Kathir ibn Abd Allah ibn Awf, Dawud ibn Qays al-Farra' and many others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. He was not broad in hadith but brilliant in fiqh {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Numayr, Ahmad ibn Salih, Sahnun ibn Sa'id, Salama ibn Shabib, al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Khallal, Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam, al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar, Ahmad ibn al-Hasan al-Tirmidhi and others narrated from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}.

## What the critics said
Abu Talib reports Ahmad ibn Hanbal saying he followed Malik's opinion and gave fatwas to the people of Medina but was not a hadith man and was narrow in it {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. Ibn Ma'in said he was reliable; al-Bukhari said he is recognised in some things and rejected in others; Abu Hatim said his memory was weak and his book sounder; al-Nasa'i said there was no harm in him; Ibn Adi said he narrated unusual reports from Malik {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. Ibn Sa'd said he attached himself closely to Malik but was below Ma'n {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. Al-Dhahabi says Ibn Adi made an unbearable error in his entry by citing a hadith narrated by Abd al-Wahhab ibn Bukht from him from Hisham ibn Urwa, since the Sa'igh took from no one until Hisham had died and was born years after Abd al-Wahhab's death {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. The museum gives each judgement to the one who made it and chooses none.

## His death
He died in Ramadan 206 AH according to Ibn Sa'd, and al-Dhahabi says this is the correct date and every other a slip or a copyist's error {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2539}. A death in 186 AH is also mentioned in the entry, and al-Dhahabi remarks that the sounder date is another {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2538}.
