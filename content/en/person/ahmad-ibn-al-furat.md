## Overview
Ahmad ibn al-Furat ibn Khalid al-Dabbi al-Razi, Abu Mas'ud, is called by al-Dhahabi the shaykh, imam, the great memoriser and authority, hadith scholar of Isfahan and resident there {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}. The sign printed with his entry shows that Abu Dawud recorded his hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}. He was born a little after 180 AH in the caliphate of Harun al-Rashid, began seeking knowledge young and was counted among the memorisers as a beardless youth {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}.

## His travels and teachers
He travelled to Iraq, al-Sham, the Hijaz and Yemen and met the elders {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}. He heard Abd Allah ibn Numayr, Abu Usama, Husayn ibn Ali al-Ju'fi, Abu Dawud al-Hafari, Yazid ibn Harun, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi, Yahya ibn Adam, Abd al-Razzaq ibn Hammam, Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, Abu Nu'aym, Affan, Muslim ibn Ibrahim and many others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}.

## His students
Abu Dawud narrated from him in his Sunan, as did Abu Bakr ibn Abi Asim, Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Manda, Ja'far al-Firyabi, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Muhallab, Abd al-Rahman ibn Yahya ibn Manda, Ahmad ibn Mahmud ibn Subayh and many Isfahanis, the last of them to die being the long-lived Abu Muhammad ibn Faris, shaykh of Abu Nu'aym the memoriser {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}. Al-Dhahabi gives through him a report from Abu Usama from Hisham ibn Urwa from his father that he had seen no one more knowledgeable in medicine than A'isha {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}.

## His death
The heading of his entry gives his death as 258 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص879}.
