## Overview
Khalifa ibn Khayyat, known as Shabib al-Usfuri, from Basra. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 324}

## His books
Ibn al-Nadim lists among his books *al-Tabaqat* (The Generations), *al-Tarikh* (The History), *Tabaqat al-Qurra'* (The Generations of the Readers), *Tarikh al-Zamnaa, al-'Urjan, al-Mardha and al-'Umyan* (The History of the Lame, the Crippled, the Sick and the Blind), and sections of the Qur'an, its tenths, sevenths and verses. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 324}

## Lineage and upbringing
He was Abu Amr Khalifa ibn Khayyat ibn Abi Hubayra Khalifa ibn Khayyat al-Laythi al-Usfuri, nicknamed "Shabab"; al-Usfuri refers to safflower (usfur) and its trade. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 5} He grew up in Basra in a scholarly household: his grandfather Abu Hubayra was a hadith scholar who died in 160 AH, and his father Khayyat also transmitted hadith. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 5} {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 6} He studied under many teachers, among them Yazid ibn Zuray', Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi and Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 6}

## Students and standing
[[person/al-bukhari|Al-Bukhari]] transmitted from him in the Sahih and al-Tarikh al-Kabir, as did Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Abu Ya'la al-Mawsili and Baqi ibn Makhlad of Cordoba, the transmitter of his History. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 7} Al-Bukhari cites him in eighteen places in the Sahih. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 9} The editor notes that at Basra he stood with the judge Ahmad ibn Riyah when some Mu'tazilis complained against him, which he takes as a sign of Khalifa's firmness and standing. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 7} {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 8}

## Death
The editor infers that he was born around 160 AH. His death is disputed: Mutayyan and Ibn Kathir give 240 AH, Ibn Zabr 246, and Ibn Khallikan Ramadan 230. The editor prefers 240, as the view of the early authorities closest to his time, and rejects 230 because the History reaches the events of 232. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 13} {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 14} His [[work/tarikh-khalifa-ibn-khayyat|History]] is the oldest book of annals to survive. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 intro p. 3}
