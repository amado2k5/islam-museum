## Summary
Moroccan hadith scholar, bibliographer and historian (1857–1927) of the learned Kattani house of Fez. His works include Al-Risala al-Mustatrafa on the famous books of the Sunna, Nazm al-Mutanathir on mutawatir hadith, and Salwat al-Anfas, a biographical dictionary of Muslim scholars. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-jafar-al-kattani}

## Life and formation

Muhammad ibn Ja'far al-Kattani was born in Fez in 1857 into the Kattani family that combined scholarship, leadership and Sufism; he studied at al-Qarawiyyin and took the highest ijazas in hadith and the sciences. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-jafar-al-kattani}

He travelled east, met the scholars of Egypt, the Hijaz and Syria, entered scholarly correspondence with the celebrities of his age, and was known as a collector of books and manuscripts until his library became one of the greatest treasuries of Fez. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-jafar-al-kattani}

## Works

His works include Al-Risala al-Mustatrafa li-Bayan Mashhur Kutub al-Sunna al-Musharrafa, a register of the principal hadith compilations, their commentaries and authors; and Nazm al-Mutanathir min al-Hadith al-Mutawatir, ordering the mass-transmitted hadiths. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-jafar-al-kattani}

Also Salwat al-Anfas, a biographical dictionary of the scholars and saints buried in Fez, and treatises on isnad, ijaza and chronology. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-jafar-al-kattani}

## Legacy

With his sons and brothers — among them Abd al-Hayy al-Kattani — he forms a scholarly family that reinvigorated hadith authorship and bibliography in Morocco; the Risala al-Mustatrafa remains a reference used by cataloguers of the heritage. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-jafar-al-kattani}

He died in Fez in 1927; his books are still printed and edited, and his list of works is a basic entry point for anyone mapping the sources of hadith. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-jafar-al-kattani}
