## Summary
The Sa'di sultan who regained Morocco with Ottoman backing and died on the field of jihad {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 258-265}.

## From exile to throne
He fled with his brother [[person/ahmad-al-mansur-al-sadi|Ahmad al-Mansur]] to the Ottoman sultan, who armed him with five thousand well-equipped fighters; he entered Fez victorious over his nephew al-Mutawakkil, then received Ottoman trainers, weapons and a corps against the invasion {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 258-259}.

## The reforms
He renewed the ships, revived maritime trade, built a regular army on the Ottoman model of arms and ranks, strengthened the diwans and formed a consultative body versed in state affairs and international politics; the contemporary French poet Agrippa d'Aubigné called him his people's fairest, sharp of mind, versed in Spanish, Italian, Armenian and Russian, an excellent Arabic poet {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 259}.

## His death
At [[event/battle-of-wadi-al-makhazin-1578|Wadi al-Makhazin]] he cut the enemy from its fleet, blew the bridge, repelled the first charge sword drawn though ill — 'since when does sickness bar Muslims from jihad?' — and died with his finger to his lips: conceal it until victory {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 264-265}.
