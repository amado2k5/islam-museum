## Summary
Abdul Kader Kane (c. 1726–1807) was a Torodbe scholar and qadi from the Saloum who became, in 1776, the first Almaami of the Islamic Imamate of Futa Toro in the Senegal valley, succeeding the revolution's leader Sulayman Bal. He ruled for thirty years, defeated the Brakna and Trarza Moors, closed his state to the French slave trade, survived capture by the king of Cayor, and was killed at the end of his reign — in 1807 by assassination on the French account, in 1806 in battle on the English one. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-fr}

## Formation and election
He was born at Pafa Warneu in the Saloum, where his father was teaching, into a line of Islamic scholars: his grandfather Lamin had made the pilgrimage to Mecca and his father Hamady had studied the Qur'an in Futa Jallon. Kane studied in Cayor and Mauritania under Moktar Ndumbe Diop, founder of the school at Coki, and further at Pir Sagnokhor; before power he was an independent qadi at Appe, near Bundu, and he did not at first join the revolt against the Denyanke. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-fr}

When Sulayman Bal was killed in battle in 1776, Kane was among the candidates to succeed him. Rudolph Ware notes that another cleric refused the post first, and that Kane hesitated — the "pious distance" Senegambian clerics traditionally kept from political power — while David Robinson cites a more obscure tradition of a longer confusion before the election. He was inaugurated as Almaami in 1776, at about fifty years of age: kept in seclusion for a week while livestock were sacrificed for him, given the turban of office by a cleric, and sworn to by at least fifty chiefs, with the Qur'an, the Ishriniyyat and the Dala'il al-Khayrat recited and their reciters bound to correct him if he fell short of them. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-ur}

## Rule
From his capital at Thilogne he first defeated the Brakna Moors, then in 1786, allied with Brakna, destroyed the Trarza emir Ali Kouri near Dagana and ended the Trarza raids and extortions on Futa Toro. He filled the territorial administration with loyal clerics, who in time made their electoral posts hereditary. In Cayor his fortune turned: after the new damel Amari Ngone Ndela repudiated Cayor's submission, Kane marched in 1796 with the largest army seen in Senegambia, but the damel poisoned the wells of his eastern provinces, the Torodbe army came out of the Ferlo desert to find neither food nor water, and it was routed at Bunxoy and Kane captured. Held honourably in Cayor until a new almami had been elected, he was then sent home. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-fr}

Against the slave trade his record is argued over. His treaty barred the French from enslaving Futa Toro's people or carrying slaves through his territory, and Thomas Clarkson's letters praised "the wise and virtuous Almaamy"; other scholars answer that his policy was the traditional Islamic one — Muslims could not be enslaved, others still could be — and that his own subjects held slaves. Both characterisations are in the sources, and the museum states them without a verdict. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-en}

## Death — two accounts
The French article states that he was assassinated on 4 April 1807, in a plot organised by the Jagoordo, the assembly that elected the almami; Wikidata records the same date. The English article instead has Bundu and Kaarta invade in 1806 and Kane shot by the Almaami of Bundu on his prayer mat at the losing battle of Gouriki, and dates his death 26 April 1806. The museum builds 4 April 1807 under the status debated, with both accounts stated; the sources do not allow the difference to be resolved here. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-fr} {cite:wikidata-abdul-kader-kane} {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-en}

## Faith
No source consulted names a legal school or a Sufi order for Kane himself. He was a scholar of the Torodbe clerical tradition, a qadi before he was a ruler, and the law he enforced was his own reading of Islam; the Dala'il al-Khayrat recited at his inauguration is a devotional text, not an affiliation, and Wikidata records no religion value for him. His faith is recorded as unattested. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-kader-kane-en} {cite:wikidata-abdul-kader-kane}
