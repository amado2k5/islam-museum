## Overview
Amir Khan (1768-1834) was a famous Pathan predatory chief and associate of Jaswant Rao Holkar. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 466}

## Early career
He was born at Sambhal in the Muradabad district of Rohilkhand. As a young man he and his followers were employed by zamindars and Maratha officials as sihbandi troops for collecting revenue. He quickly became a leader of bandits and was successively employed by the rulers of Bhopal, Indore and Jaipur. In 1798 Jaswant Rao Holkar gave him the title of nawab. In the following year he plundered Saugor and the country around it, and in 1809 he tried to advance into Berar, but Lord Minto sent troops to the area and frustrated him. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 466}

## Treaty and state
By 1817 his army numbered 8,000 infantry, 20,000 cavalry and 200 guns. In the same year, aware of British strength, he concluded a treaty with Lord Hastings, the governor-general: provided he disbanded his army, his territories were guaranteed to him. He thus became the founder of the state of Tonk, which was merged into the Union of Rajasthan in 1948. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 466-467}
