## Summary
Khosrow II Parviz in the sources is the last strong Sasanian king: he tore the Prophet's letter when it was read to him and said his word on them, and was killed in his son Shirawayh's revolt shortly before the conquests.

## In the texts
The Sira tells the affair of the Prophet's letter to Khosrow: when it was read to him he tore it, and when that reached the Prophet he spoke his saying on them — in al-Bukhari, the Prophet said on hearing of Khosrow's killing, 'may God tear his rule' or as he said {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the Sira and history
Khosrow Parviz son of Hormizd IV fought Rome twenty years until he took Jerusalem and Egypt, then was defeated before Heraclius, so his son Shirawayh rose and killed him in 628 CE; then the turmoils ran on Persia's throne until 'Umar's conquests — Khosrow's days closed the stable Sasanian kingship {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For younger visitors
Khosrow is Persia's king in the Prophet's time; he tore his letter, and the Prophet's saying on the fall of his rule came — he was killed soon after.

## Notes for researchers
'Kisra' is the Persian kings' title, like 'Caesar' for Rome — in the Prophet's reports it means Parviz, not Khosrow Anushirvan; keep the title and the person apart.
