# Enver Pasha

## Summary
İsmail Enver Pasha (23 November 1881 – 4 August 1922) was an Ottoman military officer and politician, Minister of War from 1914 to 1918 and, with Talaat and Cemal, one of the "Three Pashas" who ruled the Ottoman Empire during the First World War. The English article calls him a convicted war criminal and one of the principal perpetrators of the Armenian, Greek and Assyrian genocides. {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-en}

## Hero of the revolution
He was born in Constantinople, the son of the bridge-keeper and later public-works official Ahmed Bey and Ayşe Dilara Hanım, and graduated from the Ottoman Military Academy in 1902 — a classmate of Mustafa Kemal. Serving in Macedonia against insurgent bands, he joined the Committee of Union and Progress through his uncle Halil Kut and became the main figure of its Monastir branch. In July 1908 he took to the mountains with Ahmed Niyazi and forced Sultan Abdul Hamid II to restore the constitution; the two were hailed as "heroes of freedom", and Enver was remembered in the March of the Deputies. As military attaché in Berlin he formed ties with the German command; he fought in Libya in 1911–1912, stormed the Sublime Porte in the coup of 23 January 1913, retook Adrianople (Edirne) in the Second Balkan War, and on 3 January 1914 became Minister of War and Chief of the General Staff, marrying the princess Naciye Sultan weeks later and so entering the imperial family as a damat. {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-en} {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-tr}

## War minister
Enver was an architect of the Ottoman–German alliance and of the empire's entry into the war. His one field command was the Caucasus offensive that ended at Sarikamish, where much of his 90,000-strong army froze or was killed in the winter of 1914–1915; the article recounts that on his return he blamed his Armenian soldiers, and later initiated the deportations and sporadic massacres of Western Armenians that culminated in the Armenian genocide. In 1918 his Army of Islam, under his half-brother Nuri Pasha, took Baku. Dismissed on 4 October 1918, he escaped from Istanbul on the night of 1–2 November with the other Unionist leaders, and the Ottoman Military Tribunal sentenced him to death in absentia for bringing the empire into the war and organising massacres against Greeks and Armenians. {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-en}

## Central Asia and death
From Berlin and Moscow he tried to aid the Turkish national movement while Mustafa Kemal blocked his return. Sent to Bukhara in November 1921 to help suppress the Basmachi revolt, he instead defected to the Basmachi and united them under his command for a pan-Turkic offensive against the Bolsheviks. On 4 August 1922 a Red Army cavalry brigade surprised his headquarters near Ab-i-Derya, by the village of Chaghan in present-day Tajikistan, and he was killed. First buried there, his remains were brought to Turkey in 1996 and reburied at the Monument of Liberty cemetery in Istanbul, where President Süleyman Demirel praised his contributions to Turkish nationalism. His birth date is not uniform in the sources: both articles give 23 November 1881, Wikidata gives 22 November, and the Turkish article also records 6 December 1882 from his autobiographies; the 23 November reading is followed and the date marked debated. {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-en} {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-tr} {cite:wikidata-enver-pasha}

## The question of his faith
The English article states no religion for him, and its filing category "Turkish Islamists" describes a political movement, not a personal affiliation; his pan-Islamic appeals and his exile seal styling him "Commander-in-Chief of all the Armies of Islam" were political instruments. The Turkish article's infobox gives Islam generically. Wikidata alone names a school — Sunni Islam — and does so as a single structured value with one URL reference. Under the museum's two-source rule, which requires two independent sources naming a school for the person personally, his faith is recorded as unknown. {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-en} {cite:wikipedia-enver-pasha-tr} {cite:wikidata-enver-pasha}
