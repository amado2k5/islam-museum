## Summary

Fadwa Tuqan (1917–2003) was a Palestinian poet from Nablus, a leading figure of women's and resistance poetry in Arabic, nicknamed 'the Poet of Palestine'.{cite:wikipedia-fadwa-tuqan}

## Life

Born into a literary Nablus family (her brother was the poet Ibrahim Tuqan), she was educated at home after being barred from school; her brother taught her poetry and she later studied at Oxford. She lived occupation, exile and intifadas in Nablus.{cite:wikipedia-fadwa-tuqan}

## Poetry and memoir

Her collections include Alone with the Days, The Night and the Horsemen, Confusion and Tears and The Fighter and the Land, with poems on homeland, prisoners and women; her memoirs A Mountainous Journey (1985) and The Harder Journey document Palestinian life and her poetic experience.{cite:wikipedia-fadwa-tuqan}

## Standing

She is described as the foremost Palestinian female poetic voice, won international prizes, and died in Nablus in 2003.{cite:wikipedia-fadwa-tuqan}
