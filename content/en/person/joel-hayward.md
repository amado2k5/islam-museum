## Summary
Joel Hayward is a New Zealand-born British historian and former Royal Air Force academic whose scholarship spans air power, strategy and the biography of the Prophet Muhammad. A convert to Islam since 2005, he writes from within the tradition while remaining a professionally trained historian. {cite:wikipedia-joel-hayward}

## Life and formation
Born in Christchurch in 1964, Hayward studied at the University of Canterbury, earning a PhD in history in 1996 with a dissertation on the eastern front of the Second World War. Early in his career a master's thesis of his became the object of public controversy in New Zealand: it was accused of Holocaust denial, the university convened a working party, and Hayward apologised, distancing himself from denial; the museum records the episode as reported, without verdict. He converted to Islam in 2005. {cite:wikipedia-joel-hayward}

## Career
Hayward taught strategy and operational art at the Royal Air Force College Cranwell, serving as head of air power studies and dean of the college, and later held professorial posts at Khalifa University in the United Arab Emirates. He has advised military audiences on air warfare and taught international security studies. {cite:wikipedia-joel-hayward}

## Contributions and legacy
His early books include Stopped at Stalingrad (1998) on the Luftwaffe and Stalingrad and For God and Glory on Admiral Nelson. After his conversion he turned to Islamic military history and ethics: Warfare in the Quran (2012), The Leadership of Muhammad (2012), a study of the Prophet as commander and statesman, Prophet of War or Prophet of Peace? (2019), and the verse epic The Warrior Prophet: Muhammad and War (2022). In these works he argues for an ethical, restrained reading of the Prophet's campaigns, engaging both Muslim apologetics and hostile polemics. He is a fellow of the Royal Historical Society and appears in the Muslim 500; he has also written fiction and poetry with Islamic themes. {cite:wikipedia-joel-hayward}
