## Summary
Muhammad Shaybani Khan (c. 1451 – 2 December 1510) was an Uzbek leader who consolidated the Uzbek tribes and laid the foundations of their rise in Transoxiana, founding the Shaybanid dynasty of the Khanate of Bukhara. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} He was a descendant of Shiban, a son of Jochi, and thus of Genghis Khan. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-ar}

## Family and rise
He was the son of Shah Budag (Budaq Sultan) and a grandson of the Uzbek conqueror Abu'l-Khayr Khan; his full name is given as Abu'l-Fath Muhammad Khan Shaybani, and he was also known as Shakhibek Khan. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} {cite:wikidata-muhammad-shaybani} Orphaned young, he and his brother were hunted by his grandfather's enemies across the Qipchaq steppe before he rose as a war-leader. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-ar} He first served the Timurid ruler of Samarkand, then won Turkistan from the Moghul khan in 1488, and turned to conquering Samarkand and Bukhara. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-ur}

## Conquests
Continuing his grandfather's policies, he ousted the Timurids from Samarkand in 1500, took Bukhara in 1501 and established the Shaybanid dynasty, fought Babur over Samarkand, and in 1507 took Herat, the Timurids' southern capital. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} His armies raided the Kazakh steppe in 1508–09 but were heavily defeated by Kasym Khan's Kazakhs in 1510. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} He maintained ties with the Ottoman Empire and Ming China and aligned with Sultan Bayezid II against the Safavid Shah Ismail I. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en}

## Religion and writings
Shaybani was a declared champion of Sunni Islam against Safavid Iran; the English article describes Ismail as angered by his "staunch support of Sunni Islam". {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} He patronised scholars such as the Yemeni Mir-i Arab, wrote a prose essay, the Risale-yi maarif-i Shaybani (1507), and a religious-philosophical work, Bahr ul-Huda (1508), both in Chagatai Turkic. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} The Arabic article's infobox names his madhhab as Hanafi, an attestation his record preserves without settling on it. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-ar}

## Death
In 1510 Ismail I invaded Khorasan; Shaybani, short of troops, shut himself in Merv and was lured out by a feigned retreat. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} In the Battle of Marv his army was destroyed and he was killed, on 2 December 1510. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-shaybani-en} {cite:wikidata-muhammad-shaybani}
