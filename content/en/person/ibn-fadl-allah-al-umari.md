## Overview
Shihab al-Din Ahmad ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari (700–749 AH), secretary of the royal secrets in the chancery of the Mamluk state. Among his works are *Masalik al-Absar fi Mamalik al-Amsar* (Paths of Vision through the Realms of the Cities) and *al-Ta'rif bi al-Mustalah al-Sharif* (An Introduction to the Noble Terminology). {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.14}

## His book on the routes
In the introduction to [[work/masalik-al-absar|Masalik al-Absar]] he apologises that he was not accused in Tihama nor more deeply versed in Iraq. His book is therefore a gathering from books and from the speech of informants, not from first-hand observation. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.14}

The Gregorian dates of his birth and death are derived from the Hijri dates, and are not stated in the source.

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Ahmad ibn Yahya Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari, Shihab al-Din: born on 3 Shawwal 700, he studied with Ibn Taymiyya among others and served in the chancery at Cairo and Damascus. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/331} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/332} He fell out with al-Nasir and was imprisoned, then released, and became confidential secretary at Damascus. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/332} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/333} His works include Masalik al-Absar in more than twenty volumes and al-Ta'rif bi-l-Mustalah al-Sharif. He died of fever on the Day of Arafa, 749. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/333}
