## Overview
Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Muhammad al-Qurashi al-Hashimi, Baha' al-Din Ibn Aqil, was one of the leading grammarians and a descendant of Aqil ibn Abi Talib; he was born and died in Cairo. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 694 AH, 1294 CE, and his death as 769 AH, 1367 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 96}.

## Origin and nisba
Some of his forebears lived in Hamadan or Amid, and perhaps moved from one of them to Egypt, where Abd Allah was born. His biographers therefore called him al-Hamadhani or al-Amidi, then al-Balisi, then al-Misri {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 96}.

## Life and character
Al-Zirikli quotes Ibn Hayyan as saying that nobody under the sky was a better grammarian than Ibn Aqil. He was dignified and held himself above mixing with people, yet his gathering was never without visitors; he was generous, gave much to his students, and had a lisp. He held the judgeship of Egypt for a short time {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 96}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists: his commentary on the Alfiyya of Ibn Malik, on grammar, printed and widely used, translated into German together with the Alfiyya; al-Ta'liq al-wajiz ala al-kitab al-aziz, an exegesis he did not finish; al-Jami' al-nafis in Shafi'i law, very extensive and unfinished; al-Musa'id, a commentary on al-Tashil in grammar, in manuscript; and Taysir al-isti'dad li-rutbat al-ijtihad, in manuscript, an abridgement of al-Jami' al-nafis; he adds that there are others {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 96}.
