## Overview
Ahmad ibn Ja'far al-Khazraji, Abu al-Abbas al-Sabti al-Marrakushi, was a Sufi. He was born at Ceuta and moved to Marrakesh, where his fame rose and people spoke of his reports. He was eloquent and a fluent speaker, and often urged giving alms. The printed text gives his birth as 524 AH and his death as 601 AH, that is 1130 and 1204 CE; the card keeps 1129 for his birth because 524 AH begins in late 1129 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}.

## His books
Al-Zirikli says the 'Zayiraja', a method for drawing out hidden things, is ascribed to him, and that 'Nuzhat al-Khatir fi Ikhraj al-Damair' is ascribed to him, in manuscript in the Rabat library (no. 41 / 2 K). He adds that his chroniclers differed about him: some regard him as a saint (wali), some call him an innovator, some say he was a sorcerer and some declare him an unbeliever. The author of 'al-I'lam bi-man Halla Marrakesh' gave his life in about a hundred pages. These are the judgements of those who made them; the museum gives none {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}.
