## Overview
The hafiz Shams al-Din Abu al-Khayr Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Sakhawi. His family came from Sakha west of Fustat; he was born in Cairo in Rabi' I 831 in the lane of Baha' al-Din 'Allu, a Shafi'i, and settled in the two holy cities. He died in Medina on Sunday 28 Sha'ban 902 and was buried in al-Baqi' beside the shrine of Imam Malik. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 431-433} {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 8/2}

## Learning
He memorised the Qur'an as a child and led tarawih with it, then memorised Umdat al-Ahkam, al-Tanbih, al-Minhaj, Ibn Malik's Alfiyya, al-Iraqi's Alfiyya, most of the Shatibiyya and Ibn Hajar's Nukhbat al-Fikar, presenting each text to his shaykhs as he finished it. His first hearing of hadith from [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] was at night with his father in 838; from 846 he wrote in the master's dictation circle until Ibn Hajar's death in 852 — his house adjoined the shaykh's, so he missed almost nothing. He took from more than four hundred teachers and was licensed by several to give fatwa, teach and dictate. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 8/2-7} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 431}

## Travels and pilgrimages
He neither travelled nor made the hajj before Ibn Hajar's death. His first pilgrimage came in 870 by sea with his mother and family: in Mecca he read inside the Ka'ba, on the caves of Thawr and Hira', at Mina and Masjid al-Khayf before more than thirty shaykhs, and on the return read facing the Prophetic Chamber in Medina. He then made the Syrian rihla to Aleppo, hearing near a hundred masters on the way through Gaza, Jerusalem, Nablus, Damascus, Homs, Hama and Tripoli. He performed the hajj again in 875, sojourning two years; in 892, sojourning two more; and in 896, staying until mid-898 before settling in Medina until his death, the people of both sanctuaries and their visitors transmitting from him. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 8/7-9, 14} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 947}

## His works
He began composing before he was fifty. Among his books: [[work/al-jawahir-wa-al-durar|al-Jawahir wa'l-Durar]], the biography of Ibn Hajar; [[work/fath-al-mughith|Fath al-Mughith]], the commentary on the Alfiyya of hadith that Ibn al-Imad calls unmatched for completeness; [[work/al-daw-al-lami|al-Daw' al-Lami']], the multi-volume dictionary of the ninth century in which — after the hadith masters' custom — he wrote his own entry; al-Maqasid al-Hasana on the current hadiths; al-Qawl al-Badi' on blessing the Prophet; and [[work/al-ilan-bil-tawbikh|al-I'lan bil-Tawbikh]], his defence of the craft of history. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 431-432} {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 8/15-16} {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 524}

## Dates
The museum's birth and death dates are derived conversions from the Hijri record; the Encyclopaedia of Islam gives his birth as Rabi' I 830 instead. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 946}
