Rashid Khalidi (born 18 November 1948) is a Palestinian-American historian, born in New York City, a son of Ismail Khalidi, a Palestinian who worked for the United Nations.{cite:wikipedia-rashid-khalidi-en} He read at Yale (BA 1970) and Oxford (DPhil 1974), taught at the American University of Beirut, the University of Chicago and Georgetown, and in 2003 became the Edward Said Professor of Modern Arab Studies at Columbia University, retiring in October 2024.{cite:wikipedia-rashid-khalidi-en}{cite:wikidata-rashid-khalidi}

He edited the Journal of Palestine Studies from 2002 to 2020 and served as president of the Middle East Studies Association. His Palestinian Identity: The Construction of Modern National Consciousness (1997) won the Albert Hourani Book Award, and The Hundred Years' War on Palestine (2020) carried his account of the conflict to a wide readership.{cite:wikipedia-rashid-khalidi-en} No source attests a personal faith or school for him — Wikidata's structured value gives Islam only — and his record accordingly leaves it unknown.{cite:wikidata-rashid-khalidi}
