Nafija Sarajlić (née Hadžikarić; 3 October 1893 – 15 January 1970) was a Bosnian writer, the first prose writer among Bosnian Muslim women.{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-en}{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-bs} Born in Sarajevo into a craftsman's family, she was one of five daughters whom her father Avdija educated to become teachers — a decision that for months drew confrontation and thrown stones when he entered the Baščaršija — and she herself taught for three years in Sarajevo.{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-en}{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-bs} Her first story, Rastanak, appeared in the Mostar magazine Zeman in 1912, and the rest of her stories, which she called Teme (Themes), appeared in Biser with the support of its editor Musa Ćazim Ćatić.{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-en}

Between 1912 and 1918 she published some twenty-three stories on the displacement of Bosniaks in the First World War, superstition, poverty and women's emancipation, and she was active in the Sarajevo association Osvitanje, which pursued the same cause.{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-en}{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-bs} At eighteen she married the writer Šemsudin Sarajlić and withdrew from literary life; her collection Teme reached print only in an improvised edition in 1922 and in full in 1986.{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-bs} She died in Sarajevo on 15 January 1970, when the writer Alija Isaković remarked that "the first prose writer among Muslim women had died" almost unnoticed; a Sarajevo primary school and streets in Sarajevo and Bihać now bear her name.{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-en}{cite:wikipedia-nafija-sarajlic-bs}{cite:wikidata-nafija-sarajlic}
