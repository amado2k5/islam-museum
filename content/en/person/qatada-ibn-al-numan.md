## Overview
Qatada ibn al-Nu'man ibn Zayd ibn 'Amir ibn Sawad ibn Ka'b — Ka'b being Zafar ibn al-Khazraj ibn 'Amr ibn Malik ibn al-Aws — al-Zafari al-Ansari, of [[group/banu-zafar-al-aws|Banu Zafar]], was known as Abu 'Amr, or Abu 'Umar, or Abu 'Abd Allah. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 616} He was the half-brother through their mother of [[person/abu-said-al-khudri|Abu Sa'id al-Khudri]], and, al-Suhayli notes, the man whom the Prophet heard reciting "Say: He is God, One" over and over, and said, "It has become due." {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 32-33}

## Campaigns
He was present at al-'Aqaba, at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and at all the campaigns; he carried the banner of Banu Zafar at [[event/conquest-of-mecca|the conquest]], and was one of the most distinguished of the Ansar. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 616}

## His eye
Accounts differ on when his eye was struck: at Badr, at the Trench, or at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]]; Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says the soundest is that it was at Uhud. The eyeball fell onto his cheek and they wanted to cut it off; then they brought him to the Prophet, who pressed the eye back into place with his hand and said, "O God, clothe it with beauty," and it became the finer of his two eyes and never ailed afterwards. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 616} In one report he told the Prophet, "I have a wife whom I love, and if she sees my eye I fear she will find me repulsive," and the Prophet put it back and it was set right, becoming the stronger and sounder of his eyes. The editor notes that Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Abi Shayba give it without its Companion link, and that it is reported by several routes, none free of weakness, which together strengthen the core of the story. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 616}

Al-Suhayli gives the report from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, and tells how a descendant of his came before [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz]], who asked who he was; the man answered with two verses saying he was the son of the man whose eye ran down his cheek and was restored by the Chosen One's hand, and 'Umar rewarded him generously. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 33} He also cites a version in which both eyes fell out and the Prophet restored them, which al-Daraqutni reports from Malik, calling it gharib, transmitted only by 'Ammar ibn Nasr, who is trustworthy. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 33-34}

## Death
He died in 23 AH, or 24, aged sixty-five; [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|'Umar ibn al-Khattab]] led his funeral prayer and his half-brother Abu Sa'id al-Khudri went down into his grave. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 616}
