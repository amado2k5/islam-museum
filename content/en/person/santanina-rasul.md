## Summary

Santanina Centi Tillah-Rasul ⁦(14 September 1930 – 28 November 2024)⁩ was a Filipina Muslim educator and politician: the first Muslim woman elected to the Senate of the Philippines, a provincial board member in Sulu, a literacy pioneer through her Magbassa Kita Foundation, and a participant in the peace work behind the 1996 settlement in Mindanao. {cite:wikipedia-santanina-rasul} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 232}

## Early life and education

Born in Siasi, in the Sulu archipelago of the southern Philippines, she studied at the University of the Philippines Diliman, taking a bachelor's degree, and later trained at the National Defense College. She married Abraham A. Rasul and raised six children while building a career in education and public service. {cite:wikipedia-santanina-rasul}

## Public service

She served on the Sulu Provincial Board from 1971 to 1976, in the years when the Moro conflict was reshaping the south. In 1987, in the first Senate elected after the fall of Marcos, she won a seat — the first Muslim woman ever to sit in the chamber — and served two terms to 1995, working on education, literacy and the concerns of the Bangsamoro. {cite:wikipedia-santanina-rasul}

## Literacy and peace work

Outside electoral politics she founded the Magbassa Kita Foundation — 'let us read' — which ran literacy programmes for Muslim communities long underserved by public schooling. Her Muslim 500 obituary records her participation in the peace negotiations that produced the 1996 agreement ending the MNLF war, the south's first durable settlement. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 232} {cite:wikipedia-santanina-rasul}

## Death and legacy

She died in Quezon City on 28 November 2024, aged 94. Her legacy in the record is double: a parliamentary first for Muslim women in a Catholic-majority republic, and a quieter decades-long project teaching the marginalised to read — the combination that made her, in the obituaries, a grandmother figure of Bangsamoro public life. {cite:wikipedia-santanina-rasul} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 232}
