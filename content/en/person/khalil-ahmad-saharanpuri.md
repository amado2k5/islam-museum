## Summary
Indian Deobandi hadith scholar (1852–1927), a founder of the Deobandi hadith school and its Shaykh al-Hadith. His works include the fourteen-volume commentary Badhl al-Majhud on Sunan Abi Dawud, Al-Muhannad 'ala al-Mufannad refuting objections on ritual details, and Itmam al-Ni'am. {cite:wikipedia-khalil-ahmad-saharanpuri}

## Life and career

Khalil Ahmad Saharanpuri was born in 1852 in Ambheta (Ambahta), India; after religious studies he entered Darul Uloom Deoband in its early years and trained under its founders. {cite:wikipedia-khalil-ahmad-saharanpuri}

He held the chair of hadith at Deoband, looked to hadith and its people across the whole subcontinent, and travelled repeatedly to the Hijaz for hajj, meeting the scholars of the two sanctuaries. {cite:wikipedia-khalil-ahmad-saharanpuri}

## Works

His most famous work is Badhl al-Majhud fi Hall Abi Dawud, an encyclopedic Arabic commentary on Sunan Abi Dawud in fourteen volumes, combining hadith commentary with a methodological defence of Hanafi fiqh, completed after his death by his students. {cite:wikipedia-khalil-ahmad-saharanpuri}

Other works include Al-Muhannad 'ala al-Mufannad, answering other schools' objections on prayer rulings, Itmam al-Ni'am, and treatises and letters in hadith and creed. {cite:wikipedia-khalil-ahmad-saharanpuri}

## Legacy

Saharanpuri is remembered as the master of Deoband's teachers; his students included Anwar Shah Kashmiri, Muhammad Zakariyya Kandhlawi, Abd al-Rahman Kamalpuri and others who led the subcontinent's hadith schools. {cite:wikipedia-khalil-ahmad-saharanpuri}

He died in Medina in 1927 and was buried in al-Baqi'; Badhl al-Majhud remains among the widest and most circulated commentaries on the Sunan in the libraries of the subcontinent and the Arab world. {cite:wikipedia-khalil-ahmad-saharanpuri}
