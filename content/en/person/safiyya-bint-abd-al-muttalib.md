## Summary
[[person/safiyya-bint-abd-al-muttalib|Safiyya]] bint [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]], aunt of [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]], mother of [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] one of the Ten, and full sister of [[person/hamza|Hamza]] {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}.

## Her lineage and marriages
Her mother is Hala bint Wahb, the Prophet's maternal aunt. First al-Harith ibn Harb ibn Umayya married her and died; then al-Awwam ibn Khuwaylid married her and she bore him al-Zubayr and al-Sa'ib {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}. She became Muslim, narrated, and lived into Umar's caliphate per Abu Umar; she emigrated with her son al-Zubayr {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}.

## The day of the Trench
Umm Urwa bint Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr relates from her grandmother Safiyya that when the Prophet went out to the Trench he placed his women in a fort called Fari' with Hassan ibn Thabit among them. A Jew came and climbed the fort until he overlooked them; Safiyya asked Hassan to kill him and he said he was not capable of it — so she went down to him, struck him with a tent-pole until she killed him, and threw his head down on the Jews at the fort's foot. They said: "we knew this one would not leave his own people's quarters unguarded" — and they scattered {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}. Ibn Hajar notes that Ibn Ishaq related it from Yahya ibn Abbad from his father, and that she was "the first woman to kill a man of the polytheists" in Yunus's account from Hisham from Urwa {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}.

## The day of Uhud
Hisham relates from his father that Safiyya came on the day of Uhud when the people had fled, spear in hand, striking at their faces — and the Prophet said to al-Zubayr: "the woman" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}.

## After the Prophet's death
Al-Tabarani relates that when the Prophet passed away she went out trailing her wrap, reciting a verse in grief; Ibn Hajar mentions that Ibn Ishaq cited verses of hers mourning the Prophet {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}. Ibn Sa'd said she died in Umar's caliphate {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1911}.
