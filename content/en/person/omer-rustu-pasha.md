# Ömer Rüştü Pasha

## Summary
Ömer Rüşdü Pasha (1843 – 23 July 1922) was an Ottoman field marshal from Kütahya who served in the army for over half a century. He commanded the Fifth Army at Damascus and the reserve forces in the Greco-Turkish War of 1897, became chief of the general staff, and in 1908 was made the first Minister of War after the new ministry replaced the Seraskerlik; he later sat in the Senate. {cite:wikipedia-omer-rustu-pasha-tr}

No English Wikipedia article exists for him; this record rests on the Turkish article and on Wikidata, which agree on his birth in 1843 at Kütahya and his death in 1922 at Istanbul. {cite:wikipedia-omer-rustu-pasha-tr} {cite:wikidata-omer-rustu-pasha}

## Career
Ömer Rüşdü, whose family was nicknamed Mekkelizâde after an ancestor's long stay in Mecca, entered the War Academy in 1860 and graduated as a staff captain in 1866. He campaigned in Montenegro, Bulgaria, Bosnia and Serbia, became chief of staff of the War College at Edirne in 1884, and after commanding the Tripoli division was promoted field marshal and given the Fifth Army at Damascus for four years. In 1897 he commanded first a division and then the reserve forces of the Alasonya army, earning a gold sword inscribed with the title Gazi. {cite:wikipedia-omer-rustu-pasha-tr}

## Minister and senator
He was chief of the general staff in 1903 and again in 1905. When the constitutional revolution of 1908 abolished the Seraskerlik and created the Ministry of War, he became its first minister in Said Halim Pasha's government, though he left at the first cabinet change; he then sat in the Senate and in 1920 in the second Council of the Sultanate, where he voted to accept the Treaty of Sèvres. He died at Istanbul on 23 July 1922 and was buried in Maçka Cemetery. {cite:wikipedia-omer-rustu-pasha-tr}
