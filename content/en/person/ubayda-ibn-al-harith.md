## Summary
Ubayda ibn al-Harith ibn al-Muttalib ibn Abd Manaf al-Hashimi, a Muhajir Companion, the eldest of the three duelists on the day of [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 216}.

## In the duel
When three of Quraysh's chiefs called for duels — Utba and Shayba, sons of Rabi'a, and al-Walid ibn Utba — and sent back the Ansaris demanding their equals of their own cousins, the Prophet ordered Ubayda, Hamza and Ali to stand {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 216}. In Ibn Ishaq's account Ubayda — the eldest of them — duelled Utba ibn Rabi'a; in Ahmad and Abu Dawud's account Ubayda duelled al-Walid {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 217} {cite:mishkat-al-masabih}.

Two blows passed between him and his match, each disabling the other; Ali and Hamza returned upon Utba, killed him, and carried Ubayda away, his leg severed {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 217}.

## His death
He remained silent until he died [[place/al-safra|at al-Safra']] four or five days after the battle of Badr, the Muslims on their way to Medina {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 217}.
