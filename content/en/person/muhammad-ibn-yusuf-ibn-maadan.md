## Summary
Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Ma'dan ibn Sulaym, called "the bride of the ascetics", ranked among the first in virtue, knowledge and worship. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 662, sequence 663, on pages 389 and 390. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/389} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/390}

## His renunciation and death
The notice says he was mentioned before, and so were his two brothers Abd al-Rahman and Abd al-Aziz. He died at Massisa and was buried beside Mukhallad ibn al-Husayn; he left his estate in asceticism. He used to say: whoever has the world as his share from God has lost. He often recited a line whose sense is that escape from the house of humiliation is in avoiding it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/390}

## Second notice: printed number 686

Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Ma'dan al-Sufi, known as al-Banna, a memorizer of prophetic reports who followed them, and author of works on the asceticism of the knowers and the conduct of those who act; he was a leader in the science of Sufism. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (printed number 686, pages 402 to 404), in which he says that knowledge is obtained from creatures while benefit is obtained only from God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/402}

## The stations of the heart
Abu Nu'aym reports his teaching that the hearts of the knowers stand in four stations: a heart with God, whose sign is intimate conversation and occupation with Him; a heart in God's dominion, passing between Paradise, the Fire, the bridge, the reckoning, the scales and the gathering; a heart in discernment; and a heart in struggle, which turns back Satan's fear of poverty while it is busy correcting a major fault. A fifth station is mentioned, but its wording is not clear in the scan. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/402}

## The causes of knowledge
He is reported to have said that the causes of knowledge are four: keenness of mind, nobility of understanding, companionship with people of experience, and intense care. These lead to mercy, and the nearest route to mercy is to disown one's own power and strength. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/402}

## Beneficial knowledge
He said that the best of things is knowledge, that the purpose of knowledge is its benefit, and that knowledge which does not benefit is worse to carry than an ember of fire. The notice notes a prophetic report in which the Prophet sought refuge from knowledge that does not benefit; its wording is not given here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/402}

## Remembrance and care of the heart
He said that the hearts of the knowers are dwellings of remembrance, that the best deeds are care of the heart, and that remembrance is the food of the heart. He added that success includes leaving regret for what has passed and concern for what is to come, and that one who wishes for quick blessings should often converse with God in solitude. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/403}

## Reports from Ibn Umar and Anas
Ahmad ibn Ishaq relates from him a series of reports, including one from Ibn Umar that a person with a bequest should not let two nights pass without having it written; one from Ibn Umar that a servant who advises his master and worships his Lord well has a double reward; one from Ibn Umar forbidding the killing of the snakes found in houses; and two from Anas, one urging charity as a ransom from the Fire and one about the stinginess of people. None of these wordings is given here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/403}
