## Summary
Aktham ibn Sayfi al-Tamimi, known to the literary tradition as the sage of the Arabs in the Jahiliyya and one of the long-lived (mu'ammarun); he lived into Islam, set out for Medina with a hundred of his people seeking Islam, and died on the road about 630 CE without meeting the Prophet {cite:nihayat-al-arab-nuwayri-vol3-scan p.11}.

## In Nihayat al-Arab
Al-Nuwayri's marginal notice describes Aktham ibn Sayfi as the sage of the pre-Islamic Arabs and one of the mu'ammarun: he lived into the Islamic era and headed for Medina with a hundred of his people who wished to become Muslim, dying on the way without seeing the Prophet {cite:nihayat-al-arab-nuwayri-vol3-scan p.11}. The book quotes a number of his proverbial sayings, among them "If you wrong, beware vengeance, for wrong gains you nothing but its like" and "Prudence is thinking ill of people" {cite:nihayat-al-arab-nuwayri-vol3-scan p.11} {cite:nihayat-al-arab-nuwayri-vol3-scan p.15}.

## For young visitors
Aktham ibn Sayfi was a famous pre-Islamic Arab sage whose wisdom became proverbial; he set out for Medina to embrace Islam but died on the road.
