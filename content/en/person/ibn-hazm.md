## Overview
The hafiz Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Hazm al-Andalusi, imam of the Zahiri school. Author of [[work/tawq-al-hamama|The Ring of the Dove]] and *al-Fasl fi al-Milal wa al-Ahwa' wa al-Nihal* (The Decisive Account on Religions, Sects and Schools). {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari}

## Origin and birth
Ibn Ghalib says that the Banu Hazm are a house other than the one from which Ibn Hazm the Zahiri came, and that they were of Persian origin. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.327}

He was born in Córdoba on the last day of Ramadan in 384 AH, and died in the village of Mant Lusham in Sha'ban 456 AH. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.80-81}

## His doctrine and learning
Ibn Hayyan and others said that he was a man of hadith, jurisprudence and disputation, and that he wrote books on logic and philosophy. He was first a Shafi'i and then became a Zahiri, and he remained so until he died. Ibn Hayyan says that the jurists reviled him, and that the kings drove him away. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.80-81}

Sa'id says in his history that he gathered together the learning of Islam more fully than any other man in al-Andalus. His son al-Fadl relates that about four hundred volumes in his father's hand were gathered at his home. Al-Ghazali said that he found a book by him on the names of God, which shows the greatness of his memory. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.80-81}

## His works
Haji Khalifa attributes to him [[work/al-ihkam-fi-usul-al-ahkam|al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam]] (The Firm Principles of the Rulings). {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan}
