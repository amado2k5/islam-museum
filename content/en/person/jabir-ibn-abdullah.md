## Summary
[[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abdullah]] ibn Amr ibn Haram al-Ansari al-Sulami, kunya Abu Abdullah — also said Abu Abd al-Rahman, also said Abu Muhammad; one of the prolific narrators from the Prophet {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}. His father is [[person/abdullah-ibn-amr-ibn-haram|Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Haram]]; both he and his father had companionship {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}.

## Aqaba and the campaigns
In the Sahih he was among those who witnessed Aqaba {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}; his name recurs in the [[event/pledges-of-aqaba|pledges of Aqaba]]. Bukhari in his Tarikh relates with a sound chain from Jabir: "I used to pour water for my companions on the day of Badr" — al-Waqidi denied this account from Jabir {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}.

Jabir says the Messenger of God campaigned twenty-one expeditions himself, of which he witnessed nineteen {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}. In Muslim: "I witnessed neither Badr nor Uhud — my father forbade me; when he was killed I never stayed behind again" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}. And from him: "the Messenger of God asked forgiveness for me twenty-five times on the night of the Camel" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}.

## The study circle
In Waki's Musannaf from Hisham ibn Urwa: Jabir ibn Abdullah had a circle in the Mosque — meaning the Prophet's — from whom knowledge was taken {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}.

## His last days and death
Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada relates that Jabir came to them after his sight had gone {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}. Some say he was the last Companion to die in Medina, but al-Baghawi says that is an error and the last was Sahl ibn Sa'd {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}. Yahya ibn Bukayr said he died in year seventy-eight; Ibn Hajar says al-Haytham ibn Adi's view — that he died in year seventy-four — agrees with al-Tabari and Bukhari's Tarikh, which have al-Hajjaj present at his funeral; it is also said year seventy-three, and it is said he lived ninety-four years {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 196}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] relates from Sahih al-Bukhari the story of Jabir's meal at [[event/battle-of-the-trench|the Trench]]: Jabir saw a stone tied to the Prophet's belly and asked leave to go home; he told his wife he had seen hunger in the Prophet, and she had a sa' of barley and a small kid; she ground the barley, he slaughtered the kid and set the pot on the fire, and she urged him not to shame her and to bring the Prophet with two or three men only. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:09:30} Jabir whispered the invitation, but the Prophet called out to all the people of the Trench, told him not to bake the dough or take down the pot until he came, and when he came he blessed them; Jabir swore that they ate until they turned away, a thousand men, and the pot and the dough were as they had been. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:11:30}

He relates the hadith of Jabir's camel, saying many narrated it from Jabir, among them al-Sha'bi, Salim ibn Abi al-Ja'd, Abu al-Mutawakkil al-Naji, Abu al-Zubayr, Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir and Zayd ibn Aslam, and that it is in the two Sahihs. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:14:30} Jabir's camel tired on a journey and he was left behind alone; the Prophet used to ride at the rear of the people urging on the weak, caught up with him, struck the camel and it rose, until it began to outpace the Prophet's own she-camel. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:17:00} The Prophet then asked why he was in a hurry; he said he had recently married a woman previously married, and when asked why not a virgin, Jabir said his father Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn Haram had left nine inexperienced daughters, and he did not want to bring them one like them but a woman who would care for them; the Prophet told him he had done right. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:21:00} He says his father told him on the eve of [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] that he expected to be among the first killed, charged him with paying his debt and caring for his sisters, and was killed the next day. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:19:00} Al-Heweny sees in Jabir, a young man of about twenty, one who put his sisters before his own wish. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:19:30}
