## Summary
Thierno Sulayman Bal (c. 1720–1775/1776) was a Torodbe scholar of Futa Toro, in the Senegal valley, who led the revolution that ended the Denyanke kingdom and founded the Islamic Imamate of Futa Toro — and then refused to be its imam. He is remembered in Senegal as a warrior-scholar who fought slavery and caste, and for the charge he left his people on how an imam must be held to account. {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-en} {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-fr}

## Life
He was born about 1720 at Bode in Futa Toro. He studied the Qur'an widely — at Cayor, in Mauritania, in Futa Jallon, at Pir and in Bundu — and the jihad of Futa Jallon under Alfa Ibrahima Nuhu showed him the model of a clerics' revolution. {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-fr} {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-en}

In 1770 he led the revolt of the Torodbe against the Denyanke kingdom and its Brakna Moor backers, who had long dominated and raided Futa Toro. An assembly of Torodbe leaders announced the deposition of Sule Bubu Gaissiri, the last Denyanke king, and the introduction of a theocracy headed by an elected imam — but Bal refused the title for himself. Under his brief rule the caste regime was ended and slavery abolished, and men of the lowest castes became Torodbe through learning. {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-en} {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-fr}

## His charge to Futa
Feeling his end near, he left the people of Futa instructions that the French article preserves: depose any imam whose fortune you see grow, and confiscate his goods; fight and expel him if he persists; never let the imamate become a hereditary kingship in which sons succeed fathers; the imam may be chosen from any tribe; choose always a learned and hardworking man; never confine the choice to one tribe; let aptitude be the only criterion. {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-fr}

## Death and succession
He was killed fighting the Moors — the narratives place his death in or just after the campaigning of 1776, while the date lines of the English and Urdu articles and his Wikidata item give 1775. The museum records 1775 under the status debated, with the 1776 reading stated in full; the conflict is in the sources themselves and is not resolved here. {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-en} {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-ur} {cite:wikidata-sulayman-bal}

He was succeeded by Abd al-Qadir Kane, who consolidated the Futa Toro state and took the title almami. {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-en}

## Faith
No source consulted names a legal school or a Sufi order for Bal himself. He was a scholar of the Torodbe clerical tradition, formed in the Qur'anic schools of the Senegal valley and Mauritania; Wikidata gives his religion as Islam only, and his faith is recorded as unattested. {cite:wikipedia-sulayman-bal-en} {cite:wikidata-sulayman-bal}
