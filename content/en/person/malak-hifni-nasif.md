## Summary
Egyptian essayist and thinker (1886–1918), known by her pen name 'Bahithat al-Badiya' (Seeker in the Desert), a pioneer of the women's emancipation movement in Egypt. She wrote in the newspaper Al-Jarida on girls' education, women's rights and social reform, collected in Al-Nisa'iyyat (Feminist Pieces), and founded the Union for Women's Education. {cite:wikipedia-malak-hifni-nasif}

## Life and career

Malak Hifni Nasif was born in Cairo in 1886 into the family of a judge and intellectual, Hifni Nasif; she graduated from the Saniyya girls' school and then its teacher-training section, and worked as a teacher in government schools. {cite:wikipedia-malak-hifni-nasif}

She had to leave teaching after marrying a tribal leader in Fayoum and living years in the desert — whence her pen name — then returned to Cairo to write and work in public life. {cite:wikipedia-malak-hifni-nasif}

## Writings

She published her articles in the liberal newspaper Al-Jarida; collected in Al-Nisa'iyyat (1910), they treated girls' education, women's work, marriage, veiling and divorce — she demanded the right to education and work while defending the veil as social protection, a complex position that sparked debate. {cite:wikipedia-malak-hifni-nasif}

She addressed the Umma Party congress in 1911 with ten feminist demands, founded the Union for Women's Education, and exchanged essays with thinkers including Qasim Amin, answering some of his arguments. {cite:wikipedia-malak-hifni-nasif}

## Legacy

Malak died in 1918 at 32 in the world influenza epidemic; Huda Sha'arawi eulogised her at a memorial attended by Cairo's elite, and a street in her district was named for her. {cite:wikipedia-malak-hifni-nasif}

In the history of Egyptian feminist thought she is a founding link: she proved a Muslim woman could mount an independent public discourse within her cultural frame, and Al-Nisa'iyyat is still read and taught. {cite:wikipedia-malak-hifni-nasif}
