## Overview
Abu Abd al-Rahman Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla al-Ansari, judge of Kufa and one of its jurists. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p.45}

## Dates
Al-Shirazi says that he was born in 74 AH and died in 148 AH, at the age of seventy-two. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p.45} The figures do not agree with the years of birth and death, and need a second source.

## Teachers and pupils
He learned jurisprudence from [[person/al-shaabi|al-Sha'bi]] and [[person/al-hakam-ibn-utayba|al-Hakam ibn Utayba]], and from him took Sufyan al-Thawri and al-Hasan ibn Salih ibn Hayy. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p.45}

Al-Shirazi relates a saying of Sufyan al-Thawri: "Our jurists are Ibn Abi Layla and Ibn Shubruma." {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p.45}

## In Kitab al-Umm

Ibn Abi Layla is one of the two Kufan jurists whose disagreements [[person/abu-yusuf-al-qadi|Abu Yusuf]] collected and [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] transmitted in [[work/ikhtilaf-al-iraqiyyin|the Disagreement of the Iraqis]] in al-Umm, titled "the book of what Abu Hanifa and Ibn Abi Layla disagreed on, from Abu Yusuf". {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.217} Among his views at its opening: if the tailor and the owner of the cloth disagree, the owner saying "I told you a shirt" and the tailor "you told me a coat", the tailor's word stands, against Abu Hanifa; and if cloth is lost with the tailor, fuller or dyer, they are liable even if their hands did nothing to it. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.217} See [[concept/tadmin-al-sunna|liability of craftsmen]].

## In Mizan al-I'tidal
[[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] writes in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]]: a truthful imam of poor memory, and he has been declared trustworthy; he narrated from al-Sha'bi, Ata and [[person/al-hakam-ibn-utayba|al-Hakam]], and Shu'ba, Waki' and Abu Nu'aym narrated from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 613} He gives his death as 148 AH, confirming the year recorded from another source. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 616}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: law and truthfulness**: al-Ijli said he was a truthful jurist, a man of the Sunna whose hadith is acceptable, a Qur'an reader and scholar with whom [[person/hamza-al-zayyat|Hamza al-Zayyat]] read; Ahmad ibn Yunus said he was the greatest jurist in the world, and Za'ida, asked about him, said: the greatest jurist among people; Mansur said the greatest jurist of Kufa was its judge Ibn Abi Layla. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 613} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 614} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 615}
- **Sunni: poor memory**: Shu'ba said: I never saw worse memory than his; al-Qattan said very poor memory; Ahmad said inconsistent in hadith; Abu Zur'a said not of the strongest; Ibn Ma'in said not that good; al-Nasa'i said not strong; al-Daraqutni said bad memory and many errors; Abu Ahmad al-Hakim said most of his hadiths are inverted; and Za'ida discarded his hadith; Ibn Hibban said he had bad memory and gross errors, so that objectionable reports multiplied in his hadith and he deserved to be abandoned — Ahmad and Yahya abandoned him — and noted that Yusuf ibn Umar appointed him judge of Kufa. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 614} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 616}
:::

**The judge.** Bishr ibn al-Walid reported [[person/abu-yusuf-al-qadi|Abu Yusuf]] saying: no one who held the judgeship was more learned in God's religion, a better reader of God's Book, more outspoken for the truth or more abstinent from wealth than Ibn Abi Layla; asked about [[person/ibn-shubruma|Ibn Shubruma]], he said: that is a man of many words. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 614} Bishr adds that when Abu Yusuf examined the judgements of Hafs ibn Ghiyath he remarked: this is from the judgements of Ibn Abi Layla. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 614} Among his reports with [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata]]: he visited him and Ata began questioning him; when his companions objected, Ata said: he knows more than I. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 615}

**Among his narrations.** Al-Dhahabi cites hadiths of his, among them one al-Thawri and others relate from him from al-Hakam from Miqsam from Ibn Abbas about the body of a man killed at the Trench which the polytheists wished to buy; al-Tirmidhi deemed it fair, while Abd al-Haqq and Ibn al-Qattan weakened it for the break between al-Hakam and Miqsam and for Ibn Abi Layla — and al-Dhahabi says al-Tirmidhi's view is preferable. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 615}
