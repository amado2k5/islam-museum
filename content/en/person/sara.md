## Overview
Sarah, wife of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]]. The introduction of al-Raheeq al-Makhtum mentions her in the origin of the Arabized Arabs {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18}.

## In Egypt
Ibrahim once came to [[place/misr|Egypt]], and the Pharaoh of Egypt attempted a scheme and an evil against his wife Sarah. But God turned his scheme back upon his own throat. The Pharaoh came to know of Sarah's strong bond with God, and out of recognition of her merit he gave her [[person/hajar|Hajar]] to serve her. Sarah then married her to Ibrahim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/474} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/34}. In a footnote the author notes that it is commonly held that Hajar was a slave-woman, but the judge Muhammad Sulayman al-Mansurfuri established that she was free and was the daughter of the Pharaoh. The matter is among those on which opinions differ {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/36-37}.

## Her jealousy of Hajar
Ibrahim returned to [[place/palestine|Palestine]], and God granted him [[person/ismail|Isma'il]] by Hajar. Sarah became jealous, to the point that she compelled Ibrahim to take Hajar and her small son out to the Hijaz, and he settled them beside the Sacred House {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/474-475}. The details are in [[event/settlement-of-hajar-and-ismail|the settlement of Hajar and Isma'il in Mecca]].

## Not covered here
These pages mention the birth of Isaac and the good tidings of him only in passing, with a reference to the Book of Genesis for the order of the story {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19}.

## For young visitors
Sarah was the wife of the Prophet Ibrahim. She lived with him in Palestine, and Hajar, the mother of Isma'il, had been her servant before she became Ibrahim's wife.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh says that Sara, 'the noble Syrian lady of good lineage', bore Ibrahim no child until both were old, and that after Hajar bore Isma'il, Sara then bore Ishaq, which he sees as part of God's decrees. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:03:00}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the twelfth episode of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]] to the two wives of Abraham, Sara and Hajar. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:01:00} He says "many reports" make Sara Abraham's cousin, married to him at sixteen, and that she believed in him and travelled with him from Iraq to Palestine and al-Sham. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:01:30} In Egypt a tyrant king heard of her beauty and had her brought; Abraham called her his sister, meaning in faith, and each time the king reached for her his hand froze, until he set her free with gifts and gave her a slave woman named Hajar. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:06:00} He holds that her name is properly pronounced to mean "she who delights". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:06:00}

Seeing Abraham's longing for a child, he says, Sara gave him Hajar, who bore Isma'il. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:08:00} Later the angels sent to Lot's people stopped first at Abraham's house, and he served them a roasted calf they did not touch. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:25:00} He takes Sara's standing there, serving the guests, as a model of a wife sharing her husband's hospitality. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:25:30} The good news they brought was threefold, in his reading: Isaac, Jacob after him, and her living to see her grandson. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:26:30} He gives their ages then as a hundred and twenty for Abraham and ninety for Sara. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:21:00} He closes with her astonishment at bearing a child so old, and with the hope that today's women follow Sara and Hajar. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:29:00}
