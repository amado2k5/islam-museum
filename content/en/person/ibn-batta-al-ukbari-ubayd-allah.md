## Summary
Abu Abd Allah Ubayd Allah ibn Muhammad Ibn Batta al-Ukbari was a Hanbali hadith scholar and jurist. He was born in [[place/ukbara|Ukbara]] near Baghdad in 304 AH, died in 387 AH, and wrote *[[work/ibana-al-kubra-ibn-batta|al-Ibana al-Kubra]]* on the creed of the hadith scholars. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 25} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 27}

## Name and lineage
Ibn Abi Ya'la gives his lineage in *Tabaqat al-Hanabila* as Ubayd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Hamdan ibn Umar ibn Isa ibn Ibrahim ibn Sa'd ibn Utba ibn Farqad, Utba ibn Farqad being a Companion. According to the Ibana editor's study, most biographers give only one or two of his grandfathers. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 25} The editor considers al-Ulaymi's naming of him "Abd Allah" in *al-Manhaj al-Ahmad* an error, since the biographers agree that he was "Ubayd Allah" with the kunya Abu Abd Allah. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 25} "Ibn Batta", with the *b* vowelled *a*, was the nickname of one of his forebears. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 25}

## Birth and upbringing
He was born in 304 AH. The editor quotes Ibn al-Jawzi's *al-Muntazam* as giving Monday, 4 Shawwal of that year. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 25} Ukbara was a small town on the Tigris, five parasangs above Baghdad. He grew up in his father's care; his father loved learning and the learned, and sent him to Baghdad before he was ten. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 25}

## Travels and seclusion
Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi says he travelled often to Basra and Syria, and Ibn al-Imad adds Mecca and the frontier posts. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 26} He then kept to his house for the rest of his life, preferring seclusion. He held no office under the ruler and devoted himself to writing and teaching. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 26} The editor notes that this seclusion did not cut him off from people. He enjoined what is right, and, according to Ibn Abi Ya'la, held a Friday class in the mosque of Ukbara and taught in the Mosque of al-Mansur in Baghdad. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 26}

## Teachers and students
His teachers included [[person/ahmad-ibn-salman-al-najjad|Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Salman al-Najjad]], the leading Hanbali of Iraq, and [[person/umar-ibn-husayn-al-khiraqi|Abu al-Qasim al-Khiraqi]]. They also included Abu Bakr Abd Allah ibn Ziyad al-Naysaburi, the leading Shafi'i of his day, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Baghandi, [[person/al-ajurri|al-Ajurri]] and Ibn Sa'id. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 27} Among his students were al-Hasan ibn Shihab al-Ukbari, Abu Hafs al-Ukbari, Abu Ishaq al-Barmaki, [[person/ibn-hamid|Ibn Hamid]] and al-Qati'i. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 27} His student Ibn Shihab al-Ukbari mourned him in an elegy. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 28}

## Standing in the school
The editor counts him among the senior Hanbali scholars of his time. He notes that Ibn Batta held his own choices in Hanbali law, some of which al-Mardawi records in *al-Insaf*. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 27} In the editor's view, his closeness to Baghdad strengthens his reports from the circle of Imam Ahmad. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 58}

## Works
His works include *al-Ibana 'an Shari'at al-Firqa al-Najiya*, known as the larger Ibana, and *al-Sharh wa-al-Ibana 'ala Usul al-Sunna wa-al-Diyana*, known as the smaller. There is also a treatise on invalidating legal stratagems (*Ibtal al-Hiyal*), published by Muhammad Hamid al-Fiqi. The editor adds treatises on law and conduct that remain in manuscript, among them on the prohibition of tale-bearing, congregational prayer, the dower becoming due through private seclusion, a reply to those who say a triple divorce does not take effect, the censure of singing, and seclusion. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 27} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 32}

## Death
He died on the day of Ashura, 387 AH. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 27}

## Not covered here
Among the biographical sources the editor lists are two books of narrator criticism, al-Dhahabi's *Mizan al-I'tidal* and Ibn Hajar's *Lisan al-Mizan*; what they say about him has not yet been read in those books themselves. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 28}
