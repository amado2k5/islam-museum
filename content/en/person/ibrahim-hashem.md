Ibrahim Hashem (1886 – 14 July 1958) was a Jordanian politician and judge from Nablus who took his law degree at Istanbul in 1910, served the Ottoman administration as a prosecutor and judge at Beirut and Jaffa, joined the secret al-Fatat society during the First World War, and taught penal law at Damascus after it.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hashem-en}{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hashem-ar} In Transjordan he was repeatedly minister of justice from 1922, and in October 1933 formed the first of his five governments.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hashem-ar}

He was the inaugural prime minister of independent Jordan in 1946, sat on the three-man Throne Council during King Hussein's minority, presided over the Senate from 1951 to 1955, and formed his last government in April 1957.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hashem-en}{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hashem-ar} As deputy head of the short-lived Arab Federation he travelled to Baghdad in July 1958, where a mob killed him outside the Iraqi Ministry of Defence on 14 July, during the coup that destroyed the Iraqi monarchy; his body was never recovered.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hashem-en}{cite:wikidata-ibrahim-hashem}
