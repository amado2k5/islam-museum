## Overview
Pedro I ruled Castile from 1350 until he was killed in 1369. The Encyclopaedia of Islam has no article on him; he appears in its article on the Nasrids of Granada, where his wars set the frame for the reigns of [[person/yusuf-i-nasrid|Yusuf I]] and [[person/muhammad-v-nasrid|Muhammad V]], and in the article on Ibn Khaldun, who went to his court. The Encyclopaedia calls him "Pedro the Cruel" in the Ibn Khaldun article. This record gathers those passages. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 858}

## Accession and Granada
The Nasrids article tells that Alfonso XI broke a truce with Granada in 1349 with an attempt to take Gibraltar and, like many of his troops, died of the Black Death in March 1350. His son Pedro I then gladly reached an accord with Yusuf I. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078} Granada's good relations with Castile, and its readiness to shelter Marinid rebel princes, gradually soured the friendship with Fez. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078} Under Yusuf's son Muhammad V, who agreed to pay tribute to Pedro, relations with Castile were good from the start. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078}

## Ibn Khaldun in Seville
The article on [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] says that at the end of 765/1364 the historian, then in Granada, was sent to Seville on a delicate peace mission to Pedro the Cruel. It says the contact with the Christian world, then changing, had an important influence on him. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 858} The museum records this as [[event/ibn-khaldun-embassy-seville-1365|the embassy to Seville]]; its date there is c. 1365 from another source, so the two dates are close but not identical.

## War with Aragon and the fate of Muhammad VI
The Nasrids article says that in 1359 the Castilian fleet, reinforced with Granadan galleys, was granted port facilities at Malaga as Muhammad V prepared to move against Aragon, before a palace revolution in Ramadan 760/August 1359 removed him. His successor Muhammad VI, called el Bermejo, aligned himself with Aragon and stopped the tribute to Castile. Pedro, after beating the Aragonese at Nájera in 1360 and concluding the Treaty of Terrer in May 1361, turned on him, having helped Muhammad V return from Fez and set up a government at Ronda. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078} In 763/1362 the alliance took fortress after fortress, and Muhammad VI fled to Pedro and threw himself on his mercy; Pedro had him put to death at Tablada, not far from Seville. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078} Muhammad V began his second reign on 20 Jumada II 763/16 March 1362. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078}

## Civil war and death
Pedro was then at war with rebels led by his half-brother Enrique of Trastamara, who was backed by Pedro IV of Aragon. Muhammad V, loyal to Castile, sent cavalry that took part in the conquest of Teruel in 1363, but Pedro had to fall back on his beloved Seville in 1364. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078} Enrique, with mercenaries recruited in France under Bertrand du Guesclin, entered Spain through Catalonia and was proclaimed Enrique II of Castile on 16 March 1366; Pedro, branded a "Moor-lover" and no longer welcome even in Seville, fled his country. Muhammad then shifted his allegiance to Enrique and concluded a peace with Aragon in Rajab 768/March 1367. Later in 1367 Pedro returned with the help of the Black Prince, and Muhammad, reversing course again, joined forces with Castile and raided Jaén, Priego, Úbeda and Baeza, but failed to take Cordova in the spring of 1368. By the end of March 1369 Pedro was dead; the article calls Enrique II his conqueror and assassin. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1078}

## Dates and doubts
Wikidata gives 30 August 1334 and 23 March 1369 in the Julian calendar. The Encyclopaedia is the source for the sequence of events above, but its wording on Muhammad's shifting alliances is compressed, and the order in 1367 should be checked against the Castilian chronicles before this record is promoted. The designation "Moor-lover" is the article's report of how his enemies branded him, not a judgement of the museum.
