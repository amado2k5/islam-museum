## Overview

Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Maqqari al-Tilimsani al-Fasi, a hafiz, historian and transmitter of hadith, author of *Nafh al-Tib min Ghusn al-Andalus al-Ratib*. He died in Egypt in 1041 AH, and was buried in the cemetery of the mujawirin, according to the biography of al-Kattani that the editor of the book reproduces, who rejected reports that he died in Damascus.{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.1 pp.5–6}

## Death and burial

The biography by al-Kattani in *Fihris al-Fahares*, which the editor reproduces in his introduction, notes that some biographers said he died in Damascus, and calls this a clear error, since many historians of Damascus and Egypt record his death and burial in Egypt. His pupil Abd al-Baqi al-Hanbali says that he had resolved to settle in Damascus and had gone to bring his family from Egypt, when death overtook him in Egypt before he left.{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.1 p.5}

## Teachers

His Tlemcen teachers included his paternal uncle Abu Uthman Sa'id al-Maqqari, mufti of Tlemcen, and his high chain of transmission also runs through Abu al-Abbas ibn al-Qadi and Ahmad Baba al-Sudani. From the East he received from Abu al-Hasan al-Ajhuri, Abd al-Ra'uf al-Manawi and al-Najm al-Ghazzi. He transmitted the six books of hadith from his uncle Sa'id.{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.1 p.6}

## Works

He wrote *Fath al-Muta'al*, on the Prophet's sandals, in Medina, and *Zahr al-Kamama*, on the Prophet's turban. He also wrote a work on the Prophetic names, *Nafh al-Tib*, *Azhar al-Riyad* and a *Tarikh Dimashq*.{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.1 p.6}

## Reputation

Abu Salim al-Ayyashi called him the hafiz of the Maghrib, and al-Qadiri wrote that no one in his time was more retentive. Abd al-Baqi al-Hanbali said no one had written a better book than *Nafh al-Tib*. The judge Ibn al-Hajj, in *Riyad al-Ward*, described the book as evidence of his scholarship, and dismissed the claim of one who reported that he was not reliable.{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.1 pp.6–7}
