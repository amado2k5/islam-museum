## Summary

Malik Kafur (died 1316) was the slave-general and viceroy of Alauddin Khalji, sultan of Delhi {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}. The English article gives no birth year for him, and none is built in this record {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}. He was of Hindu descent and converted to Islam after his capture in 1299, a conversion the Urdu article also states {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en} {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-ur}. Because no text names a legal school or an order for him, his faith is recorded as unknown, with the conversion stated in full {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}.

## The thousand-dinar slave

He was a eunuch slave of a Khwaja of Khambhat, said to have been bought for a thousand dinars, from which came his epithet Hazar-Dinari {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}. During the Delhi army's invasion of Gujarat in 1299, Nusrat Khan captured him at Khambhat {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-ar}. He was presented to Alauddin at Delhi and rose in the sultan's service {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}.

## The southern campaigns

As Alauddin's commander he defeated the Mongol invaders in 1306 {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-ar}. He then led the sultanate's great expeditions into the south — against Devagiri in 1308, Warangal in 1310, Dwarasamudra in 1311 and the Pandya country in the far south {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}. He was made governor of Devagiri from 1313 to 1315 {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}.

## Viceroy and death

Recalled to Delhi, he became na'ib — viceroy — of the sultanate in 1315 {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}. Within weeks of Alauddin's death he was assassinated at Delhi by the late sultan's former bodyguards, about 11 February 1316 {cite:wikipedia-malik-kafur-en}.
