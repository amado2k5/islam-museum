## Overview
"Ali ibn Abi Jafar al-Alawi" is the man who, in the book, answers the poem of [[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-malik-ibn-ghitrif-al-wadii|the Wadi'i]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.288}.

## Detail
His poem runs from p. 288 to the top of p. 290, describing the abode, his turning to forbearance and courtesy and his clinging to al-Hadi, and addressing Ka'b of Hamdan as his partners in religion and merit {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.288} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.289} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.290}. He is called "al-Alawi", of the line of Ali, and the book gives no descent further back. The father's kunya "Abu Ja'far" is that of [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] in the book, so the poet is probably [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]]; but the book names two sons Ali for the man called Abu Ja'far, and the museum neither links the two records nor rules the link out.
