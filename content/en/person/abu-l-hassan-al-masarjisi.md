## Overview
Abu l-Hasan Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Sahl ibn Muslih al-Masarjisi, a Shafi'i jurist. Ibn Khallikan calls him one of the imams of the Shafi'is in Khurasan and the one most knowledgeable of the school, its arrangement and its branch rulings {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}. Ibn al-Imad calls him the shaykh of the Shafi'is and says he was from Nishapur {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 443}.

## His nisba
The nisba is to Masarjis, the forebear of Abu Ali al-Hasan ibn Isa ibn Masarjis al-Nishapuri; Masarjis was a Christian who accepted Islam at the hands of Abdallah ibn al-Mubarak, and the jurist Abu l-Hasan was the son of that Abu Ali's daughter, so the name passed to him, according to Ibn Khallikan {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}. Ibn al-Imad calls him the daughter's son of al-Hasan ibn Isa ibn Masarjis {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 443}.

## Study and travels
Ibn Khallikan says he studied jurisprudence in Khurasan, Iraq and the Hijaz, kept the company of Abu Ishaq al-Marwazi and studied under him, went with him to Egypt and stayed with him until his death, and then returned to Baghdad {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}. In Baghdad he took the sessions of Ibn Abi Hurayra after that scholar had left them {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}. Ibn al-Imad quotes al-Hakim that in Baghdad he became the repeater of the lessons of Abu Ali ibn Abi Hurayra {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 443}.

## Teachers
He heard from his maternal uncle al-Mu'ammal ibn al-Hasan ibn Isa al-Masarjisi, and in Egypt from the companions of al-Muzani and from Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la al-Sadafi {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}. Ibn al-Imad adds that he transmitted from Abu Hamid ibn al-Sharqi and his generation and wrote down much in Iraq, the Hijaz and Egypt {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 443}.

## Teaching in Nishapur
He went back to Khurasan in 344 AH and taught in Nishapur; its jurists took from him, and the qadi Abu l-Tayyib al-Tabari studied under him {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}. Ibn al-Athir calls him the teacher of Abu l-Tayyib al-Tabari in Nishapur {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 7 p. 464}. Ibn Khallikan quotes al-Hakim Abu Abdallah ibn al-Bayyi' that he himself arranged the dictation session for him at Dar al-Sunna in Rajab 381 AH {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}.

## What was said of him and reported from him
Ibn al-Imad quotes al-Hakim that he was the most knowledgeable of his colleagues in the school and its arrangement {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 443}. He adds that al-Rafi'i reported from him a preference for making the first rak'a longer than the second, and, in the chapter on blood money, a remark that he had seen a hunter shoot game at a distance of two farsakhs {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 444}.

## His son
Ibn al-Imad, summarising al-Isnawi, says he had a son named Muhammad, with the kunya Abu Bakr, who studied jurisprudence with his father, heard hadith in many lands, and died in Jumada I 389 AH at the age of thirty-four, buried in his house {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 444}.

## Death
Ibn Khallikan says he died on the evening of a Wednesday and was buried on the evening of Thursday, the sixth of Jumada II 384 AH, aged seventy-six, and adds that Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi in his Tabaqat gives 383 AH {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 4 p. 202}. Ibn al-Imad places his death in 384 AH and, quoting al-Isnawi, says he died in Khurasan on the Wednesday evening, was buried on Thursday evening, the sixth of Jumada II, at the age of seventy-six {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 pp. 443-444}. Ibn al-Athir, for his part, lists the death among the events of 383 AH {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 7 p. 464}.
