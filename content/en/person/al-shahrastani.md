## Overview
Abu'l-Fath Muhammad b. Abi'l-Qasim 'Abd al-Karim b. Abi Bakr Ahmad al-Shahrastani, mutakallim on al-Ash'ari's madhhab; an outstanding imam, jurist and theologian — he read fiqh under Ahmad al-Khwafi and Abu Nasr al-Qushayri, read kalam under Abu'l-Qasim al-Ansari and was singular in his art. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His works and entry to Baghdad
He wrote Nihayat al-iqdam fi 'ilm al-kalam, al-Milal wa'l-nihal, al-Mudara'a and Talkhis al-aqsam li-madhahib al-anam — full of memorisation, fine of discourse, preaching to the people; he entered Baghdad in 510, dwelt three years and won wide acceptance among the common folk. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam presents al-Shahrastani (died 1153) as a man of Khurasan, in Iran, who was a historian of religions as well as a scholar, philosopher and theologian, concerned mainly with religious and philosophical worldviews. It says he is most famous for the Kitab al-milal wa'l-nihal (The book of sects and systems of thought), which surveys the religious ideas of the Muslim sects and of other peoples worldwide. According to the Dictionary, he identifies himself in that work as an Ashari Sunni and is generally seen as one, but his sectarian affiliation has been disputed, which raises questions about how his contribution to the Muslim tradition is assessed. A handful of his contemporaries charged him with secret Ismaili allegiance, and some modern scholars agree, pointing, among other evidence, to resemblances between Ismaili doctrines and the view of the divine order set out in his commentary on the Qur'an. These are the Dictionary's reports of a dispute; they are recorded here without choosing among them, and the record above follows Ibn Khallikan in describing him as an Ashari. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2157}
