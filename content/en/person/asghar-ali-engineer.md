Asghar Ali Engineer (10 March 1939 – 14 May 2013) was an Indian reformist writer and social activist, internationally known for his work on liberation theology in Islam and as the leader of the Progressive Dawoodi Bohra movement.{cite:wikipedia-asghar-ali-engineer-en} Born Asghar Ali Sheikh Kurban at Salumbar in Rajasthan, the son of a Bohra priest, Shaikh Qurban Hussain, he was trained in Qur'anic tafsir, tawil, fiqh and hadith and learned Arabic, then graduated in civil engineering from Vikram University and served for twenty years in the Bombay Municipal Corporation.{cite:wikipedia-asghar-ali-engineer-en} From 1965 he published under the name "Asghar Ali Engineer", the professional name that stayed with him.{cite:wikipedia-asghar-ali-engineer-en}

In 1972 Engineer took voluntary retirement to devote himself to the Bohra reform movement that had risen in Udaipur, and in 1977 he was unanimously elected general secretary of the Central Board of Dawoodi Bohra Community; in 2004, after his criticism of the Dawoodi Bohra religious establishment, he was expelled.{cite:wikipedia-asghar-ali-engineer-en} He founded the Institute of Islamic Studies in Mumbai in 1980 and the Centre for Study of Society and Secularism in 1993, wrote more than fifty books on Islam, communalism and women's rights, and published his autobiography, A Living Faith, in 2011.{cite:wikipedia-asghar-ali-engineer-en} He received the Right Livelihood Award in 2004 and died in Mumbai on 14 May 2013.{cite:wikipedia-asghar-ali-engineer-en}{cite:wikidata-asghar-ali-engineer}
