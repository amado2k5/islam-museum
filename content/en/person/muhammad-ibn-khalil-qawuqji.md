## Overview
Muhammad ibn Khalil ibn Ibrahim, Abu al-Mahasin al-Qawuqji, was a scholar of hadith and a Hanafi jurist and researcher, from the people of Tripoli in Syria. The entry dates his life to 1224 to 1305 AH, equal to 1809 to 1888 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 23}.

## Life
He was born in Tripoli and received the first sciences there. In 1239 AH he travelled to Egypt, studied jurisprudence at al-Azhar and stayed twenty-seven years before returning to his town. He died on pilgrimage at Mecca. He was an eloquent preacher {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 23}.

## Standing
He was the musnid of the Levant in his time, the one holding the chains of transmission. Al-Zirikli quotes the author of Fihris al-Fahaaris as saying that in most of Egypt, the Levant and the Hijaz the reliance today rests on his chains {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 23}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli says he wrote about a hundred books. Among them he names: Ma'din al-La'ali fi al-Asanid al-Awali, in manuscript, a list in which he recorded his teachers; Rabi' al-Jinan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an; Raf' al-Astar al-Musdala fi al-Ahadith al-Musalsala, in manuscript; al-Maqasid al-Saniyya fi Adab al-Sufiyya; Ruh al-Bayan fi Khawass al-Nabat wa al-Hayawan; al-Lu'lu' al-Marsu', printed, on fabricated hadiths; Tanwir al-Qulub wa al-Absar, on hadith; collections of pulpit sermons; a travel account gathering the curiosities of his journeys in Egypt, the Hijaz and the Levant; al-Dhahab al-Ibriz, printed, a commentary on al-Mu'jam al-Wajiz of al-Marghani; al-Jami' al-Fayyah li al-Kutub al-Thalatha al-Sihah, meaning the Muwatta, al-Bukhari and Muslim; al-Bahja al-Qudsiyya fi al-Ansab al-Nabawiyya; Kawakib al-Tarsif fima li al-Hanafiyya min al-Tasnif; Lata'if al-Raghibin, in manuscript, on the principles of hadith, theology and religion; Ghunyat al-Talibin min Ahkam al-Din, printed; Shawariq al-Anwar, in manuscript; Safinat al-Naja, printed, a treatise on jurisprudence; al-I'timad fi al-I'tiqad; and Tuhfat al-Muluk fi al-Sir wa al-Suluk {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 23}.

## Death
He died on pilgrimage at Mecca in 1305 AH, equal to 1888 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 23}.
