## Summary
[[person/umm-atiyya|Umm Atiyya]] al-Ansariyya; her name is Nusayba (with plain s and single b, diminutive) — or it is said with fatha on the n and kasra on the s — known by name and kunya {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2019}. She is bint al-Harith, or it is said bint Ka'b; Abu Umar denied that, for Nusayba bint Ka'b is Umm Umara {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}.

## Narration
She related from the Prophet and from Umar; from her related Anas, Muhammad and Hafsa — sons of Sirin — Isma'il ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Atiyya and Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}. Ibn Hajar mentions that her hadith on washing the Prophet's "aniyya" is famous in the Sahih — so in the printed text; perhaps a misprint for washing his daughter. He says a group of the Tabi'in scholars took that ruling from her, and Ibn Sirin took the washing rules from Umm Atiyya until he washed the dead himself {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}.

## Her hadiths in the two Sahihs
Among her hadiths: "the Messenger of God commanded that the young women and the screened ones come out on the two Eids"; "the Prophet took our pledge that we not wail"; "we used not to count the brownish and yellowish after purity as anything"; "we were forbidden to follow funerals, but it was not made binding on us" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}. And the hadith of the Prophet entering on A'isha asking "do you have anything?" — she said: nothing but something Nusayba sent us from the ewe sent to her of the charity — he said: "it has reached its place" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}.

## Campaigning
In Sahih Muslim, from her: "I campaigned with the Messenger of God in seven campaigns, staying behind with their baggage" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}.

## After the Prophet
In the Sahih, from Hafsa bint Sirin: Umm Atiyya came to Basra and lodged at the palace of Banu Khalaf {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}. Ibn Sa'd said of Umm Sharahil, freedwoman of Abu Atiyya: "Ali ibn Abi Talib used to nap at Umm Atiyya's, and I would pluck his armpit hair with her" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}.

## Distinguishing her from another
Ibn Hajar distinguishes her from "Umm Atiyya al-Ansariyya al-Khafida", whom Ibn Manda and al-Mustaghfiri give their own entry; he mentions that Abu Musa allowed that she is the same one {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2020}.
