# Adib Ishaq

## Summary
Adib Ishaq (21 January 1856 – 12 June 1885) was a Syrian writer, journalist and poet of Armenian origin, one of the brilliant short-lived talents of the Nahda. Schooled by the Lazarists in Damascus, he worked in customs houses and then in the press of Beirut and Egypt, became in Cairo a disciple of Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, founded the journal Misr al-Qahira in Paris in 1879, and died at twenty-nine at al-Hadath in Lebanon; his essays and poems were collected after his death as Al-Durar. {cite:wikipedia-adib-ishaq-en} {cite:wikipedia-adib-ishaq-ar}

## Life
He was born in Damascus to an Armenian family — his Armenian name was Adib Zalmatian — and was enrolled at the Lazarists' school, where he studied Arabic and French. Before he was twelve he left school to help support his family at the customs house, an experience that also made him proficient in Turkish. At fifteen he joined his father in Beirut to work at the post office, then at the Beirut customs house, but writing drew him to journalism and he contributed to al-Taqaddum. He moved to Egypt in 1876, and in Alexandria he worked with Salim al-Naqqash, newly arrived like him, on writing and staging plays; in Cairo he met Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, was deeply influenced by him, and founded the newspaper Misr for him in 1877. He held a senior secretarial post under the Urabi movement, left Egypt in 1881 as it disintegrated, and in Paris — where he had gone in 1880 — he published the Arabic journal Misr al-Qahira with the help of Abdallah Marrash, mixing with French men of letters including Victor Hugo, and was known there as the "prodigy of the East". Recalled to Cairo by Sharif Pasha, he headed the bureau of translation and composition and served as secretary of the Chamber of Deputies, before withdrawing from the revolutionary camp and writing against the Urabis in al-I'tidal. He returned to Beirut and died at his summer estate in al-Hadath. {cite:wikipedia-adib-ishaq-en} {cite:wikipedia-adib-ishaq-ar}

## Works and the date of his death
His works include Tarajim Misr fi hadha al-asr (Biographies of Egypt in this Age), the play al-Barisiyya al-hasna, Gharayib al-ittifaq, and translations of the plays Andromaque and Charlemagne; his collected essays and verse, Al-Durar, were published by Jirjis Mikha'il Nahhas in Alexandria in 1886, and again, edited by his brother Awni, in Beirut in 1909. His most quoted verses run: "To kill one man in a forest is a crime unforgivable; to kill a safe people is a matter open to debate" — four lines only are quoted here, as the museum's rule requires. The sources disagree about the year he died: the English article's lead gives 12 June 1885, citing Mustapha Khayati's study of him, and Wikidata agrees, while the same article's infobox and categories give 1884 and the Arabic article carries both years among its categories. His record follows 1885 and preserves the 1884 reading. {cite:wikipedia-adib-ishaq-en} {cite:wikipedia-adib-ishaq-ar} {cite:wikidata-adib-ishaq}
