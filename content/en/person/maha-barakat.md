## Summary
Dr Maha Taysir Barakat is an Emirati endocrinologist and health official who moved from clinical medicine to running a health system to health diplomacy. The Muslim 500 lists her in Science & Technology as Assistant Minister for Medical Affairs and Life Sciences at the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs — a post that puts a physician at the intersection of medicine and foreign policy. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}

## Medical training
Barakat trained in the United Kingdom in internal medicine and endocrinology — the specialty of hormones, metabolism and diabetes — and built her early career as a clinician and researcher, with particular attention to diabetes, one of the Gulf region's most prevalent public-health burdens. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}

## Leading Abu Dhabi's health system
She rose to director-general of the Health Authority of Abu Dhabi ⁦(HAAD)⁩, the emirate's health regulator, where she oversaw hospitals, licensing, public-health programmes and health-insurance reform for the capital's rapidly growing population — one of the most senior health posts in the UAE, and a landmark appointment for a woman physician in the Gulf. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}

## Health diplomacy
Barakat later moved into foreign affairs, serving at the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation as Assistant Minister for Medical Affairs and Life Sciences, carrying the country's health expertise into international policy — pandemic-era cooperation, humanitarian health and the life-sciences agenda — and representing the UAE in multilateral health forums. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}

## Recognition and boards
She has served on academic and advisory boards including a professorial role at Imperial College London's Abu Dhabi programme and seats on international health organisations, and her career has been cited in listings of influential Arab women in medicine and government. The Muslim 500 counts her among the year's influential Muslims in science and technology. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Barakat is the physician-diplomat of the Gulf's health institution-building: an endocrinologist who went from treating diabetes to regulating an emirate's hospitals to carrying health policy into a foreign ministry — medicine practised at the scale of a state. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}

## Prevention before the crisis
Barakat's public-health philosophy is recorded in her policy work: that the UAE's epidemic risk is a chronic-disease story before it is an infectious-disease story. The emirates' diabetes and obesity rates became her signature problem — and her signature argument was that the systems built to prevent them were the same systems that would matter when the next infection arrived. It was an argument that aged well; when COVID-19 arrived, Abu Dhabi's public-health apparatus — built partly on her watch — was among the region's most responsive. That vindication is the quiet record the Muslim 500 lists. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}

## The physician as policymaker
Barakat's record is the museum's example of the physician who moves from the clinic to the ministry: the doctor who understands that the diseases she treats are the symptoms of a system, and who takes the government post that lets her treat the system. The pattern — clinical expertise converted into public-health policy — is the one her Muslim 500 entry records; the museum keeps it as the profile of a woman who made the case for prevention in a region that was still learning to pay for it. {cite:muslim500-maha-barakat}
