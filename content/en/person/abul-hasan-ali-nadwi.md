## Summary

Abul Hasan Ali al-Nadwi (1914–1999) was an Indian Islamic scholar and thinker, rector of Nadwatul Ulama in Lucknow, among the most famous carriers of Indian Islamic thought to the Arab world through eloquent Arabic writing.{cite:wikipedia-abul-hasan-ali-nadwi}

## Life

Born in Takiyya Kalan in the Rae Bareli region to a scholarly family, he learned Arabic and the Quran early, graduated from Nadwatul Ulama, and served it as student, teacher then rector, helping found the World Assembly of Muslim Literature.{cite:wikipedia-abul-hasan-ali-nadwi}

## Works and method

His books include Madha Khasira al-'Alam bi-Inhitat al-Muslimin (1951), a civilisational diagnosis of Muslim decline, Al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, Al-Arkan al-Arba'a and Rijal al-Fikr wal-Da'wa fi al-Islam. His method joins the Deobandi-Nadwi heritage to Arab reform and world da'wa.{cite:wikipedia-abul-hasan-ali-nadwi}

## Standing

He is described as the greatest contemporary Indian writer in Arabic and a bridge between the Indian Islamic movement and the Arab world, and was a member of Arabic language academies and international Islamic institutions. He died in 1999; Nadwatul Ulama and his works continue.{cite:wikipedia-abul-hasan-ali-nadwi}
