## Overview
'Asim ibn Abi'l-Najud, the great imam and Reader of the age Abu Bakr al-Asadi, their client of Kufa; his father's name was Bahdala. He was born in Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan's governorship. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2097}

## Teaching
He read the Qur'an under Abu 'Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami and Zirr ibn Hubaysh al-Asadi, and narrated from them and from Abu Wa'il, Mus'ab ibn Sa'd and other senior Successors — he counts among the junior Successors. He presided over Qur'an teaching in Kufa for a time: Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash, Hafs ibn Sulayman, al-A'mash and many more read under him, and Shu'ba, al-Thawri, Hammad ibn Salama, Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna and others narrated from him. He died around 165 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2097}

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

He is 'Asim ibn Abi al-Najud ibn Bahdala al-Asadi al-Kufi, imam and reader of Kufa. He read on the blind Abu 'Abd al-Rahman [[person/abu-abd-al-rahman-al-sulami-al-muqri|al-Sulami]], on Abu Maryam [[person/zirr-ibn-hubaysh|Zirr ibn Hubaysh]] and on Abu 'Amr Sa'd ibn Ilyas al-Shaybani; those three read on [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud]], al-Sulami and Zirr also read on 'Uthman and 'Ali, and al-Sulami also on Ubayy and Zayd, and the chain reaches the Prophet. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 166} 'Asim died at the end of 127 AH, or in 128, and was the imam to whom the headship of teaching in Kufa passed after Abu 'Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami; Ibn al-Jazari says he combined fluency with exactness and good recitation, and that his voice for the Qur'an was the finest of the people. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 166} He quotes Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash hearing Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i say he had seen nobody read the Qur'an better than 'Asim, and 'Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal reporting that his father called him a righteous, reliable and good man. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 166} His two transmitters are [[person/shuba-abu-bakr-ibn-ayyash|Shu'ba]] and [[person/hafs-abu-umar-ibn-sulayman|Hafs]], and Ibn al-Jazari counts a hundred and twenty-eight lines of transmission. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 166}
