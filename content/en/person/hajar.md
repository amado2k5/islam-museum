## Summary
Hagar, mother of [[person/ismail|Isma'il]]. The Pharaoh of Egypt gave her in service to [[person/sara|Sara]], who then gave her in marriage to [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]]. Ibrahim settled her with her suckling child in the valley of [[place/mecca|Mecca]], where Zamzam burst forth for them, and she granted [[group/jurhum|Jurhum]] leave to settle {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18}.

## Her origin
The Pharaoh of Egypt recognised the strong bond Sara had with God, and so he gave Hagar to her in service as a mark of respect for her. Sara then gave her in marriage to Ibrahim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/474}. The author notes in a footnote that it is commonly said Hagar was a slave woman, but Judge Muhammad Sulayman al-Mansur Furi established that she was free and that she was the daughter of the Pharaoh {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/36-37}. Her origin is therefore a point of disagreement between the widely known report and al-Mansur Furi's finding.

## To the valley of Mecca
God granted Ibrahim Isma'il by Hagar, and Sara grew jealous until she forced Ibrahim to take Hagar and her small son away. He brought them to the Hijaz and settled them in a barren valley beside the Sacred House, placing them beneath a tree above Zamzam at the highest part of the mosque. No one was in Mecca at that time, and there was no water. He left with them a bag of dates and a waterskin, and returned to Palestine {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/474-475}. Within a few days the provisions and water ran out, and there the well of Zamzam burst forth and became their sustenance and their provision for a time. The full story is well known {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18}. See [[event/settlement-of-hajar-and-ismail|The settlement of Hagar and Isma'il in Mecca]].

## Jurhum
A Yemeni tribe, the second Jurhum, came and settled in Mecca with the permission of Isma'il's mother. The report of al-Bukhari states that they came down to Mecca after Ibrahim settled them there and before Isma'il grew up {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/474-475}.

## Her death
Isma'il's mother died after Isma'il had grown up and married among Jurhum, and before the second journey of Ibrahim that is recorded {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/475-476}.

## For young visitors
Hagar is the mother of Isma'il. She stayed in Mecca with her suckling child, and God gave them water from the well of Zamzam, which still stands.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh cites Hajar on trust in God: when her water ran low with her infant beside her, she ran between al-Safa and al-Marwa seven times, which for him is the taking of means, having already put her trust in God on the day she learned that being left there was God's command and said He would not let them perish. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep02 00:06:30} He observes that the water did not spring at al-Safa or at al-Marwa, and that she asked according to her measure and God gave according to His, so that there was Zamzam, from which pilgrims, visitors and the people of Mecca drink. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep02 00:07:30}
In another episode he mentions her with her infant in a barren valley as an example that good may lie in what one dislikes, since from Isma'il's line came the best of creation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep06 00:08:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh says Hajar was not a slave-girl as some history books say, but of a line of kings whose state had fallen, taken captive to Pharaoh's palace and given to Ibrahim in Egypt (needs_source: this is his claim). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:02:00} He relates that when Ibrahim left her with her infant at the House she asked him: did God command you to do this? When he said yes, she said: then He will not let us perish; he sees in this the true relief; then Zamzam sprang forth, Jurhum came, Isma'il married among them, and from his line came the Prophet. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep08 00:10:00}

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Ish al-Lahza'
[[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] mentions Hagar in the twenty-second episode of his programme [[work/ich-allahda|'Ish al-Lahza']], on the moment of confrontation, and makes her a model for every woman whose husband is away or has divorced her and who carries the role of both man and woman. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep22 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep22 00:24:00} He says God commanded Abraham to go on to Palestine and leave her and her infant in a place with no person and no water; she asked him whether God had commanded this, he nodded, and she was certain God would not abandon them; he recalls Abraham's prayer by the Sacred House. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep22 00:24:00} All the blessings of Mecca came, he says, because she bore that moment. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep22 00:24:30}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
In the twelfth episode of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]], [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] tells Hajar's story after Sara's. He says she was the slave woman the king of Egypt gave to Sara, who gave her to Abraham, and that Isma'il was born when Abraham was eighty-six. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:08:00} God then commanded Abraham to take her and the infant to Arabia, to the valley of Mecca, empty of buildings and people. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:09:00} When she asked where he was leaving them, he did not answer, and when she asked whether God had commanded it and he said yes, she trusted that God would not abandon them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:11:30}

When the water ran out and the baby cried, she climbed al-Safa looking for help, walked towards al-Marwa, running in the bottom of the valley between them, and turned back each time, unable to bear being far from the child, seven times in all. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:15:30} He points out that the pilgrims' running between the two hills preserves the memory of a woman. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:17:00} He relates that Gabriel struck the ground beside Isma'il and Zamzam welled up, and that Hajar walled the water in so it would not run off, which the Prophet recalled when he said it would otherwise have been a flowing spring. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:19:00} The Jurhum tribe, seeing birds circling, came and asked to settle, and the rent they paid for the water was her living. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep12 00:19:30}
