## Brief
Malik ibn Awf was the young commander who fielded Hawazin's whole confederation against the Muslims at Hunayn — and within weeks came to the Prophet at al-Ji'rana, accepted Islam, and led his people home.

## Life
After the conquest of Mecca, [[group/hawazin|Hawazin]] and Thaqif gathered against the new order; Malik — barely thirty in the sources — insisted on bringing the tribes' women, children and flocks to the field at [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]] so the men would fight to the end {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

Defeated, he withdrew into the fortress of Ta'if. Then, during the [[event/siege-of-taif|siege]], he came out to the Prophet at al-Ji'rana, accepted Islam, and was set over the Muslims of his own people — recovering their captives and property in the famous amnesty {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Hunayn's story is often told as the battle won and lost in a morning; Malik is its other half — the defeated commander whose conversion closed the war weeks later.

## For young visitors
Malik lost a battle to the Muslims — then joined them, and was given his people's captured families and flocks back.
