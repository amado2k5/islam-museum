# Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar

## Summary
Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar (23 March 1853 – January 1907) was the fifth shah of the Qajar dynasty, reigning from 1 May 1896 until his death. After thirty-five years as crown prince, mostly spent governing Azerbaijan, he inherited a state strained by debt and concessions; his reign is remembered for the D'Arcy oil concession of 1901 and, above all, for the constitution he granted in December 1906 under the pressure of the Constitutional Revolution, days before he died. {cite:wikipedia-mozaffar-ad-din-shah-qajar-en}

The day of his death is split across the sources: the current English article gives 8 January 1907, Wikidata carries 3, 6 and 9 January from different references, and his son's reign is dated from 3 January. This record follows 3 January, with the whole split stated. {cite:wikidata-mozaffar-ad-din-shah-qajar} {cite:wikipedia-mozaffar-ad-din-shah-qajar-en}

## Crown prince
He was born at Tehran, the fourth son of Naser al-Din Shah and Shokouh al-Saltaneh. Named crown prince in 1861, he governed Azerbaijan from Tabriz for most of the thirty-five years he waited for the throne — a long apprenticeship in the province that was the heir's customary appanage, during which his father toured Europe three times and was assassinated in 1896. {cite:wikipedia-mozaffar-ad-din-shah-qajar-en}

## Reign
His reign was marked by royal extravagance against inadequate revenue. To fund his own European journeys he took two large loans from Russia, and in 1901 he signed the D'Arcy concession, granting William Knox D'Arcy the oil rights over most of Persia for sixty years — the grant from which the country's oil industry descends. Public anger mounted as further concessions and monopolies were sold to European interests. {cite:wikipedia-mozaffar-ad-din-shah-qajar-en}

## The constitution and death
In 1905–06 the protests of merchants, clergy and reformers swelled into the Constitutional Revolution. Taken ill and pressed by a general strike and sanctuary-taking crowds, the Shah conceded a National Consultative Assembly in the summer of 1906 and signed the Fundamental Law at the end of December 1906; he died days later, of a heart attack, in Tehran, and was buried in the Imam Hussein Shrine at Karbala. The slogan Adl-e Mozaffar, "the Justice of Mozaffar", inscribed on the Baharestan Palace, honoured him for the grant. His son Mohammad Ali Shah succeeded him — and set about undoing his work. {cite:wikipedia-mozaffar-ad-din-shah-qajar-en} {cite:wikidata-mozaffar-ad-din-shah-qajar}
