## Overview
Muhammad ibn Kathir al-Farghani, author of "al-Fusul al-Thalathin". {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.519}

Al-Mas'udi counts him among those who engaged in the sciences of the heavens. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.519}

His death and the content of his book need an additional source.

## His book on the science of the spheres
The title page of the Latin edition printed at Amsterdam in 1669 names the author "Muhammad ibn Kathir al-Farghani," gives the book's Arabic title as "Jawami' 'ilm al-nujum wa-usul al-harakat al-samawiyya" (Elements of Astronomy and the Principles of the Heavenly Motions), and its Latin title as "Elementa astronomica" {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 5}. The index lists thirty chapters {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 10}, which agrees with al-Mas'udi's mention of the "Thirty Chapters" {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 519}.

## What the book shows about its author
The chapters show al-Farghani estimating the earth's circumference from a degree measured by the cubit in al-Ma'mun's days {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 100}, and counting the seven climes with Farghana placed in the fourth {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 93}. The pages read give no date of his death, which therefore remains unsourced from this book.

## Pages 389 to 549: Golius's notes
In his commentary Golius notes that Farghana is the author's homeland, and observes that it is no wonder the author mentions his homeland when occasion offers {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 411}. He defends the author's figures for the fourth clime, holding that the error attributed to him goes back to Abu'l-Fida's copy {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 413}.

## Pages 201 to 388: what Golius says of the author
Golius records that Ibn Kasta (al-Qifti) and the old Latin translation call the author "Ahmad," while the manuscript gives "Muhammad ibn Kathir," and that the two names belong to one person, styled "al-Hasib" for his skill in reckoning {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 246}. He places his time in al-Ma'mun's era, counting him among the astronomers of that age, and notes that the author mentions al-Ma'mun's observatories in chapters five and eight {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 246}. He also had a book on constructing the astrolabe, and Golius notes that the inclination of the sphere in it differs by between thirty-three and thirty-five minutes from the book before us {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 315}. Golius concedes that the date of his death is unknown and that he lived before Ibn Yunus — recorded here without a final judgement {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 246}.
