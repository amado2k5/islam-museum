## Overview
Al-Hasan ibn al-Sabbah al-Himyari, lord of the fortress of [[place/alamut|Alamut]] and leader of the Ismaili Batiniyya. Ibn al-Imad in "Shadharat al-Dhahab" reports that he died in 518 AH, and that his son was Abu al-Fath Nasr Allah. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.538}

## Ibn al-Imad's judgement of him
Ibn al-Imad, who was a Sunni historian, described him as "a cunning, deceitful heretic, one of the devils of mankind". This is a judgement attributed to Ibn al-Imad alone, and it is not the voice of the museum. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.538}

Not mentioned here: the account of the Ismailis themselves, and the academic view. Both need an additional source.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "A Story and an Idea"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the eleventh episode of his programme [[work/kissa-wa-fikra-program|"A Story and an Idea"]] to Hasan-i Sabbah, as the founder of a movement that specialised in assassination, which he calls a failure to answer ideas with ideas. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:01:30} What follows is his account; its sectarian descriptions are his and are attributed to him.

He says Hasan was born in 430 AH / 1037 CE in Qum and at seventeen moved with his family to Rayy, which al-Suwaidan calls the main eastern centre of the Isma'ilis. There he rose through the Isma'ili ranks and swore allegiance to the movement's chief in Iran and Iraq, Ibn Attash, who called him "the man of secret missions". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:03:00} He was sent to Egypt and stayed three years at the court of the Fatimid [[person/al-mustansir-billah|al-Mustansir]], where he clashed with the vizier [[person/badr-al-jamali|Badr al-Jamali]], who jailed him in Alexandria and expelled him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:04:30}

He reached Isfahan in 1081 and, al-Suwaidan says, was close to the vizier [[person/nizam-al-mulk|Nizam al-Mulk]], who did not know his beliefs. Nizam al-Mulk answered the Batini movements with ideas, founding the Nizamiyya schools and bringing al-Shirazi and [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|Abu Hamid al-Ghazali]] to teach in them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:06:30} For nine years Hasan preached in secret among peasants and Bedouin, avoiding the cities. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:08:30} When Badr al-Jamali's son al-Afdal put al-Musta'li on the throne and passed over the elder son, [[person/nizar-ibn-al-mustansir|Nizar]], the Isma'ilis split into Musta'lis and Nizaris; Hasan backed Nizar, and al-Suwaidan calls him the founder and eastern leader of the Nizaris. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:11:00}

He chose a fortress some two thousand metres high, reached by a single path wide enough for one person at a time: Alamut. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:14:30} Of two accounts of how he took it, al-Suwaidan prefers the one in which he bought it for three thousand gold dinars; Hasan never left it again, spending his time reading, recruiting and having killed the opponents he could not persuade. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:18:00} He relates the story, linked with Marco Polo, of a paradise-like garden where recruits drugged with hashish were made to believe they were in Paradise, and says it was invented by the movement's opponents, adding that one should not repeat a rival's rumours, "even about a criminal". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:16:00} In his view the name ran the other way: Europeans called them "assassins", and the Arabic "hashishin" followed, and it was never proven that they used hashish. This derivation is his claim. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:17:00}

In Ramadan 485 AH / 1092, he says, a fida'i disguised as a poor Sufi with a petition stabbed Nizam al-Mulk with a poisoned dagger, and the dying vizier pardoned his killer. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:20:00} Hasan's title was "the Old Man of the Mountain". He died in his fortress in 1124 without children and was succeeded by Buzurg Umid, leaving an autobiography and a work of theology. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:22:00} Al-Suwaidan traces to the movement the later killings of [[person/mawdud|Mawdud]] of Mosul in the mosque of Damascus, of the Abbasid caliph al-Mustarshid in his tent, and of the Fatimid caliph al-Amir in Cairo in 1130, and repeated attempts on Saladin. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:24:30} The Mongols under [[person/hulagu|Hulagu]] burned Alamut and its writings, and al-Zahir Baybars ended the movement in Syria in 1273. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:25:00} His lesson: thought drives behaviour, and disaster begins when a person hands his mind to someone who persuades him that the world is evil and that only total obedience leads to Paradise. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep11 00:27:00}
