## Overview
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Mukhtar al-Tijani, the shaykh and founder of the Tijaniyya order. He was born at Ain Madi in Algeria. The encyclopaedia says he was born around 1737 CE (1150 AH) and died around 1815 CE (1230 AH). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

Both dates are recorded as reported dates and need an additional source. The details of the order and its spread need an additional source.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
Ahmad al-Tijani (died 1815) is presented by The Oxford Dictionary of Islam as the Algerian who founded the Tijani Sufi order after first introducing a revived Khalwati order to the Maghreb. The entry recounts that in 1782 he had a vision of Muhammad in which he received a new, self-standing tariqah and told to break with all other orders and masters. His welcome was warm, it says, among opponents of popular Sufism, his order pairing strict legal observance with a refusal of ascetic withdrawal. The entry also reports his claim to the rank of khatm al-awliyah — making him, in that claim, the link between Muhammad and the saints of every age — and his promise of paradise without the surrender of wealth, which drew prosperous merchants and high officials to him. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2383}
