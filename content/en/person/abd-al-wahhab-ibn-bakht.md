## Overview
Abd al-Wahhab ibn Bakht, whom al-Safadi heads as the companion of al-Battal, was a freedman of the house of Marwan from the people of Mecca. He died in 113 AH, which corresponds to 731 CE by the museum's conversion, and Muslim and the four Sunan compilers took hadith from him {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 197}.

## Life
Al-Safadi says he left Medina to take part in raids and was much given to pilgrimage and the lesser pilgrimage {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 197}. He campaigned with al-Battal in 113 AH; when the troops gave way around al-Battal, Abd al-Wahhab threw his helmet off, shouted his own name, asked the Muslims whether they were fleeing from Paradise, then fought into the enemy's front and was killed {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 197}.

Ibn al-Athir tells the same event under the year 113 AH: he had raided the land of the Byzantines with Abd Allah al-Battal, the men broke and fled, and Abd al-Wahhab charged while speaking to his horse, then threw off his helmet, cried out and pressed into the enemy's front. Passing a man who called out that he was thirsty, he told him to go forward, for water lay ahead; he joined the fighting and was killed, and his horse with him {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 4 p. 403}.

## As a hadith transmitter
He narrated on the authority of Ibn Umar, Anas, Abu Hurayra, Nafi', the freedman of Ibn Umar, Abu al-Zinad, Ata ibn Abi Rabah and others {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 197}. Malik ibn Anas, Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani, Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Ansari, Hisham ibn Sa'd, Usama ibn Zayd al-Laythi and others narrated from him; al-Safadi calls him reliable, truthful and upright {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 197}.
