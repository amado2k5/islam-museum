## Summary
Ulubatlı Hasan was the standard-bearer of Sultan Mehmed II who, on the morning of 29 May 1453, climbed the walls of Constantinople among the first, planted the Ottoman banner on the tower of St Romanos (Topkapı), and held it there, struck by arrows, stones and spears, until his companions reached him and he collapsed with twenty-seven arrows in his body. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-en} Seeing the banner raised heartened the Ottoman troops and disheartened the defenders until the city fell. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-en} He has become one of the emblematic figures of the conquest in Turkish memory — played in the films İstanbul'un Fethi (1951) and Fetih 1453 (2012) and in the 2024 series Mehmed: Sultan of Conquests. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-en}

## A man or a legend?
For much of the twentieth century Hasan's existence itself was contested. The story rests on the Chronicon Maius ascribed to George Sphrantzes, the Byzantine emperor's chief guard; some Greek and Romanian historians argued from the 1930s that the long chronicle was an expansion by Makarios Melissenos, that Hasan is absent from Sphrantzes' original short chronicle, and that no one could reliably have recorded who first raised a banner amid the chaos of the assault. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-tr} The Turkish article sets out the counter-case built by the historian Hakan Yılmaz: the long chronicle's own colophon states that Sphrantzes expanded it with his own hand on 29 March 1478; a waqf deed of 828/1425, drawn up by Molla Fenari, styles Hasan the "planter of the banner of Islam"; his grave has been found in Istanbul's Fatih district; and a miniature of 1460, drawn from the accounts of two participants, shows the banner-bearer climbing the Romanos tower — an elderly bearded sekbanbaşı, not a youth. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-tr} {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-en}

## The two births
The popular account makes Hasan twenty-five at the conquest, hence born about 1428 at the village of Ulubat near Karacabey — the birth year the English article and Wikidata still carry. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-en} {cite:wikidata-ulubatli-hasan} Yılmaz's documents tell a different life: a son of Abdullah, born about 1390, an official palace standard-bearer since the reigns of Çelebi Mehmed and Murad II, and by 1453 the head of the sultan's sekban guard — a veteran entrusted with the hardest task of the assault precisely because of his experience. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-tr} This record follows the traditional dating and preserves Yılmaz's in full; the death itself, on 29 May 1453, is the one date no account disputes. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-en} {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-tr}

## Faith
No source names a branch or a school for Hasan. Wikidata gives his religion as Islam alone. {cite:wikidata-ulubatli-hasan} His waqf deed's style for him — "planter of the banner of Islam" — is a contemporary description of his office and his deed, and the army's dawn prayer before the final assault was the army's practice; neither attests a school for the man, and none is recorded. {cite:wikipedia-ulubatli-hasan-tr}
