## Summary
Tharik Hussain is a British author, journalist and travel writer of Bangladeshi origin whose work maps the Muslim heritage of Europe and the West; The Muslim 500 lists him among influential figures in arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-tharik-hussain} {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## Career
He worked in British radio and journalism before turning to long-form travel writing; his reporting has appeared in the BBC, The Guardian, Al Jazeera and National Geographic Traveller, often seeking out overlooked Muslim communities and monuments in Europe. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## Books
His travel book Minarets in the Mountains ⁦(2021)⁩ — a family journey through the indigenous Muslim communities of the Western Balkans — was a Stanford Dolman Travel Book of the Year finalist; a later journey through the Muslim Americas and Europe produced further books and broadcasts. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## Discoveries
His fieldwork has drawn attention to little-documented Muslim sites — among them early Ottoman-era prayer sites and a unique whitewashed 'White Mosque' in Bosnia — that enter the public record largely through his reporting. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## Assessment
Reviewers credit him with opening a travel-literature niche — indigenous European Islam — previously unwritten in English; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain} {cite:muslim500-tharik-hussain}


## Minarets in the Mountains
His 2021 travel book Minarets in the Mountains: A Journey into Muslim Europe — tracing Ottoman-era Muslim communities from Bosnia to Bulgaria — was shortlisted for the British Guild of Travel Writers award and praised by reviewers as a landmark account of indigenous European Islam. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## Broadcasting
He has presented BBC radio and television travel and faith programmes, and appears as a commentator on Muslim-British affairs; his documentary work on European Muslim heritage reaches audiences far beyond travel writing. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## The Muslim Europe argument
His central argument — that Islam in the Balkans is not migrant but centuries-indigenous, with mosques, mektebs and tekkes older than some European states — is developed across his journalism, talks and a planned documentary series. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain} {cite:muslim500-tharik-hussain}

## Origins
Born in London to a Bangladeshi family and raised in Saudi Arabia, he trained as a journalist and worked across British media before specialising in travel and Muslim-heritage writing. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## The book's journey
Minarets in the Mountains grew out of a family road trip — travelling with his wife and daughters through the Balkans' Islamic heartland — a structure critics praised for binding personal narrative to recovery of forgotten history. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## A wider project
His journalism, broadcasting and speaking form a connected project: arguing that Europe's Muslim communities are indigenous, not imported, and that their mosques and memory belong inside the story of Europe itself. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain} {cite:muslim500-tharik-hussain}

## Muslim Europe's indigenous presence
Tharik Hussain's work is built around a single argument: that Islam in the Balkans is not migrant but centuries-indigenous, with mosques, mektebs and tekkes older than some European states. His 2021 travel book Minarets in the Mountains — shortlisted for the British Guild of Travel Writers award — developed this argument through a family road trip across the Ottoman-era Muslim heartland, a structure critics praised for binding personal narrative to recovery of forgotten history. His broadcasting and journalism extend the same project into public media. {cite:wikipedia-tharik-hussain}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists him among the most influential Muslims in media and arts, citing his book's role in recovering the story of indigenous European Islam and his broadcasting work on Muslim heritage in Europe. {cite:muslim500-tharik-hussain}



## Standing in the field

Within travel writing and broadcasting, Tharik Hussain stands as the author of Minarets in the Mountains, a landmark account of indigenous European Islam — a position that explains the scale of public attention the career has attracted. The Muslim 500's listing places Tharik Hussain among the most influential Muslims of the present generation in this field, reflecting both the size of the audience the work reaches and the extent to which the figure has come to represent it beyond specialist circles. {cite:muslim500-tharik-hussain}
