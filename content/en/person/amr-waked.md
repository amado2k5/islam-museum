## Summary
Amr Waked is an Egyptian actor and producer who became the most internationally visible Egyptian screen actor of his generation — and then a political exile. The Muslim 500 lists him in Arts & Culture as an actor and political activist known for roles in Syriana and Lucy; Wikipedia records his career and the military sentence that keeps him abroad. {cite:muslim500-amr-waked} {cite:wikipedia-amr-waked}

## Egyptian career
Born in Cairo in 1972, Waked began on the Egyptian stage and in independent cinema, building a reputation through the 1990s and 2000s as one of the most interesting actors of Egypt's new film generation — work that ranged from commercial Egyptian cinema to the independent productions he also produced through his company Zad Communication. {cite:wikipedia-amr-waked}

## International roles
His international breakthrough came with Syriana ⁦(2005)⁩ opposite George Clooney, and he went on to parts in major international productions: a Yemeni sheikh in Salmon Fishing in the Yemen, a scientist in Luc Besson's Lucy, Farouk Hassan in the American series Ramy, and Marco Polo on television — a run that made him the Egyptian face of the era's international productions. {cite:wikipedia-amr-waked} {cite:muslim500-amr-waked}

## Politics and exile
Waked supported Egypt's 2011 revolution and spoke increasingly against military rule. In 2019 an Egyptian military court sentenced him in absentia to eight years in prison on charges including spreading false news and insulting state institutions — a verdict he has called political; he has lived since in Spain. The sentence made him a symbol of the costs Egyptian artists can pay for political speech; the museum reports the facts and the positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-amr-waked}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Waked stands for the actor between two worlds: the Egyptian who carried his country's cinema to international screens and paid for his politics with exile — a career that reads as both filmography and political biography. {cite:muslim500-amr-waked} {cite:wikipedia-amr-waked}

## The price of the voice
Waked's biography holds the cost of speaking: after years as Egypt's most internationally recognised actor, his public criticism of the post-2013 order — expressed in interviews and on social media — made him an exile, convicted in absentia in military courts and stripped of his Egyptian property, according to rights organisations and press reports. He settled in Madrid and continued working — in Spanish and international productions — while remaining, in absentia, a figure of the Egyptian opposition's cultural wing. The museum's record describes the career and attributes the political claims to the organisations that document them; the listing records his international standing as an actor. {cite:wikipedia-amr-waked} {cite:muslim500-amr-waked}

## The actor as exile
Waked's record is the museum's example of the actor who treats his voice as a responsibility: the Egyptian star who understood that his international fame gave him a platform his government could not ignore, and who used it to speak against the order that had made him.
