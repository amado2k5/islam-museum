## Summary
Evliya Çelebi, son of Dervish Mehmed, was an Ottoman traveller of the eleventh century AH and author of the [[work/seyahatname|Seyahatname]]. His translator [[person/joseph-von-hammer-purgstall|Joseph von Hammer]] says in his sketch of him that he was born in Constantinople in the reign of Sultan Ahmed I, on 10 Muharram 1020 AH (1611 CE), and that his father, Dervish Mehmed, was chief of the city's goldsmiths. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.9}

## Family and upbringing
Hammer relates that Evliya records the building of the [[building/blue-mosque|Sultan Ahmed Mosque]], begun when he was six, whose gate was made under his father's supervision; that his father had in youth been standard-bearer to Sultan Süleyman; and that his grandfather was standard-bearer at Mehmed II's conquest of Constantinople and received a house in the flour market, on whose ground he built a hundred shops endowed for a mosque. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.9} His mother was an Abaza, and he counted the great Shaykh Ahmad Yasawi among his ancestors. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.9} He spent a short time in the palace, continued studying calligraphy, music, grammar and the Qur'an, and was then enrolled among the Sipahis. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.10} {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.11}

## The dream and the travels
Hammer says that on the anniversary of his twenty-first birthday, 10 Muharram, Evliya dreamt he was in the [[building/ahi-celebi-mosque|mosque of Ahi Çelebi]]; meaning to ask for intercession (*shafa'a*) he asked by mistake for travel (*siyaha*); his shaykhs interpreted the dream as promising the favour of monarchs and visits to the tombs of saints, and he resolved to spend his life travelling; Hammer sees in this the reason for his fondness for tombs and places of pilgrimage. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.11} He styled himself Evliya, Hafiz and Siyyah. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.11}

According to Hammer he travelled for forty-one years through the Ottoman lands in Europe, Asia and Africa except Tunis, Algiers and Tripoli; went with the Ottoman embassy to Vienna in 1664 as its secretary and went on through the German lands, the Netherlands and Sweden, returning by the Crimea; and was present at twenty-two battles. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.12} {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.18} {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.19} His journeys included Brussa, Nicomedia, Trebizond, the siege of Azov, Crete, Erzurum, Tabriz and Damascus, and he followed his uncle and patron Melek Ahmed Pasha in his governorships. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.12} {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.13} {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.14} {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.16} Hammer thinks he spent his last years at Adrianople writing his travels and probably died there. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v1 p.19}

## Note
Everything here comes from Hammer's sketch of the author in the introduction to his 1834 English translation, including his views on the number of volumes and the place of death.
