## Overview
Al-Amir 'Ala' al-Din Abu al-Hasan 'Ali ibn Balban ibn 'Abd Allah al-Farisi, the Egyptian Hanafi, was a jurist, grammarian and hadith scholar and the author of [[work/al-ihsan-fi-taqrib-sahih-ibn-hibban|*al-Ihsan fi Taqrib Sahih Ibn Hibban*]]. He was born in 675 AH, and his biographers describe him as one of the most deeply learned men of his time in both principles and applied law. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 44}

## Teachers
He studied law with al-Fakhr ibn al-Turkumani and [[person/ahmad-ibn-ibrahim-al-saruji|Shams al-Din Abu al-'Abbas Ahmad al-Saruji]], grammar with [[person/abu-hayyan-al-gharnati|Abu Hayyan]] and legal theory with al-'Ala' al-Qunawi, and heard hadith from [[person/al-dimyati|the hadith master al-Dimyati]], Muhammad ibn 'Ali ibn Sa'id, Baha' al-Din ibn 'Asakir and others. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 44} Ibn Abi al-Wafa' al-Qurashi, of the generation of his students, adds that he also studied with the chief judge al-Qunawi the Shafi'i, Rashid al-Din ibn al-Mu'allim and Najm al-Din ibn Ishaq al-Halabi, and that he gave legal opinions and built up a collection of books. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 45}

## What was said of him
[[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] wrote in *al-Mu'jam al-Mukhtass* that he heard from al-Baha' ibn 'Asakir with al-Dhahabi reading, and that he was "a Turk, learned and dignified"; he also called him quick of understanding, good in discussion, handsome and of great standing. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 44} [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] says he was a companion of Arghun the viceroy, rose high in the days of al-Muzaffar Baybars, and was once designated for a judgeship because of his calm, his learning and his reserve. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 44}

## Works
He arranged Ibn Hibban's *al-Taqasim wa-al-Anwa'* by chapter, and arranged [[work/al-mujam-al-kabir|al-Tabarani's *al-Mu'jam al-Kabir*]] well by the chapters of law; Ibn Hajar says he did so at the suggestion of [[person/al-halabi-qutb-al-din-abd-al-karim-ibn-abd-al-nur|al-Qutb al-Halabi]]. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 45} In the preface of *al-Ihsan* he says that Ibn Hibban's ingenious design had made his book hard to draw on, so he set out to bring it within reach by putting each hadith in its chapter, and completed it in a short time. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 49-50} [[person/ahmad-muhammad-shakir|Ahmad Muhammad Shakir]] judges that he did nothing to the book but rearrange it, dropping not a letter. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 10}

## Death
He died at his house on the bank of the Nile in Egypt on 9 Shawwal 739 AH and was buried in his tomb outside [[building/bab-al-nasr|Bab al-Nasr]], as Ibn Abi al-Wafa' al-Qurashi reports. All his biographers give the year 739 except al-Suyuti in *Husn al-Muhadara*, who gives 731; since al-Suyuti himself gives 739 in *Bughyat al-Wu'at*, Shakir judges the other date a slip on his part. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 45}

## Sources for his life
Shakir lists among the sources for his biography *al-Jawahir al-Mudiyya* of 'Abd al-Qadir al-Qurashi, Ibn Hajar's *al-Durar al-Kamina*, al-Maqrizi's *al-Suluk*, Ibn Taghri Birdi's *al-Nujum al-Zahira*, al-Suyuti's *Bughyat al-Wu'at* and *Husn al-Muhadara*, and al-Laknawi's *al-Fawa'id al-Bahiyya*. {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 44}
