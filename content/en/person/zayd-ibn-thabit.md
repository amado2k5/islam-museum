## Overview
Zayd ibn Thabit was an Ansari Companion of the Prophet [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]], remembered above all for his part in the collection of the Qur'an. The Encyclopaedia of Islam article describes a boy from Medina who learned to write early, served as one of the Prophet's scribes, and went on to hold financial and administrative posts under several caliphs {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}. The article calls the collection of the Qur'an a subject of much controversy and says little about its details.

## Family and early years
Zayd belonged to the Banu al-Najjar of the [[group/al-khazraj|Khazraj]], more precisely to the line of Abd Awf ibn Ghanm ibn Malik ibn al-Najjar. His mother, al-Nawar bint Malik, was of the Adi ibn al-Najjar. The article notes that much of the detailed biographical information about him was kept by hadith transmitters from among his own descendants, who took a keen interest in the life of their ancestor {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

His father was killed in the battle of Bu'ath, when Zayd was six years old, and Zayd was eleven at the Hijra {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}, as the earlier tradition also reports {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. For the article, the years in between were decisive for his later career. Before the Hijra he learned Arabic writing at a school kept by a Jewish group called Masika, and his knowledge of the Hebrew or Syriac script presumably goes back to the same period {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}. Elsewhere in the tradition the learning of Syriac and Hebrew is told as a report that the Prophet set him to it {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

The article infers that his first son was probably Sa'id. Sa'id's mother, Umm Jamil bint al-Mujallil of the [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]], would then have been Zayd's first wife. She was widowed in [[place/ethiopia|Ethiopia]] and came to Medina with two small children after the conquest of [[event/battle-of-khaybar|Khaybar]] in 7 AH, so she may have been married twice before she married the young Zayd {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

## Scribe and man of figures
Zayd was one of the Prophet's scribes, and he wrote down both verses of the Qur'an and letters to the kings. He is also reported to have been the scribe of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], and to have acted as judge under Umar and [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

His literacy went with a head for arithmetic. He was a famous expert on the division of inheritances, the subject of [[concept/ilm-al-faraid|the science of the shares]], and was counted an authority on calendrical calculation {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

## Public and financial posts
The article lists several tasks that fell to him:

- Abu Bakr told him to work out the fifth due on the captives of the [[group/kinda|Kinda]] brought to Medina from al-Nujayr in [[place/hadramawt|Hadramawt]], and Zayd later collected their ransom.
- After the battle of [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmuk]] he divided the spoils, as he had done for the Prophet at Khaybar and at [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]].
- When food sent by Umar's governor in Egypt was to be distributed, Zayd drew up written orders for the release of supplies, made on papyrus and stamped at the foot. The article calls him the first Muslim to prepare such orders {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

## Under Uthman
According to the article, Uthman put Zayd in charge of the treasury, the [[concept/bayt-al-mal|bayt al-mal]], after his predecessors had resigned over Uthman's unlawful use of public money, and later allowed him to keep a surplus of more than 100,000 dirhams. The article adds that a source favourable to Uthman denies all of this. In that account the former officials had simply grown old and weak, the surplus was only 1,000 dirhams, and Zayd spent it on building work in the Prophet's mosque {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

When Uthman was [[event/siege-of-uthman-35|besieged]] in his house, Zayd defended him against the wishes of his fellow Ansar, most of whom supported [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]]. [[person/abu-ayyub-al-ansari|Abu Ayyub al-Ansari]] accused him of acting for the sake of the many palm trees Uthman had given him. The article asks readers to remember that at the [[event/saqifa|saqifa]] just after the Prophet's death, Zayd, whose first wife was probably of the Quraysh, spoke eloquently for the political leadership of the [[group/muhajirun|Muhajirun]] over the Ansar {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

## Later years and death
Zayd was one of the few Ansar who did not pledge allegiance to Ali. A source sympathetic to the Shi'a explained that Uthman had appointed him over both the diwan and the treasury. He was evidently not part of Ali's administration, but at the start of [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Muawiya]]'s reign he is found again in charge of the diwan of Medina, and he advised Muawiya and the governor of Medina, [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]], on legal matters {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

The years given for his death range from 42, or 662 to 663, to 56, or 675 to 676 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}. The earlier biographical tradition gives 45 AH as the majority view, with other years also reported {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 510}. He left so much gold and silver that it had to be cut up with a broad iron tool, and in addition orchards and estates valued at 100,000 dinars, or by another report 150,000 dinars and 700,000 dirhams. The article judges that, given his humble start, his career in the first half-century of Islam was phenomenal {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

## The collection of the Qur'an
The article refers readers to its separate entry on the Qur'an for the collection, and states one point as certain: when Uthman looked for a qualified scholar to oversee the preparation of the official edition, Zayd's complete commitment to the Qurashi cause was taken into account {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 495}.

In the earlier tradition recorded in the museum, after the wars of the Ridda had killed many reciters, Abu Bakr charged Zayd, then a young man, with gathering the Qur'an from written fragments and from memory; he is reported to have found the task as heavy as carrying a mountain {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. The sheets he assembled passed into the keeping of [[person/hafsa|Hafsa]], and about a decade later Uthman named a committee headed by Zayd, which produced the standard copy sent to the cities {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Between spoken revelation and the written book stands an editor, and the tradition names him. Every recitation in the world today descends from the text Zayd ibn Thabit wrote out.

## For young visitors
Zayd was a boy who could write, so the Prophet had him write down the Qur'an, and later two caliphs trusted him to gather it into the book we have now.
