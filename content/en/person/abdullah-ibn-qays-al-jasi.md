## Summary
Abdullah ibn Qays al-Jasi, an ally of [[group/banu-fazara|Banu Fazara]], commanded the Muslim fleet in Syria during the caliphate of [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]]; [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]] put him in charge of the sea, and he led fifty raids, in winter and in summer. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.195}

## Context: permission to campaign at sea
[[person/ali-muhammad-al-sallabi|Al-Sallabi]] relates that Umar ibn al-Khattab refused to carry Muslims on the sea after Amr ibn al-As described it to him, and that Mu'awiya renewed the request under Uthman until he was allowed, on condition that he took his own wife with him and forced no one to go; Mu'awiya then ordered the people of the coasts to repair the ships and bring them to the fort of Acre. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.192} The Cyprus campaign of 28 AH followed, and al-Sallabi narrates it directly before his entry on Abdullah ibn Qays. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.193} {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.195}

## His command
Al-Sallabi reports that Abdullah ibn Qays made fifty raids at sea in which none of his men drowned or met disaster, and that he used to pray that God would keep his men safe and not try him with the loss of any of them. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.195}

## His death
One day he went out alone in his skiff as a scout and reached an anchorage in Byzantine territory where beggars were gathered, and gave them alms. A woman among them went back to her village and told the men that Abdullah ibn Qays was at the anchorage; when they scoffed that she could know him, she rebuked them, and they rushed at him. He fought them and was killed alone, while the boatman escaped to his companions. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.195} Asked how she had recognised him, the woman said: he looked like a merchant, but when I asked of him he gave like a king. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.196}

Sufyan ibn Awf al-Azdi succeeded him over the troops and fought the Byzantines, berating and cursing his men, until Abdullah's slave-girl told him that this was not how Abdullah spoke in battle: he would say, "The depths, then they clear from us." Sufyan took up the saying. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.195} The narrator calls that day the end of the time of Abdullah ibn Qays al-Jasi. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.195}

## In al-Sallabi's reading
Al-Sallabi sees in the report the model of a commander who scouts in person, is gentle with his men and generous even to the poor of the enemy's land, and contrasts his steadiness with the agitation of his successor Sufyan, which he attributes to Sufyan's inexperience in command. {cite:sallabi-uthman-tawzi-2003 p.196} See also [[event/first-raid-on-cyprus|the first raid on Cyprus]].
