# Firaq Gorakhpuri

**Firaq Gorakhpuri** (28 August 1896 – 3 March 1982) was the pen name of **Raghupati Sahay**, an Indian Urdu poet and critic and one of the leading figures of twentieth-century Urdu literature: he received the Jnanpith Award, India's highest literary honour, for his collection Gul-e-Naghma. His place in this list comes from his standing inside the Urdu poetic canon, not from any Muslim affiliation, which he never held.{cite:wikipedia-firaq-gorakhpuri-en}{cite:wikipedia-firaq-gorakhpuri-ur}

## Early life and formation

Firaq was born at Banwarpar in Gorakhpur district into a well-to-do and educated Hindu Kayastha family, and was married at fourteen against his wish. He passed the Indian Civil Service examinations, then resigned his deputy collectorship to join Gandhi's Non-cooperation movement, spending eighteen months in jail.{cite:wikipedia-firaq-gorakhpuri-en}

## Teaching and letters

Firaq lectured in English at Allahabad University until 1958, founded India's first association of progressive writers in 1936, and presided over the Progressive Writers' Association at Lucknow. His works include the collection Rooh-e-Kainat and the critical treatise Adab ki Haqiqat.{cite:wikipedia-firaq-gorakhpuri-en}{cite:wikipedia-firaq-gorakhpuri-ur}

## Death and funeral

Firaq died at New Delhi on 3 March 1982. The Urdu article, citing Malik Ram's Tazkira, records that his body was taken to Allahabad and cremated at the confluence of the Ganga and the Jamuna, the meeting of rivers held sacred in Hindu tradition.{cite:wikipedia-firaq-gorakhpuri-ur}{cite:wikidata-firaq-gorakhpuri}

## A note on affiliation

No article states Firaq's own religion in its text: the English article describes his family as Hindu (family background), Wikidata's religion value is Hinduism (one structured value), both editions file him among Hindu poets, and the Urdu article records his cremation at the Sangam on Malik Ram's authority.
