## Summary
Ataç İmamoğlu is a Turkish-Swiss physicist whose experiments helped found the field of quantum optics in semiconductors — the study of how light and matter interact inside engineered crystals — and who now works on the quantum technologies built on that physics. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology; Wikipedia records his professorship at ETH Zurich and his research across quantum optics, semiconductor physics and nonlinear optics. {cite:muslim500-atac-imamoglu} {cite:wikipedia-atac-imamoglu}

## Formation and career
İmamoğlu was born in Istanbul, studied electrical engineering in Turkey, then took his doctorate at Stanford University, where he worked on quantum optics with theorist and experimentalist collaborators. After research posts he held a professorship at the University of California, Santa Barbara before moving to ETH Zurich, where he leads the Quantum Photonics Group — the laboratory he has directed for two decades. {cite:wikipedia-atac-imamoglu}

## Science
His signature contributions include early demonstrations of electromagnetically induced transparency and slow light in atomic and solid-state systems, and pioneering work on exciton-polaritons — hybrid particles of light and matter in semiconductor microcavities that can condense and interact like a quantum fluid. His group helped show that solid-state systems could host coherent quantum phenomena once thought possible only in isolated atoms, feeding ideas into quantum dots, single-photon sources and quantum computation. {cite:wikipedia-atac-imamoglu} {cite:muslim500-atac-imamoglu}

## Honours
His recognitions include election to bodies such as the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and the Turkish Academy of Sciences, and major prizes in quantum electronics and optics; he is among the most cited researchers in his field. {cite:wikipedia-atac-imamoglu}
