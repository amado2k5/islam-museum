## Overview
Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali al-Shalmaghani, known as Ibn Abi al-Azaqir. Ibn al-Nadim says he had a standing in the craft of alchemy, and that he wrote the books Al-Khamaʾir (The Ferments), Al-Hajar (The Stone), a commentary on Jabir's Book of Mercy, and Al-Branniyat. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.507}

## In Ibn al-Athir
Ibn al-Athir relates that he founded an extreme doctrine of Shi'ism, transmigration and the indwelling of divinity. He was close to al-Muhsin ibn Abi al-Hasan ibn al-Furat during his father's third vizierate, and they held him in belief. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.218-219}

According to Ibn al-Athir, he was arrested in Shawwal 322 AH during the vizierate of Ibn Muqla, and was crucified and burned in Dhu al-Qa'da. He is credited with the view that God indwells in everything. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.218-219}
