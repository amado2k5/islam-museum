## Overview
Plato, a Greek sage. Al-Mas'udi relates that on the stone threshold of the gate of the Sabian assembly at Harran, there was written in Syriac: "Whoever knows himself becomes divine." Also written there is that the human being is a heavenly plant, like an inverted tree whose root is in the heavens. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.542-547}

## Thought
Al-Mas'udi attributes to some people the claim that the soul is a substance that is not a body, living, knowing and governing bodies, and that its death is its passing from one body to another. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.542-547}

## His life in Ibn al-Nadim
Ibn al-Nadim, citing the book of Plutarch, says his name was Plato, son of Arsatn, and that his father was called Isthun and was one of the Greek notables. In his early years he inclined to poetry, then attended the session of Socrates and left it, and then moved to the saying of Pythagoras. Aristotle took from him. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.343-344}

The source differs on his age: it is said he was eighty-one years old, and in Ishaq's report, eighty. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.343-344}
