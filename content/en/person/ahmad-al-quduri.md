## Overview
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Ja'far ibn Hamdan, Abu al-Husayn al-Quduri, was a Hanafi jurist. He was born and died in Baghdad, and the leadership of the Hanafis in Iraq came to him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 212}. Al-A'lam gives his years as 362 to 428 AH, that is 973 to 1037 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 212}.

## His works
He composed the abridgement known by his name, «al-Quduri», in Hanafi jurisprudence, which is printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 212}. Among his books is «al-Tajrid», in seven parts, which covers the differences between al-Shafi'i and Abu Hanifa and his companions; its first volume survives in manuscript at the Chester Beatty library under number 3523; and a book on marriage («al-Nikah»), printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 212}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam names him Ahmad ibn Muhammad Abu al-Husayn al-Baghdadi al-Quduri (died 1037) and calls him one of the foremost Hanafi scholars in Baghdad, belonging to the classical age of Islamic law. It says he is most famous for his Mukhtasar, a handbook of law that has drawn many commentaries and is still studied in the Hanafi establishments of traditional Islamic learning. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1941}
