## Overview
Muhammad Raghib ibn Mahmud ibn Hashim al-Tabbakh al-Halabi was the historian of Aleppo and one of the city's eminent scholars, born and died there, 1293 to 1370 AH, 1877 to 1951 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 122}.

## Life
He was taught at one of Aleppo's primary schools, then read under its scholars and memorised many of the standard texts, becoming cultured and learned in jurisprudence, and he worked in trade {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 122}. He then founded the Scientific Press (al-Matba'a al-'Ilmiyya) in 1341 AH, wrote much in newspapers and journals, especially the journal of the Arab Academy (al-Majma' al-'Ilmi al-'Arabi), of which he was a member {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 122}. He taught at the Shari'a College in Aleppo and was then chosen as its director {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}.

## His books
His best-known book is "I'lam al-Nubala' bi-Tarikh Halab al-Shahba'," printed in seven volumes {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He also wrote "al-Anwar al-Jaliyya fi Mukhtasar al-Athbat al-Halabiyya," printed, which he closed with the licences (ijazas) given to him by his teachers and biographies of some of them; "al-Matalib al-'Aliyya fi al-Durus al-Diniyya," a printed school text in three small parts; "'Izat al-Abna' bi-Tarikh al-Anbiya'," a printed abridgement; a treatise on prosody in manuscript; "Dhu al-Qarnayn wa-al-Sadd" and "al-Thaqafa al-Islamiyya," printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He compiled "al-Rawdiyyat," printed, in which he gathered the scattered verse of Abu Bakr al-Sanawbari, and "al-'Uqud al-Durriyya," printed, the divans of three Aleppo poets of the eleventh century AH, the first being the divan of Ahmad ibn al-Husayn al-Jazari, gathered by the subject of the entry {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}.
