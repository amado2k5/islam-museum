## Overview
Nur al-Din Mahmud ibn Zangi, lord of Aleppo and then of Syria. In 543 AH, together with his brother Ghazi, lord of Mosul, he lifted the Frankish siege of Damascus, and he kept up the fight against the Franks. He took Damascus in Safar 549 AH and took several fortresses and strongholds. The caliph al-Muqtafi sent him a commission to govern Egypt and gave him the title "al-Malik al-Adil" {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.673-675}.

## Death and legacy
He died in 569 AH while lord of Damascus, and his son al-Malik al-Salih Isma'il succeeded him as a boy. The Franks then moved against the coasts, and were pacified with money {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.684}.

The author of "Shadharat al-Dhahab" describes him as the finest, most just, most pious and most active in jihad of the kings of his time. He says that he conquered more than fifty fortresses in Frankish lands, abolished unlawful taxes, and built schools, the wall of Damascus, houses for orphans, hospices and a hospital, and a house of justice {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.708-709}.

He is also reported to have been approached by a man who made a claim against him while he was playing polo. He went to the judge's session, and when the man's claim could not be proven, he gave him the thing he had claimed. Ibn al-Athir is quoted as saying that after the rightly guided caliphs and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz he had not seen a ruler of better conduct {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.708-709}.
