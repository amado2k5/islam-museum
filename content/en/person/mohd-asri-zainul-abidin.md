## Summary
Mohd Asri bin Zainul Abidin, known by his acronym Dr Maza, is a Malaysian Islamic scholar, preacher and writer born in Bukit Mertajam, Penang, on 1 January 1971, who serves as Mufti of Perlis — a post he first held from 1 November 2006 to 11 November 2008 and has held again since 2 February 2015. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en} {cite:oxford-bibliographies-mohd-asri}

He remains a permanent associate professor at Universiti Sains Malaysia while serving in the Perlis Mufti's office, and he is known to the public, especially the young, through his blog Minda Tajdid and his columns in Malay newspapers. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en} {cite:oxford-bibliographies-mohd-asri}

His verified Instagram account counted 215,646 followers on 15 November 2026. {cite:instagram-drmaza-official}

## Education
He was educated at the religious secondary school Al-Irsyad in Seberang Perai, Penang, and then at the Islamic College Klang. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en}

He received his bachelor's degree in Arabic and Sharia, with honours, from the University of Jordan, a master's degree in Islamic studies from Universiti Sains Malaysia, and a PhD in Islamic Revealed Knowledge and Heritage — Qur'an and Sunnah studies — from the International Islamic University Malaysia. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en} {cite:oxford-bibliographies-mohd-asri}

## Work and writing
As mufti he advises on matters of Islamic law in Perlis, and he has published many books since 2003, among them works against fanaticism in the schools of jurisprudence and on fabricated hadith. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en}

He was a columnist for the Malay newspapers Mingguan Malaysia and Sinar Harian. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en}

## Views, as the sources describe them
The English article describes the teaching he propagates as Mufti of Perlis, called "Sunnah Perlis", as a Salafi version of a revival and reform agenda that calls Muslims back directly to the Qur'an and the Sunnah over the interpretations of the schools, and it says he accepts moderate ideas from the Salafi school while urging Malaysian Muslims not to treat the Shafi'i school as the only source of law. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en}

The same article records that some have accused him of subscribing to Wahhabism, while others characterise him as a moderate and progressive scholar — positions this museum reports without adopting. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en}

His school is accordingly recorded as unknown on his record: the article's description is a single attestation, its infobox's "Known for: Salafism" is structured data, and his office settles nothing. {cite:wikipedia-mohd-asri-zainul-abidin-en} {cite:oxford-bibliographies-mohd-asri}
