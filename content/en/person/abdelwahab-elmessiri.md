## Summary

Abdelwahab Elmessiri (1938–2008) was an Egyptian thinker and professor of English literature, joining literary criticism, sociology and epistemology in a project of critiquing Western modernity from an Islamic angle.{cite:wikipedia-abdelwahab-elmessiri}

## Life

Born in Damanhur, he studied in Alexandria and at Columbia and Rutgers in the United States, where he took a doctorate in English literature, taught at Ain Shams University, and joined opposition political activity in his last years.{cite:wikipedia-abdelwahab-elmessiri}

## Works

His Encyclopedia of Jews, Judaism and Zionism: A New Interpretive Model (8 volumes, 1999) offers an alternative to standard definitions; other books include Partial and Comprehensive Secularism, Language and Metaphor, My Intellectual Journey and Epistemological Bias.{cite:wikipedia-abdelwahab-elmessiri}

## Method and standing

He deconstructed concepts like 'epistemological bias' and proposed an Islamic interpretive model. Islamic thinkers honour him as a theorist of civilisational critique, while studies criticise his distance from foreign archives or his generalisations — the museum records both.{cite:wikipedia-abdelwahab-elmessiri}
