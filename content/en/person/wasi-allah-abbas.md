## Summary
Wasi Allah ibn Muhammad Abbas is a contemporary hadith scholar and editor; the title page of the second edition of his edition of [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]] describes him as associate professor at Umm al-Qura University in Mecca {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.4}.

## His edition of Fada'il al-Sahaba
Wasi Allah Abbas made the edition of [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]]'s book the subject of his doctoral thesis {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.8}. Umm al-Qura University printed it at its Centre for Scientific Research in 1403 AH; when that printing ran out he revised and corrected it and gave it to Dar Ibn al-Jawzi, writing the preface to the second edition in Mecca on 25 Rabi' II 1420 {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.5-6}. In his introduction he thanks his teacher al-Sayyid Ahmad Saqr for his advice despite a continuing illness, Dr. Muhammad Abu Shahba for his guidance, and two students of the Faculty of Shari'a in Mecca who helped him collate the text and correct the print {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.9}.

## His method
He relates that he copied the unique manuscript, collated it and numbered its hadiths, and that the hardest part of the work was a detailed biography of every transmitter, not limited to *al-Taqrib* or *al-Kashif* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.44}. His reason is that he found transmitters whom Ibn Hajar's *al-Taqrib* labels "acceptable", "concealed" or "unknown", yet for whom *al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil* or *Tarikh Baghdad* record, with sound chains, that an imam declared them reliable — so the verdict on the hadith changes with the verdict on the transmitter {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.45}. By this method he treated more than 1,762 transmitters, graded each chain as sound, good or weak, recorded the judgements of the early imams and set out, with reasons, where his own view differed {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.45}. He traced each hadith with its corroborating routes, made a point of grading every route he cited, explained places and rare words, and compiled the indexes {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.45-46}.

## His views in the introduction
The editor prefers Ibn Hajar's definition of a Companion as, in his view, the most acceptable one {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.11}. In his first chapter he states that Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a agree on the uprightness of the Companions, citing passages of al-Khatib al-Baghdadi and Ibn Kathir {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.18-19}. In the same introduction he criticises, in strong terms that are his own opinion, those among the Rafida who disparage the Companions, and quotes from Shi'i sources the report that people apostatised except three in order to rebut it {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.8} {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.19}. In his biography of Ahmad he notes that he had begun editing Ahmad's *Kitab al-Ilal wa-Ma'rifat al-Rijal*, and adds in the second edition that the edition has been completed and printed {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.29}.
