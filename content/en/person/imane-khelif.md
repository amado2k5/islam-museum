## Summary
Imane Khelif (born 2 May 1999) is an Algerian boxer who won the welterweight gold medal at the Paris 2024 Olympics — the first Algerian, Arab and African woman to take an Olympic boxing title. Her victory followed one of the loudest eligibility controversies in Olympic history, in which the International Olympic Committee affirmed her right to compete as a woman after her disqualification from the 2023 World Championships by the International Boxing Association. {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-en} {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-ar} {cite:themuslim500-imane-khelif}

## Early life and career
Khelif was born in the village of Aïn Sidi Ali in Tiaret province, in Algeria's north-west, and grew up in modest circumstances — she has recounted selling scrap copper and bread to pay for bus fare to training. She began boxing as a teenager against her father's initial reluctance, fought at the 2018 and 2019 World Championships without a medal, placed fifth at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, and took silver at the 2022 World Championships in Istanbul. At the 2022 Mediterranean Games and 2023 Arab Games she won gold. {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-en}

## The eligibility controversy
In March 2023 the International Boxing Association — the Russian-led body by then expelled from the Olympic movement over governance and integrity failures — disqualified Khelif and Taiwan's Lin Yu-ting from the World Championships in New Delhi, citing gender-eligibility tests whose details it never fully published and whose legitimacy the IOC disputed. The IOC stated that Khelif was born female, raised as a woman, registered and competing as a woman, and that the IBA's procedure was arbitrary; other bodies and commentators contested the IOC's position. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-en}

## Paris 2024
In Paris, Khelif became the centre of a global media storm: after her opening opponent withdrew injured early in their bout, politicians, authors and online campaigners attacked her participation, often misidentifying her as transgender, while Algerian public opinion rallied behind her and her father publicly defended her. She fought through the welterweight draw and on 9 August 2024 defeated China's Yang Liu in the final at Roland Garros, taking gold — Algeria's first Olympic boxing gold and the first Olympic boxing title won by an Algerian, Arab or African woman. She later filed legal complaints in France over online harassment, naming the abuse she received during the Games. {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-en} {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-ar}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists her as a national symbol of perseverance. In Algeria she has been decorated by the state and celebrated in mass public receptions; she has also faced continued litigation and public scrutiny over eligibility rules issued later by boxing bodies. No source read names a religious school for her; coverage describes her as a Muslim Algerian athlete, and this record records no school. {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-en} {cite:themuslim500-imane-khelif}

## The woman behind the storm
Beyond the controversy, Khelif's biography is one of rural ascent through sport. She has described training on rough ground, travelling hours to gyms, and carrying the expectations of a country where women's boxing had little institutional support before her generation. Her gold medal was celebrated across Algeria with public holidays of a kind, wall murals and the naming of sports venues, and she was received by the president. In the months after Paris she turned professional ambitions towards Los Angeles 2028 while continuing to contest, in courts and public statements, the eligibility rulings that followed from boxing's governing bodies — a dispute in which the positions of the IOC, the IBA and their successor federation remained in open conflict. {cite:wikipedia-imane-khelif-en}
