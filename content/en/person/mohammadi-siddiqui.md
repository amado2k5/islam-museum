## Summary
Mohammadi Siddiqui is a Nepali Muslim politician and human-rights activist whose public work centres on one of South Asia's least-documented minorities: the Muslim women of Nepal's plains districts. She is best known as the founder of the Fatima Foundation, established in 2003 to advocate for the rights, education and economic standing of Muslim women, and she has carried that advocacy into national political life. {cite:muslim500-mohammadi-siddiqui}

## Context
Muslims form a small minority within Nepal's population, concentrated in the southern Terai region, and Muslim women there have historically faced the combined pressures of poverty, early marriage, low literacy and under-representation in public institutions. Siddiqui's career has been built inside that gap: she speaks both as a Muslim and as a woman, arguing that the community's advancement depends on the schooling and social standing of its daughters. {cite:wikipedia-mohammadi-siddiqui}

## Work with the Fatima Foundation
The Fatima Foundation, which she founded in 2003 and has led since, works on women's empowerment through awareness campaigns, skills programmes and advocacy with state institutions. Its activities have ranged from campaigning against child marriage and domestic violence to pushing for Muslim women's access to identity documents, inheritance rights and education — questions where religious community norms and national law meet. The foundation has also sought to place Muslim women inside mainstream women's-rights platforms rather than leaving their issues to be handled within the community alone. {cite:muslim500-mohammadi-siddiqui}

## Public and political role
Beyond the foundation, Siddiqui has represented her community in Nepal's political sphere, where Muslims have pressed for census recognition, representation quotas and protection of religious identity within the republic's secular framework adopted after 2007. She has worked with interfaith bodies and national women's commissions, presenting the position of a minority within a minority. {cite:wikipedia-mohammadi-siddiqui}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles her as one of the most visible Muslim women in Nepali public life — a figure whose influence rests less on office than on sustained institution-building in a community where few national advocates for Muslim women existed before her work. {cite:muslim500-mohammadi-siddiqui}
