## Overview
Al-Adid, the last of the Fatimid caliphs, whom the source calls the "Ubaydid" state. He succeeded al-Fa'iz and died on Ashura, after the Abbasid khutba had been given in Egypt in 567 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.679-683}

## Vizierate and the fall of the state
He appointed Asad al-Din Shirkuh vizier in 564 AH, and Saladin after him. In 567 AH Saladin cut off his khutba and gave the khutba for the Abbasid caliph al-Mustadi, while al-Adid was gravely ill. Saladin took possession of the palace with its treasures, and its sale continued for ten years. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.679-683}

Shadharat al-Dhahab says he was born at the beginning of 546 AH and lived twenty-one years, and that Saladin showed grief for him and sat for condolences. The causes of his death are given in several accounts: that he died of grief, that he died of excessive diarrhoea, or that a poisoned ring was slipped to him. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.702-703}

## Ibn al-Athir's account
Ibn al-Athir says that his household hid the cutting of the khutba from him until he died. In his severe illness he summoned Saladin, who suspected a trick and did not go, and when he died Saladin regretted it. Ibn al-Athir describes him as generous and gentle. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.34}
