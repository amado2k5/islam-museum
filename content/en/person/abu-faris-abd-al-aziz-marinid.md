## Overview
Abd al-Aziz ibn Ahmad ibn Ibrahim, Abu Faris al-Marini, styled the sultan al-Mustansir bi-llah and headed by al-Zirikli as al-Mustansir the Second, was one of the Marinid kings of the far Maghrib. Al-Zirikli gives his death as 799 AH, 1396 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 14}.

## Career
He was with his father, Abu al-Abbas al-Mustansir the First, in the detention of the sons of Marinid kings in the Alhambra of Granada, and went with him to the Maghrib when he secured his entry into Fez {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 14}. His father put him in command of the army sent to subdue Tlemcen and he set out for it; his father died at Taza, the men of the state summoned him and swore allegiance to him there in 796, Tlemcen and the rest of the Maghrib submitted, and he reigned three years and a month and died in Fez {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 14}. Al-Zirikli describes him as very compassionate and tender-hearted, slow to shed blood, skilled in horsemanship, with some learning in letters and some verse {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 14}.
