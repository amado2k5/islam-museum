## Overview
The historian and traveller Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Mas'udi al-Baghdadi, who died around 336 AH, author of "Muruj al-Dhahab". {cite:muruj-al-dhahab}

## His works and travels
The data record among his books "Muruj al-Dhahab", "Ma'adin al-Jawhar", "al-Tanbih" and "al-Ishraf". He travelled through the lands from Persia and India to the Maghrib and Egypt, and gathered in his books the reports of nations and cities, of creeds and sects. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab}

## The end of the Muruj
At the end of "Muruj al-Dhahab" he apologised for any error or misreading that might appear because of his many journeys, and he warned that whoever distorts his book or ascribes it to another would incur God's anger. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 750-751}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam presents al-Masudi (died 957) as a prolific writer and polymath. It says that more than twenty of his works are known, on religious and worldly topics, among them history, Islamic and universal, geography, natural science, philosophy and theology, and that only two survive, both historical and geographical: Muruj al-dhahab wa maadin al-jawahir and Akhbar al-zaman. According to the Dictionary he wrote for readers eager to gain useful knowledge outside the traditional religious sciences, who valued a fluent, non-technical style. Baghdad was his birthplace, and he worked in Syria and Egypt and died in the lands where he worked. It adds that as a young man he was associated with the Mutazilis and is known for starting the Arabic way of writing history by topic instead of strictly by date. The Dictionary's death year, 957, differs from the around 336 AH on the card above; both are reported and neither is settled. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1463}
