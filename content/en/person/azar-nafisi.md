## Summary
Azar Nafisi is an Iranian-American writer and professor of English literature, born in Tehran. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} She is best known for Reading Lolita in Tehran: A Memoir in Books (2003), which remained on the New York Times bestseller list for 117 weeks and has been translated into dozens of languages. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-ar}

## Life
Nafisi was born in Tehran, the daughter of Ahmad Nafisi, the city's mayor from 1961 to 1963, and of Nezhat Nafisi, one of the first women elected to Iran's parliament. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} At thirteen she was sent to Lancaster, England, for her schooling, continued her studies in Switzerland, and took her doctorate in English and American literature at the University of Oklahoma. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} Her birth year is given differently across sources: Wikidata records 1948, while the Arabic and Urdu articles give 1955 and the English article states no birth date; the museum keeps 1948 at year precision as a debated date. {cite:wikidata-azar-nafisi} {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-ar}

She returned to Iran in 1979 and taught English literature at the University of Tehran, from which she was expelled in 1981 for refusing to wear the mandatory Islamic veil. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} She later taught at Allameh Tabataba'i University, which expelled her in 1995 after refusing her resignation. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} From 1995 to 1997 she held a Thursday-morning book group for former students at her home, reading Lolita, Madame Bovary and other novels — the experience at the heart of her best-known book. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} In June 1997 she emigrated to the United States, and she became a US citizen in 2008. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en}

## Work
Reading Lolita in Tehran (2003) describes her experiences as a secular woman living and working in the Islamic Republic of Iran after the Revolution. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} She followed it with Things I've Been Silent About (2008), a memoir of her mother; The Republic of Imagination: America in Three Books (2014); That Other World, on Nabokov and exile; and Read Dangerously (2022). {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} She directed the Dialogue Project and Cultural Conversations at Johns Hopkins University's School of Advanced International Studies and has been a Centennial Fellow at Georgetown University's School of Foreign Service. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-en} She lives in Washington DC with her husband and two children. {cite:wikipedia-azar-nafisi-ar}
