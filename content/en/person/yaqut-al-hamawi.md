## Overview
Abu Abd Allah Yaqut ibn Abd Allah al-Rumi, Jamal al-Din al-Hamawi al-Baghdadi (574 or 575/1179 – 626/1229), a geographer, lexicographer and man of letters, author of the Mujam al-Buldan and the Mujam al-Udaba' {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 264-266}.

## From slavery to scholarship
He was born in Byzantine territory of non-Arab parents, enslaved while still very small, and carried to Baghdad at five or six, where the nearly illiterate merchant Askar ibn al-Nasr al-Hamawi (d. 606) bought him, named him Yaqut, and put him in the kuttab to serve the business {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 264}. His master employed him on trading voyages to Kish, Oman and Syria; a quarrel brought his manumission and dismissal in 596/1199-1200, and he lived as a copyist — three hundred mujallads between 596 and 603 — until his master relented and made him a mudaraba partner. Sent again to Kish, he returned in 606 to find Askar dead; he satisfied the heirs, kept the remainder as capital, and made books his trade {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 121} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 264}.

## Travels and pursuits
He roamed the lands as merchant and scholar: Baghdad–Amid–Syria in 593-4, Baghdad–Syria–Tabriz in 607-10, meeting the vizier al-Qadi al-Akram al-Qifti at Aleppo in 609; then Damascus in 613, where a debate in its market provoked a riot that nearly killed him, and he fled to Aleppo, Mosul, Irbil and Khurasan, avoiding Baghdad for fear of recognition {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 121} {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 277-278}. He stayed about two years at Nishapur and three at Marw among its ten waqf libraries, where in 615 the idea of the Mujam al-Buldan came to him in the course of his shaykh Abu al-Muzaffar Abd al-Rahim ibn al-Sam'ani. In 616 the Mongol advance drove him from Marw to Nasa and Khwarazm, where he met their outbreak and fled 'like one risen from his grave on the Day of Resurrection', reaching Mosul, then Sinjar, then Aleppo in 617; from Mosul he wrote al-Qifti a letter on his distress at the Mongol advance {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 265} {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 278}.

## Settlement at Aleppo and death
He settled in the khan outside Aleppo under al-Qifti's wing, who helped him greatly with his great dictionary; the Mujam al-Buldan draft was finished at Aleppo on 20 Safar 621, its definitive copy begun on 21 Muharram 625 — a revision that never reached the article 'Rusafat Baghdad' — while al-Mushtarik had been composed in 623 and the second Egypt journey made in 624 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 265-266} {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 278-279}. He endowed his books to the Zaydi mosque at Darb Dinar in Baghdad, delivered through Izz al-Din ibn al-Athir, and once famous took the name Ya'qub. He died on Sunday 20 Ramadan 626/12 August 1229 in the khan outside Aleppo {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 122}.

## Works
The Irshad al-Arib ila Ma'rifat al-Adib (Mujam al-Udaba') in four volumes; the Mujam al-Buldan; the Mujam al-Shu'ara' (lost); al-Mushtarik wad'an wa-l-muftariq suq'an; al-Mubda' wa-l-Ma'ad on history and dynasties; al-Muqtadab fi-l-Nasab; Majmu' kalam Abi Ali al-Farisi; and 'Unwan Kitab al-Aghani {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 121-122} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 265-266}.
