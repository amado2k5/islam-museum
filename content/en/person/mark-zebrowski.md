## Summary
Mark Zebrowski wrote [[work/deccani-painting-zebrowski|Deccani Painting]] (1983). His acknowledgements recall that the idea of studying Indian painting came to him in 1968 while he was in the American Peace Corps near [[place/hyderabad-deccan|Hyderabad]], Deccan, and that a meeting with Muhammad Ashraf of the Salar Jang Museum opened the world of the Deccani kingdoms to him. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 4} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 6}

## Approach
He calls his book the first attempt to bring together all the major Deccani paintings — some two hundred important works, all published in it — and assign them to schools and artists; since Deccani paintings are rarely signed or dated, he names the great anonymous masters after the homes of their masterpieces: the Bikaner, Bodleian, Leningrad and Dublin painters. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 1} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 10} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 12}
