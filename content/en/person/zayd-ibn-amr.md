## Brief
Zayd ibn Amr was a Meccan who refused the idols of his people and searched for "the religion of Abraham" — dying just before the revelation arrived, in the tradition's most poignant near-miss.

## Life
The sira tells that Zayd, of the clan of Adi, stood at a feast of Quraysh and refused the meat sacrificed to idols: "I will not eat what you slaughter in the name of your gods." He searched Syria and beyond for a pure monotheism, rejected Judaism and Christianity as he found them, and was driven by his own people out of Mecca to live in the hills above it {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He died a few years before the first revelation — in one account killed by brigands near al-Ta'if {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. The tradition remembers the Prophet later asked about him, and answering that he would be raised as a nation by himself {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Zayd is the museum's window onto the *hanifs* — the handful of pre-Islamic monotheists the sources preserve: proof that the question Islam answered was already being asked in Mecca.

## For young visitors
Before Islam, a man in Mecca said "I don't believe in these idols" and was thrown out of his own city for it. He died just before the answer arrived.
