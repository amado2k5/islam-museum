## Summary
Egyptian writer and thinker (1945–1992) known for his advocacy of secularism and public criticism of political-Islam currents. His books include The Absent Truth, Before the Fall and Dialogue on Secularism. He was assassinated in Cairo in 1992 by extremists, and his killing became a landmark in Egypt's public debate on freedom of thought. {cite:wikipedia-farag-foda}

## Life and career

Farag Foda was born in 1945 in Damietta governorate, graduated from the Faculty of Agriculture and taught there for years before turning full-time to writing and public work. He took part in Egyptian cultural life as a newspaper columnist and a member of civic institutions. {cite:wikipedia-farag-foda}

In the late 1980s and early 1990s he engaged in public debates on the relationship between religion and state, arguing in his books and lectures for a civil state and for separating political institutions from religious discourse, becoming one of the most prominent Egyptian secular voices of his time. {cite:wikipedia-farag-foda}

## Writings

His books include The Absent Truth, Before the Fall, Dialogue on Secularism, Al-Mal'un and Zawaj al-Mut'a. His articles and books revolved around criticising religious-state projects and defending equal citizenship, combining historical analysis with the Egyptian irony typical of his style. {cite:wikipedia-farag-foda}

Foda held televised and press debates with advocates of political Islam, most famously a Cairo debate in 1992; the press at the time carried accusations of unbelief levelled at him by opponents, which he answered in his lifetime by describing himself as a Muslim calling for a tolerant reading of Islam. {cite:wikipedia-farag-foda}

## Assassination and legacy

On 8 June 1992 armed extremists shot Farag Foda outside his Cairo office, and he died of his wounds. The assassination was the first major incident of its kind against a prominent secular intellectual in modern Egypt and drew broad condemnation inside and outside the country. {cite:wikipedia-farag-foda}

His name became a symbol in the debate over freedom of opinion and political violence in the Arab world; an association bearing his name was founded in Egypt to defend the civil state, and his books are still read and still stir the same controversy he engaged in life. {cite:wikipedia-farag-foda}
