## Overview
Al-Muhtadi billah, an Abbasid caliph, Muhammad ibn al-Wathiq. He was given the allegiance in Rajab 255 AH after al-Mu'tazz was deposed, and was deposed in Rajab 256 AH and died. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.9 p.391} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.9 p.456}

## The allegiance
Al-Mas'udi relates that when the Turks besieged al-Mu'tazz, they sent for al-Muhtadi to the City of Peace (Baghdad), and he was brought to Samarra in a day and a night. He refused to sit on the throne or accept the allegiance until he had seen al-Mu'tazz. He was brought to him, embraced him, and they sat together on the throne. Al-Mu'tazz released him from his allegiance, and the people accepted it. Al-Mu'tazz had exiled him and imprisoned him. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.553}

## His conduct as described by the sources
Al-Mas'udi says that he turned to religion, drew near to the scholars, and raised the status of the jurists. He told the Hashimites to call him to follow the path of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz. He ordered the gold and silver vessels broken and minted into dinars and dirhams, erased the images from the assemblies, and lowered the cost of his table to about a hundred dirhams a day. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.560-563}

Al-Suyuti describes him as pious, devout, just and brave, and says he had fasted without ceasing from the time he was appointed until he was killed. He says that after his killing a chest was found containing a woollen robe and a cloak in which he used to pray at night. He relates from him that he forbade entertainments and forbade singing, and that he would sit to examine the registers on Mondays and Thursdays. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.560-562} Al-Mas'udi also says that he was killed leaving seventeen sons and six daughters. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.560-563}
