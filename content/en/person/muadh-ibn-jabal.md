## Brief
Mu'adh ibn Jabal was a young man of Medina who pledged allegiance to the Prophet at Aqaba, and became the man whom tradition counts the most knowledgeable of the community in what is lawful and unlawful.

## Life
A youth of the [[group/khazraj|Khazraj]], Mu'adh embraced Islam at the [[event/pledges-of-aqaba|second Pledge of Aqaba]] in 621 CE, at about eighteen years of age {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He witnessed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and the other battles of the Medinan years.

Near the end of the Prophet's life, the Prophet sent him to Yemen to teach Islam, collect the alms and judge between people {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. The account preserves their farewell exchange. The Prophet asked him by what he would judge. He answered: "By the Book of God; if I do not find it there, by the Sunna of His Messenger; and if I do not find it there, I will exert my own reasoning." It is a formula that the schools of law still recite {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He was brought back to Medina, then went out with the armies to Syria, and died there in 639 CE in the plague of Amwas {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
His answer to the Prophet, which set out the Book, then the Sunna, then independent reasoning, became the skeleton of Islamic jurisprudence. It is the ancestor of the chapter on the sources of rulings that appears in every textbook.

## For young visitors
The Prophet sent Mu'adh, while he was still young, to teach a whole country. His answer about how to judge became a rule Muslims still study.

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
The Prophet's letter to the kings of Himyar names him as the commander of its envoys to them: "Their commander is Mu'adh ibn Jabal, and he shall not return except content" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/236}. Ibn Ishaq relates, on the authority of Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr, the Prophet's instruction to him when he sent him: make things easy and do not make them hard; give good news and do not drive people away; and the answer to give the People of the Book when they ask about the key to Paradise. See [[event/mission-of-muadh-to-yemen|Mu'adh's mission to Yemen]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/237}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy recounts that the Prophet described Mu'adh as filled with faith to the marrow of his heart {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:07:00}.

He recounts that Mu'adh, on returning from Persia, bowed before the Prophet, and Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds, as his own opinion, that only God is to be bowed to, which is the dignity of the believer {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 01:08:30}.
