## Brief
Mu'adh ibn Jabal was a teenager of Medina when he pledged to the Prophet at Aqaba — and grew into the man the tradition calls the most learned of the community in what is lawful and forbidden.

## Life
A youth of the [[group/khazraj|Khazraj]] tribe, Mu'adh embraced Islam at the second [[event/pledges-of-aqaba|pledge of Aqaba]] in 621, aged about eighteen {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and through the Medinan years.

Near the end the Prophet sent him to Yemen — to teach Islam, collect the alms and judge disputes {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. The tradition preserves their parting dialogue: asked how he would judge, Mu'adh answered "by the Book of God; failing that, by the Prophet's example; failing that, by my own reasoning" — a formula the law schools still quote {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

Recalled to Medina, he went on to Syria with the armies and died there in 639 CE in the plague of Amwas {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
His answer to the Prophet — book, example, reason — became the skeleton key of Islamic jurisprudence: the ancestor of every textbook's chapter on sources of law.

## For young visitors
The Prophet sent Mu'adh to teach a whole country when he was still young — and his answer about how to judge became a rule Muslims still study.
