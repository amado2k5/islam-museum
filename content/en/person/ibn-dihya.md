## Summary
The master-hafiz Abu'l-Khattab 'Umar ibn al-Hasan, Zahiri in madhhab; he travelled the cities of al-Andalus and the opposing shore, made hajj in middle age, heard in Egypt from al-Busiri, in Iraq the musnad of Imam Ahmad, at Isfahan al-Tabarani's Mu'jam and at Nishapur the Sahih of Muslim with lofty isnads — and he used to say he had memorised the entire Sahih. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His impugnment
A group weakened him; al-Dhahabi said he is not strong and has refuted claims; he was granted the shaykhdom of Cairo's dar al-hadith, then his condition was exposed — it was taken from him and he was humiliated; he debated Shaykh Taj al-Din al-Kindi at Damascus, who faulted him for ascribing himself to Dihya al-Kalbi since by the muhaddiths' consensus Dihya left no offspring; he died in Cairo. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to Umar ibn al-Hasan, Abu al-Khattab ibn Dihya al-Andalusi the traditionist, saying he is "suspect in what he transmits, though he was a vessel of knowledge". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 186} He notes that Ibn Dihya heard a great deal in al-Andalus, taught at Tunis around 590, travelled into Persia, met Abu Ja'far al-Saydalani and heard al-Tabarani's hadith with a short chain, and was expert in hadith — its language, men and meanings. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 186} He tutored [[person/al-malik-al-kamil|al-Malik al-Kamil]] in his youth, and when al-Kamil came to rule Egypt, Ibn Dihya gained wealth and position. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 186}

**His claimed descent from Dihya al-Kalbi.** Ibn Dihya traced himself through a long chain to [[person/dihya-al-kalbi|Dihya ibn Khalifa al-Kalbi]], and al-Dhahabi rejects the lineage on three grounds: Dihya left no descendants; the names in the chain bear the marks of Berber names; and even if it were real, ancestors must have dropped out, since there cannot be only ten persons between them. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 186} He adds that Ibn Dihya signed himself "of the two lineages, between Dihya and al-Husayn"; that his link to al-Husayn came through the mother of his grandfather Ali, nicknamed al-Jumayyil, who was a daughter of the sharif Abu al-Bassam al-Alawi al-Husayni of Kufa and later al-Andalus; and that "al-Kalbi" may in his case refer to a place on the coast of Denia. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 187} He reports Ibn Musdi saying that the skilled scholars of the Maghrib went no further in his lineage than his ancestor Farh and the family name Banu al-Jumayyil. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 188}

**The charge of recklessness.** The hafiz [[person/diya-al-din-al-maqdisi|al-Diya']] said: I did not like his state; he often attacked the imams; and he was told that the shaykhs of the Maghrib wrote down their criticism and weakening of him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 186} Al-Dhahabi examines his claim to have heard the *Muwatta'* with a short chain from Ibn Hunayn al-Kinani and Ibn Khalil al-Qaysi: Ibn Khalil lived in Marrakesh and Fez, and Ibn Hunayn left al-Andalus never to return and died at Fez in 569, so at best Ibn Dihya had it from them by licence — yet he claimed to have heard it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 186} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 187} He quotes [[person/ibn-wasil|Ibn Wasil]], judge of Hama: al-Kamil ordered Ibn Dihya to write notes on the book *al-Shihab*; days later he claimed to have lost them and asked for another copy, and the second contradicted the first; the sultan saw that what was said of him was true and dismissed him from the Kamiliyya hadith school, appointing his brother Abu Amr Uthman — though it is also said he was dismissed because he had changed and begun to become confused. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 187} He reports Ibn Nuqta saying that Ibn Dihya claimed to know Sahih Muslim and al-Tirmidhi by heart, and that Abu al-Qasim ibn Abd al-Salam tested him with hadiths from al-Tirmidhi, the Musnad and the forgeries, none of which he recognised. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 188} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 189}

**His judgeship at Denia.** In the material he takes from Ibn Musdi, al-Dhahabi records that Ibn Dihya served as judge of Denia, punished an adulterer by disfiguring him and mutilated a slave of his own; this was reported to al-Mansur, the ruler of the time, and Ibn Dihya hid, then fled in fear towards Ifriqiya and the East and never returned. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 188} He also notes that Ibn Dihya taught al-Hariri's *Maqamat* in al-Andalus from Ibn al-Jawzi from the author, "and this is not sound". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 188}

**Birth and death.** Al-Dhahabi gives his birth as 542 or after, and his death in Rabi' al-Awwal 633. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 188} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 189} The birth date differs from the one implied in *Shadharat al-Dhahab* (about 546); both are kept. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 161}
