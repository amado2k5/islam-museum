Amina bint Wahb ibn Abd Manaf ibn Zuhra, of [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]] of [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]], was the mother of the Prophet Muhammad. [[person/abdullah-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Muttalib]] married her, then died in Yathrib, leaving her pregnant with him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

When her son reached six, she took him to Yathrib to visit his father's grave among his maternal uncles, with her servant [[person/umm-ayman|Umm Ayman]]. She stayed a month. Then illness struck them on the return road, and she died in the village of [[place/al-abwa|al-Abwa']], between Mecca and Medina, and was buried there {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

Her father-in-law [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] took the orphan back to Mecca and held him in his lap until he died, two years after her {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.


## Her choice, marriage and elegy for her husband (The Sealed Nectar, p.53)
[[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] chose Amina bint [[person/wahb-ibn-abd-manaf|Wahb]] ibn Abd Manaf ibn Zuhra ibn Kilab for his son Abd Allah. At that time she was counted the best woman of Quraysh in lineage and standing, and her father was the chief of Banu Zuhra in lineage and nobility. Abd Allah consummated the marriage in Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/156-158}. When news of his death reached her from Medina, she mourned him in an elegy which the author describes as among the finest elegies. In it she recalls the emptiness that the son of Hashim left in the open ground of the Batha, his answering the call of death, and that he was generous and very compassionate {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad 1/62}. Among what she inherited from him was the Abyssinian slave-girl Barakah, [[person/umm-ayman|Umm Ayman]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul-al-najdi p.12}. See [[event/death-of-abdullah-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|Abd Allah's death in Medina]].


## Birth and the journey to Yathrib (The Sealed Nectar, pp.54–57)
Amina gave birth to her son in the Shi'b (valley) of Banu Hashim in Mecca, on the morning of Monday, the ninth of Rabi' al-Awwal, in the Year of the Elephant. She sent to Abd al-Muttalib to give him the news of his grandson {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.54} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/159-160}. Ibn Sa'd relates from her that when she bore him she saw a light by which the palaces of Syria were illuminated {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.54} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad 1/63}. Two years later, in the desert of Banu Sa'd, [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima]] asked her to leave the boy with her, for fear of the plague of Mecca, and Amina sent him back with her {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.56} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/162-164}. When he reached six, she took him to Yathrib to visit his father's grave, with [[person/umm-ayman|Umm Ayman]] and his guardian Abd al-Muttalib. She stayed a month, then died on the way back, at [[place/al-abwa|al-Abwa']] ([[event/death-of-amina|Amina's death]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.57} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/168} {cite:talqih-fuhum-ahl-al-athr p.7}.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.) describes Amina as Muhammad's mother. Her father was Wahb b. 'Abd Manaf of the clan of Zuhra of the Quraysh, and her mother was Barra bint 'Abd al-'Uzza of the clan of 'Abd al-Dar. She is said to have been the ward of her uncle Wuhayb b. 'Abd Manaf, on the day of whose betrothal of her to 'Abd Allah b. 'Abd al-Muttalib he also betrothed his own daughter Hala to 'Abd al-Muttalib, a report the article suggests may reflect a forgotten marriage custom. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 460}

Amina seems to have remained with her own family, where 'Abd Allah visited her, though he is usually said to have died before Muhammad's birth. Muhammad was in her charge while she lived, and presumably lived with her family, except when sent to a wet-nurse among a nomadic tribe. She is said to have died at al-Abwa', between Mecca and Medina, on the way back from visiting Muhammad's kinsmen at Medina, when he was six. The stories of her pregnancy, including her report of a light that lit up the palaces of Busra, are part of the accounts the article considers. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 460}

## In Abla al-Kahlawi's programme 'In the Love of al-Mustafa'
[[person/abla-al-kahlawi|Abla al-Kahlawi]] begins her account of the Prophet's mothers in her programme [[work/fi-hub-al-mustapha|'In the Love of al-Mustafa']] with Amina bint Wahb of the Banu Zuhra, whom she describes as pure, chaste, trustworthy and calm. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:02:00} Her husband Abd Allah, she says, died in the first months of her pregnancy, leaving her a young widow at the start of her life, yet she felt none of the heaviness and fatigue women complain of, as if the child were carrying her. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:04:00} She recalls visions that came to Amina again and again until she was sure her pregnancy was no ordinary one: that she would give birth to the master of this community, and that she should name him Muhammad; and she says Amina told Halima that on the day of his birth she saw a wondrous light that filled the corners of the room and lit up what lies between east and west, and three banners raised, in the east, in the west and above the Ka'ba. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:06:30}

In episode 7 she relates that the boy came back to his mother from the desert at about six or seven, and that Amina longed to visit her husband's grave and wanted her son to know his maternal uncles, so the three of them, she, her son and her maid Baraka, travelled to Medina. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:06:00} Fever struck her as she entered Medina; she completed the visit and stood at Abd Allah's grave as if to tell him that their son had become a handsome youth, holding back her tears for the boy's sake, and then, the fever growing worse on the road, she breathed her last at al-Abwa'. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:07:00}
