## Summary
Tayyaba Bukhari Khanum is a Pakistani religious scholar born on 15 August 1974 in Faisalabad, one of very few women to reach public religious teaching and preaching; she speaks six languages and founded the Bukhari Relief Foundation to aid people displaced by disasters. {cite:muslim500-tayyaba-bukhari} {cite:prideofpakistan-tayyaba-bukhari}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Peoples Colony, Faisalabad, to a religious family, her parents sent her in 1984, before her tenth year, to Qom in Iran, where she completed schooling and religious studies at the Zainabiyya school and Jamiat al-Zahra until 2001 — Arabic, Persian, Islamic studies, usul, fiqh, tafsir, philosophy and history under teachers of the Qom hawza — then continued in Pakistan through a master's in Arabic. {cite:prideofpakistan-tayyaba-bukhari}

## Preaching and teaching
She addresses majalis and lectures and appears on international television channels in live and recorded broadcasts, lecturing in six languages — Urdu, Arabic, Persian, English, Punjabi and Siraiki — and her profiles count lessons delivered in more than forty cities in fourteen countries, drawing audiences in Pakistan, Europe, North America and Australia. {cite:muslim500-tayyaba-bukhari} {cite:prideofpakistan-tayyaba-bukhari}

## Themes and honours
Her recorded lectures cover a wide range — prayer, patience, the philosophy of worship and fasting and pilgrimage, women's rights, ethics and child-rearing — some preserved as ten-lecture series; her honours include the Quran-e-Hakim Award of 2007 and a Wonder Women Award of 2012 per reports. {cite:prideofpakistan-tayyaba-bukhari} {cite:pakistantoday-tayyaba-bukhari}

## Relief work
She founded the Bukhari Relief Foundation, a non-governmental charity helping people displaced by major natural disasters across Pakistan with food, shelter and education, and in 2020 presented a five-million-rupee cheque to the Prime Minister's COVID relief fund per press reports. {cite:muslim500-tayyaba-bukhari} {cite:prideofpakistan-tayyaba-bukhari}

## A woman in a male field
The Muslim 500 describes her as one of very few female religious scholars in Pakistan with significant influence — a rare description in a country where the majlis pulpits were long reserved for men; threats drove her abroad in 2014, whence she continued her discourse on television and among the diaspora. {cite:muslim500-tayyaba-bukhari} {cite:pakistantoday-tayyaba-bukhari}

## Legacy
Her career is read on two counts: a woman scholar preaching from pulpits once closed to women in her country, and a bridge between the Qom hawza and a broad Pakistani public. Sources describe her as a Shia scholar educated in Qom, which the museum labels as documented rather than the Sunni default, and The Muslim 500 files her among Preachers & Spiritual Guides. {cite:pakistantoday-tayyaba-bukhari} {cite:muslim500-tayyaba-bukhari}
