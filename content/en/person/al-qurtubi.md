## Biography
Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Bakr ibn Faraj al-Ansari al-Khazraji al-Qurtubi al-Andalusi, a Maliki scholar expert in hadith best known for his Qur'an commentary. Born in al-Andalus, he was among those who left it for the lands of the East; al-Dhahabi says of him, "he travelled, he wrote and he took lessons from the masters" {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 550}. He heard part of al-Mufhim, the commentary on Sahih Muslim, from its author Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Umar al-Qurtubi, and transmitted from Abu al-Hasan al-Yahsubi and the hafiz Abu Ali al-Bakri {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari 2/211}; al-Dhahabi also names Ibn Rawah, Ibn al-Jumayzi and Ibn al-Muzayyin among his teachers {cite:siyar-alam-nubala 17/97}.

He settled at Munyat Bani Khusayb in Upper Egypt and became its resident, labouring there on his great commentary and filling it with every rarity {cite:siyar-alam-nubala 17/97}. He was a pious, scrupulous and ascetic man, free of affectation, walking in a single garment with a small cap, occupied with what concerned him of the hereafter between devotion, worship and writing {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari 2/210}. His Qur'an commentary ran to fifteen volumes; he also wrote al-Asna on the Most Beautiful Names in two volumes, al-Tadhkira on the matters of the hereafter, a commentary on al-Taqassi and other works {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari 2/210-211}; al-Dhahabi said caravans carry his great commentary {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari 2/211}. He died at Munyat Ibn Khusayb on the night of Monday 9 Shawwal 671 and was buried there {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari 2/211}; Ibn al-Imad calls him "an imam of learning, one of the divers into the meanings of hadith, fine in composition and sound in transmission" {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 335}.
