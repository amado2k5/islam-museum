## Overview
'Isa ibn Mina' ibn Wardan ibn 'Isa al-Madani, client of the Ansar, Abu Musa, known as Qalun, was one of the renowned Qur'an readers; of Medina by birth and death in 220 AH (835 CE), the leadership in the Arabic sciences and the reading in his time in the Hijaz ended with him. {cite:al-aalam v.5 p.110}

He was deaf: the Qur'an was recited to him while he watched the reciter's lips and sent back every slip and error. 'Qalun' is a title the reader Nafi' gave him for the excellence of his reading; in the language of the Greeks it means 'good'. {cite:al-aalam v.5 p.110}

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

Ibn al-Jazari says Qalun died in 220 AH on the correct view and was born in 120; he read on [[person/nafi-al-madani|Nafi']] in 150 and was closely attached to him, so much so that it was said he was his wife's son. The nickname Qalun was given to him by Nafi' for the quality of his reading, since qalun in the language of the Greeks means 'good'. He was the reader of Medina and its grammarian, and was deaf, unable to hear a horn, though he could hear when the Qur'an was read to him. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 122} Ibn al-Jazari quotes him saying he had read Nafi's reading to him several times and written it down from him, and that Nafi' would tell him to sit by a pillar until he sent someone to read to him. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 122} Among his companions were Abu Nashit (d. 258), described as reliable, exact, an eminent reader and a verifier, and al-Hulwani (d. 250), a great master and imam of the readings who travelled twice to Qalun in Medina. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 122} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 123}
