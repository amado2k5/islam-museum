## Summary

[[person/taqi-al-din-ibn-maruf|Taqi al-Din ibn Ma'ruf]] was an Ottoman scholar working in astronomy, mathematics, optics and mechanics, and a teacher and judge before directing the [[place/istanbul|Istanbul]] observatory. His life cannot be reduced to the observatory's demolition or one device: his writings encompass arithmetic, zij works, clocks, balances and optics {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea biography and works} {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid biography and works}.

## Name, beginnings and identity

TDV gives his name as Abu Bakr Taqi al-Din Muhammad ibn Zayn al-Din Maruf ibn Ahmad al-Rasid al-Dimashqi. It connects the Sahyuni nisba with a fortress near Latakia, not a modern political concept. It dates his Damascus birth to4Ramadan932/14June1526, identifying Atai's Egypt927/1521 account as erroneous; Fazlioglu also gives Damascus1526. Tekeli1986 instead says Egypt in a footnote. These are differing witnesses, not identical settled statements {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid opening biography} {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea heading} {cite:tekeli-trigonometry-1986 p.249}.

His Muslim identity has an individually documented religious, educational and judicial basis: religious studies, madrasa teaching and service as a judge in Egypt. It is not inferred from his name, country or Hanafi epithet alone. Sunni is separately the museum's declared frame for the Ottoman setting, not a certificate of a detailed personal creed we have read {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea education and biography} {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid biography}.

## Learning, teaching and judicial work

His father Maruf began his education. TDV's Damascus teachers include Qutb al-Din Ibn Sultan, Kamal al-Din Ibn Hamza, Shams al-Din Ibn Tulun, al-Balatunusi, al-Qari, Shihab al-Din Ahmad al-Ghazzi and Ibn Maghush. Istanbul teachers included Chivizade, Ebussuud, Qutb al-Dinzade and Sachli Emir. This identifies the biography's educational network, not books by all these teachers personally inspected here {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid education}.

He taught in Cairo's Shaykhuniyya and Sarghatmishiyya, then Istanbul's Edirnekapi madrasa, returned to Cairo for family reasons according to TDV, and deputized for Chivizade in judicial work. Both biographies connect his deeper astronomical study with Abd al-Karim Chelebi's encouragement and milieu. Fazlioglu records books by al-Kashi, Qadizade and Ali Qushji, with instruments, reaching him through Qushji's grandson. Access and education are important roles without making every result a presumed collective invention {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid biography} {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea biography}.

## The observatory and chronological limits

Fazlioglu describes his return to Istanbul in1570 and appointment as head astronomer in1571. The biographies discuss observations from Galata Tower and a site overlooking Tophane. Observatory beginnings differ: Tekeli says1575, TDV describes use from1577, and Fazlioglu reports a building edict in early1579. We do not invent one founding day by silently combining these accounts {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea biography} {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid biography} {cite:tekeli-trigonometry-1986 p.249}.

Both biographies describe state-ordered demolition on22January1580, but explanations differ in emphasis. TDV foregrounds political conflict and a fatwa; Fazlioglu mentions politics and failed astrological predictions. Each interpretation is attributed, not converted into a museum verdict about religion, every science or all Ottomans. Fazlioglu dates his death in Istanbul to1585 {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid observatory narrative} {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea biography}.

## Mathematics and zij works

Sidrat muntaha al-afkar aimed to correct and complete Ulugh Beg's zij using observations from Egypt and Istanbul. Fazlioglu describes its sexagesimal functions and an unfinished observational programme. [[work/jaridat-al-durar|Jaridat al-durar]] and Tashil zij al-ashariyya instead appear in a discussion of decimal angular parts, operations and tables, retaining a fixed-star-table exception. Distinguishing the works is fundamental to [[invention/taqi-decimal-trigonometric-tables|the contribution recorded here]] {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea Sidra and decimal-zij discussions}.

Bughyat al-tullab discusses Indian and sexagesimal arithmetic and decimal fractions; al-Nisab al-mutashakila concerns algebra. TDV states that decimal places were expressed verbally without a modern separator. We neither attribute all decimals to him nor substitute modern values for tables whose manuscripts we have not inspected {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid arithmetic and works17–18}.

## Optics, instruments and other writings

The biographies describe al-Kawakib al-durriyya on clocks and al-Turuq al-saniyya on machines. Fazlioglu notes access to Samiz Ali Pasha's library and European clocks and dates the clock book's writing at Nablus to1559. This is not invention of mechanical clocks without predecessors. Nawr hadiqat al-absar treats light and vision within the inheritance of Ibn al-Haytham and Kamal al-Din al-Farisi and was dedicated to Abd al-Karim Chelebi {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea mechanics and optics} {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid works14–16}.

TDV's list also includes projection, sundials, quadrant use, horizons, worship times, calendars, a natural balance determining two metals, a work on training and treating hunting birds, and Tarjuman al-atibba. These fields establish breadth of writing, not correctness of every theory or priority for every device. A description of seeing distant things in an optical account is not turned into a verified telescope-invention certificate here {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid works1–22}.

## Six-cylinder design: no verified workshop

In [[work/turuq-saniyya-taqi|Al-Turuq al-saniyya]] he described [[invention/taqi-six-cylinder-pump|six cylinders]] with weights, valves and a common outlet. The text prefers three units for smoother delivery while allowing one or two: not constant-flow certification or a physical ban below three. Resemblance to later engines proves no direct transfer; the editor's priority formulation remains his {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp47–50}.

Modern modeling assumes most dimensions and produces a quarter-scale mock-up without establishing historical manufacture. We do not transfer the observatory's building date or call researchers' metric choices authenticated authorial units. This addition is distinct from decimal tables, preserving both contributions, earlier biography and attribution {cite:cabeza-pump-2021 sections2–6}.
