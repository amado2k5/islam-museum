## Overview
Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, Abu Abd Allah al-Bajali al-Ahmasi, a client of the tribe, of Kufa, is called by al-Dhahabi the memoriser and great imam {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1103}. His father's name was Hurmuz, or Sa'd or Kathir by other reports; his brothers were Ash'ab, Khalid and Sa'id {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1103}. He was the hadith scholar of Kufa in his time alongside al-A'mash, and had more chains than he {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1103}. The sign printed with his entry shows his hadith is in the six canonical books {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1103}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from the Companions Abd Allah ibn Abi Awfa, Abu Juhayfa Wahb al-Suwa'i, Amr ibn Huraith al-Makhzumi and Abu Kahil Qays ibn A'idh, and al-Dhahabi counts him among the younger Followers; he also narrated from Qays ibn Abi Hazim, Zayd ibn Wahb, Zirr ibn Hubaysh, al-Harith ibn Shubayl, Hakim ibn Jabir, Tariq ibn Shihab, al-Sha'bi and others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1103}. Those who narrated from him include al-Hakam ibn Utayba, Malik ibn Mighwal, Shu'ba, Sufyan, Sharik, Jarir, Abbad ibn al-Awwam, Abd Allah ibn Numayr, Waki', Yahya al-Qattan, Yazid ibn Harun and Hafs ibn Ghiyath {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1103}.

## What the critics said
Al-Bukhari reports from Ali ibn al-Madini that he has about three hundred hadith, and Ibn al-Mubarak reports from Sufyan that the memorisers of the people are three, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Sulayman and Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Ansari {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1103}. Yahya al-Qattan said Isma'il knew al-Sha'bi better than anyone and was most firm in him; Ahmad ibn Hanbal said the most sound hadith from al-Sha'bi is Ibn Abi Khalid's; Ibn Ma'in, Ibn Mahdi and others called him reliable; Ya'qub ibn Shayba said reliable and firm; Abu Hatim said he put none of al-Sha'bi's students before him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1104}. Al-Dhahabi says the scholars agreed on his precision and on relying on him, and that he was never accused of Shi'ism or innovation {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1104}.

## His death
Abu Nu'aym says he died in 146 AH, which al-Dhahabi judges sounder than the view that it was 145 {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1104}.
