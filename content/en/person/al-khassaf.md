## Overview
Ahmad ibn Amr ibn Muhayr al-Shaybani, Abu Bakr al-Khassaf, was one of the jurists of the Iraqi schools. Al-Safadi describes him as accomplished, expert in the law of inheritance and in reckoning, and learned in the positions of his school fellows; he adds that he was ascetic and devout and lived from the work of his own hands, and that he died in 261 AH {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 7 pp. 174-175}. The Gregorian equivalent is derived, about 874 CE.

## His teachers
Al-Safadi lists among those he narrated from Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik (as the text gives the name), Ibrahim ibn Bashar al-Ramadi, Musaddad ibn Musarhad, Abdallah ibn Maslama al-Qa'nabi, Yahya ibn Abd al-Hamid al-Himmani, al-Waqidi, Abdallah ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Shayba, Abu Muawiya al-Darir, Ali ibn al-Madini and others, and says there were many more {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 7 pp. 174-175}.

## Standing with al-Muhtadi
He stood high with the caliph al-Muhtadi bi-llah, so that people spoke of him in terms recalling Ibn Abi Duad; the wording of this passage in the printed text is unclear and is not read further here. He wrote for al-Muhtadi his book on the land tax (al-Kharaj). When al-Muhtadi was killed, al-Khassaf was plundered and some of his books were lost {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 7 pp. 174-175}.

## Works
Al-Safadi lists: Kitab al-Hiyal, al-Wasaya, al-Shurut al-Kabir, al-Shurut al-Saghir, al-Rida', al-Mahadir wa-l-Sijillat, Adab al-Qadi, al-Nafaqat, al-Iqrar bi-l-Waratha, al-Asir wa-Ahkamuh, Ahkam al-Wuquf, and a book on the measurement of the Kaaba, the mosque and the grave {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 7 pp. 174-175}.

## A report of his scrupulousness
Al-Safadi relays from Ibn al-Nadim that Abu Sahl Muhammad ibn Umar told, from some of his teachers in Balkh, that on entering Baghdad he found a man on the bridge calling out for three days that the qadi Ahmad ibn Amr al-Khassaf had been asked about a case and had given a mistaken answer; the man stated the correct answer and asked that it be conveyed to the person who had asked {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 7 pp. 174-175}.
