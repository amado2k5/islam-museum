## Summary
Nawab Viqar-ul-Mulk, born Mushtaq Hussain (24 March 1841 – 27 January 1917), was an Indian Muslim politician of the Aligarh Movement and a founder of the All India Muslim League, whose inaugural session he presided over as its first general secretary. He served the state of Hyderabad for seventeen years, rising to Deputy Prime Minister. His article on the English Wikipedia appears under the spelling "Waqar-ul-Mulk"; he has no Wikidata item and no Arabic or Urdu article, and this record rests on that single article, as the museum's publication standard allows. {cite:wikipedia-viqar-ul-mulk-en}

## Early life
Mushtaq Hussain was born on 24 March 1841 at Meerut into the Kamboh Nawab family of Meerut, the son of Sheikh Fazal Hussain; his great-grandfather, Diwan Abdul Momin Khan, had been a minister at the Mughal court. His social welfare work in the famine-affected areas around Moradabad was noticed by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan in 1861. In 1866, at twenty-five, he began his political career as a worker of the Aligarh Movement and a member of its Scientific Society, for which he translated a book on the French Revolution and Napoleon. In 1870 he won a prize in an essay competition of the Society for the Promotion of Education among Muslims. {cite:wikipedia-viqar-ul-mulk-en}

## Hyderabad
He served Hyderabad State as Law Secretary, then in the Revenue Department by order of the Nizam, then as Secretary, Personal Secretary and adviser to the Prime Minister, Nawab Bashir-ud-Daula, and eventually as Deputy Prime Minister of Hyderabad State. On 9 December 1890 he received the title Nawab Viqar-ul-Mulk; the British Government of India later honoured him with the title of Nawab (1908), and the Nizam gave him the further titles Waqar-ud-Daula, Viqar-ul-Mulk and Intisar-e-Jang. {cite:wikipedia-viqar-ul-mulk-en}

## Aligarh
Viqar-ul-Mulk was among the most ardent followers of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan. As a member of the College Fund Committee he raised some 750,000 rupees for the establishment of the MAO College at Aligarh. He joined the college's orbit in October 1892 and in 1907 was appointed its Honorary Secretary, succeeding the era of Nawab Mohsin-ul-Mulk. {cite:wikipedia-viqar-ul-mulk-en}

## Founding of the Muslim League
In December 1906, at the All India Muhammadan Educational Conference in Dacca, Viqar-ul-Mulk together with the Aga Khan III and Nawab Salimullah Khan of Dhaka launched the All-India Muslim League; Viqar-ul-Mulk became its General Secretary and presided over its inaugural session. The article calls him a founding father of the League, and through it of the later state of Pakistan. {cite:wikipedia-viqar-ul-mulk-en}

## Death and faith
He died on 27 January 1917. No source read for this record names a school or denomination for him, and the museum records none: his Aligarh and League work is political activity, not a school attestation. {cite:wikipedia-viqar-ul-mulk-en}
