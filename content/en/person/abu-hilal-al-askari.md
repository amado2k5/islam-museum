## Overview
Al-Hasan ibn Abd Allah ibn Sahl ibn Sa'id ibn Yahya ibn Mihran al-Askari, Abu Hilal: a scholar of letters and a poet, named after Askar Mukram in the districts of Ahwaz; he died after 395 AH (1005 CE). He was the nephew and pupil of Abu Ahmad al-Askari. Yaqut reports that nothing reached him about his death except a note at the end of the author's al-Awa'il recording that he finished dictating the book on Wednesday the tenth of Sha'ban, 395. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.196}

His works include al-Talkhis on language, a philological Mu'jam, the printed Jamharat al-Amthal, the epistle al-Hathth ala Talab al-Ilm, the printed Kitab al-Sina'atayn on verse and prose, a commentary on the Hamasah, the epistle al-Awa'il, al-Farq bayn al-Ma'ani, al-Umda, Ma Talhan fihi al-Khassa, al-Mahasin — a Qur'an commentary in five volumes — Diwan al-Ma'ani in two parts, al-Furuq on language, al-Dirham wa-l-Dinar, and his own diwan. The author of Kashf al-Zunun says he was the first to write a book of 'firsts.' {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.196}
