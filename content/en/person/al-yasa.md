## Summary

Al-Yasa' (Elisha) is a prophet of the Children of Israel in Islamic tradition. Volume 14 of [[person/al-nuwayri|al-Nuwayri]]'s [[work/nihayat-al-arab-fi-funun-al-adab|Nihayat al-Arab]] brings together two accounts of him with [[person/ilyas|Ilyas]] (Elijah): that of al-Kisa'i in his stories of the prophets, and that of [[person/abu-ishaq-al-thalabi|al-Tha'labi]] from Ibn Ishaq and the narrators of reports {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 7} {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 27}. These are story-reports that al-Nuwayri passes on as he found them, often introducing them with "they claim" and "as they claim" {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 38} {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 39}.

## In al-Kisa'i's account

Al-Kisa'i relates that when Ilyas was told to go back to his people after years of drought, he stopped with an old woman who worshipped their idol Ba'l, whose son al-Yasa' followed Ilyas's religion; the boy died of hunger, Ilyas prayed, and al-Yasa' rose bearing witness that there is no god but God, and the old woman believed {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 25}. Later, when the people kept to their unbelief after the drought was lifted, Ilyas made al-Yasa' his successor over the believers, and al-Yasa' said: I am weak among an unbelieving people {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 26}. Elsewhere the text names him "al-Yasa' ibn Akhtub" {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 26}.

## In al-Tha'labi's account

In al-Tha'labi's account Ilyas sets out with al-Yasa' ibn Akhtub to the place he was commanded, and al-Yasa' calls to him: Ilyas, what do you command me? {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 39}. Al-Tha'labi then says that when God raised Ilyas up, He made al-Yasa' a prophet and sent him as a messenger to the Children of Israel, supporting him as He had supported Ilyas; the Children of Israel believed in him and honoured him, "and God's rule stood among them until al-Yasa' left them" {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 40}.

## For young visitors

Old books tell that al-Yasa' was a young boy when he met the prophet Ilyas, then became his student, and when Ilyas was gone, al-Yasa' was the one who called his people to worship God {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 25} {cite:nihayat-al-arab-dar-al-kutub-v14-1943 p. 40}.
