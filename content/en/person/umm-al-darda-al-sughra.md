## Overview
Hujayma bint Huyayy al-Wasabiyya, known as Umm al-Darda al-Sughra ("the younger"), was a female jurist and hadith transmitter of the Tabi'un generation, from the people of Damascus, attributed by al-Zirikli to al-Wasab, one of the Himyar tribes {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. Ibn al-Imad lists her in the events of 81 AH as Umm al-Darda al-Sughra al-Wasabiyya al-Himyariyya {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 1 p. 333}.

## Upbringing and marriage
Al-Zirikli says she grew up an orphan in the care of Abu al-Darda Uwaymir ibn Malik in Damascus {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. She wore a hooded cloak, prayed in the men's rows and sat in the circles of the reciters, until Abu al-Darda ordered her to join the women's rows {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. He then married her and died leaving her, and when Mu'awiya proposed she refused, out of loyalty to her first husband {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. Ibn al-Imad likewise says that Mu'awiya proposed to her after Abu al-Darda and she declined {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 1 p. 333}. A footnote by the editors of the Shadharat, quoting al-Dhahabi's Siyar A'lam al-Nubala, reports that at his death she told Abu al-Darda he had asked for her from her parents in this world and they had married her to him, and that she now asked for him from himself in the next; he told her not to marry after him; and when Mu'awiya proposed she told him this, and he said to her: keep to fasting {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 1 p. 333}.

## Learning and standing
Ibn al-Imad says she had a large share of knowledge and works and great esteem in Syria {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 1 p. 333}. Al-Zirikli says she lived honoured among the Umayyads, spending six months at Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem) and six at Damascus {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. One of her reports there: when the call to the sunset prayer sounded while she and Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan were at the Rock in Jerusalem, she rose leaning on Abd al-Malik and he led her into the mosque; she sat with the women while he went to the maqam and led the people in prayer {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. Among her sayings al-Zirikli records that the best knowledge is knowing (ma'rifa) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah transmitted hadith for her {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}.

## Distinguishing her from the elder
The editors of the Shadharat note that the printed text and the manuscript read "Umm al-Darda al-Kubra", which they call an error; the correction rests on Ibn Asakir's history of Damascus, Usd al-Ghaba, Siyar A'lam al-Nubala and al-Isaba, and her name was Hujayma, or Juhayma, while Umm al-Darda al-Kubra was Khayra bint Abi Hadrad al-Aslami {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 1 p. 333}. Al-Zirikli agrees: he describes the elder as Khayra bint Abi Hadrad, called al-Kubra to distinguish her from al-Sughra, whose name was Hujayma bint Huyayy {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 328}.

## Death
Al-Zirikli prints the year of her death as "after 81 AH" (after 700 CE) and gives no birth year {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 77}. Ibn al-Imad mentions her in the events of 81 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 1 p. 333}.
