## Summary
Mohamed Bechari is a French-Moroccan jurist and among the leading figures of institutional Muslim life in Europe; he heads several bodies that represent French and European Muslims in public life and serves in international juristic institutions. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bechari}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1967 in Oujda, Morocco, received his primary and secondary education there and continued his higher studies in France. His institutional biographies record a doctorate, variously described as in computer science from the University of Paris and as in Islamic jurisprudence and its principles; the museum records the accounts as the sources give them. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bechari}

## Institutional leadership
Since its founding, Bechari has been secretary-general of the World Muslim Communities Council, based in Abu Dhabi, a body concerned with Muslim minorities and communities outside the Muslim world. In France he presides over the French National Federation of Muslims — one of the largest organisations structuring Muslim affairs there — and has been vice-president of the French Council of the Muslim Faith ⁦(CFCM)⁩. At the continental level he is secretary-general of the European Islamic Conference, an umbrella body seeking to represent Europe's Muslims before European institutions. He founded and headed the Ibn Sina ⁦(Avicenne)⁩ Institute for Human Sciences in Lille. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bechari}

## Juristic and international memberships
He is an appointed member of the International Islamic Fiqh Academy of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, representing the Muslim community of France, and has participated in its sessions as researcher and discussant. He is also a trustee of the Forum for Promoting Peace in Muslim Societies, a member of the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute for Islamic Thought in Jordan, a member of the advisory board of the King Abdullah bin Abdulaziz International Centre for Interreligious and Intercultural Dialogue ⁦(KAICIID)⁩ in Vienna, and a member of the European Council of Religious Leaders in Norway. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bechari}

## Writings and legacy
His writing concerns contemporary Islamic thought and the challenges of integration; titles recorded in the biographies include "The Question of Tolerance: An Introduction to a Human Civilizational System", "The Interplay between Philosophy and Ethics", and "Trends in the International System in the Post-Covid World" ⁦(2023)⁩, besides a juristic study on striving and ijtihad published in 2024. He has received the King Abdullah II Medal of Excellence and Egypt's Order of Arts and Sciences, and Muslim 500 editions rank him among influential Muslim figures; his career is widely read as a model of institutional Islam in the West. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bechari}

## Method and intellectual contribution
Bechari's intellectual work revolves around a central question: the organisation of Muslim life outside the Muslim world — the jurisprudence of minorities, the adaptation of religious institutions to European civil systems, and the training of imams and counsellors. The bodies he has led, from the French National Federation of Muslims to the European Islamic Conference to the Ibn Sina Institute, all work on building representative and educational frameworks rather than producing preaching discourse; his 2025 paper to the International Islamic Fiqh Academy on the rulings of electronic games illustrates the extension of this maqasid-based method to newly arising questions. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bechari}
## Notes on the record
Bechari's standing rests more on institution-building than on books: he belongs to the type that distributes its effort across boards, secretariats-general and juristic academies, which makes him a representative of the generation of "Islamic institution managers" in the West. Because of the shared surname he should be distinguished from other Moroccan figures named Bechari in other fields; the museum's record rests on his Muslim 500 profile, since he had no Wikipedia article at the time of editing. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bechari}
