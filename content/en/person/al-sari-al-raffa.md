## Overview
Al-Sari ibn Ahmad ibn al-Sari al-Kindi, Abu al-Hasan, known as al-Sari al-Raffa' (“the mender”), was a poet and littérateur of Mosul. In his youth he mended and embroidered clothes in a shop there, which earned him his name; when his verse matured he went to Sayf al-Dawla in Aleppo, praised him and stayed with him a while, then moved to Baghdad after the prince's death and praised a group of viziers and notables. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.81}

His poetry sold well until the two Khalidi brothers, Muhammad and Sa'id sons of Hashim, took him on in a flying match that drove him from the salons of the great; his world narrowed and he was forced into warraqa work, copying and selling his own verse and copying for others for pay, and he died in Baghdad in 366 AH (976 CE). He was sweet of phrase and gifted in simile and description; his works include his diwan and al-Muhibb wa-l-Mahbub wa-l-Mashmum wa-l-Mashrub. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.81}
