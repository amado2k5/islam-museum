## Summary
Muhammad ibn Sa'd ibn Mani' al-Hashimi by clientage, Abu 'Abd Allah of Basra, known as Ibn Sa'd and as the secretary of [[person/al-waqidi|al-Waqidi]], because he stayed long with his teacher Muhammad ibn 'Umar al-Waqidi and wrote for him. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 17} He is the author of [[work/al-tabaqat-al-kubra-ibn-sad|al-Tabaqat al-Kubra]]. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 55}

## Lineage and birth
- The sources differ on his lineage. Some make him a client of the Banu Hashim, through al-Husayn ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Ubayd Allah ibn al-'Abbas or one of his ancestors; others make him a Zuhri by clientage or by descent. The editor thinks the clientage was his grandfather's, perhaps his father's too, since al-Husayn died twenty-seven years before Ibn Sa'd was born. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 17-18}
- He was born in Basra in 168 AH and grew up there. He then studied in Kufa, Baghdad, Medina and Mecca, taking most in Mecca from [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna]], and finally settled in Baghdad until he died. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 18-19} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 36}

## His learning
The editor says his studies covered the Qur'an, hadith, law, genealogy, history, the science of transmitters, rare words, language and grammar. Ibn al-Jazari reports that he transmitted Qur'anic readings from al-Waqidi. He wrote no book but al-Tabaqat al-Kubra; a shorter al-Tabaqat al-Saghir survives in manuscript in Istanbul, according to Sezgin. A tract on al-Hasan al-Basri and a poem on the boasting of the Qahtanis over the 'Adnanis are also ascribed to him. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 42} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 56-57}

## In the inquisition
The historians relate that in 218 AH [[person/abd-allah-al-mamun|al-Ma'mun]] wrote to Ishaq ibn Ibrahim in Baghdad to test judges and hadith scholars on the createdness of the Qur'an. He ordered seven men brought to al-Raqqa, among them Ibn Sa'd, Yahya ibn Ma'in and Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Dawraqi. He tested and threatened them, and all of them gave the required answer; he sent them back to Baghdad, where their answer was made public before the shaykhs. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 36} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 490} The editor compares their stand with Ahmad ibn Hanbal's. He says he does not doubt the soundness of Ibn Sa'd's and his fellows' creed, since a person may weaken before the sword for fear of death. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 37}

## His standing as a critic of transmitters
- Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi relates with his chain that [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]] called him a liar over one report, and then excuses Ibn Sa'd and turns Ibn Ma'in's criticism away from him, as the editor reports. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 42}
- Al-Harith ibn Abi Usama and al-Husayn ibn Fahm, the two transmitters of al-Tabaqat, narrated from him; according to al-Mizzi and Ibn Hajar, Abu Dawud has a single report from him. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 54}
- After comparing his judgements in this section with those of other critics, the editor places him among the moderate critics, and quotes al-Sakhawi that his judgements on transmitters are "good and accepted". {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 77}

## Death
There are three dates for his death. Al-Safadi gives Sunday 4 Jumada al-Akhira 222 AH, and Ibn Abi Hatim 236 AH. His student al-Husayn ibn Fahm gives Sunday 4 Jumada al-Akhira 230 AH in Baghdad, with burial in the Bab al-Sham cemetery at the age of sixty-two. Most sources follow the last date, and the editor prefers it. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 58}
