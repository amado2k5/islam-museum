## Summary
Saniyasnain Khan is an Indian author and publisher, son of the well-known Islamic thinker Wahiduddin Khan, who founded Goodword Books in Delhi in 1999 and built in it the largest contemporary Indo-Arab project of Islamic children's literature. He is counted as the most prolific writer in the field worldwide, with millions of copies sold and translations into dozens of languages. {cite:wikipedia-saniyasnain-khan}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1959 in Azamgarh, Uttar Pradesh, and grew up in a scholarly household: his father Wahiduddin Khan was among India's best-known writers of Islamic thought and exegesis, from whose intellectual centre Saniyasnain began his own path, combining literary formation with running his father's publishing project before turning to children's literature. {cite:wikipedia-saniyasnain-khan}

## Goodword Books
He founded Goodword Books with a specific mission: to produce Islamic books for children in English and world languages presenting concepts, stories and values without direct sermonising, with illustration and production at global publishing standard. The house has issued hundreds of titles — prophets' stories for children, simplified biographies, values and manners series, dictionaries and activity books — many authored by himself, with his own works estimated at over a hundred titles. He has also hosted children's television programmes and runs a channel carrying his educational project. {cite:wikipedia-saniyasnain-khan} {cite:muslim500-saniyasnain-khan}

## Reach
His books have been translated into more than twenty languages including French, German, Spanish, Russian, Chinese, Arabic, Urdu, Turkish and Malay, distributed at international book fairs, with total sales estimated in the millions; his sources record his titles entering bestseller lists for Islamic children's literature in several markets. {cite:wikipedia-saniyasnain-khan}

## Awards
He has received literary and educational awards including the Kolkata Street award and publishing prizes in India and abroad, and his works appear in listings of the most influential Muslim publishers. {cite:wikipedia-saniyasnain-khan}

## Legacy
His career reads as the conversion of Islamic children's literature from local sermonising into a multilingual global publishing industry: the son of a reformist thinker who built a schoolbook series of stories and values reaching the homes of Muslims in the West and beyond — a model of religious children's publishing that achieves general-industry quality without abandoning the Islamic reference. {cite:wikipedia-saniyasnain-khan}
