## Summary

Nazik al-Malaika (1923–2007) was an Iraqi poet and pioneer of free verse; her poems of 1947, alongside al-Sayyab's, broke the classical 'amud and founded the modern Arabic poetic movement.{cite:wikipedia-nazik-al-malaika}

## Life

Born in Baghdad to a literary family, she graduated from the Higher Teachers' College then in comparative literature at the University of Wisconsin, worked as a university professor in Iraq and Kuwait, and spent her last years in Egypt.{cite:wikipedia-nazik-al-malaika}

## Poetry and criticism

Her collections include 'Ashiqat al-Layl (1947), Shazaya wa Ramad and Qararat al-Mawja, and her poem 'Cholera' is among the most famous modern Arabic poems. Her theoretical book Issues of Contemporary Poetry (1962) is a systematic defence of free verse.{cite:wikipedia-nazik-al-malaika}

## Standing

She is called the co-founder of free verse beside al-Sayyab, taught generations, received Arab honours, and her name remains bound to the rhythmic revolution in Arabic poetics.{cite:wikipedia-nazik-al-malaika}
