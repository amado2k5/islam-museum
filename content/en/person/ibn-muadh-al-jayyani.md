## Overview
Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Muadh al-Jayyani, a mathematician and astronomer from an Andalusian family, attributed to Jaén. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## Dates
The encyclopaedia says he was born around 379 AH and died around 471 AH. These two dates come from one source, so they need an additional source. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

The source counts him among the scholars of mathematics and astronomy.

## Chronology and identification

This extension uses additional read sources without re-certifying every inherited date. MacTutor qualifies identification with the namesake born in989:describing the1079eclipse would make him over ninety. Calvo reports scholarship proposing an early-eleventh-century birth. The989date therefore remains qualified tradition,not a verified exact birthday {cite:jayyani-mactutor biography} {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1}.

Death was after1079,not necessarily in that year as the old card could imply. The last chronological anchor is the eclipse;TDV writes after471/1079. We infer no new Hijri death year. His setting included Jaen judges and jurists;MacTutor reports residence in Cairo1012–1017. A geographical name does not establish every journey or observing location {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1} {cite:tdv-ibn-muadh-dizer biography} {cite:jayyani-mactutor biography}.

## Identity and fields

MacTutor explicitly calls him an Islamic mathematician and TDV individually calls him a Muslim scholar. That documents identity;the declared Sunni museum frame is separate from a personal denominational certificate and is not inferred from birthplace or office. Correct calculation does not establish faith,and an imperfect model does not refute it {cite:jayyani-mactutor summary} {cite:tdv-ibn-muadh-dizer twilight}.

Calvo describes the Jaen tables,built on al-Khwarizmi's tradition with additions involving lunar visibility,spherical trigonometry and stars. Chapter18includes qibla azimuth with a possible al-Biruni connection. We preserve that as scholarly attribution,not proof that every input passed directly from one author {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1}.

The unknown-arcs treatise offers trigonometry relatively independent of astronomy. Calvo calls it probably the earliest in the medieval West,not an unqualified global first. His ratio commentary defends Euclid's fifth book and clarifies its assumptions. These explain the scholar without counting every topic as another invention {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF2} {cite:jayyani-mactutor ratio-and-spherical-trigonometry}.

## Twilight and translated works

The [[work/ibn-muadh-twilight|twilight treatise]] links solar depression to [[invention/ibn-muadh-twilight-height|an assumed layer's height]]. Its52mile result is not the modern end of all air. TDV and Smith report Sabra's correction of the old Ibn al-Haytham attribution. The Arabic original is not known extant;Hebrew,Latin and Italian witnesses are reported by scholars,not inspected by the museum {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1-2} {cite:smith-muadh-twilight-1992 abstract-and-note3}.

The biography also reports a Hebrew eclipse translation and a projection-of-stellar-rays work. We do not settle the mechanical treatise in his favour:Smith's notes record objections to an attribution proposed elsewhere. Secondary sources,abstract and notes were read—not manuscripts or all his originals. That limit remains explicit alongside the richer biography {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1-2} {cite:smith-muadh-twilight-1992 note1}.
