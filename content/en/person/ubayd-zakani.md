Khwaja Nizam al-Din Ubayd Allah al-Zakani, known as Ubayd Zakani, came of a learned and official family of Qazvin descended from the Zakani branch of the Banu Khafaja, an Arab tribe settled in the Qazvin region at the start of the Islamic era. No birth year is recorded for him: Iranica places his birth probably before 1319, and the Persian biography puts it in the late 7th or early 8th century AH. His contemporary and fellow Qazvini Hamdallah Mustawfi already describes him in the Tarikh-i guzida of 1329 as a gifted poet and a well-educated writer, and one of his first works, the Nawadir al-amthal, belongs to that same year.{cite:iranica-obayd-zakani}{cite:wikipedia-ubayd-zakani-en}{cite:wikipedia-ubayd-zakani-fa}

After the fall of the Ilkhanate in 1335 Ubayd fled to Shiraz, then under Injuid rule, and joined the court of Abu Ishaq Inju (r. 1343–1357), to whom he wrote many panegyrics and his masnavi Ushshaq-nama (1350); Abu Ishaq's circle of poets also held the elder Khwaju Kermani and the young Hafez. When the Muzaffarid Mubariz al-Din Muhammad captured Shiraz in 1357 and executed Abu Ishaq, Ubayd left for the Jalayirid court of Shaykh Uways, to whom he dedicated qasidas, and later returned to Shiraz under Shah Shoja, whom he also praised. He preferred Shiraz to his native Qazvin.{cite:wikipedia-ubayd-zakani-en}

His most famous work is Mush-o Gorbeh, "Mouse and Cat", a political satire in verse attacking religious hypocrisy; among his other works are the Akhlaq al-Ashraf, the Resala-ye Delgosha, the Sad Pand and the Rish-nama. His satire is usually divided among religion, politics and ethics, and it was risky work in his own time. Edward Granville Browne called him perhaps the most remarkable parodist and satirical writer produced by Persia, and his style has been compared to Voltaire's.{cite:wikipedia-ubayd-zakani-en}

The sources disagree on his death, and the record preserves the disagreement in full: Iranica heads its entry "d. ca. 770/1370" but its text, following the Shahed-e Sadeq and the Kholasat al-ash'ar, gives 771/1369-70 or 772/1370-71, confirming he was alive in 768/1366 when he copied an astronomical manuscript; the Persian biography likewise puts the death between 768 and 772 AH but places it in Isfahan or Baghdad, while the English biography gives 1369–1371, possibly in Shiraz. The record follows the conventional year 1370, as debated.{cite:iranica-obayd-zakani}{cite:wikipedia-ubayd-zakani-fa}{cite:wikipedia-ubayd-zakani-en}
