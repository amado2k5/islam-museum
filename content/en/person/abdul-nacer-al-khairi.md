## Summary
Abdul-Nacer al-Khairi is an Algerian sheikh and a master of the Shadhili tariqa, of the lineage of the Moroccan saint Abd al-Salam ibn Mashish per his Muslim 500 profile, with a following in Algeria, Tunisia, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Malaysia and several European countries, gathered around his teaching of remembrance and recitation. {cite:muslim500-abdul-nacer-al-khairi}

## The Sufi method
He belongs to the Shadhili order, one of the largest Sunni Sufi paths of North Africa, and his profile names two pillars of his teaching: recitation of the Qur'an, and abundant blessing upon the Prophet — a method that suits mixed classes, drawing students from every walk of life in the countries he visits. {cite:muslim500-abdul-nacer-al-khairi}

## The Shadhili order
The Shadhiliyya is among the largest Sufi paths of the Muslim world, founded in the Arab Maghreb by Abu al-Hasan al-Shadhili in the thirteenth century and spread through Egypt, the East and the diaspora; al-Khairi counts among its contemporary Algerian sheikhs who joined the traditional zawiya to the journey of teaching disciples in distant lands. {cite:muslim500-abdul-nacer-al-khairi}

## Abd al-Salam ibn Mashish
His profile names his lineage to Abd al-Salam ibn Mashish, the Moroccan saint of the Prophet's house who lived in the twelfth-thirteenth centuries and ranks as a pole of Maghrebi Sufism; the descent ties al-Khairi's teaching to the oldest chains of the path in the Islamic West. {cite:muslim500-abdul-nacer-al-khairi}

## Geographic reach
His following is densest in his native Algeria and neighbouring Tunisia, then in the Arabian peninsula and Egypt, while his order's presence reaches Malaysia and European countries with Muslim diasporas, making him a link between Maghrebi Sufism and the communities of the diaspora. {cite:muslim500-abdul-nacer-al-khairi}

## Place in contemporary Sufism
The Muslim 500 files him under Preachers & Spiritual Guides, the category gathering tariqa sheikhs and teachers of purification from many lands; his recurring presence there points to a stable teaching circle in an era of competing religious authorities. {cite:muslim500-abdul-nacer-al-khairi}

## Legacy
His mark lies in carrying the Shadhili heritage to a new mobile generation across continents: a simple formula of recitation and blessing on the Prophet, and a lineage tying his zawiya to the classic Sufism of Morocco. The public record on him is brief, so the museum has kept to what its source states, noting that a fuller biography awaits further sources. {cite:muslim500-abdul-nacer-al-khairi}
