## Summary
Hadji Kamlon, also known as Maas Kamlon, was a Tausūg leader from Sulu who fought during the Second World War and afterwards led his own uprising against the Philippine government from 1948 to 1955, seeking to assert the sovereignty of the Sultanate of Sulu and North Borneo over the Tausūg. The sources record no birth or death year for him. He is regarded as a folk hero among the Tausūg. {cite:wikipedia-hadji-kamlon-en} {cite:wikidata-hadji-kamlon}

## The rebellion
Before the war a peaceful farmer, Kamlon surrendered to the authorities in 1947, then in 1948 launched his rebellion with a core of 25 men, his following growing to about 100 and later to some 300 fighters. The Philippine government branded him a bandit and outlaw, and in July 1952 deployed more than 5,000 troops with tanks, aircraft and naval units against him under Defense Secretary Ramon Magsaysay. Kamlon surrendered at Lahing-Lahing beach on 31 July 1952 after a secret meeting with Magsaysay, returned to rebellion a week later, surrendered again that November in Manila, was convicted with 23 of his men and sentenced to life imprisonment, was paroled under President Quirino with a grant of 5,000 hectares on Tawi-Tawi that he and his followers did not settle, and fought on — in August 1955 his men routed an entire government platoon — until his unconditional surrender on 24 September 1955 after the battle of Tandu Panuan. {cite:wikipedia-hadji-kamlon-en}

## Imprisonment, pardon and memory
His life sentence was upheld, the government holding that he had broken the conditions of his earlier partial pardon; Nur Misuari, later chairman of the Moro National Liberation Front, was among those who worked for his release, and aging rebels and their successors viewed him as a pioneer of the fight for Moro independence. He was finally pardoned by President Ferdinand Marcos. Ramon Revilla portrayed him in the 1981 film Kamlon. {cite:wikipedia-hadji-kamlon-en}
