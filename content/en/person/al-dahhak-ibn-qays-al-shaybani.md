## Overview
Al-Dahhak ibn Qays al-Shaybani, the Kharijite amir in the last trial of the Umayyad dynasty. He rose in al-Jazira until he prevailed there and took Mosul. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 p.641-643}

## Entering Iraq
He set out in Dhu al-Qa'da 127 AH and entered Iraq. Abd Allah ibn Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, the governor of Marwan, made peace with him, on condition that Kufa and its Sawad and what he had taken should be his, and that Ibn Umar should have Wasit, Maysan, Tustar, al-Ahwaz and Fars. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 p.641-643}

He appointed Mulhan al-Shaybani over Kufa, then al-Muthanna ibn Imran al-A'idhi. He gathered forces such as no Kharijites had gathered before him, until it was said they reached a hundred and twenty thousand. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 p.641-643}

## The Kufa dirham of 128 AH
The Umm Hajara hoard contains a dirham struck at Kufa in 128 AH with "no judgement but God's", the motto raised by the Kharijites, in the margin of its obverse. Muhammad Abu al-Faraj al-Ush connects it with the control of Kufa by al-Dahhak ibn Qays al-Shaybani al-Haruri in 128 AH, before al-Dahhak was killed in 128 or 129 and Yazid ibn Umar ibn Hubayra, Marwan II's governor, took Iraq; he cites al-Tabari for the truce between Abd Allah ibn Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and al-Dahhak dividing Kufa, its countryside and Kaskar. {cite:ush-kanz-umm-hajara-1972 p. 52}
