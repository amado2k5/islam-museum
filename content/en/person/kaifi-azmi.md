Kaifi Azmi (14 January 1919 – 10 May 2002) was an Indian Urdu poet, film lyricist and screenwriter, born Athar Husain Rizvi at Mijwan village in Azamgarh district, the son of a zamindar; "Kaifi" was his pen name and "Azmi" came from Azamgarh.{cite:wikipedia-kaifi-azmi-en}{cite:wikipedia-kaifi-azmi-ur} He wrote his first ghazal at eleven, abandoned his Persian and Urdu studies during the Quit India movement, and in 1943 became a full-time worker of the Communist Party of India, rising within the Progressive Writers' Movement to become one of the defining voices of socially conscious Urdu poetry, in collections from Jhankar (1943) to Awaara Sajde.{cite:wikipedia-kaifi-azmi-en}

In cinema he brought Urdu literary diction to the Hindi film song — Buzdil (1951), Kaagaz Ke Phool (1959), Haqeeqat (1964) and Pakeezah (1972) — and wrote the entire dialogue of Heer Raanjha (1970) in verse, besides the script and dialogues of Garam Hawa (1973).{cite:wikipedia-kaifi-azmi-en} His beliefs are contested in the sources: his daughter Shabana Azmi states publicly that her father was an atheist,{cite:movietalkies-shabana-azmi-2026} a centenary feature calls him "a confirmed Leftist and thus atheist",{cite:bollywood-hungama-kaifi-azmi-2019} while the Arabic biography records his birth into a Shia Muslim family{cite:wikipedia-kaifi-azmi-ar} and Wikidata carries Islam and atheism side by side.{cite:wikidata-kaifi-azmi} He died at Mumbai on 10 May 2002, having spent his last years developing his native Mijwan.{cite:wikipedia-kaifi-azmi-en}
