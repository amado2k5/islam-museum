## Overview
Constantine XI, the last Byzantine emperor, who led Constantinople's defence against Mehmed II in 857/1453.

## Before the siege
He strove desperately to turn the sultan away with gifts and money, then begged aid from Europe above all the Pope — moving to proclaim union of the Orthodox church with Rome, which enraged his Orthodox until one of their chiefs said, 'I would rather see the Turks' turbans in Byzantine lands than the Latin hat' {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 93-94}.

## Refusing to leave
Twice his councillors urged him to quit the city and summon relief; he refused, resolved to share his people's fate {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 100-103}. When Mehmed offered safe-conduct for a surrender, he answered that he would pay tribute — but for Constantinople he had sworn to defend her to his last breath: 'either I keep my throne or am buried beneath her walls' {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 104}.

## His end
Seeing the Ottoman banners on the towers he cast off his clothes so as not to be known, dismounted, and fought until he was killed on Tuesday 20 Jumada I 857 / 29 May 1453 {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 110}.
