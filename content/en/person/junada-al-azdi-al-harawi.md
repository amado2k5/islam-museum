## Summary
Junada ibn Muhammad ibn al-Husayn al-Harawi al-Azdi, Abu Usama — a philologist and grammarian who came to Egypt from Herat and settled there; he was killed in Dhu al-Hijja 399 AH. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 499}

## Details
Junada arrived in Egypt from Herat and stayed, keeping company with the hadith master Abd al-Ghani ibn Sa'id — Egypt's leading memorizer of the time — and with Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Sulayman al-Muqri' the Antioch grammarian; the three were close friends who met in the book house holding sessions of literary debate, and Junada lectured on language at the mosque of the Nilometer, Abu Sahl al-Harawi among his pupils. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 499}

When the Nile failed to rise in a dry year it was whispered to the ruler of Egypt — one of the Alid-line sovereigns — that Junada was an unlucky man who sat at the Nilometer teaching grammar and conjuring the river, so he ordered his death on 13 Dhu al-Hijja 399; his companion Ali ibn Sulayman was killed with him, while Abd al-Ghani went into hiding until he was safe, dying in 409. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 499}
