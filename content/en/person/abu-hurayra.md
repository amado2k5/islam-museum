## Summary
Abu Hurayra kept company with the Prophet for only about three years, yet he narrated more of the hadith than anyone in the Sunni collections. How one man produced thousands of reports is a question scholars still ask.

## Life
A man of the tribe of Daws in the Yemeni highlands, he came to Medina and embraced Islam about 629 CE, around the time of [[event/battle-of-khaybar|Khaybar]] {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. For the rest of the Prophet's life, the tradition pictures him at the edge of the mosque: poor, often hungry, and committing things to memory {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

After 632 CE he became a flood: reports from him fill the Sunni collections, on law, worship, the afterlife and the Prophet's sayings. He governed Bahrayn for a time on behalf of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], and died in Medina about 678–681 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
However the reports are weighed, a large part of what the Sunni tradition knows of the Prophet's sayings reaches us through this one man's memory.

## For young visitors
Abu Hurayra lived with the Prophet for three years and remembered so much that his name stands beneath thousands of stories and sayings, more than anyone else's.
