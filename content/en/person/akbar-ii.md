## Summary

Akbar II (22 April 1760 – 28 September 1837), born Mirza Akbar, was the nineteenth Mughal emperor from 1806 to 1837, the second son of Shah Alam II {cite:wikipedia-akbar-ii-en}.

## A pensioned emperor

He was born at Mukundpur while his father was in exile, and was crowned on 19 November 1806, the day of Shah Alam II's death {cite:wikipedia-akbar-ii-en}. By then the emperor's authority barely ran beyond the Red Fort: Delhi had been under British protection since 1803, and Akbar II reigned as a pensioned sovereign while the East India Company governed {cite:wikipedia-akbar-ii-en}. He pressed his claims on the Company through diplomacy, conferring the title of Raja on Ram Mohan Roy and sending him as his envoy to England {cite:wikipedia-akbar-ii-en}. He died at Delhi on 28 September 1837 and was succeeded by his son Bahadur Shah II, the last of the Mughal emperors {cite:wikipedia-akbar-ii-en} {cite:wikidata-akbar-ii}.

## Faith

His legal school is unattested in the sources consulted.
