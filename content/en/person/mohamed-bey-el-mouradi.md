# Mohamed Bey El Mouradi

Mohamed Bey El Mouradi (died 14 October 1696) was a Muradid Bey of Tunis, the eldest son of Murad II Bey and, through his mother, a grandson of Yusuf Dey {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-en} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-fr}. No article read gives his birth year; the Wikidata item's birth statement is decade-precision only, for the 1650s, so the queue row's 1655 is a midpoint guess no text attests {cite:wikidata-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi}. The English article dates his beylicate from 1675, the year of his father's death, while the French and Arabic articles count his effective tenure from 1686, when he took sole power after a long struggle {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-en} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-fr}.

His reign was consumed by the Muradid civil wars. His uncle Muhammad al-Hafsi contested him and was exiled in 1679; his brother Ali bin Murad, disappointed with his share of power, took refuge in the Beylik of Constantine and brought the northwestern tribes under Muhammad ben Cheker over with promises of gold and silver, besieged Tunis, and drove Muhammad Bey to Kairouan; Muhammad broke the siege and met Ali at the Battle of El Kerima on the Fahs plain in 1677, which Ali decisively won, taking Tunis while his brother stayed besieged in Kairouan {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-en}. Mediation by the Dey of Algiers produced a treaty in 1679, but the peace failed: the janissaries elected Ahmed Chelebi, the Algerians defeated him and sacked Tunis in 1686, and Muhammad, suspecting Ali of backing them, killed him and seized power for himself {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-en}. In 1694 the Dey Hadj Chabane invaded with the pretender Muhammad ben Cheker and annexed Tunis for months, appointing ben Cheker bey; in 1695 the Tunisians revolted, defeated ben Cheker at Kairouan, and restored the Muradid dynasty {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-en}.

Amid these troubles — the period called the Revolutions of Tunis — he built monuments that stand: the Sidi Mahrez Mosque in Tunis, modelled on the mosques of Istanbul with a great central dome; the rebuilt chachia souk; mosques and madrasas at Beja, El Kef, Gafsa, Tozeur and Gabes, among them the Al Bey Mosque at Kairouan; and in 1690 the Tebourba bridge-dam on the Medjerda {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-fr} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-ar}. He died on 14 October 1696 and was buried in the mausoleum of his grandfather Hammuda Pasha Bey; his sons Mourad and Hassan were too young to reign, so his brother Ramadan Bey succeeded him {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bey-el-mouradi-fr}.
