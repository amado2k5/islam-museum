## Overview
Amr ibn al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam al-Asadi was a son of al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam and a brother of [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr]]. Al-Dhahabi gives his mother as Umm Khalid bint Khalid ibn Sa'id of the Umayyad clan. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 198} The sources read know him mostly through one episode, the Umayyad attempt in about 60 AH to bring Abd Allah to allegiance by force, in which he led the expedition against his own brother. They disagree on how he died, and they give no date for his birth.

## His profile in al-Dhahabi
Al-Dhahabi says that Amr heard from his father and from his brother, that no transmitted report from him is known, and that he went as a delegate to Mu'awiya and to his son. He adds that there was a dispute between Amr and his brother Abd Allah. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 198} Citing al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar, he records that Amr was of exceptional beauty, so that people remarked that whoever spoke to him would regret it; he describes Amr as formidable and hard to approach, a man who sat down in a town and laid down his staff so that no one stepped over it without his leave, and who kept two hundred slaves. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 198}

## The expedition against Mecca
Ibn Kathir places the story in the year 60 AH. After the oath of allegiance to Yazid, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr went to Mecca and found [[person/amr-ibn-said-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn Sa'id ibn al-As]] there, and Yazid soon added Medina to Amr ibn Sa'id's command. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 518} According to Ibn Kathir, Amr ibn Sa'id set Amr ibn al-Zubayr, who was an enemy of his brother, against Abd Allah, and sent forces towards Mecca. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 518}

Al-Dhahabi, following al-Waqidi, tells how Yazid wrote to Amr ibn Sa'id to send troops against Abd Allah. When Amr ibn Sa'id asked who was most hostile to Abd Allah, he was told it was Amr, his brother, and appointed him chief of police in [[place/medina|Medina]]. Amr flogged people of Quraysh and the Ansar, calling them partisans of Abd Allah. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 199} Ibn Kathir reports from al-Waqidi that the men beaten included al-Mundhir ibn al-Zubayr and his son. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 518}

Amr then marched on [[place/mecca|Mecca]] with about a thousand Syrians, according to al-Dhahabi, with Unays al-Aslami in the vanguard; Ibn Kathir has two thousand men in all. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 199} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 518} Both accounts have him camp near Mecca and exchange messages with his brother. He is quoted as saying he wished Abd Allah to give his allegiance to Yazid and keep his oath, and Abd Allah replied that he would pray behind him and obey, but that he would not be led to Syria with a chain on his neck. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 199} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 518} Marwan ibn al-Hakam is reported to have advised against the campaign. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 518}

Abd Allah then sent a force under Abd Allah ibn Safwan. In al-Dhahabi's account Unays was killed and the troops with Amr scattered; Ibn Kathir has Unays defeated, and Amr's followers dispersing. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 199} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 518} Amr's other brother, Ubayda ibn al-Zubayr, gave him protection. Abd Allah accepted the protection as far as his own claim went, but said he would take retaliation for those whom Amr had harmed in Medina. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 199}

## How he died
Al-Dhahabi, citing Ibn Sa'd from al-Waqidi, says that people came forward each day to demand retaliation for the blows they had received, and that when Mus'ab ibn Abd al-Rahman said Amr had flogged him a hundred times, Abd Allah ordered a hundred lashes for Amr in return, from which he died. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 199} He then gives a second version: Amr survived the beating; Abd Allah later saw him sitting at his door after his release from prison and ordered him dragged back to prison, where he died before reaching it. His body was thrown in Shi'b al-Khayf, the place where Abd Allah was later crucified. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 200} Ibn Kathir says that Abd Allah had everyone whom Amr had flogged in Medina flog him in turn, except al-Mundhir ibn al-Zubayr and his son, who refused to take retaliation; that Amr was jailed with a man called Arim, so that the place was known as Arim's prison; and that it was also said he died under the whip, a point the author leaves to God's knowledge. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 519} The sources read do not date his death precisely; al-Dhahabi places his entry in the volume for the years 61 to 80 AH.

## What is not known here
No date of birth is given, and the reports differ on how he died. The museum records them as reports from al-Dhahabi and Ibn Kathir, not as findings. A search of the local text of the Encyclopaedia of Islam found no article on him.
