## Summary
Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Nuri was a Sufi of Baghdad. [[person/abu-nasr-al-sarraj|Abu Nasr al-Sarraj]] treats him in [[work/kitab-al-luma-fi-al-tasawwuf|al-Luma']], in the book on interpreting ecstatic sayings, and says he lived in the days of al-Muwaffaq. {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 546} He describes him as "among the ecstatics and the people of subtle allusions", with difficult sayings and many verses, one who "drew from a great sea". {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 547}

## His trial before al-Muwaffaq
Al-Sarraj relates that [[person/ghulam-khalil|Ghulam Khalil]], who denounced him, reported to [[person/abu-ahmad-al-muwaffaq|al-Muwaffaq]] that there was a zindiq in Baghdad whose blood was lawful, and testified that he had heard al-Nuri say he loved God passionately and God loved him; al-Nuri cited the verse of love [[passage/quran-5-54|al-Ma'ida 54]] and said passionate love is no more than love, and al-Muwaffaq wept. {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 546} They also testified against words he said on hearing the call to prayer and the barking of dogs, which he explained as jealousy for God's remembrance and glorification without show; and he was brought before the caliph again for saying he had been in his house with God, citing the verse of nearness [[passage/quran-50-16|Qaf 16]], after which the caliph let him speak. {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 546} {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 547} See [[event/mihnat-ghulam-khalil|the trial of Ghulam Khalil]].

## Reports and sayings
Al-Sarraj reports from Abu 'Amr ibn 'Alwan that three hundred dinars, the price of property sold for him, were brought to al-Nuri; he climbed the bridge over the Sarat canal and threw them into the water one by one, saying: "My Beloved, would You beguile me away from You with the like of this?" When some people found fault with this, al-Sarraj said that if he knew the dinars would distract him from God for the blink of an eye, he ought to have thrown them in all at once. {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 547} Among his sayings is "the nearness of nearness", in the sense of what al-Sarraj calls "the farness of farness", which al-Sarraj likens to the saying "the sins of those brought near are the good deeds of the righteous". {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 547} He quotes verses al-Nuri wrote to [[person/abu-said-al-kharraz|Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz]] on keeping a secret. {cite:luma-sarraj-jadir-dar-al-fath p. 548} These are reports al-Sarraj relates, and his explanations of them are Sufi explanations.
