## In brief
Musa b. Uqba, the memoriser al-Asadi of Medina, client of the family of al-Zubayr b. al-Awwam; he heard Umm Khalid bint Khalid al-Muhabbiyya, Urwa, Salim, Abu Salama b. Abd al-Rahman, al-A'raj, and a group, and compiled the Maghazi. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.143-144}

## Transmission and the Maghazi
Ibn Jurayj, Malik, Ibn Uyayna, Hammad b. Isma'il, Ibn al-Mubarak, Abu Damra, Muhammad b. Fudayl, and others narrated from him; al-Waqidi said "Musa was a mufti and jurist", Ahmad b. Hanbal said "you must have the maghazi of Musa b. Uqba, for they are reliable", and al-Dhahabi read Musa's Maghazi at Mezza under Abu Nahr al-Farisi. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.144}

## His death
He died in 141 AH. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.144}

## In al-Umari's al-Sira al-Nabawiyya al-Sahiha
[[person/akram-diya-al-umari|Akram Diya' al-Umari]] describes him (d. 140 AH) as "a trustworthy hadith scholar, a student of al-Zuhri", and quotes Malik's praise of his book of campaigns as the soundest, Yahya ibn Ma'in's "the book of Musa ibn Uqba from al-Zuhri is among the soundest of these books", and al-Shafi'i's "there is nothing in the campaigns sounder than the book of Musa ibn Uqba, despite its small size and its freedom from most of what is in others' books". {cite:sahih-sira-umari-ulum-hikam-1994 p.55} He cites al-Dhahabi that it fills a volume of modest size, "mostly sound, with good mursal reports, but brief and in need of further explanation and completion", and notes that Ibn Hajar saw it and held a licence to transmit it. {cite:sahih-sira-umari-ulum-hikam-1994 p.56} He holds that the hadith books support the biographies of Ibn Ishaq and Musa ibn Uqba in most aspects of the sira. {cite:sahih-sira-umari-ulum-hikam-1994 p.41}
