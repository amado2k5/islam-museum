## Overview
Mazdak, the founder of a doctrine that appeared in the reign of Kavad ibn Firuz, king of the [[polity/sasanian-empire|Persians]]. The *Sealed Nectar* describes it as a call to licence, and Kavad followed him as many of his subjects did. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}

## Effect in al-Hira
Kavad sent to [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-ma-al-sama|al-Mundhir ibn Ma al-Sama]], king of al-Hira, calling him to this doctrine. He refused, so Kavad removed him, and appointed [[person/al-harith-ibn-amr-al-kindi|al-Harith ibn Amr al-Kindi]] after he had answered the call. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}

## His death
Kavad was succeeded by [[person/khosrow-i-anushirvan|Khosrow Anushirvan]], who strongly disliked this doctrine, so he killed Mazdak and many who professed it, and restored al-Mundhir to al-Hira. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}

## What is not stated here
The description of the doctrine as licence is the author's wording. The description of it by Ibn al-Nadim as a doctrine of "sharing and abandoning domination" is in this entry's data. Modern academic views on Mazdakism are not yet added.

## For young visitors
Mazdak was a man in Persia who called for a new doctrine which the king himself followed, and then the next king killed him.
