## Overview
Ahmad ibn al-Husayn, Abu al-Qasim ibn Qasi, was the first rebel in al-Andalus when the state of the Almoravids was faltering. The entry dates his death to 546 AH, equal to 1151 CE, and gives no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}.

## Origin and beginnings
He was of Roman origin from the countryside of Silves; he became Arabised, acquired polite learning and composed verse, then devoted himself to preaching and gained many followers {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}.

## The revolt
He claimed guidance and took the title of imam. He was sought and went into hiding, and a group of his companions was seized and taken to Seville. From his hiding place he advised those who remained to attack the fortress of Mertola in the west of al-Andalus; they took it and Ibn Qasi joined them. His position then weakened and they deposed him, and he was restored {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}.

## The end
In 540 AH he went over to the Almohads, disowning what he had claimed; they trusted him and appointed him over his town, Silves, but he returned to opposition and the people of Silves killed him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}.

## A book attributed to him
Al-Zirikli says it appears that he is the author of Khal' al-Na'layn fi al-Wusul ila Hadrat al-Jam'ayn, a short work on Sufism that Muhyi al-Din ibn Arabi commented on. His wording presents this as likely rather than certain {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}.

## Death
He died in 546 AH, equal to 1151 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}. The entry gives no place of death beyond his killing by the people of Silves.
