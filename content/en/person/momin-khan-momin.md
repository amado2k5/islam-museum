# Momin Khan Momin

Momin Khan Momin (1800–1852) was an Urdu poet and hakim (Unani physician) of late Mughal Delhi, a contemporary of Ghalib and Zauq who never achieved their fame {cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-en}. He was born in Delhi in 1800 into a Muslim family of Kashmiri origin whose profession was Unani medicine — he was also known by the name Hakeem Khan — and he trained as a physician under his father {cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-en}{cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-ar}.

Poetry, which he had begun at about ten years old, gradually took over his life; his takhallus (pen name) was Momin, a name local tradition connects with the Sufi saint Shah Abdul Aziz {cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-en}. He left a divan and six masnavis, and wrote in genres from ghazal and qasida to rubai and marsiya, the elegy on Karbala {cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-en}. The famous story that Ghalib offered him his entire divan in exchange for one of Momin's verses is retold by Altaf Hussain Hali {cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-en}.

He died at Delhi on 14 May 1852 — the English article pairs the day with 24 Rajab 1268 AH — after falling from the roof of his house, and is buried in Delhi's Mehdiyan cemetery {cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-en}{cite:wikidata-momin-khan-momin}. No source read names a school or order for him personally: his articles' categories file him among Sufi saints, and his second marriage was into the family of the Sufi poet Khwaja Mir Dard — a filing and a family connection, neither of which the museum treats as an affiliation {cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-en}{cite:wikipedia-momin-khan-momin-ur}.
