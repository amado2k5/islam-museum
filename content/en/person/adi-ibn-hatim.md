## Summary
[[person/adi-ibn-hatim|Adi ibn Hatim]] ibn Abdullah al-Ta'i, son of the famed generous Hatim, kunya Abu Tarif. He became Muslim in year 9 AH — or year 10, it is said — having been Christian before {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1014}.

## His conversion
Abu Ubayda ibn Hudhayfa relates from Adi: "when the Prophet was sent I hated him with a severe hatred", so he went to the furthest land on the Roman side and hated his place more; he said: "if I went to him — if he is a liar it will not escape me, and if truthful I will follow him" — so he came. When he reached Medina the Prophet told him: "Adi, become Muslim and be safe"; he said: "I have a religion"; he said: "I know your religion better than you — do you not head your people? ... do you not take the quarter-tribute? ... that is not lawful for you in your religion" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1015}.

In another account from Abbad ibn Hubaysh, he came to the Prophet in the mosque with no safe-conduct and no letter; he took his hand and asked him: "do you know of any god besides God? ... do you know of anything greater than God?" He said no; the Prophet said: "the Jews are those angered against, and the Christians are astray" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1015}.

## In the Ridda and after
He stood firm in his Islam through the Ridda, brought his people's alms to Abu Bakr, witnessed the conquest of Iraq, then settled in [[place/kufa|Kufa]], witnessed Siffin with [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], and died past sixty, well advanced {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1014}. Al-Sha'bi relates that Umar told him: "you believed when they disbelieved, recognised when they denied, were loyal when they betrayed, and came forward when they drew back — and the first alms to whiten the faces of the Messenger of God's companions was the alms of Tayy" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1015}.

## His age
Khalifa said he reached one hundred twenty; Abu Hatim al-Sijistani said one hundred eighty {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1014}. Adi said: "no prayer has been called since I became Muslim without me being in ablution" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1014}.

## The white thread hadith
Ibn Hajar mentions that the two Sahihs carry the story of his taking God's saying [[passage/quran-2-187|al-Baqara 187]] literally, and the Prophet's saying to him: "your pillow is indeed wide" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1015}.
