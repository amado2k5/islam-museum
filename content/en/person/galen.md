## Summary
Galen is an ancient physician who appears in the Canon as an authority in anatomy. Ibn Sina calls him the most excellent of physicians and gives him as the source of views on the shapes of the head and on the sensation of the teeth. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.44} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.47}

## What Ibn Sina takes from him
In the anatomy of the skull Ibn Sina reports his view that a head lacking both projections becomes like a ball with equal dimensions, so that the division of its sutures is equal; that the head cannot have a fourth unnatural shape with length less than breadth without some part of the ventricles or substance of the brain being lacking; and that he upheld [[person/buqrat|Hippocrates]] in counting only four shapes of the head. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.44} In the anatomy of the teeth he gives Galen's saying that experience shows the teeth do feel, helped by a power that comes from the brain, so that they tell hot from cold. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.47}

## A note on the name
The museum has another record named [[person/jalinus|Jalinus]], a commander who fought the Muslims at the Euphrates as al-Mas'udi tells it; he is a different person. This record is for the physician Ibn Sina cites. No birth, death or homeland is entered, because the pages of the Canon read here state none.
