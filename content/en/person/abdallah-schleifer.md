## Summary

Abdallah Schleifer ⁦(1935 – 27 March 2025)⁩ — born Marc Schleifer in the Bronx — was an American journalist, professor and editor who embraced Islam in the 1960s and built a second life in Cairo: NBC and ABC correspondent, Adham Center director at the American University in Cairo, Middle East editor of The Muslim 500, and a longtime student of the Sufi path. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-schleifer} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 233}

## First life: New York to Jerusalem

Born Marc Schleifer in the Bronx, New York, in 1935, he worked in the 1950s and 1960s across American journalism and the city's avant-garde — he wrote for The Nation, covered the civil rights movement, and as a foreign correspondent for NBC covered the 1967 Six-Day War. His reporting from Jerusalem and Amman, and the years he spent among Sufi communities, preceded his conversion to Islam, when he took the name Abdallah. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-schleifer}

## Cairo

Settling in Cairo, he became Middle East correspondent and then bureau chief for NBC, later producing for ABC News; he interviewed the region's heads of state across four decades, including Anwar Sadat. At the American University in Cairo he founded and directed the Kamal Adham Center for Television Journalism, where a generation of Arab broadcast journalists trained, and served as professor emeritus of journalism. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-schleifer} {cite:auc-abdallah-schleifer}

## The Muslim 500

As senior editor of The Muslim 500 — the Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre's annual survey of the world's most influential Muslims — he wrote and edited the profiles and obituaries the volume is known for; his own entry in the 2026 edition appears among its obituaries, a volume published in his lifetime's editorial circle. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 233} {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-schleifer}

## Death

He died in Cairo on 27 March 2025, aged 89 — on, the obituary notes, the 27th night of Ramadan. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-schleifer} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 233}
