## Summary
Ahmad ibn Ja'far ibn Hani, filled with meaning and free of idleness, a man of lofty states. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (printed number 688, page 405), which runs over into the top of page 406. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## Reasoning from signs
He reasoned from firm pillars (one word in the scan is unclear), reflected on proofs and signs, and took lessons from the proofs set up as guidance and from the marks of blame. His habit was to race ahead, looking for the gifts and lights that reach the hearts. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## His sayings on help and fear
Abu Nu'aym relates from his father that the servant does not receive help from his master while he leans on another; that a servant who gives his master sincere counsel in his dealings is clothed with one of the master's robes, whose light and vision show on him; that one who does not let piety and watchfulness judge between himself and his master is veiled from unveiling and vision; and that one who prefers his master is protected from the filth of the world. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## Guidance and success
He used to say that whoever makes the world his road to Paradise will have a signpost of guidance set up for him, so that he does not lose his way. He also said that when fear settles in the heart, the knowledge of success is seen in the limbs. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## Service and nearness
He used to say that whoever keeps to service inherits the stations of nearness, and that these stations bring the sweetness of intimacy. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## A report through Abu Khallad
He relates, through a chain that names Abu Qurra and Abu Khallad, who had companionship with the Prophet, a report that when one sees a man given asceticism in the world and little speech, one should draw near him, because he is taught wisdom. The wording is not given here, and the chain is garbled in two places in the scan. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## The tale of Jesus
At the top of page 406 he relates, through a chain from Musa ibn Tarif, that Jesus came to a sleeping man and asked him to rise. The man said he had left the world to its people, and Jesus told him to sleep where he was. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/406}
