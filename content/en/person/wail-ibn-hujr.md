## Summary
[[person/wail-ibn-hujr|Wa'il ibn Hujr]] al-Hadrami; Ibn Hajar mentions several sayings on his lineage. His father was "of Yemen's ayqal" (crowned chiefs), and he himself came in delegation to the Prophet {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1559} {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1560}. The Prophet marked off a land and granted it to him, sending Mu'awiya with him to take possession — "in his well-known story with him" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1560}.

## What Abu Nu'aym relates
Abu Nu'aym said: the Prophet seated him on the minbar, made him a land-grant and wrote him a covenant, saying: "this is Wa'il, chief of the ayqal"; then Wa'il came down to [[place/kufa|Kufa]] and his descendants stayed there {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1560}.

## What Ibn Hibban relates
Ibn Hibban said: he was of the remnant of kings' sons at Hadramawt; the Prophet granted him land and sent Mu'awiya with him. Mu'awiya said: "mount me behind you" — he said: "I am not for kings' riders". When Mu'awiya later became caliph, Wa'il came to him; Mu'awiya received and honoured him — Wa'il said: "I wished I had carried him before me" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1560}.

## Narration and death
He related from the Prophet; from him related his sons Alqama and Abd al-Jabbar, his wife Umm Yahya, a client of theirs, Kulayb ibn Shihab, Hujr ibn Anbas and others. Wa'il died in Mu'awiya's caliphate {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1560}. Ibn Sa'd said he settled at Kufa {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1560}.
