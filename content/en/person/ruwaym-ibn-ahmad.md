## Summary
Abu Muhammad Ruwaym ibn Ahmad was a man of Baghdad whom [[person/al-qushayri-abu-l-qasim|al-Qushayri]], in [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|the Risala]], counts "among the great shaykhs"; he says Ruwaym died in 303 AH and was a Qur'an reciter and a jurist of the school of Dawud. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 163}

## His sayings in the Risala
- Part of the wisdom of the wise is to be lenient with his brethren in rulings and strict with himself, for leniency towards them is following knowledge and strictness with himself is the rule of scrupulousness. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 163}
- Asked by Abu 'Abd Allah ibn Khafif for counsel, he said: this affair is nothing but giving up one's soul; if you can enter it on that condition, do so, otherwise do not busy yourself with the trifles of the Sufis. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 163}
- Sitting with any class of people is safer than sitting with the Sufis, for people sit on outward forms while this community sits on realities, demanding of themselves the reality of scrupulousness and constant truthfulness. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 163}
- If God grants you both speech and deeds and then takes the speech and leaves the deeds, it is a blessing; if He takes the deeds and leaves the speech, it is a calamity; and if He takes both, it is a punishment. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 164}

## The story of the jug
Al-Qushayri relates that Ruwaym passed through a street in Baghdad in the midday heat, thirsty, and asked for water at a house; a girl opened the door with a jug, and on seeing him said: "A Sufi drinking by day!" After that he never broke his fast again. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 164}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 574 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] and describes him as learned in the Qur'an and its meanings. He records that Ruwaym was named after his grandfather, Ruwaym ibn Yazid the Qur'an reader, who transmitted from al-Layth ibn Sa'd; for that reason he ends the notice with a hadith transmitted by the grandfather. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/296} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/301-302}

**Short sayings.** [[person/jafar-al-khuldi|Ja'far al-Khuldi]] relates from him that sincerity is no longer seeing your own act, and that futuwwa (spiritual chivalry) is to excuse your brothers' slips and not to treat them in a way that leaves you owing them an apology. He used to say that resting in spiritual states is self-deception. A man asked him for counsel and he said: there is nothing but giving up the spirit; otherwise do not busy yourself with the trifles of the Sufis, for this matter is built on principles. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/296-297}

**His treatise on the causes of error.** The longest text in the notice is one Ruwaym wrote when, as he says, he saw seekers bewildered and novices slackening, and saw people who claimed the knowledge of gnosis belittling spiritual states and growing lax in works. He traces this to two causes: hurrying to a rank before its time, and ignorance of the road to it together with neglect of piety. He then sorts the claimants into groups; some had drowned in talk of union and separation, and some no longer told truth from falsehood or Creator from creature. Against them he sets the group he considers sound: those who kept each state in its time with fidelity and each work with sincerity, and did not rise to a station before mastering the one before it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/297-301}

**Definitions.** Patience is giving up complaint. Contentment is finding affliction sweet. Certainty is witnessing. Trust is no longer seeing intermediaries. Intimacy is to feel estranged from all but your Beloved. Love is conformity in every state. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/301}
