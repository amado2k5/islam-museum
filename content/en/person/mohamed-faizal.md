## Summary
Mohamed Faizal Mohamed Abdul Kadir is a Singaporean judge appointed to the High Court in March 2026 after eighteen years in the Singapore Legal Service. In 2020 he became the first Muslim — and the youngest lawyer — appointed Senior Counsel in the country, and he is counted among the most prominent legal and academic faces of Singapore's Muslim minority community. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-faizal}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1980 in Singapore, attended Bedok View Secondary School, and graduated from the National University of Singapore Faculty of Law in 2005 with first-class honours, collecting several prestigious prizes and scholarships as a student. He continued at Harvard Law School, taking his LLM in 2009, and his thesis won the International Insolvency Institute's Gold Medal prize. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-faizal}

## Legal career
He spent eighteen years in the Singapore Legal Service across many posts: justices' law clerk and assistant registrar at the Supreme Court, then deputy public prosecutor in the Attorney-General's Chambers, general counsel and director (legal) of the Singapore Medical Council, rising to Second Chief Prosecutor of the Crime Division. He handled prominent criminal cases including the prosecution arising from the death of the Myanmar domestic worker Piang Ngaih Don, which drew wide national attention. Appointed Senior Counsel in 2020 — the first Malay-Muslim and youngest holder of the rank in the Service's history — he became Judicial Commissioner of the Supreme Court in 2024 and a Judge of the High Court in March 2026. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-faizal} {cite:muslim500-mohamed-faizal}

## Community and educational work
Outside the courtroom he founded several educational scholarships and awards, including the Phoenix Grant at the NUS Faculty of Law supporting lower-income students, school-level education initiatives, and served on bodies including the Appeal Board of the Islamic Religious Council of Singapore (MUIS). {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-faizal}

## Recognition
The World Economic Forum named him a Young Global Leader in 2016; he has received the Public Administration Medal (Bronze), the President's Volunteerism and Philanthropy Award, the Association for Conflict Resolution's International Outstanding Leadership Award — the first Asia-Pacific recipient — and the title International Mediator of the Year from the UK Civil Mediation Council and the College of Mediators, as well as Berita Harian's Anugerah Jauhari achiever award. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-faizal}

## Legacy
His career is read as a new opening for Muslims at the top of Singapore law and presented in local media as evidence of the Malay-Muslim minority's integration into the country's professional elite; his educational giving is attributed to a model of a judge repaying the society that raised him. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-faizal}
