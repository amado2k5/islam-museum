## Overview
A Circassian Mamluk sultan, titled al-Mu'ayyad and given the kunya Abu Nasr. He came to the sultanate in 815 AH after al-Nasir Faraj was deposed. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 108}

## Rise to the sultanate
He defeated al-Nasir Faraj and killed him. The caliph al-Musta'in entrusted him with managing the kingdom and gave him the title "Order of the Kingdom". {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 767-770} Then he asked the caliph to entrust the sultanate to him, and the caliph made a condition that he come down from the citadel, to which he did not agree. He prevailed over the sultanate, deposed al-Musta'in in Sha'ban 815 AH, and gave the allegiance to Dawud al-Mu'tadid. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 767-770}

The judges of Syria ruled that it was not lawful to depose the caliph, and Nawruz fought him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 767-770}

## The Mu'ayyadiyya madrasa
The Mu'ayyadiyya madrasa was completed in his days in 822 AH, and he appointed as its shaykh al-Shams ibn al-Diyari. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 767-770}

## Notes for researchers
- The reports come from al-Suyuti and from Ibn Taghribirdi in the "Shadharat", and each judgement is attributed to its author.

## In al-Sakhawi's al-Daw' al-Lami'
[[person/al-sakhawi|Al-Sakhawi]] gives an entry for Shaykh al-Mahmudi, later al-Zahiri, al-Mu'ayyad Abu al-Nasr, Circassian by origin: born about 770, he came to Cairo aged twelve. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/308} He was captured by Timur's army at Aleppo and escaped, and reigned as sultan for eight years, five months and eight days. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/309} He died at the beginning of al-Muharram 824 according to Ibn Hajar, or on 9 al-Muharram according to al-Maqrizi, and was buried in the dome of his mosque. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/309} {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/310} He built his mosque at Bab Zuwayla on the site of the Khizanat Shama'il prison, in fulfilment of a vow made when he was imprisoned there. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/309} {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/310} Al-Sakhawi gives both Ibn Hajar's praise of him and al-Maqrizi's censure. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/310} {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 3/311}

## In Yeomans
Yeomans writes that al-Mu'ayyad Shaykh (1412-1421) had been Barquq's mamluk and was imprisoned several times, once in the Khazanat al-Shama'il near Bab Zuwayla, where he vowed to destroy the prison and build a mosque in its place; he built [[building/al-muayyad-mosque-cairo|his complex]] (1416-1421) with its minarets on Bab Zuwayla, and a maristan near the Citadel (1418-1420). {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.208} He seized power after a short interregnum of the caliph al-Musta'in, and Yeomans sees the succession thereafter as a bitter struggle that weakened the state. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.208}
