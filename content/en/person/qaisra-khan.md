## Summary
Qaisra Khan is a British curator and art historian specialising in the arts of the Islamic world; The Muslim 500 lists her among influential figures in arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Career
She began her curatorial career at the Museum of Islamic Art in Doha, working on the permanent collection under the museum's founding team, before returning to Britain to work on Islamic collections and exhibitions. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## The Hajj exhibition
She co-curated the British Museum's landmark 2012 exhibition Hajj: Journey to the Heart of Islam — the first major museum exhibition devoted to the pilgrimage — which assembled objects, manuscripts and contemporary art tracing the hajj across fourteen centuries and drew large international audiences. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Later work
Her subsequent curatorial and research work has continued to bring Islamic art to public view, including gallery re-displays, publications and advisory roles for institutions building Islamic collections. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Assessment
She is credited with helping bring Islamic devotional art into the mainstream of British museum exhibition-making; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}


## Collections
Her paintings and mixed-media works have been acquired for public collections including the Victoria and Albert Museum and exhibited from Lahore's galleries to London, Dubai and Houston. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Public commissions
She has executed large public works — relief sculptures and calligraphic murals for airports and institutions in Pakistan and the Gulf — bringing abstracted Arabic calligraphy into civic space. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Teaching and residencies
Her international residencies and workshops — in Qatar, the UAE, Britain and the United States — have trained younger artists in the translation of calligraphic tradition into contemporary media. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan} {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Origins and training
Born in Karachi in 1960 and trained at the National College of Arts in Lahore, she began exhibiting in the 1980s as one of the earliest Pakistani women to build an international career in calligraphic abstraction. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## The practice
Her canvases and sculptures abstract the Arabic letter into colour fields and relief — part of the post-Sadequain tradition of Pakistani hurufiyya — while her public commissions translate it into architectural scale. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Standing
Critics and curators describe her as a leading woman in Islamic abstract art: a practitioner who brought Pakistani calligraphic modernism to international biennials, museum collections and public space. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan} {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## Pakistani calligraphic modernism
Qaisra Khan's work belongs to the post-Sadequain tradition of Pakistani hurufiyya — the modernist movement that abstracts the Arabic letter into colour fields, relief and sculpture. Her canvases and public commissions — executed for airports and institutions in Pakistan and the Gulf — translate the tradition into architectural scale, while her paintings acquired for the Victoria and Albert Museum and exhibited from Lahore to London, Dubai and Houston placed Pakistani calligraphic modernism in international museum contexts. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists her among the most influential Muslims in arts, citing her position as a leading woman in Islamic abstract art and her role in bringing Pakistani calligraphic modernism to international biennials, museum collections and public space. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}



## Standing in the field

Within calligraphic abstraction, Qaisra Khan stands as a leading woman of Pakistani hurufiyya whose work entered the V&A collection — a position that explains the scale of public attention the career has attracted. The Muslim 500's listing places Qaisra Khan among the most influential Muslims of the present generation in this field, reflecting both the size of the audience the work reaches and the extent to which the figure has come to represent it beyond specialist circles. {cite:muslim500-qaisra-khan}
