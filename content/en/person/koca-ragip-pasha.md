## Overview
Muhammad Ragib Pasha was a self-made Turkish statesman and a scholar of Arabic, born and died in Istanbul, which al-Zirikli calls al-Asitana {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 1110 AH, 1698 CE, and his death as 1176 AH, 1763 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}.

## Life
He rose through the offices of the state from a junior clerk to treasury accountant to secretary of the grand vizierate {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He was appointed governor of Egypt in 1159 to 1161 AH, and al-Zirikli says he struck hard at the Mamluks {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He then governed al-Raqqa, then Aleppo in 1168 AH, then Syria with the command of the pilgrimage caravan in 1170 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He was appointed grand vizier and stayed in that office for six years and some months, under the sultans Uthman III and [[person/mustafa-iii-uthmani|Mustafa III]] {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He married Salihah Sultan, the sister of Sultan Mustafa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He assembled a rich library that is known by his name, and was buried beside it in Istanbul; his own works are in it {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He left building works in Aleppo and elsewhere {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}.

## Works
He wrote Safinat al-Ragib wa-Dafinat al-Talib, printed, a collection of literature and studies in Arabic, which is also called Safinat al-Ulum {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He also compiled selections, in manuscript, from the verse of earlier poets, which include some of his own, and a treatise on prosody, in manuscript {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}. He composed verse in Arabic, Turkish and Persian, with a diwan in each {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 123}.
