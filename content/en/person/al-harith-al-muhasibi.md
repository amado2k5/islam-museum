## Overview
Al-Harith ibn Asad al-Muhasibi al-Baghdadi, one of the ascetics among the theologians, known for worship, renunciation of the world, and exhortation. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.261}

## His life and works
He was a jurist and theologian, leading in the books of hadith, and learned in the doctrines of the ascetics. He died in 243 AH according to the "Fihrist". He wrote "Kitab al-Tafakkur wa al-I'tibar". Al-Khatib says he had many books on asceticism, the principles of religion, and refutations of the Mu'tazilis. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.261}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 465 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]]; he calls him the watchful witness of God's regard and the strict reckoner of his own self, firm in the principles of knowledge, averse to idle disputation, a suppressor of opponents and a counsellor of disciples. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/73-74}

**A morsel that his heart refused.** Al-Junayd tells that al-Harith once took a morsel of food, and when he had eaten it his self was disturbed; he would not swallow it, and threw it into a passage. He said: if this food were pleasing to God my self would accept it, but I have thrown away that morsel. He described his need as severe, but he strove to obtain food, and his self would not accept what was not pleasing to God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/75}

**Sayings.** He said: if you do not hear God's call, how will you answer His prayer? Whoever is content with something apart from God has made it his decree. He said that the wrongdoer is regretful and the wronged is safe, even if people praise the one and blame the other; the contented is rich even when hungry, and the covetous is poor even when he owns much. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/76}

**A divine address to the guides.** On page 77 he gives an address in the voice of the Truth to "the guides", in which God says: whoever comes to you sick from my absence, cure him; whoever flees from my service, bring him back; whoever forgets my favours and bounties, remind him. I have addressed you because I am forbearing, and the forbearing use nothing but forbearance. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/77}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Al-Harith's words on love, longing, and the standing between Paradise and the Fire continue on pages 78–82 of the tenth volume. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/78-82}

**Love and longing.** Al-Harith said that love of God is intense longing, because longing in itself is the heart's recollection of the sight of the Beloved. He noted that scholars differed on the nature of longing: one group said it is the heart's waiting for the moment of meeting. Love is known by its marks on the bodies of lovers and in their words, and the greatest of their benefits is the lasting of union with the Beloved. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/78-79}

**When longing is sound.** Once, in the assembly of al-Walid ibn Shuja', he asked a man when longing would be sound for one who claims it; the man answered: when he keeps his state and is anxious for it against the blights of time. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/78}

**An address to David.** Al-Harith relates a divine address to David in which God says that grief should not mix with the heart, lest it diminish the inheritance of sweetness of the spiritual; that God is the lamp of His servants' hearts; that David once meant to eat bread while claiming to be devoted to God, which is a reminder that provision is guaranteed, and that God provides for the worm in the earth. It says God punishes no one more than those who despair of His mercy, and that He begs His servants from those who wronged them, and gives to the one who is begged for. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/80-81}

**The Garden and the Fire.** He said that whoever is saved from the Fire has no station but Paradise, and there is no station between them; he pointed to the verse about two parties, one in Paradise and one in the blaze. Whoever wants the honour of love must be content with parting from beloveds and with solitude with the Lord of lords. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/82}

**Ibrahim ibn Adham's counsel.** Al-Harith relates that Ibrahim ibn Adham told a brother in God: if you want to be a friend of God who loves Him, leave this world and the next. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/82}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Al-Harith's words on pages 83–88 of the tenth volume continue the notice. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/83-88}

**The people of Paradise.** He describes the assembly of the people of Paradise, the prophets, the saints and the near ones, where David began to recite the Psalms with a still heart; joy rose until his voice rose to complete their joy, and the high chambers were opened. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/83}

**The sign of one given the cup of love.** He asked: what is the sign of one whom God has given the cup of His love? He answered that its sign is a heart sick with remembrance of the return, slowness of slackness in all matters, and much silence. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/84}

**The asceticism of love.** He relates of another group who were ascetic about Paradise rather than about this world, lest their Beloved say to them: what did you leave for Me? They would say: I left the world for You. He would say: and what is the world worth? They would say: its worth is a gnat's wing. Then shame before God would seize them, that they should say: I left for You what is worth a gnat's wing; but You know, my Lord, that I did not worship You for the reward of Paradise alone, and I want nothing but You; and what is Paradise beside Your remembrance? {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/85}

**How people differ in asceticism.** Asked how people differ in asceticism, he said: according to the soundness of the intellect and the purity of the hearts. The most excellent is the most intelligent; the most intelligent is the one who best understands God; the one who best understands God is the one who best accepts from Him; the best acceptor is the one who hastens to what He commands; the swiftest to what He calls to is the most ascetic about this world; and the most ascetic about this world is the most eager for the next. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/86}

**What weighs on the heart most.** He said that what weighs most on the heart is busyness with this world, and grief that follows sorrow, and that what most stirs the generosity of souls in giving up appetites is longing for the meeting with the Mighty and Great. What most removes hardships on the way to high stations is the heart's constant clinging to the love of the Merciful. The most delightful thing to the hearts of the worshippers, and the most lasting in their joy, is longing for God's nearness, for hearing His speech, and for looking at His face. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/87-88}

**Five qualities.** He said that God has pointed to these five qualities in His Book and in the Sunna of His Prophet, and in what the wise and learned among His servants have spoken. Whoever is not distracted in his world from obedience, and does not forget gratitude, has a reward like that of one who left the world for the next; this is the asceticism of those who love God and honour Him. He related from al-Harith ibn Asad that knowledge begets fear, asceticism begets rest, and gnosis begets return to God; and that the best of this community are those whose next world does not distract them from their world, nor their world from their next world. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/88}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Al-Harith's words on pages 89–94 of the tenth volume continue the notice: on gratitude, patience, humility, remembrance of death, trials and watchfulness. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/89-94}

**Gratitude and patience.** Asked what gratitude is, he said that it is a person's knowledge that the blessing is from God alone, and that no one in the heavens or on earth has a blessing whose root is not from God; so he thanks God on his own behalf and on behalf of others, and that is the furthest reach of gratitude. Asked about patience, he said it is standing where God is pleased: leaving complaint and holding the self in the stations of servanthood, with a proud refusal to complain. He distinguished the one who makes himself patient from the patient man. The one who makes himself patient forces his self to bear hardships and swallows bitterness, seeking reward; the patient man seeks to reach the peaks of the goals, and finds less pain than the one who forces himself, because he sees God looking at him in his patience. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/89}

**Contentment.** Asked the way to the station of contentment, he said: that the heart knows the Master is just in His decree and does not wrong, and that God's choice for him is better for him than his choice for himself. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/89}

**Three degrees of humility.** He said that humility has three degrees. The highest is not to see any merit in yourself over anyone, and to prefer in your heart and conscience everyone you see. The second is that the servant be humble in his heart, affectionate toward those he knows, not despising those who oppose him, and not domineering over those present. The third is obligatory on the servants, and is prostration to God; a hadith says that whoever sets his forehead down for God is free of pride. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/90}

**Striving with the self.** He said to a disciple: fight your enemies with your friends, your anger with your forbearance, your heedlessness with your reflection, your forgetfulness with your watchfulness; for you have been tried with the meanings of your natures and the struggle of your desire; and hold fast to humility. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/90}

**Remembrance of death.** Asked whether remembrance of death is a station of the beginner or of the gnostic, he said: it is first a station of the beginner and last a station of the gnostic. The beginner has remembrance dominate his heart, so he leaves off error for fear of punishment, and whenever the remembrance of death stirs in his heart, his desires die in him. The gnostic remembers death out of love for it, choosing it over life, weary of the world, longing for the meeting with God, and hoping to look at His face and to dwell in His neighbourhood, from the good opinion of his Lord that dominates his heart. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/91}

**Abu Sulayman's saying.** Al-Harith related Abu Sulayman al-Darani's saying: no one who has arrived returns to the homeland. He explained that it may be an exhortation to disciples lest they grow slack; it may mean that no one who has reached pure work returns to folly; or that no one who has entered the lofty stations returns to the desolation of slackness; or that no one who has reached the good spirit of certainty returns to the servility of creatures. He said that he saw in a dream a rider who pointed with his hand and said: no one who has reached sincerity returns to diminishment. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/92}

**Trial.** Asked the cause of God's trial of believers, he said: trial has three purposes: for the mixers, it is punishment and penalties; for the beginners, it is the purging of faults; and for the gnostics, it is the way of testing. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/92}

**How the gnostics receive trial.** He described how the gnostics receive the address in trial: with strength of understanding from God, so that they seem to hear from Him, and He is nearer to them in trial than they are to their own bodies. They drop from their hearts all choice and possession, cast themselves toward Him, and are wholly given in servitude to Him. He guards them from the causes of slackness, gives them good knowledge and surrender, so that they commit their affairs to Him and lean on the strong fortress of trust. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/93}

**Three kinds of watchfulness.** He said that watchfulness is in three degrees, according to people's intellects and knowledge of their Lord: the first is fear of God, the second is shame before God, and the third is love of God. The fearful watches with intense caution and overwhelming dread; the one who is shy before God watches with intense humility and overwhelming submission; and the lover watches with intense joy, overwhelming vigour and generosity of soul, with compassion. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/94}
