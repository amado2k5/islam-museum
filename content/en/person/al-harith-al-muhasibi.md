## Overview
Al-Harith ibn Asad al-Muhasibi al-Baghdadi, one of the ascetics among the theologians, known for worship, renunciation of the world, and exhortation. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.261}

## His life and works
He was a jurist and theologian, leading in the books of hadith, and learned in the doctrines of the ascetics. He died in 243 AH according to the "Fihrist". He wrote "Kitab al-Tafakkur wa al-I'tibar". Al-Khatib says he had many books on asceticism, the principles of religion, and refutations of the Mu'tazilis. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.261}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 465 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]]; he calls him the watchful witness of God's regard and the strict reckoner of his own self, firm in the principles of knowledge, averse to idle disputation, a suppressor of opponents and a counsellor of disciples. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/73-74}

**A morsel that his heart refused.** Al-Junayd tells that al-Harith once took a morsel of food, and when he had eaten it his self was disturbed; he would not swallow it, and threw it into a passage. He said: if this food were pleasing to God my self would accept it, but I have thrown away that morsel. He described his need as severe, but he strove to obtain food, and his self would not accept what was not pleasing to God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/75}

**Sayings.** He said: if you do not hear God's call, how will you answer His prayer? Whoever is content with something apart from God has made it his decree. He said that the wrongdoer is regretful and the wronged is safe, even if people praise the one and blame the other; the contented is rich even when hungry, and the covetous is poor even when he owns much. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/76}

**A divine address to the guides.** On page 77 he gives an address in the voice of the Truth to "the guides", in which God says: whoever comes to you sick from my absence, cure him; whoever flees from my service, bring him back; whoever forgets my favours and bounties, remind him. I have addressed you because I am forbearing, and the forbearing use nothing but forbearance. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/77}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Al-Harith's words on love, longing, and the standing between Paradise and the Fire continue on pages 78–82 of the tenth volume. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/78-82}

**Love and longing.** Al-Harith said that love of God is intense longing, because longing in itself is the heart's recollection of the sight of the Beloved. He noted that scholars differed on the nature of longing: one group said it is the heart's waiting for the moment of meeting. Love is known by its marks on the bodies of lovers and in their words, and the greatest of their benefits is the lasting of union with the Beloved. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/78-79}

**When longing is sound.** Once, in the assembly of al-Walid ibn Shuja', he asked a man when longing would be sound for one who claims it; the man answered: when he keeps his state and is anxious for it against the blights of time. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/78}

**An address to David.** Al-Harith relates a divine address to David in which God says that grief should not mix with the heart, lest it diminish the inheritance of sweetness of the spiritual; that God is the lamp of His servants' hearts; that David once meant to eat bread while claiming to be devoted to God, which is a reminder that provision is guaranteed, and that God provides for the worm in the earth. It says God punishes no one more than those who despair of His mercy, and that He begs His servants from those who wronged them, and gives to the one who is begged for. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/80-81}

**The Garden and the Fire.** He said that whoever is saved from the Fire has no station but Paradise, and there is no station between them; he pointed to the verse about two parties, one in Paradise and one in the blaze. Whoever wants the honour of love must be content with parting from beloveds and with solitude with the Lord of lords. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/82}

**Ibrahim ibn Adham's counsel.** Al-Harith relates that Ibrahim ibn Adham told a brother in God: if you want to be a friend of God who loves Him, leave this world and the next. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/82}
