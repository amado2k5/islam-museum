## Summary
Hussain Muhammad Ershad (1 February 1930 – 14 July 2019) was a Bangladeshi military officer and politician who served as president of Bangladesh from 1983 to 1990. He seized power in a bloodless coup on 24 March 1982, founded the Jatiya Party in 1986, and after a mass uprising forced his resignation in December 1990 he returned to parliament, winning his Rangpur-3 seat at every general election from 1991 until his death. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-ar}

## Early life and military career
Ershad was born at Dinhata in the Cooch Behar princely state, British India, to Mokbul Hossain, a lawyer who had served as a minister of the Maharaja of Cooch Behar, and Mazida Khatun, in a Bengali Muslim family of Nashya Shaikh origin; his parents migrated to East Bengal in 1948 after Partition. He studied at Carmichael College, Rangpur, and graduated from the University of Dhaka in 1950, was commissioned into the Pakistan Army in 1952, and completed advanced courses at the Command and Staff College, Quetta, in 1966. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en}

Interned in West Pakistan during the Liberation War of 1971, he was repatriated in 1973 and appointed adjutant general of the Bangladesh Army. President Ziaur Rahman made him deputy chief of army staff in 1975 and chief of army staff, with the rank of lieutenant general, in 1978, and he became Zia's closest politico-military counsellor. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en}

## Coup and presidency
After Ziaur Rahman's assassination in 1981 Ershad remained loyal to the elected government of Abdus Sattar, then overthrew it on 24 March 1982, ruling as chief martial law administrator before taking the presidency himself on 11 December 1983. His government carried out a land reforms ordinance in 1984, promised divestment of nationalised industries, and introduced the upazila system of rural administration. In June 1988 his parliament passed the constitutional amendment that made Islam the state religion of Bangladesh, abandoning the secularism of the original constitution — a decision of state policy by his government, recorded here as such. He played a key role in the founding summit of SAARC at Dhaka in 1985. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en}

Facing mass demonstrations mobilised by Sheikh Hasina's Awami League and Khaleda Zia's BNP, Ershad resigned on 6 December 1990; Chief Justice Shahabuddin Ahmed succeeded him as acting president. In 2011 the Supreme Court declared Ershad's military rule illegal, while leaving its acts in force until parliament decided their fate. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en}

## Later career
Ershad's Jatiya Party contested the elections of the democratic era, sometimes in alliance with the Awami League, and he won parliamentary seats at Rangpur and elsewhere in 1991, 1996 and 2008, on the last occasion winning three constituencies at once. After the 2014 election the Jatiya Party became the official opposition while also joining the government benches, and Ershad served as a special envoy of the prime minister from 2014 to 2018 and as leader of the opposition in 2019. He faced a long series of corruption prosecutions, with mixed outcomes, over these decades. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en}

## Religious views, as reported
The English article's short "Religious views" section reports one personal fact: that Ershad was a devotee of the Sufi Pir Atroshi and spent considerable time with him during his presidency, citing Sufia M. Uddin's scholarly study of religion and politics in Bangladesh (2006). The museum records this as a single attestation of personal devotion; the Arabic article's infobox gives his religion as Islam without naming a school, and no second independent source names an affiliation for him, so his faith is recorded as unknown in the data, with the attestations preserved in the faith note. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-ar}

## Death
Ershad was admitted to the Combined Military Hospital, Dhaka, on 26 June 2019 and died there on 14 July 2019; the Arabic article names myelodysplastic syndrome as the cause. A state funeral was held two days after his death. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussain-muhammad-ershad-ar}
