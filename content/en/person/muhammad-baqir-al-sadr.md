## Summary
Iraqi Shia jurist and thinker (1935–1980) of the Sadr scholarly family, who attained the rank of marja' in the Najaf seminary. With colleagues he founded a school of Islamic economics and philosophy through his books Iqtisaduna (Our Economics) and Falsafatuna (Our Philosophy); he was executed with his sister Bint al-Huda by the Iraqi Ba'athist regime in 1980. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-baqir-al-sadr}

## Life and formation

Muhammad Baqir al-Sadr was born in 1935 in Kadhimiya, Baghdad, into the renowned Sadr scholarly family; orphaned young, he was raised by relatives and moved to Najaf in 1949 to study in its seminary. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-baqir-al-sadr}

He excelled in jurisprudence and legal theory under the seminary's teachers until he reached the rank of mujtahid in his twenties, taught advanced fiqh and usul in Najaf, and combined the hawza tradition with readings in Western philosophy and modern economics. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-baqir-al-sadr}

## Works and activity

He wrote Falsafatuna (Our Philosophy, 1959) answering dialectical materialism from an Islamic philosophical standpoint, Iqtisaduna (Our Economics, 1961) attempting an Islamic economic theory beside capitalism and socialism, Al-Bank al-La-Ribawi on interest-free banking, and treatises on legal theory known as the Halaqat lessons. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-baqir-al-sadr}

He joined the movement to reform the seminary and co-founded the Society of Scholars in Najaf; his name was linked to the Islamic Dawa Party, so he was watched by the Ba'ath regime and detained several times before tensions escalated after the Iranian revolution. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-baqir-al-sadr}

## Execution and legacy

In April 1980 the Iraqi regime arrested al-Sadr and his sister Bint al-Huda and executed them, an event documented by numerous sources and widely condemned; their memory became one of the markers of modern Iraqi history. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-baqir-al-sadr}

He is counted among the most influential Twelver Shia thinkers of the twentieth century: his works on economics and philosophy are taught in seminaries and universities, and his students spread across Iraq, Iran, Lebanon and the diaspora. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-baqir-al-sadr}
