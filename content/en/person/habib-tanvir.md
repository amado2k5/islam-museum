Habib Tanvir (1 September 1923 – 8 June 2009) was an Indian playwright, theatre director, poet and actor, born Habib Ahmed Khan at Raipur to Hafiz Ahmed Khan, who had come from Peshawar; he passed his matriculation at Raipur, took his BA at Morris College, Nagpur, in 1944, and studied for a year at Aligarh Muslim University.{cite:wikipedia-habib-tanvir-en}{cite:wikipedia-habib-tanvir-ur} In Bombay from 1945 he worked as a producer at All India Radio, wrote film songs, and became part of the Progressive Writers' Association and the Indian People's Theatre Association; in Delhi in 1954 his Agra Bazar, on the eighteenth-century poet Nazir Akbarabadi, brought village performers and students together in an open marketplace staging.{cite:wikipedia-habib-tanvir-en}

After training at RADA and the Bristol Old Vic and watching Brecht's Berliner Ensemble in Berlin, he returned to India in 1958 and in 1959 founded the Naya Theatre company at Bhopal, building a "theatre of roots" with Chhattisgarhi folk performers who, from the 1970s, performed in their own language and forms such as nacha and Pandavani.{cite:wikipedia-habib-tanvir-en}{cite:wikidata-habib-tanvir} His Charandas Chor (1975) won a Fringe First at Edinburgh in 1982; he was a nominated member of the Rajya Sabha (1972–1978) and received the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award (1969), the Padma Shri (1983), the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship (1996) and the Padma Bhushan (2002).{cite:wikipedia-habib-tanvir-en} He died on 8 June 2009 — at Bhopal in the article's body text, Singrauli in its infobox.{cite:wikipedia-habib-tanvir-en}
