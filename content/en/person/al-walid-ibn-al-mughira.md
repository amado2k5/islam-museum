## Summary
Al-Walid ibn al-Mughira was the grand elder of Banu Makhzum. He was the arbiter of Quraysh's councils, the father of Khalid ibn al-Walid, and a poet-critic whose judgements gave Mecca its first vocabulary for naming the Prophet's recitation.

## Life
The most famous council in the sira is his. The elders asked him what they should call the Prophet: a soothsayer, a madman, a poet? Al-Walid weighed each word and rejected it, and settled on "a sorcerer who separates a man from his family" {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. That judgement became the slogan of the campaign.

He was among the elite who pressed the [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|boycott of Banu Hashim]], and died around 620 CE, before Badr {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. The generation of his sons took the story further: [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid]] fought on both of its sides.

## Legacy
Al-Walid is the museum's exhibit of opposition through rhetoric. It is the moment Mecca tried to classify the revelation, and, in the sources' own account, he took it seriously enough to argue over the right word.

## For young visitors
The elders of Mecca asked their wisest man what name to give to the Prophet's words. He answered with what was meant as an insult, and it tells us that they knew the words had power.

## At the rebuilding of the Kaaba (The Sealed Nectar, pp.61–62)
When Quraysh agreed to rebuild the [[building/kaaba|Kaaba]], they feared to pull it down. Al-Walid ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumi began the demolition. When people saw that nothing had happened to him, they followed, and they kept demolishing until they reached the foundations of Abraham ([[event/rebuilding-of-the-kaaba|Rebuilding of the Kaaba]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.61} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.62} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/192-197}.

## At al-Walid's council (The Sealed Nectar, pp.81–82)
Quraysh gathered around him to deliberate on a phrase to say to the Arabs about the Prophet. The discussion ended with his statement that he is a sorcerer, who separates a man from his father, brother, wife and clan {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.81} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.82} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/271}. Sixteen verses of the sura al-Muddaththir were revealed about him ([[event/walid-council|Al-Walid's council]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.82}.
