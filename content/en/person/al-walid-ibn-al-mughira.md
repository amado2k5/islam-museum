## Brief
Al-Walid ibn al-Mughira was the grand elder of Banu Makhzum — arbiter of Quraysh's councils, father of Khalid ibn al-Walid, and the poet-critic whose verdicts gave Mecca its first vocabulary for the Prophet's recitation.

## Life
The sira's most famous council-scene is his: the elders asking him what to call the Prophet — soothsayer? madman? poet? — and al-Walid weighing each word and rejecting it, settling on "a sorcerer who splits a man from his family" {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. The verdict became the campaign line.

He was of the elite that pressed the [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|boycott of Banu Hashim]], and died about 620 CE, before Badr {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. His sons' generation would take the story further — [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid]] fighting on both sides of it.

## Legacy
Al-Walid is the museum's exhibit of opposition by rhetoric: the moment Mecca tried to taxonomize the revelation — and, in the sources' own telling, respected it enough to argue about the right word.

## For young visitors
The elders of Mecca asked their wisest man what to call the Prophet's words. His answer was meant as an insult — and it tells us they knew the words were powerful.
