## Summary
Muna AbuSulayman is a Saudi media personality and businesswoman who reached a mass Arab audience as co-host of the daily talk show Kalam Nawaem on MBC, and who led major philanthropic and development work as executive secretary general of Alwaleed Philanthropies. She is counted among the first Saudi women to combine popular television presence with the management of large giving institutions. {cite:wikipedia-muna-abusulayman}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1973 in Philadelphia, United States, where her father Abdul Hamid AbuSulayman, the noted Muslim thinker and academic, was completing graduate studies, and she grew up between the United States, Malaysia and Saudi Arabia. She earned a bachelor's and a master's degree in English literature from George Mason University and worked early in university teaching and research before moving into media. {cite:wikipedia-muna-abusulayman}

## Media career
Her mass-audience breakthrough came in 2002 when she joined Kalam Nawaem, a daily women's talk show on MBC1 that treated social and cultural questions with unusual candour and drew millions of viewers for years. The programme made her a recognised face across the Arab world, a visibility she later carried into international roles, including appointment in 2007 as the first Saudi Goodwill Ambassador for the United Nations Development Programme. {cite:wikipedia-muna-abusulayman} {cite:muslim500-muna-abusulayman}

## Leading philanthropy and development
She served as executive secretary general of Alwaleed Philanthropies, the charitable arm of the Kingdom Holding group, overseeing charitable, relief and cultural programmes across continents, and chaired several advisory initiatives in education and media. She has also sat on academic and advisory boards and works as a consultant and speaker on social change leadership and communication. {cite:wikipedia-muna-abusulayman}

## Recognition and public standing
She has been named in lists of influential Arab personalities several times and described by international media as one of the best-known Saudi women in public life. Her early televised appearance without a head covering on MBC prompted wide social debate about the image of Saudi women in media; the sources record that debate as part of her public biography. {cite:wikipedia-muna-abusulayman}

## Legacy
Her career models a Saudi generation combining academic training, mass-media presence and leadership of development institutions: she opened Arab television to a daily social conversation led by women and carried Gulf institutional philanthropy into international partnerships. Some commentators read her public profile as an early example of widening space for Saudi women before later reforms; the museum reports these readings attributed. {cite:wikipedia-muna-abusulayman}
