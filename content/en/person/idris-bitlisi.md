## Overview
The mawla Idris ibn Husam al-Din al-Bidlisi, an Ajami (from the non-Arab east) who later became one of the Rumis (the Ottoman lands), a Hanafi and a learned man of merit, is recorded by Ibn al-Imad among those who died in 925 AH, with the word "approximately" {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 10 p. 184}.

## Career
Ibn al-Imad quotes al-Shaqa'iq for the statement that he was a secretary-attestor (muwaqqi') in the chancery of the emirs of the Ajam. When the strife of Ibn Ardawil broke out he moved to the lands of Rum, where Sultan Abu Yazid honoured him greatly, assigned him a monthly and yearly allowance, and he lived contentedly under his protection {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 10 p. 184}. The sultan commissioned him to write the "Tawarikh Al Uthman" (histories of the House of Osman) in Persian, and he composed it {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 10 p. 184}. The author of al-Shaqa'iq calls him without peer or equal, and says no later writer matched his prose {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 10 p. 184}.

## Writings
Ibn al-Imad, again quoting al-Shaqa'iq, adds that he left countless poems in Arabic and Persian and remarkable epistles on assorted topics {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 10 p. 184}.
