## Summary
Abu Bakr ibn 'Abdallah ibn Qutlubak, astrologer and poet, died in Safar 812 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 105}.

## Details
Ibn al-'Imad gives him the double description "al-munajjim al-sha'ir" — the astrologer-poet — saying he practised astrology and letters, excelled in versification and jesting, and held poetic exchanges with the wits of his age: first Shams al-Din al-Mazzini, then Khatib Zar', then 'Ali al-Baha'i; he was famed for a light spirit and entertaining anecdotes {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 105}. The chronicle quotes two lines of his lampooning a Hanafi schoolteacher — "had al-Nu'man seen him in the lesson-circle, al-Nu'man would have said: this is my brother" — showing the playful jab at the Hanafi school typical of the period's learned jesting {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 105}. He died in the month of Safar that year; the source adds no further details beyond his literary character and his reputation among the wits of the time {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 105}.
