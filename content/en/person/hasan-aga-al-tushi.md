## Summary
[[person/khayr-al-din-barbarossa|Khayr al-Din Barbarossa]]'s deputy over Algiers and governor after him — 'wise, pious, of wide learning' in al-Salabi's phrase {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 216}.

## Securing the interior
He set to consolidating security and gathering the country's ends round the central authority: subjecting Mostaganem, then taking Biskra, capital of the Zab, where he built a fort and garrison {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 223}.

## The 949 raid
In Jumada I 949/September 1539 the Ottoman force — 1,300 men on thirteen ships — landed and took the town, ranging over the southern Spanish coast; on the return a large Spanish fleet barred the way and the battle was fierce, ships of both sides sunk, the Spanish losses great {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 223-224}.

## The defence of 1541
When [[person/charles-v|Charles V]] anchored off Algiers on 15 October 1541 he gathered the city's notables and summoned them to jihad, answering the emperor's threat-letter: 'I am the servant of Sultan Süleyman… this land gives an invader only death.' In the author's account the victory owed to wise leadership, the people's rallying, and the God-sent storm; the sultan rewarded him with the rank of pasha {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 224-227}.

## Death
He served until his death in 951/1544; the diwan made [[person/al-hajj-bakir|al-Hajj Bakir]] interim governor until the Porte appointed [[person/hasan-ibn-khayr-al-din|Hasan b. Khayr al-Din]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 229}.
