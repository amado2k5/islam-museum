## Overview
Sultan Bayezid II, son of [[person/mehmed-ii|Mehmed the Conqueror]], who ruled from 886 to 918 AH. Al-Salabi describes him as a gentle sultan, a lover of literature, learned in the law, and passionate about astronomy. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.163}

He used Greek and Bulgarian experts to improve roads and bridges. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.163}

At the start of his rule his brother Prince Jem opposed him, and the people of Bursa proposed dividing the state into two parts, European and Asian. Bayezid refused the division and marched with his army to Bursa, and Jem fled to the Mamluk sultan Qaytbay in Egypt. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.163}

He died in 918 AH; the Gregorian conversion is derived. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.163}

## The fuller portrait in al-Salabi
The book adds that Bayezid sealed peace with the Mamluks in 1491 by sending [[person/qaytbay|Qaytbay]] the keys of the frontier fortresses {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 164}, received the first Russian ambassador in 1492 under [[person/ivan-iii-moscow|Ivan III]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 165}, answered the Andalusian Muslims' verse petition with a fleet under [[person/kemal-reis|Kemal Reis]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 173}, won the naval victory at Lepanto in 905/1499 and took the city the next year {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 173}, weathered the [[event/earthquake-istanbul-bayezid|great earthquakes]] that ruined 1,070 houses and 109 mosques in forty-five days {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 174}, settled ten thousand akçe on the mufti and between seven and two thousand on the madrasa teachers, and gathered the dust of his campaigns from his clothes — ordering at death that a brick be struck from it and laid beneath his right cheek in the grave {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 174}.

## For Scholars
Sultan Bayezid II, son of Mehmed the Conqueror, ruled 886–918 AH. Al-Salabi describes him as a mild sultan fond of literature, versed in the law, passionate about astronomy; he used Greek and Bulgarian experts to improve roads and bridges. Early in his rule his brother Prince Cem broke with him and the people of Bursa proposed splitting the state into European and Asian halves; Bayezid refused, marched on Bursa, and Cem fled to the Mamluk sultan Qaytbay in Egypt. He made peace with the Mamluks in 1491 CE by sending the border fortresses' keys to Qaytbay, received the first Russian envoy in 1492 CE under Ivan III, answered Andalusian Muslims' plea with a fleet to the Spanish shores under Kemal Reis, won a naval victory over the Venetians at Lepanto gulf in 905 AH (1499 CE) and took the city the next year; in his time the great earthquakes struck Constantinople, wrecking a thousand seventy houses and a hundred nine mosques over forty-five days. He allotted the mufti ten thousand Ottoman dirhams and the sultanic schools' teachers between seven and two thousand, and would gather the dust of his campaigns from his clothes, ordering a brick made of it laid under his right cheek in the grave. He died in 918 AH — the Gregorian conversion is derived.
