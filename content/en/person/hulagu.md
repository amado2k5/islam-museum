## Overview
Hulagu Khan, the Tatar commander who entered Baghdad in 656 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.715-719}

## The fall of Baghdad
Al-Suyuti reports that he came to Baghdad with two hundred thousand men, defeated the caliph's army, and entered it on the day of Ashura, and that the caliph al-Musta'sim was killed. He reports that the vizier al-Alqami did not achieve his aim of setting up an Alid caliph, and died of grief. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.715-719}

He also transmits from letters that Hulagu sent to al-Nasir, lord of Damascus, threatening him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.715-719}

## Ibn Khaldun's account
In Ibn Khaldun's account, he marched on Iraq in 652 AH, having taken al-Rayy, Isfahan and Hamadhan, pursued the Ismaili fortresses, and made for the fortress of Alamut in 655 AH. Then the letter of Ibn al-Alqami reached him, and he marched to Baghdad; al-Musta'sim came out to him and was killed. After the conquest he marched to Syria in 658 AH. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.900-901}

Note: al-Suyuti and Ibn Khaldun differ on some details, such as how the caliph was killed. They have not been reconciled here.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
In the episodes on the fall of Baghdad (thirteen to seventeen) of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]], [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] makes Hulagu an example of one who took up the material causes of victory and won, saying that the causes of victory are "neutral": whoever takes them up wins, Muslim or not. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:05:00} He describes him in the harshest terms, as a bloodthirsty leader like his forebear [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]], yet says his preparation was "dazzling" and his planning "extraordinary". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:04:30}

### His place in the Tatar state
Al-Suwaidan relates that Genghis Khan founded the Tatar state, his son Ögedei followed him, and then Möngke took power and began to plan the overthrow of the Abbasid caliphate, since Iraq lay on his road to Syria, Egypt and Africa; his brothers helped him and divided the regions among them, and his third brother Hulagu became responsible for Persia and its surroundings. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:04:00} The aim in Hulagu's mind, he says, was clear: to bring down Baghdad, capital of the caliphate, and then go beyond it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:04:30}

### Five years of preparation
Al-Suwaidan says Hulagu set to work in 649 AH with speed and precision and kept preparing for five full years until 654 AH, which he counts as the patience and steadfastness that are among the causes of victory. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:06:00} He repaired the roads from China to Baghdad and made them ready for the armies, built many bridges over the rivers in the mountain country, had giant carriers made to bring the great siege engines from China, and took the cities and centres that controlled the route. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:07:30} He then cleared the main road from China to Baghdad of herds, wild and tame, so that the grass would be enough to feed the army's horses, camels and beasts without carrying fodder, and even arranged how the tents would be moved. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:08:00} Al-Suwaidan cites [[passage/quran-11-15|Hud 15]] on those who take up the means of this world being given it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:05:30}

### Alliances
Al-Suwaidan relates that the Tatars, who had not known alliances before, were ready to make them for the sake of their aim: in 651 AH Möngke received a Crusader embassy from [[person/louis-ix|Louis IX]] of France meant to unite the two against the Muslims, which failed because Möngke, he says, accepted only one power in the world and knew only followers. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:09:30} Then Hethum, king of Armenia, a small state of under thirty thousand square kilometres, went himself to Karakorum; Möngke gave him a grand reception, but the terms of the agreement fitted only a master and a follower, and Hethum became something like the Tatar ambassador in the region. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:14:00} Möngke promised the Crusader princes of Syria Jerusalem if they allied with him, and only the prince of Antioch, Bohemond, responded. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:15:00} He also made treaties with the Christian kingdom of Georgia. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:15:30}

He says that worse still, Hulagu, delegated by his brother, made treaties with some Muslim princes: [[person/badr-al-din-lulu|Badr al-Din Lu'lu']], lord of Mosul, came to him, as did the two Seljuk sultans Kaykaus II and Kilij Arslan IV; [[person/al-nasir-yusuf|al-Nasir Yusuf]], lord of Aleppo and Damascus, submitted and sent his son al-Aziz to be with his army; and the Ayyubid al-Ashraf, lord of Homs, offered him loyalty. Here al-Suwaidan cites [[passage/quran-5-52|al-Ma'ida 52]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep13 00:18:00} Then, he relates, Hulagu reached the vizier [[person/al-alqami|Ibn al-Alqami]] inside the Abbasid court. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:08:00}

### Letters and psychological war
Al-Suwaidan says Tatar letters tried to deceive people that they were Muslims come to lift oppression, and he reads a letter Hulagu sent to a Muslim prince that presents the Tatars as soldiers of God taking vengeance on the tyrannical and proud, boasts of laying lands waste and destroying people, and warns that there is no escape from their swords; he says such a letter, falling into the hands of a frightened ruler, left him unable to move, and that was its purpose. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep14 00:17:30}

### The army
Al-Suwaidan relates that in 654 AH Hulagu judged the time ripe for the attack and gathered the largest Tatar army since the founding of Genghis Khan's state, of which those assigned to the direct attack on Baghdad exceeded two hundred thousand fighters. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:09:30} The army was composite: the original Tatar army; a division he called up from the Tatar army in Russia; a division stationed in Anatolia under the Mongol commander Baiju, which passed among the Muslim emirates with no one standing in its way; Hethum of Armenia in person at the head of part of his army; a Georgian division; and a thousand Chinese archers famed for fire-bearing arrows; at the head of his army he placed Kitbuqa. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:11:30} He says the army thus held three of the most famous Tatar commanders: Hulagu, Kitbuqa and Baiju. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:12:00} Hulagu asked Bohemond of Antioch for help; Bohemond excused himself from crossing Syria but promised help in Syria if Baghdad fell; al-Nasir Yusuf sent his son al-Aziz, and Badr al-Din Lu'lu' sent a unit, so that there were two Muslim units in the Tatar army, small, al-Suwaidan says, but full of meaning. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:12:30}

### The Ismailis
Al-Suwaidan says Hulagu saw that the Ismailis, based in the mountains of western Persia and eastern Iraq, might threaten his army's rear if he attacked Baghdad, since they were known for their fighting strength and strong fortresses and had an old feud with the Tatars; he says the Tatars brought with them in their army the daughter of one of their dead, to avenge him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:16:30} So he decided to begin with them; orders came from Karakorum, and the armies surrounded their strongest fortress, [[place/alamut|Alamut]]. Their leader Rukn al-Din Khurshah declared his submission and readiness to hand over the fortress, but its commander refused and fought, so it was taken by force and all inside were slaughtered. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:17:30} Hulagu sent Khurshah to Möngke, who refused to meet him, and he was killed on the way in obscure circumstances; then Hulagu had the Ismailis gathered and killed them all, men, women and children, which further broke morale in Baghdad. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:19:00} Al-Suwaidan says this was done in 655 AH, leaving the road to Baghdad safe and open, and that Hulagu spent about a year besieging their castles. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep15 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:06:00} (See [[event/siege-of-alamut-1256|the siege of Alamut]].)

### The march and the letters to the caliph
Al-Suwaidan relates that Hulagu set out at the head of a hundred and twenty thousand of the best-trained Mongol soldiers, left Karakorum in 651 AH, crossed the Oxus (Jayhun) on 1 Dhu al-Hijja 653 AH, which he equates with January 1256, and passed through the Muslim lands of Central Asia. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:05:30} He then camped close to the Abbasid capital and sent the caliph [[person/al-mustasim-billah|al-Musta'sim bi'llah]] a letter full of threats, ordering him to demolish the fortresses, fill in the trenches, hand the kingdom to his son and come to meet him, promising to leave him his rule if he obeyed and warning that if he resisted he would not escape even if he climbed into the sky. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:07:30} When the caliph answered harshly, he sent a still harsher letter saying he was heading for Baghdad with an army like ants and locusts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:09:30} With money and posts he won over some lords of the neighbouring regions, among them Badr al-Din Lu'lu' of Mosul and Abu Bakr Sa'd, ruler of Fars. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep16 00:11:30}

### In Baghdad
Al-Suwaidan says Hulagu refused the caliph's request for peace on the ground that it was too late, insisted that the Dawatdar and Sulayman Shah, leaders of the resistance, come out before any negotiation, then killed the soldiers who surrendered along with the two commanders, and killed those who came out with the caliph. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:08:00} He then entered Baghdad himself to see the caliph's palace, had the caliph brought and made him reveal his possessions and jewels, visible and hidden, and divided them among his wife and his army commanders and leading horsemen. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:09:30} On 10 Safar 656 AH he ordered the caliph killed with his sons, uncles, relatives and the notables of Baghdad. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:12:00} He then announced an end to the killing and ordered the dead buried for fear of plague in his army, left two commanders in Baghdad with three thousand soldiers, and departed for [[place/hamadan|Hamadan]] in Jumada al-Ula 656 AH. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:15:00} Al-Suwaidan says Hulagu's aim was to destroy the Abbasid caliphate, plunder its wealth and extend Mongol influence, and from there to dominate the world: "a clear aim and five years of planning". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep17 00:16:30}
