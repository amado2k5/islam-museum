## Overview
[[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] was given allegiance to the caliphate after the death of Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 72} {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 73}. In his time he dealt with the governors and the Kharijites, and he had a dispute with [[person/yazid-ibn-al-muhallab|Yazid ibn al-Muhallab]] {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 78}.

## Allegiance
al-Tabari reports that when people dispersed from the writing after the death of Sulayman, Umar feared that something of the matter had been assigned to him, so he asked Raja' ibn Haywa {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 70}. Umar led the funeral prayer over Sulayman after his death, according to the report {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 72}. When the news of the people's allegiance to him reached Abd al-Aziz ibn al-Walid, Umar said: "If you had given allegiance and taken up the command, I would not have disputed it with you" {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 73}.

## Governors and officials
Umar sent Ibn Hatim ibn al-Nu'man al-Bahili against the Turks who had raided Azerbaijan, and they killed them {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 74}. His agent in Medina was Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Amr ibn Hazm, according to the annals {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 74}. He sent the letter of appointment of Abd al-Rahman ibn Nu'aym to the war in Khurasan and Sistan, together with Abd Allah ibn Sakhr al-Qurashi {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 85}.

## The Kharijites
Umar wrote to Abd al-Hamid ibn Abd al-Rahman, his agent in Iraq, about the rising of the Kharijites, ordering him to call them to act by the Book of God and the sunna {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 75}.

## Yazid ibn al-Muhallab
Umar disliked Yazid ibn al-Muhallab and his household, and said they were tyrants, according to the report of Hisham from Abu Mikhnaf {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 78}. He told him that he could find no way but to take all his money {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 79}. When he wanted to exile him, he was spoken to about him {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 87}. When he came to the caliphate, he wrote to him saying that Sulayman had been a slave of God's slaves. Yazid said that this was not the speech of those of his household who had gone before {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 91}.

## Notes for researchers
- Most of the reports are "according to the report": al-Tabari transmits them from named narrators, above all Abu Mikhnaf. They have not yet been compared with other sources.
- The references use the scan page number. The years are not given, because their reading in the OCR is not reliable, except what is reported from the annals.
- The sentence on page 91 is rendered in sense, and should be checked against the scan before it is relied on.
- Reports with OCR damage have been left out of the entry, and will be added after the page images are checked.

## For Sprouts
A just and humble caliph who loved people, spread justice and mercy, and lived simply to support the poor and needy.

## For Explorers
Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz was the pious Umayyad caliph often remembered as the fifth of the Rightly Guided Caliphs for his profound justice and humility. Assuming office, he restored misused properties to the treasury, abolished tribute on new Muslims, and appointed upright governors, fostering widespread prosperity where charity could hardly find anyone in need.

## For Scholars
Abu Hafs Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz (61–101 AH / 681–720 CE), the eighth Umayyad caliph, universally acclaimed for his justice and piety, widely celebrated as the fifth Rightly Guided Caliph and the first historical renewer (mujaddid) of Islam. Raised and educated in Medina among master traditionists, he succeeded to the caliphate in 99 AH (717 CE) and instituted sweeping moral and structural reforms: returning expropriated lands to the state treasury, dismissing corrupt governors, abolishing the jizya tax on converts, and officially ordering the first systematic state codification of Hadith under Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 09)

The Sheikh relates that the Umayyads put poison in the food of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz after they had grown weary of his conduct toward them, and that when he knew the end was near he told his wife Fatima to leave the room (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:18:30}.

He says that he told her he saw faces in the room that were neither human nor jinn, who neither eat nor drink and love only a sweet fragrance and the remembrance of God, and that Fatima went out but did not wish to miss those historic moments (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:19:00}.

He then recounts that a shepherd in the desert, not knowing that Umar had died, said that the wolves had attacked the sheep, and the Sheikh concludes that the presence of righteous people is a security for the earth (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:19:30}.

He relates that Fatima put her ear to the door and heard him welcoming Adam, father of mankind, then Nuh, then Ibrahim, then Musa, one after another (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:21:00}.

He adds that Umar welcomed Isa and the Prophet, then welcomed the angel of death as a visitor who had come at an appointed time, then pronounced the two testimonies and his soul departed (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:21:30}.

He tells how Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, on entering the mosque, stepped on a man's foot, and the man jumped up in fright and seized the Commander of the Faithful by the collar (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:32:00}.

He says the man asked him, are you blind, and Umar answered no; the members of his retinue wanted to discipline the man, but Umar said he had only asked him a question and he had answered it; the Sheikh then moves on to al-Hasan al-Basri, saying that his fame spread across the horizons (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:32:30}.

The Sheikh narrates an anecdote: a boy went in to Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, and the caliph told him to leave speaking to someone older. ## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy says that when Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz governed Egypt on behalf of his father, he secured the people's religion and their livelihood {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:43:30}. He cites Umar's letter to his governor of Egypt: "Reform their hereafter, and their world will be reformed" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:25:30}.

He describes an assembly of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, who held five sessions a week, one of them a session on literature, poetry and proverbs, in which Umar wept when a verse from the Mu'allaqa of Tarafa was recited before him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:31:00}. He says Umar listened to those who chose the finest speech, as one picks the finest fruit, and walked among the people at night {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:32:30}.

He recounts that when Umar sent officials to collect zakat in Africa, they returned saying they had found no poor person to take it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:10:00}. He says Umar returned to officials the gifts they offered him, and did not take them for himself {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:56:00}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh relates that Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz had several daughters, and when death came he was asked whether he would leave anything for them; he said he did not fear poverty for them, for he had taught them [[passage/quran-56-1|Surat al-Waqi'a]]; the Sheikh says that after his death each daughter married a prince (needs_source: the report as the Sheikh tells it). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep16 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep16 00:04:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh relates that a man on his way to Damascus saw thirty dogs with a flock of sheep and thought it too many; the shepherd said they were wolves that had grazed with the sheep since Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz took office; when Umar heard it he said: we set right what was between us and our Lord, and God set right what was between the wolf and the sheep. He draws the lesson for spouses that whoever sets right what is between himself and God, God sets right what is between him and his spouse (needs_source: the report as the Sheikh tells it). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep25 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep25 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep25 00:10:30}

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
In the fourteenth episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] mentions that Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz wrote to his governors in the far provinces that none should spur his horse to a gallop without need, and that none should strike his mount with a stick tipped with iron, and sees in this kindness to animals becoming a matter of state. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:10:00} He relates that Umar wrote to Egypt, on hearing that camels there were loaded with a thousand ratls, threatening to punish anyone who loaded one with more than six hundred. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:10:30} He relates that when wealth grew in the state Umar married off the unmarried and paid debts until no one was left who was owed zakat, and then bought wheat and laid it on the mountain tops so that the birds would not go hungry in the lands of the Muslims. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:23:00} In the eleventh episode he mentions that Umar gave people money to marry and to acquire mounts, as an example of what he calls the right of sufficiency. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:25:00} These are his tellings, without naming their sources (needs_source).
