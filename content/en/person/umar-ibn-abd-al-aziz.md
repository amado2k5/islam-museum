## Overview
[[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] ibn Marwan was an Umayyad caliph, born most probably in 61 AH in [[place/medina|Medina]]; his mother was Umm Asim, daughter of Asim, the son of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.157-161}. [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|Al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]] made him governor of Medina in Rabi' al-Awwal 87 AH, where he formed a consultative council of ten of the city's jurists {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.168-173}. He was given allegiance as caliph after the death of [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik]] at [[place/marj-dabiq|Marj Dabiq]] in Safar 99 AH, by a testament Sulayman had written for him {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 72} {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 73} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 172-176}.

Al-Sallabi writes that he began the return of unjustly taken property with himself and then with the Umayyads, and that he wrote to his governor of Egypt to lift the [[concept/jizya|jizya]] from those who became Muslim {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.184-193} {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.194-203}. He ordered the sunna collected and written down: he wrote to [[person/abu-bakr-ibn-hazm|Abu Bakr ibn Hazm]] to record the hadith of the Prophet because he feared that knowledge would fade, and he ordered [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri]] to gather it {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.297-302}. In his time he dealt with the governors and the [[group/khawarij|Kharijites]], writing to his governor of Iraq to call them to act by the Book of God, and he had a dispute with [[person/yazid-ibn-al-muhallab|Yazid ibn al-Muhallab]] {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 75} {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 78}. He died at Dayr Sim'an in the district of [[place/homs|Homs]] in Rajab 101 AH, and [[person/al-masudi|al-Mas'udi]] gives his caliphate as two years, five months and five days {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 172-176}. Al-Sallabi reports that the scholars counted him among the Rightly Guided Caliphs {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.159}.

## In the Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition

The article in the Encyclopaedia of Islam presents [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]], called Abu Hafs al-Ashajj, as the fifth caliph of the Marwanid branch of the [[polity/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad dynasty]], who reigned from 99/717 to 101/720. His father was Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan, and his grandfather was Marwan ibn al-Hakam {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}. The sections below follow that article. The later sections of this page, drawn from other sources, are kept as they were.

## Birth and upbringing

The article says Umar was probably born in [[place/medina|Medina]] around 60/680, although some sources place his birth in Egypt. He spent his early years in both places, above all at [[place/hulwan|Hulwan]] in Egypt, where his father lived as governor from 65 to 86/686 to 705. His education, however, took place in Medina, where he is said to have first kept company with prominent pious men and hadith scholars {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

After his father died, his uncle the caliph [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik]] summoned him to Damascus to confirm a marriage between him and the caliph's daughter Fatima. Shortly afterwards Abd al-Malik's son and successor [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid]] named Umar governor of Medina. The article reads both acts as conciliatory gestures meant to soften relations between two branches of the Marwanid family {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

## Governor of Medina

Umar arrived at his post in Rabi' I 87, February to March 706. His jurisdiction later took in [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and [[place/taif|Taif]]. Little is known of what he did there, but it is said that he set out at once to govern in close association with the jurists of the Prophet's city. Most accounts show him as a just governor, who often led the pilgrimage and showed special reverence to pious men such as [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]]; other accounts describe the young Umar as worldly and indulgent {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

At al-Walid's request he oversaw the expansion of the [[building/prophets-mosque|Mosque of the Prophet]]. The article suggests that his lenient rule in the Hijaz may have brought about his removal from it. Some people of Iraq had fled to the Hijaz to escape the harsher treatment of [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]], the powerful governor of Iraq, and under pressure from al-Hajjaj Umar was recalled in Sha'ban 93, May to June 712 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

## At court and the succession

For the next years Umar seems to have been attached to the Umayyad court in Damascus. There, with the influential [[person/raja-ibn-haywa|Raja' ibn Haywa]], he acted as an unofficial counsellor to the caliph [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik]]. In 97/716, for example, he accompanied Sulayman on pilgrimage to Mecca and stopped with him at [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]] on the way back {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

In 98/716 Sulayman fell ill at Dabiq in northern Syria while on campaign. Raja' persuaded him to name Umar as his successor, with [[person/yazid-ibn-abd-al-malik|Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik]] to follow. This ran against Abd al-Malik's earlier wish that the succession stay among his own descendants. The change drew some opposition from Abd al-Malik's line, but allegiance to Umar was secured, and he was proclaimed caliph on 10 Safar 99, 22 September 717 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

## Military and frontier affairs

The article notes that Umar has often been portrayed as a pacifist caliph, but judges it more likely that his stance on military affairs was dictated by concern over the dwindling caliphal treasury. In 99/717 he ordered the armies engaged in the costly and probably fruitless [[event/siege-of-constantinople-717|siege of Constantinople]] to withdraw, and the frontier with Byzantium to be pulled back to the region of [[place/malatya|Malatya]]. In that same year, though, he was willing to send troops against the Turks who had launched destructive raids into [[place/azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]], and the traditional summer raids continued without a break {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

In 100/718 he ordered first an Iraqi army and then a Syrian one against the Kharijite rebels led by Shawdhab, also called Bistam, al-Yashkuri in Iraq, although some sources relate that the rebels were calmed by diplomacy {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}. On the [[group/khawarij|Khawarij]] in general see their own record.

## Governors and fiscal policy

Like other caliphs, Umar appointed and removed provincial governors as he saw fit. The most famous case is his removal and imprisonment of the Azdi notable [[person/yazid-ibn-al-muhallab|Yazid ibn al-Muhallab]], who had evidently gone back on promises about revenues he had raised as Sulayman's governor of Iraq and the East {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876}.

The article says Umar is best known for his fiscal policies, though these remain unclear. His famous fiscal rescript is not a document in its own right but a text preserved in a later biography of the caliph by Ibn Abd al-Hakam, and its details remain open to different interpretations. The article finds it clear that, in the rescript and in other fiscal matters, Umar sought to raise the revenues due to the state while also granting new converts the same lenient fiscal status as other Muslims. It adds that his successors did not pursue these policies and that their long-term importance for the caliphate's fiscal system was small {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 876} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 877}.

## Death and burial

On the road from Damascus to [[place/aleppo|Aleppo]], perhaps heading for his nearby estate at Khunasira, Umar fell ill. Some sources allege that the family of Abd al-Malik poisoned him. He died on 20 or 25 Rajab 101, 5 or 10 February 720, in the village of Dayr Sim'an near [[place/maarrat-al-numan|Ma'arrat al-Nu'man]], and was buried in a plot he had bought himself. The ruins of his tomb, of uncertain date, can still be seen {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 877}. The museum also has a record of his [[building/omar-ibn-abdel-aziz-tomb|tomb]].

## The later image

The article holds that Umar's significance lies as much in the complexity of his later image as in what he achieved as caliph. To later generations he was an example of piety, fairness and humility. It was often stressed that his mother was a granddaughter of the Rightly-Guided caliph [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]], and the links between the two men were elaborated until some commentators counted Umar II among the Rightly-Guided caliphs. The article adds that there is some evidence that Umar saw himself as the redeeming [[concept/al-mahdi|Mahdi]] and as a renewer, mujaddid, of Islam as the community approached the end of its first century {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 877}.

Another strand of anecdote shows him, especially in youth, as a typical Umayyad prince fond of pomp and luxury. The article suggests these accounts may serve as a foil that heightens the image of the virtuous mature caliph. His reputation for piety also reached beyond Muslim audiences, and the article takes this to explain a Christian polemical pseudo-correspondence attributed to him and the Byzantine emperor Leo III. To writers of the Abbasid era and later he was something of an enigma: a prince of the Umayyad house, which many disliked, yet a pious statesman worthy of nostalgia and imitation. He is one of the few early figures who became the subject of separate biographical works early on {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 877}.

## Allegiance
al-Tabari reports that when people dispersed from the writing after the death of Sulayman, Umar feared that something of the matter had been assigned to him, so he asked Raja' ibn Haywa {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 70}. Umar led the funeral prayer over Sulayman after his death, according to the report {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 72}. When the news of the people's allegiance to him reached Abd al-Aziz ibn al-Walid, Umar said: "If you had given allegiance and taken up the command, I would not have disputed it with you" {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 73}.

## Governors and officials
Umar sent Ibn Hatim ibn al-Nu'man al-Bahili against the Turks who had raided Azerbaijan, and they killed them {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 74}. His agent in Medina was Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Amr ibn Hazm, according to the annals {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 74}. He sent the letter of appointment of Abd al-Rahman ibn Nu'aym to the war in Khurasan and Sistan, together with Abd Allah ibn Sakhr al-Qurashi {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 85}.

## The Kharijites
Umar wrote to Abd al-Hamid ibn Abd al-Rahman, his agent in Iraq, about the rising of the Kharijites, ordering him to call them to act by the Book of God and the sunna {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 75}.

## Yazid ibn al-Muhallab
Umar disliked Yazid ibn al-Muhallab and his household, and said they were tyrants, according to the report of Hisham from Abu Mikhnaf {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 78}. He told him that he could find no way but to take all his money {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 79}. When he wanted to exile him, he was spoken to about him {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 87}. When he came to the caliphate, he wrote to him saying that Sulayman had been a slave of God's slaves. Yazid said that this was not the speech of those of his household who had gone before {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 91}.

## For Sprouts
A just and humble caliph who loved people, spread justice and mercy, and lived simply to support the poor and needy.

## For Explorers
Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz was the pious Umayyad caliph often remembered as the fifth of the Rightly Guided Caliphs for his profound justice and humility. Assuming office, he restored misused properties to the treasury, abolished tribute on new Muslims, and appointed upright governors, fostering widespread prosperity where charity could hardly find anyone in need.

## For Scholars
Abu Hafs Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz (61–101 AH / 681–720 CE), the eighth Umayyad caliph, universally acclaimed for his justice and piety, widely celebrated as the fifth Rightly Guided Caliph and the first historical renewer (mujaddid) of Islam. Raised and educated in Medina among master traditionists, he succeeded to the caliphate in 99 AH (717 CE) and instituted sweeping moral and structural reforms: returning expropriated lands to the state treasury, dismissing corrupt governors, abolishing the jizya tax on converts, and officially ordering the first systematic state codification of Hadith under Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri.

He relates that Fatima put her ear to the door and heard him welcoming Adam, father of mankind, then Nuh, then Ibrahim, then Musa, one after another (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:21:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy says that when Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz governed Egypt on behalf of his father, he secured the people's religion and their livelihood {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:43:30}. He cites Umar's letter to his governor of Egypt: "Reform their hereafter, and their world will be reformed" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:25:30}.

He describes an assembly of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, who held five sessions a week, one of them a session on literature, poetry and proverbs, in which Umar wept when a verse from the Mu'allaqa of Tarafa was recited before him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:31:00}. He says Umar listened to those who chose the finest speech, as one picks the finest fruit, and walked among the people at night {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:32:30}.

He recounts that when Umar sent officials to collect zakat in Africa, they returned saying they had found no poor person to take it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:10:00}. He says Umar returned to officials the gifts they offered him, and did not take them for himself {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:56:00}.

In the fourteenth episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] mentions that Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz wrote to his governors in the far provinces that none should spur his horse to a gallop without need, and that none should strike his mount with a stick tipped with iron, and sees in this kindness to animals becoming a matter of state. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:10:00} He relates that Umar wrote to Egypt, on hearing that camels there were loaded with a thousand ratls, threatening to punish anyone who loaded one with more than six hundred. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:10:30} He relates that when wealth grew in the state Umar married off the unmarried and paid debts until no one was left who was owed zakat, and then bought wheat and laid it on the mountain tops so that the birds would not go hungry in the lands of the Muslims. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:23:00} In the eleventh episode he mentions that Umar gave people money to marry and to acquire mounts, as an example of what he calls the right of sufficiency. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:25:00} These are his tellings, without naming their sources (needs_source).

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "A Story and an Idea"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the nineteenth episode of his programme [[work/kissa-wa-fikra-program|"A Story and an Idea"]] to Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, under the idea of reform from within the system of rule and gradual change, and opens the twenty-fourth, on public money, with him as well. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:00:30} What follows is his account.

He opens with Umar's answer to his son, who urged him to carry justice through at any cost: he was dealing with something the young had grown up on and the old had grown old with, and would delay a just measure until he could pair it with some worldly good so that people would accept it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:01:00} He calls him the fifth rightly guided caliph and gives his birth as 61 AH in Medina; he grew up among the surviving Companions, and the Successors who came to teach him found themselves learning from him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:02:30} [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] married him to his daughter [[person/fatima-bint-abd-al-malik|Fatima]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:02:30} [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|Al-Walid]] made him governor of Medina at twenty-five on his conditions that he rule justly and take no more than was due; he called ten jurists of Medina as a council and asked them to report any unjust official, and on al-Walid's orders rebuilt and enlarged the Prophet's Mosque. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:06:00} He kept out of the Umayyad hostility to the Alids and honoured the Prophet's family. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:07:00} He disliked [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj]] and asked that he not pass through Medina, and al-Hajjaj's complaints brought about his dismissal in 93 AH. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:09:00}

When al-Walid asked whether a man who insulted the caliphs should be killed, Umar asked whether the man had killed anyone, and told no, said he should be disciplined, not killed. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:10:30} He refused to repudiate Sulayman as heir when al-Walid wished to name his own son, holding to the oath already sworn. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:11:30} When [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman]] became caliph in 96 AH Umar became his counsellor; al-Hajjaj's deputies were dismissed and the prisons emptied. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:13:00} When Sulayman marvelled at the Hajj crowds, Umar told him they were his subjects today and his opponents before God tomorrow. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:14:00}

Dying, Sulayman consulted [[person/raja-ibn-haywa|Raja' ibn Haywa]], who advised against a son not yet of age and against the absent Dawud; Sulayman named Umar in a sealed letter with Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik after him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:17:30} The Umayyads swore to the unnamed man; Umar begged Raja' to tell him so that he could ask to be excused, and Raja' refused. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:18:30} When the letter was read in the mosque of Dabiq, Umar could not stand and was lifted onto the pulpit. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:20:30} In his first sermon he said he had been chosen without his consent or the Muslims' counsel and released them from their oath to choose freely, and they cried out that they chose him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:21:30}

His rule became known for justice and plenty, so that a man carrying his zakat could find no poor person to take it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:22:30} He returned to the treasury the lands and gifts given to himself and his family, required the Umayyads to return state grants, dismissed unjust governors and lifted the jizya from converts, telling a worried official that Muhammad had been sent as a guide, not a collector of taxes. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:23:30} He ordered the recording and collection of hadith, and met Kharijite leaders and answered them on everything but the succession. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:24:30} He planned to return the succession to consultation but died in 101 AH before it was settled, with not one prisoner left in his jails. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:26:00} Accounts of his illness differ, al-Suwaidan notes, one saying he was poisoned; in the account he cites, Umar asked the servant why he had poisoned him, was told it was for a thousand dinars and his freedom, put the money in the treasury and let him go. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:26:30}

In the twenty-fourth episode he tells of Umar at his death calling his eleven sons, having left only a dozen or so dinars, five of which bought his grave: a righteous son, he said, God would care for, and a sinful one he would not equip for sin. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:01:00} He also tells how Umar, eating lentils and onions, answered his aunt's request for a larger stipend by heating a dirham, saying he would answer for every dirham on the Day of Judgement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:14:00} His lesson from the nineteenth episode: reform from within rule is better than reform from outside, reform from outside is better than revolution, and revolution is better than despotism. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep19 00:29:00}
