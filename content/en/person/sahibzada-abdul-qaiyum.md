## Early life and education

Sahibzada Abdul Qayyum (12 December 1863 – 4 December 1937) was born at Topi in Swabi district into a well-known religious family; his paternal line is traced back to the Lodi dynasty and his maternal line to Husayn ibn Ali.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en} His mother died when he was three and his father was assassinated when he was ten; raised by his maternal uncle at Kotha, he studied at the local madrassah before a visiting missionary, the Reverend Hughes, persuaded the family to send him to Peshawar, where he passed his school examinations in 1880 and 1883 and the Naib-Tehsildar examination in 1886.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en}

## In the administration

He entered the British Indian administration as Naib Tehsildar in 1887, served with the Black Mountain expedition of 1888 — his daily reports were commended and he received the 'Hazara 88' silver medal — and rose through settlement and revenue posts to Assistant Political Agent in Khyber and Chitral.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en} In 1893 he attended the Durand Line commission at Parachinar alongside Sir Mortimer Durand, on the British Indian side of the demarcation of the Indo-Afghan boundary.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en}

## Islamia College and politics

His lasting foundation is Islamia College, Peshawar, established on Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's policy of educating Muslims; in 1913 the Haji Sahib of Turangzai laid its foundation stone at his invitation.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en}{cite:wikipedia-turangzai-haji-saheb-en} He sat in the Indian Legislative Assembly from 1924 to 1932, served on the 1928 committee on education in the Frontier, Delhi and Ajmer-Merwara, and represented the province at the Round Table Conferences of 1931–33.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en} When the North-West Frontier Province became a governor's province, he became its first Chief Minister on 1 April 1937; the Congress considered him the spokesman of British imperialism, a vote of no confidence removed him that September, and Dr Khan Sahib succeeded him.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en} His short ministry funded the Malakand power plant, expanded educational facilities, and lifted the ban on political activity in the province.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en}

## Death

A delegation from Islamia College visited him at Topi on 3 December 1937 to plan the college's silver jubilee; after seeing his guests off he collapsed, fell into a coma, and died in the early hours — the article's infobox and lead give 4 December 1937, Radio Pakistan marks the anniversary on that date, and Wikidata gives 3 December, so the death is carried as debated in his record.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en}{cite:wikidata-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum} The Eastern Times called him, in its report of his funeral, incomparably the greatest man the province had produced.{cite:wikipedia-sahibzada-abdul-qaiyum-en}
