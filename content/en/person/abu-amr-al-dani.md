## Overview
Uthman ibn Sa'id ibn Uthman al-Dani, Abu Amr, also called Ibn al-Sayrafi, a client of the Umayyads: a hadith master and a leading authority on the Qur'an, its readings and its exegesis, from Denia in al-Andalus. He lived from 371 to 444 AH (981–1053 CE). He travelled east, made the pilgrimage, visited Egypt, and returned home to die in his own town. {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.205}

He left more than a hundred works, among them al-Taysir on the seven readings, al-Ishara on readings, al-Muqni' on the orthography and pointing of the codices, al-Ihtida on pause and beginning, al-Mawdih on the schools of the readers, Jami' al-Bayan on the readings, and Tabaqat al-Qurra'. A copy of his Fihras Tasanif survives in the Azhar mosque library in Cairo, and a scholar compiled Fawa'id Abi Amr al-Dani, which is his chain of transmission in the readings. {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.205}
