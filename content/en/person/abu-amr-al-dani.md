## Overview
Uthman ibn Sa'id ibn Uthman al-Dani, Abu Amr, also called Ibn al-Sayrafi, a client of the Umayyads: a hadith master and a leading authority on the Qur'an, its readings and its exegesis, from Denia in al-Andalus. He lived from 371 to 444 AH (981–1053 CE). He travelled east, made the pilgrimage, visited Egypt, and returned home to die in his own town. {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.205}

He left more than a hundred works, among them al-Taysir on the seven readings, al-Ishara on readings, al-Muqni' on the orthography and pointing of the codices, al-Ihtida on pause and beginning, al-Mawdih on the schools of the readers, Jami' al-Bayan on the readings, and Tabaqat al-Qurra'. A copy of his Fihras Tasanif survives in the Azhar mosque library in Cairo, and a scholar compiled Fawa'id Abi Amr al-Dani, which is his chain of transmission in the readings. {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.205}

## His life in the preface to the al-Taysir edition
Otto Pretzl, editor of [[work/al-taysir-fi-al-qiraat-al-sab|al-Taysir]], gives his life from Ibn al-Jazari's Ghayat al-Nihaya and others: Abu Amr Uthman ibn Sa'id ibn Uthman al-Dani, an Umayyad client of [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]], known in his day as Ibn al-Sayrafi, a Maliki, called al-Dani because he lived in Denia; born in 371 AH, he began studying in 386 AH. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.10} He travelled east in 397 AH, spent four months in Qayrawan and a year in Egypt, made the pilgrimage in 398 AH and returned to al-Andalus in 399 AH; he then lived seven years in Saragossa, came to Denia in 417 AH and settled there until he died on Monday, mid-Shawwal 444 AH, the ruler walking before his bier. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.11} He is reported to have said: I never saw anything without writing it down, never wrote anything without memorising it, and never memorised anything and forgot it. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.11} He studied the readings with Abu al-Qasim Khalaf ibn Ibrahim ibn Khaqan of Egypt (d. 402 AH), on whom he relied for Warsh's reading, with Abu al-Fath Faris ibn Ahmad al-Himsi and Abu al-Hasan Tahir ibn Ghalbun, and transmitted Ibn Mujahid's Kitab al-Sab'a from Abu Muslim Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Katib of Baghdad. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.11} {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.12} His students included Abu Dawud Sulayman ibn Najah, the most eminent of them, and Ibn al-Bayyaz, who heard al-Taysir from him. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.12} He is said to have written some 120 works, among them Jami' al-Bayan fi al-Qira'at al-Sab', al-Muqni' on the script of the regional copies, al-Muhkam on dotting, al-Muhtawa on anomalous readings, a poem on the principles of the sunna and Tabaqat al-Qurra'. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.13} [[person/ibn-al-jazari|Ibn al-Jazari]], in al-Nashr, says al-Dani stated that a valid reading is one that meets the three pillars. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p.19}
