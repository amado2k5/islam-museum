## Overview
Al-Ala ibn al-Harith ibn Abd al-Warith al-Hadrami, Abu Wahb, also called Abu Muhammad, of Damascus, was one of the companions of Makhul al-Shami; the Six Books other than al-Bukhari's narrate from him {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 478} {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 483}. Ibn Sad says he transmitted little hadith but was the most knowledgeable of Makhul's companions and the earliest of them, and that he died in 136 AH, at the end of the caliphate of Abu al-Abbas {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 467}.

## Teachers
Al-Mizzi lists as those from whom he narrated Hizam ibn Hakim al-Dimashqi, Rabia ibn Yazid, Zayd ibn Artat, Sulayman ibn Musa, Abd Allah ibn Busr al-Mazini, described as a companion of the Prophet, Abd Allah ibn Dinar, Ali ibn Abi Talha, Amr ibn Shuayb, al-Qasim Abu Abd al-Rahman, Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, Makhul al-Shami and Abu al-Ashath al-Sanani {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 479}.

## Students
Those who narrated from him include Sadaqa ibn Abd Allah al-Samin, Abd Rabbih ibn Maymun al-Hasi al-Ashari, Abd al-Rahman ibn Thabit ibn Thawban, Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr al-Awzai, Uthman ibn Husn ibn Ullaq, Abu Muhammad Isa ibn Musa al-Qurashi, al-Faraj ibn Fudala, Muawiya ibn Salih al-Hadrami, Muawiya ibn Yahya, al-Haytham ibn Humayd al-Ghassani and Yahya ibn Hamza al-Hadrami {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 479}.

## What the critics said of him
- Muawiya ibn Salih reported that Ahmad ibn Hanbal called him sound in hadith, and al-Mufaddal ibn Ghassan al-Ghallabi said the same {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 479}.
- Ibn Main, as reported by Abbas al-Duri, called him reliable; when asked whether there was anything in his hadith, he said no, but that he held the doctrine of qadar {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 480}.
- Ali ibn al-Madini called him reliable, and Yaqub ibn Sufyan said that he is reliable {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 480}.
- Abu Ubayd al-Ajurri reported from Abu Dawud: reliable, held qadar, and his mind changed {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 480}.
- Duhaym said he stood ahead of Makhul's companions and was reliable; Abu Hatim said he heard Duhaym speak of him, put him first and magnify his standing, and that al-Awzai narrated three hadith from him {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 480}.
- Abu Hatim said he knew none among Makhul's companions more trustworthy; asked by Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Kinani al-Isbahani, he said he held qadar, was a Damascene, one of the best of Makhul's companions, truthful in hadith and reliable {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 480}.
- Abu Zura al-Dimashqi reported from Duhaym, from Abu Mushir, that when Sulayman ibn Musa died people sat with al-Ala ibn al-Harith, and when he died Ibn Suraqa asked who was the jurist now; they said Qays ibn Musa the blind, and he said that was when they were lost {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 481}.
- Abu Zura asked Abd al-Rahman ibn Ibrahim which was the firmer, al-Ala or Thabit ibn Thawban; he answered that al-Ala had the deeper understanding of hadith and Thabit transmitted little {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 481}.
- Abu Zura said he used to see Abu Mushir put three of Makhul's companions first: Sulayman ibn Musa, Yazid ibn Yazid ibn Jabir and al-Ala ibn al-Harith {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 481}. Abu Mushir related that Said ibn Abd al-Aziz told him that Makhul's book on the pilgrimage was taken from al-Ala ibn al-Harith {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 482}.
- Al-Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn Bakkar reported from Abu Mushir that the highest of Makhul's companions was Sulayman ibn Musa, with Yazid ibn Yazid ibn Jabir, then al-Ala ibn al-Harith and Thabit ibn Thawban, to whom Makhul left his bequest {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 482}.
- Yaqub ibn Sufyan asked Hisham ibn Ammar which of Makhul's companions stood highest; he said Sulayman ibn Musa, and that al-Ala ibn al-Harith came next {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 482}.

## Death
Ali ibn Abd Allah al-Tamimi, Khalifa ibn Khayyat, Muhammad ibn Sad, Yahya ibn Bukayr, Abu Ubayd, al-Mufaddal ibn Ghassan and Abu Sulayman ibn Zabr said he died in 136 AH, and al-Tamimi added that he was seventy years old {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 482}. Abu Mushir said he died the day he died as the jurist of the Jund, in another report the most learned jurist of the Jund {cite:tahdhib-kamal-mizzi-risala vol. 22 p. 482}.

## Notes
Critics who described his view on qadar, namely Ibn Main, Abu Dawud and Abu Hatim, are quoted above; Abu Dawud adds that his mind changed, and Ibn Sad says he gave legal opinions until he became confused {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 467}. The nisba al-Hadrami in al-Mizzi's heading is printed as such; neither al-Mizzi nor Ibn Sad gives his date or place of birth.
