## Summary
Ahmad Nayrizi was an Iranian calligrapher active between 1682 and 1739, credited by Maryam Ekhtiar with codifying and perfecting "revival naskh", a large, elegant and clear form of [[concept/naskh-script|naskh]] meant to make it bigger and more readable. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 29}

## His style
His lines are well spaced and his letters bold, well formed and vocalised. Revival naskh was used mainly for Qur'ans, prayer books and other holy texts, as part of a movement in later seventeenth-century Iran to make Arabic religious texts more accessible to Persian speakers, which is why Nayrizi's Qur'ans often include interlinear Persian translations. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 29}

## Teacher and followers
He followed the late Safavid calligrapher Muhammad Ibrahim Qummi, as signatures at the foot of a few of his works acknowledge. The poet-calligrapher Muhammad Shafi' Visal-i Shirazi (1779–1846) and his sons kept his style alive into the nineteenth century. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 29} {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 30}
