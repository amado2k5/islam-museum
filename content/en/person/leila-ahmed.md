## Summary
Leila Ahmed is an Egyptian-American professor at Harvard and a founder of the field of women’s studies in Islam in the American academy. Her project combines social history with memoir. {cite:wikipedia-leila-ahmed}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Cairo in 1940 to a cultured Egyptian family, she studied and taught in Britain and the Emirates before settling in the United States as professor at the University of Massachusetts Amherst and then Harvard. {cite:wikipedia-leila-ahmed}

## Career and works
Her books include Women and Gender in Islam (1992), tracing gender discourses through Islamic history; her memoir A Border Passage on a childhood between Egypt and the West; and A Quiet Revolution on the veil’s return in America after she had earlier written of its decline. {cite:wikipedia-leila-ahmed}

## Method and contribution
She practises a critical historical method reading gender representations as products of context, while candidly invoking her own experience. She is honoured as a field founder, criticised by conservatives for her judgements and by some feminists for shifting positions; the museum attributes these descriptions to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-leila-ahmed}
