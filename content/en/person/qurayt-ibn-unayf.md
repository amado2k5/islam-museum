## Overview

Qurayt ibn Unayf al-Anbari al-Tamimi, a pre-Islamic poet whose life is obscure; al-Zirikli's entry gives no dates.{cite:al-aalam vol. 5 p. 195}

## The occasion of his poem

Mu'ammar ibn al-Muthanna reports his story: some of Banu Shayban raided him and took thirty of his camels, his own people abandoned him, and he appealed to Banu Mazin, who seized a hundred camels from Banu Shayban and gave them to him. He then recited the well-known verses that begin "Had you been of Mazin, my camels would not have been plundered".{cite:al-aalam vol. 5 p. 195}

## The Hamasa

Abu Tammam opened his *Diwan al-Hamasa* with a selection from these verses. He said they belonged to a poet of Banu al-Anbar without naming him.{cite:al-aalam vol. 5 p. 195} A footnote in the 1916 edition of the Hamasa adds that al-Tibrizi named the poet as Qurayt ibn Unayf, and that al-Baghdadi counted him an Islamic-era poet, whereas al-Zirikli calls him pre-Islamic.{cite:hamasa-abu-tammam-ed-1916 p. 6}
