## Summary

Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut (1928–2016) was a hadith scholar and text editor of Albanian descent who lived between Damascus and Amman, and whose name became a byword for accuracy in editing Islamic heritage in the twentieth century.{cite:wikipedia-shuayb-al-arnaut}

## Life

Born in Damascus to a family that had emigrated from Albania, he studied hadith and jurisprudence under the scholars of Syria and worked for decades editing manuscripts until his editions became standard references for libraries and researchers.{cite:wikipedia-shuayb-al-arnaut}

## Editions

His best-known supervised editions include Musnad Ahmad ibn Hanbal in fifty volumes for Mu'assasat al-Risala, Sahih Ibn Hibban, Siyar A'lam al-Nubala' of al-Dhahabi, and Tarikh Madinat Dimashq of Ibn Asakir — editions built on collating manuscripts and documenting hadiths.{cite:wikipedia-shuayb-al-arnaut}

## Method and standing

He was famed for careful verification of hadith transmission and precise brief annotations, and for a team of editors trained at his hand. Researchers rank him among the last of the great editors who carried out the huge heritage projects.{cite:wikipedia-shuayb-al-arnaut}
