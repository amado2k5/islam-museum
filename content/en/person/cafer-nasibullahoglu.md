## Summary
Cafer Nasibullahoglu is the Mufti of St Petersburg and north-western Russia and imam of the city's cathedral mosque, among the longest-serving figures of Russia's Muslims, having led the great mosque's imamate for decades according to press reports, serving a community numbering in the hundreds of thousands. {cite:muslim500-cafer-nasibullahoglu}

## Office and community
His Muslim 500 profile describes him as imam of the St Petersburg Mosque and mufti of a city home to almost one million Muslims — Tatars, Kazakhs, Uzbeks, Tajiks and Arabs among others — with the sermon often delivered in Arabic, Tatar and Russian. In 2004 the Kuwait News Agency reported his meeting with the Kuwaiti Awqaf minister and quoted him saying the city's Muslims neared 600,000 and planned a second mosque and a school for their children. {cite:muslim500-cafer-nasibullahoglu} {cite:kuna-cafer-nasibullahoglu}

## Imam of the cathedral mosque
The St Petersburg Mosque opened in 1913 under the Russian Empire and was then among Europe's largest mosques. Press reports describe Nasibullahoglu as its imam for some four decades until 2016; at the centenary he recalled that the city held eight thousand Muslims when the mosque was built, growing to about 700,000 from twenty-two nationalities. {cite:muslim500-cafer-nasibullahoglu} {cite:kuna-cafer-nasibullahoglu}

## The Russian context
He worked in a city whose Muslims have known parallel religious administrations; he kept his own role as imam of the cathedral mosque while other boards ran neighbouring religious affairs, and the museum reports these roles attributed to their sources. {cite:kuna-cafer-nasibullahoglu}

## Legacy
His significance lies in institutional continuity: the imamate of a venerable mosque in a former imperial capital, a trilingual sermon for a diverse migrant congregation, and official dialogue with awqaf bodies in the Muslim world. The Muslim 500 lists him under Administration of Religious Affairs as one of eastern Europe's Muslim authorities. {cite:muslim500-cafer-nasibullahoglu}
## The mosque and its centenary
The cathedral mosque marked its hundredth year in 2010 before government officials and ambassadors of Muslim countries, and Nasibullahoglu told the press how the city's Muslims had grown from eight thousand at the opening to hundreds of thousands, and how the venerable mosque needed a second site to hold the faithful. {cite:kuna-cafer-nasibullahoglu} {cite:muslim500-cafer-nasibullahoglu}

## Pattern of service
His work represents the big-city imamate: a sermon moving between Arabic for the text and the languages of congregation and country, administration of a multi-ethnic community, and formal ties with awqaf institutions and visiting religious figures from the Muslim world, as in the 2004 meeting with the Kuwaiti minister. {cite:kuna-cafer-nasibullahoglu}
