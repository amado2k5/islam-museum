## Summary
Aryat was the commander of the Abyssinian army that occupied [[place/yemen|Yemen]] in 525 CE with seventy thousand soldiers. He governed it on behalf of the king of [[polity/aksumite-kingdom|Abyssinia]] until he was assassinated by [[person/abraha|Abraha]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 25} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}.

## The occupation
The Roman emperors incited the Abyssinians and equipped them with a naval fleet, as vengeance for what Dhu Nuwas had done to the Christians of Najran. Seventy thousand soldiers from Abyssinia landed and occupied Yemen a second time under Aryat's command in 525 CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 24} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 25}. See [[event/abyssinian-occupation-of-yemen|the second Abyssinian occupation]].

## The end
Aryat remained governor on behalf of the king of Abyssinia until [[person/abraha|Abraha]], one of his army's commanders, assassinated him. Abraha then ruled in his place, after winning the king's favour {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 25}.

## Notes for researchers
The name appears in the book's edition as "Arbat" and "Aryat"; the name familiar in the biographical literature is "Aryat".
