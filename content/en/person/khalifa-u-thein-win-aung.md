## Summary

Khalifa U Thein Win Aung is a Sufi spiritual leader serving Myanmar's Muslim minority: a khalifa of the Islamic Sufi Tariqah League Myanmar and a leader of the KAFTG Garana Organization, whose following The Muslim 500 estimates at more than two hundred thousand Myanmar Muslims. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 176}

This record rests on a single source — the figure's entry in The Muslim 500 — and it says so plainly: public documentation of the religious life of Myanmar's Sufi networks in Western languages is thin, and the museum records what is attested and no more.

## Context: Islam in Myanmar

Muslims are a small minority in Myanmar — a few percent of the population — concentrated in Rakhine State, Yangon, Mandalay and the towns of the north. Their communities carry a layered history: Persian and Arab traders on the coast, Indian Muslim settlement under British rule, Sufi orders spreading along the same roads that carried commerce. Sufi practice — dhikr gatherings, veneration of saints, tariqah affiliation — remains a living strand of Myanmar Islam alongside reformist and revivalist currents. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 176}

In recent decades that minority has lived through communal violence and displacement, most severely in Rakhine State but also in the anti-Muslim riots that struck central Myanmar towns in 2013–2014. It is against this background that the community's religious leaders carry a double charge: spiritual guidance and material relief. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 176}

## Role and work

The title 'Khalifa' marks him as a deputy — a successor authorised to guide disciples — within his order's chain. As a leader of the Islamic Sufi Tariqah League Myanmar, he stands at the head of an organisational expression of Sufi affiliation in a country where such orders have historically been loosely coordinated. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 176}

His Muslim 500 entry credits him with serving over two hundred thousand Myanmar Muslims and with aiding communities affected by the riots — relief work folded into spiritual leadership, the ordinary pattern of Sufi headship in embattled communities. The same entry names the KAFTG Garana Organization, a community organisation through which that service is channelled. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 176}

## Assessment

Figures of this kind — the heads of minority religious networks in closed or conflicted countries — rarely leave a documentary trail in international sources, and Khalifa U Thein Win Aung is no exception. What the record holds is his listing among the world's influential Muslims for the community he serves: a reminder that the census of Muslim leadership runs well past the famous names into the zawiyas and relief committees of communities that rarely make headlines. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 176}
