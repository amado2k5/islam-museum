## Summary
Ahmad al-Tayyeb is the Grand Imam of Al-Azhar al-Sharif, the highest religious office in institutional Sunni Islam. Chosen in 2010, he combines administration of the ancient Azhar establishment with global dialogue projects. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-tayyeb}

## Life and upbringing
Born in 1946 in the village of al-Qurna, Luxor, Upper Egypt, to a Sufi family, he was educated at al-Azhar to a doctorate in creed and Islamic philosophy, teaching at al-Azhar and the Islamic University of Madinah. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-tayyeb}

## Career and works
He rose through the offices: mufti of Egypt (2002–2003), president of al-Azhar University, then Grand Imam in 2010. He founded the Muslim Council of Elders, co-signed the Document on Human Fraternity with Pope Francis in Abu Dhabi in 2019, and wrote academic works in Islamic philosophy including al-Janib al-Naqdi. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-tayyeb}

## Method and contribution
He represents the centrist Azhar method: Ash’ari creed, openness to the four schools, opposition to takfir. His interfaith-dialogue stance is respected worldwide, while some institutional decisions draw criticism from various currents inside and outside Egypt; the museum attributes these descriptions without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-tayyeb}
