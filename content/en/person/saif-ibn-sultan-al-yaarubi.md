## Overview
Saif ibn Sultan ibn Saif ibn Malik al-Ya'rubi was one of the Ibadi imams of Oman and the fourth of the Ya'rubids. Al-Zirikli gives his death as 1123 AH, which is 1711 CE, and prints no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 149}. The Ibadis are a school of Islam distinct from the Sunni schools, and what follows about his imamate is al-Zirikli's account of it.

## Life
He rose against his brother, the imam Bil'arab ibn Sultan, because of an estrangement between them, fought him and besieged him in the fort of Yabrin. Bil'arab died under siege, and the oath of allegiance to Saif was completed in 1104 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 149}.

## Rule and achievements
He took the Omani realm in hand and his conduct was good. He was nicknamed Qayd al-Ard, the fetter of the land, for his control of the country, and al-Zirikli calls him brave and resolute {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 149}. He attacked the Portuguese at Daman, north of Bombay, and on the island of Salsette near Bombay, took 1,400 of them captive, and rescued Mombasa from their hands in 1110 AH (1698 CE); Zanzibar submitted to him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 149}. He also raided the borders of India and Persia with an army said to hold ninety-six thousand horsemen {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 149}. In his days Oman flourished through the palms and trees planted in it, and he assembled a fleet equipped with the largest guns of his age. He ruled until he died at al-Rustaq {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 149}.
