## Summary
Ferdinand Wüstenfeld edited al-Bakri's [[work/mujam-ma-istaajam|Mu'jam ma sta'jam]] "from the manuscripts of Leiden, Cambridge, London and Milan"; the first volume of his edition appeared in Göttingen and Paris in 1876. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p.5}

## His work on the edition
In his German preface he says he gathered what the manuscripts contain about [[person/al-bakri-abu-ubayd|al-Bakri]], whose life and works Dozy and then de Slane had already compiled from the Arabic biographers. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p.7} He tells how, staying for some weeks as Dozy's guest in Leiden, he was allowed to take the Leiden manuscript home to copy it for himself, finishing the first part at Christmas and the second "in the troubled days of the year 1848", and how he went on correcting the text until, at the start of 1865, he obtained the Cambridge manuscript through his pupil Sidgwick of Trinity College. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p.9} He notes that he based his study of the dwellings and migrations of the Arab tribes on al-Bakri's introduction, which he translated in full there. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p.9}
