# Hasan Sabri Ayvazov

Asan Sabri Ayvazov (6 May 1878 – 17 April 1938), recorded here under the name Hasan Sabri Ayvazov, was a Crimean Tatar writer, literary critic and educator formed in the circle of Ismail Gasprinsky. He was born at Alupka in Crimea into a poor peasant family, finished a madrasa and taught at Alupka, then studied at the pedagogical institute in Istanbul in 1892–1896, publishing as a student in Gasprinsky's newspaper Tercüman and in the Istanbul and Baku press.{cite:wikipedia-hasan-sabri-ayvazov-uk}{cite:wikipedia-hasan-sabri-ayvazov-crh}

Back in Crimea he opened a new-method school at Alupka, taught at Bakhchysarai from 1913 and for a time at the Lazarev Institute of Oriental Languages in Moscow. From 1914 he edited Tercüman itself. In the revolution of 1917–1918 he edited the newspaper Millet, was twice elected chairman of the Crimean Qurultay, in January and May 1918, and served as the Crimean Regional Government's ambassador to the Ottoman Empire.{cite:wikipedia-hasan-sabri-ayvazov-uk}

Under Soviet rule he worked in the translation department of the Crimean Central Executive Committee, from 1922 as a senior assistant at the Crimean University and a teacher of Arabic and Ottoman at the pedagogical institute, and he supported the transfer of Crimean Tatar to the Latin alphabet. He was arrested in 1930 and released after two months; arrested again on 5 April 1937, he was shot on 17 April 1938.{cite:wikipedia-hasan-sabri-ayvazov-uk}{cite:wikidata-hasan-sabri-ayvazov}

The Ukrainian article is the fullest account of him — he has no English article — and the shorter Crimean Tatar article and Wikidata agree with it on his dates. None of the sources names a religion or school for him: his madrasa schooling and his teaching of Arabic and Ottoman are education and work, not attestation, so his faith is recorded as unknown.
