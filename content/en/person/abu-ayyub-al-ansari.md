## Brief
Khalid ibn Zayd ibn Kulayb al-Najjari, Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, the Prophet's host in Medina {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## Taking up the saddle-bag
When the Prophet's she-camel knelt at the spot where the Prophet's Mosque stands today — among the Banu al-Najjar, his maternal kin — Abu Ayyub hurried to the saddle-bag and took it into his house. The Prophet used to say: "A man is with his baggage" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.173}.

In Anas's narration in al-Bukhari, the Prophet asked: "Which of our people's houses is nearest?" Abu Ayyub said: "I, O Messenger of God; this is my house and this is my door." He said: "Go and prepare a resting place for us" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## Afterwards
The Prophet stayed with him until the mosque and its apartments were built. Abu Ayyub was killed at the walls of Constantinople and, according to the best-known account, was buried there {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad}.

## The Prophet's lodging in his house (The Sealed Nectar p.173)
When the Prophet entered Medina, his she-camel passed by the houses. He would tell whoever took hold of her rein, "Let her go, for she is commanded," until she knelt at the spot of the Prophet's Mosque. Abu Ayyub said his house was the nearest of the houses and invited the Prophet to enter it. The Prophet said: "Go and prepare a resting place for us" ([[event/hijra-journey-to-medina|The Hijra journey and entry into Medina]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.173} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/556}.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.) entry on Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, a Companion of the Prophet, says that the Prophet stayed in his house on the emigration to Medina before his own mosque and house were built. He took part in all the Prophet's expeditions and served under 'Amr b. al-'As in the conquest of Egypt. 'Ali appointed him governor of Medina, but he had to rejoin 'Ali in Iraq when Busr b. Abi Artat approached the city with 3,000 men, and he fought in 'Ali's battles there. Under Mu'awiya he joined the invasion of Cyprus and the expedition against Constantinople, where he died of dysentery in 52/672 (the article also gives 50, 51 and 55), and was buried under the city walls at his request. Of the 150 hadiths attributed to him, the article says al-Bukhari admitted thirteen. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 127}
