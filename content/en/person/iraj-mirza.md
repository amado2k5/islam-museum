# Iraj Mirza

Iraj Mirza (1874–1926), titled Jalal-ol-Mamalek, was a Qajar prince and Persian poet, considered the first master of colloquial Persian poetry and one of the most popular poets of the late Qajar period. {cite:wikipedia-iraj-mirza-en}{cite:iranica-iraj-mirza}

## Formation and career

He was born at Tabriz in October 1874, great-grandson of Fath-Ali Shah; his father Gholam-Hossein Mirza was poet laureate to the crown prince there. Educated at Tabriz's Dar ul-Funun, he succeeded his father as court poet, then left the court for government posts in Tabriz, Tehran, Kermanshah and Mashhad, serving in the customs, education and finance administrations. He died of a heart attack at Tehran on 14 March 1926 — Iranica gives 12 March, a split the museum's record discloses — and was buried at Zahir-od-dowleh. {cite:wikipedia-iraj-mirza-en}{cite:iranica-iraj-mirza}

## Poetry

Writing in everyday speech enriched with classical craft, Iraj made his name with satire aimed at dishonest clergy, merchants and statesmen, and with poems on women, children and education, among them "Mother", the Story of the Veil, the Arefnameh against the poet Aref Qazvini, and the unfinished verse tale Zohreh-o Manuchehr after Shakespeare's Venus and Adonis. {cite:wikipedia-iraj-mirza-en}
