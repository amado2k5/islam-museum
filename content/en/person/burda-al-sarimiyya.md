## Summary
Burda al-Sarimiyya was a devout woman of [[place/basra|Basra]]; Ibn al-Jawzi gives her entry in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]] (no. 596), and Thabit al-Bunani describes her as a woman of the first generations who wept so much that her sight was ruined. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.712}

## Guests in a strange land
When asked how she was that morning, she would say: "We have woken as guests seeking pasture in a land of exile, waiting to answer the caller." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.712}

## With al-Hasan al-Basri
She was told: "Fear God — are you not afraid your sight will go?" She replied: "If I am of the people of the Fire, may God put me and my sight far away; if I am of the people of the Garden, God will give me eyes better than these." [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|Al-Hasan al-Basri]] visited her and said, "Burda, your body has a right over you, and your sight has a right over you," and she answered him in the same words. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.712}

## Her intimate prayer
From Ata' ibn al-Mubarak: she was an eminent devout woman who stood the night, and when all movement stilled and eyes were at rest she would call out in a sorrowful voice, telling her Lord she was alone with Him with His love in her heart, and asking Him not to punish her. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.712} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.713} Al-Humaydi reports Sufyan saying that none of those women who lived as neighbours of the sanctuary was more strenuous than she; she wept until her sight went, and when she heard thunderclaps she cried out until she fainted. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.713}

## For young visitors
Burda was a woman of Basra who wept much from awe of God; when people feared for her eyes, she said God would give her better eyes in Paradise.
