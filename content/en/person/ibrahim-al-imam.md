## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas, known as Ibrahim al-Imam, was head of the Abbasid da'wa before the rise of their state. The chiefs of the mission in Kufa answered to him; it was he who changed the name of the man known as Abu Muslim to Abd al-Rahman ibn Muslim, married him to the daughter of Imran ibn Isma'il al-Ta'i, and to him Sulayman ibn Kathir and his companions brought the mission's funds. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.4}

## Entrusting the da'wa to Abu Salama
Bakir ibn Mahan wrote to him on his deathbed that he had appointed Abu Salama Hafs ibn Sulayman as successor; Ibrahim wrote ordering Abu Salama to take charge of the partisans, and wrote to the people of Khurasan that he had entrusted their affair to him. Abu Salama went on to Khurasan, where they believed him, accepted his authority and handed over the Shi'a's funds to him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.4}
