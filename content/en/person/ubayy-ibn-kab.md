## Summary
[[person/ubayy-ibn-kab|Ubayy ibn Ka'b]] ibn Qays of Banu al-Najjar al-Ansari, kunya Abu al-Mundhir and Abu al-Tufayl; Ibn Hajar calls him "master of the reciters" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}. He was of the people of the [[event/pledges-of-aqaba|second Aqaba]], and witnessed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and all the campaigns {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}.

## His standing with the Prophet and Umar
Ibn Hajar relates that the Prophet said to him: "may knowledge be sweet to you, Abu al-Mundhir", and told him: "God has commanded me to recite to you" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}. [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] used to call him "master of the Muslims" and say to him: "recite, Ubayy"; he asked him about cases and turned to him on hard questions {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}. Masruq counted him among the six men of fatwa {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}.

## Writing and appearance
Al-Waqidi said he was the first to write for the Prophet and the first to write at a document's end "wrote So-and-so son of So-and-so"; he was of medium height, white-bearded, his greyness unhidden {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}.

## Those who related from him
Of the Companions, Umar, Abu Ayyub, Ubada ibn al-Samit, Sahl ibn Sa'd, Abu Musa, Ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayra, Anas, Sulayman ibn Surad and others related from him, and the imams brought his hadiths in their Sahihs {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}.

## His illness
Ahmad, Abu Ya'la and Ibn Abi al-Dunya relate — Ibn Hibban authenticated it — from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that the Prophet was asked about illnesses and said: "expiations" — Ubayy said: Messenger of God, even when little? He said: "even a thorn and what is above it" — so Ubayy prayed never to be free of fever until he died, so long as it did not bar him from hajj, 'umra, jihad or the congregational prayer {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 27}.

## His death: the differing sayings
Ibn Hajar transmits from Ibn Ma'in that he died in year twenty or nineteen, and from al-Waqidi that Ubayy's family and companions say year twenty-two, and that Umar said: "today the master of the Muslims died" — then al-Waqidi mentions those who say he died in Uthman's caliphate in year thirty and calls it "the firmest of the sayings" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26}. Ibn Abd al-Barr said the majority hold he died in Umar's caliphate; Abu Nu'aym authenticated that he died in Uthman's caliphate in year thirty, arguing from Zar ibn Hubaysh's meeting him in Uthman's caliphate; Ibn Hibban said year twenty-two; al-Baghawi relates from al-Hasan that he died a week before Uthman's killing {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 26} {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 27}.

## For Explorers
Ubayy ibn Ka'b ibn Qays of Banu al-Najjar the Ansari, kunya Abu al-Mundhir and Abu al-Tufayl, whom Ibn Hajar calls 'the master of the reciters'. He was at the second Aqaba and saw Badr and every witness field. Ibn Hajar relates the Prophet told him 'rejoice in the knowledge' and 'God commanded me to recite to you'; Umar called him 'the lord of the Muslims', saying 'recite, Ubayy', and turned to him in knotty cases — Masruq counted him among the six who gave rulings. Al-Waqidi said he was the first to write for the Prophet and first to write at a letter's end 'so-and-so wrote'; he was short and white-bearded. Umar, Ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayra, Anas and others related from him, and the imams included his hadith in their sahihs.

## For Scholars
Ubayy ibn Ka'b ibn Qays of Banu al-Najjar the Ansari, kunya Abu al-Mundhir and Abu al-Tufayl, whom Ibn Hajar calls 'the master of the reciters'. He was at the second Aqaba and saw Badr and every witness field. Ibn Hajar relates the Prophet told him 'rejoice in the knowledge, Abu al-Mundhir' and 'God commanded me to recite to you'; Umar called him 'the lord of the Muslims', saying 'recite, Ubayy', asked him on new cases and turned to him in the knotty ones, and Masruq counted him among the six who gave rulings. Al-Waqidi said he was the first to write for the Prophet and the first to write at a letter's end 'so-and-so son of so-and-so wrote'; he was a short, white-bearded man whose grey never dyed. Of the Companions, Umar, Abu Ayyub, Ubada ibn al-Samit, Sahl ibn Sa'd, Abu Musa, Ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayra, Anas and Sulayman ibn Surad and others related from him, and the imams included his hadith in their sahihs.
