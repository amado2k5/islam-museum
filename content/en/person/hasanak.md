## Overview
Hasanak, properly Abu Ali Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn Abbas, was the last vizier of [[person/mahmud-ghaznawi|Mahmud of Ghazna]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 275} The Encyclopaedia of Islam gives his death as 423/1032. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 276}

## Governor and pilgrim
The article says Hasanak became governor of [[place/khurasan|Khurasan]] at an early age. He went on the pilgrimage in 414/1023 and, on the advice of others as reported by the chronicler Bayhaqi, returned by way of [[place/cairo|Cairo]], where he accepted a robe of honour from the Fatimid caliph [[person/al-zahir-fatimid|al-Zahir]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 275}

## The Abbasid suspicion
Because of that robe, the Abbasid caliph [[person/al-qadir-billah|al-Qadir]] suspected him of being an adherent of the Fatimid caliphate. After Hasanak returned to Ghazna, the caliph asked Mahmud to have him executed as a Qarmati; the article notes that this shows Baghdad then classed Fatimids and Qarmatis together. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 275} Mahmud evidently thought the charge unfounded: he went so far as to make Hasanak vizier in 415/1024, while the previous vizier, Maymandi, was thrown into prison. He tried to calm the caliph by sending Baghdad the robe and other presents Hasanak had received. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 275}

## Vizier of Mahmud
During the last six years of Mahmud's reign, the article says, Hasanak exerted a remarkable influence over him, but seems to have opposed Mahmud's son [[person/masud-ibn-mahmud|Mas'ud]] and to have supported the descendants of Mas'ud's brother Muhammad. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 275}

## Fall and death
This brought about his downfall after Mahmud died on 23 Rabi II 421, 30 April 1030. He was banished to Herat at once and accused of offending Mas'ud. Chiefly through the efforts of the finance minister Abu Sahl Sawsani, he was tried on the old charge of being a Qarmati, and the caliph al-Qadir, apparently offended that his wishes of 415/1024 had gone unmet, interfered again. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 275} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 276} Bayhaqi describes the trial in detail, in an account the article calls clearly sympathetic to Hasanak. Dressed only in a shirt, Hasanak was strangled on Wednesday 28 Safar 423, and his head was given in derision to Sawsani; his body stayed tied to a pillory for seven years. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 276}

## The article's judgement
The article concludes that Hasanak did not die merely through court intrigue and the rivalry of Mahmud's two sons. In its view he was also a victim of the deep fear that the Abbasid caliphs and the Ghaznavids felt of an Ismaili revolution, a fear not without grounds given the subversive currents of fifth/eleventh-century western Asia and Persia, even if in individual cases it struck the innocent. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 276}

## Dates and doubts
The article itself notes that 14 February 1032, the Gregorian date corresponding to 28 Safar 423, was a Monday, not a Wednesday, so the weekday and the date do not agree. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 276} The museum therefore records the year 423/1032 only.
