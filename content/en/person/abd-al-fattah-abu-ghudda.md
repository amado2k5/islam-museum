## Summary
Abd al-Fattah ibn Muhammad ibn Bashir ibn Hasan Abu Ghudda al-Khalidi al-Halabi was a Syrian Hanafi scholar, hadith specialist and critical editor, born in Aleppo on 17 Rajab 1336 AH (9 May 1917) and died in Riyadh on 16 February 1997. He taught at Damascus University and at Saudi universities, and his critical editions of classical works of hadith and Hanafi jurisprudence became standard editions in seminaries and universities; he published more than seventy authored and edited works. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en} {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-ar} {cite:wikidata-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda}

The English article describes him in its opening line as a "Sunni Hanafi Muslim scholar", and Wikidata's religion statement is "Sunni Islam"; two sources independently give his school. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en} {cite:wikidata-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda}

## Early life and education
He was born and raised in Aleppo. His father Muhammad was a textile merchant known for piety, and the family claims descent from Khalid ibn al-Walid. He studied first at a private Islamic school in Aleppo, then at the school of Shaykh Muhammad Ali al-Khatib, where he studied the Qur'an and calligraphy, then at the Khusrawiyya school (today the Sharia secondary school) between 1936 and 1942. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

He then went to Egypt and studied at al-Azhar University, taking his degree from the Faculty of Sharia in 1948 and from the Faculty of Arabic Language in 1950, with advanced training in psychology and education. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

## Teaching and scholarly work
He returned to Syria in 1950, rose to prominence in Aleppo's scholarly circles, and taught at the school where he had studied. In 1960 he became an instructor at Damascus University, teaching the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh), Hanafi fiqh and comparative fiqh. He stood in the 1961 parliamentary elections, and President Nazim al-Kudsi appointed him Mufti of Aleppo. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

In his Saudi exile he taught at the Faculty of Sharia at Riyadh University and guest-lectured at the Omdurman Institute in Sudan, then taught at the University of Jeddah, devoting his later years to research, writing and editing. He was a serious book collector, and the article notes how much he missed his personal library in exile. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

## The Brotherhood, prison and exile
He lived in Cairo between 1944 and 1950, where he met Hassan al-Banna, founder of the Muslim Brotherhood, and joined the organisation; on his return to Syria in 1950 he became a member of its Syrian branch. The article reports that he was critical of the Syrian Brotherhood's Supreme Guide Issam al-Attar, holding him unrestrained in power, and that when al-Attar stepped down Abu Ghudda replaced him as Supreme Guide — although the article contradicts itself on the year, giving 1973 in its introduction and 1962 in its text, a date that needs a second source before it can be relied on. The article adds that his leadership was organisational while he himself did not ascribe to all of the Brotherhood's beliefs. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

He opposed the 1966 coup that brought Salah Jadid to power, urged scholars to boycott the state, and preached against the regime in Aleppo's mosques. He was arrested and held for eleven months in the remote Tadmor Prison, then released in the general amnesty of 1967 after the Six-Day War. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

He left Syria for exile in Saudi Arabia, continued to oppose the Syrian government, and served as Inspector General of the Syrian Muslim Brotherhood from 1976 to 1983, leading its uprising; after the uprising failed he abandoned politics and turned to academia. He returned to Syria in December 1995 under an arrangement with the government that he refrain from politics and focus on scholarship and religion. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of positions reported in the sources, not as the museum's judgement on any of them.

The article notes that Abu Ghudda manifested differences of view with al-Dhahabi and Ibn Taymiyyah. In the early 1970s he published a tract against al-Albani's re-evaluation of Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim — one of the best-known scholarly disputes in hadith studies of the period. His student Mahmud Sa'id Mamduh, who studied with him, wrote further rebuttals of al-Albani, and the al-Albani article quotes Mamduh's objections in his own words. The museum records this scholarly disagreement as a fact documented in the two articles and takes no side in it. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en} {cite:wikipedia-al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din-en}

## Critical editions and authored works
Abu Ghudda published more than seventy works, and many of his critical editions of classical texts on the legal and hadith sciences became the standard editions distributed in seminaries and universities. Among his editions:
- Al-Raf' wa-l-takmil fi-l-jarh wa-l-ta'dil, Al-Ajwiba al-fadila li-l-as'ila al-ashara al-kamila and Iqamat al-hujja, all three by Abd al-Hayy al-Lucknawi.
- Risalat al-mustarshidin by al-Harith al-Muhasibi, an early work on Islamic spirituality.
- Al-Tasrih bi-ma tawatara fi nuzul al-Masih by Anwar Shah Kashmiri.
- Al-Ihkam fi tamyiz al-fatawa an al-ahkam by Shihab al-Din al-Qarafi.
- Fath Bab al-Inaya bi-sharh Kitab al-Nuqaya by Ali al-Qari, a Hanafi commentary.
- Al-Manar al-munif fi-l-sahih wa-l-da'if by Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya, and Al-Masnu' fi ma'rifat al-hadith al-mawdu' by Ali al-Qari.
- Fiqh ahl al-Iraq wa-hadithuhum by Muhammad Zahid al-Kawthari, and Qawa'id fi ulum al-hadith by Zafar Ahmad Usmani al-Thanawi. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

Among his authored works are Safahat min sabr al-ulama ala shada'id al-ilm wa-l-tahsil (Pages from the Patience of Scholars under the Hardships of Learning), one of his best-known books, and Mas'alat khalq al-Qur'an wa-atharuha, on the effect of the early controversy over the createdness of the Qur'an on the hadith sciences. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

## Students
His notable students include Muhammad Awwamah; Muhammad Taqi Usmani, who wrote an obituary in his memory; Salman Hussaini Nadwi; Muhammad Abdul Malek; and Mohammad Abu al-Fath al-Bayanouni. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

## Death
In Syria he suffered a heart attack and eye problems; in mid-1996 he returned to Riyadh for treatment, and died there at dawn on 16 February 1997 (9 Shawwal 1417 AH). President Hafez al-Assad sent condolences to his family and an official delegation visited them; the use of the president's personal plane to fly the body to Syria was offered, but he was buried at al-Baqi cemetery in Medina. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-en}

## Personal life
The articles record little of his family life; his nephew Abd al-Sattar Abu Ghudda was a well-known Islamic economist. The museum publishes no family detail beyond what understanding his work requires. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-fattah-abu-ghudda-ar}
