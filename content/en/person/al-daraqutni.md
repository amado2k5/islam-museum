## Summary
Ali ibn Umar Abu al-Hasan al-Daraqutni (306–385 AH), the hadith master of Baghdad and unrivalled scholar of his age, with whom the science of transmitted reports, the knowledge of narrators and the study of hadith defects reached their culmination. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.183}

## Unrivalled in his age
Al-Hakim Abu Abd Allah used to say: "Al-Daraqutni has never seen anyone like himself." It is related that in his youth he attended the session of Ismail al-Saffar while copying a part he had with him, and the teacher was dictating. He was told: "Your hearing is not valid while you are copying." He said: "My understanding of the dictation differs from yours." He then immediately repeated eighteen hadiths, with their chains and texts, in the order of the dictation until he reached the last of them, and people marvelled. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.184}

## What he combined
Alongside knowledge of hadith, he combined knowledge of the readings (qira'at), grammar, jurisprudence and poetry, with trustworthiness, integrity and soundness of belief. It is said that the book "al-Ilal," which is transmitted from him, was in fact copied by al-Barqani from his dictation to Abu Mansur ibn Hamdan al-Sayrafi in his sessions; this is the book of Ilal that people transmit from al-Daraqutni. He died on Tuesday, the seventh of Dhu al-Qa'da in the year 385 AH, and was buried in the Maruf cemetery, aged seventy-nine years and two days. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.184}

## In the introduction to al-Ta'liq al-Mughni
[[person/shams-al-haqq-al-azimabadi|Shams al-Haqq al-'Azimabadi]] gives his life in the introduction to [[work/al-taliq-al-mughni|al-Ta'liq al-Mughni]]: born in 306 AH, he heard Abu al-Qasim al-Baghawi, Abu Bakr b. Abi Dawud, Yahya b. Muhammad b. Sa'id, Ibn Durayd and a great many others in Baghdad, Basra, Kufa and Wasit, and in middle age travelled to Egypt and Syria. {cite:sunan-daraqutni-ibn-hazm-2011 p. 7} Among the works he lists are [[work/sunan-al-daraqutni|the Sunan]], *al-Mukhtalif wa-l-Mu'talif*, *al-'Ilal* and *al-Istidrak 'ala al-Sahihayn*; he quotes [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] that the Istidrak rests on some hadith scholars' very weak rules, contrary to the majority. {cite:sunan-daraqutni-ibn-hazm-2011 p. 7}

He quotes [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib]] that he had heard al-Daraqutni studied Shafi'i fiqh with Abu Sa'id al-Istakhri — or, some said, with a companion of Abu Sa'id — and that he knew by heart the diwans of several poets, among them that of al-Sayyid al-Himyari, and was for that reason linked to Shi'ism. {cite:sunan-daraqutni-ibn-hazm-2011 p. 8} Among the praise he cites is the judge Abu al-Tayyib al-Tabari's phrase "al-Daraqutni is the commander of the faithful in hadith", and 'Abd al-Ghani al-Azdi's habit of calling him "my teacher". {cite:sunan-daraqutni-ibn-hazm-2011 p. 8} He gives his death as 8 Dhu al-Qa'da 385 AH and explains the nisba as referring to Dar al-Qutn, once a large quarter of Baghdad. {cite:sunan-daraqutni-ibn-hazm-2011 p. 8}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Badr al-Din al-'Ayni (Hanafi)**: in *al-Binaya sharh al-Hidaya*, in the section on recitation, he said al-Daraqutni "deserves to be weakened", as al-'Azimabadi quotes him. {cite:sunan-daraqutni-ibn-hazm-2011 p. 9}
- **Sunni: Shams al-Haqq al-'Azimabadi**: holds al-'Ayni's remark to be discourteous and not worth heeding, since 'Abd al-Ghani of Egypt, al-Hakim, Abu al-Tayyib al-Tabari, al-Khatib and al-Azhari, and among later scholars al-Sam'ani, Ibn al-Athir, al-Dhahabi and al-Nawawi, declared him reliable and praised him. {cite:sunan-daraqutni-ibn-hazm-2011 p. 9}
:::
