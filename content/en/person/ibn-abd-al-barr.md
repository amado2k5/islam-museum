## Summary
Abu 'Umar Yusuf b. 'Abd Allah b. Muhammad b. 'Abd al-Barr b. 'Asim al-Namari of Cordoba was the leading hadith scholar and jurist of al-Andalus in the fifth Islamic century, author of [[work/al-tamhid-ibn-abd-al-barr|al-Tamhid]] and [[work/al-istidkar|al-Istidhkar]]. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 10} Ibn Khallikan describes him as the imam of his age in hadith, athar and what relates to them. {cite:wafayat-ayan p. 66-67}

## Birth and family
He was born in [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]] on a Friday while the imam was giving the sermon, five days before the end of Rabi' al-Akhir 368, which the Tamhid's editor equates with 29 November 978 CE. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 10} His family was Arab, of Taym Allah b. al-Namir b. Qasit, and had settled in Cordoba. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 10}

Ibn al-Abbar wrote a notice on his grandfather Muhammad b. 'Abd al-Barr, describing him as a devout man known for night prayer, a companion of Yahya b. Mujahid, who died seven months before his son 'Abd Allah at the age of eighty. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 10} His father, [[person/abd-allah-ibn-muhammad-ibn-abd-al-barr|'Abd Allah b. Muhammad]], was a Maliki jurist, born in 330 AH, who heard hadith at the age of eight from Ahmad b. Duhaym, studied closely with the jurist Ishaq b. Ibrahim b. Masarra, and died in 380 AH aged fifty; Abu 'Umar heard nothing from him. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 10-11}

## Study and teachers
He began to seek knowledge before 390 AH, at about twenty, and so reached the great teachers of the day. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 11} Among those from whom he transmitted most, as the Tamhid's editor lists them:
- Abu Muhammad 'Abd Allah b. Muhammad b. 'Abd al-Mu'min, known as Ibn al-Zayyat (d. 390 AH), from whom he took Abu Dawud's *Sunan* in Ibn Dasa's transmission and Abu Dawud's *al-Nasikh wa-al-Mansukh*, and who handed him Ahmad's *Musnad* in al-Qati'i's transmission. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 11-12}
- [[person/khalaf-ibn-qasim-ibn-al-dabbagh|Khalaf b. al-Qasim b. Sahl, Ibn al-Dabbagh]] (d. 393 AH), whom, as al-Humaydi reports, he placed above all his other teachers. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 12}
- 'Abd Allah b. Muhammad b. 'Abd al-Rahman b. Asad al-Juhani (d. 395 AH), from whom he took the book of 'Abd Allah b. 'Abd al-Hakam. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 12}
- [[person/abd-al-warith-ibn-sufyan|'Abd al-Warith b. Sufyan]] (d. 395 AH), through whom he received the learning of [[person/qasim-ibn-asbagh|Qasim b. Asbagh]], reading with him Qasim's *Musannaf* and Ibn Qutayba's *al-Ma'arif*. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 13}
- Abu 'Umar Ahmad b. 'Abd Allah al-Baji (d. 396 AH), with whom he read the *Musannaf* of Ibn Abi Shayba from beginning to end, and the books of Ibn al-Jarud. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 13-14}
- Muhammad b. 'Abd al-Malik b. Dayfun al-Rusafi (d. 394 AH), with whom he read the *Tafsir* of Muhammad b. Sanjar. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 14}
- [[person/said-ibn-nasr-ibn-abi-al-fath|Sa'id b. Nasr b. Abi al-Fath]] (d. 395 AH), source of the transmission of the *Muwatta'* he used in the Tamhid. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 14} {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 201}

He never travelled outside al-Andalus to study, contenting himself with licences (ijazat) from distinguished Eastern transmitters, among them Abu al-Qasim al-Saqati, 'Abd al-Ghani b. Sa'id of Egypt, [[person/abu-dharr-al-harawi|Abu Dharr al-Harawi]], transmitter of Sahih al-Bukhari, and Abu Muhammad Ibn al-Nahhas of Egypt. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 14-15} In al-Andalus he studied closely with Abu 'Umar Ahmad b. 'Abd al-Malik the jurist and with [[person/abu-al-walid-ibn-al-faradi|Abu al-Walid Ibn al-Faradi]], as Abu 'Ali al-Jayyani says. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 17}

## School
The Tamhid's editor says Ibn 'Abd al-Barr began as a traditionist with Zahiri leanings and later became a Maliki, with a clear inclination towards al-Shafi'i's fiqh on some questions. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16} This is al-Dhahabi's statement, which he qualifies with "so it is said", adding that he was one of those who reached the rank of the mujtahid imams. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 18} Ahmad Zaki Yamani, in his foreword, holds that he often departed from the imams of his own Maliki school when he found the truth with others. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 6}

## His bond with Ibn Hazm
A close friendship grew between him and the Zahiri [[person/ibn-hazm|Ibn Hazm]] (d. 456 AH), unaffected by their difference of school; Ibn Hazm quotes him in *al-Muhalla*, although he died before him. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16} In his epistle on the merits of al-Andalus, written while Abu 'Umar was alive and "not yet of old age", Ibn Hazm praised his Tamhid and Istidhkar, his *al-Kafi* on Maliki fiqh in fifteen books, and his book on the Companions. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16}

## What scholars said of him
- Al-Humaydi (d. 488 AH): a jurist, a prolific hadith master, learned in the Qur'an readings, in juristic disagreement and in the sciences of hadith and narrators, early in his hearing and with many teachers. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16}
- Abu 'Ali al-Jayyani (d. 498 AH): he was not below Qasim b. Muhammad and Ahmad b. Khalid al-Jabbab; he excelled in hadith beyond all earlier men of al-Andalus and had wide learning in genealogy and history. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16-17}
- Ibn Bashkuwal transmits, with his chain, the words of [[person/al-baji-abu-al-walid-sulayman|Abu al-Walid al-Baji]]: "There was none like Abu 'Umar b. 'Abd al-Barr in al-Andalus in hadith", and "Abu 'Umar is the greatest memoriser of the people of the Maghrib". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 17} {cite:wafayat-ayan p. 66-67}
- Qadi 'Iyad: the shaykh of the scholars of al-Andalus and its greatest hadith scholar in his time. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 17}
- Al-Dhahabi: "the hafiz of the Maghrib, shaykh al-Islam", describing him as devout, trustworthy, exact and deeply learned, a man of Sunna and following. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 18}

## Works
Among his compilations are *al-Tamhid li-ma fi al-Muwatta' min al-Ma'ani wa-al-Asanid* in seventy parts; *al-Istidhkar*, which Ibn Hazm calls the abridgement of the Tamhid; [[work/al-kafi-ibn-abd-al-barr|al-Kafi fi al-Fiqh 'ala Madhhab Malik wa-Ashabih]]; his book on the Companions, [[work/al-istiab|al-Isti'ab]]; and *al-Intiqa' fi Fadl al-Thalatha al-Fuqaha'*, into which he moved his biography of Malik from the Tamhid. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16} {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 42} In the Tamhid he says he devoted a full, sufficient book to answering those who reject single reports. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 193}

## Moves within al-Andalus and death
He left Cordoba and spent some time in the west of al-Andalus, then moved east and lived in [[place/dania|Denia]], [[place/valencia|Valencia]] and [[place/shatiba|Xàtiva]]. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 18} He died in Xàtiva at the end of Rabi' al-Akhir 463 AH and was buried on the Friday at the afternoon prayer; the editor equates this with 4 March 1071 CE, giving him ninety-five lunar years. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 18} {cite:wafayat-ayan p. 71}
