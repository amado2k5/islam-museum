Mir Sayyid Jalaluddin Husayn al-Naqvi al-Bukhari, known as Makhdum-i Jahaniyan Jahangasht, was a Suhrawardi shaykh born at Uch in the Punjab on 14 Sha'ban 707 AH; his articles render the day variously as 7, 8 and 9 February 1308 while agreeing on the year and its Hijri counterpart.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-ur}{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-en}{cite:khan-constructing-islam-on-the-indus-2016} His father, Ahmad Kabir, was the youngest son and chosen successor of Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari, so Jahangasht was born into the headship of Uch's line of the Suhrawardi order.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-en}

He received the khirqa of khilafa first from his father Ahmad Kabir and a second from Shaykh Ruknuddin, known as Shah Rukn-e-Alam; Khan's scholarly monograph likewise reports that Shah Rukn-e-Alam initiated him into the Suhrawardi order, making him head of the order's Uch line in the fourteenth century.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-ur}{cite:khan-constructing-islam-on-the-indus-2016} The Urdu article opens by calling him the most prominent figure of the Suhrawardi silsila of his age.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-ur}

His laqab Jahangasht, "world-traveller", came from his journeys: he travelled through Kazerun, Egypt, Syria-Palestine, Mesopotamia, Balkh, Bukhara and Khurasan, and is said to have performed the Hajj thirty-six times.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-en} Sultan Muhammad ibn Tughluq appointed him Shaykh al-Islam and granted him forty khanqahs in Siwastan, but he left the office for the Hajj; he later became close to Firuz Shah Tughluq, visiting Delhi periodically, accompanying the sultan on his Thatta campaign and exerting a major influence on Firuz's religious policies.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-en} At Pandua in Bengal he led the funeral prayer of the court scholar Alaul Haq Pandwi, and his banner is still carried in the urs processions of Akhi Siraj Bengali.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-en}

He died at Uch; the Urdu article gives 10 Dhu al-Hijja 785 AH, 3 February 1384, his death following the Eid al-Adha prayer, and the English article and Wikidata agree on 1384; his tomb at Uch still stands.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-ur}{cite:wikidata-jahaniyan-jahangasht} His descendants, surnamed Naqvi Bukhari, spread from Uch to Jalalpur Pirwala, Depalpur, Karmanwala and Tando Jahania and across the subcontinent.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-en}
