## Overview
Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Hamdan. The caliph gave him the title "Sayf al-Dawla" in the year 330 AH, when he found him and his brother Nasir al-Dawla at Tikrit {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 609-610}.

## Some of his battles
He fought al-Buraydi near al-Mada'in and defeated him, then drove on to Wasit, and al-Buraydi fled to Basra. The commanders in Wasit rose against him, and he fled into the desert intending to reach Baghdad {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 609-610}.

al-Mas'udi reports that the caliph al-Muttaqi wrote to him and to his brother Nasir al-Dawla, asking them to come to his aid {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 692}.

According to Ibn al-Jawzi, in the year 343 AH he defeated the Domestikos, commander of the Byzantines, and killed many of his companions {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 668}.

According to Ibn al-Athir, in the year 351 AH the Byzantines entered Aleppo before he knew of them. He went out against them, most of his companions were killed, and the Domestikos took his house {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 400}.

## His death
He died in Safar in the year 356 AH, aged about fifty-some years, of paralysis (falaj), though it is also said of difficulty in urination {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 28-29}.

Ibn al-Imad reports from al-Tha'alibi's "al-Yatima" that at his door gathered more poets than had gathered at the door of any king since the caliphs, and that he himself was a poet. He was succeeded by his son Sa'd al-Dawla {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 28-29}.
