## Summary
Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Ash'ari, Abu Ja'far, known as Hamdis al-Qattan — said to be of the progeny of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari — a Maliki jurist among the companions of Suhnun. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16246} The text describes him as a sea of merit and a model of goodness, firm in the doctrines of the people of the Sunna, thoroughly pious, trustworthy and keeping his distance from the people of whims and from rulers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16246} He travelled and met Abu Mus'ab and others in Medina, and the companions of Ibn al-Qasim, Ibn Wahb and Ashhab in Egypt; a group transmitted from him, among them Ibn al-Labbad and al-Abyani. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16246} The encyclopedia dates him 230–289 AH. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16246}
