Prem Nath Bazaz (13 July 1905 – 1984) was a Kashmiri Pandit politician, journalist and historian. Born in Srinagar to a Kashmiri Hindu family,{cite:wikipedia-prem-nath-bazaz-en} he was a central figure in the making of modern, secular Kashmiri politics: with Sheikh Abdullah he founded the weekly Hamdard in 1935, and through it pressed for a politics open to all Kashmiris regardless of religion.{cite:wikipedia-prem-nath-bazaz-en}{cite:kashmir-sentinel-bazaz}

He resigned from the National Conference in 1940 as he came to see it as communal and authoritarian, and went on to found the Kashmir Socialist Party and to lead the Kisan Mazdoor Conference.{cite:kashmir-sentinel-bazaz}{cite:wikipedia-prem-nath-bazaz-en} His books include The History of Struggle for Freedom in Kashmir (1954) and Democracy Through Intimidation and Terror (1978).{cite:wikipedia-prem-nath-bazaz-en} A committed secularist, he was treated as a renegade by many in his own Pandit community, and opposed by the Muslim Conference side as well.{cite:kashmir-sentinel-bazaz} He died in 1984.{cite:wikidata-prem-nath-bazaz}
