## Summary
Abu Ayyub, client of the Banu Hashim, kept company with wise worshippers and learned from them the time of return and the hereafter. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 488 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], on page 137. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/137}

## Sayings on the world and on toil
Abu Bakr ibn Ubayd relates from Abu Ayyub a saying of certain men: whoever looks at the world with the eye of admonition, the obliteration of his heart's sight is in proportion to that heedlessness. The next sentence is unclear in the scan, and the passage the edition marks in brackets as an addition from another manuscript is not rendered here. He used to say: beware of preferring ease and leaning toward comfort, for toil is of two kinds: painful thinking, and the labour of the limbs. The learned of this world and the scholars of the hereafter have both spent themselves in toil alongside ease, so do not step aside from either group; the group that deserves to be followed is the one whose deeds are for the hereafter. Those who sought what is with their Lord gave themselves to persistent painful thinking and to heavy labour of the body. If you seek their way, gather your concern so that your mind is present and roams the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. The heart's structure is one that does not stop it from fighting its enemy, and the enemy is not unable to fight it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/137}
