## Summary
Aichatou Mindaoudou is a veteran Nigerien diplomat who combined the highest national diplomatic office — Minister of Foreign Affairs of Niger twice, in two different decades — with leading UN peacekeeping missions in Africa's most inflamed theatres: Darfur and Côte d'Ivoire. She is among the most prominent African women diplomats of her generation. {cite:wikipedia-aichatou-mindaoudou}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1959 in Dogondoutchi, Niger, studied law and pursued diplomatic training, entering the foreign service early and climbing its ranks over three decades. {cite:wikipedia-aichatou-mindaoudou}

## National diplomacy
She was appointed Minister of Foreign Affairs and African Integration of Niger for the first time (1999–2001), then served as Minister of Planning (2003) before being recalled as foreign minister (2011–2016) under President Mahamadou Issoufou, leading the country's diplomacy through the Sahel crises, the Mali intervention and the Libyan neighbourhood — the longest modern tenure of a Nigerien foreign minister. {cite:wikipedia-aichatou-mindaoudou} {cite:muslim500-aichatou-mindaoudou}

## At the United Nations
Between her ministerial terms she served as a UN envoy: appointed in 2009 joint special representative of the Secretary-General in the African Union–United Nations Hybrid Operation in Darfur (UNAMID), heading the largest active peacekeeping operation of the time, then special representative and head of the UN Operation in Côte d'Ivoire (UNOCI) (2011–2013) during the post-election crisis and stabilisation — among the few women to have led two major peacekeeping missions. {cite:wikipedia-aichatou-mindaoudou}

## Recognition
She holds senior Nigerien national decorations and UN and regional commendations, and appears in rankings of influential African diplomatic figures. {cite:wikipedia-aichatou-mindaoudou}

## Legacy
Her career reads as a model of African diplomacy in multilateral institutions: a woman from a majority-Muslim Sahel country who led her country's foreign service across eighteen broken years and between them commanded the continent's largest UN peace missions — a reference for a new African generation in crisis leadership. {cite:wikipedia-aichatou-mindaoudou}
