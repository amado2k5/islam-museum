## Summary
Faisal Kutty is a Canadian lawyer, legal scholar and newspaper columnist whose work sits where Islamic law, international human rights and the position of Muslims in North America meet. He co-founded the Toronto law firm KSM Law and has taught law at universities in the United States. {cite:wikipedia-faisal-kutty}

## Legal and academic work
Called to the Ontario bar, Kutty practised in human-rights, civil-liberties and international cases, including work on behalf of clients caught in post-9/11 security measures. As an academic he taught at American law schools — including Valparaiso University and Southwestern Law School — writing on Islamic jurisprudence, the compatibility questions between Islamic and international law, and the legal treatment of Muslim minorities. {cite:wikipedia-faisal-kutty}

## Public commentary
For years he has been a regular columnist in Canadian and international media — the Toronto Star among them — arguing on civil liberties, foreign policy and Muslim community affairs, and he has served on the boards of Muslim civil-society bodies. His public argument has generally paired defence of Muslim civil rights with criticism of illiberal readings inside Muslim communities themselves. {cite:muslim500-faisal-kutty}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as part of a generation of Muslim public lawyers in the West: figures who argue community cases in courtrooms and community questions in print, in the language of both the shari'a and the charter of rights. {cite:muslim500-faisal-kutty}
