## Overview
The imam Abu Zakariya Yahya al-Nawawi, a Sunni jurist and hadith scholar, whom Ibn Rajab describes as the imam jurist, ascetic and model. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 26}

## The Forty
He compiled "al-Arba'in" of comprehensive hadiths ([[work/arbaun-nawawi|the Forty Hadiths of al-Nawawi]]). He began from the twenty-six hadiths in the session of "comprehensive hadiths" of Ibn al-Salah, and added to them until they reached forty-two hadiths. It became well known, its memorising spread, and commentators took care of it. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 26}

## His commentary on Sahih Muslim
The data attribute to him the book "Sharh Sahih Muslim", which appears in the references of the book's editor. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents al-Nawawi as the model of God-granted acceptance: many wrote collections of forty hadiths, yet only his Forty spread through the world, and Riyad al-Salihin is hardly absent from a Muslim home beside the Qur'an {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:02:30}.
Of his life: born in Nawa in the Hawran in Muharram 631 AH, he moved to Damascus at nineteen and lived there until death; he says he attended twelve lessons daily under twelve shaykhs — two on al-Wasit, and lessons on al-Muhadhdhab, al-Luma', logic, language, morphology, legal theory, transmitters' names and theology — keeping this up seven years without interruption {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:05:30}.
He memorised the Six Books, the Muwatta' and the musnads of al-Shafi'i and Ahmad; when challenged on a citation from al-Wasit he answered 'I have studied it four hundred times'; and asked by Ibn Jama'a about sleep he said 'when sleep overcomes me I lean on the books a moment then awaken' — he slept upon his books in a room of the Rawahiyya school beside the Umayyad Mosque {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:08:00}.
Of his works: al-Majmu' (his commentary on al-Muhadhdhab) — Ibn Kathir said had it been completed it would have had no peer — Rawdat al-Talibin, Minhaj al-Talibin, Tahdhib al-Asma' wa-l-Lughat, the commentary on Sahih Muslim, Riyad al-Salihin, al-Adhkar, al-Tibyan fi Adab Hamalat al-Qur'an, Manaqib al-Shafi'i and the Forty {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:10:30}.
And when Ibn al-Attar ordered a thousand of his notebooks washed, he washed them with regret at heart — judging them lacking precision {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:12:00}.
Of his asceticism: he never combined two dishes, ate treacle, vinegar or oil with bread, meat once a month, one meal after the evening prayer and one drink at dawn; he says he stayed two years in the school without laying his side on the ground, living on its stipend {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:16:30}.
Abu Abdallah ibn Abi al-Fath the Hanbali saw him praying by a pillar in the dark Umayyad Mosque repeating 'stop them, for they are to be questioned' until dawn {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:19:00}.
His stand with al-Zahir Baybars: when the sultan asked the scholars to sign a levy of extra taxes, all signed except al-Nawawi, who said 'you may take from the people only after the treasury's funds are spent and you, your wives, mamluks and princes return your surplus to it'; the sultan ordered his salary cut and was told he had none — he lived on what his father sent {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:22:00}.
He became head of the Ashrafiyya hadith-house in Damascus until death, outshining his century's peers — Ibn al-Salah, Ibn Khallikan, Yaqut al-Hamawi, Abu Shamah and others {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:22:30}.
He notes al-Nawawi never married, commenting that balance and the Prophet's way are better, yet one who lacks a side does not lose all sides — he had the spiritualities with the knowledge {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep11 00:17:30}.

## His life in dates
Born in Muharram 631 (October 1233) at Nawa in the Hawran, Jawlan; brought to Damascus in 649/1251 to lodge at the Rawahiyya madrasa; made the hajj with his father in 651/1253; began writing around 655; headed the al-Ashrafiyya dar al-hadith from 665/1267 after Abu Shama's death; finished the Minhaj and Rawda in 669 and al-Tahrir fi alfaz al-Tanbih in 671; alone refused to sign the fatwa approving Sultan Baybars's exactions and was expelled from Damascus to Nawa; died in his father's house at Nawa in Rajab 676 (December 1277) {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1096} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 293-294} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 354-356}.
