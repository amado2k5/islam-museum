## Overview
Sinan Pasha (Koca Sinan Pasha) was an Ottoman vizier and commander by sea and land under Sultan [[person/selim-ii-uthmani|Selim II]], who led the campaigns that took Tunis and Yemen.

## The conquest of Tunis
Selim II ordered Sinan Pasha and his kapudan Qilij Ali to prepare for the final conquest of Tunis. The Ottoman fleet sailed under their command on 23 Muharram 982 AH (14 May 1574 CE), struck the Calabrian coast and Messina, then crossed the open sea; the Ottomans took La Goletta and besieged the city of Tunis until the Spaniards fled it. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.285}

## The Yemen campaign
When the Zaydi imam al-Mutahhar took Sana'a and defeated the Ottomans, the government resolved on a major expedition under Sinan Pasha, since Yemen was part of the strategy of closing the Red Sea to the Portuguese threat and shielding the Hijaz. Sinan Pasha reached Egypt and gathered the troops, then moved via Yanbu to Mecca, where its people received him. He stayed in Jazan, where the Arabs came seeking his authority, hurried to relieve the besieged Ottoman governor of Taiz and routed the Zaydis at Mount al-Aghbar, then prepared two expeditions to take Aden — one by sea under his brother Qurtoghli. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.286-287}
