Mustafa Cerić is a Bosnian scholar who led his country's Islamic Community through the war of the 1990s and its aftermath as Grand Mufti. He was born on 5 February 1952 in the village of Veliko Čajno, near Visoko, and educated at the Gazi Husrev-beg Madrasa in Sarajevo, from which he graduated in 1972, and at Al-Azhar in Cairo, where he graduated in 1978{cite:wikipedia-ceric-bs}{cite:wikidata-ceric}. After serving as imam of the White Mosque in Gračanica, he became imam at the Islamic Cultural Center of Greater Chicago in Northbrook in 1981 and took his PhD in Islamic studies at the University of Chicago, under Fazlur Rahman, with a dissertation on the theology of Abu Mansur al-Maturidi dated 24 September 1986; the book that grew from it, Roots of Synthetic Theology in Islam, appeared in Kuala Lumpur in 1995{cite:wikipedia-ceric-bs}{cite:wikipedia-ceric-en}.

Cerić returned to Yugoslavia as chief imam of the Zagreb mosque in 1987 and began lecturing at the Faculty of Islamic Theology in Sarajevo; elected head of the Islamic Community in Bosnia and Herzegovina on 28 April 1993, he held the Grand Muftiate until 19 November 2012{cite:wikipedia-ceric-en}. Asked in a published interview what schools Bosnian Islam follows, he answered in his own words: "Basically we have only Hanafi madhhab in Bosnia… Hanafi is the official madhhab. This is the madhhab in prayers and in our fatwas"{cite:islamskazajednica-ceric-interview-2012}. After office he co-founded the Bosniak Academy of Sciences and Arts (2011) and the World Bosniak Congress (2012), which he presides over; he was a signatory of A Common Word Between Us and You (2007), co-received the UNESCO Félix Houphouët-Boigny Peace Prize in 2003, stood unsuccessfully for the Bosniak seat of the state Presidency in 2014, and was granted Saudi citizenship by royal order in November 2021{cite:wikipedia-ceric-en}{cite:wikipedia-ceric-ar}.
