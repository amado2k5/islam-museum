## Summary
Lahiq ibn Humayd, known as Abu Yajliz. After the killing of Qutayba ibn Muslim, Waki' ibn Abi Sud wrote to him with his patent of appointment over [[place/marw|Marw]]; he accepted it and the people were pleased with him, as al-Baladhuri records. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 597}

## Details
In his account of Qutayba ibn Muslim's death, al-Baladhuri relates that the people set about stripping the Bahilis, which Waki' forbade; Waki' then wrote to Abu Yajliz Lahiq ibn Humayd with his appointment over Marw, and Lahiq accepted it and the people approved {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 597}. Qutayba was fifty-five years old when killed. Having seized control of Khurasan, Waki' ibn Abi Sud was considered by Sulayman for its governorship, but he was told that Waki' was raised by sedition and pulled down by the multitude, and that he had the roughness of a Bedouin {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 597}. Waki' held on for nine months until Yazid ibn al-Muhallab arrived from Iraq; Sulayman wrote to him appointing him over Khurasan, and Yazid sent his son Mukhallad ahead, who called Waki' to account and jailed him, saying "Render God's wealth," to which Waki' answered, "Was I ever God's treasurer?" {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 597}
