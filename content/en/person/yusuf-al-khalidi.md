Yusuf Diya al-Din al-Khalidi (1842–1906) was an Ottoman Palestinian politician and scholar from Jerusalem who served several terms as the city's mayor and represented it in the first Ottoman parliament.{cite:wikipedia-yousef-al-khalidi-en}{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-khalidi-ar} Educated in Jerusalem, Malta and Istanbul, he taught Semitic languages in Vienna and compiled the first Kurdish–Arabic dictionary.{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-khalidi-ar}

In March 1899 al-Khalidi wrote, through the chief rabbi of France, to Theodor Herzl, warning that Palestine was already inhabited and that the Zionist project would endanger the relations of Muslims, Christians and Jews; Herzl's reply is the only letter he is known to have sent to an Arab leader.{cite:wikipedia-yousef-al-khalidi-en}{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-khalidi-ar} He died at Istanbul on 25 January 1906.{cite:wikipedia-yousef-al-khalidi-en}{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-khalidi-ar}
