## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] narrates from Sahih Muslim, from the hadith of Ubada ibn al-Walid ibn Ubada ibn al-Samit, that he and his father went out to seek knowledge from this clan of the Ansar before they passed away, and met Abu al-Yusr, the Companion of the Messenger of God, with his young slave, who carried a bundle of written sheets. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 01:07:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 01:08:00} In his telling Abu al-Yusr was owed a debt by a man of the Banu Haram; when he came to claim it the man hid, his small son showed where he was, and the man said he had nothing to pay with and had feared to lie to a Companion of the Messenger of God; so Abu al-Yusr erased the debt, citing the hadith that whoever grants respite to one in hardship or remits his debt, God will shade him in His shade on the day when there is no shade but His. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 01:09:00} Al-Heweny places the story in his discussion of the Companions' obedience to the Prophet's word once it reached them, and pairs it with the story of Ka'b ibn Malik and Ibn Abi Hadrad, when the Prophet signalled to Ka'b to remit half his debt and he did. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 01:13:30}
