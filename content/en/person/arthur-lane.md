## Summary
Arthur Lane, of the Victoria and Albert Museum in London, wrote three books in the Faber Monographs on Pottery and Porcelain: *Early Islamic Pottery*, *French Faience* and *Greek Pottery*. {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 3} {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 6}

## His approach to Islamic pottery
- He relies on dated excavations such as Samarra, occupied by the caliphs between 856 and 885 and then abandoned, so that its sherds are closely dated. {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 26}
- He warns that dates on *minai* wares are often forged and that overglaze colours are skilfully imitated by forgers. {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 61}
- He uses the potters' own inscriptions, such as "the work of Ibrahim … in Misr" on the Fatimid elephant bowl and the signatures of "Muslim" and "Sa'd". {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 36} {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 37}
- He cites the technical treatise of Abu al-Qasim of Kashan, written at Tabriz in 1301, and thinks he may himself have been a potter of the family that made the Kashan lustre tiles. {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 58} {cite:lane-early-islamic-pottery-1947 p. 60}
