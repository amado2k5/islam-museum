## Overview
Al-Husayn ibn Mansur al-Hallaj, a figure on whom the sources differ widely. This page sets out what the listed sources say, and each judgement is attributed to its source.

## His death
Al-Tabari says that al-Muqtadir ordered him killed and burned in 309 AH, and that he was given a thousand lashes and had his hands and feet cut off. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.11 p.79} Ibn al-Jawzi says that his head was taken out of the palace on the last day of Dhu al-Qa'da 309 AH to be carried to Khurasan. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.459}

## What al-Suyuti says
Al-Suyuti says he was brought into Baghdad on a camel and crucified alive, with a proclamation that he was one of the missionaries of the Qaramita, and then he was imprisoned until he was killed on the fatwa of the judge Abu Umar and the jurists that his blood was lawful. He says it was spread about him that he claimed divinity and held the doctrine of incarnation. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.588}

## What Ibn al-Nadim says
Ibn al-Nadim says that people differed about his homeland, some saying he was from Nishapur, some from Marw, some from Taliqan, and others from al-Rayy or the mountains. He relates from the handwriting of Tayfur and others negative descriptions of him, among them that he claimed to know every science. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.675} He relates that his affair appeared in 279 AH. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.270}

## What Ibn al-Athir says
Ibn al-Athir says that he showed asceticism, Sufism and miracles, and that people differed about him as they differed about Christ. Some said he held incarnation, some considered him a saint, and some a conjuror. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.93} He says he stayed for years in Mecca in the Hijr without sheltering under any roof. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.94}

The Sufi view of him and the modern academic view are not set out here, and need additional sources.

## Al-Hallaj in Ibn al-Jawzi's Talbis Iblis
In the Sufi chapter of [[work/talbis-iblis|Talbis Iblis]], [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] gives reports about al-Hallaj. One, through al-Sulami, is from 'Amr ibn 'Uthman al-Makki, who was walking with him in the lanes of Mecca reciting the Qur'an when al-Hallaj said he could say the like of it, so 'Amr left him; 'Amr ibn 'Uthman used to curse him for this. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 174} Another tells of a man at Dinawar found carrying a letter of al-Hallaj headed "From the Most Merciful, the Compassionate, to so-and-so son of so-and-so"; brought to Baghdad, al-Hallaj acknowledged the handwriting as his and said he did not claim lordship, but that this was what they called "the essence of union" ('ayn al-jam'): the writer is God and the hand is an instrument. Asked who was with him, he named Ibn 'Ata', Abu Muhammad al-Jurayri and al-Shibli: al-Jurayri said this was unbelief for which its speaker is killed, [[person/al-shibli|al-Shibli]] said its speaker should be restrained, and Ibn 'Ata' held al-Hallaj's view. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 174} {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 175}

He reports that Ibn Khafif, asked about verses attributed to al-Hallaj on his "humanity" (nasut) appearing through the secret of his "divinity" (lahut), cursed their author, and that Ibn Furak said: if this was his belief he was an unbeliever, though the words may have been falsely put in his mouth. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 175} Ibn al-Jawzi then says: "The scholars of the age agreed that al-Hallaj's blood was lawful"; the first to say so was Abu 'Umar the judge, the scholars agreed with him, and only Abu al-'Abbas ibn Surayj held back, saying "I do not know what he is saying." {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 175} He notes that a group of Sufis took his side, quotes al-Nasrabadi's saying that if there was a true monotheist after the prophets and the truthful it was al-Hallaj, says that most storytellers and Sufis of his own day held this view, and says that he himself compiled a book of al-Hallaj's reports setting out "his tricks and deceptions". {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 176} In chapter 11 he returns to what he reports as al-Hallaj's "tricks" and calls his belief "an ugly belief". {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 388} {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 389} All these are the judgements of Ibn al-Jawzi and those he quotes.
