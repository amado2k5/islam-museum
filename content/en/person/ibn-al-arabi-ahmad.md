## Summary
Abu Sa'id Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ziyad ibn Bishr ibn Dirham, known as Ibn al-A'rabi, of the tribe of Anaza, from Basra and resident in Mecca. The Mawsu'at A'lam al-Ulama' wa-l-Udaba' describes him as a hadith imam and hafiz, historian, ascetic and Sufi, learned in hadith and the generations of scholars. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.153} [[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]] gives him an entry in [[work/tarikh-dimashq|Tarikh Dimashq]] because he heard hadith in Damascus. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 353}

## Teachers and students
In Damascus he heard Abd al-Samad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Yazid, Muhammad ibn al-Abbas ibn al-Dirafs, Ahmad ibn Anas ibn Malik, Ibrahim ibn Duhaym and others; in Ramla Muhammad ibn Isma al-Utrush; in Egypt Ahmad ibn Hammad Zughba and others; and he narrated from [[person/al-hasan-ibn-muhammad-al-zafarani|al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Za'farani]], Muhammad ibn Ishaq al-Saghani, Sa'dan ibn Nasr, Abbas al-Duri and very many more. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 353} Abu Abd Allah [[person/ibn-khafif|Muhammad ibn Khafif of Shiraz]], Abu Bakr ibn al-Muqri', Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ishaq ibn Manda, Abu Muhammad Abd al-Rahman ibn Umar ibn al-Nahhas, Ibn Jumay' of Sidon and others narrated from him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 354} Ibn Asakir records that he dictated hadith in Mecca in 337 AH. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 354}

## Shaykh of the Sanctuary
Ibn Asakir quotes [[person/al-sulami|Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami]]: of Basran origin, he lived in Mecca and died there, and was the shaykh of the Sanctuary in his day; he kept company with [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]], [[person/amr-ibn-uthman-al-makki|Amr al-Makki]] and others, wrote books for the Sufis, among them on the nobility of poverty, wrote down and narrated much hadith, and was trustworthy. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 355} Ahmad ibn Ata said: Abu Sa'id ibn al-A'rabi studied law and inclined to the school of the hadith scholars and the Zahiris. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 355} [[person/al-qushayri-abu-l-qasim|Al-Qushayri]] says he lived beside the Sanctuary and kept company with al-Junayd, Amr ibn Uthman al-Makki and [[person/abu-al-husayn-al-nuri|al-Nuri]]. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 355} Al-Khalili said: trustworthy; he heard al-Hasan ibn al-Sabbah, al-Mukharrimi, Sa'dan, al-Duri and other Baghdadi shaykhs; trustworthy by agreement, praised by every one of his colleagues who met him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 355} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 356}

## Sayings
Ibn Asakir relates his saying: the greatest loser of all is the one who shows people his good deeds and openly does wrong before the One who is nearer to him than his jugular vein — an allusion to [[passage/quran-50-16|Surat Qaf 16]]. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 355} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 356} Al-Astarabadhi heard him say in his mosque in Mecca in 333 AH: God made His blessing the means of knowing Him, His grace the means of obeying Him, His protection the means of avoiding disobedience, His mercy the means of repentance, and repentance the means of His forgiveness and of nearness to Him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 356} Asked about the character of the poor (the Sufis), he said: calm in poverty, unease in plenty, intimacy with cares and estrangement in times of joy. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 356}

## Death
The year of his death in Mecca is given differently: al-Sulami said 341 or 340, al-Qushayri 341, and Ishaq ibn Ibrahim al-Harawi 340. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 355} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 356} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 357} The editor of Tarikh Dimashq quotes Siyar A'lam al-Nubala' that he was born some years after 240 and died in Mecca in Dhu al-Qa'da 340, aged ninety-four and some months. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 357} The Mawsu'a gives his birth as 246 AH and death as 340 AH. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.153}
