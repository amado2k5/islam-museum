## In «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'» by Abu Ishaq al-Heweny
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] relates that he visited his shaykh Hammad al-Ansari at his house in Madina, whose library filled a whole floor and held manuscripts, and that he had earlier given him his book «Badhl al-Ihsan». {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:16:30} When he visited a year later, al-Ansari told him that Shaykh Abd al-Qadir al-Sindi had been with him an hour before, had read him al-Heweny's criticism of him in the book, and wished to see him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:17:00} Al-Heweny had written that what al-Sindi said about Ibn Jurayj's concealment in chains was "obvious slips". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:15:30}

He went to al-Sindi's house, where al-Sindi read out that phrase to him and then asked his leave to read a reply he had written in fourteen pages. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:19:00} Al-Heweny says he was pouring with sweat; he listened to the reply, weighed it, and promised to publish it in the next edition with his own comments, as a matter of scholarly honesty; he says he learned more from the man's manners than from scholars, and vowed to use the gentlest wording in criticism even when he finds a gross error. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:22:30}
