# Muhammad al-Ifrani

Muhammad al-Ifrani (1669/1670–c. 1743), in full Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Ifrani al-Susi al-Marrakushi and called al-Saghir, was a Moroccan historian and biographer {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-en}. He was born at Marrakesh into the Ifran, a Shilha Berber tribe of the Draa River basin in the Sous region {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-en}. He began his studies in his hometown under scholars including Ahmad ibn Ali al-Madasi al-Marrakushi, then went to Fes and studied at al-Qarawiyyin under Ahmad ibn Abd al-Hayy al-Halabi and Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd al-Qadir al-Fasi {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-ar}. Finishing at Fes in 1130 AH/1717 CE, he returned to Marrakesh, taught Qur'an commentary, hadith and law, and served as imam and khatib of the Yusufi mosque {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-ar}. The Portuguese and Dutch texts add, only as a probability, a post at the court of the Alawite sultan Moulay Ismail {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-pt}.

His best-known work is the Nuzhat al-Hadi bi-Akhbar Muluk al-Qarn al-Hadi, the standard chronicle of the Saadian sultans — the dynasty of Ahmad al-Mansur — translated by Octave Houdas as a history of the Saadian dynasty in Morocco, 1511–1670 {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-fr}. His Safwat man Intashar biographies the saints of seventeenth-century Morocco; the Rawdat al-Ta'rif (also called al-Zill al-Warif) treats his contemporary, the Alawite sultan Moulay Ismail; the Durar al-Hijal treats the Seven Saints of Marrakesh; al-Mu'rib fi Akhbar al-Maghrib is a general history of Morocco to his own time; and al-Maslak al-Sahl comments on the muwashshah of the Andalusian poet Ibn Sahl {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-ar}.

He died at Marrakesh and was buried in the Rawda near the Ben Yusuf mosque, but the year of his death is the most disputed date of his life: the English article gives about 1742/1743; the Arabic article's body reports a range of 1138–1156 AH (1726–1744) while its lead gives about 1156 AH; the French gives 1744; the Portuguese runs 1743–1747; the Catalan text puts the death between 1743 and 1745 while its infobox gives 1747; and the Dutch text gives 1743 or 1745 while its lead gives 1747 {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-ar} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-nl} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-ifrani-ca}.
