## Summary
Ahmed Aboutaleb (born 29 August 1961) is a Dutch politician of Moroccan origin who has served as mayor of Rotterdam since January 2009 — the first Muslim to lead one of Europe's largest cities — and won the World Mayor Prize in 2021. A member of the Labour Party (PvdA), he built his career across journalism, local administration and national government before becoming the longest-serving mayor in Rotterdam's modern history. {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-aboutaleb-en} {cite:themuslim500-ahmed-aboutaleb}

## Early life and move to the Netherlands
Aboutaleb was born in Beni Sidel, a small village in the Rif region of northern Morocco near Nador, the son of an imam. He moved to the Netherlands in 1976 as a teenager and studied electrical engineering and telecommunications, graduating in 1987 with a qualification in telecommunications engineering. Dutch was, by his own account, his fourth language; he has described learning it while working and studying, a path that later made him a pointed speaker on the duties and opportunities of integration. {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-aboutaleb-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-aboutaleb-ar}

## Journalism and civil service
Before entering elected politics, Aboutaleb worked as a reporter for Veronica, NOS-radio and RTL Nieuws, and held communications posts in the Ministry of Health, Welfare and Sport. In 1998 he became director of the Forum Institute for Multicultural Development in Utrecht, and in 2002 he was appointed director of the Social, Economic and Cultural Development sector of the municipality of Amsterdam. He also briefly lectured at the Free University of Amsterdam and served as a research officer at the Cordaid foundation. {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-aboutaleb-en}

## National politics
From 2004 to 2007 Aboutaleb served as alderman for work and income, education and diversity in Amsterdam under mayor Job Cohen. In February 2007 he entered national government as State Secretary for Social Affairs and Employment in the fourth Balkenende cabinet. When he accepted the post, the question of his dual Dutch–Moroccan nationality drew public comment; he kept both passports, arguing that a nationality is part of one's identity. {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-aboutaleb-en}

## Mayor of Rotterdam
In December 2008 Aboutaleb was nominated mayor of Rotterdam and took office on 5 January 2009. He has been reappointed for successive terms, remaining in office into his third term. His tenure has been marked by a direct, sometimes blunt public style: after the Charlie Hebdo attack in Paris in 2015 he told a television interviewer that Muslims who object to Western freedoms should, in his phrase, pack their bags — a remark widely reported and debated. He has also pressed for harder policing of drug crime in the port city while advocating interfaith calm after attacks and international crises. In 2021 the City Mayors Foundation awarded him the World Mayor Prize, citing his calm leadership during the COVID-19 pandemic and his work on equality. {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-aboutaleb-en} {cite:themuslim500-ahmed-aboutaleb}

## Faith and public standing
Aboutaleb is reported as a practising Muslim and the first Muslim mayor of a major European city, a description carried by the sources read for this record; none of them names a religious school for him, and this record records none. His public framing of Islam and citizenship — defending the place of Muslims in European society while demanding unqualified loyalty to its civic order — has made him a reference point in European debates on integration, praised by supporters as bridge-building and criticised by others as too hard on his own community. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-aboutaleb-en} {cite:themuslim500-ahmed-aboutaleb}
