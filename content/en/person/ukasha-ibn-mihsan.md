## Overview
[[person/ukasha-ibn-mihsan|Ukasha ibn Mihsan al-Asadi]], an ally of the Banu Abd Shams among the Companions, who was killed at Buzakha in the caliphate of Abu Bakr {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.133}.

## Buzakha
[[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] sent him ahead to Buzakha, together with [[person/thabit-ibn-aqram|Thabit ibn Aqram al-Balawi]], an ally of the Ansar. Hubal ibn Khuwaylid met them and killed them both. Then [[person/tulayha-ibn-khuwaylid|Tulayha ibn Khuwaylid]] came out, with his brother Salama, and they met Ukasha and Thabit and killed them. Tulayha composed his well-known verse about them {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.133}.

Ukasha took part in [[event/battle-of-buzakha|the battle of Buzakha]]. His year of death is derived from the same report and is approximate, around 11 AH.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the fourth episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] presents Ukasha ibn Mihsan as one of the heroes among the Companions of the [[group/muhajirun|Muhajirun]], to whom the Prophet gave the glad tidings that he would enter Paradise without punishment or reckoning {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:01:00}. He says Ukasha was among the young men who were early to Islam, bore persecution in Mecca and then emigrated to Medina when the Prophet permitted the emigration {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:02:00}, and that he took part in the campaigns with the Prophet and died in Abu Bakr's time fighting the apostates {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:01:30}; this agrees with Futuh al-Buldan's report that he was killed at Buzakha in the caliphate of Abu Bakr {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.133}. He describes [[group/ansar|the Ansar]] as generous and self-denying people who opened their hearts to the emigrants before their homes and their wealth {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:02:30}.

He says the Prophet saw in Ukasha the signs of excellence and leadership and put him in command of a raiding party of forty men to a place called al-Ghamr {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:03:00}; the polytheists there fled when they learned he was coming, he sent out scouts who found where their livestock was, took it, and returned to Medina with two hundred camels without any of his men being harmed {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:03:30}. He reports from [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]]'s al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya that Ukasha's sword broke at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and the Prophet gave him a stick of firewood which, when he brandished it, became a strong white sword in his hand, and he fought with it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:04:00}; he says the sword was called al-'Awn, and counts it a miracle granted to Ukasha and a sign of the Prophet {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:05:00}.

He relates from Ahmad, al-Bukhari and Muslim, through [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]], the hadith of the nations shown to the Prophet: a prophet with a small band, a prophet with one or two men, a prophet with no one, then a great multitude that he was told was the community of [[person/musa|Moses]], then a multitude filling the horizon that he was told was his own, with seventy thousand among them who would enter Paradise without reckoning or punishment; he places this during [[event/isra-and-miraj|the Night Journey and Ascension]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:05:30}. He comments that some prophets preached for thirty years and only one person believed, and yet they did not fall short, since the task of a prophet or a preacher is to remind and guidance is in God's hand {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:06:00}. He relates that the Companions debated who the seventy thousand were, some saying the Companions and some those born in Islam who had never associated anything with God, until the Prophet came out and told them they are those who do not ask others for ruqya, do not seek cautery and rely on their Lord; he mentions a version that adds that they do not perform ruqya for others and do not take ill omens {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:07:00}. Then Ukasha ibn Mihsan stood and asked the Prophet to pray that God make him one of them, and the Prophet told him he was one of them; another man stood and asked the same, and was told that Ukasha had beaten him to it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:08:00}.

He cites a hadith that he calls agreed upon, to the effect that whoever is closely examined at the reckoning is punished, concluding that the reckoning itself is a punishment {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:09:30}, and passes on the advice of Sheikh [[person/saleh-al-maghamsi|al-Maghamsi]], imam of the Quba mosque, that whoever asks God for Paradise should ask for it without punishment or reckoning {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:09:30}. He relates from Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Hibban, graded sound by al-Albani in al-Jami' as he says, through [[person/abu-umama-al-bahili|Abu Umama]], that God promised the Prophet that seventy thousand of his community would enter Paradise without reckoning or punishment, with every thousand of them another seventy thousand, and three handfuls of his Lord's handfuls {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:10:30}; only God, he adds, knows the measure of the Lord's handful {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:11:00}. He relates from Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, al-Hakim and al-Tabarani, which he calls sound, through Sulayman ibn Burayda from his father, that the people of Paradise are a hundred and twenty rows, eighty of them from this community and forty from all the other nations, and concludes that two-thirds of the people of Paradise are from Muhammad's community {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:12:00}.

He then turns to the qualities of the seventy thousand. He reports [[person/muhammad-ibn-salih-al-uthaymin|Ibn Uthaymin]]'s view that the added phrase "they do not perform ruqya" is not authentic in the hadith, since treating the sick with ruqya is a kindness and the Prophet used to do it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:13:30}, and others who accept it and take it to mean performing ruqya only with what has come in the Qur'an and the Sunna or with what contains nothing contrary to them, such as the two protective suras, the Throne Verse and the Prophet's own prayer for the sick {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:14:30}. He explains not asking for ruqya as a sign of complete trust, and notes the scholars' disagreement about one who seeks it out of necessity, because of magic or envy: there is no sin on him, but they differ over whether he loses the rank of the seventy thousand or whether only seeking it without cause is meant {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:16:00}. He explains avoiding cautery by its being a torment, citing a hadith that places healing in three things, honey, cupping and cautery, together with the Prophet's dislike of cautery, and the Arab saying that cautery is the last remedy {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:17:30}; and he holds that one with no remedy but cautery commits no sin, since the Prophet cauterised the arm vein of [[person/sad-ibn-muadh|Sa'd ibn Mu'adh]] when an arrow struck him and the bleeding would not stop {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:17:30}. He explains not taking [[concept/tatayyur-omens|omens]] as not believing in bad luck, attributing to Abu Dawud that taking omens is shirk {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:18:00}, and lists common forms of it, by months, days, numbers, sounds and colours, such as the crow's call, a ringing in the ear, a twitching eye, the number 13 and an "unlucky hour" on Friday, stating that Friday holds an hour when prayer is answered, not an hour of ill luck {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:18:30}. He states that Islam has no ill omens, only good hope, reports Abu Hurayra that the Prophet liked a good word, and relates that when a bird cried out in Ibn Abbas's company and a man said "good, good", Ibn Abbas said that it holds neither good nor harm {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:19:30}.

He defines [[concept/tawakkul|tawakkul]] as taking the means while entrusting the outcome to God: if the hoped-for thing comes, the credit is God's, and if not, one says it is God's decree {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:20:30}. He illustrates it with [[person/hajar|Hajar]], left by [[person/ibrahim|Abraham]] with her infant in the desert of Mecca: the food and water ran out, she ran seven times between Safa and Marwa, then she heard the sound of water, and there was [[person/jibril|Gabriel]], who had struck the ground with the tip of his wing so that Zamzam gushed out, and who told her that God does not abandon His people and that Abraham would build a House for God here with his son {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:21:00}; she could have contented herself with prayer, he says, but she took the means, to teach the community trust in God {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:22:00}. He gives a second example in [[person/maryam|Mary]], who after giving birth was told, as Surat Maryam tells, to shake the trunk of the palm despite her weakness; he says the palm was dry and turned green and bore fruit within seconds, and a spring flowed beneath her feet, and he draws the lesson that a servant must make the effort {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:23:00}. He then returns to Ukasha, saying that he fought in the campaigns with the Prophet and that his longing for Paradise grew after the Prophet's death; at this point, at the twenty-fourth minute, the available recording breaks off, so the rest of his account is not known {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep04 00:24:00}.
