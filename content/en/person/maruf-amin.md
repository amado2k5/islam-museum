## Summary
Ma'ruf Amin is an Indonesian Shafi'i scholar and politician, vice-president of Indonesia from 2019 to 2024 alongside President Joko Widodo — the oldest person to hold that office — and before that the supreme leader (rais aam) of Nahdlatul Ulama and chairman of the Indonesian Ulema Council. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-amin}

## Life and formation
He was born on 11 March 1943 in Tangerang, Banten, and educated in the pesantren world of Banten and Jakarta before a long career inside Nahdlatul Ulama — preacher, legislator in Jakarta's assembly, and eventually rais aam, the organisation's highest religious office, from 2015. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-amin}

## Scholar and mufti
As chairman of the Indonesian Ulema Council from 2015 he presided over its fatwas, including the contested rulings of the Ahok blasphemy period in 2016–17, and steered NU doctrine on the state and pluralism. His scholarship is in Shafi'i law; his public standing rests on the network of pesantren and kiai loyal to NU's traditionalist line. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-amin}

## Vice-presidency
Chosen by Joko Widodo in 2018 to balance the ticket with religious credentials, he served as vice-president 2019–2024, championing the halal economy and sharia finance and representing the government in religious affairs. Supporters credit him with bringing Muslim constituencies inside a pluralist coalition; critics argued the office was largely ceremonial and that his fatwa record sat awkwardly with pluralist rhetoric. The museum records both readings attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-maruf-amin}
