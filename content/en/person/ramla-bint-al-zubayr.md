## Summary
Ramla bint [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]], famous for the story of [[person/khalid-ibn-yazid-ibn-muawiya|Khalid ibn Yazid ibn Mu'awiya]] falling in love with her when he saw her circumambulating the House at the Hajj {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p. 465}.

## Abd al-Malik's suit
Khalid lagged behind to see Ramla and told [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] what was in him; Abd al-Malik said "I never thought desire could master one like you," and directed him to seek her hand for Khalid {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p. 465}. When it was mentioned to her she said "No, by God — or he divorces his wives," and he divorced two wives he had and she was joined to him {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p. 465}.

In Hajjaj's account, when Khalid ibn Yazid proposed to Ramla he rebuffed him, telling him the house of al-Zubayr had contended with your father for the caliphate; Khalid answered that Quraysh contends within itself {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p. 466}. Verses of Khalid's on her are transmitted, opening "Does Yazid's journey not pass every night" {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p. 466}.

## After Ibn al-Zubayr's killing
When Ibn al-Zubayr was killed, Khalid ibn Yazid made the Hajj and proposed to Ramla; in al-Tabari's account [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] himself proposed and she refused him, telling his envoy "I do not trust myself with one who killed my brother" {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p. 467}. The book also records the estrangement of [[person/sakina-bint-al-husayn|Sakina bint al-Husayn ibn Ali]] from her husband Abdullah ibn Uthman, whose mother is Ramla bint al-Zubayr, and her appeal to Abd al-Malik {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p. 467}.
