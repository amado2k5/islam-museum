## Summary
Nizam Yaquby is a Bahraini jurist counted among the most influential scholars in the modern Islamic-finance industry; joining traditional training in the Islamic sciences with university qualifications in economics, his opinion has become a reference for banks, indices and standards bodies across continents. {cite:muslim500-nizam-yaquby}

## Early life and education
Born in Bahrain in 1961, he received a traditional sharia education in Bahrain and then in Mecca — some institutional biographies add study in India and Morocco — under senior scholars. On the academic track he took a degree in economics and comparative religion at McGill University in Montreal, Canada, then a doctorate in Islamic law, which banking biographies variously attribute to the University of Wales in Britain and to Lahaye University in the Netherlands; the museum records the two accounts as given. {cite:muslim500-nizam-yaquby}

## Teaching and writing
He has taught tafsir, hadith and fiqh in Bahrain since 1976, and hundreds of his lessons have been recorded in Arabic and English; he has edited Arabic manuscripts, published research in both languages, and lectured as a visitor at universities including Harvard. {cite:muslim500-nizam-yaquby}

## Sharia supervision
He is among the most engaged scholars in the supervision of financial institutions: his memberships and chairmanships of sharia supervisory boards have exceeded seventy across Islamic banks and the Islamic arms of global banks such as HSBC, Lloyds, Barclays, BNP Paribas, Credit Agricole and Citigroup, and Gulf institutions including Bahrain Islamic Bank, Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank and Qatar Islamic Bank. He is a member of the sharia standards council of the Accounting and Auditing Organisation for Islamic Financial Institutions ⁦(AAOIFI)⁩, of the International Islamic Financial Market ⁦(IIFM)⁩ council, of the Dow Jones Islamic Market Index and of the Central Bank of Bahrain's sharia committee. Bloomberg has described him as the "gatekeeper" of the Islamic-finance market, and he is seen as a bridge between the Gulf and Southeast Asian schools of Islamic finance. {cite:muslim500-nizam-yaquby}

## Recognition and legacy
He has received Bahrain's Order of Merit and the Euromoney award for innovation in sharia supervision, and the Muslim 500 ranks him among influential Muslim figures; his role is widely read as a model of the traditional sharia scholar becoming a normative authority inside a modern global financial system. {cite:muslim500-nizam-yaquby}

## Place in the Islamic-finance movement
Yaquby's role belongs to a larger twentieth-century shift: the migration of the rules of transactions from the school and the study-circle to cross-continental financial institutions working by uniform standards. The sharia supervisory boards on which he sits examine contracts and financial products and certify their conformity with sharia rules, as industry specialists describe them, and he works at both the Gulf and international levels, chairing councils and giving opinions on instruments such as sukuk and structured products. His institutional reach — more than seventy positions by the cited accounts — makes him one of the contemporary jurists most directly involved in the market's actual decisions, which is what earned him the financial press's "gatekeeper" label. {cite:muslim500-nizam-yaquby}
