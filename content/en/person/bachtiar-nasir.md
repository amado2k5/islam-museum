## Summary
Bachtiar Nasir is an Indonesian preacher and scholar born in Jakarta on 26 June 1967, known for his examination of the sciences of the Qur'an and for leading the Ar-Rahman Qur'anic Learning Center, the AQL Islamic Center, in Jakarta. {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en}

He came to national prominence as leader of the GNPF-MUI movement in the protests of 2016 and 2017 against Jakarta's governor, and he often appeared on national television, including as a judge on the Hafidz Indonesia programme. {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en} {cite:reuters-bachtiar-nasir-2017}

## Early life and education
He completed his secondary education at Pondok Modern Darussalam Gontor in Ponorogo, East Java, and at Pondok Pesantren Daarul Huffazh in Bone, South Sulawesi. {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en}

He continued his studies at the Islamic University of Medina in Saudi Arabia. {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en}

## Offices and work
The article records that he leads the AQL Islamic Center and has served as secretary general of the Intellectual Assembly of Young Ulama Indonesia (MIUMI), as chairman of the Saudi Arabia alumni association in Indonesia and of the Islamic University of Medina alumni in Indonesia, and on the central executive of the Indonesian Ulema Council (MUI). {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en}

## The protests of 2016-17, and reported controversies
As leader of the National Movement of Fatwa Guards of the Indonesian Ulema Council (GNPF-MUI), he spearheaded the protest movement that began in November 2016 against then-Jakarta governor Basuki Tjahaja Purnama, who stood accused of blasphemy and who subsequently lost his re-election bid and was jailed. {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en}

In a 2017 interview with Reuters, he called the wealth of Indonesia's ethnic Chinese minority a problem of economic inequality to be addressed; critics responded that he was exploiting inequality to scapegoat the minority, an accusation of racism that this museum reports as the interview and its critics stated it, without adopting it. {cite:reuters-bachtiar-nasir-2017} {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en}

In May 2019, police named him a suspect in a money-laundering case involving the transfer of funds from the Justice for All Foundation (YKUS), as the Jakarta Globe reported and the article records; the same reporting says he did not comply with a third summons for questioning that month, having gone to Saudi Arabia. {cite:wikipedia-bachtiar-nasir-en}
