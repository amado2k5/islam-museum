Aisha Abdurrahman Bewley is an American-born translator of classical Islamic texts, born in the United States in 1948 and formed by the universities of two continents: a BA in French and an MA in Near Eastern Languages from the University of California, Berkeley, and fellowship study at the American University in Cairo{cite:wikipedia-aisha-bewley-en}{cite:wikipedia-aisha-bewley-ar}{cite:wikidata-aisha-bewley}. She converted to Islam in 1968 and married Shaykh Abdalhaqq Bewley, with whom she has translated many of the central books of the tradition and raised three children{cite:wikipedia-aisha-bewley-en}.

Her own translations began with The Darqawi Way, the letters of Mawlay al-Darqawi, published by Diwan Press in 1981, and Al-Muwatta of Imam Malik ibn Anas (1989); with her husband she produced The Noble Qur'an: A New Rendering of Its Meaning in English (1999), and she went on to translate Ibn Taymiyyah's The Madinan Way (2000), Ibn al-Arabi's treatise on bearing witness (2002) and the multi-volume Tafsir al-Qurtubi (from 2003){cite:wikipedia-aisha-bewley-en}{cite:dar-al-taqwa-islamic-will-bios-1995}. Her books of her own include The Islamic Will (1995, with Abdalhaqq Bewley and Ahmad Thomson), A Glossary of Islamic Terms (1998), Islam: The Empowering of Women (1999) and Muslim Women: A Biographical Dictionary (2004), and the WorldCat union catalogue credits her as author or translator of 73 works{cite:wikipedia-aisha-bewley-en}. A scholarly overview of women Qur'an translators describes the Bewleys jointly as Maliki in jurisprudence and as Sufis of the Habibiyya branch of the Shadhili-Darqawi order{cite:abhath-bewley-women-translators-2021}; the museum's record notes that single description without settling her own school on it.
