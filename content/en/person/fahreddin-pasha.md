# Fahreddin Pasha

## Summary
Ömer Fahreddin Türkkan (1868–1948), commonly known as Fahreddin Pasha or Fakhri Pasha, was a Turkish career officer who commanded the Ottoman forces at Medina and governed the city from 1916 to 1919. His determined defence through the siege of Medina earned him the name Defender of Medina, and his British opponents called him the Lion of the Desert and the Tiger of the Desert. {cite:wikipedia-fahreddin-pasha-en}

## The defence of Medina
He was born in 1868 at Rusçuk (Ruse), the son of Mehmed Nahid Bey and Fatma Adile Hanım; the family moved to Istanbul after the Russo-Turkish War. A career officer, he was serving as deputy commander of the Fourth Army when the First World War began — the Turkish article records that in that post he dealt with the resettlement of deported Armenians and suppressed the Armenian revolts at Urfa, Zeytun, Musa Dagh and Haçin. In 1916 he took command at Medina and governed the city. When the Arab Revolt cut the Hejaz railway, he held Medina from 10 June 1916 to 10 January 1919, refusing to surrender even after the Ottoman Empire signed the Armistice of Mudros, and gave up the city only when his own officers arrested him. The English article preserves, quoting Peters (1994), his words to his officers: "I am a Muhammadan. I am an Ottoman. I am the son of Bayer Bay. I am a soldier." {cite:wikipedia-fahreddin-pasha-en} {cite:wikipedia-fahreddin-pasha-tr}

## Kabul and his last years
After the war he joined the Turkish national movement, and from 1922 to 1926 he served as Turkey's ambassador to Kabul, Afghanistan. He retired from the army in 1936. On 22 November 1948 he suffered a heart attack on a train near Eskişehir and died; as he wished, he was buried at Aşiyan Cemetery in Istanbul. Wikidata agrees on the day of his death but places it at Istanbul, his burial city, rather than on the train — the articles are followed here. Wikidata also carries an unsupported day-precision birth value of 4 January 1868, against the year 1868 that both articles give. {cite:wikipedia-fahreddin-pasha-en} {cite:wikidata-fahreddin-pasha}

## The question of his faith
His defence of Medina was a military command held under orders, and the museum does not read service as attestation. What the sources give is this: neither article states a religion for him in text or infobox; the one faith-adjacent text is his own address quoted above, a general self-identification naming no school; and Wikidata gives Islam generically, with a single URL reference. Under the two-source rule his faith is therefore recorded as unknown, with the evidence preserved in his data record. {cite:wikipedia-fahreddin-pasha-en} {cite:wikipedia-fahreddin-pasha-tr} {cite:wikidata-fahreddin-pasha}
