## Overview
Abu Ali al-Husayn ibn Shu'ayb ibn Muhammad al-Marwazi al-Sinji was a Shafi'i jurist, the leading scholar of the people of Merv in his time {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 12 pp. 234-235}. Ibn Khallikan calls him one of the precise imams and the jurist of Merv in his age {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 135}. Al-Safadi gives his death as 430 AH, which corresponds to 1039 CE by the museum's conversion; Ibn Khallikan says only that he died in a year beyond thirty and four hundred, without giving it exactly {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 12 p. 235} {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 136}.

## Teachers
Al-Safadi says he studied fiqh under Abu Bakr al-Qaffal al-Marwazi and kept his company until he excelled, travelled, heard hadith, and held a view of his own in the school {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 12 p. 235}. Ibn Khallikan says he took fiqh in Khurasan from al-Qaffal al-Marwazi, together with al-Qadi Husayn and Shaykh Abu Muhammad al-Juwayni, father of Imam al-Haramayn {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 135}.

## Works
He wrote a commentary on the Furu' of Ibn al-Haddad al-Misri that, in al-Safadi's words, no one approached despite the many commentaries on it; his own teacher al-Qaffal and al-Qadi Abu al-Tayyib had also commented on it {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 12 p. 235}. He also wrote a large commentary on al-Talkhis of Abu al-Abbas ibn al-Qass, which is rare today, and a book al-Majmu', from which al-Ghazali quoted in al-Wasit; he was the first to combine the Iraqi and Khurasani methods {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 12 p. 235}.

## Standing
Ibn Khallikan reports a saying of his age that there were three imams in Khurasan: one prolific and exacting, one sparing and exacting, and one prolific but not exacting. The first was Abu Ali al-Sinji, the second Abu Muhammad al-Juwayni, and the third Nasir al-Marwazi {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 pp. 135-136}.

## His name
Ibn Khallikan explains that al-Sinji, spelled with kasra on the sin, sukun on the nun and then jim, refers to Sinj, a large village among the villages of Merv {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 136}.
