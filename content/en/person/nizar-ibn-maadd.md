## Overview
Nizar ibn [[person/maadd-ibn-adnan|Maadd]] ibn [[person/adnan|Adnan]]. The branches of Maadd spread out from his son Nizar. Some say Maadd had no other son. Nizar had four sons, from whom four great tribes branched: [[group/iyad|Iyad]], [[group/anmar-ibn-nizar|Anmar]], [[group/rabia-ibn-nizar|Rabi'a]] and [[group/mudar-ibn-nizar|Mudar]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/14-15}.

## His sons
Rabi'a and Mudar are the two whose branches grew many and whose clans spread widely {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20}. In the account of al-Rahiq, his mother was Mu'ana bint Jurhum ibn Jalhama al-Jurhumi, whom Maadd married when he returned to Mecca after the pressure of Nebuchadnezzar had been exposed {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.28} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/48}.

## Not covered here
The story of Nizar dividing his inheritance among his sons, as al-Mas'udi tells it, is recorded in the data of this entry.

## For young visitors
Nizar was the son of Maadd. His four sons became four great tribes, the most famous of which are Mudar and Rabi'a.
