## Overview
Abu al-Atahiya ibn Bishr is, in the book, the man who held the affairs of Sana'a in the days of [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s call. He corresponded with him and supplied troops and money in his wars; [[person/muhammad-ibn-abbad-al-tamimi|Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Abbad al-Tamimi]] and Umayya ibn Sudus ibn Shayban were over his chancery, so that his affairs issued only from their hands and were settled only on their counsel, and he inclined to al-Hadi and his school. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.17} His name is uncertain: on p. 17 the machine-read text gives "Abdullah ibn Yashir" (probably Bishr) and on p. 19 "Abu al-Atahiya ibn Bishr", to be checked on the page image.

## Inviting al-Hadi and the oath
Abu al-Atahiya invited al-Hadi to Sana'a and asked him to come, and al-Hadi set out from Sa'da with those who answered him from Hamdan, Khawlan, Banu al-Harith and others; Abu al-Atahiya showed nothing of what lay between them and made no declaration, then met him, handed over all he held and swore allegiance to him together with the Yemeni commanders and men with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.17}

## After the entry
He asked al-Hadi to imprison [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]] and Al Tarif; the two agreed on it, and he and Ibn Abbad were the men closest to al-Hadi, affairs issuing from their counsel and consultation. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.18} When al-Da'am and he asked that those held by al-Hadi be put to death, al-Hadi refused; and he left Shibam with Abu al-Atahiya ibn Bishr for Bayt Nud, a name the editor notes the geographer al-Hamdani spells with a zayn. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.19}

## In the account of Dhu al-Hijja 285 and Muharram 286 AH
On p. 110 the book introduces him as Abd Allah ibn Bishr, with the kunya Abu al-Atahiya, governor of Sanaa and its districts, whose vizier was Muhammad ibn Abi Abbad of Tamim; the two had written to al-Hadi, desired his justice and affirmed his imamate. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.110} He promised to supply horse and men, and then, seeing the soldiers' dislike of going to al-Hadi, sent his brother [[person/jarrah-ibn-bishr|Jarrah]] with fifty horsemen in the last days of Muharram 286 AH. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} The book's index refers "Abd Allah ibn Bishr" to "Abu al-Atahiya". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.450} See [[event/abu-al-atahiya-promises-horse-and-sends-his-brother-jarrah-285]].

## In the war at Sana'a, Sha'ban to Shawwal 288 AH, pp. 227 to 233
Al-Hadi sends him out on the evening of Friday night to Ayman, where he fights at Warraqatayn and draws off to Nuqm {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.227} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.228} ([[event/abu-al-atahiya-fights-at-ayman-and-warraqatayn-and-withdraws-to-nuqm-288|the account]]); he is on al-Hadi's left in the fight at Hidayn {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.232} and spends a night in the fort of Alab {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.232}; the narrator then says he was hit by an arrow at Hidayn and died {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.233} ([[event/the-deaths-of-ali-ibn-sulayman-and-abu-al-atahiya-at-hidayn-288|the deaths of Ali ibn Sulayman and Abu al-Atahiya]]). The text gives neither a burial nor his age.
