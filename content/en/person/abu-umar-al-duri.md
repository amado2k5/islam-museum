## Overview

Abu 'Umar Hafs ibn 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Suhban al-Duri of Baghdad, who was blind, is a transmitter of the reading of [[person/abu-amr-ibn-al-ala|Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala']] through al-Yazidi, and also of the reading of [[person/al-kisai|al-Kisa'i]]. He died in Shawwal 246 AH. Ibn al-Jazari calls him the leading reader of his age and the head of those who taught the readings in his time, reliable, firm and exact. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 144}

## The first to gather the readings

Ibn al-Jazari states that al-Duri was the first to gather the readings in one work, and adds that he himself received the ten readings through al-Duri's line. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 144} The remark matters for the history of the discipline: al-Duri died in 246 AH, some eighty years before Ibn Mujahid (d. 324 AH), so the gathering of readings began before the Book of the Seven.

## Chain and pupils

Al-Duri read on Abu Muhammad Yahya ibn al-Mubarak al-Yazidi in the transmission of Abu 'Amr, and Ibn al-Jazari lists Abu al-Zar'a' and Ibn Farah among those who read on him. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 140} Al-Dani, as quoted there, calls Abu al-Zar'a' one of the greatest and most reliable of al-Duri's companions; Ibn Farah is described as a trustworthy, eminent and exact teacher who read all of al-Duri's readings and knew Qur'anic commentary. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 144}
