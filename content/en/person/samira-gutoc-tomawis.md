## Summary
Samira Ali Gutoc-Tomawis is a Maranao civic leader, journalist and women's-rights advocate from Mindanao: a former member of the Regional Legislative Assembly of the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (ARMM) and of the Bangsamoro Transition Commission, best known for resigning public office in protest during the siege of Marawi and for organising relief for its displaced civilians. {cite:wikipedia-samira-gutoc-tomawis} {cite:muslim500-samira-gutoc-tomawis}

## Life and formation
She was born on 19 December 1974 in Jeddah, where her father, diplomat Candidato Gutoc, was posted, and grew up between Saudi Arabia and Marawi. At the University of the Philippines Diliman she studied broadcast communication, became the first Muslim woman to head the UP Muslim Students Association and the Metro Manila Muslim Youth and Students Alliance, then took a master's in international studies and a law degree from Arellano University, with a fellowship at the Oxford Centre for Islamic Studies. {cite:wikipedia-samira-gutoc-tomawis}

## Public office
Appointed by President Benigno Aquino III, she sat in the 7th ARMM Regional Legislative Assembly in 2012–2013 as sectoral representative for women, pushing gender and development measures and women's participation in peace processes. In 2017 she joined the Bangsamoro Transition Commission drafting the Bangsamoro Basic Law, then resigned after the imposition of martial law in Mindanao and President Duterte's remarks about soldiers and rape, which she publicly condemned. {cite:wikipedia-samira-gutoc-tomawis} {cite:muslim500-samira-gutoc-tomawis}

## Marawi and after
During the 2017 siege of Marawi she co-founded the Ranao Rescue Team, bringing relief and legal assistance to fleeing civilians and speaking for the evacuees in national and international media. She later co-founded Ako Bakwit, an organisation for the rights of internally displaced people, ran twice for the Senate (2019, 2022) without success, and was listed in The Muslim 500. {cite:wikipedia-samira-gutoc-tomawis} {cite:muslim500-samira-gutoc-tomawis}

## Assessments
Supporters see in her a rare Moro woman who turned elite education into advocacy for the displaced; critics of her senatorial bids questioned whether activism translated into a national programme. The museum records both readings attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-samira-gutoc-tomawis}
