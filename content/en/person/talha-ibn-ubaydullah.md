Talha ibn Ubaydullah al-Taymi al-Qurashi was one of the eight of the first vanguard. He became Muslim through the call of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]] during the years of the [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|secret call]] in Mecca. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## During the secret call (The Sealed Nectar p.76)
The book counts him among those who became Muslim through the call of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] during the years of the [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|secret call]]. He was one of the eight who went before the people, forming the first vanguard and the front line of Islam. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. The book refers to him by his tribal lineage, al-Taymi. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## Family and background in the Encyclopaedia of Islam
The sections above rest on Sunni sources. The sections that follow add the article on Talha in the second edition of the Encyclopaedia of Islam, written by the modern academic W. Madelung. Where it differs from the Sunni accounts, it is labelled as the article's own account. The article counts Talha among the first eight converts to Islam and among the ten to whom the Prophet had promised Paradise. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 176} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

He belonged to the clan of [[group/banu-taym|Taym]] ibn Murra of [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]], so he was a kinsman of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], though about twenty years younger. The two were closely associated and were known as "the two mates". The article gives the prevalent explanation, that during the early persecution they were once tied together with a single rope, and a second explanation, that Talha's father made the young man a close associate of Abu Bakr. His mother was al-Sa'ba, daughter of Abd Allah ibn Imad al-Hadrami, a confederate of Harb ibn Umayya. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

## Conversion, migration and the first campaigns
The article reports that, by his own account, a monk in [[place/busra|Busra]] told Talha of the coming of the last of the prophets. He hurried back to [[place/mecca|Mecca]], was introduced to Muhammad by Abu Bakr and accepted Islam. He kept up his caravan trade in Syria and did not join the emigration to Abyssinia. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

When Muhammad and Abu Bakr made the hijra, Talha met them at al-Kharrar as he was coming from Syria. He gave them clothes from Syria and news of the Muslims in [[place/medina|Medina]], then went on to Mecca and later escorted Abu Bakr's family, including [[person/aisha|Aisha]], to Medina. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177} Just before [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], Muhammad sent him with [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Said ibn Zayd]] to spy on the Meccan caravan. They therefore missed the battle but were given a share of the booty. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

At [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], according to the article, he distinguished himself by shielding the Prophet in person and killed two Meccans. He received many wounds, one of which left one or two of his fingers paralysed, and the article says Muhammad promised him Paradise on that occasion for his bravery. He took part in all the later campaigns and battles of the Prophet. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

## After Muhammad, under Abu Bakr and Umar
The article says Talha backed his kinsman Abu Bakr after Muhammad's death. It calls unreliable the isolated reports of [[person/ibn-ishaq|Ibn Ishaq]] and others that he first stayed with [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and refused to pledge allegiance to Abu Bakr. It says he played a prominent part in the battle of Dhu al-Qassa, which opened the [[event/ridda-wars|Ridda wars]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

The article adds that, before Abu Bakr's death, Talha is said to have protested in vain against the choice of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] as successor, complaining of Umar's harshness towards Muslims. The author considers it not impossible that Talha already aspired to the caliphate himself; this is the author's conjecture. Umar appointed Talha one of the six electors and candidates for the succession. At Umar's death Talha was away from Medina and so took no part in the election of [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]]. On his return he voiced dissatisfaction at his exclusion, remarking that he was not a man whose views could be ignored. The article says Uthman is reported to have offered to resign so that a fresh election could be held, but Talha, having made his point, pledged allegiance. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

## Relations with Uthman, in the article's account
This section gives the article's academic reconstruction, which is not the only account. It reports that Uthman first made special efforts to win Talha's backing with generous gifts, which Talha's son Musa put at 200,000 dinars. Uthman also let him exchange his share in the oasis of Khaybar for the lucrative estate of al-Nashtastadi near [[place/kufa|Kufa]]. Soon afterwards, the article says, Talha turned against the caliph and became his harshest critic among the major Companions. It states that he wrote to the provincial garrison towns summoning them to revolt, and that during the siege of Uthman's house in Medina he took the keys of the treasury and kept close contact with the besieging Egyptian rebels. The article concludes that he evidently shared their aim of removing Uthman and, with strong backing from Aisha, expected to be chosen as successor. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

## The caliphate of Ali and the Camel, in the article's account
After Uthman was killed, the article says, Talha at first had some support among the Egyptian rebels and the Quraysh in Medina, but the Kufan rebels favouring Ali, backed by the great majority of the [[group/ansar|Ansar]], soon gained the upper hand. The Kufan leader [[person/malik-al-ashtar|al-Ashtar]] dragged him to the mosque to pledge allegiance to Ali against his will, though, the article adds, no physical force or threats were used in Ali's presence. Talha and [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]], who also pledged unwillingly, left for Mecca within days. There they joined Aisha in demanding vengeance for Uthman's blood and in holding Ali responsible for the killing. Together they led a Meccan army to [[place/basra|Basra]], seeking support and promising a new election once Ali was removed. The article notes an intense rivalry between Talha and al-Zubayr, since al-Zubayr, having backed Uthman loyally to the end, thought himself better entitled to the succession. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

## How Talha died: the accounts
The Sunni accounts given above, from al-Sallabi, say Talha was struck by an arrow at the start of the [[event/battle-of-the-camel|Battle of the Camel]], and that some historians name [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]] as the archer, a report al-Sallabi refutes. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 95} The summary of this record also cites al-Mas'udi, who names Marwan as the man who shot him. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 658-659}

The article places the death in the Battle of the Camel on 15 Jumada I 36, or 8 December 656, as Ali and the Kufans were gaining the upper hand. It says Marwan, fighting in the Meccan army, hit Talha's knee with an arrow and that he bled to death, and it calls this a treacherous murder. It records that L. Caetani and other modern scholars have dismissed the reports of Marwan's act as anti-Umayyad fiction. The article answers that they are supported by the fact that Marwanid propaganda in Syria during the second civil war proudly proclaimed Marwan the first avenger of Uthman for what he had done. The accounts are set side by side here without the museum choosing among them. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

## His sons and his wealth
Talha's eldest son Muhammad, described as pious, was also killed in the Battle of the Camel. Another son, Musa, survived the battle, was pardoned by Ali and was allowed to take possession of Talha's vast fortune for his family, including land in Iraq and in the Sarat in Arabia. The article judges that Talha's wealth seems to have been surpassed only by Uthman's among the early Companions, and that, like Uthman, he is described as a generous benefactor of the early Muslim community. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 177}

## For Sprouts
Talha ibn Ubaydullah al-Taymi al-Qurashi, one of the eight who were the first band of Islam; he entered Islam at Abu Bakr al-Siddiq's invitation in the secret-call years at Mecca.

## For Explorers
Talha ibn Ubaydullah al-Taymi al-Qurashi, one of the eight who were the first band of Islam; he entered at Abu Bakr's invitation in the secret-call years. Al-Sallabi reports a monk at Busra told him a prophet had come out of Mecca, and Abu Bakr brought him to the Prophet; he fought at Uhud until he took twenty-four wounds, and the Prophet said he had made Paradise due. He divided his wealth and kept none overnight — seven hundred thousand from Hadramawt lay on his mind until he divided it by morning. An arrow struck him at the start of the Camel; Ali passed his body wiping the dust from his face: 'would I had died twenty years before this'. Some historians name Marwan ibn al-Hakam as the shooter, a report al-Sallabi refutes.

## For Scholars
Talha ibn Ubaydullah al-Taymi al-Qurashi, one of the eight who were the first band of Islam; he entered at Abu Bakr al-Siddiq's invitation in the secret-call years at Mecca, the book naming him by his Taymi line. Al-Sallabi reports he embraced it after a monk at Busra told him a prophet had come out of Mecca, Abu Bakr bringing him to the Prophet; he fought at Uhud until he took twenty-four wounds, and the Prophet said of him he had made Paradise due for what he did. Al-Sallabi tells that Talha divided his wealth and kept none overnight — money reached him from Hadramawt worth seven hundred thousand and he lay restless until he divided it in the morning. In the Camel he was struck by an arrow in the knee or the neck at the start of the battle, and Ali passed his body wiping the dust from his face saying 'would I had died twenty years before this'. Some historians relate Marwan ibn al-Hakam shot him, a report al-Sallabi refutes.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In episode 25 of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] calls Talha ibn Ubayd Allah «the living martyr», and says he shielded the Prophet with his own body at Uhud, that the Prophet used to say Talha was among those who had fulfilled their vow, and links this to the verse praising believing men who were true to their covenant with God. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:01:30} He says Talha accepted Islam through [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], and so counts among Abu Bakr's good deeds, citing the hadith that whoever points the way to a good deed is like the one who does it. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:02:00} He notes that Talha was of [[group/banu-taym|Banu Taym]], Abu Bakr's clan, and one of the six whom [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] chose for the caliphate. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:02:00} He says Talha was born in the twenty-eighth year before the hijra and died in the thirty-sixth year after it. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:02:30} His mother was al-Sa'ba bint al-Hadrami, sister of [[person/al-ala-ibn-al-hadrami|al-Ala' ibn al-Hadrami]], whom he describes as a Companion known for wonders. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:03:00}

He says Talha grew up in Mecca in the powerful clan of Taym, learned horsemanship and then became a merchant, always coming and going. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:03:00} He narrates that Abu Bakr, after his own conversion, sent for Talha because of his character and their kinship, offered him Islam, and Talha accepted; Abu Bakr took him to the Prophet, where he declared his Islam when the Muslims could be counted on the fingers {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:03:30}; that he accepted Islam at Abu Bakr's invitation is also stated in this record from The Sealed Nectar. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} He says that despite his wealth, family and standing he was persecuted by the polytheists, and later emigrated to [[place/medina|Medina]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:04:30} He relates from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]] that everything in Medina lit up on the day the Prophet entered it and everything in it darkened on the day he died, and that they had hardly shaken the dust of his burial from their hands before they found their hearts changed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:05:00} He says Talha missed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] in the second year because the Prophet had sent him with [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Sa'id ibn Zayd]] to gather news of Abu Sufyan's caravan; they waited at al-Hawra', the caravan passed, and the battle was fought before they returned, and Talha grieved deeply. He attributes this to [[person/muhammad-ibn-sad|Ibn Sa'd]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:05:30}

He describes the plan at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]]: fifty archers on the hill under [[person/abd-allah-ibn-jubayr|Abd Allah ibn Jubayr]], ordered never to leave it whatever befell the Muslims. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:06:30} When the archers saw the polytheists fleeing they went after the booty, some of them coming down despite their commander's order; [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid]] saw the gap, wheeled round the army and killed the archers who remained; the tide turned, seventy Companions were killed, and a rumour spread that the Prophet had been killed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:07:30} He lists the Prophet's wounds that day: Utba ibn Abi Waqqas struck him with a stone, gashing his head and breaking a front tooth; rings of his helmet were driven into his cheek; and Ibn Qami'a struck his shoulder with a sword, which pained him for a month but did not cut through, since he wore two coats of mail. The speaker counts this as taking the proper means. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:08:30} He relates from [[work/sahih-muslim|Muslim]], narrated by Anas, that at Uhud the Prophet was left with seven of the [[group/ansar|Ansar]] and two men of Quraysh; when the polytheists pressed on him he promised Paradise to whoever drove them back, and the Ansar went forward one after another until all seven were killed; the two men of Quraysh were [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd]] and Talha. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:09:00} Talha then stood defending the Prophet with his sword until his fingers were cut off and his hand was crippled, and that day he took, by his count, thirty-nine wounds; when his fingers were cut he cried out in pain, and the Prophet said that had he named God, the angels would have lifted him up as the people watched. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:10:30} He cites the hadith that whoever would like to look at a martyr walking on the earth should look at Talha, attributing it to [[person/al-tirmidhi-muhammad-ibn-isa|al-Tirmidhi]] with [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|al-Albani]]'s grading of sound. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:11:30} He cites the hadith that Talha «made it binding», that is, earned Paradise, by what he did for the Prophet, attributing it to [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] and al-Tirmidhi with al-Albani's grading of sound {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:11:30}; this saying is also given in this record from al-Sallabi. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 93} He explains, from [[person/ibn-ishaq|Ibn Ishaq]], that the Prophet, weighed down and wearing two coats of mail, wanted to climb a rock and could not, so Talha crouched beneath him until he climbed up on his back; Talha had a limp, and out of reverence made himself walk straight, and his leg was cured of the limp. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:11:30}

He relates from [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]]'s Siyar, narrated by [[person/musa-ibn-talha|Musa ibn Talha]] from his father, that seven hundred thousand dirhams came to Talha from [[place/hadramawt|Hadramawt]] and he spent a restless night; his wife [[person/umm-kulthum-bint-abi-bakr|Umm Kulthum bint Abi Bakr]] advised him to distribute it in the morning, and he gave it out to the [[group/muhajirun|Muhajirun]] and the Ansar in purses of a thousand dirhams, leaving only one purse for his household {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:13:30}; the account of this money is also given in this record from al-Sallabi. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 94} Among those who received it he names [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar]], [[person/suhayb-ibn-sinan|Suhayb]], [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman]], Sa'd and Sa'id. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:15:00} He relates from al-Dhahabi's Siyar that a Bedouin asked him for help on the strength of a distant kinship; Talha said no one had ever asked him by that tie before, and offered him a plot of land that [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] had offered to buy for three hundred thousand dirhams, letting him choose the land or its price; the man chose the price, and Talha gave it to him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:16:00} He relates from al-Tirmidhi, which he says al-Tirmidhi graded good, narrated by [[person/malik-ibn-abi-amir-al-asbahi|Malik ibn Abi Amir]], that a man told Talha that this Yemeni, meaning [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], reported things they did not hear from anyone else; Talha replied that he had no doubt heard what they had not, for they were men with households who came to the Prophet morning and evening, while Abu Hurayra was poor and stayed at his door; and no one with any good in him would attribute to the Prophet what he had not said. The speaker draws from it a lesson in thinking well of fellow Muslims. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:17:00}

He relates from al-Dhahabi's Siyar, adding that Ahmad narrated it, that Talha's daughter [[person/aisha-bint-talha|Aisha]] saw him in a dream complaining of water seeping into his grave; they moved him thirty years after his death and found nothing of him changed except a few hairs on one side of his beard. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:22:00} He says Talha left fourteen sons, whom he named after prophets, among them Muhammad al-Sajjad, Musa, Isa, Ya'qub and Yusuf. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:22:30} He says each of Talha's wives had a sister among the Prophet's wives: Umm Kulthum bint Abi Bakr, sister of [[person/aisha|Aisha]]; a daughter of Jahsh, sister of [[person/zaynab-bint-jahsh|Zaynab]]; a daughter of Abu Sufyan, sister of [[person/umm-habiba-bint-abi-sufyan|Umm Habiba]]; and a daughter of Abu Umayya, sister of [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep25 00:22:30} He also discusses the strife after Uthman's killing, the Battle of the Camel and Talha's death, which are not written here.
