# Muhammad Ali Jauhar

Mohammad Ali Jauhar (10 December 1878 – 4 January 1931) was an Indian politician, activist, journalist and poet, a co-founder of the All-India Muslim League and of Jamia Millia Islamia, and a leading figure of the Khilafat movement {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}. He was the younger brother of Shaukat Ali {cite:wikipedia-shaukat-ali-en}.

## Early life and education

He was born at Rampur in 1878 into a wealthy family with roots in Najibabad; his father Abdul Ali Khan died when he was five, and his mother Abadi Bano Begum, known as Bi Amman, drove her sons' education and their later struggle {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}. He attended Aligarh Muslim University and Allahabad University, then moved to England in 1898 to study modern history at Lincoln College, Oxford {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-ar}. On his return he served as education director of Rampur state and then in the Baroda civil service, writing for major British and Indian newspapers {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}.

## Journalism

He launched the English weekly The Comrade in Calcutta in 1911, which quickly gained international circulation and influence; moving to Delhi in 1912, he launched the Urdu daily Hamdard in 1913 {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}. The Urdu biography notes that his prose was read eagerly inside and outside India, and that Hamdard became a byword for fearless expression {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-ur}. He worked to expand the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh and co-founded Jamia Millia Islamia in 1920 {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}.

## Khilafat and Congress

He attended the founding meeting of the All-India Muslim League at Dacca in 1906 and served as its president in 1918, remaining active in it until 1928 {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}. In 1919 he travelled with the League's delegation to England over the future of the Ottoman caliphate; the British rejection of the demands led to the Khilafat committee and movement {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}. In 1921 he built a broad coalition with Shaukat Ali, Abul Kalam Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Mukhtar Ahmed Ansari and Gandhi; he was arrested and imprisoned for two years for a speech at the Khilafat Conference, and punished again with two and a half years in Karachi central jail {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}. He was elected president of the Indian National Congress in 1923, but his tenure lasted only a few months amid disagreements over the ending of the Non-Cooperation Movement {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}.

Disillusioned by the collapse of the Khilafat movement, he restarted Hamdard, left Congress, opposed the Nehru Report and supported Jinnah's Fourteen Points {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}.

## London, death and burial

Sick with diabetes and weakened by his imprisonments, he attended the first Round Table Conference in London in 1930; his reported words to the British government were that he would not return to India alive unless the country was set free: "I would prefer to die in a foreign country so long as it is a free country, and if you do not give us freedom in India, you will have to give me a grave here." {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en} He died of a stroke in London on 4 January 1931 and was buried in Jerusalem, in the Khatuniyya Madrasa near the Dome of the Rock, by the choice of his relatives, friends and admirers {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-ur}.

## Faith, as the sources attest it

The articles present a Muslim political leader whose cause was the defence of the caliphate and of India's Muslims: the Arabic article calls him an Indian Muslim leader, politician, journalist and poet {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-ar}, and the English and Arabic infoboxes give his religion as Islam {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-ar}; Wikidata gives Islam only {cite:wikidata-muhammad-ali-jauhar}. No source read names a legal school, order or branch for him, and the museum's data record says so.

He also wrote Urdu poetry under the pen name Jauhar; the Urdu article quotes his ghazals and poems in a mujahid spirit {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-ur}. India Post and Pakistan Post both issued commemorative stamps for him in 1978, and universities, colleges and neighbourhoods in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh carry his name {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ali-jauhar-en}.
