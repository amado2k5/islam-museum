## Overview
Ya'rub ibn Bil'arab ibn Sultan ibn Sayf ibn Malik al-Ya'rubi was the seventh of the Ya'rubi imams of Oman. Al-Zirikli classes him among the Ibadis, the school of the Omani imamate and not a Sunni body, so what follows is the Ibadi imamate as al-Zirikli reports it. He gives his death as 1135 AH, 1723 CE, and prints zero for the year of birth, meaning it is not known {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 pp. 191-192}.

## Life
He rose against the imam Muhanna ibn Sultan in 1132 AH and killed him, and for a year ruled the country in the name of Sayf ibn Sultan, who according to al-Zirikli died in 1155 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 pp. 191-192}. Ya'rub then called people to his own imamate and repented of his rebellion against Muhanna; the oath was taken to him in 1134 AH and he settled in Nizwa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 pp. 191-192}. Revolt then broke out, and al-Rustaq, Muscat, Nakhl and Samail withdrew from obedience to him; his position weakened and he was deposed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 pp. 191-192}. He asked to live in the fort of Jabrin and was granted it, but soon entered Nizwa and fortified himself there; some emirs supported him and he held on until he died in Nizwa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 pp. 191-192}.
