## Overview
Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, a scholar of Medina. He was with [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik]] at Marj Dabiq when death came to him. He wrote his testament and had it witnessed by Raja' ibn Haywa and Makhul. When he was buried, al-Zuhri rose and asked the people: "Are you content with the one whom the Commander of the Faithful named in his testament?" Then he read the document, in which were the name of [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] and after him Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.172-173}

Al-Salabi says that Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz ordered him to collect the Sunan, and he wrote them out, volume by volume, and sent them to every land that had a ruler. He relates a saying of Umar: "Hold to Ibn Shihab, for no one remains who knows a past Sunna better than he." {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.298-300}

Al-Mas'udi says that he died in 123 AH; al-Waqidi mentions that he died in 124 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.192}

## In al-Dhahabi's Siyar
Al-Dhahabi gives him as Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn 'Ubaydallah ibn 'Abdallah ibn Shihab of Medina, resident in Syria; his birth year is disputed between 50 and 51 AH, with one report placing it at 56. Per al-'Ijli he heard three hadiths from Ibn 'Umar, and he narrated from Sahl ibn Sa'd, Anas ibn Malik, al-Sa'ib ibn Yazid, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab — whose company he kept for eight years — 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, 'Ali ibn al-Husayn, 'Ubaydallah ibn 'Abdallah ibn 'Utba, Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith, al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, Kharija ibn Zayd and Aban ibn 'Uthman. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.3700}

'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, though older than him, narrated from him, as did 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb, Qatada, Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani, al-Awza'i, Malik ibn Anas, al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Ibn Ishaq and a host of scholars; 'Ali ibn al-Madini estimated his corpus at about two thousand hadiths, and Abu Dawud said his hadiths numbered two thousand two hundred, half of them with full chains. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.3700}

## In Ibn Sa'd's al-Tabaqat al-Kubra
His notice in the completing section of [[work/al-tabaqat-al-kubra-ibn-sad|al-Tabaqat al-Kubra]] is no. 70, the first of the fourth generation of Medinan Successors and the longest in that section. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 7} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 157}
- **Lineage**: Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn 'Ubayd Allah ibn 'Abd Allah the younger ibn Shihab ibn 'Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn Zuhra ibn Kilab; his mother was 'A'isha bint 'Abd Allah the elder ibn Shihab; his kunya was Abu Bakr. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 157}
- **Seeking knowledge**: from al-Waqidi, Ibn Sa'd relates that al-Zuhri said he grew up a boy without means and learned his people's genealogy from 'Abd Allah ibn Tha'laba ibn Su'ayr. When he saw that man unable to answer a question on divorce and send the questioner to Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, he left him. He sat instead with Sa'id, 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, 'Ubayd Allah ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Utba and Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith ibn Hisham until he became learned in law. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 157-158}
- **In Damascus**: he travelled to Syria and sat in Qabisa ibn Dhu'ayb's circle in the mosque of Damascus. When he told them 'Umar ibn al-Khattab's ruling on slave mothers of children, Qabisa brought him to 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 158-159} He then attended al-Walid, Sulayman, 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz and Yazid ibn 'Abd al-Malik, who made him a judge together with Sulayman ibn Habib al-Muharibi. After that he attached himself to Hisham, went on pilgrimage with him in 106 AH, and was placed with his sons to teach them law and hadith. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 162}
- **On writing**: Ibn Sa'd relates from Salih ibn Kaysan that al-Zuhri wrote down the sunan and what came from the Companions, and from Ma'mar his saying: "We used to dislike writing down knowledge until these rulers compelled us to it; then we decided not to withhold it from any Muslim" (see [[question/why-did-al-zuhri-write-down-hadith|Why did al-Zuhri write hadith down?]]). {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 168-169}
- **Birth and death**: al-Waqidi said he was born in 58 AH, at the end of Mu'awiya's caliphate. In 124 AH he went to his estates at Thuliya in Shaghb and Bada, fell ill there, and died on 17 Ramadan 124 AH aged seventy-five. He asked to be buried by the roadside. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 185} From his nephew Ibn Sa'd relates that he and one of Hisham's sons had agreed to flee to Jabal al-Dukhan if Hisham died. He died some months before Hisham, and al-Walid ibn Yazid regretted not having laid hands on him. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 184}
