## Summary
Bakr bin Laden is a Saudi businessman, long the chairman of the Saudi Binladin Group (SBG) — the construction conglomerate founded by his father that for decades has been the kingdom's principal builder, including the great expansions of the Grand Mosque in Mecca and the Prophet's Mosque in Medina. {cite:wikipedia-bakr-bin-laden}

## The Binladin Group
The company was founded by Mohammed bin Laden in the 1930s and grew with Saudi oil revenues into one of the largest construction firms in the world, responsible for airports, roads, palaces and — its signature work — the successive enlargements of the holy mosques commissioned by the Saudi state since the 1950s. Bakr, one of Mohammed's elder sons, became the family patriarch and group chairman after the deaths or retirements of earlier brothers. {cite:wikipedia-bakr-bin-laden}

## Projects and crisis
Under his chairmanship the group executed the third Saudi expansion of the Grand Mosque, the King Abdullah Expansion, and built the Abraj Al-Bait clock-tower complex overlooking the Haram. In 2015 a crawler crane owned by the group collapsed inside the mosque, killing over a hundred pilgrims — a disaster that led to the group being temporarily barred from new state contracts. In November 2017 Bakr was detained in the Ritz-Carlton purge and released in 2018; the state later took a management stake in the restructured group. {cite:wikipedia-bakr-bin-laden}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as the steward of the company that physically built much of the Two Holy Mosques' modern fabric — a builder whose family firm's history is inseparable from the twentieth-century transformation of Mecca and Medina. {cite:muslim500-bakr-bin-laden}
