## Summary
Tayseer Rajab al-Tamimi served for nearly three decades as the Chief Islamic Justice of Palestine — the head of its sharia court system — while also becoming one of the most quoted Palestinian religious voices on Jerusalem and interfaith questions. {cite:muslim500-tayseer-rajab-al-tamimi} {cite:wikipedia-tayseer-rajab-al-tamimi}

## Formation
Born in 1952 in Hebron, he studied at Al-Azhar in Cairo, where he took his doctorate in Islamic philosophy, then returned to Palestine to work in the religious judiciary. {cite:wikipedia-tayseer-rajab-al-tamimi}

## Career
Appointed head of the Palestinian sharia courts after the creation of the Palestinian Authority in 1994, he also served as deputy head of its Supreme Judicial Council and chaired the Palestinian Centre for Religion and Civilization Dialogue in Hebron. Through sermons and frequent media appearances he became an outspoken commentator on the conflict, particularly on Jerusalem and the status of the Haram al-Sharif. {cite:muslim500-tayseer-rajab-al-tamimi} {cite:wikipedia-tayseer-rajab-al-tamimi}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 notes his reputation as an outspoken thinker on Palestinian-Israeli relations; his remarks on the conflict have at times been praised by Palestinian audiences and criticised by Israeli ones, a dispute the museum records attributed and without verdict. {cite:muslim500-tayseer-rajab-al-tamimi}
