## Overview
Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Ali ibn Fath ibn Labbal, the judge Abu al-Hasan al-Sharishi, known as Ibn Labbal. Al-Safadi vowels the name Labbal with a damma on the first lam, a doubled ba' and a second lam after the alif. He says the line of descent goes back to Abd al-Rahman ibn Mu'awiya ibn Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik; the sentence is garbled in the local text. He died in Sharish in 583 AH, according to al-Safadi in al-Wafi bi-l-Wafayat {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 20 p. 90}.

## Poetry
Al-Safadi quotes love verses describing a beloved and nights of embrace {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 20 p. 90}, verses on old age in which he likens his bent back to a bow without a string, and verses on charcoal and fire {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 20 p. 91}.

For the charcoal piece al-Safadi reports that Ibn al-Abbar said Ibn Labbal fell short of what Muhammad ibn Sara had said on the same image. Al-Safadi answers that Ibn Labbal did not fall short, and that the one who fell short was Ibn Sara: the down on a red cheek is a closer comparison than the cheeks of maidens behind veils, since veils cover the cheeks, whereas cheek and down show together. He then quotes lines by others on the same image. He adds a further piece by Ibn Labbal on a moon-like beloved whom beauty has clothed in a garment of modesty, and comments that it is good poetry {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 20 p. 91}.
