## Summary
Mohamed Zakariya is an American calligrapher who converted to Islam and carried classical Arabic calligraphy from Istanbul’s circles into American institutions, becoming a link between the Ottoman calligraphic tradition and the West. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-zakariya}

## Life and upbringing
Born in California in 1942, he embraced Islam as a young man and turned to calligraphy, travelling to Istanbul to study under masters such as Hasan Çelebi and Hamid al-Amidi and receiving ijazas. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-zakariya}

## Career and works
He devoted his life to teaching and artistic production, training generations of calligraphers in America and the Arab world. He designed the US Postal Service Eid stamp (2001); his Ottoman-style panels hang in museums such as the Freer and Sackler, and he runs teaching circles in calligraphy. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-zakariya}

## Legacy
Zakariya is described as the bridge between the classical calligraphy school and its students in the West, and he is cited in contemporary Islamic-art literature as a teacher who transmitted the heritage living. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-zakariya}
