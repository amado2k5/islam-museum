## Summary
Aisha bint Ibrahim ibn al-Siddiq, in the entry of [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']] drawn from Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina, was an accomplished hadith transmitter and Qur'an reciter who knew it by heart, devout, scrupulous and ascetic; born in 661 AH, she was the wife of the hafiz [[person/jamal-al-din-al-mizzi|al-Mizzi]]. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.8}

## Her learning and teaching
She heard hadith from Abu al-Fadl ibn Asakir and others and transmitted; she taught women to recite, and several completed the Qur'an with her and benefited from her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.8}

## Ibn Kathir's praise
[[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] said of her: "She was without equal in the abundance of her worship and the beauty of her recitation of the Qur'an, surpassing many men in that." {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.8}

## Her death
She died in Jumada al-Ula 741 AH. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.8}

## For young visitors
Aisha was a woman of Damascus who knew the Qur'an by heart and taught it to women until they finished it; her husband was a great scholar of hadith.
