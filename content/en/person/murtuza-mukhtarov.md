# Murtuza Mukhtarov

## Summary
Murtuza Mukhtarov (1857–1920 by the reading this record follows; the Russian article and Wikidata give 1855) was an Azerbaijani oil industrialist and millionaire, a self-taught drilling engineer whose inventions were exported worldwide, and the builder of Baku's Mukhtarov Palace and of mosques at Amirjan and Vladikavkaz. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-ru} {cite:wikidata-murtuza-mukhtarov}

## Early life and career
He was born into a poor family at Amirjan village near Baku — the Azerbaijani article says a cart-driver's family — and as a boy carried cargoes between Baku and Tiflis with his brother. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-az} In 1874 he began work at the mines around Zabrat and Balakhani, where a contractor recognised his ability, taught him drilling equipment and eventually sold him the workshop rig; the villagers of Amirjan collected money to help him, and he taught himself technical drawing and engineering. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} In 1890 he founded his own drilling company, which grew to two divisions and 2,500 workers manufacturing derrick machinery and boring wells across the Baku fields and at Maykop and Grozny. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} His "Podrat" drilling rig served the Balakhani, Surakhani, Ramana and Sabunchu districts; in 1895 he built the first drilling rig with metal rods — his patented "Baku Drilling System" — and his drilling tool, known simply as the "Mukhtarov", was exported to many countries; he also opened a drilling-equipment plant at Bibiheybat, the first oil-equipment enterprise of its kind, with three-storey housing for its workers. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en}

## Philanthropy and buildings
Mukhtarov funded a mosque and school at Amirjan (1910) — its twin minarets rise 46 metres, and the mosque still serves as a place of worship — seventeen buildings across Absheron, a dome over the grave of Akhund Mirza Abu Turab at Pirhasan, and the mosque on the Terek at Vladikavkaz. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} He was patron of the Baku Real College and the Temirkhan-Shura girls' gymnasium, a member of the St Petersburg Muslim Charitable Society, and founder of forty scholarships; with his wife Liza-Khanum he sponsored the singer Fatma Mukhtarova and ran a boarding school for poor and orphan girls in their palace, seat from 1914 of the first Female Muslim Philanthropic Society, which Liza-Khanum founded. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} In 1911–1912 he had Józef Plośko build the Gothic Revival palace in central Baku as a gift for his wife; it is now the Palace of Marriage Registrations. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en}

The English article tells the Vladikavkaz mosque's story as a courtship: visiting the city, Mukhtarov fell in love with the Ossetian noblewoman Liza-Khanum, whose parents refused a suitor not born to nobility — whereupon he built the magnificent mosque on the bank of the Terek, after which refusal was impossible. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} The couple had no children and devoted themselves to philanthropy. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en}

## Death
On 28 April 1920, as the Bolshevik takeover of Azerbaijan unfolded, Red Army soldiers entered his palace; Mukhtarov shot several of them and then killed himself in the residence. {cite:wikipedia-murtuza-mukhtarov-en} {cite:wikidata-murtuza-mukhtarov}
