## Summary
Ma'bad ibn Ka'b ibn Malik al-Ansari al-Salami al-Madani, a trustworthy Successor of the third generation-layer, son of the Companion-poet Ka'b ibn Malik and brother of Abd Allah ibn Ka'b, his father's guide; he transmitted the account of the Aqaba night from his brother from their father {cite:tahdhib-tahdhib p.1050} {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.772}.

## His family
His father Ka'b ibn Malik al-Ansari, the poet, was one of the nuqaba' of the Aqaba night and protagonist of the famous repentance story; his brother Abd Allah ibn Ka'b was among the most learned of the Ansar and led his father when the old man went blind {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.772}. Ma'bad was the youngest of the brothers {cite:tahdhib-tahdhib p.1050}.

## His narration
Ibn Ishaq writes: "Ma'bad ibn Ka'b told me that his brother Abd Allah — one of the most learned of the Ansar — related to him that their father, who had witnessed the Aqaba, said: we went out among our people's pilgrims having prayed and studied, and al-Bara' ibn Ma'rur our elder and chief was with us," and he tells the story of the Aqaba night at length {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.139}. Al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah all narrate from him, as the Tahdhib's register shows {cite:tahdhib-tahdhib p.1050}.

## Accreditation
Ibn Hibban listed him in al-Thiqat; the Tahdhib register fixes him as trustworthy, third layer {cite:tahdhib-tahdhib p.1050}.

## For younger visitors
Ma'bad ibn Ka'b was the son of the Companion-poet whose repentance God accepted; through his brother Abd Allah he passed down their father's account of the Aqaba pledge — one of the most reliable reports of early Medina.

## Notes for researchers
- His place in the Tahdhib is 10/224; the Dhahabi–Ibn Hajar register in the composite scan is at p. 1050.
- Ibn Ishaq's narration from him is explicit in al-Isaba (entries of al-Bara' ibn Ma'rur and Abu Ubada); he has no stand-alone entry in the scan because he is a Successor, not a Companion.
