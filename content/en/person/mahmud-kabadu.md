## Overview
Mahmud ibn Muhammad (or Ali) Qabadu, the Tunisian Abu al-Thana', was the poet of his age in Tunis and mufti of its Malikis; his origin was Sfax and he was born and raised at Tunis, devoted to the sciences of rhetoric. He died in 1288 AH (1871 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.184}

He became a Sufi and read widely in the books of the school, especially Ibn 'Arabi's, ending in detachment so that he would walk the markets of Tunis barefoot and bareheaded raising his voice in tahlil; he then left home as a wandering ascetic, entering Tripoli and settling at Istanbul, until the Tunisian vizier Ahmad ibn Abi al-Diyaf persuaded him to return in 1257 AH, when he was given teaching at the Zaytuna, the judgeship of Bardo and finally the Maliki fatwa office in 1285 AH. Famed for poetry and quick wit, he was also deep in fiqh and the arts, consulted on hard problems of algebra and Euclid, and unmatched in dating events in verse by the abjad reckoning. {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.184}
