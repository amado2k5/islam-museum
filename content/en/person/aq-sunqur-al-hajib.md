## Summary
Aq Sunqur al-Hajib was a mamluk of the Seljuk sultan Malik-shah and governor of Aleppo from 1087. He was defeated by Tutush in 1094, and he was the father of Zangi.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), article signed H. A. R. Gibb, describes Aq Sunqur b. 'Abd Allah, known as Qasim al-Dawla and al-Hajib, a mamluk of Malik-shah. Malik-shah appointed him to the government of Aleppo in 480/1087. At first he supported the efforts of the Seljuk prince Tutush to establish himself in Syria. After Malik-shah's death, he and the other governors of northern Syria and the Jazira declared for Barkiyaruk, and he was defeated by Tutush near Aleppo in Jumada I 487/May 1094. He was the father of Zangi, later atabeg of Mosul, and he is praised for his justice and good government. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 336}
