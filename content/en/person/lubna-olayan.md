## Summary
Lubna Olayan is a Saudi businesswoman who for more than three decades ran Olayan Financing Company — the Middle East arm of one of the kingdom's great family conglomerates — and became the first woman to chair a publicly listed bank in Saudi Arabia. She is widely described as the most prominent woman in Saudi business. {cite:wikipedia-lubna-olayan}

## Formation
Born in 1955 into the family of Sulaiman Olayan, founder of the Olayan Group, she studied agriculture at Cornell and business administration at Indiana University, then trained at J.P. Morgan before joining the family group in the early 1980s — at a time when women in Saudi senior management were almost unheard of. {cite:wikipedia-lubna-olayan}

## Olayan Financing Company
As CEO from the 1980s to 2019 she ran the group's sprawling regional portfolio: stakes and joint ventures in manufacturing, consumer goods, food services and banking partnerships with multinationals from Coca-Cola to Kimberly-Clark — a quietly managed empire that made her a fixture on Forbes and Fortune global power lists. She also served on international boards including Schlumberger and the INSEAD council. {cite:muslim500-lubna-olayan}

## Firsts
In 2004 she became the first woman elected to the board of a Saudi listed company (Saudi Hollandi Bank); in 2019 she was named chair of the merged Saudi British Bank — later Bank Alawwal — the first woman to chair a Saudi bank. She was among the first Saudi women to speak publicly before a mixed audience, at the Jeddah Economic Forum in 2004. {cite:wikipedia-lubna-olayan}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles her as the emblematic pioneer of Saudi women's business leadership: a career built inside a conservative economy that ended in chairmanships, not exceptions. {cite:muslim500-lubna-olayan}
