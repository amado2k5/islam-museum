## Summary
Salman al-Ouda is an internationally known Saudi preacher whose career passed through two distinct phases: the protest discourse of the 1990s Sahwa, then a later discourse of tolerance and openness. {cite:wikipedia-salman-al-ouda}

## Life and upbringing
Born in 1956 in al-Bukayriyya in the Qassim region, he received a traditional religious education and taught in religious institutes, rising to fame through preaching and recorded lectures in the 1980s and 1990s. {cite:wikipedia-salman-al-ouda}

## Career and works
He was imprisoned in the 1990s during the Sahwa events and released in 1999, after which his discourse shifted towards tolerance and dialogue with the other; he founded the IslamToday website and authored books including As’ilat al-Thawra after the Arab Spring. He was detained again in September 2017 and remains in detention according to available sources. {cite:wikipedia-salman-al-ouda}

## Legacy and debate
Researchers read al-Ouda’s transformation as a model of Islamic discourse evolving from Sahwa to reform, while critics on both sides attack him: some say he abandoned his old positions, others doubt the sincerity of the change. His detention is followed by international rights organisations; the museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-salman-al-ouda}
