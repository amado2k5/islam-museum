## Summary
Edna Adan Ismail is a Somaliland nurse-midwife, diplomat and activist whose career spans seven decades across obstetric medicine, state service and the fight against female genital cutting. She built the Edna Adan Maternity Hospital in Hargeisa from her retirement savings — now a reference for training midwives and cutting maternal deaths in the Horn of Africa. {cite:wikipedia-edna-adan-ismail}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1937 in Hargeisa under British colonial rule, in a family that opened education to her early, studied nursing and midwifery in London in the 1950s and 60s — among the first Somali women to study in Britain — then returned to serve in Somali public health. {cite:wikipedia-edna-adan-ismail}

## International and government career
She worked at the World Health Organization on midwifery and training files in Africa, becoming the first Somali woman to hold a senior UN position, and after the civil war and Somaliland's declared independence she held ministerial portfolios including Health and then Foreign Affairs (2003–2006), becoming the first female foreign minister of the breakaway region. {cite:wikipedia-edna-adan-ismail} {cite:muslim500-edna-adan-ismail}

## The Edna Adan Hospital
In 2002 she sold her property, car and retirement savings and founded, on the ruins of a hospital destroyed in the civil war, the Edna Adan Maternity Hospital in Hargeisa, with a declared aim: to train a generation of midwives and cut maternal and infant mortality in one of the most dangerous regions of the world for motherhood. Over two decades the hospital became a non-profit teaching centre graduating hundreds of midwives and nurses and treating tens of thousands of patients yearly. {cite:wikipedia-edna-adan-ismail}

## The anti-FGM campaign
Since the 1970s she has led awareness and advocacy campaigns against female genital cutting (FGM), challenging tradition within her own society, and won global renown for the fight; her long record tracks an institutional shift from individual advocacy to local laws combating the practice in several regions. {cite:wikipedia-edna-adan-ismail}

## Recognition and legacy
She received the 2023 Templeton Prize for her humanitarian work, holds UN and rights prizes and honorary doctorates, and wrote the memoir A Woman of Firsts / «امرأة من الصوماليلاند». Her life is read as the most complete humanitarian medical biography in the Horn of Africa: a midwife who became a diplomat who became the builder of a health institution, carrying the flag of ending FGM before it became a global cause. {cite:wikipedia-edna-adan-ismail}
