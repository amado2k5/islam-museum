## Summary
Abu'l-Hasan Tahir ibn Ahmad ibn Babashadh, the Egyptian jeweller-grammarian and author; he entered Baghdad trading in gems and learned from its scholars, then served 'Amr in the chancery — no katib's work would be forwarded until reviewed by him, with a stipend for it. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His renunciation and works
Then he renounced the world, left service, was content with God, and his house was roofed over him — kindness followed him until he died; among his works are the Muqaddima and its commentary, a commentary on the Jumal, a commentary on Ibn al-Sarraj's Usul, and drafts unfinished at his death near fifteen volumes; it is said he died falling from a roof; his origin was Daylam, and Babashadh is a Persian word meaning 'son of the good man'. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
