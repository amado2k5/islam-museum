# Ali Suavi

Ali Suavi (1839–1878) was an Ottoman journalist, educator and religious reformer, remembered as "the Turbaned Revolutionary" for the palace raid in which he died. {cite:wikipedia-ali-suavi-en}

## Teacher and preacher

Born at Istanbul on 8 December 1839, the son of a paper merchant from Çankırı, he performed the Hajj with his father at seventeen, taught at Simav and Bursa, and won fame preaching at the Şehzade Mosque in Istanbul. His articles in Muhbir and a political piece against Sultan Abdülaziz brought arrest and exile to Kastamonu in 1867. {cite:wikipedia-ali-suavi-en}{cite:wikipedia-ali-suavi-tr}

## Exile, return and the Çırağan raid

Rescued by Mustafa Fazıl Pasha, he fled to Paris and then London, where he ran Muhbir for the Young Ottomans before breaking with them and publishing his own journal Ulûm at Paris. Pardoned only in 1876, he returned to head the Galatasaray school, fell out with the ministry, and on 20 May 1878 led some 150 men into Çırağan Palace to restore Murad V; he was clubbed to death on the spot by Yedi Sekiz Hasan Pasha and buried near Yıldız Palace. {cite:wikipedia-ali-suavi-en}{cite:wikipedia-ali-suavi-tr}

## Affiliation

His affiliation is recorded as unknown. The sources present a preacher, pilgrim and reformer who took Islam as his reference and argued for reform and for the khutba in each nation's own language — practice, office and published positions — and his writings on Abu Hanifa and his translation of Ghazali are repertoire; none of it names a madhhab for him personally, and Wikidata gives only the generic value Islam. {cite:wikipedia-ali-suavi-en}{cite:wikipedia-ali-suavi-tr}{cite:wikidata-ali-suavi}
