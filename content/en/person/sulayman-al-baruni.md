# Sulayman al-Baruni

## Summary

Sulayman al-Baruni (c. 1870 – 1 May 1940) was a Libyan Ibadi scholar, poet and statesman from the Jabal Nafusa, a leading figure of Libyan resistance to the Italian invasion and afterwards a minister in Oman.{cite:wikipedia-sulayman-al-baruni-en}

## Education

Born about 1870 in the Jabal Nafusa — Wikidata and the Arabic article's categories specify Jadu — into an Ibadi scholarly family, he studied at the Zitouna in Tunis, at al-Azhar in Cairo, and in the M'zab under the Mozabite Ibadi scholar Muhammad ibn Yusuf Atfayyash.{cite:wikipedia-sulayman-al-baruni-en} In Cairo he ran a press and published the journal al-Asad al-Islami.{cite:wikipedia-sulayman-al-baruni-ar}

## Politics and war

After the Young Turk revolution of 1908 he sat in the Ottoman Chamber of Deputies for the Jabal Gharbi.{cite:wikipedia-sulayman-al-baruni-en} When Italy invaded Libya in 1911 he fought in the resistance; defeated at al-Asaba'a in March 1913, he later returned as the Ottoman-appointed wali of Tripolitania and was one of the four representatives of the Tripolitanian Republic proclaimed in November 1918.{cite:wikipedia-sulayman-al-baruni-en}

## Exile and death

Forced into final exile in 1921, he settled in Oman, where he served as finance minister, and died at Mumbai on 1 May 1940 while travelling with the Sultan of Oman.{cite:wikipedia-sulayman-al-baruni-en} His remains were brought back to Libya in 1970 and buried in Tripoli.{cite:wikipedia-sulayman-al-baruni-en}
