## Summary
Khanzada Humayun was the wife of [[person/husayn-nizam-shah-i|Husain Nizam Shah I]] of Ahmadnagar; the [[work/tarif-i-husain-shahi|Tarif-i Husain Shahi]] praises her virtues alongside his, and she was regent after his death (1565–69). After bitter clashes with Bijapur she grew unpopular, and her son [[person/murtaza-nizam-shah-i|Murtaza]], influenced by his tutor, imprisoned her in Shivner fort in 1569. Mark Zebrowski notes that few women held such political power in medieval India. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 16}

## Her images
She appears in six of the twelve Tarif paintings, in an orange sari, sharing the sultan's throne; for the author the paintings are a visual record of her rise and fall — painted in 1565 at the height of her power, her figure scratched out of the court scenes in 1569, probably at her son's order. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 17}
