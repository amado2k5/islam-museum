## Overview
Abu Ya'la Ahmad ibn 'Ali ibn al-Muthanna ibn Yahya ibn 'Isa ibn Hilal al-Tamimi al-Mawsili was the hadith scholar of [[place/mosul|Mosul]] and author of the [[work/musnad-abi-yala|*Musnad*]]. He was born on 3 Shawwal 210 AH, which made him five years older than [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]] and higher in his chains, as [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] says in the biography reproduced by the editor of the Dar al-Ma'rifa edition. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 4}

## Study and teachers
He met the senior scholars and travelled to the great cities while still young, first under the care of his father and his maternal uncle Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi al-Muthanna, then on his own initiative. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 5} He heard a great many people, listed in his *Mu'jam*, among them Ahmad ibn Mani', Ishaq ibn Abi Isra'il, Abu Khaythama Zuhayr ibn Harb, Abu al-Rabi' al-Zahrani, Shayban ibn Farrukh, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba, 'Ubayd Allah ibn 'Umar al-Qawariri, 'Ali ibn al-Ja'd, 'Ali ibn al-Madini, 'Uthman ibn Abi Shayba, Hudba ibn Khalid and [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]], and, among the people of Mosul, Ghassan ibn al-Rabi' and Mu'alla ibn Mahdi. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 5-8}

He said: "Most of what I heard in Basra I heard together with [[person/abu-zura-al-razi|Abu Zur'a]]." {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 9} Abu 'Ali the hadith master said that had Abu Ya'la not been busy studying [[person/abu-yusuf-al-qadi|Abu Yusuf's]] books with [[person/bishr-ibn-al-walid-al-kindi|Bishr ibn al-Walid]], he would have reached Sulayman ibn Harb and Abu al-Walid al-Tayalisi in Basra; al-Dhahabi adds that he made do with their companion 'Ali ibn al-Ja'd. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 9-10}

## Students
Those who narrated from him include al-Nasa'i in his *al-Kuna*, naming him after his grandfather; the hadith master Yazid ibn Muhammad al-Azdi; [[person/ibn-hibban|Abu Hatim ibn Hibban]]; Abu 'Ali al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Naysaburi; Hamza al-Kinani; [[person/al-tabarani|al-Tabarani]]; Abu Bakr al-Isma'ili; Ibn 'Adi; Ibn al-Sunni; Abu 'Amr ibn Hamdan al-Hiri and his father; Abu Bakr ibn al-Muqri'; Abu al-Shaykh; and many others. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 7-8}

## What scholars said of him
- Abu Musa al-Madini reports that the father of Ibn Manda travelled to him and told him: "I travelled to you only because the people of this age agree on your reliability and precision." {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 8}
- Al-Daraqutni said: trustworthy, reliable. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 8}
- Yazid ibn Muhammad al-Azdi wrote in his *History of Mosul* that he was truthful and forbearing, narrated much, wrote the *Musnad* and books on renunciation and softening hearts, and was intelligent, forbearing, patient and well-mannered. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 8}
- Abu 'Amr ibn Hamdan preferred him to al-Hasan ibn Sufyan, although al-Hasan's *Musnad* was bigger and his teachers higher, "because Abu Ya'la narrated for God's reward and al-Hasan ibn Sufyan narrated for gain." {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 9}
- Ibn Hibban: one of the precise, constant in guarding religion and the means of obedience; Ibn 'Adi: "I never heard a *Musnad* as it should be except Abu Ya'la's, because he narrated for God." {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 9}
- Al-Hakim: Abu 'Ali the hadith master admired his memory and precision, so that little of his hadith escaped him; and he is trustworthy and reliable. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 9}
- Al-Khalili: trustworthy by agreement, author of the *Musnad* and the *Mu'jam*, approved by the masters, who included him in their *Sahih*s; [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]]: a fine and virtuous memoriser, a good compiler, upright in what he narrates and exact in what he transmits. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 10}

## His school of law
The hadith master 'Abd al-Ghani al-Azdi said that Abu Ya'la was one of the firm and trustworthy and followed Abu Hanifa's view; al-Dhahabi comments: "Yes, because he took law from the companions of Abu Yusuf." {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 9}

## Works
The editor lists the *Musnad*, printed several times; the *Mu'jam* of his teachers; and *al-Mafarid*. Among manuscripts of the *Musnad* he names copies in Istanbul (Şehid Ali, Fatih), Fez, India and Ankara, and he notes al-Haythami's *al-Maqsad al-'Ali fi Zawa'id Abi Ya'la al-Mawsili* on its additional material ([[person/ali-ibn-abi-bakr-al-haythami|Nur al-Din al-Haythami]]). {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 10-11} Al-Azdi also credits him with books on renunciation and softening hearts. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 8}

## His judgements on narrators
The editor notes that the critics of narrators quoted his verdicts — Ibn Hajar in *Tahdhib al-Tahdhib*, al-Mizzi in *Tahdhib al-Kamal*, Ibn 'Adi and al-Dhahabi among them — such as his calling Hudba ibn Khalid trustworthy, his weakening of 'Amr ibn Malik al-Nukri, and his dismissal of Ahmad ibn Abi Nafi' al-Mawsili. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 16-17} In the editor's view he coined no term of his own in this field but used the vocabulary of those before him. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 16-17}

## Death
He lived into the year 307 AH; Abu al-Husayn ibn al-Munadi recorded the date as 14 Jumada I. Al-Dhahabi says the highest chains came to rest with him, hadith students crowded round him, and he lived ninety-seven years. {cite:musnad-abu-yala-marifa-2005 p. 11}
