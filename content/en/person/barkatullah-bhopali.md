# Barkatullah Bhopali

Abdul Hafiz Mohamed Barakatullah (7 July 1854 – 20 September 1927), known as Maulana Barkatullah Bhopali, was an Indian revolutionary and a leading figure of the anti-British Indian freedom movement {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. "Barakatullah" forms part of his full name in Wikidata {cite:wikidata-barkatullah}, and the Urdu article gives the same full name and dates {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-ur}.

## Bhopal, England and America

He was born on 7 July 1854 at Itawra mohalla in Bhopal {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. He came to England and moved in the circles of London's Indian revolutionaries; at the 1897 meetings of the Muslim Patriotic League he protested against the government's treatment of Muslims {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. In 1906 he centralised the scattered Indian revolutionary groups in America and established the Pan-Aryan Association in New York City with Samuel Joshi and others {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. In 1907 he wrote a letter in Urdu Mualla calling on Hindus and Muslims to unite, naming the two chief duties of Muslims at that time {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}.

## Professor at Tokyo, and the Ghadar Party

In February 1904 he was appointed Professor of Hindustani at the Imperial University, Tokyo {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. His appointment in Japan lasted until 31 March 1914, when it was terminated; while there he published the paper El Islam, which the British government banned in India on 22 March 1913 {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. He is said to have converted three Japanese to Islam in Tokyo in December 1911, the first Japanese to profess Islam {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. In 1913 he was among the founders of the Ghadar Party, established at San Francisco {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}.

## Prime minister at Kabul, and final years

During the First World War he reached Kabul, and on 1 December 1915 the Provisional Government of India was proclaimed there with Raja Mahendra Pratap as president and Barkatullah as its first prime minister {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. He travelled on against the British alliance and in later years continued his writing and organising from exile {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. He died in San Francisco on 20 September 1927 and was buried in the Old City Cemetery at Sacramento, California {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}. In 1988 Bhopal University was renamed Barkatullah University in his memory {cite:wikipedia-barkatullah-en}.
