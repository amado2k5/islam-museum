# Shabdan Jantayev

Shabdan Jantayev (29 October 1839 – 6 April 1912), known as Shabdan Baatyr, was a Kyrgyz manap of the Sarybagysh tribe who became a military and civil leader in Russian service in Semirechye {cite:wikipedia-shabdan-jantayev-ru}.

## From Kokand hostage to Russian officer

Born in the Chong-Kemin valley, the son of the manap Jantay Karabekov, Shabdan was sent as a boy hostage to the court of the Kokand khan, fought at Uzun-Agach in 1860, and was made bek of Hazret-i Sultan (Turkestan) for his bravery in defending Tashkent {cite:wikipedia-shabdan-jantayev-ru}. After the Russian annexation of northern Kyrgyzstan he entered imperial service, rescued the Tokmak district chief Zagryazhsky from a night ambush, and was rewarded with medals, a Cossack rank equivalent to colonel in 1883, and a place among the Semirechye delegates at the coronation of Alexander III {cite:wikipedia-shabdan-jantayev-ru}. He held the Tokmak senior office from 1883 until his death.

## Mediation and schools

Shabdan is widely credited with mediating the 1876 reconciliation between the Russian authorities and Kurmanjan Datka, and with petitioning successfully for the release of more than a hundred Kyrgyz and Kazakhs condemned to death {cite:wikipedia-shabdan-jantayev-ru}. Despite his own limited schooling he promoted education among his people: he sent Kyrgyz children to the Russian grammar school at Verny, petitioned the emperor for Muslim assemblies and for madrasas and schools teaching in Kyrgyz, and in 1909 opened a private madrasa at Kemin staffed by noted Central Asian teachers {cite:wikipedia-shabdan-jantayev-ru}. He died in the Tokmak district on 6 April 1912, in poverty according to the official record {cite:wikipedia-shabdan-jantayev-ru}.
