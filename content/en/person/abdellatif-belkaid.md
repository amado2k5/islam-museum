## Summary

Shaykh Mohamed Abdellatif Belkaïd Al Idrissi Al Hassani ⁦(28 October 1937 – 26 February 2025)⁩ was the head of the Belkaïdia El-Hebria ⁦(Hibriyya)⁩ tariqah — a Sufi order of the Darqawi-Shadhili line centred on its zawiya at Sidi Maârouf in Oran — and one of Algeria's most revered living Maliki scholars at his death. {cite:algeriepresse-belkaid} {cite:muslim500-abdellatif-belkaid}

## Formation and succession

Born in Tlemcen on 28 October 1937, he received his education and formation from his father, Shaykh Mohamed Belkaïd, and his uncle Shaykh Abdelkader Benhadj, learning jurisprudence, the Prophet's biography, the religious sciences and the Arabic language. His obituaries describe his orientation as Maliki in legal school, Ash'ari in creed and Junaydi in method — the classical triad of Maghrebi Sufism — and his spiritual chain ran through his order to Sidi Muhammad al-Hibri, the Darqawa and the Shadhili masters behind them; the museum reports the lineage as the order itself records it. {cite:algeriepresse-belkaid}

He inherited leadership of the tariqah and of the zawiya at Sidi Maârouf, on the eastern edge of Oran, from his father, and under his shaykhdom it served as a living centre of dhikr, Qur'anic exegesis and moral instruction, drawing disciples from across Algeria and abroad. {cite:algeriepresse-belkaid} {cite:muslim500-abdellatif-belkaid}

## Teaching and works

His signature institution was the Dourous Mohammadia — a cycle of scholarly lectures he instituted each Ramadan, gathering scholars and preachers from Algeria and abroad for two decades, which his funeral orators credited with making the zawiya a beacon of the moderate religious reference of the Algerian state. He opened further zawiyas across the country for the teaching of Qur'an and the Islamic sciences, and according to his Muslim 500 obituary his circle of disciples reached into Algeria's officialdom; the same profile records that the Egyptian scholar Muhammad Mutawalli al-Sha'rawi sought his counsel in his later years — a detail the museum carries as the obituary's own report. {cite:algeriepresse-belkaid} {cite:muslim500-abdellatif-belkaid}

## Death and succession

He died on 26 February 2025, aged 88, and was buried at the zawiya's seat in Sidi Maârouf in a funeral attended by the president's adviser for religious affairs, the head of the High Islamic Council, the rector of Djamaa El-Djazaïr and large numbers of disciples. President Abdelmadjid Tebboune sent condolences describing him as 'a servant of Sufism and imam of the scholars'. His eldest son, Dr Mohamed Noureddine Belkaïd, was designated by the disciples to succeed him at the head of the tariqah and zawiya. {cite:algeriepresse-belkaid}

## Assessment

Belkaïd's record is the zawiya model at its most institutional: inherited charisma converted into teaching institutions, an annual scholarly gathering, and a clientele spanning ministers and villagers — the form in which Algerian Sufism survived the civil war years and entered the twenty-first century as a pillar of the official religious landscape. {cite:algeriepresse-belkaid} {cite:muslim500-abdellatif-belkaid}
