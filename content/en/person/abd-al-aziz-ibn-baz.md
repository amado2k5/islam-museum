## Summary

Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz (1912–1999) was Grand Mufti of Saudi Arabia, head of the Council of Senior Scholars and of the General Presidency for Scholarly Research and Ifta, among the most influential scholars on Saudi religious policy in the twentieth century; he lost his sight young.{cite:wikipedia-abd-al-aziz-ibn-baz}

## Life and formation

Born in Riyadh, he memorised the Quran and studied under Najd's scholars, served as judge in al-Kharj, then vice-president then president of the Islamic University of Medina, and Mufti of the Kingdom from 1993 until his death.{cite:wikipedia-abd-al-aziz-ibn-baz}

## Works and method

His best-known works are Majmu' Fatawa wa Maqalat Mutanawwi'a (30 volumes) and his radio programme Nur 'ala al-Darb, in which he answered thousands of questions. His fatwas rest on the Quran, the Sunna and the salaf's understanding, and he was known for replies against creeds, innovations and calls to tawhid.{cite:wikipedia-abd-al-aziz-ibn-baz}

## Controversy and standing

He is remembered as Saudi Arabia's highest fatwa reference and official standard; some rulings were criticised in other Islamic circles — such as permitting foreign troops during the Gulf crisis and some social rulings — the museum records both without a verdict.{cite:wikipedia-abd-al-aziz-ibn-baz}
