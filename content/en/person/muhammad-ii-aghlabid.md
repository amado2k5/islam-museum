## Summary
Abu al-Gharaniq Muhammad ibn Ahmad, Muhammad II, was the Aghlabid emir of Ifriqiya from 250 to 261 AH (864 to 875 CE), in the line of [[polity/aghlabid-emirate|the Aghlabid emirate]]. He took the emirate after his uncle Ziyadat Allah II, who died a year and six days after succeeding his own brother Ahmad {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.3 printed p.165, scan169}.
## The reign
Hasan Ibrahim writes that he was called Abu al-Gharaniq because he loved to hunt cranes, so much that he built a palace to go out to for it, at a cost of thirty thousand mithqals of gold {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.3 printed p.165, scan169}. The historian adds that nothing worth recording happened in his time except the continuing conquest of Sicily, the frequent appointment of governors to the island and the killing of some of them, whether in their wars with the Byzantines or by stirring tribal feeling among the Arabs there. Following Ibn Idhari, he places fighting between the Muslims and the Byzantines in Sicily from 255 to 259 AH, with attempts on Syracuse and other main cities of the island and of southern Italy {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.3 printed p.166, scan170}.

Ibn Idhari, as Hasan Ibrahim reports him, describes the emir as lavish in giving and good to his subjects, before pleasures overcame him {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.3 printed p.166, scan170}.
## Death
He died in Jumada I 261 AH after ruling Ifriqiya and its dependencies for ten years, five months and a half. In that time four Abbasid caliphs reigned in turn, al-Musta'in, al-Mu'tazz, al-Muhtadi and al-Mu'tamid, and each confirmed him in his post, as was the custom of the age {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.3 printed p.166, scan170}.
