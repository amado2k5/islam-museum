# Abida Parveen

## Summary

Abida Parveen (born 20 February 1954) is a Pakistani singer, composer and musician of Sufi music, as well as a painter and entrepreneur; often called 'the queen of Sufi music', she is one of the highest-paid and most decorated vocalists in Pakistan. {cite:wikipedia-abida-parvin-en} {cite:wikidata-abida-parvin}

## Early life

She was born and raised at Ali Goharabad in Larkana, Sindh, into a Sindhi Sufi family, and trained with her father, Ustad Ghulam Haider, who ran a music school; she sang with him at the shrines of Sufi saints, and he chose her over his two sons as his musical heir. Later Ustad Salamat Ali Khan of the Sham Chaurasia gharana also taught her. {cite:wikipedia-abida-parvin-en}

## Career

She sang at dargahs and urs from the early 1970s; her breakthrough came on Radio Pakistan in 1973 with the Sindhi song 'Tuhinje zulfan jay band kamand widha', she became an official Radio Pakistan singer in 1977, and in 1980 she began on 'Awaz-o-Andaz'. She sings ghazal, thumri, khayal, qawwali and above all kafi, in Urdu, Sindhi, Punjabi, Arabic and Persian. {cite:wikipedia-abida-parvin-en}

She has toured the world since 1993, her first concert abroad being at Buena Park, California; her 1989 concert at Wembley in London was broadcast by the BBC, and she has sung at the Royal Albert Hall (2010) and New York's Sufi Music Festival (2010). She has appeared on Coke Studio since 2010, including 'Chhaap Tilak' with [[person/rahat-fatih-ali-khan|Rahat Fateh Ali Khan]], and the article records her saying she learned 'Man Kunto Maula' from the [[group/sabri-brothers|Sabri Brothers]]. {cite:wikipedia-abida-parvin-en}

The article, citing the Deccan Herald (2010), says she declines offers to sing for films, Bollywood among them, on account of her Sufi faith and its commitments. {cite:wikipedia-abida-parvin-en}

## Honours

She received the [[award/pride-of-performance|Pride of Performance]] in 1984, the Sitara-e-Imtiaz in 2005, the Hilal-e-Imtiaz in 2012 and the Nishan-e-Imtiaz, Pakistan's highest civil award, on 23 March 2021, and was named a SAARC Peace Ambassador in 2017. {cite:wikipedia-abida-parvin-en}
