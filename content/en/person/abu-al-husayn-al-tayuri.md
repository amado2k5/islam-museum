## Summary
Abu al-Husayn al-Mubarak ibn Abd al-Jabbar ibn Ahmad ibn Qasim al-Sayrafi, known as Ibn al-Tuyuri, was a major hadith transmitter of Baghdad who died in 500 AH {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}.

## In the chain of Fada'il al-Sahaba
He heard Ahmad ibn Hanbal's [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]] from [[person/ibn-al-allaf-abu-tahir|Abu Tahir Ibn al-Allaf]], read aloud before him, in Dhu al-Qa'da 436; [[person/ibn-nasir-al-sulami|Abu al-Fadl Ibn Nasir]] then read it to him in Rajab 500, the year of his death {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.55} {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}. His transmission from the beginning of the book to the end of the merits of Ali is from Ibn al-Allaf, and from the beginning of the merits of Uthman to the end of the merits of Ali also from [[person/abu-ishaq-al-barmaki|Abu Ishaq al-Barmaki]], both from Ibn Malik al-Qati'i {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.54}.

## Praise from scholars
Al-Silafi said: a great, useful and scrupulous transmitter who never occupied himself with anything but hadith and gathered what no one else gathered — and he praised him at length {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}. Ibn Makula mentioned him: our friend Abu al-Husayn, known as Ibn al-Hamami (without doubling), a man of goodness, chastity and righteousness {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}. Abu Ali Ibn Sukkara said: he was a righteous shaykh, trustworthy, firm, discerning, chaste and precise; he kept company with the hafizes and trained with them, and told me he had about a thousand fascicles in the hand of [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}. The editor also quotes Ibn Nasir's *Amali* calling him "the trustworthy, truthful jurist" {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}.

## Al-Mu'taman al-Saji's accusation
Ibn al-Sam'ani said: he was a prolific, righteous, honest and truthful transmitter with sound originals, scrupulous and of good bearing, and transmission from him spread widely; al-Mu'taman al-Saji thought ill of him, accused him of lying and said so openly {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}. Ibn al-Sam'ani adds that he saw none of his trustworthy teachers agree with al-Saji, and that he asked Abd al-Wahhab al-Anmati, Ibn Nasir and others, who praised him and testified to his learning, truthfulness and trustworthiness {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}. [[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] said: a well-known, prolific, trustworthy shaykh; no hadith scholar paid attention to al-Mu'taman al-Saji's charge of lying against him {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.39}.
