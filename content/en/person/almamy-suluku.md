## Summary
Almamy Suluku (c. 1820 – 1906) was a powerful Limba ruler in northern Sierra Leone who maintained his independence as long as possible through brilliant political manoeuvring, in the words of the Sierra Leone Web biography, which agrees with Wikidata on his approximate years. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} {cite:wikidata-almamy-suluku} He was the son of Sankailay, chief of the Biriwa country, with its capital of Bumban, near modern Kamabai; as a young man he became the Kurugba, or war captain, and under his military leadership Biriwa became one of the largest kingdoms in Sierra Leone, and he was crowned Gbaku (king) over a kingdom covering almost ten percent of the Sierra Leone hinterland. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes}

## Early life
The Sierra Leone Web biography places his upbringing in the house of his father Sankailay, chief of Biriwa, and names no school and no teacher for him; the formation it records is one of war and command — he became Kurugba (war captain) as a young man, enlarged his father's realm until Biriwa was one of the largest kingdoms in Sierra Leone, and was crowned Gbaku over it. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} The text of the deleted English Wikipedia article on him, surviving in mirrors and resting on Fyle's monograph, places his birth in 1820 at Kamabai in the Biriwa country and has him succeed his father as chief of Biriwa on his father's death; the museum records that attribution in his data record without citing the mirrors as a source. {cite:wikidata-almamy-suluku} {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes}

## Career
Suluku was not satisfied with territory alone and set out to make his country wealthy as well: he fostered the trade in gold, ivory, hides and foodstuffs that passed through Bumban on the way southwest to Freetown, and gave effective police protection to the traders in his realm; his progressive rule impressed the British administration in Freetown, which sent him annual gifts throughout the 1880s. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} When Samori Touré's Mandinka forces occupied Biriwa in 1884, Suluku pretended to co-operate while sending urgent messages to the British warning of a disruption in trade if the Mandinka did not withdraw; the British accepted his arguments and persuaded the Mandinka to leave Biriwa country — so, while other Sierra Leonean kings suffered costly defeats in futile military resistance, Suluku managed to have his way through political strategy alone. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} In the 1890s, as British power increased, he pursued his own independent policy while making the British believe he was their loyal ally: he sent frequent messages of friendship to the British Governor and entertained royally every British delegation that arrived in Bumban, but did exactly as he pleased; some lower-ranking officers warned of his deception, but Freetown was convinced of his loyalty. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} When the 1898 rebellion broke out, Suluku sent warriors and weapons to Bai Bureh; but when the British complained, he sent them a letter expressing his support for their position and offering his services as mediator. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} After the Protectorate was established the British wanted to break up his kingdom into small chiefdoms, but his subjects refused to co-operate as long as the old Gbaku was still alive; and when a British official asked the very aged Suluku to name his successor under the new and tightly controlled colonial structure, the old Gbaku replied: "Suluku will never die". {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} He died in 1906, as all the sources read agree. {cite:wikidata-almamy-suluku} {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes}

## Positions and criticism
This section is read as a presentation of positions reported from the sources, not as a verdict of the museum.

The sources present Suluku as the successful counter-model to military resistance in Sierra Leone: where Bai Bureh fought a war that was ultimately lost, Suluku preserved his kingdom for decades by diplomatic deception alone — professed loyalty to Britain, secret support for its enemy, and letters of friendship in every direction. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} The museum reports that portrayal as the biography gives it, neither as praise nor as blame. His title "Almamy" is the imamate title of Futa Jallon (from al-imam), adopted by Muslim rulers across the region, but no source read states Suluku's own religion, school or order; his faith is therefore recorded as unknown in his data record, because a borrowed regnal title describes an office, not the man's creed. {cite:sierra-leone-web-heroes} {cite:wikidata-almamy-suluku} His dates themselves are approximate in every source: about 1820 to 1906. {cite:wikidata-almamy-suluku}
