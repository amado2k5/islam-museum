## Summary
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Salt ibn al-Mughallis, Abu al-Abbas al-Himmani — also called Ahmad ibn al-Salt and Ahmad ibn Atiyya — was the nephew of Jubara ibn al-Mughallis, a Baghdadi of Kufan origin. He claimed to have heard Hisham ibn Ammar in Damascus, so [[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]] gave him an entry in [[work/tarikh-dimashq|Tarikh Dimashq]]. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 373} The editor notes that [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib]] entered him in Tarikh Baghdad under three names in three places. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 373} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 374}

## His claimed teachers and his transmitters
By his own account he narrated from Thabit ibn Muhammad the ascetic, [[person/al-fadl-ibn-dukayn|Abu Nu'aym al-Fadl ibn Dukayn]], Abu Ghassan al-Nahdi, Affan ibn Muslim, Isma'il ibn Abi Uways, Ibrahim ibn al-Mundhir, [[person/abu-ubayd-al-qasim-ibn-sallam|Abu Ubayd]], [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]], his uncle Jubara ibn Mughallis, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba and others; Abu Amr ibn al-Sammak, Abu Ali ibn al-Sawwaf, Abu al-Fath al-Azdi, the judge Mukram ibn Ahmad, Ibn Miqsam, al-Ji'abi and Isa ibn Hamid al-Rukhkhaji narrated from him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 374} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 375}

## The critics' verdicts
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: a liar who forged hadith (al-Khatib, Ibn Adi, al-Daraqutni, Ibn Abi al-Fawaris, Ibn Qani', Abu Nu'aym)**: al-Khatib said he narrated from Thabit the ascetic, Abu Nu'aym and others hadiths most of which were false and of his own making, and that he related from [[person/bishr-al-hafi|Bishr ibn al-Harith]], [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]] and [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ali ibn al-Madini]] reports he collected after fabricating them, in praise of [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]]. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 375} Ibn Adi said: I saw him in 297 narrating from Thabit the ascetic, Abd al-Samad ibn al-Nu'man and other early shaykhs who had died long before he was born, and I never saw a liar with less shame; he would stay with booksellers, carry off bundles of their books and narrate what was in them from whoever's name was on the book. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 376} [[person/al-daraqutni|Al-Daraqutni]] said: abandoned, he forged hadith; Muhammad ibn Abi al-Fawaris: he used to forge; Abd al-Baqi ibn Qani': not trustworthy; and [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]]: he narrates well-known and objectionable reports from Ibn Abi Uways, al-Qa'nabi and shaykhs he never met — worthless. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 377} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 378}
:::

Al-Khatib gives a story in which Ahmad ibn Abi Khaythama told his son to write from him because he had been writing alongside them in the sessions for seventy years, and comments: I would not be surprised if this story were fabricated; its chain has several unknown men, and Ibn al-Salt's condition is too plain for doubt to arise. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 376} See [[concept/wad-al-hadith|the forging of hadith]].

## Death
Isa ibn Hamid al-Rukhkhaji said he died in Muharram 302; al-Khatib said this was an error, the correct date being the one Ibn Qani' gives — Shawwal 308 — which Ali ibn Umar al-Harbi also found in his brother's book. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 378}
