Sálim Moizuddin Abdul Ali (12 November 1896 – 20 June 1987) was an Indian ornithologist and naturalist, known as the Birdman of India. He was the first Indian to conduct systematic bird surveys across India, and his bird books popularised ornithology in the country.{cite:wikipedia-salim-ali-en} He was born in Bombay into a Sulaimani Bohra family, the ninth and youngest child of Moizuddin Abdul Ali; orphaned young, he was brought up by his maternal uncle Amiruddin Tyabji, and a yellow-throated sparrow he had shot as a boy — identified for him by W. S. Millard of the Bombay Natural History Society — turned him towards the serious study of birds.{cite:wikipedia-salim-ali-en}

After 1947 Ali became the key figure behind the Bombay Natural History Society, using his influence to win government support for it, to establish the Bharatpur bird sanctuary (now Keoladeo National Park) and to prevent the destruction of the Silent Valley in Kerala.{cite:wikipedia-salim-ali-en} His Book of Indian Birds appeared in 1941, and with Sidney Dillon Ripley he wrote the ten-volume Handbook of the Birds of India and Pakistan; his autobiography, The Fall of a Sparrow, was published in 1985.{cite:wikipedia-salim-ali-en}{cite:wikipedia-salim-ali-ur} He received the Padma Bhushan in 1958 and the Padma Vibhushan in 1976, and died in Bombay on 20 June 1987.{cite:wikipedia-salim-ali-en}{cite:wikidata-salim-ali}
