## Overview
Jahm ibn Safwan al-Samarqandi, Abu Mahraz, a client of the Banu Rasib, is described by al-Zirikli as the head of the Jahmiyya; al-Dhahabi calls him astray and an innovator who perished in the age of the junior Followers having sown great evil. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.141}

He acted as judge in the army of al-Harith ibn Surayj, who rebelled against the amirs of Khurasan, and was seized by Nasr ibn Sayyar; when Jahm asked for his life, Nasr replied, “You shall not rise against us with the Yemenis more than you have already risen,” and ordered him killed in 128 AH (745 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.141}
