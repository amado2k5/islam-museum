## Summary
Loujain al-Hathloul is a Saudi human-rights activist, best known for leading campaigns for women's right to drive and for ending male guardianship, and for paying a heavy personal price: repeated arrests and nearly a thousand days in prison before conditional release in 2021. Her case became one of the most visible Saudi human-rights files in international media. {cite:wikipedia-loujain-al-hathloul}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1989 in Jeddah, studied French at the University of British Columbia in Canada, and returned to Saudi Arabia active in the generation of the "right to drive" and "end guardianship" campaigns. {cite:wikipedia-loujain-al-hathloul}

## Campaigns and arrests
She rose to prominence in 2014 when she drove her car from the United Arab Emirates to the Saudi border in a symbolic campaign and was detained for 73 days. In 2016 she joined thousands of women in signing the petition to end male guardianship addressed to the king. In March 2018 she was detained in the UAE and deported to Saudi Arabia, arrested again in May 2018 alongside women and men activists in a wide security sweep, convicted in December 2020 on charges related to state security and seeking to change the system of government, and released in February 2021 under conditions including a five-year travel ban. Her family and rights organisations said she was subjected to harsh treatment in detention, which Saudi authorities denied — the museum reports both positions attributed. {cite:wikipedia-loujain-al-hathloul} {cite:muslim500-loujain-al-hathloul}

## Context and debate
Her case sits in what many writers call a contradiction: the driving ban on women was officially lifted in June 2018 — her principal demand — while she remained in prison for demanding it; rights organisations conclude from this that her detention was political, while the authorities present the charges as security matters. Civil and criminal cases relating to hacking and surveillance of activists were also filed by her side in France and the United States, and the sources note those proceedings remain in train. {cite:wikipedia-loujain-al-hathloul}

## Recognition
She was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in 2019 and 2020 by parliamentarians, received international rights recognition including the 2020 Václav Havel Prize nomination and a later Havel laureateship, and wide international advocacy. {cite:wikipedia-loujain-al-hathloul}

## Legacy
Her case is read as the face of Saudi Arabia's incomplete transformation: partial social opening that granted the driving she campaigned for, while prosecuting the activism that demanded it; her name remains, in human-rights literature, a symbol of the cost of demanding reform directly in a closed environment. {cite:wikipedia-loujain-al-hathloul}
