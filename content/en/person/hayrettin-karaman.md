## Summary
Hayrettin Karaman is one of the most prominent scholars of Islamic law in modern Turkey, long a professor of fiqh at the Faculty of Theology of Marmara University in Istanbul, and described in the Muslim 500 as the pre-eminent scholar of shari'a in the country. {cite:wikipedia-hayrettin-karaman}

## Life and formation
Born in Çorum in 1934 to a family originating from Erzurum, he studied Arabic and the Islamic sciences privately after primary school and entered the Konya Imam Hatip school, one of Turkey's first imam-hatip institutions, graduating in 1959 among its second cohort; during those years he served as imam and preacher in Konya's mosques. He graduated from the newly opened Istanbul Higher Islamic Institute in 1963, among its first graduates, and became an assistant in Islamic law there in 1965, submitting a thesis on ijtihad in Islamic law from the beginnings to the fourth century AH. {cite:wikipedia-hayrettin-karaman}

## Career
He taught fiqh at Izmir from 1971 and returned to Istanbul in 1975; when the higher Islamic institutes became faculties of theology he rose through the ranks to full professor, teaching at Marmara University until retiring in 2001. He published the journal Nesil between 1976 and 1980 and wrote a regular column for the daily Yeni Şafak until 2019, when he stopped for health reasons. In 2013 he was chosen for the government's "wise people" committee formed around the Kurdish peace process. He serves on the advisory boards of Turkish participation (Islamic) banks. {cite:wikipedia-hayrettin-karaman}

## Contributions and legacy
Karaman's multi-volume works include a large comparative fiqh text and studies on ijtihad and renewal; he argues that the "door of ijtihad" should never be considered closed, placing him in the reform tradition running from Cevdet Pasha through Abduh and Rashid Rida. Admirers across the Muslim world read his fatwa collections and columns for applied guidance; critics within Turkish public debate have contested some of his fatwas and his closeness to government positions — the museum notes the contest without verdict. He received the Turkish Presidential Grand Award for Culture and Arts in 2022. {cite:wikipedia-hayrettin-karaman}
