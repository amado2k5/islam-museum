## Overview

Abu Muhammad Ya'qub ibn Ishaq ibn Zayd ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Abi Ishaq al-Hadrami, a client of the Hadramis in Basra, was the leader of the reading in Basra after [[person/abu-amr-ibn-al-ala|Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala']] and the owner of the ninth of the ten readings in the order of al-Nashr. He died in 205 AH aged eighty-eight and was imam of Basra's congregational mosque for years. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 196}

## Chain

He read on Abu al-Mundhir Salam ibn Sulayman al-Tawil, Shihab ibn Shurayfa, Abu Yahya Mahdi ibn Maymun and Abu al-Ashhab Ja'far ibn Hayyan al-'Utaridi, and it was also said that he read on Abu 'Amr himself. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 196} Salam read on [[person/asim-ibn-abi-al-najud|Asim]] and on Abu 'Amr; Abu al-Ashhab read on Abu Raja' 'Imran ibn Milhan al-'Utaridi, who read on [[person/abu-musa-al-ashari|Abu Musa al-Ash'ari]], who read on the Prophet. Ibn al-Jazari calls this chain extremely sound and high. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 196}

## Standing and those who took from him

Ibn al-Jazari quotes Abu Hatim al-Sijistani that he was the most knowledgeable man he had seen in the letters, the differences between readings, their reasons, their schools and the schools of grammar, and quotes al-Dani that after Abu 'Amr most Basrans followed his choice, that the imam of the congregational mosque would read only his reading, and that the imams of that mosque were still on it in the days of al-Dani's teachers. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 197} His two well-known transmitters are [[person/ruways-muhammad-ibn-al-mutawakkil|Ruways]] and [[person/rawh-ibn-abd-al-mumin|Rawh]]. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 196}
