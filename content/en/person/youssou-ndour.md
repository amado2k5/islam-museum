## Summary
Youssou Madjiguène N'Dour is a Senegalese singer, songwriter and politician, born at Dakar on 1 October 1959, and called the "king of mbalax". {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-fr} He helped develop mbalax, Senegal's popular style; Rolling Stone described him in 2004 as "perhaps the most famous singer alive" in Senegal and much of Africa, and ranked him in 2023 at number 69 on its list of the 200 Greatest Singers of All Time. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} His worldwide audience peaked with "7 Seconds" (1994), a duet with Neneh Cherry, and his devotional album Egypt (2004), and he served as Senegal's Minister of Culture and Tourism from April 2012 to September 2013. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en}

## Career
He was born to a Serer father and a Toucouleur mother and grew up in Dakar's Médina quarter; culturally he is Wolof, and his mother, Ndeye Sokhna Mboup, was a Wolof griotte — though he was not raised in the griot tradition, which he learned instead from his siblings. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-fr} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-ar} He started performing at the age of twelve, sang with the Star Band, and in 1976, at sixteen, signed a contract to sing with Ibra Kassé's Star Band at the Miami club in Dakar, where he became a sensation. In 1978 he followed the musicians who formed Étoile de Dakar; when that group split, his side became Super Étoile de Dakar, his backing band — the French article dates his founding of the Super Étoile to 1979, and the Arabic article places the parent group in the same year. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-fr} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-ar}

By 1991 he had opened his own recording studio, and by 1995 his own record label, Jololi; his collaborators include Peter Gabriel, Paul Simon, Sting and Wyclef Jean. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} His biggest international hit was the trilingual duet "7 Seconds" with Neneh Cherry (1994); he wrote and performed the official anthem of the 1998 FIFA World Cup with Axelle Red; and his album Egypt won the Grammy Award for best contemporary world music album in 2005 — the French article dates the award to 13 February 2005 and adds two gold discs. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-fr} In 2006 he played the abolitionist Olaudah Equiano in the film Amazing Grace, and he is the subject of the films Return to Gorée (2007) and Youssou N'Dour: I Bring What I Love (2008), the latter following the recording of Egypt and its repercussions. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en}

In 2003 he founded Groupe Futurs Médias, which owns L'Observateur, one of Senegal's most widespread newspapers, the radio station RFM and, since 2010, the television channel TFM. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en}

## Works
His albums include Immigrés (1984); The Lion (1989); Set (1990); Eyes Open (1992); The Guide (Wommat) (1994); the two Joko albums (2000); Nothing's in Vain (Coono Du Réér) (2002); Egypt (2004); Alsaama Day (2007); Rokku Mi Rokka (2007), named one of Rolling Stone's top fifty albums of its year; Dakar – Kingston (2010); Fatteliku (2014); #Senegaal Rek (2016); History (2019); and Mbalax (2021). {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-ar}

His honours include the Prince Claus Award (2002), an honorary doctorate in music from Yale University (2011), a share of Sweden's Polar Music Prize (2013), given for his music and for promoting understanding between faiths, and election to the Royal Swedish Academy of Music in October 2020. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-fr} The English article also records a 2026 Grammy nomination for his album Eclairer le monde – Light the World in the Best Global Music Album category. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**His religious affiliation as the sources state it.** The English article describes him as a Mouride disciple (taalibé in Wolof), "a Muslim of the Mouride brotherhood, one of the large four Sufi orders in Senegambia", who often incorporated aspects of Islamic music and chants into his work; the French article, in different words and in a different section, states that he is "of Muslim confession, a member of the Mouride brotherhood of Senegal". {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-fr} On these two independent attestations his record carries the affiliation as sufi, documented; no source names a legal school (madhhab) for him. {cite:wikidata-youssou-ndour}

**Activism.** He was nominated a Goodwill Ambassador of the FAO on 16 October 2000, organised a concert for the release of Nelson Mandela in 1985, performed on Amnesty International's Human Rights Now! tour in 1988, worked with the United Nations and UNICEF, and started Project Joko to open internet cafés in Africa. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en} In 2003 he cancelled an upcoming American tour to deny support for the coming invasion of Iraq, saying in substance that the responsibility for disarming Iraq should rest with the United Nations and that performing in America at that time could be perceived as support for the policy. He performed at three Live 8 concerts in 2005, and in 2008 his microfinance organisation Birima was launched with the United Colors of Benetton. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en}

**Politics.** At the beginning of 2012 he announced his candidacy in the Senegalese presidential election against President Abdoulaye Wade, but was disqualified over the legitimacy of the signatures endorsing his campaign; he backed the opposition candidate Macky Sall, who won, and was appointed Minister of Culture and Tourism in April 2012. His portfolio was later modified to Tourism and Leisure; he was dismissed on 2 September 2013 and appointed instead Special Adviser to the President, with the rank of minister, tasked with promoting the country abroad. {cite:wikipedia-youssou-ndour-en}
