## Overview
Abu Mansur Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hasan ibn Hibat Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Husayn al-Dimashqi, titled Fakhr al-Din and known as Ibn Asakir, was a Shafi'i jurist. Ibn Khallikan says he was the imam of his time in his learning and religion {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 135}.

## Legal training and teaching
He studied jurisprudence under Shaykh Qutb al-Din Abu al-Ma'ali Mas'ud al-Naysaburi, kept his company for a time and benefited from it, and married his daughter; then he became independent {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 135}. He taught for a time in Jerusalem and in Damascus, and a great many people studied under him and became imams and scholars {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 135}. Ibn Khallikan describes him as sound in his fatwas {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 135}.

## Family
He was the son of the brother of the hafiz Abu al-Qasim Ali Ibn Asakir, author of Tarikh Dimashq, and Ibn Khallikan says that a number of scholars and leading men came out of their house {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 135}.

## Birth and death
Ibn Khallikan says his birth was in 550 AH by supposition, adding that the man himself wrote in his own hand that his birth was in that year; he does not name the place of birth {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 135}. He died on Wednesday, the tenth of Rajab 620 AH, in Damascus, and Ibn Khallikan says he visited his grave many times in the Sufi cemeteries outside Damascus {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 135}.
