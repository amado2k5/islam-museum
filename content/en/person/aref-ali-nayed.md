## Summary
Aref Ali Nayed is a Libyan theologian, philosopher and diplomat, among the most visible Muslim figures in interfaith relations, who combines academic training in philosophy and theology with institution-building and political work in post-2011 Libya. {cite:wikipedia-aref-ali-nayed}

## Early life and education
Born in Benghazi in 1962 and raised in Tripoli into a Wirfalla-tribe family — his father was one of Libya's leading infrastructure contractors — he studied engineering at the University of Guelph in Canada, then moved to philosophy, taking an MA in philosophy of science and a PhD in hermeneutics. He pursued Islamic philosophy and theology at the University of Toronto and then Christian theology at the Pontifical Gregorian University in Rome. {cite:wikipedia-aref-ali-nayed}

## Scholarly career
A university professor for more than twenty years, he taught at the Pontifical Institute for Arabic and Islamic Studies in Rome and at the International Institute for Islamic Thought and Civilization (ISTAC) in Malaysia, and in Libya taught theology, logic and spirituality at the restored Uthman Pasha Madrasa in Tripoli. In 2009 he founded Kalam Research & Media, a non-profit think tank honoured by the John Templeton Foundation, and the Libya Institute for Advanced Studies; his centre has helped restore historic Libyan religious institutions and supports the League of Libyan Ulema. He was one of the 138 original signatories of "A Common Word" (2007) initiated by King Abdullah II of Jordan, sits on the Templeton Foundation's board of advisors, is a senior advisor to the Cambridge Inter-Faith Programme and a senior fellow of the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute in Jordan. {cite:wikipedia-aref-ali-nayed} {cite:muslim500-aref-ali-nayed}

## Diplomatic and public work
After the 17 February 2011 revolution Nayed entered public life: he served as Libya's ambassador to the United Arab Emirates, then as special envoy of the president of the House of Representatives to the United States, the United Kingdom and the European and African Unions, founded the Ihya Libya (Reviving Libya) movement, and was an announced candidate in the postponed 2021 presidential race; his centre also owns Libya's Channel. {cite:wikipedia-aref-ali-nayed}

## Writings and legacy
His books and more than seventy articles range across comparative theology, hermeneutics and dialogue; his best-known lecture, "Ecologies of Peace" at Cambridge in 2009, argued for a "theology of compassion" at the centre of Islam. The Muslim 500 has listed him for years among the world's fifty most influential Muslims, and his project is widely read as an attempt at an institutional, academic Islamic discourse joining Libyan heritage to dialogue with the West. {cite:muslim500-aref-ali-nayed} {cite:wikipedia-aref-ali-nayed}

## In the context of Libyan thought and dialogue
Observers often note that Nayed's project joins two threads rarely combined in one figure: rigorous training in comparative theology and Western philosophy on one side, and rootedness in Libya's own institutions of Islamic learning on the other. The Uthman Pasha Madrasa where he taught dates to the seventeenth century and was among the Maliki centres of Tripoli, and its revival formed part of a wider effort to restore the old city's historic religious institutions. Internationally, his role in "A Common Word" and his participation in the Cambridge, Rome, Amman and Geneva dialogues recall the phase of institutional Muslim-Christian dialogue that followed the 2006 Regensburg lecture — a phase he helped translate from passing conferences into permanent research institutions. {cite:wikipedia-aref-ali-nayed} {cite:muslim500-aref-ali-nayed}
## Notes on the record
In the museum's record Nayed represents the "scholar-official" pattern that grew more visible in the Arab world after 2011: a multilingual university figure who builds research centres while also leading political and diplomatic work. It should be noted that assessments of his politics are disputed within Libya's plural scene, and the museum records here only the attributed facts of offices and institutions, leaving political evaluation to its readers. {cite:wikipedia-aref-ali-nayed}
