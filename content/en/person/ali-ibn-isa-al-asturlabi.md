## Summary
Ali ibn Isa al-Asturlabi was an astrologer of the caliph al-Mansur's court — the first caliph, in al-Mas'udi's account, to draw astrologers close and govern by the judgments of the stars. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 669}

## Details
In his sketch of al-Mansur, al-Mas'udi says in Muruj al-Dhahab that he was the first caliph to bring astrologers near and to act on the decrees of the stars. With him were Nawbakht the Magian astrologer — who embraced Islam at his hands and became the ancestor of the Nawbakht family — Ibrahim al-Fazari the astrologer, author of a famous ode on the stars, and Ali ibn Isa al-Asturlabi the astrologer. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 669}

Al-Mas'udi adds that al-Mansur was the first caliph for whom books were translated from the foreign tongues into Arabic: Kalila wa-Dimna, the Sindhind, the logical and other works of Aristotle, Ptolemy's Almagest, the Arithmetic, Euclid, and the rest of the ancient books from Greek, Latin, Middle Persian, Persian and Syriac. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 669}
