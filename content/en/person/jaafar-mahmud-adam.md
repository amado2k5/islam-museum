# Ja'afar Mahmud Adam

Ja'afar Mahmud Adam (12 February 1960 – 13 April 2007) was a Nigerian scholar from Daura, Katsina State, who studied at the Islamic University of Madinah under Ahmad Bamba and was influenced by the writings of Ibn Taymiyyah, Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab and Abubakar Gumi {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-en}. The English article calls him "a Nigerian Salafi Islamic scholar and a member of the Izala Society", and the Arabic article's text repeats that description on the same citation {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-en} {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-ar}.

## Preaching and teaching

Based mainly in Kano, Ja'afar became one of northern Nigeria's best-known preachers through his annual Ramadan tafsir in Maiduguri and his recorded lessons, distributed on cassettes and discs and later broadcast on radio and television {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-en}. His teaching centred on hadith, tafsir and tawhid, and on warning against practices he held to be innovations against the Sunnah {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-en} {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-ar}. Brigaglia's scholarly study of his career dates him "ca. 1961/1962" in its title, against the 1960 date of the articles and Wikidata — a variant kept in the data record's note {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-en}.

## Assassination

Ja'afar was shot dead during the dawn (Subh) prayer in his mosque in Kano on 13 April 2007 {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-en} {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-ar}. Responsibility was disputed at the time — some blamed Boko Haram, others political motives — a later book by Abu Musab al-Barnawi attributed the killing to the "Nigerian Taliban", and later research has pointed to factional violence within early Boko Haram-related groups as the likeliest explanation {cite:wikipedia-jaafar-mahmud-adam-en}.
