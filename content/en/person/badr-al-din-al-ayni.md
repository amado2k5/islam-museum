## Early life
Badr al-Din Abu Muhammad Mahmud b. Ahmad al-'Ayni was born on 17 Ramadan 762 AH (21 July 1361) at 'Aynṭab, a place between Aleppo and Antioch. He came from a family of scholars (his father was a qadi) and began his studies young, first in his birthplace and then in Aleppo. At the age of 29 he visited Damascus, Jerusalem and Cairo. In Cairo he was initiated into the mystical doctrines of Sufism and for a time entered the dervish monastery of the Barquqiyya, which had recently been founded. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 839}

## Service to the Mamluk sultans
He settled finally in Cairo, where in 801/1398-9, under al-Malik al-Zahir, he was appointed muhtasib, the inspector of the markets. He was dismissed and reappointed several times, and in 803/1400-1 obtained the much envied post of inspector of pious foundations (nazir al-ahbas). When al-Malik al-Mu'ayyad Shaykh came to the throne in 815/1412 he was disgraced, but shortly afterwards he was back in favour and again muhtasib. His knowledge of Turkish made him acceptable to the sultans al-Mu'ayyad, al-Malik al-Zahir Tatar and al-Malik al-Ashraf Barsbay. He translated al-Quduri's legal treatise into Turkish for Tatar, and read his Arabic chronicle aloud in Turkish, translating as he went, to al-Ashraf in long and frequent audiences. He also composed panegyrics for his masters, among them a life of al-Mu'ayyad and a eulogy of al-Ashraf. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 839}

Appointed chief Hanafi qadi in 829/1425-6, he held the post for twelve consecutive years. In 846/1442-3 he combined the offices of muhtasib, inspector of pious foundations and chief Hanafi qadi, a unique achievement according to his biographers. He was also professor at the Mu'ayyadiyya madrasa. He lost favour in 853/1449-50 and died two years later, on 4 Dhu l-Hijja 855 (28 December 1451). He was buried in the 'Ayniyya madrasa he had founded, where another commentator on al-Bukhari, al-Kastallani, later also found his resting place. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 839-840}

## Rivalries and works
His life shows the relations of the scholarly class with the Mamluk sultans. He was in contact, though on bad terms, with two of the outstanding Muslim scholars of his period, al-Maqrizi and the Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani. He succeeded al-Maqrizi as muhtasib, which earned him Maqrizi's hatred, and he carried on a sharp dispute with Ibn Hajar over his commentary on al-Bukhari's Sahih. His works are very numerous, mostly in Arabic and some in Turkish. The three best known are: his general history, 'Iqd al-Juman fi Tarikh Ahl al-Zaman; his commentary on the poetic examples in four commentaries on Ibn Malik's Alfiyya, al-Maqasid al-nahwiyya; and his great commentary on the Sahih of al-Bukhari, 'Umdat al-Qari li-sharh al-Bukhari, printed in Cairo in 1308 AH and in Istanbul in 1309-1310 AH. In the last, for each hadith he proceeds in order: the connexion between the hadith and the chapter heading; the isnad, its peculiarities and its authorities; the other works or chapters of the Sahih in which the hadith occurs; the literal sense; and the legal or ethical rules that can be drawn from it. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 840}

## A note on the date of death
An earlier draft card gave his death as 1453. The Encyclopaedia's date, 4 Dhu l-Hijja 855 AH (28 December 1451), is the one kept here; the 1453 date is not checked against a primary account. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 839}

## In al-Sakhawi's al-Daw' al-Lami'
[[person/al-sakhawi|Al-Sakhawi]] gives an entry for Mahmud ibn Ahmad ibn Musa, Badr al-Din Abu Muhammad al-Ayni, of Aleppan origin, born at Aintab, of Cairo, the Hanafi: born at Aintab in Ramadan 762, according to his own hand. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 10/131} He became market inspector of Cairo in 801, was an intimate of al-Ashraf Barsbay, reading history to him in Turkish, and became Hanafi chief judge in Rabi' al-Akhir 829. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 10/132} His works include Umdat al-Qari and his history. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 10/133} He died on the eve of Tuesday 4 Dhu al-Hijja 855. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 10/133}

## In the biography printed with 'Umdat al-Qari
The biography printed at the head of the Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya edition says his father, the judge Shihab al-Din Ahmad, was born in Aleppo in 725 AH and later became judge of 'Ayntab, where Badr al-Din was born on 17 Ramadan 762 according to Ibn Hajar and al-Sakhawi, or on the 26th according to Abu al-Mahasin; he studied law with his father and deputised for him as judge. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 5} He made the pilgrimage in 788, met 'Ala' al-Din al-Sirami, and came with him to Cairo when al-Zahir Barquq summoned al-Sirami to teach at the Barquqiyya, staying close to him. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 6}

Among the teachers it names are [[person/al-iraqi-zayn-al-din|Zayn al-Din al-'Iraqi]], with whom he heard Sahih al-Bukhari in the Citadel in 788, [[person/siraj-al-din-al-bulqini|Siraj al-Din al-Bulqini]], [[person/ali-ibn-abi-bakr-al-haythami|Nur al-Din al-Haythami]], and Taqi al-Din al-Dujawi, with whom he heard all six core books. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 6} Among his students: [[person/ibn-al-humam|Kamal al-Din Ibn al-Humam]], [[person/qasim-ibn-qutlubugha|Qasim b. Qutlubugha]], [[person/al-sakhawi|al-Sakhawi]] and [[person/ibn-taghribirdi-yusuf|Abu al-Mahasin Ibn Taghri Birdi]]; he taught hadith at the Mu'ayyadiyya for about forty years. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 8-9}

He became market inspector (muhtasib) of Cairo in Dhu al-Hijja 801, replacing [[person/al-maqrizi|al-Maqrizi]], the two alternating in the post; then supervisor of endowments under al-Mu'ayyad Shaykh; then chief Hanafi judge in Rabi' al-Akhir 829 under [[person/barsbay|al-Ashraf Barsbay]], for whom he read history in Arabic and explained it in Turkish; he lost the judgeship in 842 and the endowments in 853. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 12-14} The biography also says he gave a fatwa that Shahrukh b. Timur's vow to clothe the Ka'ba was not binding, which settled the matter. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 10} He died on the eve of Tuesday, 4 Dhu al-Hijja 855, aged ninety-three, was prayed over at al-Azhar and buried in his own madrasa near it, where he had endowed his books. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 14}

### His relation with Ibn Hajar
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: the biography printed with the edition**: says [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] was twelve years younger, that there was the rivalry usual between contemporaries, yet Ibn Hajar heard hadiths from him and counted him among his teachers; it holds that Ibn Hajar was partial against his contemporaries, citing his removal of a Friday sermon al-'Ayni had established in a mosque. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 8} {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 11} {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 13}
- **Sunni: Ibn Hajar in Inba' al-Ghumr, as the biography reports**: says that when al-'Ayni was teased in verses about the leaning minaret of the Mu'ayyadiyya, he had someone compose a two-line reply and passed it off as his own, the lines being al-Nawaji's; the biography rejects this, noting that al-Maqrizi firmly attributed the lines to al-'Ayni. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 13}
- **Sunni: al-Sakhawi, as the biography reports**: faulted al-'Ayni for the miscopyings and dropped names in his history, due to his fast pen, while saying he knew no one after his own teacher Ibn Hajar with more works; the biography quotes Taqi al-Din al-Tamimi that this is no fault given how much al-'Ayni wrote. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 10}
