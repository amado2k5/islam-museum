# Sultanbek Khodzhanov

Sultanbek Khodzhanov (10 September 1894 – 10 February 1938) was a Kazakh Soviet statesman, scholar and publicist, born in the village of Aksumbe in Syr-Darya Oblast, of the Qongrat tribe. His surname is written "Kozhanov" in the titles of the Russian and Kazakh articles and "Khodzhanov" in the Russian article's lead. Educated at a Russian-Kazakh school and at the Tashkent teachers' seminary, he was politically active from his student years in the underground youth organisation Kenes, and in 1917 in Tashkent he worked with Mustafa Shokay and others to create the Turkestan Autonomy and to publish the newspaper Birlik Tuy.{cite:wikipedia-sultanbek-khodzhanov-ru}{cite:wikipedia-sultanbek-khodzhanov-kk}

In the famine years he organised feeding points across Turkestan as a member of the central commission against hunger. At the end of 1920 he was appointed people's commissar of internal affairs of the Turkestan ASSR, and in October 1921 people's commissar of education, where he argued for teaching in the language of the local population and founded the newspaper Aq Jol. He played a leading part in the national delimitation of Central Asia, in restoring the corrected name "Kazakh" in place of the tsarist-era "Kirgiz", and in moving the Kazakh capital from Orenburg to Ak-Mechet; from November 1924 he was second secretary of the Kazakh regional party committee. Later he worked in the central party apparatus, organised a branch of the agricultural academy in Tashkent and became the first rector of the Central Asian Cotton and Irrigation Polytechnic Institute.{cite:wikipedia-sultanbek-khodzhanov-ru}

Khodzhanov was arrested on 16 July 1937, sentenced to death on 8 February 1938 and shot in Moscow's Butyrka Prison on 10 February 1938; he was posthumously rehabilitated in 1957.{cite:wikipedia-sultanbek-khodzhanov-ru}{cite:wikidata-sultanbek-khodzhanov}
