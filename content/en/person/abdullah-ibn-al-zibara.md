## Summary
[[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zibara|Abdullah ibn al-Ziba'ra]] (with kasra on the zay) ibn Qays ibn Adi al-Sahmi al-Qurashi; his mother Atika bint Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Wahb ibn Hudhafa ibn Jumah {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 875}. "He was among Quraysh's most poetic men, and was harsh on the Muslims, then became Muslim at the conquest" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 875}.

## After the conquest
Ibn Ishaq said: when the Prophet [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquered Mecca]] Hubayra ibn Abi Wahb and Abdullah ibn al-Ziba'ra fled to Najran; [[person/hassan-ibn-thabit|Hassan]] lampooned them in verses which reached Abdullah, who came and became Muslim {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 875}.

## His verse on his Islam
Of his verse when he became Muslim: he told the Prophet his tongue was "a mender of what I had split, when I was ill-starred", that he had raced Satan down the paths of error, and that today his heart believed in the Prophet Muhammad {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 875}.

## What was said of his poetry
Al-Marzubani said: he was known as Abu Sa'd — Quraysh's poet — then became Muslim and praised the Prophet, who ordered a suit for him {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 875}. Al-Zubayr said: in my view Dirar's poetry is stronger than his and less loose {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 875}.
