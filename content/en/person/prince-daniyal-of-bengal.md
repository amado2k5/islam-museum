## Overview
Prince Daniyal was a son of Sultan [[person/alauddin-husain-shah-bengal|Ala al-Din Husain Shah]] of Bengal (r. 1493-1518); Simon Digby, who first translated his inscription, identified him as one of Husain Shah's eighteen sons. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 130}

## His foundations
In his father's reign he built a congregational mosque at Gaur, dated 10 Dhu al-Hijja AH 905 (7 July 1500) on its [[object/dedicatory-inscription-of-prince-daniyal-gaur|dedication slab]], now in the Metropolitan Museum. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 129} {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 130} His name also appears on a tomb at Monghyr dated 1497. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 130} The title of Digby's article listed in the catalogue's references, "The Fate of Daniyal", points to scholarly interest in his career. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 130}
