## Summary
[[person/durra-bint-abi-lahab|Durra]] bint [[person/abu-lahab|Abi Lahab]] ibn Abd al-Muttalib al-Hashimiyya, the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]]'s cousin. She became Muslim and emigrated {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1867}.

## Her marriage
Ibn Abd al-Barr mentions she was with al-Harith ibn Nawfal ibn al-Harith ibn Abd al-Muttalib, bearing him Uqba, al-Walid and others. Ibn Sa'd said al-Harith ibn Amir ibn Nawfal ibn Abd Manaf ibn Qusayy married her and she bore him al-Walid and Abu al-Hasan; he became Muslim then was killed at Badr a disbeliever, and Dihya ibn Khalifa al-Kalbi succeeded him over her {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1867}. Al-Baladhuri mentions Zayd ibn Haritha married her — perhaps before al-Harith ibn Nawfal — and it is said Dihya al-Kalbi married her {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1868}.

## What is told of Medina's women taunting her
Ibn Abi Asim, al-Tabarani and Ibn Manda relate — through Abd al-Rahman ibn Bishr, who is weak — that Durra came to Medina an emigrant and lodged in Rafi' ibn al-Mu'alla's house; women of Banu Zurayq told her: "you are the daughter of Abu Lahab, in whom God's saying descended in [[passage/quran-111-1|al-Masad 1]]". She went to the Prophet and told him; he prayed the noon prayer with the people, sat on the pulpit and said: "people, why am I being hurt in my family? By God, my intercession reaches my kin — even Suda', Hukm and Sulhab will it reach on the Day of Resurrection" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1867}.

Ibn Manda recorded, through Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik al-Nawfali who is feeble, a similar story about Subay'a bint Abi Lahab. Ibn Hajar says: she may have had two names, or one of them a nickname, or the story may have recurred for two women; Abu Nu'aym said Durra is the right one {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1868}.

## Another hadith
Al-Daraqutni in al-Ikhwa, Ibn Adi and Ibn Manda record from Ali from Durra her saying: the Prophet said: "the living are not to be hurt by the dead" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1868}.
