## Brief
Khadija bint Khuwaylid was a wealthy merchant of Mecca who employed a young trader named Muhammad, then married him about 595 CE. When he began to preach, tradition counts her the first person who believed.

## Life
Born around 555 CE into the clan of Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza of [[group/quraysh]], Khadija ran a trading business that sent caravans to Syria and Yemen {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. Impressed by reports of the honesty of [[person/muhammad]], she employed him in her trade and later proposed marriage; the wedding is remembered as the event [[event/marriage-to-khadija]], when he was about twenty-five {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

When the Prophet returned shaken from [[event/first-revelation]], tradition tells that Khadija answered, "God will never disgrace you," and took him to her cousin [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal]], who recognized what had happened as prophecy {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. For about twenty-five years she was his only wife and the mother of his daughters, including [[person/fatima]]. She shared the hardship of [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim]] and died about April 619 CE, in the same season as her husband's protector [[person/abu-talib]] — the year remembered as [[event/year-of-sorrow]] {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
In Sunni tradition Khadija is among the most honored women: the first believer, the mother of the Prophet's daughters, and a model of trust and generosity. She was buried at al-Hajun cemetery in Mecca.

## For young visitors
Imagine being the very first person to believe a friend's astonishing story — and standing by them, with your money and your courage, for the rest of your life. That was Khadija.
