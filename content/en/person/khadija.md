## Brief
Khadija bint Khuwaylid was a wealthy Meccan merchant who employed the young trader Muhammad, and then married him around 595 CE. Muslims count her as the first to believe in his message.

## Life
Born around 555 CE into the clan of Banu Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza of [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]], she ran a trading business that sent caravans to Syria and Yemen {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. Impressed by what reached her of the truthfulness of [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]], she hired him for her trade and then proposed marriage to him. Their marriage is recorded in the event [[event/marriage-to-khadija|his marriage to Khadija]], when he was about twenty-five {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

When the Prophet returned trembling from the [[event/first-revelation|first revelation]], the account relates that Khadija said to him, "By God, God will never disgrace you," and then took him to her cousin [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa ibn Nawfal]], who explained that what had come to him was prophethood {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. For about twenty-five years she was his only wife and the mother of his daughters, among them [[person/fatima|Fatima]]. She shared the hardship of the [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|boycott of Banu Hashim]]. She died around April 619 CE, in the same season in which her husband's protector [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]] died; that year is known as the [[event/year-of-sorrow|Year of Sorrow]] {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
In Sunni tradition, Khadija is among the most honoured women: the first to believe, the mother of the Prophet's daughters, and a model of trust and generosity. She was buried in the Hajun cemetery in Mecca.

## For young visitors
Imagine being the first person to believe a friend's astonishing story, and standing beside him with your money and your courage for the rest of your life. That is what Khadija was.

## In the youth chapter of *The Sealed Nectar* (pp. 60–61)
The author describes her, quoting Ibn Ishaq, as a woman merchant of honour and wealth who hired men to work her money on terms of profit-sharing {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.60} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/187-188}. When news reached her of the truthfulness of Muhammad's words, the greatness of his trustworthiness and the nobility of his character, she offered him a journey to Syria with her goods, on better terms than she gave others, together with her servant [[person/maysara|Maysara]] ([[event/trade-journey-to-syria-for-khadija|the Syria journey]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.60}. When he returned, she saw in her goods a trustworthiness and blessing she had never seen before. The leading men of the Quraysh kept seeking her hand, but she refused. So she confided what was in her heart to [[person/nafisa-bint-munya|Nafisa bint Munya]], who went between them until the [[event/marriage-to-khadija|marriage]] was concluded two months after his return, with a bridal gift of twenty young she-camels, when she was forty and he was twenty-five {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.61} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/189-190} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p.59}. She was then the finest of her people in lineage, wealth and intellect. She was the first woman he married, and he took no other wife while she lived. All his children were from her except Ibrahim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.61} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/190-191}.

## At the beginning of revelation (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 67–68)
The Prophet returned from Hira trembling in heart, entered upon her, and asked to be wrapped up. He told her he feared for himself. She said that God would never disgrace him, because of his keeping of family ties, his bearing of others' burdens, his earning for the destitute, his hospitality to guests and his aid in the calamities of truth. Then she took him to her cousin [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa ibn Nawfal]] ([[event/first-revelation|the beginning of revelation]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.67} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/2-3}. In Ibn Hisham's account, when he was slow to return she sent messengers to seek him. When he came back and told her what had happened, she gave him good news and said she hoped he would be the prophet of this community. Then she went to Waraqa and informed him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.68} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/237-238}. She is among the foremost of the early believers who embraced Islam on the first day of the call ([[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|the secret call]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 1/50}.

## Her death (*The Sealed Nectar*, p. 116)
She died in Ramadan of the tenth year of the mission, aged sixty-five, while the Prophet was fifty. This was about two or three months after the death of [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]] ([[event/death-of-khadija|the death of Khadija]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.116}.

## For Sprouts
Khadija bint Khuwaylid was the Prophet's first wife and the first person to believe in him. She was a rich Qurashi merchant who hired the young Muhammad for her trade and then married him, and she bore his children. She stood with him through the hardest years and died around 619 CE.

## For Explorers
Khadija bint Khuwaylid ibn Asad al-Qurashiyya, one of Mecca's noblest women and its merchants, hired Muhammad to lead her trade caravan to Syria — the sira records the reports of her servant Maysara on his honesty — then proposed marriage, and they married around 595 CE. She was the first to believe in his call when the revelation came, praying with him from the very beginning. She bore his children: al-Qasim and Abd Allah (al-Tahir, al-Tayyib), Zaynab, Ruqayya, Umm Kulthum and Fatima. She stood with the Prophet through the three-year blockade in the quarter and sheltered him through the years of persecution until she died around 619 CE; the Prophet praised her as the one who believed him when people denied and supported him with her wealth. For Sunnis she is a Mother of the Believers, the first of wives and the first of the believing women.

## For Scholars
Khadija bint Khuwaylid ibn Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza al-Qurashiyya al-Asadiyya, among the noblest women and merchants of Quraysh, was born traditionally about 555 CE. She hired the young Muhammad to trade her caravan to Syria; the sira preserves Maysara her servant's reports of his honesty and goodness, and she proposed marriage — herself or through Nafisa bint Munya — and they married when she was about forty and he twenty-five on the usual accounts, bearing him six children: al-Qasim and Abd Allah (al-Tahir, al-Tayyib), Zaynab, Ruqayya, Umm Kulthum and Fatima. First to affirm his revelation: when he came to her trembling with the story of the cave of Hira, she believed and took him to her cousin Waraqa ibn Nawfal; Muslims count her the first of the believing women. She stood with the Prophet through the three-year boycott and spent her wealth on the call until it was said her fortune was spent in God, dying about 619 CE — the Year of Sorrow — some three years before the Hijra, buried in al-Hujun in Mecca. For Sunnis she is a Mother of the Believers, first of the wives, and among the best women of the worlds with Maryam, Asiya and Fatima as the hadith says; her place in the sira is bound to prophethood itself.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh sees in Khadija the model of the righteous wife who stands by her husband in hardship and does not remind him of her favours; he mentions that when the Prophet told her what he had seen in the cave she said: by God, God will never disgrace you; you bear the burden of the weak, honour the guest and help against the misfortunes of time; and she took him to [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa ibn Nawfal]], who knew of revelation and the earlier scriptures. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:03:30} He digresses to the Arab mother's counsel to her daughter on her wedding night, 'be earth to him and he will be sky to you; be a servant to him and he will be a servant to you', advises mothers not to belittle their daughter's husband in front of her, and holds that a wife wants attention and a husband wants respect. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:04:30}

## In Abla al-Kahlawi's programme 'In the Love of al-Mustafa'
[[person/abla-al-kahlawi|Abla al-Kahlawi]] says in her programme [[work/fi-hub-al-mustapha|'In the Love of al-Mustafa']] that the hijra began on the day of Khadija, 'the great woman', whose standing beside the Prophet was a gift and a choice from God, because she was a strong and tender woman who joined the two. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:05:00} She relates that when revelation came in the cave of Hira and he returned asking to be wrapped and covered, Khadija gave him good tidings and steadied him, although she had known nothing of revelation before. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:05:30}

She says that before her marriage Khadija saw in a dream the sun come out of her house and light the house, then the place, then the whole town, and went to her cousin Waraqa ibn Nawfal, who told her that a light would come from her house and thought it would be the awaited prophet; then her marriage to the Prophet took place. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:06:30} (needs_source: the speaker gives no source for this dream.) It was Khadija, she says, who after the revelation proposed that they go to Waraqa to make sure of the message. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:06:30} In the episode on the boycott she says that Khadija fell ill during the siege in the valley and died after it of a grave illness. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:08:30}

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] devotes a whole episode to Khadija. He says she is the greatest woman in Islam and that no one comes before her in his heart because no one came before her in the Prophet's heart; this is his own statement. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:02:00} He says the Prophet said he had been granted her love, and that [[person/aisha|A'isha]] said, as in the two Sahihs, that he mentioned her so often that she grew jealous. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:03:00} From the Musnad of Ahmad, through Masruq ibn al-Ajda' from A'isha, he relates that the Prophet answered her that God had not given him anyone better than Khadija, and recalled that she had supported him with her wealth when people deprived him, believed him when people called him a liar, and that God gave him children by her. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:04:30}

He dwells on her part in [[event/first-revelation|the beginning of revelation]]: the Prophet came back to her with his heart trembling, and she reassured him that God would never disgrace him, listing his qualities, keeping ties of kinship, bearing the burden of the weak, honouring guests and helping against the calamities that befall the right, and then took him to her cousin [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa ibn Nawfal]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:10:30} From this he draws the picture of the virtuous wife who knows her husband's "keys", with the parable of the railway pointsman who in truth steers the train. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep04 00:13:30}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In episodes 12 and 13 of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']] [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] tells the life of Khadija, saying that before Islam she was called al-Tahira, "the Pure" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:01:30}. He lists her firsts: the very first to believe in the Prophet, the first to pray behind him, the first to receive greetings from her Lord through Gabriel, his first wife and the mother of all his children except [[person/ibrahim-ibn-muhammad|Ibrahim]], the first woman into whose grave he descended, and the first of his wives given glad tidings of Paradise; her house, he says, was the best in Mecca after the Ka'ba {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:02:30}. He gives her lineage as Khadija bint Khuwaylid ibn Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza ibn Qusayy ibn Kilab, of Quraysh and of Banu Asad, Umm al-Qasim {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:03:30}. He says the Prophet loved none of his wives as he loved her, and that [[person/aisha|Aisha]] said, in a report he attributes to al-Bukhari, that she was jealous of no woman as she was of Khadija, because the Prophet spoke of her so often {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:04:00}; she believed in him when people disbelieved, gave to him when people withheld, supported him with her person and her wealth, and endured with him until she died {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:04:30}. A sign of her standing with him, he says, is that he took no other wife and no slave-woman during her life, although his milieu traded in slave-women, and when she died he grieved and stayed unmarried for a year {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:05:00}.

He relates that before her marriage she dreamt that the sun fell from the sky of Mecca and came to rest in her house, filling it with light, which then spread across the land; she told it to her cousin [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa ibn Nawfal]], who interpreted it as the light of prophethood entering her house {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:06:00}; he describes Waraqa as a scholar of the People of the Book who had become a Christian and was deeply read in the Gospel and the Torah {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:06:30}. In his account she was a merchant of standing and wealth who gave men her capital to trade with; hearing of [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]]'s honesty and truthfulness she offered him a partnership, and he went to Syria with her servant [[person/maysara|Maysara]] and came back with a profit such as she had never seen {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:07:30}. Maysara told her what he had seen of his truthfulness and trustworthiness and she was drawn to him; she spoke to her friend [[person/nafisa-bint-munya|Nafisa]], who went to Muhammad and asked what kept him from marrying; he said he lacked the means, she proposed Khadija, and he agreed if Khadija would {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:08:30}. He spoke to his uncles [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]] and [[person/hamza|Hamza]], who asked for her hand from her uncle, and Abu Talib gave the marriage address, taking pride in Quraysh's descent from Abraham and Ishmael and their custody of the House, and saying that his nephew outweighed any man he was weighed against in goodness, merit, honour and intellect {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:09:30} ([[event/marriage-to-khadija|the marriage]]). He says she married him at forty when he was twenty-five, having been married twice before and having children, so that she was fifteen years his senior {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:07:00}, the same ages The Sealed Nectar gives {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.61}. They were blessed first with [[person/al-qasim-ibn-muhammad|al-Qasim]] and then other children followed; all the Prophet's children were from her except Ibrahim, whose mother was [[person/maria-al-qibtiyya|Mariya the Copt]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:12:00}.

He says that every Ramadan the Prophet went into seclusion in the [[place/ghar-hira|cave of Hira']], on a mountain at the edge of Mecca from which the Ka'ba can be seen, and Khadija supplied him with food and drink while he worshipped according to what remained of Abraham's religion {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:12:30}. He narrates the first revelation: Gabriel commanded him to read three times as he answered that he could not read, and then the opening of Surat al-Alaq was revealed {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:13:30}. He came home trembling and asked to be wrapped up, and when he had calmed he told Khadija, who reassured him that God would never disgrace him, since he kept ties of kinship, honoured guests, carried the burdens of others, gave to the destitute and helped in genuine misfortune {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:15:00} ([[event/first-revelation|the first revelation]]). She then took him to Waraqa, who said this was the Namus that had come down to Moses, which the preacher explains as the trusted keeper of good secrets, Gabriel {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:16:30}; Waraqa wished he could be alive when his people would drive him out, told him that no one had ever brought what he brought without meeting hostility, and, in his account, died a few days later believing in him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:17:30}. He cites [[person/ibn-al-athir-izz-al-din|Izz al-Din Ibn al-Athir]] that Khadija was the first of God's creation to accept Islam by the consensus of the Muslims and that all her daughters became Muslims, and [[person/muhibb-al-din-al-tabari|al-Muhibb al-Tabari]] that her house is the finest place in Mecca after the Sacred Mosque {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:18:30}.

He says that when Quraysh insulted, abused and beat the Prophet, Khadija comforted him and steadied him, and he holds her up as a model of the loyal wife in hardship {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:19:00}. He relates that Quraysh wrote the boycott document and hung it inside the Ka'ba, forbidding trade and marriage with them, and the Prophet and the Muslims went into the [[place/shib-abi-talib|ravine of Abu Talib]] for three years, eating the leaves of trees and binding stones to their bellies {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:19:30} ([[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|the boycott]]), until termites ate the document except for the words "In Your name, O God"; the Prophet told his uncle, Quraysh found it as he had said, and the siege was lifted {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:21:00}. Khadija left the ravine ill and exhausted, past sixty by his count, she who had once been a wealthy merchant, and died soon after; Abu Talib also died soon after leaving it, and the Prophet grieved for them both {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:22:00}; some Muslims called the year of her death the [[event/year-of-sorrow|Year of Sorrow]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:05:00}. He says she endured with him for ten years from his mission, raised his children and was his true support {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:23:00}, and was buried at al-Hajun in Mecca; the Prophet went down into her grave first and lay in it, then prayed over her, lowered her in, covered her with earth and shed tears {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep12 00:23:30} ([[event/death-of-khadija|her death]]).

In episode 13 he relates, in a report he attributes to the Sunan of al-Nasa'i from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]] and whose chain he calls hasan sahih, that [[person/jibril|Gabriel]] came to the Prophet while Khadija was with him and told him to convey to her greetings from her Lord and from Gabriel, and to give her glad tidings of a house in Paradise made of qasab, with no clamour in it and no toil {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:03:00}; in one version he mentions, Gabriel said she was coming with a vessel of food or drink {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:06:30}. He says she replied that God is Himself Peace, and returned the greeting to Gabriel and to the Prophet, which he takes as proof of her understanding, since she did not return a greeting to God {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:03:30}. He explains qasab as hollowed gold strung with pearls and rubies, sakhab as clamour and nasab as fatigue {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:04:00}, and draws the lesson that the reward matches the deed: because she never quarrelled with him and gave him a calm home she was rewarded with a house without clamour, and because she never wore him out with demands, with a house without toil {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:07:00}; he advises wives to let a husband home from work rest rather than greet him with complaints {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:07:30}. He cites the hadith of the best women of Paradise, which he attributes to Ahmad and al-Tabarani from Ibn Abbas with a chain he calls sound: Khadija bint Khuwaylid, [[person/fatima|Fatima]] bint Muhammad, [[person/asiya|Asiya]] bint Muzahim the wife of Pharaoh, and [[person/maryam|Maryam]] daughter of Imran {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:13:00}.

He relates from Aisha, in a report he attributes to Sahih Muslim, that she was jealous of none of the Prophet's wives except Khadija, whom she never met, and that whenever the Prophet slaughtered a sheep he sent some of it to Khadija's friends; when she once provoked him by speaking of Khadija, he said he had been granted her love {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:20:00}. He comments that love is a gift from God that cannot be bought with money or rank {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:20:30}. In a report he attributes to Ahmad with a chain he calls hasan, Aisha, jealous at how often he praised Khadija, said God had given him better than her, and he replied that God had not given him better: she believed in him when people disbelieved, gave to him when they withheld, consoled him with her person and her wealth, and through her alone he was given children {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:21:30}. He relates that [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima al-Sa'diyya]], the Prophet's wet-nurse, came to him complaining of hardship; he spoke to Khadija, who gave her forty sheep, a camel to carry water and provisions for her journey home {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:22:30}. He closes by repeating that she was the first of his wives given glad tidings of Paradise, the first to believe in him and pray behind him, and that he loved none of his wives as he loved her {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep13 00:24:30}.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the sixteenth and seventeenth episodes of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]] to Khadija bint Khuwaylid, "al-Tahira", Mother of the Believers and mother of the Prophet's children. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:01:00} He says she had married twice before, both husbands dying, and that her son by Abu Hala was Hind ibn Abi Hala. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:05:30} She was noble, beautiful and wealthy, and, hearing of the young Muhammad's honesty, asked him to trade for her; he went to al-Sham with her servant Maysara, sold there, bought Syrian goods to sell in Mecca, and brought her all the profit. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:08:30} Maysara told her of his manners and of a cloud that shaded them on the road. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:09:30}

A woman named Nafisa carried the proposal; Khadija hesitated over their ages, she forty and he twenty-five, and he had not married for lack of means. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:11:30} He praises her for proposing herself, and urges families to seek suitable husbands for their daughters. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:14:00} They married before the mission and had six children: al-Qasim, Abd Allah, Zaynab, Ruqayya, Umm Kulthum and Fatima. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:15:30} She supported his retreats in the cave of Hira' and sent him food. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:17:00} At the first revelation he was forty and she fifty-five; he came home shaken asking to be covered, she covered him and let him calm before asking, then reassured him and took him to her cousin Waraqa ibn Nawfal. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep16 00:28:30}

In the seventeenth episode he counts fifteen years of marriage before the mission and ten after, twenty-five years in which she was his only wife. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:01:00} He relates Gabriel bringing her God's greeting and the promise of a house in Paradise, and says she was the first human being to believe, at once and without questions. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:05:00} Her household followed: Ali, Zayd ibn Haritha, Hind ibn Abi Hala, her daughters and Umm Ayman. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:06:00} He holds that she handed her wealth to managers so that the Prophet could give all his time to his call. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:10:00} During the boycott in the valley of Abu Talib she, though not of Banu Hashim, chose to share it, and shared out among the Muslims the food her nephew Hakim smuggled in. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:13:30} He recalls the Prophet's lasting love: his emotion on hearing her sister Hala's voice, his gifts to her friends, and Aisha's words that she was jealous of no woman as of Khadija, who had died three years before he married her. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:19:00} He says she died only three days after Abu Talib, in the tenth year of the mission, and that the year was called the Year of Sorrow. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep17 00:20:00}
