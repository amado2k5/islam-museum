## Summary

Ghazi al-Gosaibi (1940–2010) was a Saudi man of letters and statesman who joined satirical fiction and modernist poetry to the highest ministerial and diplomatic posts, becoming the emblem of the "intellectual-minister" in the Gulf.{cite:wikipedia-ghazi-al-gosaibi}

## Life and career

Born in al-Ahsa, he graduated in law from Cairo University and took a master's and doctorate in international relations from Southern California and London. He held the industry and electricity ministry (1976), then health, then labour, and served as Saudi ambassador to Bahrain (1984–1992) and Britain (1992–2002).{cite:wikipedia-ghazi-al-gosaibi}

## Literary work

His novel An Apartment Called Freedom (1994) is political satire about Gulf youths in Cairo; Al-'Usfuriyya critiques the mentality of corruption; Saba' is a story collection; his poetry includes collections such as Poems from the Pearl Islands. His memoir A Life in Administration documents Gulf civil service.{cite:wikipedia-ghazi-al-gosaibi}

## Standing

He is remembered as a poet-minister who joined official office to a free pen; his poem "Hatta Takuna Filastiniyyan" drew public discussion after replying to the Israeli government. He died in 2010 and remains a reference in Gulf literature and administration.{cite:wikipedia-ghazi-al-gosaibi}
