# Rafi Ahmed Kidwai

Rafi Ahmed Kidwai (18 February 1894 – 24 October 1954) was an Indian politician, a Congress man who served as a minister in the provincial government of the United Provinces and, after independence, as the first Minister of Communications of the Republic of India {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}. The Urdu article gives the same dates and a fuller account of his family {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-ur}, and Wikidata confirms his dates and his Barabanki birthplace {cite:wikidata-kidwai}.

## Early life and nationalist politics

He was born on 18 February 1894 at the village of Masauli in Barabanki district, the eldest of five brothers; he studied at the Muslim Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}{cite:wikipedia-kidwai-ur}. He joined the Khilafat and non-cooperation movements from 1920, was arrested in the struggles of the early 1930s, and served as the United Provinces' minister for revenue and jails in the 1937 provincial government {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}. In April 1946 he became Home Minister of the United Provinces {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}.

## Union minister

From 15 August 1947 to 15 April 1952 he served as the first Union Minister of Communications in Jawaharlal Nehru's government {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}. From 13 May 1952 he held the food and agriculture portfolio, through the years of food scarcity and rationing {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}. He was a member of the Constituent Assembly and a Member of Parliament for Bahraich {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}. A memoir, Rafi Ahmed Kidwai: His Life and Work, was written by Ajit Prasad Jain in 1965 {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}.

## His affiliation, as the sources state it

The sources place him in Muslim public life — educated at the Anglo-Oriental College, active in the Khilafat movement, filed by the English article under Indian Muslims and Muslim socialists {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}, and described by the Urdu article as sometimes called an Islamic socialist {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-ur} — but no source names a denomination or legal school for him: the English officeholder infobox carries no religion field, and Wikidata gives Islam only {cite:wikidata-kidwai}. An ideology and movement participation are not school attestations, so the museum's data record leaves his school unrecorded under the two-source rule, the plain Khan configuration.

He died in office at Delhi on 24 October 1954 {cite:wikipedia-kidwai-en}.
