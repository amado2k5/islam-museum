## Overview
Abu al-Sa'adat Hibat Allah ibn Ali, known as Ibn al-Shajari. He died in 572 AH, and the conversion to the Gregorian calendar is derived. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 597}

He wrote *al-Amali* (Dictations), which *Kashf al-Zunun* says covers five branches of literature in eight volumes. See [[work/amali-ibn-al-shajari|Amali Ibn al-Shajari]]. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 597}

## In al-Tanahi's study
- He was al-Sharif Diya' al-Din Abu al-Sa'adat Hibat Allah ibn 'Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Hamza, whose lineage goes back to al-Hasan ibn 'Ali ibn Abi Talib. The name "al-Shajari" is explained in two ways. Yaqut takes it from the Shajari family on his mother's side; Ibn Khallikan takes it from Shajara, a village in the district of Medina, or from an ancestor named Shajara. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 15}
- He was born in Baghdad in Ramadan 450 AH and died there in Ramadan 542 AH. He was buried the next day in his house in al-Karkh, and the preacher Abu al-Hasan 'Ali ibn al-Husayn al-Ghaznawi led the funeral prayer. His father was marshal (naqib) of the Talibids in al-Karkh; Ibn 'Inaba says his line died out. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 16}
- He lived in Baghdad under Seljuk rule, through the caliphates from al-Qa'im to al-Muqtafi. He studied lexicon and literature with al-Khatib al-Tabrizi, and taught Ibn al-Khashshab and Abu al-Barakat al-Anbari; Abu Sa'd al-Sam'ani read with him part of Tha'lab's Amali. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 17-19}
- Abu al-Barakat al-Anbari relates that when [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] came to Baghdad on pilgrimage, Ibn al-Shajari called to welcome him and recited verses in his praise. Al-Zamakhshari answered with the report of Zayd al-Khayl. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 19}
- His best-known book is [[work/amali-ibn-al-shajari|the Amali]], in eighty-four sessions; his editor considers him a pure Basran in grammar. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 8} {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 189}
