## Overview
Nasr ibn Asim al-Du'ili, or, as some say, al-Laythi; according to those who dissent from the school of Abu'l-Aswad it was he who laid down grammar; he was among the Qur'an readers and eloquent men, and Abu Amr ibn al-Ala and others took from him. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 75}

## On the dotting of the mushaf

Al-Zarkashi quotes al-Jahiz, in his Kitab al-Amsar, that Nasr ibn 'Asim was the first to dot the mushafs, and that he was called Nasr of the letters, and he sets beside this other accounts, among them that the first to dot the mushaf was Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali and that Ibn Sirin had a mushaf dotted for him by Yahya ibn Ya'mar. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p. 177} Al-Suyuti gives the same disagreement over who first set the vowel signs and dots: Abu al-Aswad on the order of 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, or al-Hasan al-Basri and Yahya ibn Ya'mar, or Nasr ibn 'Asim al-Laythi. {cite:itqan-suyuti-calcutta-1852 p. 868} In al-Nashr, Nasr ibn 'Asim and Yahya ibn Ya'mar read on Abu al-Aswad, and Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala' and 'Abd Allah ibn Abi Ishaq al-Hadrami read on them. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 143}
