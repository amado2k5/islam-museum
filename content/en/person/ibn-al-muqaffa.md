## Overview
Abd Allah ibn al-Muqaffa', the well-known secretary of the chanceries (dawawin). Al-Baladhuri relates from him the Persian system of the reins and the seal, and the conduct of the chanceries. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Name and upbringing
Ibn al-Nadim says that his name in Persian was Ruzbih, and that before he became a Muslim he was known as Abu Amr; after his conversion he was known as Abu Muhammad. His origin was in Juz, a town in the districts of Fars. Ibn al-Nadim says that the title al-Muqaffa' came from the fact that [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]] struck his father al-Mubarak in Basra over money he had kept back from the sultan's funds, so that his hand became contracted (taqaffa'at). {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 172}

## Writing
He first wrote for Dawud ibn Umar ibn Hubayra, and then for Isa ibn Ali in Kerman. Ibn al-Nadim describes him as a writer and poet at the height of eloquence and rhetoric. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 172}

Ibn al-Nadim relates from him that the Persian languages are five: Pahlavi, Dari, Persian, Khuzi and Syriac. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 31-32}
