## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Yaqub ibn Mujahid, Abu Abd Allah al-Ta'i al-Baghdadi, was a theologian of the circle of Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari. Al-Zirikli calls him a scholar of kalam, a Maliki, and a man of Basra, gives his death as 370 AH, 980 CE, and states no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 311}. Al-Safadi names him al-Ta'i al-Ash'ari and describes him as the theologian and companion of Shaykh Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 2 p. 35}.

## Career
Al-Zirikli says he kept the company of al-Ash'ari and settled in Baghdad, where Abu Bakr al-Baqillani read kalam under him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 311}. Al-Safadi agrees that he came to Baghdad, taught kalam there and wrote on it, and that the judge Abu Bakr al-Baqillani studied the discipline under him {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 2 p. 35}.

Al-Safadi reports from al-Khatib al-Baghdadi that more than one person told him of the man's reserve and his good religious conduct {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 2 p. 35}. In al-Zirikli's entry, the judge Iyad says he saw the man's own hand-written record of a hearing in the book of al-Asili {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 311}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists a book on the principles of jurisprudence according to the school of Malik, a treatise on creeds according to the Sunni position, and a book titled Hidayat al-Mustabsir wa-Ma'unat al-Mustansir {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 311}. Al-Safadi says only that he wrote works and names none of them {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 2 p. 35}.

## Death
Al-Zirikli gives 370 AH, 980 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 311}. Al-Safadi says he died in the decade of the seventies of the fourth century AH, approximately, without fixing the year {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 2 p. 35}.
