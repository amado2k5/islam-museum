## Summary
Muhammad bin Abdul-Rahman al-Arifi is a Saudi preacher of the generation that accompanied the new-media revolution, combining university credentials in creed with a mass-audience style that made him one of the most followed preachers in the Arab world. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-arifi} {cite:muslim500-muhammad-al-arifi}

## Early life and education
Born on 15 July 1970 in al-Quway'iyya in the Riyadh region and raised in Dammam where his father worked, he began memorising the Qur'an in secondary school and completed it at university. He joined the Faculty of Usul al-Din at Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University, taking his bachelor's in 1991, a master's in 1996 with a critical edition of Ibn Qayyim's al-Kafiya al-Shafiya, and a doctorate in 2001 on "Ibn Taymiyya's views on the Sufis, collected and studied", each with first honours. He holds Qur'anic-reading licenses from masters in Yemen and Egypt and hadith licenses from scholars including Isma'il al-Akwa' and Muhammad al-Umrani. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-arifi}

## Career
He worked as religious adviser in the Saudi armed forces' religious-affairs branch in 1992, joined the teaching faculty of King Saud University in 1993, and led prayers and sermons in several mosques before settling as preacher of al-Bawardi mosque in Riyadh from 2006. He cooperated with the Ministry of Islamic Affairs as a preacher inside and outside the kingdom, is a member of the Muslim World League and the Association of Muslim Scholars, and headed the World Union of Preachers. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-arifi}

## Style and reach
His preaching mixes storytelling with argument and the use of contemporary data, and he holds that a preacher must keep pace with the media of the age; this made him one of the most active presences on religious channels and, with more than twenty million Twitter followers by 2016, one of the most followed religious figures online before the account's suspension in 2018. He met Saudi crown prince Mohammed bin Salman in announced encounters and wrote about them on his accounts. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-arifi}

## Controversy and restrictions
Switzerland barred his entry in 2013 for five years, citing what it described as extremist views, and the British Home Office barred his return to Britain in 2014; the press attributes to him positions that drew objections, and in 2021 reports said he was under Saudi government monitoring. The museum reports these decisions attributed to their issuers without endorsement; al-Arifi himself states that he belongs to no political current and follows "the way of the righteous predecessors". {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-arifi}

## Writings and place in contemporary da'wa
His printed and recorded output is wide, ranging from accessible booklets to academic works, and his career is read as a model of Saudi da'wa's journey from cassette tape to digital platforms: he belongs to the generation that moved from the sermon and the tape to television programmes and then to Twitter, where his audience reached figures unprecedented for an Arab preacher of his time. Scholars place his discourse among the "post-Sahwa preachers" — a generation formed inside the Sahwa educational milieu that then carved a media line less engaged in politics; he himself states that he belongs to no political current. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-arifi}
