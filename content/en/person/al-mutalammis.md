## Overview
Jarir ibn Abd al-Uzza, or Abd al-Masih, of the Banu Dubay'a of Rabi'a, was a pre-Islamic poet from Bahrain, known as al-Mutalammis; he was the maternal uncle of Tarafa ibn al-Abd {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 115}. He was a drinking companion of Amr ibn Hind, king of Iraq, and then satirised him; Amr wanted to kill him, so he fled to Syria, joined the Jafnid kings and died at Busra, in the district of Hawran in Syria {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 115}. Al-A'lam puts his death about 50 years before the Hijra, about 569 CE, and gives no birth year {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 115}.

## In the proverbs
Al-Zirikli then notes that among the proverbs is the saying «more ill-omened than the letter» («ashamu min sahifa»). The text read here stops at that phrase, so nothing beyond it is written {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 115}.
