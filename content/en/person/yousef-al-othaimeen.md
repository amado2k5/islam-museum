## Summary
Yousef bin Ahmad al-Othaimeen led the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, the world's second-largest intergovernmental body after the United Nations, as its secretary-general from 2016 to 2021 — the culmination of a Saudi administrative and academic career. {cite:muslim500-yousef-al-othaimeen} {cite:wikipedia-yousef-al-othaimeen}

## Formation
Born in 1955 in al-Khubar, he studied sociology at King Saud University in Riyadh, took a master's degree at Ohio University and a doctorate in sociology at the George Washington University in the United States, then taught and worked in Saudi government service. {cite:wikipedia-yousef-al-othaimeen}

## Career
He served as Minister of Social Affairs from 2007 to 2015, overseeing labour-market and welfare programmes, and acted as an adviser in the royal court. In 2016 he became OIC secretary-general, representing 57 member states on questions from Palestine to Islamophobia and humanitarian crises, until Hissein Brahim Taha succeeded him in 2021. {cite:muslim500-yousef-al-othaimeen} {cite:wikipedia-yousef-al-othaimeen}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him in its Administration of Religious Affairs section as the public face of the OIC's diplomacy — a technocratic figure rather than a religious scholar, whose influence flowed through the institution he headed. {cite:muslim500-yousef-al-othaimeen}
