## Overview
Rifa'a ibn Badawi al-Tahtawi (around 1801–1873 CE), an Egyptian scholar whom al-Zirikli counts among the pillars of Egypt's modern renaissance {cite:al-aalam tarjama}.

## His life and education
He was born in Tahta and went to Cairo in 1223 AH, where he studied at al-Azhar. According to al-Zirikli, his lineage connects him to al-Husayn the grandson {cite:al-aalam tarjama}.

## The mission and translation
The Egyptian government sent him as imam for prayer and preacher with the mission of young men to Europe. He studied French and translated works on geography and history. When he returned he was made head of translation at the medical school, and he founded the newspaper "al-Waqai' al-Misriyya" {cite:al-aalam tarjama}.

## His works
al-Zirikli names among his books, as translator, "Qala'id al-Mafakhir fi Gharib Awa'id al-Awa'il wa al-Awakhir"; "al-Murshid al-Amin fi Tarbiyat al-Banat wa al-Banin"; "Ghayat al-Ijaz" on the prophetic biography; "Anwar Tawfiq al-Jalil" on the history of Egypt; and an Arabic version of the French civil code {cite:al-aalam tarjama}.
