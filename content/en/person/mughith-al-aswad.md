## Summary
Mughith al-Aswad was a preacher of the finest counsel and of the most binding reminders. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 502, sequence 503, on page 143. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/143}

## Reminders of graves, Paradise and Fire
A man of the Quraysh relates that Mughith al-Aswad used to say: visit the graves each day in your thought, picture each day the comprehensive good of Paradise with your minds, look at where the two groups go, to Paradise or to Hell, with your resolve, and let your hearts and bodies feel the mention of the Fire, its hooks and its layers. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/143}


## A second notice: the monk at the monastery
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a second notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 529 (the scan prints it as "5٣a"), sequence 530, on page 160. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/160}

Yusuf ibn al-Hakam al-Raqqi relates from Fayyad ibn Muhammad ibn Sinan, who said: Mughith al-Aswad, one of the best clients of the Banu Umayya, told me that a monk at the monastery of the creatures said: why do I see you with so much grief? I said: my absence has been long, my clothes are worn out, and travel is very hard on me. He said: to God we belong and to Him we return; I thought you were one of God's agents on His earth. I asked what he had found amiss, and he said: I thought your grief was for yourself, but you grieve for others. Do you not know that the seeker's grief over himself is renewed through the nights and the days; the hours of his joy are at the hours of his lapses; he weeps all his days and grieves, and has no rest on earth? He then wept until he fainted. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/160}
