## Summary
Indonesian hadith scholar (1868–1920) from Java, settled in Mecca where he taught in the Sacred Mosque. A leading authority of isnad in Southeast Asia; his works include Manhaj Dhawi al-Nazar, a commentary on Tirmidhi's versified hadith text, Kifayat al-Mustafid and Mawhibat Dhi al-Fadl. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-mahfuz-al-tarmasi}

## Life and career

Muhammad Mahfuz ibn Abdullah al-Tarmasi was born in 1868 in Tremas, Central Java, into a learned family, and travelled young to Mecca where he studied under its hadith scholars and Shafi'i jurists. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-mahfuz-al-tarmasi}

He was taught by the shaykhs of the Haram, such as Sayyid Ahmad Dahlan and Shaykh Muhammad Abbas al-Maliki, until he excelled in isnad and narrator criticism, and was appointed a teacher in the Sacred Mosque. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-mahfuz-al-tarmasi}

## Works

His works include Manhaj Dhawi al-Nazar fi Sharh Manzumat 'Ilm al-Athar, a commentary on Tirmidhi's versified text in hadith science, which became a reference in the Haram school and in Southeast Asia. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-mahfuz-al-tarmasi}

Others are Kifayat al-Mustafid, Mawhibat Dhi al-Fadl — a commentary on the Alfiyya — and treatises on ijazas, isnads and textual questions. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-mahfuz-al-tarmasi}

## Legacy

Al-Tarmasi is remembered as the link between the hadith school of the Haram and the Javanese Muslim world: Indonesia's greatest scholars studied under him and carried his isnad to the pesantren schools, including men who later founded Nahdlatul Ulama. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-mahfuz-al-tarmasi}

He died in Mecca in 1920 and was buried there; his books are still read in Indonesian and Malaysian schools, and he is counted among the most famous Indonesian scholars of the two sanctuaries. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-mahfuz-al-tarmasi}
