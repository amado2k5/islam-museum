## Overview
Qutb al-Din al-Razi, known as al-Qutb al-Tahtani, was one of the leading authorities of the rational sciences. Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina enters him as Mahmud ibn Muhammad al-Razi and notes that some say his name was Muhammad, which Ibn Kathir, Ibn Rafi' and Ibn Habib held, while al-Asnawi held the first {cite:durar-kamina-hyderabad-1972 vol. 6 p. 99}. Ibn Hajar's earlier cross-reference reads "Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Qutb al-Tahtani, who comes under Mahmud" {cite:durar-kamina-hyderabad-1972 vol. 5 p. 520}.

## Learning and works
Ibn Hajar records that he took from al-Adud and others, came to Damascus and wrote a commentary on al-Hawi, a gloss on al-Kashshaf, and commentaries on al-Matali' and al-Isharat {cite:durar-kamina-hyderabad-1972 vol. 6 p. 99}. He quotes al-Asnawi that he had many sciences and Ibn Kathir that he was the foremost of the theologians in logic and the sciences of the ancients, delicate in expression, weak of sight, and a man of means {cite:durar-kamina-hyderabad-1972 vol. 6 p. 99}.

## His question to al-Subki
Ibn Hajar says he saw a question that al-Qutb put to [[person/taqi-al-din-al-subki|Taqi al-Din al-Subki]] about the hadith "every child is born on the fitra" and al-Subki's reply to what he found difficult; al-Qutb rebutted that reply with great thoroughness. Al-Subki answered him in turn, spoke freely against him, charged him with failing to grasp the aims of the Law and with clinging to the letter of the rules of logic, and went far in blaming him, as Ibn Hajar relates {cite:durar-kamina-hyderabad-1972 vol. 6 p. 99}. This is a report of an exchange between two scholars; the entry does not decide between their views.

## Residence and death
He lived at the Zahiriyya in Damascus until he died there in Dhu al-Qa'da 766 AH, having passed seventy {cite:durar-kamina-hyderabad-1972 vol. 6 p. 99}. Dhu al-Qa'da 766 AH falls in July and August 1365 CE by the museum's derived conversion, matching the card's year. Ibn Hajar gives no birth year; the card's 1295 is consistent with his having passed seventy.

## Why he is called al-Tahtani
Ibn Hajar quotes al-Asnawi that he was called al-Tahtani to distinguish him from another Qutb who lived with him at the top of the school {cite:durar-kamina-hyderabad-1972 vol. 6 pp. 99-100}.
