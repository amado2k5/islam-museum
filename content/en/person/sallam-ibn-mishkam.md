## Summary
Sallam ibn Mishkam in the Sira is one of the rabbis and wealthy men of Banu al-Nadir: he used to sit with the Prophet and question him, and was killed by night at the hands of some of his own people in the accounts of Banu al-Nadir.

## In the texts
Ibn Ishaq names him among Banu al-Nadir's rabbis, their sittings with the Prophet and the story of his death: when Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf's fate reached Banu al-Nadir they sent to ask the Prophet, and he answered them and they used to sit with him; then Sallam ibn Mishkam was killed by night in his house at the hand of 'Amr ibn Jihash on the famous report, and God revealed concerning it {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## In the Sira and history
Sallam was among Medina's richest Jews and of the council of Ibn Salam — 'Abdallah ibn Salam was of his circle before his Islam; some of his own people killed him before the affair of al-Nadir, one of the kindling events that preceded it {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}.

## For younger visitors
Sallam ibn Mishkam was a Jewish rabbi of Banu al-Nadir; some of his own people killed him by night before the affair of Banu al-Nadir.

## Notes for researchers
Do not confuse him with Salman al-Farisi or with 'Abdallah ibn Salam who became Muslim; three different names with close-sounding stories — check the lineages.
