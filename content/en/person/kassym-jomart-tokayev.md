## Summary
Kassym-Jomart Kemeluly Tokayev is the second president of Kazakhstan, in office since March 2019. A career diplomat who rose through the Soviet and Kazakh foreign services to head the United Nations Office at Geneva, he inherited the presidency from Nursultan Nazarbayev and has since presented himself as the architect of a "New Kazakhstan" of controlled political reform. {cite:wikipedia-kassym-jomart-tokayev}

## Life and formation
He was born on 17 May 1953 in Alma-Ata (now Almaty), into an intelligentsia family — his father Kemel was a well-known writer of detective fiction. Educated at the Moscow State Institute of International Relations (MGIMO), he joined the Soviet foreign service and was posted to Beijing, mastering Chinese, before Kazakhstan's independence carried him into the new state's diplomacy. {cite:wikipedia-kassym-jomart-tokayev}

## Diplomatic career
Tokayev served as Kazakhstan's foreign minister three times between 1994 and 2007, and as prime minister from 1999 to 2002. He chaired the Senate from 2007, then moved to Geneva in 2011 as director-general of the UN Office there — the highest UN post reached by a diplomat from the former Soviet space — before returning to the Senate chairmanship in 2013. {cite:wikipedia-kassym-jomart-tokayev}

## Presidency
When Nazarbayev resigned in March 2019, Tokayev, as Senate chairman, assumed the presidency and won the snap election that June. The "Qantar" unrest of January 2022 — protests that began over fuel prices and turned violent — was suppressed after he requested Collective Security Treaty Organization assistance; he then dismissed Nazarbayev from the Security Council chair and stripped the former president's remaining privileges. A reformed constitution followed, and he won a new seven-year term in November 2022. {cite:wikipedia-kassym-jomart-tokayev}

## Assessments
Supporters credit him with careful de-Nazarbayevification, a more open parliament and balanced diplomacy between Russia, China and the West. Critics argue the reforms remain cosmetic, that opponents still face prosecution, and that his power rests on the same system he inherited.
