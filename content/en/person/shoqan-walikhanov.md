# Shoqan Walikhanov

## Summary
Shoqan Walikhanov (November 1835 – 10 April 1865) was a Kazakh scholar, ethnographer, historian and traveller, the first Kazakh to work inside the Russian scholarly tradition and, in the judgement of the Historical Dictionary of Kazakhstan, the father of modern Kazakh history and ethnography. A descendant of Ablai Khan, he was trained at the Omsk Cadet Corps, travelled to Issyk-Kul and in disguise to Kashgar, recorded the Kyrgyz epic Manas for the first time, and died of tuberculosis at twenty-nine. {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-en} {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-ru}

## Origins and schooling
He was born at the Kushmurun fort in November 1835, the son of Chingis (Shyngys) Walikhanov, senior sultan of his district, and he was given the Muslim name Muhammed-Qanafiya; Shoqan began as his pen name and became his official name. He began at a small school at Kuntimes, where the Russian article lists his early studies — Kazakh, Chagatai, Arabic and Persian — then lived at his grandmother Aiganym's estate at Syrymbet, and in 1847 entered the Siberian (Omsk) Cadet Corps, where he read Russian and English literature and met the exiled Dostoevsky, whose friendship and letters accompanied him for the rest of his life. {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-en} {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-ru}

## Expeditions
His first expedition, in 1855–56, took him to the Issyk-Kul region; in 1856 he made the first scholarly recording of an episode of the Manas epic. In 1857 he was called to St Petersburg and elected to the Russian Geographical Society. Then came the journey that made his European reputation: from 28 June 1858 he travelled with a merchant caravan of 43 men in disguise, crossed the Chinese border unsuspected, and spent about six months in Kashgar taking meticulous notes on its towns, bazaars, languages and customs, until rising suspicion forced the caravan out in April 1859. In St Petersburg in 1860–61 he was received as the leading Russian authority on Central Asia and decorated with the Order of St Vladimir. {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-en}

## Last years
Tuberculosis drove him from the capital in the spring of 1861. Back in the steppe he gathered materials on Kazakh judicial custom that fed his 1864 memorandum on judicial reform, accompanied Colonel Cherniaev's advance toward Aulie-Ata while unsuccessfully urging a negotiated outcome, and spent his final months in the village of Sultan Tezek — whose sultan's sister, Aisary, he married — reporting to General Kolpakovski on the Muslim revolt then spreading through the Qulja region. He died there on 10 April 1865 and was buried at Kochen-Togan. Among his writings is a study titled "On Islam in the Steppe". {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-en} {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-ru}

## Faith
His faith is recorded as unknown, flagged. He was born into a Muslim family and bore a Muslim name, and no source names a school or an order for him; Wikidata carries no religion value. The English article describes the adult Walikhanov as a staunch westernizer critical of the influence of Islam in his homeland — a documented stance, which the museum preserves — but criticism of a religion's social influence is not an attested personal renunciation, and it does not meet the museum's bar for recording unbelief. If the founder counts the stance as a departure, the reading to weigh is departure, not any school. {cite:wikipedia-walikhanov-en} {cite:wikidata-walikhanov}
