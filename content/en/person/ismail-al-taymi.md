## Overview
The great hafiz Qawam al-Sunna Abu'l-Qasim Isma'il ibn Muhammad al-Taymi al-Talhi al-Isbahani the Shafi'i; Abu Musa al-Madini called him "imam of the imams of his time, teacher of the scholars of his age and exemplar of the Sunnis of his era"; he was paralysed in Safar 434 and remained afflicted; an imam in tafsir, hadith, language and letters, knowing the texts and isnads, he dictated close to three thousand sessions at the Isfahan mosque. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## Works and death
He composed the thirty large volumes of Tafsir al-Jami', the Idah in four, the Mawdih in three, the Mu'tamad in ten, a Persian tafsir, the Targhib wa'l-tarhib, sharhs of the two Sahihs which his son completed, Dala'il al-nubuwwa and the Tadhkira in some thirty parts; the imams of Baghdad said "no better or more retentive scholar travelled to Baghdad after Ahmad ibn Hanbal"; born in 457, he died on the morning of the Festival of Sacrifice, 535. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}
