# Zeynalabdin Taghiyev

## Summary
Hajji Zeynalabdin Taghiyev (born January 1838 by the reading this record follows — 1 September 1924) was an Azerbaijani oil magnate and the most celebrated philanthropist of the Baku oil boom: a shoemaker's son who became one of the richest men in the Russian Empire and spent the fortune on theatres, schools and pipelines. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-az} His birth year is disputed in every source: the English article gives 25 January in each of 1821, 1823 or 1838, the Azerbaijani article leads with 1838 and notes 1823, and the Russian article gives "1823 or January 1838" while reporting his daughter's memoir that he lived 86 years — which points to 1838, as does Wikidata. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-az} {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-ru} {cite:wikidata-zeynalabdin-taghiyev}

## Early life and the oil fortune
He was born into the poor family of the Baku shoemaker Muhammedtaghi Taghiyev and his wife Umukhanum, in the Old City of Baku; after his mother's death he learned masonry to help feed the family, and by eighteen he was a contractor. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} In 1873, with two partners, he bought land near the oil town of Bibi-Heybat; his partners sold out to him, and in 1877 oil gushed from one of the wells, making him instantly one of the richest men in the empire. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en}

## Business
Taghiyev diversified far beyond crude oil: a textile factory — one of only twenty-eight in Russia — with a mosque, evening courses, a school for workers' children, a pharmacy, a first-aid post and a mill built for its employees at a cost of over six million gold roubles; industrial fisheries on the Caspian; and, in 1890, the Caspian Steamship Company, which he renovated into a fleet of ten steamboats. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} He sold his oil interests to the Anglo-Russian Oil Company for five million roubles while keeping shares in the successor Oleum company, and continued investing across the textile, food, construction and shipbuilding industries. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en}

## Philanthropy
In 1883 he sponsored the first Azerbaijani national theatre, later known as Taghiyev's Theatre, and paid to repair it after it was burned in 1909; in 1911 he covered the full cost of the building that became the Azerbaijan State Academic Opera and Ballet Theatre. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} Between 1898 and 1900 he spent 184,000 roubles building the first secular Muslim school for girls in the region, personally obtaining the Empress Alexandra's permission through correspondence, and he also funded an agricultural school at Mardakan (1894) and the first technical school in the Baku Governorate (1911). {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-ar} He was twice awarded the Order of Saint Stanislaus. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} His Baku mansion now houses the Azerbaijan State Museum of History. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en}

## Later life and death
After the Sovietisation of Azerbaijan in 1920 his houses and possessions were confiscated, but in recognition of his past generosity he was allowed to choose his place of residence, and he stayed at his summer cottage in the village of Mardakan. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} He died there of pneumonia on 1 September 1924; the cottage was then confiscated and his family evicted, and his widow Sona died in poverty in Baku in 1938. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} {cite:wikidata-zeynalabdin-taghiyev}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum.

**The girls' school controversy.** The Azerbaijani article recounts that conservative clergy denounced Taghiyev's girls' school as unbelief, arguing from scripture against it, while Taghiyev persisted; the school as built taught the rulings of the sharia alongside sewing, weaving, cooking and literacy in Azerbaijani and Russian. {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-az}

**His confession.** Wikidata carries a single religion value for Taghiyev — Shia Islam — but no article in any consulted language attests a branch or school for him: the English text says nothing about his religion, and the Azerbaijani, Russian and Arabic articles' religion-adjacent material concerns the institutions he funded and the controversies they caused. {cite:wikidata-zeynalabdin-taghiyev} {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-en} {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-az} {cite:wikipedia-zeynalabdin-taghiyev-ru} A lone structured-data value and a lifetime of mosque and school patronage do not meet the museum's two-source rule for attestation, so his faith is recorded unknown, with the Wikidata value preserved in his data record. {cite:wikidata-zeynalabdin-taghiyev}
