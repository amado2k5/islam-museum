## Overview
Abu Abd al-Rahman Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak al-Marwazi, client of the Banu Hanzala. Al-Shirazi says that he studied jurisprudence with Sufyan and Malik, and died at Hit, a town on the Euphrates near al-Anbar, in "around eighty and a hundred". {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p. 50} The Sufyan is not named in the text; for Malik see [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik ibn Anas]].

Al-Shirazi quotes Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi: "The imams are four: Sufyan al-Thawri, Malik, Hammad ibn Zayd and Ibn al-Mubarak." {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p. 50}

## Date of death
The date of death differs: al-Shirazi says "around eighty and a hundred", and the editor's note on Ibn Qutayba makes it 181 AH. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p. 50}

## In al-Dhahabi's Siyar
Al-Dhahabi describes him as the imam, shaykh al-Islam, scholar of his age and commander of the devout in his time: Abu 'Abd al-Rahman al-Hanzali by clientage, the Turkic then the Marwazi, the memoriser and frontier fighter, whose mother was Khwarazmian; he was born in 118 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2468}

He sought knowledge at twenty. The first shaykh he met was al-Rabi' ibn Anas of Khurasan, whom he reached in prison by a ruse and heard some forty hadiths from; he set out travelling in 141 AH, learned from the last Followers, and kept travelling until his death — seeking knowledge, campaigning, trading, spending on his brothers and equipping them for the pilgrimage. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2468}

He heard Sulayman al-Taymi, 'Asim al-Ahwal, Humayd al-Tawil, Hisham ibn 'Urwa, al-Jurayri, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, 'Abdallah ibn 'Awn, Musa ibn 'Uqba, al-Awza'i, Abu Hanifa, Ibn Jurayj, Ma'mar, al-Thawri, Shu'ba, Ibn Abi Dhi'b, Malik, al-Layth, Ibn Lahya, Hushaym, Isma'il ibn 'Ayyash, Ibn 'Uyayna and a multitude, and composed useful works; for al-Dhahabi his hadith is proof by consensus, and it stands in the musnads and the canonical collections. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2468}

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme (part 1 of 2)
Tareq al-Suwaidan dedicates two episodes to Ibn al-Mubarak and declares a personal attachment to him since his own youth {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:04:30}.
He presents him as the model of balance: 'commander of the faithful in hadith' — he says one who reaches that rank memorises over seven hundred thousand hadiths, sound and weak alike (needs_source) — ascetic, devout, jurist, mujahid and shaykh of Islam, of whom they said 'he rivalled the Companions in deeds, surpassed only by their companionship of the Prophet and their jihad with him' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:04:00}.
He tells of his father: a freedman guarding an orchard in Khurasan who gave a third of his wage in alms and never tasted its fruit because his master had not permitted him — seeing in his scrupulousness the root of his son's righteousness {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:09:00}.
He uses him to denounce racial discrimination in the ummah — a mawla who mastered Arabic until he became among its greatest poets — citing that no Arab has merit over a non-Arab except by piety {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:06:30}.
Of his life: born in 118 AH; he detected four thousand fabricated hadiths slipped among the sound ones in three days; and Sufyan al-Thawri's correction 'he is the scholar of the people of the East and the West' when someone called him 'the scholar of the people of the East' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:12:30}.
Of his generosity: he would gather his companions' hajj funds labelled with each owner's name, spend on them from his own money, and return the sums on their return, saying God had blessed their expenditure; he gave Sufyan al-Thawri ten thousand dirhams yearly; and he excused his trading — 'were it not for you I would not have traded' — so as to spend on the scholars {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:21:00}.
The story of Kufa: he saw a girl picking a dead crow from a rubbish heap during a famine, wept, ordered the caravan to divide all its money among the people of Kufa and turned back without performing hajj — then heard in a dream 'an accepted hajj, a thanked striving, a forgiven sin' (transmitted with his caveat on dreams) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:24:30}.
And the story of al-Raqqa: he entered it the day Harun al-Rashid entered it, and the people left the caliph to welcome him until sandals broke under the crowd, whereupon a woman of the palace said 'this, by God, is the king — not the one who gathers people by might and sword' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:19:30}.
He applies his quartets to him: the talent of memory and languages + a mighty will + balance between his field and worship, generosity, character and care for people {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep04 00:25:30}.
