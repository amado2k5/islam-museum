## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Ibrahim ibn Saba', Abu Ishaq, Burhan al-Din ibn al-Farkah al-Fazari: one of the senior Shafi'is, Egyptian in origin, of a Damascus family of learning, who lived from 660 to 729 AH (1262–1329 CE). He was offered the chief judgeship of Syria and declined it, devoting himself to teaching and worship, and died in Damascus. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.45}

His works include Ta'liq ala al-Tanbih in Shafi'i fiqh; Ta'liq ala Mukhtasar Ibn al-Hajib in legal theory; Ba'ith al-Nufus ila Ziyarat al-Quds al-Mahrus on visiting Jerusalem; al-A'lam bi-Fada'il al-Sham on the merits of Syria; al-Mana'ih li-Talib al-Sayd wa-l-Dhaba'ih on hunting and slaughter; and a book of his teachers of which a manuscript fragment survives in the Zahiriyya. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.45}
