Mohamed Hassan Ouazzani (1910–1978) was a Moroccan journalist and politician, born at Fez on 17 January 1910 into the sharifian Ouazzani family. He memorised the Quran and learned Arabic at a Fez Quranic school before passing to modern education, and in 1927 left for Paris, where he studied political science at the École libre des sciences politiques and attended the School of Oriental Languages and the Institute of Journalism.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-ar}{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-fr}{cite:wikidata-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani}

He came to notice early in the national cause: in 1930 he organised the protest against the Berber Dahir from the Qarawiyyin mosque, was arrested, publicly bastinadoed and imprisoned at Taza, and in Paris, Geneva and Madrid worked in journalism and Maghrebi activism alongside Shakib Arslan.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-fr} On 4 August 1933 he founded at Fez the newspaper L'Action du Peuple, the first French-language nationalist newspaper in Morocco, addressing the coloniser in his own language.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-en}{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-fr} In 1934 he co-founded the Moroccan Action Committee with [[person/allal-al-fassi|Allal al-Fassi]], then split with him in 1937, each leading his own movement; after the Meknes events of that year Ouazzani was arrested and deported to the Moroccan south — Goulmima, Assa, Tata, Tagounit and finally Itzer — until 1946.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-en}{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-fr}

On his return he founded the Democratic Independence Party at Casablanca, demanding full independence and a constitutional monarchy, published the dailies Al-Ray al-'Am, As-Siyasa and As-Saiha, and spent years in Cairo and abroad pleading Morocco's case at the United Nations and at Bandung.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-fr} After independence he became a minister of state in Hassan II's government in 1961, opposed the 1962 constitution and left the government, and was elected deputy for Ouezzane in 1963.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-en}{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-fr} In the coup attempt of 1971 he lost his right hand, learned to write with his left, and continued to campaign for democratic reform.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-en} He died at Fez on 9 September 1978 and was buried there beside his wife and his ancestors.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani-fr}{cite:wikidata-mohamed-hassan-ouazzani}
