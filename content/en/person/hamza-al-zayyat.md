## Overview
Hamza ibn Habib al-Zayyat, one of the Seven Readers, known as Abu Umara, a client of the family of Ikrima ibn Rabi' al-Taymi. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.76}.

## His life
Ibn al-Nadim reports that he would bring oil from Kufa to Hulwan, and carry cheese and walnuts from Hulwan to Kufa. He belonged to the fourth class of the people of Kufa and was a jurist. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.76}.

He died in 156 AH in the caliphate of Abu Ja'far. He wrote a book on the reading of Hamza and a book on the laws of inheritance. Among those who transmitted from him were Khalid ibn Yazid, al-Kisa'i, al-Hasan ibn Atiyya and Ubayd Allah ibn Musa al-Absi. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.76}.

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

He is Abu 'Umara Hamza ibn Habib ibn 'Umara ibn Isma'il al-Kufi al-Zayyat, imam of Kufa in the reading. He read on [[person/al-amash-sulayman-ibn-mihran|al-A'mash]] by full recitation (some say he took only the letters), on Hamran ibn A'yan, Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i, Ibn Abi Layla, Talha ibn Musarrif and Ja'far al-Sadiq, and their chains reach 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud and 'Ali ibn Abi Talib and the Prophet. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 175} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 176} He died in 156 AH on the sound view and was born in 80; he led the people in the reading in Kufa after 'Asim and al-A'mash. Ibn al-Jazari calls him reliable, great, an authority, learned in the laws of inheritance and in Arabic, a keeper of hadith, devout; he carried oil from Iraq to Hulwan and brought cheese and walnuts from there to Kufa. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 176} He quotes Abu Hanifa telling him there were two things in which he had overcome them and they would not contend with him, the Qur'an and the laws of inheritance; al-A'mash calling him, when he saw him, the learned master of the Qur'an; and Hamza himself saying he never read a letter of God's Book except by a transmitted report. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 176} His transmitters are [[person/sulaym-ibn-isa-al-hanafi|Sulaym]] and through him [[person/khallad-ibn-khalid-al-sayrafi|Khallad]] and [[person/khalaf-ibn-hisham|Khalaf]]; Ibn al-Jazari counts a hundred and twenty-one lines of transmission. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 175}
