## Upbringing and education

Abu al-Ma'ali Abd al-Malik ibn Abdallah ibn Yusuf al-Juwayni, styled Diya' al-Din, head of the Shafi'is at Nishapur; Abu Sa'd al-Sam'ani described him as "the imam of the imams absolutely, agreed upon in his imamate east and west, whose like the eyes have never seen" {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 230}. He was born in Muharram 419 — 17 February 1028 — at Bushtanikan, a village on the outskirts of Nishapur in the district of Juwayn to which his nisba refers {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 605}; the Muntazam, the Kamil and Tarikh al-Khamis give 417 instead {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 230}.

He studied fiqh under his father, the imam Abu Muhammad Abdallah ibn Yusuf al-Juwayni, until he had mastered nine of his works; his father died in Dhu al-Qa'da 438/1047 when he was twenty, and he was seated in his place to teach before completing twenty — teaching while also going out to al-Bayhaqi's madrasa, perfecting the usul under Abu al-Qasim al-Isfara'ini al-Iskaf, and living off his inheritance and his teaching stipend {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 230-231} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala j.18 pp. 2574-2575} {cite:wafayat-ayan}.

## The ordeal and the exile

When factional strife broke out and the vizier Amid al-Mulk al-Kunduri ordered the Ash'aris cursed from the pulpits of Khurasan in 445/1053, affairs fell into disorder; al-Juwayni was forced to leave Nishapur for the army camp and then Baghdad, where he kept company with the vizier Abu Nasr al-Kunduri, travelling with him and disputing the great scholars in his presence until he grew seasoned and famous {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 230} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala j.18 pp. 2574-2575}. Then the sultan's order came to seize him together with al-Qushayri and Ibn al-Muwaqqiq and exile them; al-Juwayni hid and left by the Kirman road for the Hijaz {cite:tabaqat-shafiiyya-subki pp. 271-272} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala}.

He reached the Hijaz in 450/1058 and resided at Mecca and Medina for four years, teaching, giving fatwas and gathering the paths of the school — and so received the title "Imam of the two sanctuaries" {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 605} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 310}.

## The return and the Nizamiyya

When Nizam al-Mulk's regime came to power it recalled the dispersed scholars and honoured them; al-Juwayni returned to Nishapur, and Nizam al-Mulk founded there a special madrasa for him, called the Nizamiyya like the one at Baghdad. He taught in it to the end of his days — close to thirty years unchallenged, the prayer niche, minbar, sermon and Friday admonition all conceded to him — with some three hundred men sitting before him every day, and a host of imams took fiqh from him, among them Abu Hamid al-Ghazali {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 231} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 pp. 605-606} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala j.18 pp. 2574-2575}.

## His works

Among his works: *Nihayat al-Matlab fi Dirayat al-Madhhab* in eight volumes, *al-Irshad* in the principles of religion, *al-Risala al-Nizamiyya* (printed as *al-'Aqida al-Nizamiyya*), *al-Shamil fi Usul al-Din*, *al-Burhan fi Usul al-Fiqh*, *al-Waraqat*, *Madarik al-'Uqul* which he left unfinished, *Ghiyath al-Umam* on the imamate, *Mughith al-Khalq fi Ikhtiyar al-Ahaqq*, and *Ghunyat al-Mustarshidin* on the controversies {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 236-237} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala j.18 pp. 2575-2576} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 pp. 605-606}.

## His death

Overcome by fever he was carried to Bushtanqan for its mild air and grew weaker; he died on the night of Wednesday 25 Rabi' II 478 — 20 August 1085 — aged fifty-nine. He was carried that same night to the town and buried in his house, then transferred years later to the Husayn cemetery beside his father; his minbar in the congregational mosque was broken and the markets closed, and his nearly four hundred pupils broke their inkwells and pens and kept the mourning for a year {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 238-239} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala j.18 p. 2576} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 605}.
