## Overview
Nadia Emara is an Egyptian Islamic preacher, an expert in women's fiqh and a television presenter who studied at the Faculty of Arts, Alexandria University, in the Arabic department's Islamic studies division. {cite:wikipedia-nadia-emara-ar} No trusted source publishes a birth date for her, so this record gives none; the Egyptian Arabic (arz) Wikipedia edition's claim of 1 January 2000 is impossible for a woman who graduated in 1998 and is not used. {cite:wikipedia-nadia-emara-ar} {cite:mawdoo3-nadia-emara}

## Education and career
She took her bachelor's degree in Arabic with Islamic studies, graduating from Alexandria University in 1998, and went on to a master's and a doctorate specialising in Qur'anic exegesis (tafsir). {cite:mawdoo3-nadia-emara}

After graduating she worked at Alexandria University, teaching Arabic to non-native speakers and teaching tafsir and tajwid to non-Arabic speakers. {cite:mawdoo3-nadia-emara}

She studied under Muhammad Metwalli al-Sha'rawi — whose weekly Alexandria sessions she attended in her third university year, studying with him briefly before his death — and under Yasin Rushdi at the Al-Muwasat mosque in Alexandria. {cite:wikipedia-nadia-emara-ar} {cite:mawdoo3-nadia-emara}

## Television work
She worked for ART Teenz, presenting Anwar Ramadan (Ramadan Lights), addressed to young people and children, then moved to the Iqraa satellite channel, where she presented Fiqh al-Nisa' (Women's Fiqh), which dealt with the fiqh questions particular to women. {cite:wikipedia-nadia-emara-ar}

Her fame began with her presentation of Qulub 'Amira (Hearts Full) on the Al-Hayah television network, a religious programme concerned with women in Islam and women's fiqh, later carried by the ON channel. {cite:wikipedia-nadia-emara-ar}

## Works
Her Goodreads author page lists two books under her name: al-Qasas al-Qur'ani fi Tafsir al-Shaykh al-Sha'rawi (The Qur'anic Stories in al-Sha'rawi's Exegesis, 2017) and Tafsir Surat al-Anbiya' (Exegesis of Surat al-Anbiya'). {cite:goodreads-nadia-emara}
