## Brief
Amr ibn al-As was Mecca's wiliest diplomat — sent to argue against the Muslims, converted at the last hour of the old order, and then the commander who won Egypt for Islam.

## Life
A Qurayshi of the Sahm clan, Amr appears in the sources first as Mecca's clever envoy: he argued before [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]] for the return of the Abyssinian refugees and failed {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. Between [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|Hudaybiyya]] and the conquest he read the wind, rode to Medina and embraced Islam about 629–630 {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

The Prophet used his diplomacy — sending him to Oman — and under Umar he took command in Syria, then asked for and received the conquest of Egypt: he took Fustat by 641 and Alexandria shortly after {cite:tabari-donner-1993}. In the first civil war he sided with [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]], and at the arbitration after Siffin outmaneuvered Ali's side — a famous trick the sources narrate with grudging admiration {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He died governing Egypt about 663 {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Egypt became Islamic under his hand — and stayed so. Politically he is the tradition's emblem of the brilliant pragmatist: the man who always landed on the winning side, including, finally, the caliphate's.

## For young visitors
Amr was so clever that he was sent to argue against the Muslims — then he joined them, conquered Egypt, and stayed its governor for the rest of his life.
