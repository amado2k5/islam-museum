## Overview
Idris ibn Ali ibn Abdallah ibn al-Hasan ibn Hamza, Abu Musa, Imad al-Din, was one of the sharifs and amirs of Yemen and was of the people of Sanaa. Al-Zirikli describes him as a horseman and man of letters who was learned in history {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 280}. He gives his death as 714 AH, 1314 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 280}.

## Office and standing
He took the emirate of al-Qahma in 699 AH. He enjoyed favour with al-Mu'ayyad al-Rasuli, the ruler of Yemen, and was put forward for the imamate of the Zaydis {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 280}.

## His history
He abridged the history of Ibn al-Athir and added to it the reports of Iraq, Egypt and Syria down to 713 AH and the reports of Yemen down to 714 AH, calling the work Kanz al-Akhyar fi Ma'rifat al-Siyar wa-l-Akhbar; al-Zirikli notes that it survives in manuscript {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 280}.
