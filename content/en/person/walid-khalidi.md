Walid Khalidi (16 July 1925 – 8 March 2026) was a Palestinian historian, born in Jerusalem, a son of Ahmad Samih Khalidi, dean of the Arab College.{cite:wikipedia-walid-khalidi-en} He took a BA from the University of London in 1945 and an MLitt from Oxford in 1951, taught at Oxford until resigning in protest at the Suez invasion of 1956, and then taught political studies at the American University of Beirut until 1982, afterwards holding research posts at Harvard.{cite:wikipedia-walid-khalidi-en}{cite:arab-center-walid-khalidi-obituary-2026}

In December 1963 he co-founded the Institute for Palestine Studies in Beirut, serving as its general secretary until 2016; the Institute's Journal of Palestine Studies became the field's flagship.{cite:wikipedia-walid-khalidi-en} His Before Their Diaspora (1984) assembled a photographic record of Palestinian society before 1948, and All That Remains (1992), which he edited, documented more than 400 Palestinian villages depopulated that year — work the IPS called him "the historian of the Palestinian cause" for, and which made him the first scholar to reveal Plan Dalet.{cite:wikipedia-walid-khalidi-en} He died in Cambridge, Massachusetts, on 8 March 2026, at the age of 100.{cite:arab-center-walid-khalidi-obituary-2026}{cite:wikidata-walid-khalidi}
