## Summary
Sheikh Mustapha Ibrahim is a Ghanaian scholar, author and humanitarian who founded in 1996 the Islamic Council for Development and Humanitarian Services (ICODEHS), which he grew from a book-printing council into the largest Islamic civil development network in Ghana, with work extending into about ten West African countries. He is among Ghana's most prominent Muslim figures and president of the Ghana section of the Mohammed VI Foundation of African Oulema. {cite:fm6oa-mustapha-ibrahim} {cite:muslim500-mustapha-ibrahim-ghana}

## Formation
He received a double formation rare among African scholars: years of traditional madrasa study under Sheikh Ahmad Babalwaiz, founder of Ghana's Wataniyyah Islamic School, in advanced Arabic and the Sharia sciences, followed by a degree in the Study of Religions at the University of Ghana, where he trained in research and translation methods. {cite:fm6oa-mustapha-ibrahim}

## Early work
He taught Islamic studies and economics at secondary-school level for years, and in 1980 resigned from the Ghana Education Service to write and translate full-time, founding in 1981/82 the Islamic Books Distribution and Translation Council, through which he translated Islamic books from Arabic into English and local languages including French, Hausa, Twi and Dagbani, printed his own books with Kuwaiti assistance until they became teaching references, opened a public library and presented a regular programme on Marhaba FM radio. {cite:fm6oa-mustapha-ibrahim} {cite:icodehs-mustapha-ibrahim}

## ICODEHS
He registered the council under its new name ICODEHS as an official NGO in 1991, and it was founded in institutional form in 1996 with a declared aim: to relieve poverty and improve the conditions of the deprived through education, relief and sustainable development in partnership with international Arab and Muslim organisations. Organisation reports and Ghanaian press credit it with building hundreds of schools across the country, more than a thousand mosques and about 3,200 water wells, orphan sponsorship, clinics and medical campaigns including free eye screening and surgery, with activity extending into about twelve West African states including Nigeria, Togo and Senegal. {cite:icodehs-mustapha-ibrahim} {cite:muslim500-mustapha-ibrahim-ghana}

## Authorship and scholarly office
He has authored and translated more than twenty books on jurisprudence and social development, best known for his reference work on the Islamic law of inheritance, to which pioneering expertise in the science of succession is attributed. He chairs the Ghana section of the Mohammed VI Foundation of African Oulema, where he leads peacebuilding, youth empowerment and interfaith dialogue programmes, and sits on the advisory board of the office of Ghana's Grand Mufti and Chief Imam. {cite:fm6oa-mustapha-ibrahim}

## Recognition and legacy
The organisation has received national and international awards accepted by its chairman, and Ghanaian press presents him as the figure who combined deep madrasa formation with institutional development work — having converted a book-printing council into the country's broadest umbrella of Islamic services. {cite:fm6oa-mustapha-ibrahim}
