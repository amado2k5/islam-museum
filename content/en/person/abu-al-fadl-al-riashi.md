## Overview
Abu al-Fadl al-Abbas ibn al-Faraj al-Riyashi was a Basran grammarian and philologist. Ibn Khallikan describes him as a learned transmitter, reliable, well versed in the battle-days of the Arabs and of wide reading {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 27}. Ibn al-Athir records his death under 257 AH, which corresponds to 871 CE by the museum's conversion, and calls him a philologist of the first rank among them {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 6 p. 234}.

## Teachers and pupils
He narrated from al-Asma'i, Abu Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna and others, and Ibrahim al-Harbi, Ibn Abi al-Dunya and others narrated from him {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 27}. Ibn al-Athir likewise says he narrated from al-Asma'i and others {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 6 p. 234}.

## A report he transmitted
Ibn Khallikan gives one of his reports from al-Asma'i: a bedouin passed by them asking after a lost son and described him as like a little dinar; they said they had not seen him, but he soon came back with a small black boy carried on his neck like a beetle, and they told him that had he asked about this boy they could have guided him, since the child had been before them all day. Al-Asma'i then recited two verses on a father's love for his child {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 27}.

## His death
Al-Riyashi was killed at Basra in the days of the leader of the Zanj, called by Ibn Khallikan al-Alawi al-Basri, in Shawwal 257 AH {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 pp. 27-28}. Ibn Khallikan adds that at the end of Dhu al-Hijja 254 AH he was asked his age and answered that he thought it was seventy-seven {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 28}.

Ibn Khallikan also reports that Ibn al-Athir, in his large history, says he was killed in 265 AH by the Zanj at Basra; he judges this an error, arguing that historians agree the Zanj entered Basra at the Friday prayer on the thirteenth night before the end of Shawwal 257 AH, killed and burned that night and the next day, returned on the Monday and killed those who had come out after being promised safety, and that the mosque burned with those inside it, al-Riyashi being in the mosque when he was killed {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 28}. In the copy of al-Kamil read for this entry, his death appears under 257 AH {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 6 p. 234}.

## His name
Ibn Khallikan explains that al-Riyashi derives from Riyash, the name given to a man of Judham whose slave was the father of the one so called, and the name stayed with him {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 28}.
