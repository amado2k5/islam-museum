## Overview
Yahya ibn Abd al-Azim ibn Yahya ibn Muhammad, Abu al-Husayn al-Jazzar ("the butcher"), Jamal al-Din, is described by al-Zirikli as a witty Egyptian poet {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}. Al-Zirikli prints his years as 601 AH and 679 AH and pairs them with 1204 and 1280 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}.

## Life
Al-Zirikli says he was a butcher at al-Fustat, as were his father and some of his relatives, then turned to letters, and his poetry carried him to sultans and kings, whom he praised, living on the gifts he received from them {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}. He exchanged banter in verse with al-Siraj al-Warraq and others {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}. He was a friend of Ibn Sa'id, author of al-Mughrib fi Hula al-Maghrib, who filled fifty pages of his book with selections of al-Jazzar's poetry {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli names al-Uqud al-Durriyya fi al-Umara' al-Misriyya, a verse chronicle in manuscript that reaches the days of al-Zahir Baybars {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}, and a small manuscript diwan he saw in the Sadiqiyya library in Tunis, which he thinks may be selections, since his diwan is large, as Ibn Taghribirdi says {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}. Others are Fawa'id al-Mawa'id in manuscript, al-Wasila ila al-Habib fi Wasf al-Tayyibat wa-l-Tib, which Brockelmann mentions, and Taqatif al-Jazzar, verse {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 153}.
