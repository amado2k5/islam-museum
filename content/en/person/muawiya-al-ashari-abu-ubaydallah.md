## Overview
Mu'awiya ibn Ubaydallah ibn Yasar, al-Ash'ari by clientage, Abu Ubaydallah, was one of the great viziers of the early Abbasid period. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 100 AH, 718 CE, and his death as 170 AH, 786 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 262}.

## Early life and vizierate
His family came from Tiberias, in the lands of the Jordan. He studied hadith and literary learning and attached himself to the Abbasid al-Mahdi before his caliphate, serving as his secretary and vizier; al-Mahdi honoured him and did not oppose him in anything he advised. When the caliphate came to [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]], he entrusted him with the running of the realm and its government offices; Mu'awiya carried the burden and gave the vizierate standing, and al-Zirikli calls him the foremost man of his age in skill, experience and writing {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 262}.

## His book on the land tax
He composed a book on the land tax (kharaj) setting out its legal rulings, fine points and rules, and al-Zirikli says he was the first to write a book on it {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 262}.

## Dismissal and death
Al-Zirikli describes him as very proud and overbearing, while also generous with good deeds. He continued until al-Rabi' ibn Yunus became al-Mahdi's chamberlain and undermined the caliph's trust in him; al-Mahdi dismissed him after killing a son of his on a charge of zandaqa, and he died out of office {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 262}. Al-Zirikli quotes Ibn al-Khatib that on the day of his death the bridges of Baghdad were filled with his clients, orphans, widows and poor people, that Ali ibn al-Mahdi led the prayer over him, and that he was buried in the Quraysh cemetery in Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 262}.
