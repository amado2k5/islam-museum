## Summary
Maher Mustafa Zain is a Lebanese-Swedish Islamic nasheed and R&B singer, songwriter and music producer, born on 16 July 1981. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} {cite:wikidata-maher-zain} Working with Awakening Records, he released his debut album Thank You Allah on 1 November 2009; promoted through Facebook, YouTube and iTunes, it spread quickly, and by the end of 2010 he was the most Googled celebrity in Malaysia for that year, with the album becoming Malaysia's highest-selling album of 2010, certified multiple platinum by Warner Music Malaysia and double platinum by Sony Music Indonesia. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar}

He sings mainly in English but has released his most popular songs in other languages: "Insha Allah" exists in English, French, Arabic, Turkish, Malay and Indonesian versions, and "Allahi Allah Kiya Karo" is sung in Urdu with the Pakistan-born Canadian singer Irfan Makki. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} The Arabic article reports, as platform figures at its revision date (May 2026), some 615 million views for the video "Ya Nabi Salam Alayka" and more than 26 million Facebook followers; these are changing figures attributed to their source, not permanent museum facts. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar}

## Early life and education
Maher Zain was born in Tripoli, Lebanon; his father sang in Beirut and was his first musical inspiration, and he received his first musical instrument at the age of ten. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar} {cite:wikidata-maher-zain} His Lebanese family emigrated to Sweden in 1989, when he was eight; he completed his schooling there and gained a bachelor's degree in aeronautical engineering. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar}

After university he entered the music industry in Sweden and in 2005 linked up with the Moroccan-born Swedish producer RedOne, following him to New York in 2006 and producing for artists such as Kat DeLuna. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} The Arabic article describes his feeling at the time that something was not right despite the success; on returning to Sweden he engaged with Stockholm's Muslim community and a local mosque, and decided to leave production to become a singer-songwriter of contemporary R&B with a strong Islamic influence. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar} {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}

## Beginnings and career
In January 2009 he began working on an album with Awakening Records; Thank You Allah, with 13 songs and two bonus tracks, was released on 1 November 2009, with percussion and French versions of some tracks following. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}

In early 2010 his music very quickly gathered a huge online following in Arabic-speaking and Islamic countries and among young Muslims in western countries, with Malaysia and Indonesia the countries of his greatest commercial success. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}

## Works
His albums continued with Forgive Me in April 2012, One in 2016, the mini album Nour Ala Nour in 2021, and Back To You in 2026. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}

His singles include "Palestine Will Be Free" (2009), "Insha Allah" (2010), "Freedom" (March 2011), inspired by the events of the Arab Spring, "Ya Nabi Salam Alayka", "Ramadan" (2013) and "Rahmatun lil'Alameen" (2022). {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} He took part in the Colors of Peace project in 2013, songs based on works by Fethullah Gülen, and in 2025 collaborated with Harris J on "Qalbi Fi Madinah". {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}

He has performed concerts around the world, including in the UK, the United States, Malaysia, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia and Egypt, has fan clubs in several countries, sat on the judging committee of the Awakening Talent Contest in 2013, and appeared in the 40-episode Indonesian TV drama Insya-Allah, aired in 2012. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}

## Recognition and philanthropy
In January 2010 his song 'Ya Nabi Salam Alayka' won Best Religious Song on Nogoum FM, a major Middle East mainstream music station, ahead of prominent singers including Hussein Al-Jismi, Mohammed Mounir and Sami Yusuf, and he was chosen as Muslim Star of 2011 in a competition organized by Onislam.net. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar}

His charitable work includes performing in Canada in 2013 on an Islamic Relief tour raising donations for typhoon victims in the Philippines, taking part in the UK "Sound of Light" event in support of the Syrian people, singing "One Day" about refugees at the 2014 Nansen Refugee Award ceremony after travelling to Lebanon with UNHCR to spend time with Syrian refugees, and asking his Facebook fans to donate to a charity building water wells in Africa, raising more than $15,000 within a few weeks. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported by the sources, not as a verdict of the museum.

The articles quote his stated aim of reaching young people the same way modern western music attracts them, since some young listeners justify that music by the absence of an alternative; he welcomed the new wave of contemporary religious singing as giving young people variety and alternatives, and defended purposeful song as able to change negative behaviours and concepts. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar}

## Personal life
The Arabic article records that he is married with children, naming them and their birth years; the museum notes the marriage only, in line with its rule of restraint. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar}

## Impact and audience
Maher Zain's impact is that of a pioneer of the generation that made Islamic song a multilingual global product distributed through platforms: multiple platinum certifications in Southeast Asia, concerts on several continents, and view and follower counts documented in his articles as changing platform figures. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en} {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-ar} The English article records that by the end of 2010 he was the most Googled celebrity in Malaysia for that year. {cite:wikipedia-maher-zain-en}
