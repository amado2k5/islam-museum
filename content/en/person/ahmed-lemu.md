# Ahmed Lemu

**Ahmed Lemu** was a Nigerian Muslim scholar, educationist and judge. He was born in the village of Lemu near Bida, in what is now Niger State — the English article's lead gives 21 December 1929, its early-life section 21 November of the same year, and the Arabic article gives 20 Rajab 1348, the December date. His father named him Shehu Ahmadu, after Ahmad al-Tijani, and he later Arabicised Shehu into Sheikh {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-ar}.

He had his early schooling in Qur'anic and government schools at Lemu and Bida, trained as a teacher of Arabic, Sharia and Islamic studies at the School of Arabic Studies at Lemu (certificate 1952), and was sent in 1954 to the School of Oriental and African Studies in London, where he took an advanced-level certificate (1961) and an honours degree in African and Oriental studies (1964) {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-ar}.

He taught Arabic, Islamic studies and English at Bida (1953–1960), became senior teacher and then principal at the School of Arabic Studies in Kano, principal of the Arabic Teachers College at Sokoto (1966), and rose to chief inspector of education for Sokoto State and head of its educational planning before retiring from the education service in 1976. He then joined the judiciary: he was the first Sharia judge of the Sokoto Court of Appeal (1976–1977) and chief Sharia judge — Grand Khadi — of the Niger State Court of Appeal (1976–1991) {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-ar}.

On 16 November 1969 he and his wife Aisha Lemu founded the Islamic Educational Trust (IET) at Minna, with Aisha as finance secretary; the trust trained and employed teachers of Islamic religious knowledge for public and mission schools across northern Nigeria and later ran schools, a publisher and the Da'wah Institute Nigeria. He also founded the Dawah Coordination Council of Nigeria and was its first president {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-en}.

He served on national bodies — the Vision 2010 Committee and, in 2011, as chairman of the Presidential Committee on Post-Election Violence — received Nigerian national honours (OFR 1999, OON 2001), and in 2014 received the King Faisal International Prize for service to Islam. Among those he had taught at school were the future heads of state Ibrahim Babangida and Abdulsalami Abubakar. He wrote school textbooks and works on fasting, prayer, zakat and hajj, and died on 24 December 2020 {cite:wikipedia-ahmed-lemu-en} {cite:wikidata-ahmed-lemu}.
