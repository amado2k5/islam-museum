## Summary
Ottoman kapudan pasha; the man sent to annex Morocco, and the last of the beylerbeys {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 270-273}.

## The Morocco bid
Once the state was settled in Tunis, Uluj Ali set his sights on annexing Morocco and was ordered to march, reaching Algiers in Jumada II 989/June 1581. But [[person/ahmad-al-mansur-al-sadi|Ahmad al-Mansur]] camped at the Tensift, sealed his passes, fortified the shores and sent an embassy to Istanbul after a near-agreement with Spain trading Larache — so the sultan accepted the fait accompli and ordered him and his deputy Jafar Pasha to abandon Morocco for the east, where the Hejaz had grown troubled. He gave up his ambition of recovering al-Andalus {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 270-271}.

## Death and aftermath
He died in 995/1587; with him ended Algiers' beylerbey system — which had made her governors near-sovereigns — replaced by the pasha system, and [[person/hasan-veneziano|Hasan Veneziano]] succeeded him as kapudan of the sea {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 272}.
