## Summary
Shabana Mahmood is a British Labour Party politician and barrister, born in Birmingham on 17 September 1980. She has been Member of Parliament for Birmingham Ladywood since 2010, and has served as Home Secretary since September 2025. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en} {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-ar}

She previously served as Secretary of State for Justice and Lord Chancellor from July 2024 to September 2025, the first Muslim to hold the lord chancellorship. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

## Early life and education
Shabana Mahmood was born in Birmingham, the daughter of Mahmood Ahmed and Zubaida. Her parents are of Pakistani origin with roots in Mirpur, Azad Kashmir. She is the eldest of four children and a twin with her brother. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en} {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-ar}

From 1981 to 1986 she lived with her family in Taif, Saudi Arabia, where her father worked as a civil engineer on desalination. She was then brought up in Birmingham, attending Small Heath School and King Edward VI Camp Hill School for Girls. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

She read law at Lincoln College, Oxford, where she was president of the Junior Common Room, and was awarded a 2:1 in 2002. She completed the Bar Vocational Course at the Inns of Court School of Law in 2003 on a scholarship from Gray's Inn. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

## Legal career
Mahmood is a qualified barrister specialising in professional indemnity law. She worked at 12 King's Bench Walk from 2003 to 2004 and at Berrymans Lace Mawer from 2004 to 2007. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

## Parliamentary career
She was elected MP for Birmingham Ladywood at the 2010 general election with 55.7 per cent of the vote. Along with Rushanara Ali and Yasmin Qureshi, she became one of the UK's first female Muslim MPs. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en} {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-ar}

In opposition she held successive shadow posts, including Shadow Minister for Prisons and for Higher Education and Shadow Financial Secretary to the Treasury (2013–2015). She became Shadow Chief Secretary to the Treasury in 2015, and resigned from the shadow cabinet after Jeremy Corbyn's election as leader, declining to serve in his. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

She returned to the shadow cabinet in May 2021 as National Campaign Coordinator, was appointed Shadow Secretary of State for Justice and Shadow Lord Chancellor in September 2023, and was re-elected in 2024 with a majority of 3,421. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

## In government
Following Labour's victory at the 2024 general election, she was appointed Secretary of State for Justice and Lord Chancellor on 5 July 2024. A week after her appointment she announced measures to reduce prison overcrowding, including early release for some prisoners after serving 40 per cent of their sentences. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

In the cabinet reshuffle of 5 September 2025 she was appointed Home Secretary, succeeding Yvette Cooper. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum. The sources describe Mahmood as belonging to the more socially conservative Blue Labour faction of the Labour Party, and the founder of that current welcomed her appointment as Home Secretary as signalling a harder line on immigration. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

In October 2025 she announced plans to let police restrict or ban repeat protests, an announcement criticised by Amnesty International and Liberty, whose former director warned about future use of such powers. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

The article quotes her 2024 press interview statement that her faith is the centrepoint of her life and drives her to public service. The museum reports this as her own description of herself, not as a classification by the museum. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}

## Reach
Her public significance lies in being one of Britain's first female Muslim MPs, the first Muslim Lord Chancellor, and then Home Secretary — offices the article treats as milestones in the political representation of Muslims in Britain. {cite:wikipedia-shabana-mahmood-en}
