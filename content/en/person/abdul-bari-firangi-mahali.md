# Abdul Bari Firangi Mahali

Abdul Bari Firangi Mahali (12 April 1878 – 19 January 1926) was an Indian Muslim scholar and writer of Firangi Mahal in Lucknow who authored 111 books {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-en}. The Urdu biography pairs his birth date itself: 10 Rabi' al-thani 1295 AH, a Friday, corresponding to 12 April 1878, born at Firangi Mahal {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ur}. His family's ancestors had moved from Sihali in Barabanki district to Firangi Mahal around 1692 {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-en}.

## Formation and the Sufi path

The Arabic biography records that he studied under Shaykh Abd al-Baqi al-Firangi Mahali, travelled to the Haramayn and to Baghdad, where he studied hadith and jurisprudence, and worked as an assistant professor at Pune in 1916 {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ar}. The Urdu biography states that he entered the Qadiriyya order as a disciple of Shah Abdul Razzaq Hansvi and received authorisation and succession in all the orders, and that he was among the close friends of Haji Waris Ali Shah {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ur}.

## Politics

During the First World War he asked the Sultan of Turkey to support Britain or remain out of the war; on 26 January 1919 he presided over a protest meeting in Lucknow against the British attitude towards Muslims, and he was highly active in the Khilafat movement {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-en}. The Urdu biography records that he issued a fatwa of jihad against the British government, and that he raised his voice against the demolition of the graves of the Companions and the elders of the faith {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ur}. He preached Hindu-Muslim unity, especially during the Khilafat agitation, was an associate of Mahatma Gandhi, and on other occasions urged Muslims not to sacrifice cows out of respect for Hindus {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-en}.

## Institutions and books

In his opposition to Western education he founded the Madarsa-i-Nizamia in Lucknow; he set up the Anjuman-i-Khuddam-i-Kabba in 1914 to prevent the desecration of Muslim holy places; and he was a founder member of Darul Musannefin, the Shibli Academy (1915–1916), and of Jamia Millia Islamia in 1920 {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-en}{cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ar}. His 111 books ranged across hadith, exegesis, jurisprudence, legal theory, inheritance law, logic, philosophy, grammar, biography and the Sufi path {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ur}.

## Faith, as the sources attest it

No article read names a legal school for Abdul Bari himself: the English article's infobox gives his religion as Islam only {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-en}, the Arabic article calls him a Muslim scholar {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ar}, and Wikidata likewise gives Islam only {cite:wikidata-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali}. His Sufi discipleship under Shah Abdul Razzaq Hansvi is attested in the Urdu article's text, as set out above {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ur}. The museum records his faith as unattested at the two-source bar and preserves the discipleship attestation in the data record.

He died on 19 January 1926, a date the Urdu biography pairs as 4 Rajab 1344 AH {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-ur}; the English article and Wikidata give only the year {cite:wikipedia-abdul-bari-firangi-mahali-en}.
