## Overview
Hibat Allah ibn Musa ibn Dawud al-Shirazi al-Salmani, Abu Nasr, known by the title al-Mu'ayyad fi'l-Din, was the chief da'i (da'i al-du'at). He was an Isma'ili, that is, not a Sunni; al-Zirikli calls him one of the leaders and writers of the Isma'ilis. The entry gives his death as 470 AH, 1078 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.

## His life
He was born and schooled at Shiraz, where his father and then he himself carried the Fatimid mission. He was forced to leave and went in disguise to al-Ahwaz in 436 AH, and stayed for a time at Hillat Mansur. He then went to Egypt and served the Fatimid al-Mustansir in the chancery (diwan al-insha'), rising until the Fatimid mission came to him in 450 AH, and he was given the titles da'i al-du'at and bab al-abwab {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.

He was later removed and sent away to Syria. He returned to Egypt and died there at about eighty; al-Mustansir prayed over him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.

## His name and a correspondence
His nisba links him to Salman al-Farisi. Al-Zirikli gives two sayings: that he was of Salman's descendants, or that his rank among the Isma'ilis was like Salman's. Around 449 AH he exchanged letters with Abu al-Ala al-Ma'arri on the eating of plants; the orientalist Margoliouth published them in the journal of the Royal Asiatic Society in 1902 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.

## His works
Al-Zirikli names: al-Murshid ila adab al-Isma'iliyya (printed); al-Majalis al-Mu'ayyadiyya (printed, two parts); al-Sira al-Mu'ayyadiyya (printed under the title Sirat al-Mu'ayyad fi'l-Din da'i al-du'at, with much of his own account); and his collected poems, Diwan al-Mu'ayyad fi'l-Din (printed). In Persian he has Asas al-ta'wil, which he translated from the Arabic; its original is by al-Qadi al-Nu'man {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.
