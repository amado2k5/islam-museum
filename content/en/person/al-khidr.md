## Overview
The servant to whom God gave knowledge from Himself, whom [[person/musa|Musa]] accompanied on the journey to the meeting of the two seas. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari-bugha 3219}

What others have said about him is not set out here and needs an additional source.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam note: al-Akhdar
In North Africa the vulgar form al-Akhdar ("the green") is in common use as a personal name for al-Khidr. Several holy men (santons), especially at Constantine, are known by this name. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 343}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh cites the story of al-Khidr with Musa: he made it a condition that Musa not ask him until he himself explained; when he holed the ship, killed the boy and rebuilt the wall, and Musa objected each time, al-Khidr said: this is the parting between me and you. The Sheikh contrasts this with God's forbearance toward a servant who errs hundreds of times a day. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep12 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep12 00:04:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme al-Munjiyat
Omar Abdelkafy mentions al-Khidr's courtesy in saying of the boat 'I wished to damage it', ascribing the damage to himself and not saying his Lord willed it, while he ascribed the good to God in the matter of the two boys. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep11 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep11 00:14:30}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme In the Courts of the Hadith Qudsi
Episode 12 of [[work/fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah|Fi rihab al-hadith al-qudsi]] explains the hadith of Musa saying he was the most knowledgeable of people, God reproaching him for not referring knowledge back to Him, and directing him to a servant at the meeting of the two seas. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep12 00:00:00} [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] says that Musa's statement is right in that a messenger knows more than his people of what is revealed to him, but not in absolute terms. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep12 00:17:30} He holds that the righteous servant drew near to God through the method the messengers bring and then went further, so God gave him another gift, knowledge from God's presence (al-'ilm al-ladunni), and that the messengers' task is to connect people with God's method. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep12 00:18:00} This is his view.

He says that Musa promised patience, but that was in theory, and when it came to practice he objected. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep12 00:18:30} He holds that asking the townspeople for food is the most sincere of requests, since no one who is full asks for a morsel. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep12 00:19:30} Of the wall he says that the righteous servant knew the intent of destiny and rebuilt it as a "timed" construction that would fall only when the two boys came of age and could guard the treasure; he concludes they were twins since they come of age together, and that in this way he punished the stingy townspeople. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep12 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep12 00:21:00}
