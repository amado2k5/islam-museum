## Brief
Ikrima, son of [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]], inherited his father's fight — commanded the only armed resistance at the conquest of Mecca — and then became a Muslim and died a commander under the new faith. His life is the tradition's emblem of the enemy's son turned soldier.

## Life
He fought on the Meccan side through the war years and led the one group that resisted inside Mecca at the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest]] — then fled toward Yemen. His wife Umm Hakim went after him with the Prophet's letter of safety, and Ikrima returned to pledge Islam {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

Under Abu Bakr he commanded against Musaylima in the Ridda wars, then marched to Syria; he was killed at the [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmuk]], around 636 CE {cite:tabari-donner-1993}.

## Legacy
The tradition draws the line explicitly: the father killed fighting the Prophet at Badr, the son killed fighting for the Prophet's community at the Yarmuk.

## For young visitors
The son of Islam's greatest enemy became one of its soldiers — and died the same death his father died, on the opposite side.
