## Brief
Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl inherited his father's fight. He led the one armed resistance on the day of the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|Conquest of Mecca]], then embraced Islam and became a soldier under the new faith, and he died a commander. In the tradition his life is an image of the opponent's son who became a soldier.

## Life
He fought in the Meccan ranks through the years of war. On the day of the Conquest he led the only group that resisted inside Mecca, and then he fled toward Yemen. His wife, Umm Hakim, caught up with him under the Prophet's safe-conduct, so Ikrima returned and pledged allegiance {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

In Abu Bakr's time he led troops against Musaylima in the wars of the Ridda, and then marched to Syria. He was killed at [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmuk]] around 636 CE {cite:tabari-donner-1993}.

## Legacy
The tradition draws the line plainly: the father died fighting the Prophet at Badr, and the son died fighting for the Prophet's community at Yarmuk.

## For young visitors
The son of the greatest enemy of the faith became a soldier in it, and died the death his father had died, but on the other side.

## His account in al-Raheeq al-Makhtum
Abu Uthman Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl, Amr ibn Hisham al-Makhzumi al-Qurashi, a noble Companion and a doughty knight. In the Jahiliyya he was among the fiercest enemies of Islam, under the influence of his father {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

He fought the Muslims at Badr, Uhud and the Trench, and he tried to repel the army that conquered Mecca at al-Khandama. When Mecca was taken he fled to Yemen. His wife Umm Hakim bint al-Harith, who had embraced Islam, followed him and secured a pledge of safety for him from the Prophet. He returned repentant and remorseful. When the Prophet saw him, he sprang up to him joyfully and said: "Welcome, rider and emigrant" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

His Islam was good, and he gave himself in God's cause, making up for what had passed. He would place the mushaf on his face and weep, exclaiming: "The Book of my Lord! The word of my Lord!" He led the armies in the Ridda wars in Oman and Mahra, and he was martyred facing forward in the glorious battle of Yarmuk in Syria, after leading the company of death {cite:tabari-donner-1993}.
