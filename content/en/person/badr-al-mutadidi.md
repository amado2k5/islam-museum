## Overview
The amir Badr al-Mu'tadidi, freedman of the caliph al-Mu'tadid and commander of his armies, was killed in 289 AH, about 902 CE (derived conversion), according to al-Safadi {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 10 p. 58}.

## Life
Al-Safadi says al-Muktafi summoned him, he grew afraid, and al-Muktafi sent him a promise of safety, then betrayed him and he was killed in cold blood {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 10 p. 58}. He governed Damascus and Isbahan for his master, and was just and of good conduct {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 10 p. 58}. Al-Safadi cites Abu Nu'aym as calling him a righteous man whose prayers were answered {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 10 p. 58}. He refers the account of his killing to the entry on al-Muktafi bi-Llah Ali ibn Ahmad, and says that al-Badriyya and Bab Badr are named after him {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 10 p. 58}.
