## Summary
Milo Cleveland Beach notes that whereas Jahangir praised [[person/abu-al-hasan-nadir-al-zaman|Abu'l Hasan]] above all other painters, it is Mansur whom he mentions most often. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 24} In his [[work/tuzuk-i-jahangiri|memoirs]] Ustad Mansur has become such a master that he bears the title Nadir al-Asr, and he and Abu'l Hasan have "no third" in Akbar's reign or Jahangir's; the emperor ordered him to record a rare falcon and a diving bird seen in a stream, and noted that the flowers of Kashmir Mansur had painted numbered more than a hundred. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 137}

## Style
All his work is more coloured drawing than painting: unpainted paper is prominent, there is minimal layering of pigment, and the lines are minute and painstaking, quite unlike the swift, independent rhythm of Iranian line. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 24} His earliest works are in manuscripts, painted but not designed by him, such as a Babur-nama page of about 1590–95 designed by Kanha and painted by Mansur that already shows his style complete; it continues in the Zebra of the [[object/minto-album|Minto Album]] of 1621. The Peafowl is probably a late work, more brilliant in colour and more controlled in composition. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 24} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 138}

## The problem of attribution
His case shows the problem of attribution in extreme form: his productivity and fame encouraged contemporary and later copies, and the Kevorkian and Wantage Albums hold works of about 1800 inscribed to him, recognisable by soft, imprecise brushwork. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 138}
