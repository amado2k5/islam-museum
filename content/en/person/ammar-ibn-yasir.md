Ammar ibn Yasir, a client of Banu Makhzum. He embraced Islam together with his parents, and all three were tortured. His [[person/yasir-ibn-amir|father]] died under the torture, and his [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|mother Sumayya]] was stabbed to death; she was the first female martyr in Islam. The Prophet gave them the good news of Paradise while they were being tortured. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Whether he is to be counted among the eighty-three who emigrated in the [[event/second-migration-to-abyssinia|second migration to Abyssinia]] is doubtful. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## The torture of his family (The Sealed Nectar p. 90)
The polytheists would drag Ammar, his father [[person/yasir-ibn-amir|Yasir]] and his mother [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya]] out to al-Abtah when the sand grew hot, and torture them with its heat. The Prophet passed by them and said: "Patience, family of Yasir, for your appointed place is Paradise." Yasir died under the torture, and Abu Jahl stabbed Sumayya in her private parts with a spear, and she died {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 90}. They increased the torment on Ammar with heat, stones and dunking, until he went along with them, under compulsion, in mentioning their gods. God then revealed the verse on compulsion concerning him, which excuses one who is forced to speak words of disbelief while his heart remains firm in faith {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 90}.

## For Sprouts
Ammar ibn Yasir, a client of Banu Makhzum; he and his parents entered Islam and suffered for it — his mother Sumayya was the first martyr of Islam, and the Prophet promised them Paradise amid their trial.

## For Explorers
Ammar ibn Yasir, a client of Banu Makhzum; he and his parents entered Islam and suffered for it — his father Yasir died in the ordeal and his mother Sumayya fell as the first martyr of Islam. The polytheists would take them to al-Abtah when the stones blazed and hurt them with its heat; the Prophet passed them saying 'patience, family of Yasir, your appointment is Paradise'. They bore down on Ammar with heat, rock and drowning until he gave in to their naming of their gods under compulsion, and God sent down the verse of compulsion in him. It is doubted whether he counted among the eighty-three who migrated in the second migration to Abyssinia.
