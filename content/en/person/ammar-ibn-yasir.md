Ammar ibn Yasir, a client of Banu Makhzum. He embraced Islam together with his parents, and all three were tortured. His [[person/yasir-ibn-amir|father]] died under the torture, and his [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|mother Sumayya]] was stabbed to death; she was the first female martyr in Islam. The Prophet gave them the good news of Paradise while they were being tortured. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Whether he is to be counted among the eighty-three who emigrated in the [[event/second-migration-to-abyssinia|second migration to Abyssinia]] is doubtful. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## The torture of his family (The Sealed Nectar p. 90)
The polytheists would drag Ammar, his father [[person/yasir-ibn-amir|Yasir]] and his mother [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya]] out to al-Abtah when the sand grew hot, and torture them with its heat. The Prophet passed by them and said: "Patience, family of Yasir, for your appointed place is Paradise." Yasir died under the torture, and Abu Jahl stabbed Sumayya in her private parts with a spear, and she died {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 90}. They increased the torment on Ammar with heat, stones and dunking, until he went along with them, under compulsion, in mentioning their gods. God then revealed the verse on compulsion concerning him, which excuses one who is forced to speak words of disbelief while his heart remains firm in faith {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 90}.

Ammar ibn Yasir, a client of Banu Makhzum; he and his parents entered Islam and suffered for it — his mother Sumayya was the first martyr of Islam, and the Prophet promised them Paradise amid their trial.

## For Explorers
Ammar ibn Yasir, a client of Banu Makhzum; he and his parents entered Islam and suffered for it — his father Yasir died in the ordeal and his mother Sumayya fell as the first martyr of Islam. The polytheists would take them to al-Abtah when the stones blazed and hurt them with its heat; the Prophet passed them saying 'patience, family of Yasir, your appointment is Paradise'. They bore down on Ammar with heat, rock and drowning until he gave in to their naming of their gods under compulsion, and God sent down the verse of compulsion in him. ## For Scholars
Ammar ibn Yasir, a client of Banu Makhzum; he and his parents entered Islam and all were tortured — his father Yasir died under it and his mother Sumayya was stabbed and fell as Islam's first martyr, the Prophet promising them Paradise while they suffered. The polytheists would take Ammar, his father Yasir and his mother Sumayya to al-Abtah when the stones blazed and torture them with its heat; the Prophet passed them saying 'patience, family of Yasir, your appointment is Paradise'. Yasir died under torture, and Abu Jahl stabbed Sumayya in her private parts with a spear and she died. They bore down on Ammar with heat, rock and drowning until he gave in to their naming of their gods under compulsion, and God sent down the verse of compulsion in him. It is doubted whether he counted among the eighty-three who migrated in the second migration to Abyssinia.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In episode 22 of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] tells the life of Ammar ibn Yasir, whom he identifies as Abu al-Yaqzan al-Ansi of Mecca, a client of [[group/banu-makhzum|Banu Makhzum]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:02:00} He says Ammar, his father and his mother all accepted Islam early and suffered great hardship and persecution, and that his mother died a martyr, the first martyr in Islam, and his father followed her as a martyr. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:01:00} He says Ammar was a veteran of Badr and was granted martyrdom at the end of his life, after he had reached ninety-three. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:01:30}

He begins with the father: [[person/yasir-ibn-amir|Yasir]] set out with his brothers al-Harith and Malik to look for a missing brother until they reached [[place/mecca|Mecca]]; the two brothers went home, and Yasir chose to stay. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:02:00} He explains that Arab custom required a stranger to ally himself with a notable, so Yasir entered the alliance of Abu Hudhayfa ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumi, who liked him for his character and married him to one of his slave women, [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya bint Khayyat]], who bore him Ammar. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:02:30} When Ammar heard of Islam he hurried to the house of [[person/al-arqam-ibn-abi-al-arqam|al-Arqam]] and accepted it before the Prophet; he went back to his parents, who asked about the light in his face, and when he told them they accepted Islam too. The speaker counts this as the height of devotion to parents. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:03:30}

He describes what Banu Makhzum did to them: they dressed them in iron mail and put them out on the open ground in the noon heat to make them give up Islam. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:04:30} He narrates that [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]] flogged Sumayya, poured boiling oil on her head and branded her with heated iron, and when she held firm he killed her with his spear, so that she was the first woman martyr in Islam {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:05:00}; that Abu Jahl killed her with a spear is also stated in this record from The Sealed Nectar. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.90} The Prophet would pass them while they were being tortured, urge them to be patient and promise them Paradise, and the speaker therefore counts Sumayya, Yasir and Ammar among those given the glad tidings of Paradise. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:05:30} After Sumayya, Yasir was killed, and the torture of Ammar grew so severe that he nearly died; they made it the condition of release that he speak ill of the Prophet and speak well of the idols, and he did. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:06:00} He cites a report he attributes to [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]], which he says al-Hakim graded sound with [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] agreeing, through Abu Ubayda ibn Muhammad ibn Ammar: Ammar came to the Prophet and told him; the Prophet asked about his heart, Ammar said it was at rest in faith, and the Prophet allowed him to do the same if they did it again; and the verse was revealed that exempts one who is [[concept/ikrah|compelled]] while his heart is at rest in faith. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:06:30} From this he draws, as his own view, the lesson that a person may utter words of disbelief without becoming a disbeliever if compelled, ignorant, reasoning from an interpretation or not meaning it, that [[concept/takfir|declaring someone a disbeliever]] has rules, conditions and the absence of impediments, and that the judgement belongs to the courts, not to preachers. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:07:30}

He says that when Ammar reached [[place/quba|Quba]] on the emigration he set up a place of prayer, and so was the first in this community to build a mosque; he attributes this to [[person/muhammad-ibn-sad|Ibn Sa'd]] and al-Hakim from al-Qasim ibn Abd al-Rahman. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:08:00} He cites a hadith he attributes to [[person/al-tirmidhi-muhammad-ibn-isa|al-Tirmidhi]] (in the chapter of merits), [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] and [[person/ibn-majah|Ibn Majah]], narrated by [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]]: Ammar asked leave to come in to the Prophet, who welcomed him and praised his goodness and purity. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:08:30} He cites the hadith that Paradise yearns for three, Ali, Ammar and [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman]], attributing it to al-Tirmidhi from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], graded sound by [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|al-Albani]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:09:30} He narrates that [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] spoke harshly to Ammar, who complained to the Prophet; the Prophet said that whoever is hostile to Ammar or hates him, God is hostile to him and hates him; and Khalid went out wanting nothing more than to win Ammar's goodwill, and did. He attributes this to Ahmad, [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]] in al-Kubra and [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]], graded sound by al-Albani. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:10:00} He cites the hadith telling the Companions to follow Abu Bakr and Umar, take Ammar's guidance and hold to the way of [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]], attributing it to al-Tirmidhi and Ahmad with al-Albani's grading of sound, and explains that Ammar held fast to the Sunna and the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:11:30} From [[person/aisha|Aisha]], in a report he attributes to al-Tirmidhi and to al-Albani's grading in al-Sahiha, Ammar was never given a choice between two things without choosing the more right-guided. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:12:00}

He says Ammar fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], missed no campaign with the Prophet, and fought the apostates in the time of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], and he recalls the merit of the people of Badr. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:13:00} He relates from [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]], as given by Ibn Sa'd, that Ibn Umar saw Ammar on the day of [[event/battle-of-yamama|al-Yamama]] standing on a rock and calling to the Muslims not to flee from Paradise, his severed ear dangling as he fought with the utmost fierceness. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:13:30} He says Ammar governed [[place/kufa|Kufa]] under [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], and that Umar's letter to its people made Ammar their amir and Ibn Mas'ud his minister, described the two as among the noblest of Muhammad's Companions and of the people of Badr, and said he had given up Ibn Mas'ud for them; he attributes this to al-Dhahabi's Siyar A'lam al-Nubala'. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:14:30} He describes his humility as governor, carrying firewood and food home on his back. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:15:30} He narrates that Umar asked the Kufans about him and they praised him and said it was God who had appointed him; Umar rejected this and said it was he who had appointed him, so that whatever good there was came from God and whatever fault from Umar. The speaker reads this as Umar closing the road to excess and flattery. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:15:30}

He describes him as a man of few words and long silences, most of whose speech was seeking refuge with God from strife. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:17:00} He says Ammar was prayed over and buried in his clothes because he died a martyr, to join his mother Sumayya, his father Yasir, and Abu Bakr, Umar and [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]], who had died before him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep22 00:22:00} He also discusses the account of Ammar's death and what passed among the Companions in the strife, which is not written here.

## In Ahmad ibn Hanbal's Fada'il al-Sahaba
The chapter on Ammar in [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]]'s [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]] has Abu Layla al-Kindi's report that Khabbab came to [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], who drew him near and said, "No one has more right to this seat than you except Ammar", whereupon Khabbab showed him the scars on his back from the torture of the polytheists {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1083}. From [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] it narrates that Ammar asked leave to enter on the Prophet, who said, "Let him in — welcome to the good, the made-good", a sound chain according to the editor {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1084}. From Amr ibn Shurahbil: the Prophet said, "Ammar is filled with faith to the marrow" — *mursal* with trustworthy transmitters, says the editor {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1084-1085}. From Abd Allah ibn Salama: two men came out of the bathhouse oiled and perfumed and told Ali, "We are of the Muhajirun", and he said, "The Muhajir is Ammar ibn Yasir" — sound according to the editor {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1085}. It narrates from Mujahid, without the Companion link, the Prophet's words "What is it with them and Ammar? He calls them to Paradise and they call him to the Fire", which the editor grades weak for that reason {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1083-1084}.
