## Overview
Ya'qub ibn Ishaq, Abu Yusuf, known as Ibn al-Sikkit, was a leading authority on language and literature. Al-Zirikli in al-A'lam gives his birth as 186 AH (802 CE) and his death as 244 AH (858 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 195}.

## Career
His family came from Khuzistan, the region between Basra and Fars, and he studied in Baghdad, according to al-Zirikli {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 195}. He became close to the Abbasid caliph al-Mutawakkil, who entrusted him with the instruction of his sons and counted him among his boon companions {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 195}.

## His death
Al-Zirikli says al-Mutawakkil killed him for a reason that is not known, and reports an account given for it: the caliph asked him which he loved more, his sons al-Mu'tazz and al-Mu'ayyad or al-Hasan and al-Husayn, and Ibn al-Sikkit answered by ranking Qanbar, the servant of Ali, above the caliph and his two sons. The caliph ordered the Turkish guards to trample his belly, or, in another version, to pull out his tongue; he was carried home and died in Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 195}.

## Works
His best-known book is Islah al-Mantiq (printed); al-Zirikli quotes al-Mubarrad as saying he had seen no better book by the Baghdadis {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 195}. Al-Zirikli also lists as printed al-Alfaz, al-Addad, al-Qalb wa-l-Ibdal, and his commentaries on the divans of Urwa ibn al-Ward and Qays ibn al-Khatim. Unprinted works he names include al-Ajnas, Sariqat al-Shu'ara, al-Hasharat, al-Amthal, commentaries on the poetry of al-Akhtal, al-A'sha, Zuhayr, Umar ibn Abi Rabi'a and the Mu'allaqat, an explanation of the poetry of Abu Nuwas of about eight hundred leaves, Gharib al-Qur'an, al-Nabat wa-l-Shajar, al-Nawadir, al-Wuhush, and the smaller and larger Ma'ani al-Shi'r {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 195}.
