## Summary
Ibrahim al-Qaramani al-Amidi was a scholar from Amida, present-day Diyarbakir in eastern Anatolia; the Süleymaniye Library catalogues give his death as 1000 AH (1591/1592). {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 17} Heinen places him in the period usually regarded as the "old age" of science among Muslims, after the closing of the Istanbul observatory. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 17}

## His works
He wrote [[work/al-haya-al-islamiyya-qaramani|"The Book of Astronomy based on the tenets of the People of Tradition and Community, not on those of the Philosophers"]], which Heinen first noticed through Aydın Sayılı's The Observatory in Islam. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 17} In Turkish libraries Heinen found many treatises with nearly identical titles by him, or Turkish translations and adaptations of them, and concluded that they were among the most popular books of the time. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 18}

## His sources
Al-Qaramani candidly admits that he took all the material of his "Islamic astronomy" from older authorities; his most important source was [[work/al-haya-al-saniyya-suyuti|al-Hay'a al-saniyya]] of [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]], written about a century before he began to write. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 19}
