## Summary
Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari, Abu al-Hasan — Abu al-Hawari's name was Maymun — was an ascetic who lived in [[place/damascus|Damascus]]; Ibn al-Jawzi gives his entry among the people of Syria in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]] (no. 764). {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.824} He is the disciple through whom most of [[person/abu-sulayman-al-darani|Abu Sulayman al-Darani]]'s sayings in the book are transmitted. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.817}

## A house of scrupulousness
He had a son named Abd Allah who was among the ascetics, and a brother named Muhammad who resembled him in scrupulousness and renunciation, and his father Abu al-Hawari was also among the scrupulous — theirs was a house of piety and renunciation. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.824} His wife [[person/rabia-bint-ismail|Rabi'a bint Isma'il]] was one of the women ascetics of Syria. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.858} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.859}

## Praise from scholars
[[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|Al-Junayd]] used to say, "Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari is the sweet basil of Syria"; [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]] said, "I think God waters the people of Syria with rain for his sake"; and Mahmud ibn Khalid said, "I do not think there remains anyone like him on the face of the earth." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.824}

## From his sayings
He is reported as saying that whoever loves to be known or mentioned for some good has associated others in his worship, and that one who worships out of love does not wish anyone but the Beloved to see his service; that when he read the Qur'an he would pause at verse after verse until his mind was bewildered, and wondered how those who know the Qur'an by heart could sleep peacefully while reciting the speech of the Merciful — if they understood what they recited and took delight in it, sleep would leave them for joy at what they had been given; and: "The higher the station of the heart, the swifter the punishment comes to it." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.824}

## Transmission and death
He transmitted from [[person/hafs-ibn-ghiyath|Hafs ibn Ghiyath]], Abu Mu'awiya, [[person/waki-ibn-al-jarrah|Waki']] and their peers, and died in 230 AH. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.824}

## For young visitors
Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari was a good man of Damascus who learned from the ascetic Abu Sulayman, and who would reflect on the verses of the Qur'an until he forgot to sleep.

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
His notice in *Hilyat al-Awliya* (volume 10, no. 457) opens with verse and the story of his casting his books into the sea and regretting study after arrival: "You were a good guide; occupation with the guide after arrival is impossible" — the story is passed through Ibrahim ibn Yusuf and Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Tabari, and he washed his books in the Euphrates once he had attained the goal. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/5-7}

Of his saying: "Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari sought knowledge for thirty years; when he reached the goal he put his books on a ship and scattered them: O knowledge, I did not do this out of disdain for you or disrespect for your right, but I sought you to be guided by you to my Lord, and once I was guided to my Lord by you, I no longer needed you." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/6}

Of his exchange with a monk in Dayr Hurmala: he asked the monk "What keeps you in this cell?" and he said "I was kept in it from the lusts of the world"; so Ahmad asked: "Would it not be right to go out here and there in the land and deny yourself the desires?" He said: "Alas, what you describe is strength, and I am in weakness; my cage has come between me and it," then explaining that the son of Adam's body was created from earth and his spirit from the kingdom of heaven — when his body hungers, is stripped and kept awake, the spirit longs for the place it came from, and when he feeds it, gives it drink, lets it sleep and rests it, the body clings to the place it came from, "so nothing was dearer to it than the world." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/5}

Of his transmitted sayings: "Whoever's intention is in the Hereafter, God fills his lap with the Hereafter"; "The renouncer of desires is content, and contentment with God is the rank of the messengers"; "It is not among the marks of love to love what your beloved hates"; "The mark of loving God is loving obedience to God"; "Whoever knows the world renounces it, whoever knows the Hereafter desires it, whoever knows God prefers His pleasure, and whoever does not know himself is deluded in his religion"; "If your soul prompts you to leave the world when it departs, that is deception; if it prompts you to leave it when it comes near, that is it." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/5-7}

He mentions hearing Utba ibn Abi al-Sa'ib say: "Three things are trials for the worshipper: illness, the hajj and marriage; whoever is steadfast in them is steadfast," and that when he complained to Abu Sulayman of hardness of heart or some worldly affair he would answer him with "what your hands have earned," and told him: "There is a station above patience." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/7}

His notice also lists the traveller's stations: from repentance to renunciation, then truthfulness, reliance, steadfastness, knowledge, remembrance, sweetness, intimacy, bashfulness and fear — and the mark of contentment is choosing nothing except what his Lord chooses for him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/8}
