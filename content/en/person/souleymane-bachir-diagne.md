Souleymane Bachir Diagne (born 1955 in Saint-Louis, Senegal — the French biography and Wikidata give 1 November, the English biography 8 November) is a Senegalese philosopher. After his baccalauréat in Senegal he prepared at the Lycée Louis-le-Grand in Paris, studied philosophy at the Sorbonne and at the École Normale Supérieure under Althusser and Derrida, took the agrégation in 1978, spent a year at Harvard, and completed doctorates at Paris I under Jean-Toussaint Desanti (1982, and the doctorat d'État in 1988) on George Boole's algebra of logic.{cite:wikipedia-diagne-fr}{cite:wikidata-diagne}{cite:wikipedia-diagne-en}

He taught at Cheikh Anta Diop University in Dakar, where he became vice-dean, served as counselor for education and culture to President Abdou Diouf (1993–1999), taught at Northwestern University (2002–2007), and then held the chair of French and philosophy at Columbia University, directing its Institute of African Studies; he ended his teaching career there in April 2025. His books move between the history of logic, the philosophy of Senghor and Iqbal, and philosophy in Islam, among them Comment philosopher en Islam (2010) and Open to Reason: Muslim Philosophers in Conversation with the Western Tradition (2018).{cite:wikipedia-diagne-en} A French press profile describes his first singularity as being at once Muslim, philosopher, democrat and rationalist, but no source names a legal school or an order for him, and his record accordingly leaves his faith unknown.{cite:les-echos-diagne-droit-2018}
