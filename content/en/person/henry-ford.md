## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Henry Ford as the man behind the 1908 assembly-line revolution, built on the principle of [[concept/taqsim-al-amal|division of labour]], which produced a great revolution in industry {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:25:00}.

## The assembly line
Al-Suwaidan recounts that cars used to be assembled by workers moving around a fixed chassis; Ford invented a moving line on which the chassis travels while workers on either side each add a part until the car is complete {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:25:30}.
He says the line takes about two days to complete a car, and that some eighteen thousand people take part in making a single car (needs_source: the figures are the speaker's, unchecked) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:27:00}.
He adds that 60 to 70 per cent of car manufacturing is now done by robots rather than people (needs_source) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:26:30}.
