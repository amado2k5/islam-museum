## Summary
Abdullah Ahmad Badawi (1939–2025) was a Malaysian statesman who served as the country's fifth prime minister from 2003 to 2009. Born at Bayan Lepas, Penang, into a prominent religious family, he worked in the civil service before entering parliament in 1978, rising through ministerial office to the deputy premiership and then succeeding Mahathir Mohamad, promoting his concept of Islam Hadhari. He is popularly known as "Pak Lah"; "Tun", by which he is styled, is an honorific title, not part of his name. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ahmad-badawi-en} {cite:wikidata-abdullah-ahmad-badawi}

## Early life and education
Abdullah bin Ahmad Badawi was born on 26 November 1939 at Kampung Perlis, Bayan Lepas, Penang, into a prominent religious family. His paternal grandfather, Syeikh Abdullah Badawi Fahim, a scholar of Hadrami descent, was among the founders of Hizbul Muslimin — the movement that later became the Pan-Malaysian Islamic Party (PAS) — and the first Mufti of Penang after independence; his father, Ahmad Badawi, was a religious teacher and UMNO member. Educated at schools in Penang, Abdullah graduated from the University of Malaya in 1964 with a degree in Islamic Studies. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ahmad-badawi-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ahmad-badawi-id}

## Civil service and early career
Abdullah joined the Malaysian Administrative and Diplomatic Corps in 1964, serving at the Ministry of Youth and Sport and on the National Operations Council, before resigning in 1978 to enter parliament as MP for Kepala Batas — a seat his father had also represented — which he held until 2013. He served in several portfolios, including education, defence and foreign affairs, and in January 1999 Mahathir appointed him deputy prime minister following Anwar Ibrahim's removal. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ahmad-badawi-en}

## Prime minister
Succeeding Mahathir in October 2003, Abdullah entered office on a reform agenda: his Barisan Nasional coalition won a landslide in the 2004 general election, he established a royal commission into police corruption, required ministers to declare their assets, and introduced Islam Hadhari, a moderate interpretation of Islam emphasising tolerance and modernity. The reform momentum faded, and in the 2008 general election the coalition lost its two-thirds majority and four state governments; he stepped down, and his deputy Najib Razak succeeded him on 3 April 2009. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ahmad-badawi-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ahmad-badawi-ar}

## Death and family
Abdullah died of multi-organ failure on 14 April 2025 at the National Heart Institute in Kuala Lumpur, aged eighty-five; he received a state funeral and was buried at the Heroes' Mausoleum near the National Mosque.
