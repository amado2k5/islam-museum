## Summary
Sultan Muzaffar Shah (d. 1459) was the fifth sultan of Malacca, the Malay trading sultanate of Southeast Asia. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} Born Raja Kassim, a son of Sultan Muhammad Shah and Tun Wati, he was installed after the death of his half-brother Abu Syahid Shah and ruled until 1459, when his son Mansur Shah succeeded him. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikidata-muzaffar-shah-malacca} The English article dates his accession to 1446 and the Malay article to 1445, with an August 1445 coronation; both years are preserved rather than harmonised. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-ms} No source consulted gives a birth year, and the museum's queue figure of 1400 is attested nowhere. {cite:wikidata-muzaffar-shah-malacca}

## Accession
According to the tradition the English article reports, his uncle Tun Ali, an influential Tamil Muslim leader, conspired to have the fourth sultan, Raja Sri Parameswara Dewa Shah, assassinated so that his nephew could ascend. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} On his installation Raja Kassim took the title Sultan Muzaffar Shah — Malay tradition counts him the first ruler of Malacca to bear the title of sultan at all — and ordered his senior officers to perform all the customs, traditions and prohibitions his late father had introduced. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-ms} He also freed Malacca from the overlordship of Ayutthaya. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-ms}

## Bendaharas, Siam and the Tun Kudu settlement
When Bendahara Seri Wak Raja I died, Muzaffar appointed his son Tun Perpatih Sedang as Seri Wak Raja II; the new bendahara drank poison after mistakenly believing the sultan displeased with him, leaving a daughter, Tun Kudu, and sons including Tun Perak. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} The sultan married Tun Kudu, sent Tun Perak away to Klang as chieftain, and made his uncle Tun Ali bendahara in gratitude for the throne. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} Siam attacked overland in 1445 and by sea in 1456; the sea attack was met at Batu Pahat by a Malacca fleet led by Tun Perak and driven back to Singapore, Malacca's first naval battle and a famous victory. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-ms} To end the dangerous feud between Tun Ali and Tun Perak, Muzaffar persuaded Tun Ali to step down on the one condition that he be allowed to marry Tun Kudu; the sultan divorced his own wife for the sake of political stability, Tun Kudu consented, the two rivals became close friends and collaborators, and Tun Perak was appointed bendahara with the title Paduka Raja around 1456. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-en}

## Law, diplomacy and death
Malay tradition credits his reign with the promulgation of the Hukum Kanun Melaka, the law code first issued under his father, and with diplomatic relations with China that strengthened Malacca's standing. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-ms} Muzaffar died in 1459, after the Siamese attack had been repelled, and his son Mansur Shah succeeded him. {cite:wikipedia-muzaffar-shah-malacca-ms} {cite:wikidata-muzaffar-shah-malacca}
