## Summary
Hassan Yang Faming (Yang Faming, born July 1965) is a Chinese Hui Muslim imam (ahong) who was elected president of the China Islamic Association in November 2016 — the state-linked body that liaises between the government of the People's Republic of China and the country's Muslim communities. He is also a member of the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC). {cite:wikipedia-yang-faming-zh} {cite:themuslim500-hassan-yang-faming}

## Origins and formation
Yang Faming was born in July 1965 in Helan County in the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, a historic heartland of the Hui — Chinese-speaking Muslims who form the most populous of China's recognised Muslim ethnic groups. His religious (scriptural) name is Hassan. He was trained as a member of the clergy and rose through the religious administration of Ningxia, serving as vice-president of the Ningxia regional Islamic association and as president of the Islamic association of Yinchuan, the regional capital. {cite:wikipedia-yang-faming-zh}

## The China Islamic Association
The China Islamic Association is the official, government-linked umbrella body for Islam in China. Founded in the 1950s, it organises pilgrimage affairs, oversees the training and accreditation of imams, publishes Qur'an editions and religious materials, and represents the country's Muslims in relations with the state. Its national congress, held once every five years, elects the leadership of the organisation. {cite:themuslim500-hassan-yang-faming}

## Presidency and national role
At the association's tenth national congress in Beijing in November 2016, more than 370 delegates from across China elected Yang Faming president, succeeding Chen Guangyuan; state media described him at the time as a renowned imam from Ningxia, then aged fifty-one. He was re-elected at the following congress, and in that capacity he addresses national conferences of clergy, signs off on the association's positions and represents Chinese Islam abroad. He entered the CPPCC — China's top political advisory body — as a member for the religious sector in 2008 and has retained his seat in successive committees. {cite:wikipedia-yang-faming-zh} {cite:themuslim500-hassan-yang-faming}

## Context and debate
The Muslim 500 profile notes that the association operates under the state's religious-affairs framework, at a time when the policy known as the Sinicization of religion has intensified, and when an estimated thirty million Muslims of several ethnicities — Hui the most numerous, Uyghurs the most concentrated in Xinjiang — live under close regulation of religious practice. Human-rights organisations attribute to this framework the internment of a large number of Uyghur Muslims; the Chinese government describes the same facilities as vocational education. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:themuslim500-hassan-yang-faming}

His entry rests principally on the Chinese-language record of his offices and on the Muslim 500 listing. {cite:wikipedia-yang-faming-zh}

## Duties of the office
As president, Yang presides over a bureaucracy of religious administration that reaches from the central secretariat in Beijing to provincial and county-level Islamic associations. The association arranges the annual pilgrimage mission to Mecca, reviews the appointment of ahongs, and issues guidance on sermons and religious education within the limits set by the state's religious-affairs regulations. Yang's public statements, carried by official media, consistently frame the work of Chinese Islam in the vocabulary of patriotism and harmony between religion and the socialist society in which it exists. {cite:wikipedia-yang-faming-zh}
