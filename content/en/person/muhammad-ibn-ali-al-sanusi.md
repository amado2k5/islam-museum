## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Sanus al-Khattabi al-Hasani al-Idrisi (d. 1275 AH / 1859 CE), founder of the Sanusi order and its first leader. Al-Zirikli says that he was born in Mostaganem in the districts of Algeria, studied in Fez, and travelled in the south of Algeria as a preacher. He then visited Tunis, Tripoli, Barqa, Egypt and Mecca. {cite:al-aalam tarjama}

## The lodge and the order
He built a lodge on Mount Abu Qubays in Mecca, then moved to Barqa and settled in the Green Mountain, where he built "the White Lodge". His disciples grew many and his order spread. The Ottoman government grew suspicious of him, so he moved to the oasis of Jaghbub and stayed there until he died. {cite:al-aalam tarjama}

## Notes
His birth date is confused in the printed text; the commonly given date is around 1202 AH / 1787 CE, and the conversion to the Gregorian calendar is derived. {cite:al-aalam tarjama}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "A Story and an Idea"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the twelfth episode of his programme [[work/kissa-wa-fikra-program|"A Story and an Idea"]] to al-Sanusi and his movement, under the theme of "jihadi Sufism": a Sufism with high spirituality and sound creed that resisted colonial rule. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:01:30} What follows is his account.

He describes the Sanusiyya as an Islamic call that arose in Libya, with centres across North Africa, Sudan, Somalia, Mecca and elsewhere, founded in the thirteenth century AH / nineteenth CE. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:02:00} He says al-Sanusi was born in 1202 AH / 1787 CE in Algeria, in a house of learning; his father died when he was two and his aunt Fatima raised him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:03:00} He memorised the Qur'an and studied seven years at the Qarawiyyin in Fez, where he formed his grounding in legal method and took up a Sunni Sufism "without deviant creed or sorcery"; he then taught in Fez and studied in Tripoli and at al-Azhar. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:05:00} He made the Hajj in 1240 AH / 1825 and settled in Mecca. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:05:30}

In Mecca, al-Suwaidan says, he studied with Hanbali teachers and with students of Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, joining Sufi method, Maghribi legal method and Salafi creed; in al-Suwaidan's view sound creed and a sound spiritual Sufism do not conflict. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:07:30} He was Maliki and departed from his school where he judged the evidence lay elsewhere, and in the Hajj season he urged pilgrims, Algerians above all, to resist French rule. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:08:30} He founded his first zawiya in Mecca as a centre of learning, conduct, creed and training; his students did not live off their learning but worked with their hands, farming and in crafts, so that the zawiya became a meeting place and a base rather than a place of withdrawal. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:11:00}

After fifteen years in Mecca he set out to join the jihad in Algeria; when French intelligence blocked his road he settled in Libya and supplied [[person/abd-al-qadir-al-jazairi|Abd al-Qadir al-Jaza'iri]] with men, money and arms. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:12:00} He placed his zawiyas at strategic points on the routes, al-Bayda being the first outside the Hijaz, and did not clash with the Ottoman state, though he did not accept its governors' injustices. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:13:30} He died in Libya in 1859, and the shaykhs pledged allegiance to his elder son [[person/muhammad-al-mahdi-al-sanusi|Muhammad al-Mahdi]], who spread the call among the tribes and united them against the French. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:15:00} Al-Mahdi died in 1902 and leadership passed to [[person/ahmed-sharif-as-senussi|Ahmad al-Sharif]]; from 1911 the Sanusis fought Italy in Libya. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:17:30} Al-Suwaidan carries the story on through [[person/omar-mukhtar|Omar al-Mukhtar]], whom he calls a Sanusi Sufi, to [[person/idris-senussi|Idris al-Sanusi]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:24:00} His conclusion: "true Sufism" has sound creed and method, does not withdraw from life, avoids superstition, resists occupiers and stands up to tyrants. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep12 00:27:00}
