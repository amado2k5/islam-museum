## Summary
The imam, hadith memorist, legal theorist and jurist Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn Musa al-Bayhaqi al-Khusrawjirdi, the shaykh of the Sunna and traditionist of his age; born in 384/994, he died at Nishapur in 458/1066 and was buried at Khusrawjird {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1190}.

## His life
He transmitted from al-Hakim Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Nisaburi and others, and travelled through many lands in pursuit of hadith until his shaykhs were said to number about a hundred; in creed he was an Ash'arite, and by character frugal, pious and scholarly {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1190}. At the opening of 445/1053 the ordeal struck Nishapur: the vizier Amid al-Mulk al-Kunduri ordered the cursing of innovators from the pulpits and turned it against the Ash'arites. Al-Bayhaqi was then in the city of Bayhaq; when the news reached him it grieved him, and he wrote to al-Kunduri his famous letter defending al-Ash'ari and asking him to quench the fire and abandon the cursing {cite:tabaqat-shafiiyya-subki pp. 269-276}. He then joined the scholars' exodus, making the Pilgrimage with Abu al-Qasim al-Qushayri and others, while Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni left for the Hijaz where he received his title {cite:tabaqat-shafiiyya-subki pp. 271-272}.

## Works and death
He was a voluminous writer, his works said to reach a thousand fascicules; most famous is the Sunan al-Kubra, of which al-Subki said he knew nothing like it for accuracy, arrangement and quality, alongside the Dala'il al-Nubuwwa, al-Adab, and al-I'tiqad wa-l-Hidaya; Imam al-Haramayn praised his writings in support of the Shafi'i school {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1190}. Toward the end of his life he moved to Nishapur and remained there until his death in 458/1066 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1190}.
