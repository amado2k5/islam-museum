## Summary
Murad II, son of Mehmed Çelebi, took the throne in 824/1421 before he was eighteen — a sultan the book describes as devoted to jihad, pious, just and merciful. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 77-79}

He ended the rising of his uncle [[person/mustafa-ibn-bayezid|Mustafa]], which the book says emperor [[person/manuel-ii-palaiologos|Manuel II]] financed, stormed Thessalonica in March 833/1431, bound Wallachia by annual tribute and the new Serbian king by renewed allegiance. On the Hungarian front the Ottomans won in 842/1438 with seventy thousand captives, though the Belgrade attempt failed. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 79-80}

After Hunyadi's twin victories in 846/1442 the Ottomans sued for the [[event/treaty-szeged-848|Treaty of Szeged]], a ten-year peace sworn in July 848/1444 — Murad on the Qur'an, Ladislas on the Gospel — whereupon Murad abdicated to his son Mehmed and retired to Magnesia. When Cardinal Cesarini and the pope preached the treaty's annulment, he returned to lead the army: forty thousand men ferried past the crusader fleet on Genoese hulls at a dinar a head, the broken treaty raised on a spear-point, and King Ladislas unhorsed by Murad's own lance at [[event/battle-varna-848|Varna]]. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 80-82}

He abdicated a second time, then returned when the Janissaries rioted at Edirne and held the throne to the end, crowned by the second Kosovo victory of 852/1448. He held a twice-weekly poets' majlis, sent 3,500 dinars yearly to the two sanctuaries and Jerusalem, and died in the Edirne palace at about forty-seven — buried by his ascetic will beside the Muradiyya mosque at Bursa. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 82-85}
