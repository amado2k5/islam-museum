## Summary
Murad II, son of Mehmed Çelebi, took the throne in 824/1421 before he was eighteen — a sultan the book describes as devoted to jihad, pious, just and merciful. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 77-79}

He ended the rising of his uncle [[person/mustafa-ibn-bayezid|Mustafa]], which the book says emperor [[person/manuel-ii-palaiologos|Manuel II]] financed, stormed Thessalonica in March 833/1431, bound Wallachia by annual tribute and the new Serbian king by renewed allegiance. On the Hungarian front the Ottomans won in 842/1438 with seventy thousand captives, though the Belgrade attempt failed. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 79-80}

After Hunyadi's twin victories in 846/1442 the Ottomans sued for the [[event/treaty-szeged-848|Treaty of Szeged]], a ten-year peace sworn in July 848/1444 — Murad on the Qur'an, Ladislas on the Gospel — whereupon Murad abdicated to his son Mehmed and retired to Magnesia. When Cardinal Cesarini and the pope preached the treaty's annulment, he returned to lead the army: forty thousand men ferried past the crusader fleet on Genoese hulls at a dinar a head, the broken treaty raised on a spear-point, and King Ladislas unhorsed by Murad's own lance at [[event/battle-varna-848|Varna]]. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 80-82}

He abdicated a second time, then returned when the Janissaries rioted at Edirne and held the throne to the end, crowned by the second Kosovo victory of 852/1448. He held a twice-weekly poets' majlis, sent 3,500 dinars yearly to the two sanctuaries and Jerusalem, and died in the Edirne palace at about forty-seven — buried by his ascetic will beside the Muradiyya mosque at Bursa. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 82-85}

## For Sprouts
Sultan Murad II ibn Mehmed I, who took rule in 824 AH before he was eighteen; he entered Thessaloniki in 833 AH and prevailed at Varna and the second Kosovo.

## For Explorers
Sultan Murad II ibn Mehmed I, who took rule in 824 AH (1421 CE) after his father's death at no more than eighteen; al-Salabi describes him as fond of jihad, known for piety and justice. He ended his uncle Mustafa's revolt, backed by the Byzantine emperor Manuel II; he entered Thessaloniki in March 833 AH (1431 CE), subdued Wallachia for a yearly tribute and Serbia's new king to his rule. The Ottomans beat the Hungarians in 842 AH (1438 CE) but failed at Belgrade. After Hunyadi's two defeats the ten-year Treaty of Szeged was made in 848 AH (1444 CE); he abdicated to his son Mehmed and withdrew to Manisa, then returned when the oath was broken and brought down King Ladislas at Varna. He prevailed at the second Kosovo in 852 AH (1448 CE), died in his Edirne palace at about forty-seven, and was buried beside the Muradiye mosque in Bursa.

## For Scholars
Sultan Murad II ibn Mehmed I, who took rule in 824 AH (1421 CE) after his father Mehmed I Jalabi's death at no more than eighteen; al-Salabi describes him as fond of jihad, known for piety, justice and mercy. He ended his uncle Mustafa's revolt, backed by the Byzantine emperor Manuel II, then entered Thessaloniki by force in March 833 AH (1431 CE); he subdued Wallachia for a yearly tribute and Serbia's new king to his rule. On the Hungarian front the Ottomans beat the Hungarians in 842 AH (1438 CE) taking seventy thousand captives, but the bid for Belgrade failed. After Hunyadi's two victories in 846 AH (1442 CE) the Ottomans asked for peace and the ten-year Treaty of Szeged was sworn in July 848 AH (1444 CE) — Murad on the Qur'an, Ladislas on the Gospel; he abdicated to his son Mehmed and withdrew to Manisa. When Cardinal Cesarini and the Pope urged breaking the oath he returned to lead the army — the Genoese fleet carried forty thousand at a dinar a man — raised the broken treaty on a lance, fought King Ladislas and brought him down: the battle of Varna. He returned and abdicated a second time, was recalled when the Janissaries rose in Edirne, held the throne to his life's end, and prevailed at the second Kosovo in 852 AH (1448 CE). He held a poets' circle two days a week and sent the two Sanctuaries and Jerusalem three thousand five hundred dinars yearly; he died in the Edirne palace at about forty-seven and was buried beside the Muradiye mosque in Bursa by his will.
