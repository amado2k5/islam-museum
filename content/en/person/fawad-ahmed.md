## Summary
Fawad Ahmed is an Australian former cricketer, a right-arm leg-spin bowler born in Swabi, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. His birth date is in conflict between the sources: the English Wikipedia article in its current revision gives 10 March 1979, while Wikidata gives 5 February 1982; this record follows the article and records the conflict in the born field. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en} {cite:wikidata-fawad-ahmed}

He left Pakistan in 2010 and sought asylum in Australia, was granted permanent residency in November 2012 and Australian citizenship in July 2013, and played three One Day Internationals and two Twenty20 Internationals for Australia in August and September 2013. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en}

## Career in Pakistan, emigration and the asylum claim
Ahmed began playing for Swabi District in local competitions and made his first-class debut for Abbottabad in 2005, resuming first-class cricket in 2009 with Pakistan Customs, for whom he took his then-best figures of 6 for 109 against the Karachi Whites in January 2009. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en}

He left Pakistan in 2010, emigrating to Australia on a short-stay visa sponsored by the Yoogali Cricket Association, and on arrival applied for refugee status, claiming he was persecuted by religious extremists for playing and coaching cricket; his hometown is in northwest Pakistan, a region bordering Afghanistan that was affected by the war in the country's northwest. He settled in Melbourne and rose quickly from local cricket to Victorian Premier Cricket with Melbourne University. His initial application was declined, but he was granted permanent residency in November 2012 and signed with the Melbourne Renegades that month under a special exemption to play in the Big Bash League. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en}

## Career in Australia
Ahmed took 10 wickets at 18.00 in five one-day matches for Victoria in the 2012–13 Ryobi One Day Cup and 16 wickets in Victoria's last three Sheffield Shield matches. On 2 July 2013 he was granted Australian citizenship, clearing him to play for the full national team; he debuted in a T20I against England at Southampton on 29 August 2013 and played his three ODIs on the same tour in September 2013. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en}

In the 2014–15 Sheffield Shield final he took 8 for 89 in 40 overs, the best bowling figures in a Shield final, and in March 2015 he was named in Australia's Test squad for the tours of the West Indies and England, without playing a Test. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en}

He went on to play franchise cricket around the world: the Sydney Thunder, Perth Scorchers and Adelaide Strikers in Australia, the Trinbago Knight Riders in the Caribbean Premier League, and Quetta Gladiators, Islamabad United and Lahore Qalandars in the Pakistan Super League, returning to the Melbourne Renegades as a replacement player in January 2023. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en}

## Faith and practice as the sources report it
This section reports what the sources say, attributed to them; it is not the museum's verdict. The English Wikipedia article describes Ahmed as "a devout Muslim" who has missed training sessions during Ramadan and is known to leave the field to pray during games. In 2013 he requested that the logos of sponsors whose activities he disagreed with be removed from his kit, and the Victoria Bitter beer logo was removed from his shirt, The Telegraph reported, because Islam prohibits alcohol. No source names a school for him, so his record's faith field stays "unknown" under the museum's two-source rule and doubt rule. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en} {cite:wikidata-fawad-ahmed}

## Reach
Ahmed's public significance lies in his story as one of only two Pakistan-born cricketers to represent Australia, in his reaching international cricket by way of an asylum claim in his thirties, in his record bowling performance in the 2014–15 Sheffield Shield final, and in the visibility of his religious practice within Australian cricket, including the removal of the beer sponsor's logo from his shirt. {cite:wikipedia-fawad-ahmed-en}
