## Summary
al-Amir Husayn, son of the Hafsid ruler Abu Faris — an aspiring rebel, captured and killed in 839 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}.

## Details
Quoting Ibn Hajar, Ibn al-'Imad calls al-Amir Husayn ibn Amir al-Muslimin Abi Faris al-Hafsi "the imam, the polymath, the mufti, the amir son of the amir." After his brother had been established on the throne of the Maghreb, Husayn sought to rise in revolt; he was overpowered and killed together with two of his brothers, and the calamity of his death was felt deeply because he had been a virtuous, clever debater {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}. The Hafsids then ruled Tunis and the central Maghreb, and the princes of the house contested the throne among themselves, so this prince perished in the struggle despite being son and heir of the ruling amir {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}. His biography joins scholarship and rule: Ibn Hajar counts him a learned mufti while also an amir's son — a common combination in Maghribi ruling houses whose princes studied the sciences alongside politics {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}.
