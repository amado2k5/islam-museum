# Fayzulla Khodzhayev

Fayzulla Khodzhayev (1 June 1896 – 15 March 1938) was a Bukharan and Uzbek Soviet politician, born in Bukhara into the family of a wealthy merchant and sent by his father to Russia at the age of eleven to study. He joined the Jadid reform movement in 1916 and, with his father's fortune, helped establish the Young Bukharan Party; after the failed attempt to form a Young Bukharan government in March 1918 he fled to Tashkent under sentence of death, returning only when the Red Army overthrew the Emir in September 1920.{cite:wikipedia-fayzulla-khodzhayev-en}{cite:wikipedia-fayzulla-khodzhayev-ru}

Having joined the Russian Communist Party in the summer of 1920, he became chairman of the Council of People's Nazirs — head of government — of the Bukharan People's Soviet Republic, surviving a Basmachi assassination attempt in July 1922. After the creation of the Uzbek SSR he chaired its Revolutionary Committee and then, from February 1925, its Council of People's Commissars, and sat as one of the chairmen of the Central Executive Committee of the USSR; although he kept the headship of the Uzbek government until 1937, from 1929 his influence was eclipsed by Akmal Ikramov, and he opposed Stalin's cotton monoculture policy. The Russian article gives his patronymic as Gubaydullaevich and the Uzbek form as Ubaydulloyevich.{cite:wikipedia-fayzulla-khodzhayev-en}{cite:wikipedia-fayzulla-khodzhayev-ru}

Removed from office on 27 June 1937 and arrested days later, Khodzhayev was a defendant at the Trial of the Twenty-One in March 1938 alongside Ikramov and Bukharin. He was sentenced to death on 13 March and shot at the Kommunarka shooting ground on 15 March 1938 — the English article's body text dates the execution itself to 13 March, a variant not followed — and was buried at Kommunarka. He was fully rehabilitated in 1965 or 1966, the two articles differing by a year.{cite:wikipedia-fayzulla-khodzhayev-en}{cite:wikipedia-fayzulla-khodzhayev-ru}{cite:wikidata-fayzulla-khodzhayev}
