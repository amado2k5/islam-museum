## Overview
Muhammad ibn Khalaf ibn Hayyan ibn Sadaqa al-Dabbi, Abu Bakr, surnamed Waki', was a judge and researcher learned in history and geography, as al-Zirikli describes him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 114-115}. Al-Safadi calls him the judge Waki' and says he was versed in the biographies (siyar) and the days of the people {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 3 pp. 36-37}. Ibn al-Imad lists him in the events of 306 AH as Muhammad ibn Khalaf, Waki' al-Qadi, Abu Bakr al-Akhbari, author of works; the part of his lineage in square brackets is an addition by the editors of the Shadharat, taken from al-Dhahabi's Siyar A'lam al-Nubala and the sources in its margin {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 33}.

## Career
Al-Zirikli says he held the judgeship of al-Ahwaz and died in Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 114-115}. Al-Safadi agrees that he held the judgeship of the districts of al-Ahwaz {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 3 pp. 36-37}, and Ibn al-Imad that he held the judgeship of al-Ahwaz {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 33}. Ibn al-Imad adds that he transmitted from al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar and his generation {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 33}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists Akhbar al-Qudat wa-Tawarikhuhum, printed in three volumes and known as Tabaqat al-Qudat; al-Tariq, also called al-Nawahi, on the reports of lands and the routes of the roads; al-Sharif, on the pattern of Ibn Qutayba's al-Ma'arif; al-Anwa'; the number of the verses of the Qur'an and the differences over it; shooting and contests; and measures and weights {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 114-115}. Al-Safadi's list is similar: the number of the verses of the Qur'an, Akhbar al-Qudat wa-Tawarikhuhum, al-Anwa', al-Sharif in the manner of al-Ma'arif, al-Ghurar with reports, al-Tarif (called al-Nawahi) on lands and routes, and a work on money, coinage and minting {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 3 pp. 36-37}. Al-Safadi quotes al-Khatib as saying it reached him that Ibn Mujahid was asked to write a book on the counting of verses and answered that Waki' had spared them the need {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 3 pp. 36-37}. He also gives two lines of verse in which Waki' describes himself as a student of knowledge whose inkwell is his ear and whose notebook is his heart {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 3 pp. 36-37}.

## Judgements of scholars
Ibn al-Imad quotes al-Dhahabi in al-Mughni that he was well known and had works, and Ibn al-Munadi that there was some softness (layyin) in him {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 33}.

## Death
He died in 306 AH, which corresponds to 918 CE by the museum's conversion (derived); al-Zirikli {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 114-115}, Ibn al-Imad {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 33} and al-Safadi {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 3 pp. 36-37} all place him in that year.
