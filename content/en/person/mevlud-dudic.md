## Summary
Mevlud Dudic is the Grand Mufti of Serbia, president of the Meshihat of the Islamic Community in Serbia since 2014, and one of the Sandzak's most prominent Muslim figures: imam and preacher, then madrasa director, faculty dean and university rector before taking his country's highest Muslim office. {cite:muslim500-mevlud-dudic} {cite:mesihat-mevlud-dudic}

## Life and upbringing
Born in 1966 in the village of Biohane in the Tutin municipality of the Sandzak, he graduated from the Alauddin Madrasa in Pristina ⁦(1981–1985)⁩ and from the Faculty of Islamic Sciences in Sarajevo ⁦(1985–1989)⁩, with a semester's specialisation at al-Azhar in Cairo in 1991 and another in Virginia, USA, in 2002. He took a master's degree in 2005 on the emergence and development of the Novi Pazar madrasa, and a doctorate in 2007 on the role of the sibjan mekteb in preserving Islam in the Sandzak, both at the International University of Novi Pazar. {cite:mesihat-mevlud-dudic}

## Academic career
He began in 1991 as preacher, imam and khatib for the Tutin board of the Islamic Community, taught qira'at, fiqh, aqida, hadith and imamate at the Gazi Isa-beg Madrasa in Novi Pazar, then directed it for twelve years ⁦(1994–2006)⁩, became dean of the Faculty of Islamic Studies in 2006 and rector of the International University of Novi Pazar for two terms ⁦(2008–2014)⁩. {cite:mesihat-mevlud-dudic}

## The muftiate
Elected in January 2014 president of the Meshihat — Serbia's chief mufti — he serves a community estimated at some 700,000. He sits on the government commission for religious education, speaks Arabic, English and Albanian, and maintains wide ties with scholars of the Muslim world and membership in its main organisations. {cite:muslim500-mevlud-dudic} {cite:mesihat-mevlud-dudic}

## Works and legacy
Among his writings is the monograph The Gazi Isa-beg Madrasa: The Al-Azhar of Sandzak, alongside hundreds of papers and articles. His path reads as the Balkan model of the institutional scholar: Sarajevo-and-Azhar formation, long educational administration, then the unified religious leadership of a Muslim minority in a majority-Orthodox state. {cite:muslim500-mevlud-dudic}
## Within the community
Before the muftiate he served the community's own institutions: member of the Islamic Community's assembly for Kosovo, Serbia and Vojvodina until 1993, when he took part in founding the Sandzak Meshihat; he was twice elected deputy president of the Meshihat and deputy mufti of Sandzak, and edited the magazine Takvim for two years. {cite:mesihat-mevlud-dudic}

## International formation
The Islamic Community's supreme leadership sent him to al-Azhar in 1991 for missionary specialisation; he went to Virginia in 2001 for training in "non-violent conflict resolution" and in 2008 to New York, Washington, Texas and Chicago for a leadership course — combining classical formation with modern institutional skills. {cite:mesihat-mevlud-dudic}
