## Overview
'Abd Allah ibn Muhammad was the seventh Umayyad amir of Cordova. He succeeded his brother al-Mundhir on 15 Safar 275/29 June 888, and reigned for about a quarter of a century until his death on 1 Rabi I 300/16 October 912. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 68}

## Reign
The chronicler Ibn Hayyan described the reign in detail, in the part of his Muqtabis preserved in an Oxford manuscript. Biographers present a flattering picture of him, stressing his sobriety, piety and Islamic culture, and do not mention his cruelty. He kept the Umayyad dynasty alive through a difficult period, against the Andalusian revolt led by the muwallads and the particularist Arab gentry of Seville and Elvira. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 68}

## In Nafh al-Tib min Ghusn al-Andalus al-Ratib — al-Maqqari
Abd Allah ibn Muhammad al-Umawi, the eighth of the Umayyad amirs in [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]], who ruled from 275 to 300 AH, about twenty-five years. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.389}

Al-Maqqari, quoting Ibn Khaldun, says that the revenue of al-Andalus before him had been three hundred thousand dinars: a hundred thousand for the armies, a hundred thousand for the expenses of calamities, and a hundred thousand in reserve. He spent the reserve when the regions of al-Andalus were in turmoil with rebels and usurpers, and the revenue fell. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.388}

Al-Maqqari gives some of his verse, and says that he wrote a lampoon to his vizier al-Nadr ibn Salama when he did not like his opinion. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari pp.388–389}
