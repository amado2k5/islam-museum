# Lalla Fatma N'Soumer

## Summary

Lalla Fatma N'Soumer (c. 1830 – September 1863), born Fadhma Si Ahmed Ou Méziane, was a Kabyle leader of the Algerian resistance to the French conquest, remembered as the heroine of the Djurdjura.{cite:wikipedia-fatma-nsoumer-en}

## Early life

She was born about 1830 at Ouerdja, near Aïn El Hammam in Kabylie, into a maraboutic family; her father headed a Qur'anic school attached to a zawiya of the Rahmaniyya order, and she received the schooling usually reserved for boys.{cite:wikipedia-fatma-nsoumer-fr} She refused the marriage arranged for her and devoted herself to prayer, teaching and the care of the poor.{cite:wikipedia-fatma-nsoumer-fr}

## The resistance

In the Dahra she met Sherif Boubaghla, who from 1850 led a rising in the Babor Mountains, and she became his ally in the resistance in Kabylie.{cite:wikipedia-fatma-nsoumer-en} After Boubaghla's death she continued the struggle in the Djurdjura, organising supplies and leading the tribes in battle against the French columns of Marshal Randon.{cite:wikipedia-fatma-nsoumer-fr}

## Capture and death

On 11 July 1857 she was captured and imprisoned in the zawiya of El-Aissaouia at Beni Slimane, then held under house arrest; she died there in September 1863, aged about 33.{cite:wikipedia-fatma-nsoumer-ar} In 1995 her remains were transferred to the El Alia Cemetery in Algiers.{cite:wikipedia-fatma-nsoumer-ar}
