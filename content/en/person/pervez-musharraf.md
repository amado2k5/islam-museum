## Overview
Pervez Musharraf (11 August 1943 – 5 February 2023) was a Pakistani general and politician who ruled Pakistan from the October 1999 coup until his resignation in August 2008, first as chief executive and then as president. Under him, Pakistan's face was on the world's screens daily: a military ruler fluent in English who allied his country with Washington after the September 11 attacks, opened it to dozens of private television channels, and saw his rule end amid a lawyers' movement that shook the country and a treason trial that followed him to his death. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en} {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-ar}

## Early life and education
Musharraf was born in Delhi, British India, to an Urdu-speaking family; his father was a diplomat in the civil service. He was four when Pakistan was created in 1947, and his family left for Pakistan days before independence. The family moved to Ankara in 1949 on a diplomatic posting, where he learned Turkish; he returned to Pakistan, attended Saint Patrick's School in Karachi and Forman Christian College in Lahore, and entered the Pakistan Military Academy at Kakul in 1961, aged eighteen. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en} {cite:wikidata-pervez-musharraf}

## Beginnings and career
Musharraf rose through the ranks of the Pakistan Army to become chief of army staff in October 1998, appointed by Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif. After the 1999 Kargil conflict, Sharif attempted to dismiss him and to prevent his aircraft from landing at Karachi; the army answered with the coup of 12 October 1999, which removed the government and brought Musharraf to power. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}

## Rule: the American alliance and the opening
Musharraf ruled as chief executive, then appointed himself president on 20 June 2001 after President Rafiq Tarar's resignation. After the September 11 attacks his government allied Pakistan with the United States in its Afghan war; he liberalised the media, as private channels multiplied, introduced local-government reforms, and his early years saw rapid economic growth. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}

His rule also saw, according to the article, constitutional manipulation, curbs on the judiciary, enforced disappearances and a growing insurgency in the North-West Frontier Province. When he dismissed Chief Justice Iftikhar Chaudhry in March 2007, a lawyers' movement and wide protests erupted; he then imposed a state of emergency in November 2007, his support eroded, and on 18 August 2008 he announced his resignation to avoid impeachment. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}

## Exile, trial and death
Musharraf lived in exile after 2008, between London and Dubai, and in 2010 founded the All Pakistan Muslim League, which won no significant seats in later elections. He returned to Pakistan in 2013 and faced several cases, chief among them the treason case over the 2007 emergency. On 17 December 2019 a special court declared him a traitor and sentenced him in absentia to death for suspending the constitution; on 13 January 2020 the Lahore High Court annulled the sentence, ruling that the special court had been formed unconstitutionally. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en} {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-ar}

Musharraf died in Dubai on 5 February 2023, aged 79, after a prolonged illness. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en} {cite:wikidata-pervez-musharraf}

## Positions and criticism
This section presents differing views as reported in the sources; it is not the museum's verdict. Assessments of his legacy are sharply divided, according to the article.

**His supporters** credit him with economic stabilisation and social liberalisation, with freeing the media and with middle-class growth in his early years. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}

**His critics** highlight the erosion of democratic institutions, the curbs on the judiciary, and the human cost of the War on Terror inside Pakistan; the judiciary convicted him of treason before the conviction was annulled for a defect in the court's formation. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}

## Writings
Musharraf published his memoir "In the Line of Fire" in 2006, recounting his upbringing, military service and years in power. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}

## Personal life
He grew up in a diplomatic family that moved between Turkey and Pakistan, and spoke Turkish alongside Urdu and English. The museum publishes no family detail beyond what understanding his public career needs. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}

## Impact and audience
Musharraf left a double mark: a more open media landscape and changed cities, and a judicial precedent that made him the first military ruler in Pakistan to be tried for subverting the constitution. {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-ar} {cite:wikipedia-pervez-musharraf-en}
