## Overview
Sukman al-Qutbi founded the state of Akhlat (Khilat), one of the [[polity/shah-arman-akhlat|Shah-i Arman]] principalities, in 493 AH (1100 CE). His amirate, in the south-west of Armenia, was the strongest of the small dynasties that arose there under Seljuk rule, even though most of its population was Armenian and it covered only about a fifth of Armenia. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 p.89}

## After the dynasty
When the Banu Sukman line died out in 581 AH (1185 CE), rule passed to the mamluk Bak Timur (1185–1197 CE), and then the Ayyubids took the country in 604 AH; the sultan al-Malik al-Adil installed his son as amir of Khilat, succeeded by his brother al-Ashraf, who ruled independently after al-Adil's death and extended his territory into the Kurdish lands. In 642 AH (1244 CE) the kingdom of Khilat fell to Hulagu; its last Ayyubid king was al-Muzaffar Ghazi. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 p.89}
