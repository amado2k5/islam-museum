## Summary
Umar ibn Uthman, Abu Hafs al-Waʿiz, known as Ibn Shahin (297–385 AH), hadith scholar of Baghdad's eastern side and author of thirty-three works. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.186-187}

## Eleven years old
Ibn Shahin said: "The first time I wrote hadith with my own hand was in the year 308 AH, when I was eleven years old. Three of my shaykhs had likewise written at that age, and I took blessing from them: Abu al-Qasim al-Baghawi, Abu Bakr ibn Ṣāʿid and Abu Bakr ibn Abi Dawud." {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.186}

## Thirty-three works
He said: "I composed thirty-three works: the great commentary (al-Tafsir al-Kabir) in a thousand parts, the Musnad in one thousand five hundred parts, the History in a hundred and fifty parts, and the book on asceticism in a hundred parts." He once reckoned up what he had bought of ink up to that time, and it came to seven hundred dirhams. He died on the twenty-first of Dhu al-Hijja in the year 385 AH and was buried in the cemetery of Bab Harb. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.186-187}
