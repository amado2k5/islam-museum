## Overview
Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Mahmud al-Baghdadi, known as al-Shabash, who settled in Basra with his father and his uncle. The sources differ on their creed, and it is most likely that they were Imami Shi'a and extreme Batiniyya. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.152-153}

## Their wealth and followers
They had wide blessings and much money, and followers from the countryside of Basra and from the Qaramita, who hid their obedience and brought to them what they reckoned as alms. Abu al-Hasan showed himself mixing with the soldiers and governors, excusing himself by saying he meant to remove suspicion from himself. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.152-153}

## The report of the birds
Ibn al-Jawzi relates, from the hand of Ibn Aqil, that he had birds that carried notes to the villages, and informed their people of their affairs, so they were astonished. It is attributed to him that he tied a letter to the leg of a sparrow and said "O Barash", giving the impression that he was addressing a devil. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.152-153}

## Notes for researchers
- This is Ibn al-Jawzi's report from Ibn Aqil, and it needs an additional source.
