## Summary
Abd al-Rahman ibn Hasan al-Jabarti al-Aqili al-Zayla'i al-Hanafi al-Khalwati was an Egyptian historian who recorded the events of Egypt and the lives of its men in the late Mamluk-Ottoman age, the time of the French expedition and the beginning of Muhammad Ali's rule, in his [[work/ajaib-al-athar|Aja'ib al-athar fi al-tarajim wa-l-akhbar]]. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.13} He was the son of [[person/hassan-al-jabarti|Hasan al-Jabarti]], Hanafi jurist and mathematician, and inherited his library. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.15}

## Teachers and sources
In his youth he attended his teacher [[person/murtada-al-zabidi|Murtada al-Zabidi]] and studied with him; al-Zabidi wrote in his dictionary that al-Jabarti "has compiled a history of his age with excellent things in it" and had helped him with some biographies. After al-Zabidi's death in 1791 al-Jabarti bought his papers from his widow; al-Muradi wrote from Damascus asking for what had been collected, but died soon after, so the biographies he and his teacher had gathered stayed with him. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.14} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.15}

## His historical works
He first wrote on the period of the French in Egypt, then Mazhar al-taqdis, condemning the French and praising the Ottomans, which was well received and ordered to be translated into Turkish; he then felt moved "by an impulse of his own" to write his great history, drawing on Mazhar al-taqdis for its third part and revising his stance after witnessing the Ottoman troops' oppression. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.17} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.18} His editor Shmuel Moreh holds that he sought the causes of Europe's superiority and of the Mamluks' and Ottomans' weakness before it, and criticised what he saw as injustice by the Mamluks, the Ottomans, the French and Muhammad Ali. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.19}

## His last years
He continued the fourth part until his son Khalil was killed on the night of 27 Ramadan 1237, after which he lost his sight and wrote no more; but he checked a copy that the copyist Ahmad ibn Hasan al-Rashidi read to him "from beginning to end" on Saturday 14 Rabi' I 1240. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.18} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.23}

## The year of his death
The year of his death is disputed:
- The Mawsu'at a'lam al-ulama' wa-l-udaba' gives his life as 1167-1237 AH. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}
- Jurji Zaydan, David Ayalon and others took the copyist's note of 1240 ("in the sight and hearing of its author, may God prolong his life") and placed his death in 1240. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.18}
- Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Hadrawi, citing Amin Efendi al-Hulwani of Medina, says he went blind after writing his book, kept teaching at al-Azhar, and died in 1241, being buried at al-Mujawirin. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.27}
- Moreh favours 24 Ramadan 1240, inferring it from the shari'a court document dated 27 Ramadan 1240 confirming his children Mahfuz and Aman and the two children of their murdered brother Khalil as administrators of his endowments, since such a confirmation usually followed the three days of condolence. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.27} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.28}
Moreh notes that the copyists did not record the year of his death and that he found no elegy for him by his friend Hasan al-Attar. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.26} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-2013-v1 p.27}
