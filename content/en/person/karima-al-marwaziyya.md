## Overview
Karima, daughter of Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hatim, known as Umm al-Kiram al-Marwaziyya, was a woman hadith transmitter who lived as a neighbour of the sanctuary at Mecca. Ibn al-Imad lists her among the deaths of 463 AH, which corresponds to 1070 CE by the museum's conversion {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 266}. Ibn al-Jawzi gives her name as Karima, daughter of Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Hatim al-Marwaziyya, and says she came from Kushmihan, a village among the villages of Merv {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 16 pp. 134-135}.

## Life and transmission
Ibn al-Jawzi calls her a learned and devout woman who heard hadith from Abu al-Haytham al-Kushmihani and others {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 16 pp. 134-135}. Ibn al-Imad says she transmitted the Sahih on the authority of al-Kushmihani, and also transmitted from Zahir al-Sarakhsi {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 266}.

Ibn al-Imad adds that she kept her book carefully and collated it against other copies, that she had understanding and sharpness, that she never married, and that it was said she reached the age of one hundred, a remark he attributes to the work al-'Ibar. He notes that Ibn al-Ahdal counted her among the hadith masters {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 266}.

## Students and death
Ibn al-Jawzi writes that leading scholars read hadith with her, among them al-Khatib, Ibn al-Muttalib, al-Sam'ani and Abu Talib al-Zaynabi {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 16 p. 135}. She died at Mecca in 463 AH {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 16 p. 135}.
