## Overview
Ahmad ibn Idris ibn Abd al-Rahman, Abu al-Abbas, Shihab al-Din al-Sanhaji al-Qarafi, was one of the Maliki scholars. Al-Zirikli gives his death as 684 AH, 1285 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 94-95}.

## His nisba and homeland
His nisba points to the Sanhaja tribe, of the Berbers of the Maghrib, and to al-Qarafa, the quarter beside the grave of Imam al-Shafi'i in Cairo. He was Egyptian by birth, upbringing and death {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 94-95}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists writings in law and legal theory, among them Anwar al-Buruq fi Anwa' al-Furuq, printed in four parts; al-Ihkam fi Tamyiz al-Fatawa an al-Ahkam wa-Tasarruf al-Qadi wa-l-Imam, printed; al-Dhakhira in Maliki law, in manuscript in six volumes; al-Yawaqit fi Ahkam al-Mawaqit, in manuscript in Rabat; Sharh Tanqih al-Fusul on legal theory, printed; Mukhtasar Tanqih al-Fusul, printed; al-Khasa'is on the rules of Arabic, in manuscript; and al-Ajwiba al-Fakhira fi al-Radd ala al-As'ila al-Fajira, printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 94-95}.

## Mechanical figures
In a remark added within the entry, al-Zirikli says that besides his mastery of many fields al-Qarafi was among the skilled makers of moving figures in astronomical instruments and similar devices. He quotes from al-Qarafi's Sharh al-Mahsul: he had heard that al-Malik al-Kamil had a candelabrum from which a door opened each hour of the night and a figure came out to stand in the king's service; he made one himself, adding that the colour of the candle changed every hour and that a lion on it had eyes shifting from deep black to white to deep red; at dawn a figure rose to the top of the candelabrum with a finger in its ear, pointing to the call to prayer; he adds that he could not make it speak {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 95}.
