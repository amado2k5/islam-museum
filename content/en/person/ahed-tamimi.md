## Summary
Ahed Tamimi is a Palestinian activist from the village of Nabi Saleh north of Ramallah, known to the world through filmed confrontations, from childhood, with Israeli soldiers in a village known for its weekly anti-settlement marches. Her image became an icon of Palestinian popular resistance and a focus of global debate about childhood under occupation. {cite:wikipedia-ahed-tamimi}

## Upbringing and early activism
She was born in 2001 in Nabi Saleh into an activist family: her father Bassem al-Tamimi and mother Nariman al-Tamimi are among the faces of the village's popular resistance, which has held weekly demonstrations since 2009 against the seizure of its land for the adjacent Halamish settlement. Ahed appeared as a child in footage circulated from 2012, including a scene in which she and her cousin confronted Israeli soldiers; for this she received Turkey's Handala Courage Award in 2012 while still a young girl. {cite:wikipedia-ahed-tamimi}

## The incident and imprisonment
In December 2017 her mother filmed a live video in which Ahed, then 16, slapped an Israeli soldier outside her home, hours after her cousin Muhammad Tamimi had been seriously wounded in the head by army fire. The clip went globally viral; she was arrested days later, tried before a military court on charges including assault and incitement, and sentenced to eight months, which she served alongside her mother, before release in July 2018 to a large public reception. {cite:wikipedia-ahed-tamimi} {cite:muslim500-ahed-tamimi}

## After release
She continued media and international activism, took part in tours and conferences, and a book based on her family's story, They Called Me a Lioness / «لن يقتلونا حتى نحب حياتنا», was published with her name attached in Arabic, while she kept speaking publicly for the Palestinian cause. {cite:wikipedia-ahed-tamimi}

## Debate
Views divide: supporters see her as an icon of popular resistance to occupation and the courage of a new Palestinian generation, while Israeli opponents describe her as a propaganda tool raised on provocation — hardline Israeli commentators wrote against her and rights organisations answered back; the museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-ahed-tamimi}

## Legacy
Her biography embodies the transformation of the icon in the camera age: a small camera filmed a child's confrontation and turned her story into a global narrative about occupation, childhood and resistance, with a single symbolic act attributed to her that reshaped international awareness of a whole generation of Palestinians. {cite:wikipedia-ahed-tamimi}
