## Summary
Ahmed Toufiq is a Moroccan historian, novelist and long-serving minister of endowments and Islamic affairs — in office continuously since 2002 under King Mohammed VI — and, since 2018, vice-president of the Mohammed VI Foundation of African Ulama. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-toufiq}

## Life and formation
He was born on 22 June 1943 in Marigha, a village in the High Atlas near Marrakech. Schooled in the Atlas and Marrakech, he took a teaching diploma, then history degrees at the Faculty of Letters in Rabat — a licence in 1968, a Moroccan history and archaeology diploma in 1969, and a postgraduate doctorate in 1976 on nineteenth-century Moroccan rural society. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-toufiq}

## Academic career
He taught at the Mohammed V University faculty of letters from 1970 to 1989, directed its Institute of African Studies until 1995, then headed the National Library and Archives until 2002. His scholarship covers Moroccan society, the Jews of Demnate and Morocco's African ties; his novels, including Jarat Abi Musa and A Tree of Henna and a Moon, won the Moroccan Book Prize. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-toufiq}

## Ministry
Appointed minister in 2002, he has remained through successive governments, overseeing the habous endowments, mosque administration, the training of imams and mourchidates, and the kingdom's promotion of a Maliki-Sufi religious field; in 2010 he was also made chairman of the Hassan II Mosque foundation. Supporters credit him with institutional stability and scholarly depth; critics of Moroccan religious policy debate the degree of state control his ministry embodies.
