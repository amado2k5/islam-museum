## Summary
[[person/umm-hakim-bint-al-harith|Umm Hakim]] bint al-Harith ibn Hisham ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumiyya, wife of [[person/ikrima-ibn-abi-jahl|Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl]] {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1992}.

## Her Islam and her seeking safety for her husband
Abu Umar said: she was present on the day of Uhud as an unbeliever, then became Muslim at the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest]]. Her husband had fled to Yemen, so she went after him with the Prophet's leave, attended with her and became Muslim {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1992}. In Ibn Manda's account from Urwa: Umm Hakim bint al-Harith was with Ikrima, and Fakhita bint al-Walid ibn al-Mughira was with Safwan ibn Umayya — both became Muslim, and Umm Hakim asked safety for Ikrima and the Prophet granted it {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1992}. Musa ibn Uqba mentions from al-Zuhri that she became Muslim on the conquest day, asked the Prophet's leave to seek her husband, and he granted it and gave him safety {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1992}.

## Marj al-Saffar
She went out with her husband to the Byzantines' campaign and he was martyred; [[person/khalid-ibn-said-ibn-al-as|Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As]] married her. On the eve of the battle of Marj al-Saffar he wished to consummate the marriage; she said: "if only you would wait until God routs these hosts" — he said: "my soul tells me I shall be killed" — she said: "then go ahead". He celebrated the wedding at al-Qantara — she became known for it, and it was called "Umm Hakim's bridge" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1992}. In the morning he held the wedding feast; they had not finished eating when the Byzantines came on them. Fighting broke out, Khalid was martyred, and Umm Hakim wrapped her garments and fought — killing seven of the Byzantines with the tent-post of the tent in which Khalid had wed her {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1992}.
