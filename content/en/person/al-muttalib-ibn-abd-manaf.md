## Summary
Al-Muttalib, son of [[person/abd-manaf-ibn-qusayy|Abd Manaf]] and brother of [[person/hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]]. After Hashim, the siqaya (provision of water to pilgrims) and the rifada (provision of food) passed to him. He was a noble, respected man of merit among his people, and Quraysh called him al-Fayyad ("the overflowing") for his generosity. He is the one who brought his nephew Shayba from Yathrib, and so the boy was called [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.50} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/137-138}

## Bringing back Abd al-Muttalib
When Shayba had become a boy or was older, al-Muttalib heard of him and set out to find him. When he saw him, his eyes filled with tears; he embraced him and seated him behind him on his mount. But the boy refused until his mother gave permission. Al-Muttalib asked her to send him along, and she refused. He said: "He is only going to his father's domain and to the sanctuary of God," and she then gave her permission. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.50} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/137-138} He brought him to Mecca riding behind him on his camel. People said: "This is Abd al-Muttalib." He replied: "Woe to you, he is only the son of my brother Hashim." The boy stayed with him until he grew up. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.50}

## His death
Al-Muttalib died at Radman, in the land of [[place/yemen|Yemen]]. After him, Abd al-Muttalib took over and maintained for his people what his forefathers had maintained for theirs. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.50} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/137-138} When al-Muttalib died, [[person/nawfal-ibn-abd-manaf|Nawfal]] seized the arkah (rights) of Abd al-Muttalib and usurped them from him. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.50} See [[event/recovery-of-abd-al-muttalib-rights|the recovery by Abd al-Muttalib of his rights]].

## In Quraysh's offices
After Hashim, the siqaya and rifada passed to his brother al-Muttalib, and then to Abd al-Muttalib. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/137} See [[concept/manasib-quraysh|the offices of Quraysh]].

## For young visitors
Al-Muttalib was the uncle of the Prophet's grandfather. He brought the boy Shayba from Medina, and people named him "Abd al-Muttalib," meaning servant of al-Muttalib, and the name stuck.
