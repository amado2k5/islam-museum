# Fayez al-Sarraj

Fayez Mustafa al-Sarraj (born 20 February 1960) is a Libyan architect and politician who chaired the Presidential Council of Libya and headed the Government of National Accord (GNA) from 2016 to 2021, as head of both state and government of the internationally recognised Libyan administration. {cite:wikipedia-fayez-al-sarraj-en} {cite:wikipedia-fayez-al-sarraj-ar}

## Early life and career
He was born on 20 February 1960 in Tripoli. He comes from a wealthy family of Kouloughli descent that owned shops and large amounts of land; his father, Mostafa al-Sarraj, was a minister during the Libyan monarchy. He took a bachelor's degree in architecture and urban planning from Al Fateh University (now the University of Tripoli) in 1982 and an MBA in 1999. He began his career as an engineer in the projects administration of the Social Security Fund, worked as an engineering consultant on specialised project committees, and co-founded the Tripolis engineering consultancy office. During the Gaddafi era he was employed at the Housing Ministry, and in 2014 he briefly served as minister of housing and utilities in the Maiteeq cabinet. {cite:wikipedia-fayez-al-sarraj-en} {cite:wikipedia-fayez-al-sarraj-ar} {cite:wikidata-fayez-al-sarraj}

## Government of National Accord
After Libya's 2014 elections the country split between the General National Congress in Tripoli and the House of Representatives in Tobruk. Under the UN-brokered Libyan Political Agreement, signed on 17 December 2015, al-Sarraj was named chairman of a new Presidential Council and head of the Government of National Accord. He and the council arrived in Tripoli on 30 March 2016 and took over the prime ministerial offices; his authority remained disputed by the rival authorities in Tripoli and the east throughout his tenure, and fighting broke out between his loyalists and forces of the rival Ghawil government. He also held the defence portfolio from September 2018 to August 2020. {cite:wikipedia-fayez-al-sarraj-en}

In September 2020, after a month of protests in Tripoli, he announced that he would step down by the end of October, then rescinded the decision on 31 October 2020. Following the Libyan Political Dialogue Forum, he transferred power on 15 March 2021 to the Government of National Unity under Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh. {cite:wikipedia-fayez-al-sarraj-en}
