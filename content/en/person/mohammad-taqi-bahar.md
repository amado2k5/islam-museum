# Mohammad-Taqi Bahar

Mohammad-Taqi Bahar (1886–1951), known as Malek osh-Sho'ara, "king of poets", was an Iranian poet, scholar, journalist and politician whose traditional verse and prose histories made him one of the great cultural figures of modern Iran. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-taqi-bahar-en}

## Formation

He was born in Mashhad on 10 December 1886, son of Mohammad Kazem Sabouri, poet laureate of the Imam Reza shrine. Tutored by his father and then by Adib Nishapuri, he memorised much of the Qur'an as a boy, chose the pen name Bahar ("spring") at eight, and on his father's death in 1904 succeeded him in the laureateship, the Shah confirming the title Malek osh-Sho'ara when he was nineteen. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-taqi-bahar-en}{cite:wikipedia-mohammad-taqi-bahar-fa}

## Politics and scholarship

At the Constitutional Revolution he laid down the laureateship and joined the movement, publishing the papers Khorasan, Now-bahar and Tazeh-bahar, and sat repeatedly in the Majlis. He co-founded the Daneshkadeh literary association and its magazine, was imprisoned in 1933 and exiled to Isfahan, and after Tehran University was founded became its professor of Persian literature. His books include Sabk-shenasi, the standard study of Persian prose styles, a concise history of Iran's political parties, and editions of early historical texts. Briefly minister of culture in 1945, he died of tuberculosis at Tehran on 21 April 1951 — the English article's lead dates the death 22 April, a split the museum's record discloses — and was buried at Zahir-od-dowleh. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-taqi-bahar-en}{cite:iranica-mohammad-taqi-bahar}
