## Overview
Al-Qa'id ibn Hammad died in Rajab of 446 AH, about 1054 CE (derived conversion), as Ibn al-Athir records among the events of that year in al-Kamil {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 8 p. 317}; the section for that year opens two pages earlier {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 8 p. 315}.

## What followed his death
Al-Qa'id left his will to his son Muhsin and charged him to treat his paternal uncles well {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 8 p. 317}. Ibn al-Athir says that when his father died Muhsin went against what he had been told and wished to remove all his uncles; when his uncle Yusuf ibn Hammad heard of his plan he opposed him, gathered a great following and built a fortress on an impregnable mountain, which he named al-Tayyara {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 8 p. 317}. Muhsin then killed four of his uncles and Yusuf's estrangement grew {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 8 p. 317}. Muhsin wrote to Balkin ibn Muhammad summoning him, and when Balkin drew near he ordered some Arab men to kill him; their commander Khalifa ibn Makin told Balkin what they had been ordered to do, because Balkin had always been kind to them {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 8 p. 317}. Balkin made ready and marched against Muhsin, who had left the fortress and fled back to it; Balkin caught up with him, killed him, took the fortress and assumed power, and his taking of the fortress is dated 447 AH {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir vol. 8 p. 317}.
