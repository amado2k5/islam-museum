## Summary
Marwan Abdelhamid, known professionally as Saint Levant, is a Palestinian-Algerian-French singer, rapper and musician born in Gaza City in 2000. Singing and rapping in Arabic, English and French, he has drawn one of the largest international audiences of any young Palestinian artist, and The Muslim 500 lists him among the influential figures of Muslim arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-saint-levant} {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## Early life
He was born in Gaza to a Palestinian father from Jerusalem and an Algerian-French mother, and spent part of his childhood in the Gaza Strip before the family moved to Jordan and later to Ramallah; he went on to study in the United States. That movement between Gaza, Amman, Ramallah and the diaspora is the raw material of his songs. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## Music
Saint Levant broke through around 2022–2023 with trilingual singles such as Very Few Friends and From Gaza, With Love, and released the album Deira in 2024, named for a Gaza hotel his father once ran and framed as a love letter to a destroyed landscape. His sound mixes 1980s Arabic synth pop, hip-hop and French chanson. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## Public presence
He has performed at major festivals including Coachella, toured internationally, collaborated with artists across the Arab world and the West, and used his platform to speak on Gaza, including projects supporting Palestinian creatives. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant} {cite:muslim500-saint-levant}

## Assessment
Critics describe his work as nostalgia made danceable — songs of exile, return and an imagined homeland delivered in the three languages of his upbringing. Admirers hear a new form of Palestinian soft power; some listeners debate whether pop packaging dilutes political content. The museum reports these readings attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}


## Collaborations and releases
Beyond Deira he has issued a steady stream of singles and features with artists from the Arab world and beyond — including collaborations with Algerian rapper Soolking, Moroccan star Marwan Pablo and Lebanese legend Ziad Rahbani's catalogue — while headlining sold-out shows in London, Paris and New York. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## Identity and language
He has described the project as the music of a generation 'between two exiles' — Palestinian, Algerian and French at once — choosing the name Saint Levant from the colonial-era term for the Eastern Mediterranean and reclaiming it for a multilingual Levantine youth culture. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## Activism
He co-founded the creative platform 2048 to fund Palestinian artists, spoke on stage about Gaza during the war, and channeled streaming revenues and merch sales to relief causes, positioning his act as cultural advocacy as much as entertainment. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant} {cite:muslim500-saint-levant}

## A sonic identity
Critics note the production signature his team developed: Levantine percussion and maqam-inflected melodies laid over trap, reggaeton and baile-funk structures, with verses that slide between English, French and Arabic inside a single track. The approach lets listeners in Detroit, Marseille or Beirut hear their own diaspora in the same song. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## 2048 and Gaza
His releases after October 2023, including tracks that address Gaza directly and benefit-linked performances, placed him among the most-streamed musical voices commenting on the war; profiles in music press describe the period as transforming him from viral act to political-cultural figure. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant} {cite:muslim500-saint-levant}

## Fashion and image
He has fronted fashion editorials and brand partnerships, cultivating a visual identity — keffiyeh motifs, Levantine jewellery, tailored streetwear — that extends the music's argument about Arab style into imagery. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## Diaspora as subject
Saint Levant's project is unusually coherent across music, visuals and public statements: the displaced Palestinian, Algerian, French and Serbian threads of his own background become the material of songs that treat the Arab diaspora as a culture in itself rather than a series of losses. Critics and profiles describe tracks like '5AM in Paris' and 'Deira' as mapping a trans-Mediterranean identity — the hotels, ports and border crossings of the mid-twentieth century refracted through contemporary club production. This framing has resonated strongly with second- and third-generation listeners in France, the Gulf and North America, who find in the music a language for their own mixed geographies. {cite:wikipedia-saint-levant}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 includes him among the most influential Muslims in the arts, citing the speed of his rise and the scale of his streaming audience across the Arab world and its diasporas. His inclusion alongside older established names reflects a shift in how influence is measured in the music field: a largely independent, social-media-native artist can now reach audiences comparable to those of state-backed or label-backed stars of earlier decades. {cite:muslim500-saint-levant}
