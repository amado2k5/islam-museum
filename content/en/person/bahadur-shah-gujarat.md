## Overview
Bahadur Shah was sultan of Gujarat from 932/1526 to 943/1537, second son of Muzaffar Shah II, and the Encyclopaedia of Islam calls him the last vigorous sultan of independent Gujarat. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 pp. 974-975}

## Accession
On bad terms with his elder brother Sikandar, he left Gujarat in 931/1525 and was at the court of Ibrahim Lodi at the time of the battle of Panipat between the sultan of Delhi and the Mughal Babur. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 974} Learning of his father's death and Sikandar's accession, and then of Sikandar's assassination by Khwush Qadam, Imad al-Mulk, he gained the support of Gujarati Muslim nobles and assumed the insignia of the sultanate at Anhalwara-Patan on 26 Ramadan 932/6 July 1526. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 974}

## Conquests
In 935/1528 he attacked Burhan Nizam Shah of Ahmadnagar, allied with Khandesh and Berar, and occupied Ahmadnagar in 936/1529; the article notes that Arabic and Persian histories say the khutba was read and coins struck in his name there, but no such coins have been found. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 974} In 937/1531 he attacked Mahmud II of Malwa and took Mandu; in 938/1532-3 he captured Ujjain, Bhilsa and Raisin; and in Ramadan 941/March 1535 his forces took Chitor at the second attempt. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 974}

## Humayun, the Portuguese and his death
War with the Mughal Humayun broke out in autumn 941/1534 because Bahadur Shah had sheltered the Lodi Afghans and Muhammad Zaman Mirza, Babur's son-in-law. Defeated at Mandasor and Mandu, and losing Champaner in Safar 942/August 1535, he turned to the Portuguese, who obtained Bassein in Jumada II 941/December 1534 and in Rabi II/October 1535 the right to build a fort at Diu. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 974} He recovered most of his dominions after Humayun withdrew in 942/1536, then visited Nuno da Cunha on his flagship at Diu, hurried back to shore on sensing treachery, and was slain by the pursuing Portuguese on 3 Ramadan 943/13 February 1537. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 pp. 974-975}
