## Overview
Shihab al-Din Ahmad ibn Abd al-Wahhab ibn Muhammad ibn Abd al-Da'im al-Qurashi al-Taymi al-Bakri al-Nuwayri al-Shafi'i (677-733/1279-1333), Mamluk chancery official and author of the Nihayat al-Arab fi Funun al-Adab, one of the four best-known encyclopaedias of the Mamluk period. Born at Akhmim — al-Zirikli says at Qus — he died at Cairo on 21 Ramadan 733 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 156} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/197} {cite:al-aalam j. 1 s.v. al-Nuwayri}.

## Formation and teachers
Raised between the Sa'id and Cairo: his father Taj al-Din Abd al-Wahhab (618-699) was born at Misr in the madrasa called Manazil al-Izz, died at the Salihiyya Najmiyya and was buried in the turba of chief qadi Ibn Makhluf al-Nuwayri al-Jazuli. Before 698 al-Nuwayri ranged over Upper Egypt, recalling the elephant tusks he saw at Qus in 697. His teachers included Abd al-Mu'min al-Dimyati, Ibn Daqiq al-Id, chief qadi Ibn Jama'a and the shaykha Zaynab bint Yahya; he heard Sahih al-Bukhari at Wazira bint al-Munadhdha's sessions in 715, the Sunan from Ibn al-Sabuni, the Shifa in 708 at the Nasiriyya, and the Muwatta' and Sahih Muslim from the Sharif Izz al-Din al-Dimashqi {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 156}.

## In the diwans
Recruited to the diwan al-khass at Cairo in 698, residing at the Nasiriyya madrasa whose waqf he reproduces — and openly criticising its administrator 'Anbar al-Lala. Sent to Syria in Jumada II 701, he stayed at Damascus two years and four months and fought the Mongols on 29 Sha'ban 702 beside Mughultay, describing the victory of Shaqhab as an eyewitness. Recalled to Cairo in Ramadan 703 as mubashir of the royal estates and the Mansuri bimaristan, he witnessed the opening of the Ibn Taymiyya affair from the Nasiriyya itself and was charged with sequestering the amir al-Turkumani's assets and building his turba at al-Qarafa. When al-Nasir abdicated to al-Karak in 708, al-Nuwayri joined him in Rabi' II 709, holding him the only legitimate sultan, and returned with him; the steward Ibn Abbada then brought him close to the sultan before having him flogged and confiscated and sent away to Syria — a misfortune al-Nuwayri never mentions. He reached Tripoli in Safar 710 as sahib diwan al-insha' then nazir al-jaysh, witnessed the Karasunkur-Aqqush defection to the Mongols in 712, renewed the amirs' oath, returned to Cairo on 20 Rajab 712 and was made nazir al-diwan of al-Dakhaliyya and al-Murtahiyya until about 716 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 pp. 157-158} {cite:suluk-maqrizi p. 91}.

## The Nihaya
Withdrawing from administration about 716, he composed his encyclopaedia in thirty-one volumes and some nine thousand pages over five funun — the cosmos, man, fauna, flora and history, which fills more than two-thirds — following the plan of al-Watwat's Mabahij al-Fikar with history added as a fifth fann, and arranging history by dynasties rather than annals so that no episode is broken between years. Written between 714 and 731, it was copied at extraordinary speed: volume one of the second copy was finished on 20 Dhu al-Qa'da 721 and volume eighteen on 26 Ramadan 722 — eighteen volumes in under ten months; he filled three notebooks a day, copied the Sahih of al-Bukhari eight times, and was deemed an excellent calligrapher and bookbinder {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 pp. 159-160} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/197}.

## Dates
Born 21 Dhu al-Qa'da 677/5 April 1279 at Akhmim per the Encyclopaedia of Islam — al-Zirikli gives Qus for birth and upbringing; died 21 Ramadan 733/5 June 1333 at Cairo {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 pp. 156, 159} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/197}.
