## Overview
Sari al-Saqati, an ascetic Sufi, and the teacher of al-Junayd. He died in the days of al-Mu'tazz {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.559}.

## His chain
In the chain of Sufism that Ibn al-Nadim read in the handwriting of Ja'far al-Khaldi: Sari took from Ma'ruf al-Karkhi, Ma'ruf took from Farqad al-Sanji, Farqad took from al-Hasan al-Basri, and al-Hasan took from Anas ibn Malik. It is reported that al-Hasan met seventy of the people of Badr {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.260}.

## In al-Risala al-Qushayriyya
Al-Qushayri gives Sari al-Saqati a notice in [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]]: he was the maternal uncle and teacher of [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]] and a pupil of [[person/maruf-al-karkhi|Ma'ruf al-Karkhi]], unique in his time in scrupulousness, spiritual states and the sciences of unification. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 112} He relates that Sari kept a shop in the market; Ma'ruf brought him an orphan boy, whom Sari clothed, and Ma'ruf prayed that God would make the world hateful to him, so that he rose from his shop with nothing more hateful to him than the world. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 112} He quotes al-Junayd: "I never saw anyone more devoted to worship than al-Sari; ninety-eight years passed over him and he was never seen lying down except in his final illness"; and reports Sari's saying that Sufism is a name for three things, among them not speaking of inner knowledge that the outward sense of the Book contradicts. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 112} He dates his death to 257 AH, and relates that for thirty years Sari sought forgiveness for having said "Praise be to God" when told his shop had escaped a fire in Baghdad, because he had wished for himself something better than what befell the Muslims. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 113}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 469 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], under the name Abu al-Hasan al-Sari ibn al-Mughallis al-Saqati, and introduces him as the maternal uncle and teacher of [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]]. Most of the reports in the notice are told by al-Junayd himself. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/116}

**Fear for himself.** Al-Junayd relates that he feared punishment if he so much as smoothed his beard with his hand for someone coming in to see him, that he looked at his nose several times a day for fear his face had turned black, and that he did not want to die where he was known, in case his grave refused him and he was disgraced. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/116}

**Scruple in food.** He said that for thirty years his self had asked him to dip a carrot in syrup and he had not given in, and that he wished for one meal for which he owed God nothing and no creature a favour. He told how, leaving Mecca, he found a bundle of herbs in a flood channel and took it, hoping it was such a meal; when a man pointed out a second bundle he refused it, because the man's pointing was a favour. He named four as the people of scruple in an earlier time: Hudhayfa al-Mar'ashi, [[person/ibn-adham-ibrahim|Ibrahim ibn Adham]], Yusuf ibn Asbat and Sulayman al-Khawwas. He condemned earning one's bread by one's religion. Sent a cough remedy without a price, he said: for fifty years we have taught people not to eat by their religion; shall we eat by ours today? {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/116-117}

**Stations of the seeker and of the fearful.** Isma'il ibn Abd Allah al-Shami relates from him five traits that make a servant brave, among them steadiness on God's command without evasion and watchfulness of God in private and in public, and two lists of stations, one for the seeker and one for the fearful, which he counts out one by one. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/117-118}

**Sayings.** He said that if a man entered a garden and every bird in it greeted him with "peace be upon you, friend of God", and his self took comfort in that, he would be its prisoner. He said that all of this world is surplus except five things: bread that fills, water that quenches, a garment that covers, a house that shelters and knowledge that is put to use. He defined trust in God as stripping oneself of power and strength. He said that whoever claims an inner knowledge that overturns an outward ruling is mistaken. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/118-119} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/121}

**With al-Junayd.** When he wanted to teach al-Junayd something he asked him a question. One day he asked him about gratitude; al-Junayd answered that it is not to disobey God by means of His blessings, and al-Sari praised the answer. Abu Ja'far al-Sammak, a man of strict seclusion, saw a group gathered round him, said "you have become a halting-place for idlers", and left. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/119}

**States.** He told that one night after his devotions he stretched out his leg in the prayer niche and heard a call: is this how one sits with kings? He never stretched it out again. Ahmad ibn Khalaf saw a new jug lying broken in his room; al-Sari explained that he had set it out to cool the water and fallen asleep, and dreamed of someone who reproached him for it and kicked the jug, and he woke to find it broken. He fell ill in [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]]; his visitors sat too long and then asked him to pray, and he prayed: O God, teach us the manners of visiting the sick. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/120-122}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Al-Sari's stories and sayings on pages 123–127 of the tenth volume continue the notice, which ends at page 128 where the notice of Ibrahim ibn Shammas begins. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/123-128}

**The distinguished man on the feast day.** Al-Khalqali relates that he went out with al-Sari after the feast prayer and met a distinguished man, to whom al-Sari gave an incomplete greeting; asked why, he said the man transmits that the Prophet said: when two Muslims meet, a hundred mercies are divided between them, ninety-nine for the more eager of them; so he wanted the other to have the greater share. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/124}

**I see no merit in myself above anyone.** Al-Junayd said: I see no merit in myself over anyone. Asked whether that includes innovators, he said: nor over the effeminate. Al-Sari said: if I miss a part of my daily portion I can never make it up. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/124}

**Blessings and afflictions.** Al-Sari said: whoever does not know the worth of blessings is stripped of them from where he does not know; whoever finds afflictions light gains their reward. He said: make your poverty a matter for God, and you will be rich through Him above all others. He said: good manners are the interpreter of the intellect, your tongue is the interpreter of your heart, and your face the mirror of your heart; on the face appears what hearts conceal. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/124}

**Three kinds of hearts.** He said hearts are of three kinds: a heart like a mountain that nothing moves; a heart like a palm whose root is firm while the wind bends it; and a heart like a feather that bends with the wind to right and left. He said: the strongest strength is your victory over your self, and whoever cannot discipline his self is less able to discipline another. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/124}

**Marks of knowledge and of being led on.** He said: do not forsake your brother on suspicion, and do not leave him without seeking his pardon. A sign of knowledge of God is to fulfil God's rights and prefer them over the self where one has the power; a sign of being led on is blindness to the faults of the self; and little truthfulness brings much error. The best livelihood is that which is free of five things: sins in earning it, humiliation in asking, cheating in trade, keeping the instruments of sin, and dealing with oppressors. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/124}

**Five further things.** He said: the best things are five: weeping over sins, correcting faults, obeying the Knower of the unseen, clearing the rust from hearts, and not making yourself a rider of what you narrate without practising it. He said that five things leave no room in the heart for anything else: fear of God alone, hope in God alone, love for God alone, shame before God alone, and intimacy with God alone. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/124-125}

**Knowledge and practice.** He said: if a person starts with hadith and then adopts asceticism, he gains understanding; if he starts with asceticism and then writes hadith, he is scattered. He said: no man is praised until he prefers his religion to his desire, and no man is destroyed until he prefers his desire to his religion. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/125}

**Visiting al-Sari.** Al-Junayd used to visit al-Sari every three days as the custom of visiting the sick. When al-Sari was dying, al-Junayd sat by his head and wept, and his tears fell on al-Sari's cheek; al-Sari opened his eyes, looked at him, and al-Junayd asked for counsel. He said: do not keep company with the wicked, and do not let the company of the righteous distract you from God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/125}

**Cause and seeking.** He said: whoever knows the cause ceases from seeking. Speaking of the people of realities among the worshippers, he said: their eating is the eating of the sick, and their sleep is the sleep of the drowned. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/125}

**A hidden display.** He related that for thirty years he had prayed the Friday prayer at a known place. One Friday a neighbour died and he wished to walk in the funeral, so he came late; as he drew near the mosque his self said: now they see you, late from your time. This grieved him, so he left that place and prayed in different places so that his place would not be known. He would wonder at this and say: I have no joy by day or night, and I do not mind whether the night is long or short. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/125}

**Green and the heart.** A monk of the monasteries (Dayrani) asked him why they admire greenery; he said: when hearts flow in the seas of thought, they are overwhelmed in sight, and when they look at greenery the breeze of life returns to them. He said: no one is able to abandon desires unless he has abandoned doubtful things. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/126}

**His prayer for the people.** He said: when I go out for congregational prayer and remember people coming to me, I say: O God, grant them worship whose sweetness they find, so that it occupies them from me. He said of people: do no deed for them, leave nothing for them, and reveal nothing to them, meaning that all your deeds should be for God. He said: whoever speaks ill of me is forgiven, except a man who wrongs me with something he knows to be other than what I am. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/126}

**Ahmad ibn Hanbal's remark.** Al-Hasan al-Bazzaz relates that Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Bishr ibn al-Harith were in Baghdad and people hoped God would keep them through them; they died and al-Sari remained, and he hoped God would keep them through him. Al-Bazzaz also reports that Ahmad was asked about al-Sari after his return from the frontier and said: is he not the shaykh known for good food? and when told yes, said: he was on his path with us before he went out. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/126}

**Learning from youth.** Al-Sari used to say to those around him: I am a lesson to you; young men, carry on, for work belongs to youth. When night fell on him he fought off sleep, and then fought again, and when it overcame him he wept and wailed. He said: there are people who, if half of them died, the other half would not be deterred; I do not think I am other than among them. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/126}

**Al-Sari as a transmitter.** It is said that he heard and related from famous scholars, but refrained from narrating, so little of his hadith was published, and he narrated from Hushaym, Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Marwan ibn Mu'awiya, Muhammad ibn Fudayl and others. When something of hadith was mentioned to him, he said: it is not provision for the grave. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/127}

**Hadiths he related.** Among his chains is a hadith from Abu Hurayra that an oath is taken according to what your companion believes you; a hadith from Ibn Umar that the Prophet came out to them holding two things and said: this is the Book of God; and a hadith from Anas that people keep asking until they say that God created creation, then who created God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/127}

**The author's note.** Abu Nu'aym notes that mentioning all who were sincerely devoted to God's remembrance is long; that God has in every age people who hasten to the race; that Abu Sa'd Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ziyad ibn al-A'rabi has treated the names of many in his book Tabaqat al-Nussak, so he limits himself here to naming some of them without their states and sayings. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/127-128}
