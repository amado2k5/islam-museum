## Overview
Sari al-Saqati, an ascetic Sufi, and the teacher of al-Junayd. He died in the days of al-Mu'tazz {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.559}.

## His chain
In the chain of Sufism that Ibn al-Nadim read in the handwriting of Ja'far al-Khaldi: Sari took from Ma'ruf al-Karkhi, Ma'ruf took from Farqad al-Sanji, Farqad took from al-Hasan al-Basri, and al-Hasan took from Anas ibn Malik. It is reported that al-Hasan met seventy of the people of Badr {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.260}.

## In al-Risala al-Qushayriyya
Al-Qushayri gives Sari al-Saqati a notice in [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]]: he was the maternal uncle and teacher of [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]] and a pupil of [[person/maruf-al-karkhi|Ma'ruf al-Karkhi]], unique in his time in scrupulousness, spiritual states and the sciences of unification. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 112} He relates that Sari kept a shop in the market; Ma'ruf brought him an orphan boy, whom Sari clothed, and Ma'ruf prayed that God would make the world hateful to him, so that he rose from his shop with nothing more hateful to him than the world. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 112} He quotes al-Junayd: "I never saw anyone more devoted to worship than al-Sari; ninety-eight years passed over him and he was never seen lying down except in his final illness"; and reports Sari's saying that Sufism is a name for three things, among them not speaking of inner knowledge that the outward sense of the Book contradicts. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 112} He dates his death to 257 AH, and relates that for thirty years Sari sought forgiveness for having said "Praise be to God" when told his shop had escaped a fire in Baghdad, because he had wished for himself something better than what befell the Muslims. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 113}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 469 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], under the name Abu al-Hasan al-Sari ibn al-Mughallis al-Saqati, and introduces him as the maternal uncle and teacher of [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]]. Most of the reports in the notice are told by al-Junayd himself. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/116}

**Fear for himself.** Al-Junayd relates that he feared punishment if he so much as smoothed his beard with his hand for someone coming in to see him, that he looked at his nose several times a day for fear his face had turned black, and that he did not want to die where he was known, in case his grave refused him and he was disgraced. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/116}

**Scruple in food.** He said that for thirty years his self had asked him to dip a carrot in syrup and he had not given in, and that he wished for one meal for which he owed God nothing and no creature a favour. He told how, leaving Mecca, he found a bundle of herbs in a flood channel and took it, hoping it was such a meal; when a man pointed out a second bundle he refused it, because the man's pointing was a favour. He named four as the people of scruple in an earlier time: Hudhayfa al-Mar'ashi, [[person/ibn-adham-ibrahim|Ibrahim ibn Adham]], Yusuf ibn Asbat and Sulayman al-Khawwas. He condemned earning one's bread by one's religion. Sent a cough remedy without a price, he said: for fifty years we have taught people not to eat by their religion; shall we eat by ours today? {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/116-117}

**Stations of the seeker and of the fearful.** Isma'il ibn Abd Allah al-Shami relates from him five traits that make a servant brave, among them steadiness on God's command without evasion and watchfulness of God in private and in public, and two lists of stations, one for the seeker and one for the fearful, which he counts out one by one. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/117-118}

**Sayings.** He said that if a man entered a garden and every bird in it greeted him with "peace be upon you, friend of God", and his self took comfort in that, he would be its prisoner. He said that all of this world is surplus except five things: bread that fills, water that quenches, a garment that covers, a house that shelters and knowledge that is put to use. He defined trust in God as stripping oneself of power and strength. He said that whoever claims an inner knowledge that overturns an outward ruling is mistaken. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/118-119} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/121}

**With al-Junayd.** When he wanted to teach al-Junayd something he asked him a question. One day he asked him about gratitude; al-Junayd answered that it is not to disobey God by means of His blessings, and al-Sari praised the answer. Abu Ja'far al-Sammak, a man of strict seclusion, saw a group gathered round him, said "you have become a halting-place for idlers", and left. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/119}

**States.** He told that one night after his devotions he stretched out his leg in the prayer niche and heard a call: is this how one sits with kings? He never stretched it out again. Ahmad ibn Khalaf saw a new jug lying broken in his room; al-Sari explained that he had set it out to cool the water and fallen asleep, and dreamed of someone who reproached him for it and kicked the jug, and he woke to find it broken. He fell ill in [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]]; his visitors sat too long and then asked him to pray, and he prayed: O God, teach us the manners of visiting the sick. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/120-122}
