## Overview
Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Muthanna ibn Abd Allah ibn Anas ibn Malik al-Ansari al-Khazraji al-Najjari al-Basri, Abu Abd Allah, is called by al-Dhahabi the imam, the great scholar, the reliable hadith narrator and judge of Basra {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3523}. He sought knowledge as a young man, and Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna al-Anazi heard him say he was born in 118 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3523}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Sulayman al-Taymi, Humayd al-Tawil, Sa'id al-Jurayri, Ibn Awn, Ash'ath ibn Abd al-Malik al-Humrani, Habib ibn al-Shahid, his father Abd Allah ibn al-Muthanna, Ibn Jurayj, Hisham ibn Hassan, Shu'ba, Humam, al-Mas'udi and many others, and descending to the jurist Zufar {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3523}, {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Abu al-Walid al-Tayalisi, Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in, Bundar, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba, al-Za'farani, al-Fallas, Ali ibn al-Madini, Qutayba, Abu Hatim, his son Abd al-Kabir, the judge Isma'il ibn Ishaq and a great many others narrated from him, the last of them Abu Muslim al-Kajji {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}.

## What the critics said
Al-Ahwas ibn al-Mufaddal reports Ibn Ma'in calling him reliable, and Ibn Ma'in said the judgeship suited him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Abu Hatim said he was truthful and that he had seen only three among the imams: Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Sulayman ibn Dawud al-Hashimi and Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Ansari {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Al-Nasa'i said there was no harm in him, while Abu Dawud said he changed severely {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Zakariyya al-Saji called him a great scholar, though not among the riders of hadith like Yahya al-Qattan and his peers, since opinion prevailed in him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Al-Athram relays Ahmad saying that only his attention to opinion lowered him among hadith people, that he had indeed heard, and that after his books were lost he narrated from the books of his slave Abu Hakim {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Yahya ibn Sa'id rejected a report of his from Habib ibn al-Shahid about being cupped while in ihram; al-Dhahabi answers that the rival version has a weak narrator and that al-Ansari also related the Yazid ibn al-Asamm report {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. The museum gives each judgement to the one who made it and chooses none.

## As judge
He took fiqh from Uthman al-Batti, Sawwar ibn Abd Allah and Ubayd Allah ibn al-Hasan al-Anbari, and it is said he studied under Zufar and Abu Yusuf, though God knows best {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. He became judge of Basra under al-Rashid after Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh, then came to Baghdad and held the judgeship of the eastern side after al-Awfi, as Ibn Qutayba reports; when al-Amin took office he dismissed him and set him over the grievances court after Ibn Ulayya {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Al-Ramahurmuzi relates that al-Ma'mun sent him fifty thousand dirhams to divide among the jurists of Basra, and a dispute arose between him and Hilal ibn Muslim over it {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}.

## His death
Ibn Sa'd and others say he died in Basra in Rajab 215 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}. Al-Dhahabi says he lived ninety-seven years, had the highest chains of his day, and left a well-known part of elevated narrations {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3524}.
