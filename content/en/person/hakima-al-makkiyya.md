## Summary
Hakima was a woman of Banu Makhzum who lived beside the sanctuary in [[place/mecca|Mecca]]; [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] places her first among the devout women of Mecca in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]]. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.402}

## In Sifat al-Safwa
He reports from Salama ibn Khalid al-Makhzumi that whenever she saw the door of the Ka'ba open she cried out like a bereaved mother until she fainted, and that she hardly left the mosque except for some unavoidable need. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.402}
One day the Ka'ba was opened while she was away on an errand; when she came back a woman who used to sit with her told her of it and of the pilgrims circling and awaiting mercy, and she cried out and kept trembling until she died. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.402}
The editor notes that Kahhala included her in *A'lam al-Nisa'*, taking her from Sifat al-Safwa. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.402}
