## Summary
Mohammad Akram Nadwi is an India-born hadith scholar based in Britain, founder of Cambridge Islamic College. He is internationally known for his encyclopaedia of female hadith scholars, which restored to view a long history of women’s transmission in hadith. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-akram-nadwi}

## Life and upbringing
Born in India in 1964, he was educated at Nadwat al-Ulama in Lucknow, then pursued research and teaching in Britain, associated with the University of Oxford and its Islamic studies projects. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-akram-nadwi}

## Career and works
He founded Cambridge Islamic College to teach Islamic sciences in the West. His chief achievement is the 43-volume al-Wafa’ bi-Asma’ al-Nisa’, documenting nearly 10,000 women hadith transmitters across Islamic history; he also wrote al-Muhaddithat in English and a study of Abu Hanifa’s life and legal method. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-akram-nadwi}

## Method and contribution
He combines the traditional isnad training he received in India with Western academic method. Supporters say his encyclopaedia changed the debate on women’s participation in hadith transmission, while some critics argue his social conclusions need scrutiny; the museum attributes these positions without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-akram-nadwi}
