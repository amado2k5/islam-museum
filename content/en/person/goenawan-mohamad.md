## Summary
Goenawan Mohamad is an Indonesian poet, essayist and editor born in Batang, Central Java, in 1941 — co-founder of Tempo magazine and one of Southeast Asia's most respected public intellectuals; The Muslim 500 lists him among influential media figures. {cite:muslim500-goenawan-mohamad} {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## Tempo
With colleagues he founded the weekly news magazine Tempo in 1971 and served as its chief editor; under the Suharto New Order the magazine became the standard-bearer of independent Indonesian journalism, surviving bans — including a closure from 1994 to 1998 — to return after Suharto's fall and grow into the Tempo media group. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## The essayist
His weekly column Catatan Pinggir ⁦(Margins Notes)⁩ — philosophical, ironic, sceptical of power — ran for decades and has been collected in dozens of volumes, making it a signature of Indonesian letters; he also writes poetry and drama. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## Institutions
He founded the Utan Kayu community arts centre and the daily radio news agency KBR, and received honours including the CPJ International Press Freedom Award; in his eighties he remains a reference voice on Indonesian politics and culture. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## Assessment
He is credited with defining modern Indonesian journalism and the essayistic public-intellectual voice; debates about his political stances are reported attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad} {cite:muslim500-goenawan-mohamad}


## Tempo and resistance
Under his founding editorship Tempo survived Suharto's 1994 ban — becoming the central symbol of Indonesian press freedom — and he remains a reference point for journalists across Southeast Asia. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## Catatan Pinggir
His long-running column Catatan Pinggir ⁦('Sidelines')⁩ — thousands of essays on politics, faith, literature and art — is a defining body of Indonesian essayism, collected in multi-volume editions. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## Poetry and art
His poetry, libretti and art writing — plus founding the Salihara arts community — make him among the few figures equally central to Indonesian journalism and letters. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad} {cite:muslim500-goenawan-mohamad}

## Origins
Born in Batang, Central Java, in 1941, he began writing in the 1960s — poetry, essays and cultural criticism — and rose to become Indonesia's leading public intellectual across literature, journalism and thought. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## Founding Tempo
He co-founded Tempo in 1971 and led it as editor-in-chief through the decades of Suharto's rule — surviving its 1994 ban and its underground persistence — making the magazine the central symbol of Indonesian press freedom. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}

## The essayist
His Catatan Pinggir column — thousands of essays across four decades — is a defining body of Indonesian essayism: a sustained public voice on politics, faith, literature and art that shaped generations of Indonesian writers. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad} {cite:muslim500-goenawan-mohamad}

## Tempo's centrality
Goenawan Mohamad's co-founding of Tempo in 1971 — and his editorship through the decades of Suharto's rule, surviving the 1994 ban and its underground persistence — made the magazine the central symbol of Indonesian press freedom. His Catatan Pinggir column — thousands of essays across four decades on politics, faith, literature and art — is a defining body of Indonesian essayism, and his poetry, libretti and founding of the Salihara arts community make him among the few figures equally central to Indonesian journalism and letters. {cite:wikipedia-goenawan-mohamad}
