## Summary
Asma' bint Abi Bakr, sister of [[person/aisha|A'isha]], nicknamed "Dhat al-Nitaqayn" (She of the Two Girdles) from her scene in the hijra {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the hijra
When Quraysh asked her about her father she said: by God I do not know where my father is — and [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]] struck her a blow that knocked her earring off {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 165} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}.

She brought the Prophet and her father their provisions when they set out from the [[place/ghar-thawr|cave]]; having forgotten to make the bundle a tie-strap, she split her waist-girdle in two — bound the bundle with one and girded herself with the other — and was named She of the Two Girdles {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 166} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## Afterwards
She lived until her son Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr was killed in 73 AH, and died after him, past a hundred on the well-known account {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy recounts that Asma bint Abi Bakr, about twelve years old, stood before her father, who struck her (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:13:00}.

He recounts that Hajjaj crucified her son Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr for three days in the heat of Mecca (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:17:30}.

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds, as his own opinion, that children should be raised on the heritage of their forebears, and he names the family of al-Qa'qa' ibn Amr, calling Asma their grandmother {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:45:00}.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the nineteenth episode of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]] to Asma' bint Abi Bakr, the first of the Companion women he profiles. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:01:00} He says her mother was Qutayla, who also bore Abu Bakr's son Abd Allah, so that she was Aisha's sister through their father; Qutayla was divorced and stayed a polytheist, and Asma' grew up with Abu Bakr and Umm Ruman. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:02:00} He puts her at about fifteen at the start of the mission. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:02:00} She saw her father beaten until he bled, cared for the younger children while he was busy, and married al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam shortly before the Hijra. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:04:00}

When the Prophet came to plan the Hijra, she was trusted with the secret; the two men hid three days in the cave of Thawr. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:06:00} When Abu Jahl came with others to the door she said she did not know where her father was, and he slapped her so hard her earring fell. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:07:00} She reassured her blind grandfather, who feared Abu Bakr had taken all his money, by letting him feel stones wrapped in cloth, and she carried the food to the cave, tearing her girdle in two to tie it, which earned her the name "she of the two girdles". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:09:30}

She emigrated while pregnant and gave birth on reaching Quba'; the Prophet put a chewed date in the baby's mouth and named him Abd Allah, and he says she was the mother of the first child born in Medina. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:13:00} Al-Zubayr was poor at first, owning only a horse, and she fed and groomed it and carried date-stones from his plot outside Medina. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:14:00} She often sent her son to Aisha to learn, which is why Aisha was called Umm Abd Allah. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:16:00}

At the end of her life, when al-Hajjaj besieged Ibn al-Zubayr in Mecca, he says her son was seventy-three and she a hundred, blind but with her mind and teeth intact. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:24:00} He relates their last conversation: if he was fighting for the truth, she told him, he should stand firm, and when she felt his armour she asked whether this was the dress of a man seeking martyrdom, and he took it off and went out to fight. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:25:00} After he was killed and his body hung up, she would not soften her words to al-Hajjaj or beg for him to be taken down, and asked whether it was not time for this rider to dismount. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:27:30} He says she died twenty days or less after her son. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep19 00:28:00}
