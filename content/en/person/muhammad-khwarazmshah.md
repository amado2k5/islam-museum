## Overview
Ala' al-Din Muhammad, Khwarazm-shah, sultan of Khwarazm. He ruled Khurasan and Transoxiana, cut the Abbasid khutba in his lands, and died in 617 AH after his dispute with the Tatars. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 687,694} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 713-714}

## With the Abbasid caliphate
Al-Suyuti says that he marched on Baghdad, but a great snow fell on his army at Hamadhan for twenty days, and he turned back. In 592 AH he crossed the Jayhun with fifty thousand, and sent to the caliph al-Nasir asking for the sultanate and the restoration of the seat of the sultanate as it had been for the Seljuq kings. The caliph demolished the seat of the sultanate and sent back his envoy without an answer. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 687,694}

Al-Suyuti describes him in this place as having been tyrannical and insolent, and as having enslaved the great kings. This is the source's description. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 687,694}

## With the Tatars
Al-Suyuti says that he betrayed the Tatar merchants and killed the envoys of Genghis Khan. Genghis Khan marched against him, and he fled to an island, and died there alone in 617 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 713-714}

In *Shadharat al-Dhahab* he disguised himself with three of his companions and entered the Tatar lines to reconnoitre them, and they were seized. Two died under the beating without confessing, and he and his companion fled at night. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## In Ibn al-Athir
Ibn al-Athir says that he ruled for twenty-one years. He says that no one after the Seljuqs ruled as he did, from the borders of Iraq to Turkestan, Ghazna, part of India, Sistan, Kirman, Tabaristan, Jurjan, the lands of the Jibal, Khurasan and part of Fars. He describes him as learned in jurisprudence and the principles, and honouring of scholars. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 407-408}

Ibn al-Athir relates from a servant of the Prophet's chamber that he met him in the middle of the iwan, forbade him to kiss his hand, seated him beside himself, and asked him about Medina. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 407-408}

## For Sprouts
Ala al-Din Muhammad Khwarazmshah was the sultan of Khwarazm, ruling Khorasan and Transoxiana. He quarreled with the Abbasid caliph al-Nasir and dropped his name from the khutba; then he clashed with the Tatars, fled to an island and died there in 617 AH.

## For Explorers
Ala al-Din Muhammad Khwarazmshah, sultan of Khwarazm, ruled Khorasan and Transoxiana. Ibn al-Athir says he reigned twenty-one years and that none after the Seljuks held such a realm — from Iraq's border to Turkestan and Ghazna, parts of India, Sijistan, Kirman, Tabaristan, Jurjan, the Jibal and Khorasan — and describes him as learned in fiqh and usul, honoring the scholars. He marched on Baghdad until heavy snow at Hamadan turned him back; he asked al-Nasir for the sultanate on the Seljuk model and was refused, and he dropped the Abbasids' khutba in his lands. Then he betrayed the Tatar merchants and had Genghis Khan's envoys put to death; the Khan marched on him, he fled to an island and died alone in 617 AH. In Shadharat al-Dhahab he went disguised into the Tatar ranks with three men to spy, was caught, and escaped by night.

## For Scholars
Ala al-Din Muhammad Khwarazmshah, sultan of Khwarazm, ruled Khorasan and Transoxiana. Ibn al-Athir says he reigned twenty-one years and that none after the Seljuks held a like realm — from Iraq's border to Turkestan and Ghazna, parts of India, Sijistan, Kirman, Tabaristan, Jurjan, the Jibal and Khorasan — describing him as learned in fiqh and usul, honoring the scholars, and telling how the servant of the Prophet's chamber met him in mid-iwan: he stopped him kissing his hand, sat him beside him and asked after Medina. With the caliphate: he marched on Baghdad until twenty days of snow at Hamadan turned him back; in 592 he crossed the Jayhun with fifty thousand and asked al-Nasir for the sultanate and the sultan's palace on the Seljuk model — the caliph demolished it and sent his envoy back unanswered; he dropped the Abbasids' khutba in his lands, where al-Suyuti calls him a tyrant who enslaved great kings. With the Tatars: he betrayed their merchants and killed Genghis Khan's envoys; the Khan marched, he fled to an island and died alone in 617 AH; Shadharat al-Dhahab has him disguised with three companions inside the Tatar ranks to spy — seized, two dying under beating without confessing while he and his friend escaped by night.
