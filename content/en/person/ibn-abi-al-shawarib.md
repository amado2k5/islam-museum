## Summary
Ibn Abi al-Shawarib, the chief judge (qadi al-qudat) of the Abbasid court, brought by the Turks with the witnesses when they deposed the caliph al-Mu'tazz in 255 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 558}

## Details
When the Turkish commanders resolved to depose al-Mu'tazz they armed themselves, came to the caliphal palace — then at Samarra — seized him, dragged him by the leg, struck him with their fists and stood him in the summer sun demanding he resign; they then brought the judge Ibn Abi al-Shawarib with the witnesses and deposed him, fetched Muhammad ibn al-Wathiq from Baghdad, and al-Mu'tazz surrendered the caliphate and gave him allegiance. He died five nights after his deposition, in Sha'ban 255. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 558}

And in 261, when al-Mu'tamid designated his son Ja'far al-Mufawwid and then his brother al-Muwaffaq Talha as heirs, dividing the lands and binding a white and a black standard for each, the deed of succession was written out and executed with the chief judge Ibn Abi al-Shawarib to be hung up in the Ka'ba. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 565}
