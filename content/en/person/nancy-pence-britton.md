## Summary
Nancy Pence Britton is described on the title page of her book as formerly assistant in the Department of Textiles of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston; she wrote *A Study of Some Early Islamic Textiles* (1938). {cite:britton-early-islamic-textiles-mfa-boston-1938 p. 1} In her foreword she thanks Gertrude Townsend for help in preparing the manuscript. {cite:britton-early-islamic-textiles-mfa-boston-1938 p. 15}

## Her views
- She finds early textile inscriptions (eighth to eleventh centuries) simpler and more precise, and later ones (from the eleventh century) more elaborate and debased, and notes that *naskh* is unusual on textiles before the twelfth century. {cite:britton-early-islamic-textiles-mfa-boston-1938 p. 20}
- She explains the change in tiraz after the reign of the Fatimid caliph al-Mustansir (1036–1094) by the caliph's diminished importance: tiraz became a short pious formula endlessly repeated in debased script among scrolled bands, which she sees as anticipating late Mamluk wood-carving and metalwork. {cite:britton-early-islamic-textiles-mfa-boston-1938 p. 23}
