## Summary
Abdul Samad Rabiu is a Nigerian industrialist, founder and chairman of the BUA Group — one of Africa's largest producers of cement, sugar and refined foods — and, alongside Aliko Dangote, one of the two dominant figures of Nigerian heavy industry. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-samad-rabiu}

## Formation
Born in 1960 in Kano, son of the prominent northern merchant Isyaku Rabiu, he studied economics in the United States and returned to take over family businesses in the 1980s before founding BUA in 1988, initially as an importer of commodities the group now manufactures itself. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-samad-rabiu}

## BUA Group
BUA grew into a vertically integrated industrial house: BUA Cement — listed on the Nigerian exchange in a landmark flotation — competes head-to-head with Dangote Cement across northern Nigeria; BUA Sugar Refinery in Lagos is among West Africa's largest; and the group's flour, pasta, edible-oil and rice mills supply the domestic market. The model mirrors the country's import-substitution push: local raw materials, local plants, local brands. {cite:muslim500-abdul-samad-rabiu}

## Philanthropy
His Abdul Samad Rabiu Africa Initiative (ASR Africa), launched in 2021 with a pledged annual giving programme, funds hospitals, university grants and security-support projects across Nigeria — making him one of the country's most visible private donors. {cite:wikipedia-abdul-samad-rabiu}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as the quieter counterpart in Nigeria's industrial duopoly: the Kano merchant's son who built the BUA empire largely outside Lagos's limelight into one of Africa's great manufacturing fortunes. {cite:muslim500-abdul-samad-rabiu}
