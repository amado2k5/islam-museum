## Overview
Bakjur was a Turkish mamluk of Qarghuya who governed Aleppo and its region in the latter half of the fourth century AH. Qarghuya had left him as his deputy in Aleppo; Bakjur grew powerful, seized his master and imprisoned him in the citadel, and held the city about six years. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.7 p.371-372}

## Homs and then Damascus
Qarghuya's partisans wrote to Abu al-Ma'ali Sharif ibn Sayf al-Dawla the Hamdanid inviting him to take Aleppo; he besieged it four months and took it while Bakjur kept the citadel. After an exchange of envoys Bakjur surrendered it in return for safe-conduct for himself, his family and property, and the governorship of Homs, which he then held for Abu al-Ma'ali, devoting himself to rebuilding it and securing the roads until it prospered; from there he moved on to the governorship of Damascus. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.7 p.372}
