William Henry Quilliam (10 April 1856 – 23 April 1932) was a Liverpool solicitor who, after converting to Islam in 1887 following a journey to Morocco, took the name Abdullah Quilliam. Raised a Methodist and active in the temperance movement, he had become a solicitor in 1878 and built a criminal practice in Liverpool.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-quilliam-en}

With a donation from Nasrullah Khan, Crown Prince of Afghanistan, he bought 8 Brougham Terrace and opened the Liverpool Muslim Institute there on Christmas Day 1889 — England's first functioning mosque — with schools, an orphanage, a museum and a science laboratory, and the journals The Crescent and Islamic World.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-quilliam-en} Sultan Abdul Hamid II granted him the title of Shaykh al-Islam of the British Isles, the Emir of Afghanistan recognised him as Sheikh of Muslims in Britain, and Persia made him a vice-consul; around 600 converts in Britain were credited to his preaching.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-quilliam-en} His scholarly biographer Ron Geaves reports that he defined himself as an Ottoman Hanafi with an allegiance to traditional Islam, though no second source names his school and the question is recorded here as unsettled.{cite:geaves-sufism-britain-quilliam} His Liverpool work ended when he fled to Turkey in 1908 ahead of being struck off as a solicitor; he later lived quietly on the Isle of Man under other names, died in London in 1932, and was buried in an unmarked grave in the Muslim section of Brookwood Cemetery.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-quilliam-en}
