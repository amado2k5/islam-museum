## Summary
Muhammad ibn Ajlan of Medina is described by [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] as a truthful, well-known imam who narrated from his father, al-Maqburi and others, with [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]], [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]] and [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya al-Qattan]] narrating from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 644} He died in 148 AH. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 647}

## Trustworthiness and the question of his memory
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: declared trustworthy**: [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]], [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]], [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Ibn Uyayna]] and Abu Hatim declared him trustworthy; Ibn Ma'in said Ibn Ajlan is more reliable than Muhammad ibn Amr and no one doubts it; asked by his son about Ibn Ajlan and Musa ibn Uqba, Ahmad said: both trustworthy — how close they are! {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 644} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 646}
- **Sunni: criticism of his memory**: al-Hakim said Muslim cites thirteen of his hadiths, all as supporting reports, and later imams spoke of his poor memory; al-Qattan said he was inconsistent in [[person/nafi-mawla-ibn-umar|Nafi']]'s hadith. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 644}
:::

Al-Dhahabi notes that al-Bukhari's al-Du'afa' quotes al-Qattan to the effect that Ibn Ajlan had reports from Sa'id al-Maqburi from his father from Abu Hurayra and from a man from Abu Hurayra, mixed them up and gave both as from Abu Hurayra; al-Dhahabi prefers this reading, since al-Maqburi was truthful and kept his two routes apart. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 645} He concludes that Ibn Ajlan was middling in memory, though reports show his sharp intelligence. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 645}

## Malik's remark
Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim reported that when Malik was told certain learned people were narrating, he asked who; told "Ibn Ajlan", he said: Ibn Ajlan did not know these things and was not a scholar. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 644} Al-Dhahabi explains that Malik said this when he heard Ibn Ajlan had narrated the hadith "God created Adam in his form", adding that Ibn Ajlan is corroborated in it and that it is in the Sahih. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 645}

## Jurist of Medina, and his ordeal
Al-Dhahabi says he was one of the righteous, God-fearing imams and a mufti, with a circle in the Messenger of God's mosque. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 645} It is reported that he joined the revolt of [[person/muhammad-al-nafs-al-zakiyya|Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Hasan]], and the governor of [[place/medina|Medina]], Ja'far ibn Sulayman al-Hashimi, wanted to flog him or cut off his hand; someone asked: if you saw al-Hasan al-Basri do the like, would you strike him? He said no; he was told: Ibn Ajlan among the people of Medina is like al-Hasan among the people of Basra — and he pardoned him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 645}

## A test of his memory
Al-Ramahurmuzi relates with his chain from Yahya al-Qattan that a group in Kufa, among them Hafs ibn Ghiyath, Malih ibn Waki' and Yusuf al-Samti, decided to test Ibn Ajlan by inverting his hadith, putting what was "from Sa'id from his father" as "from his father" and vice versa; al-Qattan said he would not take part. At the end of the book the shaykh noticed, restored everything to its place and invoked against them, and al-Qattan recounts what later befell them. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 645} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 646} Al-Abbas ibn Nasr also relates from Safwan ibn Isa an odd report about his birth. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 646}
