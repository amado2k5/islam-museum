## Overview
Musa, a prophet among the prophets of the Children of Israel. Al-Bukhari relates reports about him in his *Sahih*, among them the story of the stone, al-Khidr and the Night Journey.

## Musa in the Qur'an, as the Encyclopaedia of Islam presents him
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, opens its article on Musa, the Arabic name of the Biblical prophet Moses, with the place he holds in the [[work/quran|Qur'an]]. What follows in this section and the next three is the article's account of what the Qur'an and Islamic tradition say about Musa. The article describes him as the forerunner of [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]], a model for him and one who announces him, and says that the two prophets are presented as sharing one belief. It adds that Musa is drawn in Muhammad's image: the accusations brought against him resemble those brought against Muhammad, including the charge that he wished to turn people away from the faith of their fathers and the charge of practising magic. According to the article, Musa and [[person/harun|Harun]] appear to be sent to the stubborn [[person/firawn|Pharaoh]] more than to the believing Israelites. Revelation is granted to Musa under several names, among them tawrat, kitab, furqan and suhuf, together with illumination, instruction and guidance. The article notes that the Qur'anic picture of him is made up of Biblical, Haggadic and new elements. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 692}

## His life as the Qur'an tells it, according to the article
The article summarises the story in order. The infant Musa is exposed, watched by his sister, refuses the milk of other nurses and is suckled by his own mother. Grown up, he comes to the help of an Israelite who is hard pressed and kills an Egyptian, then repents of the deed, which the article says Satan had tempted him to commit. Pursued, he escapes to [[place/madyan|Madyan]], waters the flocks of the two daughters of a shaykh there, and is invited to the shaykh's home by one of them, who comes modestly. He receives her as his wife in return for eight to ten years of service. The article adds that this early history is told in one place, while the mission itself is mentioned many times. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 692}

At the burning bush in the holy valley of Tuwa, Musa receives a message and a voice that tells him to take off his shoes, then the message for Pharaoh and the signs of his mission: the rod, the snake and the hand that turns white. The article records that his speech is difficult to understand and that Harun accompanies him as his wazir. Pharaoh reproaches him with ingratitude, saying he was brought up among the Egyptians, and gathers his magicians, whose rods are swallowed by the rod of Musa. The magicians then profess belief in God and are mutilated as punishment. Pharaoh wishes to be prayed to as a god and orders [[person/haman|Haman]] to build him a tower from which to reach the God of Musa. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 692} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

The article lists nine miracles that Musa performs, following al-Tabari: the rod and the snake, the white hand, the deluge, locusts, lice, frogs, blood, darkness and the separation of the waters of the sea. Musa spends thirty and then ten nights with God and brings instruction and admonition on tablets. While he is away, al-Samiri makes the lowing golden calf; Musa breaks the tablets. He desires to see God, and the hill is crumbled to dust. Israel fears war and is made to wander for forty years in the wilderness. The article closes the sketch by saying that his enemies [[person/qarun|Qarun]], Pharaoh and Haman perish. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

## The journey with the wise man
The article calls the story of Musa accompanying a wise man on a journey one that seems to have no parallel, and notes that the wise man is [[person/al-khidr|al-Khidr]]. It says that attempts are often made to distinguish this Musa of the al-Khidr story, as Musa son of Manasseh, from another Musa; the rest of that sentence is cut off in the scanned text and is left out here. Elsewhere on this page, al-Bukhari's report has Ibn Abbas rejecting the claim that the companion of al-Khidr was a different Musa; the article does not cite that report. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

## Kalim Allah, the one God spoke to
The article reports that Islamic tradition gives Musa a special title, Kalim Allah, and that its exact meaning calls for discussion. The word kalim can be read as one who speaks to someone or as one who is spoken to. [[person/al-baydawi-abd-allah-ibn-umar|Al-Baydawi]] gives the first meaning, treating kalim as equal to mukalim, and [[person/ibn-manzur|Ibn Manzur]] follows him while adding that [[person/abu-mansur-al-azhari|al-Azhari]], in his Tahdhib, chooses the second. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 694}

Several Qur'anic passages speak of a direct conversation between God and Musa, notably in sura IV. The article says that the use of the cognate infinitive there shows the conversation is to be taken literally and not as a metaphor, and suggests that these passages' emphasis on God's speech to Musa may be the reason for al-Azhari's reading. It also records a variant reading in sura II, with a verbal form of the root that is absent from the official text, which al-Baydawi considered a possible origin of the title, although that passage does not concern Musa. The article further mentions the epithet nadji applied to Musa in sura XIX, which al-Baydawi equated with munadji. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 694}

## Differences from the Biblical account: the academic comparison
The following is the article's comparative and historical account of the Qur'anic story, offered by its author as scholarship; it is not the Muslim understanding of the story, which is given above and in the sections drawn from Muslim sources. The article lists details in which the Qur'anic account differs from the Biblical one: it is the wife of Pharaoh, not his daughter, who rescues the infant; Musa helps two shepherdesses, not seven; the Qur'an speaks of nine miracles, not ten plagues; and Musa strikes twelve springs from the rock, one for each tribe, which the article sees as a memory of the twelve springs of Elim. The article calls the divergence greater where Haman is made a minister of Pharaoh. Among what it calls new features are Musa's repentance for the killing, his seeing the burning bush at night and wishing to take a brand from its fire for his house, and the death of Pharaoh's magicians for their belief. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

The author traces other details to the Haggada, the Jewish narrative tradition. God forbidding the infant to be suckled by an Egyptian mother, he says, matches a Haggadic account that the mouth destined to speak with God could not take anything impure. God tilting the mountain over Israel he explains from a Haggadic report that Israel hesitated to accept the Pentateuch, so that Sinai was tilted over them. The turning of sabbath-breakers into apes recalls a Haggadic story of the builders of the tower of Babel. Qarun as an immensely rich man, whose keys many strong men could hardly carry, matches the Haggadic Korah who found a hidden Egyptian treasure, with three hundred mules carrying the keys. The article treats the Qur'anic believer at Pharaoh's court who wants to save Musa as not quite clear and asks whether the Haggadic Jethro, who advises clemency at Pharaoh's court, should be compared. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

## Musa in later legend
The article states that the histories of the prophets, especially that of [[person/abu-ishaq-al-thalabi|al-Tha'labi]], supplement the Qur'anic story with much from the Bible, the Haggada and folklore. It lists the following examples, which it presents as legend and not as part of the Qur'anic account. Pharaoh's sick daughters are cured when they touch the cradle of Musa. The infant scratches Pharaoh's chin and Pharaoh wishes to kill him; on the intercession of [[person/asiya|Asiya]] he tests the child with gold and jewels on one side and burning coals on the other, and Musa reaches for the gold but Gabriel directs his hand to the coal. He puts the burned hand to his tongue and so becomes a stammerer. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

The article says that features of other legends are woven in. From the legend of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]] and Nimrod come Pharaoh frightened by dreams and persecuting infants, Musa hidden in a burning oven whose fire turns cool, and Pharaoh who has a tower built and shoots an arrow at heaven, which returns blood-stained so that he boasts he has killed God, as al-Tabari reports. From the story of Jacob and Laban comes Musa's service of eight to ten years for his wife and the spotted lambs offered by his father-in-law. The article also mentions frequent references in al-Tha'labi to a pious Egyptian woman martyred by Pharaoh with her seven children, which it takes to be modelled on the martyr mother of the Maccabees. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

Musa's rod receives particular attention. In al-Tabari an angel brought it; the article adds that Musa obtained it from his wife, that his father-in-law disputed its ownership and that an angel decided in favour of Musa. According to al-Kisa'i, earlier prophets, among them [[person/adam|Adam]], [[person/idris|Idris]], [[person/nuh|Nuh]], [[person/hud|Hud]], [[person/salih|Salih]], Ibrahim, [[person/ismail|Ismail]], [[person/ishaq|Ishaq]] and [[person/yaqub|Yaqub]], had used it before him. Al-Tha'labi describes its wonders: it shines in the dark, gives water in a drought, becomes a fruit-bearing tree when set in the ground, yields milk, honey and fragrance, turns into a double dragon against an enemy, pierces mountains and rocks, leads across rivers and sea, serves as a shepherd's staff and keeps beasts of prey from the flocks. When Musa slept, it once killed a dragon and once seven of Pharaoh's assassins. The article judges that the Biblical, Haggadic, legendary and fairy-tale features are blended into a very full picture, which in al-Tha'labi forms a regular romance. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 693}

## The story of the stone
In al-Bukhari the stone fled with Musa's garment, and he followed it until God cleared him before his people. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 278}

## With al-Khidr
Al-Bukhari relates the story in full on the authority of Ibn Abbas, including his refutation of those who claimed that the companion of al-Khidr was not Musa. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3401}

## The Night Journey
The Prophet saw him on the Night Journey as a tall man with straight hair. He returned to him, and Musa asked him to get the obligations reduced, until they came down to five. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3207}

## For Sprouts
Moses was a courageous prophet whom God spoke to, who stood for justice and mercy to guide Pharaoh and help his people.

## For Explorers
Moses is known as the one who spoke with God, a noble messenger of resolute determination who lived in Egypt. Blessed with signs such as his staff and glowing hand, he confronted Pharaoh with the call to monotheism, seeking freedom for the Children of Israel from oppression, leading them across the sea to safety with enduring courage.

## For Scholars
The interlocutor of God (Kalim Allah) and one of the five resolute messengers, whose prophetic life is the most extensively chronicled in the Qur'an. Raised in Pharaoh's court, he journeyed to Midian before receiving the divine commission in the sacred valley of Tuwa to summon Pharaoh to divine unity and liberate the Israelites. Bestowed with signs including the staff and the parting of the sea, he was given the Torah as guidance and light, leading his community with patience through trials and establishing foundational moral laws.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 03)

The Sheikh speaks of the seventy elite whom Musa chose to apologise on behalf of Bani Israel after the affair of al-Samiri and the worship of the calf (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:30:30}.

He describes them as the purest of his people, compares their standing to that of Abu Bakr, Umar and the ten promised Paradise among the Companions, on the Israelite side, and says that as they drew near they were told to lower their voices because God was about to speak to Musa {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:31:00}.

He says that even this elite told Musa they would not believe him until they saw God openly, and he finds this astonishing, asking about the state of those who are not among the elite {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:31:30}.

He mentions that after God raised them up again and they returned to their people, the sun beat down on them in the Sinai desert, so God shaded them with clouds and sent down manna and quails, and then the mountain was lifted over Bani Israel like a canopy and they thought it would fall on them, so that the mountain became a roof instead of standing on the ground {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:32:30}.

He says that Musa had one hope, to enter the Holy Land and be buried in it, so he sent men to scout out news of the giants (the Amalekites) in Bayt al-Maqdis {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:33:00}.

He distinguishes between tahassus, which is for good, and tajassus, which is for evil, mentions al-Qa'qa' ibn Amr who led an army at seventeen, and then says the men returned to Musa and told him that a mighty people were in it (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:33:30}.

He says that Bani Israel refused to enter it while the giants were there, that each of the giants was a colossus who could carry one of them in his hand, and that when fear takes hold of people they cannot say no; Musa answered that he controlled only himself and his brother {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:34:00}.

He then mentions the going of Musa and Harun to Pharaoh and their fear of his violence, that God reassured them that He was with them, hearing and seeing, and told them to speak to him gently in the hope that he might take heed {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:34:30}.

He notes that the address was to both men, yet in his reply Pharaoh singled out Musa by name, and the Sheikh thinks the reason may have been that Pharaoh was the one who raised Musa in his house {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:35:00}.

The Sheikh likens Musa, speaking loosely, to a head of department and Harun to his assistant, and points out that in some verses of the Qur'an Harun's name comes before Musa's while at other times Musa comes before Harun {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:35:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 10)

The Sheikh holds that Musa drew Bani Israel out and pulled them away from Pharaoh, from their materialism and from his disbelief in God, just as the angels draw out and extract the souls of believers and disbelievers from their bodies {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:37:00}.

He mentions that the sacred valley is named Tuwa, and suggests that God may have sanctified it again and again throughout history, so that it is a sacred tract of land {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:37:30}.

He says that when Musa left his family it was winter, and he saw a fire, so he told them he would bring them a brand from it or find guidance there, and he was glad when he found the fire {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:38:00}.

He mentions that Musa was lost in the Sinai desert and wanted someone to show him the way to Egypt, and found the fire moving: when he moved it moved ahead of him and when he stopped it stopped, so he knew the matter was not natural, and then the fire entered a thorn bush with large thorns of the kind that grows in the Sinai desert {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:38:30}.

He says that the fire entered the dry tree and lit all of it without burning it, and he mentions that he visited Mount Tur and felt a strange awe when he called the history to mind {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:39:00}.

He says that Musa was called from the right bank of the valley, in the blessed spot by the tree, that God is the Lord of the worlds, and then points out that elsewhere the Qur'an told the Prophet that he was not on the western side {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:39:30}.

He mentions that Musa was told to take off his sandals in the sacred valley of Tuwa, saying that the place is holy and so wearing sandals does not suit it, and that God knows best the wisdom in that {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:40:00}.

He says that God called him, holds that among the conditions of prophethood is fatana, meaning intelligence and eloquence, and asks how a messenger could be inarticulate; hence Musa asked that his brother Harun, who was more fluent of tongue, help him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:43:00}.

The Sheikh holds that a tongue free of an outward defect in the letters is among the conditions of prophethood, explains what was in Musa's speech by the awe of conversing with God, and likens it to a small child who has heard about a famous scholar and becomes flustered before him as he tests him on the Qur'an {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:43:30}.

He says that the child, in his confusion, may forget the Fatiha in front of the examiner, and in the same way Musa stumbled before the majesty of God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:44:00}.

He says that Musa is the only prophet whom his Lord spoke to, that his halting speech came from the splendour and majesty of the occasion, and he cites al-Husayn, who had an endearing lisp (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:44:30}.
