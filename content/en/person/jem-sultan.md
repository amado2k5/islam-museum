## Summary
Prince Cem, the younger surviving son of [[person/mehmed-ii|Mehmed II]], rose against his brother [[person/bayazid-ii|Bayezid II]] after their father's death and ended his days a hostage in crusader hands {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 163-164}.

## The contested throne
At Bursa when news of his father's death reached him, the city's people acknowledged him sultan. He offered his brother peace, then a partition — Europe to Bayezid, Asia to himself. Bayezid refused to divide what their forefathers had laboured to unite and marched on Bursa; Cem fled to the Mamluk sultan [[person/qaytbay|Qaytbay]] in Egypt, who honoured him and equipped him for the hajj {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 163}.

## The letter and its answer
On returning from the hajj, Cem received Bayezid's letter: "Since you have performed your religious duty of the hajj, why chase worldly things, when kingship was my lot by God's command?" Cem answered: "Is it just that you lie on a bed of ease surrounded by servants, while I lay my head on thorns?" He roused his Anatolian followers and was defeated by Bayezid twice {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 163-164}.

## Hostage of the West
Cem took refuge at Rhodes with the [[group/knights-of-st-john|Knights of St John]]; his compact with their Grand Master was broken under Bayezid's pressure, and he became their prisoner — a precious hostage that bought privileges now from Bayezid, now from Cem's backers in Cairo — until they sold him to Pope [[person/innocent-viii|Innocent VIII]] for a great price. On Innocent's death Cem passed to [[person/alexander-vi|Alexander VI]] until he was killed; the chronicles, as al-Salabi relays them, accused Bayezid II {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 164}.
