## Overview
Abu Hazim al-A'raj, an ascetic and preacher, whom al-Mas'udi mentions and whose conversation with [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik]] he relates. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 167-168}

## His answer on the dislike of death
Sulayman asked him why we dislike death. He answered that they had built up their worldly life and ruined their Hereafter, so they dislike moving from a populated place to a ruin. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 167-168}

He was asked how the coming before God would be, and he said that the good man is like the absent one who returns to his family happy, and the wrongdoer is like the runaway slave who comes to his master sad. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 167-168}

## His advice to the caliph
The book "The Umayyad State" by al-Salabi says that Sulayman summoned him when he made the pilgrimage in 97 AH; he advised him to fear God and to present his deeds to the Book of God, and explained that among the best deeds is performing the obligations while avoiding the forbidden things, and he refused his gift. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.149-151}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 9)
The Sheikh tells again that Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, a man of awe-inspiring presence, said he wished he had washed and shrouded the dead and had not governed the Muslims for even a single day, that Abu Hazim paused on hearing this, then told his assistant to pour water on the dead man, and praised God that at their death they wish for our life (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:16:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lectures on Sharh Kitab al-Fawa'id

The Sheikh narrates that Abu Hazim entered on Ishaq ibn Abd al-Malik and took off his shoes, saying that he takes off his shoes before his Lord five times a day and night (needs_source: narration with no source given in the lecture). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep02 01:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep02 01:06:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series Nafahat Ramadaniyya (the faults of the tongue)
The Sheikh relates that Harun al-Rashid saw Abu Hazim at the Sanctuary and said 'ask us your need', and Abu Hazim said he was ashamed to ask anyone but God in God's house; Harun waited until he left the bounds of the Sanctuary and asked again, and Abu Hazim asked: a need of this world or of the next? When Harun said he did not own the next world, Abu Hazim said he had not asked this world of the One who owns it, so how would he ask it of one who does not. The Sheikh tells it as an example of strong certainty and of calling on none but God (needs_source: the story is the Sheikh's narration). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:11:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh retells in this programme the story of Abu Hazim with the Commander of the Faithful at the Sanctuary, adding the saying: when you see rulers at the doors of scholars, rejoice; when you see scholars at the doors of rulers, there is no good. He tells it as an example of sincerity and of dignity through God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:06:30}

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says he has long loved Abu Hazim Salama ibn Dinar, and describes him as the imam, ascetic and exemplar, the great scholar, al-A'raj ("the lame"), author of admonitions and of words that resemble the words of prophets, whom the scholars agreed in praising. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:02:00} He says his sayings, if gathered, would make a small volume, and a commentary on them would run to more than several volumes. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:02:30} He says Abu Hazim was born in the time of Ibn Umar and heard from no Companion except [[person/sahl-ibn-sad-al-saidi|Sahl ibn Sa'd al-Sa'idi]], as his son [[person/abd-al-aziz-ibn-abi-hazim|Abd al-Aziz]] said when asked whether his father had heard from Abu Hurayra: whoever tells you my father heard from anyone but Sahl has lied. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:04:30}

Among his sayings that al-Heweny explains: that the burden of religion and of the world has grown heavy, because one finds no helpers for religion, and one reaches for nothing of the world without finding that a wicked man got there first; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:03:00} that kings have no friend, the envious have no rest, and looking at consequences fertilises minds; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:09:00} and that one is not a scholar until he has three traits: he does not transgress against one above him, does not despise one below him, and takes no worldly gain for his knowledge. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:21:00} Al-Heweny says each phrase is drawn from the words of the Prophet, the words of God or the wisdom of the Arabs. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:21:30}

In an episode on good character he relates from [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Sufyan ibn Uyayna]] from Abu Hazim that no one sets right what is between him and God without God setting right what is between him and people, and that humouring one face is easier than humouring all faces. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:25:30} In an episode on keeping one's good deeds he quotes him: a servant may do a good deed that pleases him and it harms him more than any sin, and do a sin that grieves him and it benefits him more than any good deed; al-Heweny explains that a good deed that breeds self-admiration can be more harmful than a sin. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep01 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep01 00:26:00}
