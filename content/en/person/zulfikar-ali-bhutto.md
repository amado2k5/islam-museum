## Summary
Zulfikar Ali Bhutto (5 January 1928 – 4 April 1979) was a Pakistani barrister and statesman who served as the fourth president of Pakistan from 1971 to 1973 and as the ninth prime minister from 1973 until his overthrow in 1977. He was the founder and first chairman of the Pakistan People's Party (PPP). His supporters call him Quaid-e-Awam, "the People's Leader"; his legacy remains among the most contentious in Pakistan's modern history. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en} {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-ar}

## Early life and education
Bhutto was born at Ratodero near Larkana in Sindh to Shah Nawaz Bhutto, dewan of the princely state of Junagadh, and Khursheed Begum, who was born Lakhi Bai into a Hindu family and converted to Islam on her marriage. He studied in Bombay, entered the University of Southern California in 1947, transferred to the University of California, Berkeley, and took a B.A. in political science in 1950; he then read law at Christ Church, Oxford, lectured in international law at the University of Southampton, and was called to the bar at Lincoln's Inn in 1953. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en} {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-ar}

## Early career and foreign ministry
In 1957 he became the youngest member of Pakistan's delegation to the United Nations, and in 1958 its youngest cabinet minister under President Iskander Mirza. Rising through Ayub Khan's cabinets, he became foreign minister in 1963, advocated of Operation Gibraltar in Kashmir — which led to the 1965 war — and negotiator of a border agreement with China. After the Tashkent Declaration he fell out with Ayub Khan and left the government in 1966. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en}

## Founding the Pakistan People's Party
Bhutto founded the PPP at Lahore on 30 November 1967 on a left-wing, socialist programme — its own founding principles, as the Arabic article lists them, were the preservation of the Islamic faith, democracy as the basis of politics, a socialist economic order, and all power to the people. The party emerged from the 1970 general election as the largest party in West Pakistan, with landslides in Punjab and Sindh, but the PPP and the Awami League, victorious in the east, could not agree on the transfer of power. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en} {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-ar}

## President and prime minister (1971–1977)
After Pakistan's defeat, the secession of Bangladesh and Yahya Khan's resignation, Bhutto assumed the presidency on 20 December 1971 under emergency rule. Through the Simla Agreement (1972) he reclaimed Indian-held territory, and through the Delhi Agreement (1973) he secured the release of 93,000 prisoners of war. He recognised Bangladesh in 1974 while hosting the Islamic Summit at Lahore, attended by leaders of 37 Muslim countries. His government, with the parliamentary opposition, promulgated the 1973 constitution, which ended emergency rule and restored parliamentary democracy, and Bhutto became prime minister. He initiated Pakistan's nuclear programme as a national priority, carried out extensive nationalisation under his socialist agenda, and ordered a military operation in Balochistan after dismissing its provincial government. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en}

## Overthrow, trial and execution
The PPP won the 1977 elections, but the opposition Pakistan National Alliance alleged rigging and violence spread; an agreement for fresh elections was overtaken on 5 July 1977 by a military coup led by army chief General Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq. Bhutto was tried for authorising the murder of the opposition politician Nawab Muhammad Ahmad Khan Kasuri, sentenced to death, and hanged at Rawalpindi's District Jail on 4 April 1979; he was buried in the Bhutto family mausoleum at Garhi Khuda Bakhsh. The trial was widely described, in Pakistan and internationally, as a judicial murder, and the Supreme Court of Pakistan later declared it unfair; he was posthumously awarded the Nishan-e-Pakistan, the country's highest civilian award. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en} {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-ar}

## Thought and religion
Bhutto was influenced by Pakistan's founder Muhammad Ali Jinnah and believed in what he called at times "Islamic socialism" and at times democratic socialism — political doctrines, not religious schools. The sources disagree about his personal religion, and the museum records the disagreement rather than resolving it: the English article on his daughter Benazir states that she, like her father, professed an adherence to Sufism; the Arabic article's infobox gives his religion as "Islam, and Shia"; the Urdu article files him in Shia categories; the daughter's article describes the family as Sunni; and his own English article names no religion for him at all. His faith is therefore recorded as unknown, with these readings preserved. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-ar} {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-ur} {cite:wikipedia-benazir-bhutto-en} {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en}

As his socialist rhetoric lost appeal, later in his rule he turned increasingly to Islamic symbolism and policies — liberalising Hajj policy, sponsoring renovations at major Sufi shrines and making public displays of religious devotion — as the scholar Sharif al Mujahid has argued. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en}

## Perspectives and criticism
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum. Supporters praise his nationalist and secular-internationalist agenda, the permanent constitution and the nuclear programme; critics cite the economic challenges, political repression and human rights abuses of his rule. His legacy remains sharply divisive in Pakistan. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en}

## Personal life
Bhutto's first marriage, arranged in 1943 when he was fifteen, was to his cousin Shireen Amir Begum. In 1951 he married Nusrat Ispahani, of Iranian Kurdish origin, with whom he had four children: Benazir, Murtaza, Sanam and Shahnawaz. His daughter Benazir served twice as prime minister, and his son-in-law Asif Ali Zardari twice as president. {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-en} {cite:wikipedia-zulfikar-ali-bhutto-ar}
