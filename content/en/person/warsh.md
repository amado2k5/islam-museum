## Overview
'Uthman ibn Sa'id ibn 'Adi al-Misri, known as Warsh, was one of the great Qur'an readers; his nickname overtook his name for his extreme fairness of skin; his origin was Qayrawan and he was born and died in Egypt in 197 AH (812 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.205}

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

Ibn al-Jazari gives his name as Abu Sa'id 'Uthman ibn Sa'id ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ibn Sulayman ibn Ibrahim al-Qurashi, a client of Quraysh, a Copt of Egypt, nicknamed Warsh. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 121} He died in Egypt in 197 AH and was born in 110; he travelled to Medina to read on [[person/nafi-al-madani|Nafi']] and completed the Qur'an with him in 155, then returned to Egypt where the leadership of teaching came to him without any rival, alongside his skill in Arabic, his knowledge of tajwid and his fine voice. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 122} Ibn al-Jazari quotes Yunus ibn 'Abd al-A'la that his reading was excellent and his voice beautiful, that he pronounced the hamza, lengthened, doubled and made the case endings clear, and that listeners never tired of him. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 122} Among his companions was Abu Ya'qub al-Azraq (d. about 240), who succeeded him in reading and teaching in Egypt and read twenty complete recitations to him, and of whom Abu al-Fadl al-Khuza'i said he found the people of Egypt and the Maghrib on his narration, knowing no other; and al-Asbahani, who died in Baghdad in 296 and was the first to bring Warsh's narration to Iraq. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 123} Ibn al-Jazari counts sixty-one lines of transmission for Warsh. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 121}
