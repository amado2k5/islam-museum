## Summary
Abd al-Rahman al-Khazini was an instrument scholar and astronomer associated with Merv and Seljuq ruler Sanjar's patronage; this record particularly concerns his hydrostatic-balance treatise of515/1121–1122 {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography; Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. He is not Abu Jafar al-Khazin or Ibn al-Haytham, and Hall finds no evidence of work in Baghdad {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}.

## Life and its evidence
Robert E. Hall reports Byzantine origin and enslavement in youth, an education in mathematics and philosophy provided by his owner, and subsequent work under Seljuq patronage at Merv {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. Much of the limited biography derives from al-Bayhaqi, with variant readings of some names and terms; that original notice was not independently read for this entry {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. No exact birth or death year is adopted from this limited evidence.

Hall relates stories of asceticism and rejected gifts, including a gift from Sanjar; these remain attributed biographical reports rather than grounds for reconstructing all his opinions or habits {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. He also questions association with the Isfahan observatory and some later prediction stories on chronological grounds; court patronage is not proof of membership in every Seljuq astronomical institution {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}.

## Identity and frame
His own preface, printed in Arabic and English, identifies Muhammad as his prophet: that is the Muslim basis, not his name, origin or employment at a Muslim court {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p. 3}. Sunni is the museum's explicit default for the documented Seljuq setting, not an attested personal legal school; Hall's sections read identify no contrary affiliation {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. The earlier card's al-A'lam reference and Wikidata claim retain their original attribution but remain independently unverified and do not support this conclusion.

## Works and the balance
Hall discusses astronomical tables dedicated to Sanjar, the balance treatise, and questions of attribution surrounding an astronomical-instrument work involving similar names {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography; Risala on Instruments; Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. The [[invention/balance-of-wisdom|balance of wisdom]] combines earlier texts and principles with a refined instrument and its applications {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. Al-Asfizari's movable pans precede it; Hall tentatively locates al-Khazini's particular refinement chiefly in beam calibration for different substances and reference liquids, not invention of every hydrostatic balance {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}.

Applications compare air and liquid weights against known materials, with qualifications for binary alloys; the author's reported high sensitivity has not been independently replicated here {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Illustrated manuscript LJS386 is a later textual witness, estimated1250?–1350? by Penn, not an autograph or surviving apparatus from Sanjar's treasury {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Origin; Summary}.
