Marhab ibn al-Harith, knight of Khaybar and champion of its Jews — a warrior-poet proverbial for valour and impregnability, dwelling in the fortress of al-Natat. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

On the day Khaybar was conquered he came out chanting, rousing his people's resolve: "Khaybar knows that I am Marhab, armed at all points, a hero tried and proven, when wars come kindled" — and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] rode out to him saying: "I am he whom my mother named Haydara, like a lion of the thicket, grim of aspect". They exchanged two blows, and Ali split with his sword Dhu al-Faqar his armour and his skull until the camp heard the stroke's sound — and his fall was the beginning of the collapse of Khaybar's fortresses. {cite:sahih-muslim}
