## Summary
Abu'l-Fadl Muhammad ibn Abi 'Abdallah al-Husayn ibn Muhammad the Katib, known as Ibn al-'Umayd; secretary of Rukn al-Dawla ibn Buya, who held high rank with him; he was named for his mother 'Umayd, daughter of Jarir ibn 'Abdallah al-Bajali, who was famed for beauty; one of the celebrated katibs of the craft; he began building a great wall around his master Rukn al-Dawla's residence. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His rivalry with al-Sahib
Between him and al-Sahib Ibn 'Abbad there was rivalry, and al-Sahib directed letters at him when he travelled to the lands of Fars; it remained a lifelong regret in Ibn al-'Umayd's heart that the governor composed for Ibn al-'Umayd negligently, despite his delicacy of spirit. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## In Miskawayh's Tajarib al-umam
[[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] served Ibn al-Amid for seven years, attending him night and day, and kept his library, so that Ibn al-Amid became one of his chief sources for the years after 340 AH. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.315} Miskawayh calls him "the master, the chief" (al-ustadh al-ra'is).
- **The Khurasani ghazis, 355 AH:** Ibn al-Amid advised Rukn al-Dawla not to let the Khurasanis into Rayy all together, and was overruled; when they entered the city from the quarter of his house he rode out against them and was speared in the forearm; his house and stores were plundered but his library, many books in every science "that would load a hundred camels and more", was spared, and he said the other stores could be replaced but this one could not. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.260} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.262} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.263} See [[event/khurasani-ghazis-at-rayy-355|the Khurasani ghazis at Rayy]].
- **His death:** Miskawayh says he died at Hamadhan in Safar, on the night of Thursday the sixth, in the year 360, after collecting the revenue of the Jibal and settling the affair of Hasanawayh. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.314} This differs from Wafayat al-a'yan, which gives the year 359. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
- **His gifts:** Miskawayh says he was the finest secretary of his age and the best versed in language, rare words, grammar, prosody and the collected works of the poets, and relates from Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn al-Qasim how Ibn al-Amid supplied lines that his son had dropped from obscure odes of al-Kumayt's diwan. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.314} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.315} Miskawayh says no poem was ever recited to him whose poet's diwan he did not know by heart, that he would repeat thirty or forty lines of Miskawayh's own verse after one hearing, and that he told him that in his youth he would wager his companions he could memorise a thousand lines in a day. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.315} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.316}
