Halil İnalcık was born in Istanbul on 7 September 1916 — the date his own CV gives, though Wikidata carries 26 May 1916 — into a Crimean Tatar family that had left Crimea for the city in 1905{cite:wikipedia-inalcik-en}{cite:wikidata-inalcik}. From the Balıkesir Teacher Training School he passed to Ankara University's history department, graduating in 1940 and taking his PhD in 1942 with a thesis on the Bulgarian question in the Tanzimat era, among the first socioeconomic studies in Turkish historiography{cite:wikipedia-inalcik-en}. His work on Bursa's judicial records and the Istanbul archives turned Ottoman history toward society and economy; he joined the Turkish Historical Society in 1947 and worked in London with Paul Wittek's circle and in Paris with Fernand Braudel{cite:wikipedia-inalcik-en}.

İnalcık taught at Ankara until 1972, then held the Ottoman history chair at the University of Chicago until 1986, and from 1994 taught at Bilkent University, where he founded the history department and trained a generation of historians, İlber Ortaylı among them{cite:wikipedia-inalcik-en}{cite:wikipedia-inalcik-tr}. Turkish letters styled him Şeyhü'l-Müverrihin, dean of historians{cite:wikipedia-inalcik-tr}. He died at Ankara on 25 July 2016, aged ninety-nine, and by decision of the Council of Ministers was buried in the cemetery of the Fatih Mosque in Istanbul{cite:wikipedia-inalcik-tr}{cite:wikidata-inalcik}.
