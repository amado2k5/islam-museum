Mir Ahmad Yar Khan Ahmedzai (1902–1979) was the eighteenth and last Khan of Kalat, the Baloch princely state that stood in subsidiary alliance first with British India and then with Pakistan. The Urdu article places his birth at Loralai; in the 1920s he served as an agent of the British intelligence service, reporting on Russian influence and the spread of pro-Marxist sympathy among the poorer Baloch, and he succeeded his father Azam Jan Khan in September 1933. He was made a Knight Grand Commander of the Order of the Indian Empire in the 1936 New Year Honours. In the early 1940s his legal adviser was Muhammad Ali Jinnah. {cite:wikipedia-mir-ahmad-yar-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-mir-ahmad-yar-khan-ur} {cite:wikidata-mir-ahmad-yar-khan}

When British paramountcy lapsed in August 1947, Kalat's position was unlike that of the states inside British India: its treaties were with the Crown, and the Khan held out for independence while Jinnah — now leading Pakistan — pressed for accession. After months of negotiation he signed the Instrument of Accession to Pakistan on 27 March 1948. His younger brother, Prince Agha Abdul Karim, rejected the decision, took refuge in Afghanistan and waged an armed resistance with little support in Balochistan itself, surrendering in 1950. {cite:wikipedia-mir-ahmad-yar-khan-en}

In October 1952 Kalat joined Kharan, Las Bela and Makran in the Baluchistan States Union, with Ahmad Yar Khan at its head as Khan-e-Azam; on 14 October 1955 the Union was dissolved into the One Unit of West Pakistan and the khanate came to an end. He briefly declared himself Khan again in June 1958; on 6 October 1958 the Pakistani government arrested him on charges of sedition, during the coup against President Iskander Mirza, and his arrest helped trigger the insurgency of Nawab Nowroz Khan. Released, he was briefly restored to his title in 1962, and in a late return to office he served as Governor of Balochistan from 1 January 1974 to 5 July 1977. He died at Kalat in 1979, and his eldest son, Suleman Dawood Jan, assumed the title of Khan of Kalat after him. His political autobiography, *Inside Baluchistan*, was published in 1975. {cite:wikipedia-mir-ahmad-yar-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-mir-ahmad-yar-khan-ur}
