Šaćir Sikirić (1893 – 22 September 1966) was a Bosnian orientalist from Oglavak near Fojnica, an Arabist and Iranist counted among the founders of modern Oriental studies in Yugoslavia.{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-bs}{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-cs} He finished the Gazi Husrev-beg Madrasa and the Sharia Judges' School in Sarajevo, then studied Oriental philology at Budapest on a provincial-government scholarship, taking his doctorate there with a dissertation on the dervish monasteries and holy tombs of Bosnia.{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-bs}{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-cs} His narrower field was the classical Persian poets, and in 1966 he took part in the first world congress of Iranists at Tehran.{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-bs}

In Sarajevo he taught Arabic at the Sharia Gymnasium from 1919, then directed the Sharia Judges' School (1929–1937) and served as professor and, from 1939 to 1945, rector of the Higher Islamic Sharia-Theological School; when the Faculty of Philosophy opened in 1950 he became its professor of Arabic and Persian language and literature, remaining in post until his death.{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-cs} With Muhamed Pašić and Mehmed Handžić he wrote an Arabic grammar for the madrasas (1936–1937), and his Persian Grammar (1951) and Persian Chrestomathy (1955) were the first of their kind in the South Slavic lands.{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-bs}{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-cs} He died of leukaemia at Sarajevo and was buried at Oglavak beside his ancestors.{cite:wikipedia-sacir-sikiric-cs}
