## Summary
Iranian sociologist and thinker (1933–1977), among the most influential intellectuals before the Iranian revolution. His lectures and writings joined Western sociology to Shia heritage; his works include Return to the Self, Red Shi'ism vs Safavid Shi'ism, Fatima is Fatima and Hajj. He died in exile in London. {cite:wikipedia-ali-shariati}

## Life and education

Ali Shariati was born in 1933 in the village of Mazinan near Sabzevar in Khorasan, into a religious teacher's family; he himself taught in primary schools and graduated from the teachers' college in Mashhad. {cite:wikipedia-ali-shariati}

He earned a doctorate at the Sorbonne in Paris, where he studied literature and the sociology of religion and absorbed the ideas of Louis Massignon, Georges Gurvitch and Frantz Fanon, and took part in the Iranian student opposition to the Shah. {cite:wikipedia-ali-shariati}

## Works and lectures

After returning to Iran he taught at the University of Mashhad and then at the Hosseiniyeh Ershad institute in Tehran, where his lectures drew thousands of young people with a blend of sociological analysis and a revolutionary re-reading of Shia heritage. {cite:wikipedia-ali-shariati}

His printed works and lectures include Islamology, Return to the Self, Red Shi'ism (Alawite versus Safavid Shi'ism) distinguishing an original historical Shi'ism from a courtly one, Fatima is Fatima, Hajj, and studies of enlightened thought; the regime banned him from teaching and imprisoned him. {cite:wikipedia-ali-shariati}

## Death and legacy

In 1977 he left Iran for London, where he died suddenly at 43 and was buried in Damascus near the shrine of Sayyida Zaynab; some accounts describe a natural death, others suspect the regime — the museum presents both. {cite:wikipedia-ali-shariati}

He is described as an intellectual engineer of the generation before the Iranian revolution: some researchers hold his discourse prepared the 1979 upheaval, while Shia clerics and thinkers criticized his sociological reading of the faith; his books are still read and translated. {cite:wikipedia-ali-shariati}
