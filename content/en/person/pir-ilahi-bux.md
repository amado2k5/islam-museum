Pir Ilahi Bux (9 July 1890 – 8 October 1975) was a Sindhi politician and educationist who served as Chief Minister of Sindh from 1948 to 1949 and, for a decade before that, as the province's education minister — the man behind the University of Sindh and a generation of Sindhi colleges, remembered in Urdu as the "Sir Syed of Sindh". His birth year is not quite settled: the English article and Wikidata give 9 July 1890, while the Urdu article gives the same day and month in 1897, and its own categories carry both years. He was born at Pir Jo Goth near Bhan Syedabad in Dadu District, into a family the English article calls a spiritual family, descendants of Makhdoom Sarwar Nooh; his father, Pir Nawaz Ali Shah, died when he was nine, and he was raised by his maternal uncle, Pir Lal Muhammad. {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-en} {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-ur} {cite:wikidata-pir-ilahi-bux}

He was educated at the primary school in Bhan Syedabad and at Naz High School, Khairpur, and went on to Aligarh Muslim University — which he left, in the Khilafat movement's fervour, for the new Jamia Millia Islamia, taking his BA there and returning to Sindh as a Khilafat leader. On Shahnawaz Bhutto's advice and scholarship he went back to Aligarh and completed an MA in History and an LLB, then practised law at Larkana. Politics followed: elected to the Sindh Legislative Assembly in 1937 and re-elected at the next term, he founded the Sindh United Front, the coalition that pressed Sindh's separation from the Bombay Presidency, and worked closely with Allah Bux Soomro and Ghulam Hussain Hidayatullah. {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-en} {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-ur}

It was as Soomro's education minister — a portfolio he held for some ten years — that he left his deepest mark. He steered the University of Sindh Act through the assembly (Jinnah personally donated Rs 150,000 to the new university), pioneered Karachi's S. M. College, presided over the Sindh Madressah Board and was the prime mover of Urdu College Karachi. After Partition, Jinnah nominated him Chief Minister of Sindh, an office he held from 3 May 1948 to 4 February 1949; in 1948 he founded Pir Ilahi Buksh Colony in Karachi to settle refugees, and he brought Professor A. B. A. Haleem from Aligarh to be the University of Sindh's first vice-chancellor. His later career was clouded: he was disqualified from politics for six years on corruption charges under Liaquat Ali Khan's government, and the Urdu article records that he co-founded the Awami Muslim League with H. S. Suhrawardy, Pir Manki Sharif and the Nawab of Mamdot. {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-en} {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-ur}

Five sons and two daughters survived him; his grandson Pir Mazhar Ul Haq became a Pakistan Peoples Party leader and provincial minister, and colleges in Karachi and Dadu carry his name. His book *Pioneers of Freedom* appeared in 1997, long after his death at Karachi's Pir Ilahi Buksh Colony — where, the Urdu article records, he was buried at the colony's Jamia Masjid after his death on 8 October 1975. {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-en} {cite:wikipedia-pir-ilahi-bux-ur} {cite:wikidata-pir-ilahi-bux}
