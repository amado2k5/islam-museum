## Summary
Sulis — the stage name of Sulis Zehra — is an Indonesian singer born on the island of Lombok in 1990, beloved as a voice of Indonesian devotional pop since childhood; The Muslim 500 lists her among influential Muslim artists. {cite:muslim500-sulis} {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## Child stardom
Discovered singing as a schoolgirl, she was paired in the late 1990s with the nasheed singer Haddad Alwi on a series of Islamic albums aimed at children — including Cinta Rasul ⁦(Love of the Messenger)⁩ — that became bestsellers in Indonesia and neighbouring Malaysia. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## Career
As an adult she released her own albums of Islamic and mainstream pop, collaborated with Indonesian composers, and performed religious repertoire for national audiences, remaining identified with the genre she helped popularise. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## Reception
Her duets with Haddad Alwi were credited in the Indonesian press with creating a family-friendly devotional genre at a moment when religious media was expanding; she remains a reference voice for a generation raised on those recordings. {cite:wikipedia-sulis} {cite:muslim500-sulis}

## Assessment
Sulis's career shows how devotional song in Southeast Asia moved from cassette stalls to mainstream entertainment; commentary on her work is uniformly warm and her influence on younger Indonesian religious singers is acknowledged. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}


## The Haddad Alwi years
The children's albums with Haddad Alwi — Cinta Rasul and its sequels — sold in the hundreds of thousands at the turn of the millennium and are credited with creating the Indonesian children's nasheed market; for a generation of Indonesians her voice is the sound of religious childhood. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## Adult career
As an adult she moved between Islamic pop and mainstream ballads, releasing solo albums and duets and performing Ramadan-season repertoire on national television, keeping her place among Indonesia's recognised devotional voices. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## Legacy
Younger Indonesian religious singers routinely cite the Alwi–Sulis recordings as formative; the duo's model of family-friendly Islamic media anticipated Indonesia's later devotional-content industry. {cite:wikipedia-sulis} {cite:muslim500-sulis}

## Origins
Born Sulis Zehra in Jakarta in 1990, she began singing in childhood religious groups before the Haddad Alwi duet albums made her, at nine years old, one of the best-known children's voices in Indonesian music. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## The Cinta Rasul phenomenon
The Cinta Rasul albums — devotional songs for children on the Prophet's life — were an industry event: mainstream Indonesian press covered their sales and their role in establishing Islamic children's music as a commercial category. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## Continuing career
Her adult catalogue spans solo albums and collaborations across the Islamic-pop spectrum, and she remains a familiar presence on Ramadan broadcasting and religious-variety television. {cite:wikipedia-sulis} {cite:muslim500-sulis}

## The children's nasheed market
Sulis's work with Haddad Alwi created the Indonesian children's nasheed market as a commercial category: the Cinta Rasul albums — devotional songs for children on the Prophet's life — sold in the hundreds of thousands at the turn of the millennium, generating an industry that later artists and labels built upon. For a generation of Indonesian Muslims her voice is the sound of religious childhood — a legacy that makes her the country's most-recognised children's devotional singer of her era. {cite:wikipedia-sulis}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists her among the most influential Muslims in arts, citing her role as a foundational voice of Indonesian children's devotional music and her continuing presence in the country's religious-broadcast calendar. {cite:muslim500-sulis}



## Standing in the field

Within Indonesian devotional music, Sulis stands as the voice of the Cinta Rasul children’s albums that built the genre’s market — a position that explains the scale of public attention the career has attracted. The Muslim 500's listing places Sulis among the most influential Muslims of the present generation in this field, reflecting both the size of the audience the work reaches and the extent to which the figure has come to represent it beyond specialist circles. {cite:muslim500-sulis}
