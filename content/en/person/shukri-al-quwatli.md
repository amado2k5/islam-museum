## Summary
Shukri al-Quwatli was a Syrian politician and statesman who was the first president of post-independence Syria, in 1943. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} He began his career as a dissident working towards the independence and unity of the Ottoman Empire's Arab territories and was imprisoned and tortured for his activism; sentenced to death by the French in 1920, he fled to Cairo, served as the chief ambassador of the Syrian-Palestinian Congress, helped finance the Great Syrian Revolt (1925–1927) through his Saudi connections, became a principal leader of the National Bloc, was elected president in 1943, and oversaw Syria's full independence in 1946. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

He was reelected in 1948 but toppled in Husni al-Za'im's 1949 coup and exiled to Egypt, returned to win the 1955 presidential election, adopted neutralism amid the Cold War, and in 1958 merged Syria with Egypt into the United Arab Republic, stepping down for Gamal Abdel Nasser, who awarded him the honorary title of "First Arab Citizen". He later backed Syria's 1961 secession from the union, left Syria after the 1963 Ba'athist coup, and died of a heart attack in Beirut weeks after the Six-Day War defeat of 1967. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

## Early life and education
Shukri al-Quwatli was born in Damascus in 1891; the Arabic article gives 21 October 1891 in the al-Shaghour district, supported by Wikidata's precise value, while the English article's lead gives 6 May 1891, and the doubly supported date is used here. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-ar} {cite:wikidata-shukri-al-quwatli} {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

The Quwatlis were a Sunni Muslim mercantile family from Baghdad which moved to Damascus in the 18th century and established itself in the district of al-Shaghour; their initial wealth stemmed from trade with Baghdad and Arabia, and after 1860 they invested in large land tracts in the Ghouta farms surrounding Damascus. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} The Arabic article relates that the family came from the Hejaz and settled in Damascus at the start of the 18th century, and that his father Mahmud al-Quwatli was a notable who died in 1914. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-ar}

He received his elementary education at a Jesuit school in Damascus, studied at the Maktab Anbar preparatory school, obtained his baccalauréat in 1908, then moved to Istanbul where he studied political science and public administration at the Mekteb-i Mülkiye, graduating in 1913; he returned to Damascus and joined the Ottoman civil service. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

## Early nationalist activities
Brought up in a pro-Ottoman environment through his family's Istanbul connections, he was influenced by the liberal Arab current whose Damascus deputies and dailies, including al-Qabas and al-Ikha' al-Arabi, shaped the Arab youth of his generation after the Young Turk Revolution of 1908. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

His first confrontation with the Ottoman authorities came in February 1914, when he refused to follow protocol and kiss the hand of Jamal Pasha, the governor of Syria, during a visit to the Damascus Governorate offices where he worked; he was thrown into prison at the Citadel of Damascus, bailed out through family connections days later, and lost his civil-service job. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

He joined the secret society al-Fatat, founded in Paris in 1911 for Arab independence and unity, and in 1915 was charged with facilitating the escape of his friend Nasib al-Bakri and his brothers, which he accomplished; he was arrested and tortured for a month, arrested again in 1916, and tried to commit suicide by cutting his wrists rather than surrender his colleagues' names, his life saved by the fellow inmate and doctor Ahmad Qadri; released on bail on 28 January 1917 after four more months in jail, he became a nationalist hero in Syria. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

After Arab forces entered Damascus on 1 October 1918 he was appointed assistant to the governor of Damascus, but, drawn to a republican rather than monarchist view, he co-founded the Independence Party on 15 April 1919, with a pan-Arab, secular, anti-British and anti-Hashemite outlook. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} When France imposed its mandate after the Battle of Maysalun of 23 July 1920, the French sentenced 21 nationalist leaders, including Quwatli, to death on 1 August 1920; he fled hours before his arrest warrant to Haifa and then Cairo, from where he travelled the Arab world and Europe as the virtual ambassador of the Syrian-Palestinian Congress, and by 1925 had solidified his position as intermediary between Ibn Saud and the Congress. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

Quwatli funneled Saudi funds and arms to the Great Syrian Revolt (1925–1927); rivals in Cairo later accused him of pocketing some of the money he raised — an accusation the article reports among the exiles' feuds. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} Pardoned under a general amnesty in 1930, he returned to Syria and joined the National Bloc, locally known by then as the "apricot king" for the revenues of his agricultural lands, and was Minister of Finance from 1936 to 1938 (the Arabic article records his joining the first National Bloc government in 1936 as Minister of Defence). {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-ar}

## First presidential term and independence
Before the 1943 elections the French authorities tried to negotiate a treaty binding an independent Syria to France in return for help in securing his election; Quwatli refused, confident the National Bloc would win regardless, and he became Syria's president on 17 August 1943. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

He pressed for independence, declared war on the Axis powers, and when French forces bombarded Damascus and other cities in May 1945 he requested British intervention; British troops entered Damascus on 1 June and halted the French campaign, and France agreed at a great-power summit to withdraw from Syria and Lebanon. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} The transfer of powers began on 1 August 1945 as he announced the establishment of the Syrian Army; France completed its withdrawal on 15 April 1946 and Quwatli declared Syria's independence day on 17 April. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

After independence the National Bloc was dissolved and replaced by the National Party, emerging leaders challenged his leadership, and his antagonism with the Hashemite kings of Iraq and Jordan grew as they sought to unite Syria under their monarchy and he countered that Iraq and Jordan should join a republican Syria under his leadership. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} In early 1947 his allies amended the constitution to let him seek re-election, meeting strong disapproval and a campaign to unseat him. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

## Second term, the 1948 war and the 1949 coup
He won a second term by a slim majority on 18 April 1948 against Khalid al-Azm. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} He had opposed the partition of Palestine, proposed the Arab Liberation Army as a volunteer force in place of regular armies, petitioned the Arab League's head not to commit Arab armies and to arm and fund the Palestinians instead, and, when that failed, ordered the Syrian Army into Palestine on 15 May. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

The Syrian Army's poor showing set Quwatli and his chief of staff Husni al-Za'im accusing each other of mismanagement and profiteering, eroded his popularity, and Syria under him refused to sign an armistice with Israel when its neighbours did. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} On 30 March 1949 Za'im overthrew him in a coup the article describes as backed and allegedly co-planned with the US Central Intelligence Agency; Quwatli was taken to Mezzeh Prison and resigned on 7 April, then, under Egyptian and Saudi pressure, was released in mid-April and exiled to Alexandria, Egypt, where King Farouk received him as a guest of honor and he befriended Gamal Abdel Nasser. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

## Third term, neutralism and the union
Quwatli returned to Syria in 1955 after the ouster of Adib al-Shishakli and won the August 1955 presidential election, defeating Khalid al-Azm 91 votes to 41 in the second round, supported by Egypt and Saudi Arabia in their shared opposition to the Baghdad Pact. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-ar}

A conservative presiding over an increasingly leftist-dominated government, he officially adopted neutralism: after the United States turned down his arms request of September 1955 he drew closer to the Eastern bloc, concluded a defence agreement with Egypt in October 1955, severed ties with Britain after the 1956 tripartite invasion of Egypt and visited Moscow to request Soviet backing for Nasser, and on 6 August 1957 established a long-term agreement with the USSR; a CIA-sponsored coup plot against his government was foiled on 12 August 1957, according to the article. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

As leftist and military influence grew and officers coordinated with Nasser independently of him, unity talks intensified; officers pressed for unity without consulting him, which he told the Egyptian ambassador amounted to a coup, but Arab nationalist pressure and fear of a communist takeover prevailed, and on 22 February 1958 the United Arab Republic was established on Nasser's terms, Quwatli resigning and Nasser becoming president, awarding him the title of "First Arab Citizen". {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

## Later life and death
By 1960 Quwatli had quarrelled with Nasser, and he condemned the land distribution and industrial nationalization of July 1961; when secessionist officers broke up the union on 28 September 1961 he supported them, and on 23 October made a televised speech saying "unity does not mean annexation", while officers' discussion of returning him to the presidency did not materialize, largely due to his age. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} After the Ba'athist coup of 8 March 1963 he left Syria and moved to Beirut, Lebanon. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

He died of a heart attack shortly after the Six-Day War: taken to hospital on 29 June 1967, he died in Beirut on 30 June, according to the Syrian historian Sami Moubayed after learning of the Arab armies' defeat; the Ba'athist government at first refused burial in Damascus and relented after diplomatic pressure from King Faisal of Saudi Arabia, and an honorary state funeral was held on 1 July 1967. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported by the sources, not as a verdict of the museum.

Critics in his own time included rival nationalists who accused him of pocketing revolt funds, opposition parties who campaigned against his 1947 constitutional amendment for re-election, a Syrian press sharply critical of his handling of the 1948 war, and the United States and Baghdad Pact countries, who by September 1957 had decided his government had to be removed; he in turn accused Egyptian rule in the union of running Syria as a police state. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} On the day of his funeral Radio Damascus called him "one of the sons of this homeland who has sacrificed and struggled for the advancement, liberation and unity of the Arab nation", and he was proclaimed a founding father of the Syrian Arab Republic. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}

## Personal life
In 1928 Quwatli married Bahira al-Dalati, the 19-year-old daughter of the nationalist Said al-Dalati, whom he had met while in jail in 1916; they had five children, and Bahira died in 1989. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en} The museum does not publish family detail beyond what understanding his public life requires.

## Impact and legacy
Historians and the Syrian population generally consider Quwatli to be one of the most renowned Syrian leaders of the twentieth century, according to the historian Sami Moubayed as reported in the article. {cite:wikipedia-shukri-al-quwatli-en}
