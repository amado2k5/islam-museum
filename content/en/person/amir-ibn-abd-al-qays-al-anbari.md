## Overview
Amir b. 'Abd al-Qays al-'Anbari was a Basran ascetic (tabili). {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 463}

## Denunciation and exile
His way of life attracted the attention of Humran b. Aban, the agent of 'Uthman, who denounced him to the caliph. He was interrogated and exiled to Damascus, where he died, probably during the caliphate of Mu'awiya. The events fall between 29 and 35/650-6. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 463}

## Austerity and its interpretation
His austerity seems to have consisted in abstinence, and he despised women. The measures taken against him may have been dictated by a wish to prevent the advocacy of celibacy at a time when Islam needed fighting men. Ibn Qutayba, by contrast, says his puritanism was suspected of Kharijism. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 463}

## In posterity
He is remembered not only as an eloquent man whose sayings were preserved, but also by Sufism, which counts him among the principal zuhhad and attributes to him a number of sayings. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 464}

## In Ibn al-Mubarak's Kitab al-Zuhd
[[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Ibn al-Mubarak]] gathers in his [[work/kitab-al-zuhd-ibn-al-mubarak|Kitab al-Zuhd]] a chapter on Amir ibn Abd Qays and [[person/sila-ibn-ashyam|Sila ibn Ashyam]]. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 294} He reports from al-Hasan that Amir told people who were talking about this world that they had many worries, and that if he could he would make all his worries one; al-Hasan says he did so until he met God. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 294} He reports from Qatada that Amir stayed behind his companions in a thicket where there was a lion and said, "I am ashamed before my Lord to fear anything besides Him", and that he asked his Lord to ease his ablutions in winter, to remove desire for women from his heart and to keep Satan from his heart in prayer. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 294} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 295} He reports from Amir's nephew that he would take his stipend and give from it to every poor man he met, yet when he came home and they counted it, it was as much as he had received. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 295}

He reports from Ma'qil ibn Yasar that he saw Amir ibn Abd Allah al-Anbari stop the oppression of a protected non-Muslim, saying, "God's covenant shall not be wronged today while I am a witness", and that Ma'qil asked him about what people said, that he ate neither meat nor clarified butter and did not marry, and he answered each point. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 298} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 299}

He reports from Bilal ibn Sa'd that Amir was denounced to Ziyad, or in another version to Ibn Amir, for having given up women and keeping silent when told that Abraham was not better than he; the matter was written to [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]], who ordered him sent to Syria on a camel saddle. Amir answered that his silence had been out of astonishment, and that he gave up women for fear that the world would divide his heart. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 299} [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] lodged him with himself in al-Khadra', where he prayed from before dawn until nightfall and would not touch his food; he declined the slaves and mounts Uthman ordered for him; and on campaign he would ask his companions to let him be their servant and caller to prayer and to spend on them. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 299} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 300}
