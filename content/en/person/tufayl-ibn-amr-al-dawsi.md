Al-Tufayl ibn Amr al-Dawsi, chief of the tribe of Daws and its master-poet, nicknamed Dhu al-Nur; he came to Mecca in the last part of the eleventh year of the prophethood, and Quraysh's chiefs went to him warning him of the Prophet and claiming his word was like magic, separating a man from his father and brother. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

So he stuffed his ears with cotton for fear of hearing any of his words, then entered the mosque and stood near the Prophet as he prayed at the Ka'ba — and God willed he should hear some of his words. He said to himself: lose your mother! By God I am a man of understanding, a poet; the fine is not hidden from me nor the ugly — what stops me from listening? So he followed the Prophet to his house, heard the Qur'an, became Muslim and bore the testimony of truth. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He returned to his people in Yemen calling them to Islam, and kept at them until he came to the Prophet at Medina during the Khaybar campaign with seventy or eighty households of his people. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
