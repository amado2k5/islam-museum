## Overview
Shu'ayb ibn al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Abu Abd al-Malik al-Fahmi by clientage (mawla), al-Misri, is the name, kunya and nisba given by al-Safadi {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 pp. 93-94}. Ibn al-Imad lists him in the events of 199 AH as Shu'ayb ibn al-Layth ibn Sa'd al-Misri, the jurist {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 2 p. 471}.

## Transmission
Al-Safadi says he transmitted from his father and from Musa ibn Ali, and that his son Abd al-Malik, Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman and Ibn Abd al-Hakam, the jurists, transmitted from him {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 pp. 93-94}.

## Standing and death
Al-Safadi describes him as an imam, a mufti and trustworthy, and notes that Muslim, Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i transmitted hadith from him {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 pp. 93-94}. He gives his death as 199 AH {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 pp. 93-94}, the year under which Ibn al-Imad also lists him {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 2 p. 471}. By the museum's conversion 199 AH corresponds to 814 or 815 CE (derived).
