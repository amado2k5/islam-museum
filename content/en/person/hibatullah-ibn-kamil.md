## Overview
Hibat Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Kamil, Abu al-Qasim, was chief da'i in Egypt for the Fatimids, whom al-A'lam calls the Ubaydis, and chief judge in the last years of their state {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}. He therefore belonged to the Isma'ili Fatimid establishment, not to the Sunni tradition. He was titled Fakhr al-Umana and had learning in adab and wrote poetry {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.

## What was said of him
Ibn Qadi Shuhba, as quoted by al-Zirikli, says he was among the great scholars of the Egyptian state and was the judge of the caliph al-Adid {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.

## Death
When their rule ended he was seized and put to death by crucifixion in Egypt; he was one of eight who tried to restore the state of the Banu Ubayd, whom Salah al-Din hanged {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}. Al-A'lam places his death in 569 AH, equal to 1174 CE, and gives no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}.
