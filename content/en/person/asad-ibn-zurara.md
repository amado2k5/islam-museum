Abu Umama As'ad ibn Zurara ibn Udas al-Najjari al-Khazraji, one of Yathrib's great chiefs; he was in the vanguard of the six Khazraj men who met the Prophet at al-Aqaba near Mecca in the hajj season of year eleven of the prophethood and became Muslim at his hands. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

As'ad was the first of them to Islam and readiness; on their return to Medina he was the shelter and backer of Mus'ab ibn Umayr in spreading the call. He witnessed both Aqaba pledges and was the chief of Banu al-Najjar; he counts among the most impactful men of the Ansar in founding the house of emigration at Yathrib. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## His entry in al-Isaba
Ibn Hajar gives him a short entry in al-Isaba under number 163 as "Asad ibn Zurara", then notes that the correct form of his name is "As'ad ibn Zurara", as Abu Musa al-Madini had pointed out {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.46}. The entry thus corrects the vocalisation of his name more than it reports his biography, which is preserved in the sira works as above.

## For younger visitors
As'ad ibn Zurara was among the first people of Medina to accept Islam; he met the Prophet at Mecca before the Hijra and pledged to him, then stood behind Mus'ab ibn Umayr when the Prophet sent him to Medina as a teacher {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## Notes for researchers
- al-Isaba fi Tamyiz al-Sahaba, entry 163 (al-Maktaba al-Asriyya scan, p. 46); the entry is a name-vocalisation correction, not a full biography.
