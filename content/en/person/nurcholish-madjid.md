## Summary

Nurcholish Madjid (1939–2005) was an Indonesian thinker, among the leading faces of Islamic renewal in Southeast Asia, author of the phrase 'Islam Yes, Islamic Party No' that transformed Indonesian Muslim organisational politics.{cite:wikipedia-nurcholish-madjid}

## Life

Born in Jombang, East Java, he graduated from Islamic sciences institutes then the University of Chicago, where he studied under Fazlur Rahman. He headed the Muslim Students' Association (HMI) and became a leader of Indonesian Islamic higher education.{cite:wikipedia-nurcholish-madjid}

## Ideas and institutions

He founded the Paramadina Foundation (1986) and its university to teach Islam through a modern pluralist method. He called for modernising Islamic thought, development and acceptance of political pluralism, opposing the merger of faith with a single party.{cite:wikipedia-nurcholish-madjid}

## Standing and debate

Nicknamed the 'Sheikh of Indonesian Islam' and father of Indonesian renewal, he was criticised for distance from political Islam and openness to the West. He died in 2005; Paramadina University carries his legacy.{cite:wikipedia-nurcholish-madjid}
