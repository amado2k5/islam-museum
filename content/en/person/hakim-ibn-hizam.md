## Summary
[[person/hakim-ibn-hizam|Hakim ibn Hizam]] ibn Khuwaylid al-Asadi al-Qurashi, nephew of [[person/khadija|Khadija]] the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]]'s wife; kunya Abu Khalid, with hadith in the Six Books {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}.

## His early life
Musa ibn Uqba relates from Abu Habiba, al-Zubayr's client, that Hakim said: "I was born thirteen years before the Elephant", and that he was a reasoning child when Abd al-Muttalib meant to sacrifice his son Abdullah; al-Waqidi adds that was five years before the Prophet's birth {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}. His father was killed in the Fijar war which Hakim witnessed, and al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar relates he was born inside the Ka'ba {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}. He held the rifada (feeding duty), did good and kept kinship-ties, and was a scholar of Quraysh's genealogies and lore {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}.

## His bond with the Prophet and his Islam
He was the Prophet's friend before the mission, loving and beloved after it, but his Islam came late, in the conquest year; the Prophet said: "whoever enters Hakim ibn Hizam's house is safe" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}. He had witnessed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] with the disbelievers and escaped, and when he swore an oath in earnest would say: "by Him who saved me on the day of Badr" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}.

He was among [[concept/al-muallafa-qulubuhum|those whose hearts are won over]] — he witnessed [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]] and was given a hundred camels of its spoils — then his Islam became good {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}. In the Sahih he asked the Prophet about things he used to do in the Jahiliyya — is there reward in them? He said: "you came to Islam upon what good had gone before from you" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}.

## Dar al-Nadwa
The Dar al-Nadwa (see [[event/plot-of-dar-al-nadwa|the Dar al-Nadwa plot]]) was in his hands; he sold it to Mu'awiya for a hundred thousand dirhams, and when Ibn al-Zubayr blamed him he answered: "nephew, I bought with it a house in Paradise" — and gave all the dirhams in alms {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}.

## His death
He lived a hundred twenty years — half in the Jahiliyya, half in Islam {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}. His year of death is disputed: fifty, or "year four" (so the printed text), or fifty-eight, or sixty {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}. Bukhari in al-Tarikh says he died in year sixty at a hundred twenty years of age {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 337}.
