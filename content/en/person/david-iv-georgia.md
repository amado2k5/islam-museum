## Summary
David, king of the Georgians, who had recently taken Tiflis — a just ruler toward his subjects; he died in 518 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 538}.

## Details
Among the dead of 518 Ibn al-'Imad names "David, king of the Kurj [Georgia], who had recently taken Tiflis," describing him as "just toward his subjects, attending Friday prayer, listening to the sermon, and honouring the Muslims" {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 538}. The entry shows the chronicler's fairness: he praises a non-Muslim king for justice to his people and kindness to Muslims, and records his attending the Friday assembly and hearing the khutba — the custom of Muslim kings {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 538}. Recorded in the same year are al-Hasan ibn al-Sabbah, lord of Alamut and leader of the Isma'ilis, and Abu al-Fath Sultan ibn Ibrahim al-Maqdisi, the Shafi'i jurist counted among the most learned of Egypt's fuqaha' {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 538}.
