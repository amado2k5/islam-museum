## Summary
Raed Salah Abu Shakra is a Palestinian religious leader from Umm al-Fahm in Israel, longtime head of the northern branch of the Islamic Movement — the country's largest grassroots Muslim organisation of that era — and a figure at the centre of sharp contest between his supporters and the Israeli state. {cite:wikipedia-raed-salah}

## Life and formation
Born on 10 November 1958 in Umm al-Fahm, he studied Islamic law at Hebron University in the West Bank. He was among the students influenced by Sheikh Abdullah Nimar Darwish, founder of the Islamic Movement inside Israel, which preached piety, social service and community organisation among Palestinian citizens of Israel. {cite:wikipedia-raed-salah}

## Career
Salah served three terms as mayor of Umm al-Fahm (1989–2001). In the 1990s the movement split over participation in Israeli parliamentary elections: Darwish led the southern branch into coalition politics, while Salah's northern branch rejected participation and built a parallel network of charities, religious education and al-Aqsa activism under the slogan that the holy sanctuary is in danger. He organised annual al-Aqsa rallies drawing tens of thousands and led foundations for the upkeep of Jerusalem's Islamic holy places. {cite:wikipedia-raed-salah}

## Confrontation with the state
Israeli authorities arrested and imprisoned Salah multiple times — including a sentence in the early 2000s over charity funding channels, and later terms connected to incitement charges and to his presence at the 2010 Gaza flotilla events. In November 2015 Israel outlawed the northern branch entirely, and Salah served further prison terms from 2017 onward. His supporters describe him as a defender of al-Aqsa and of Palestinian citizens' religious life; Israeli officials describe him as an inciter — the museum records both positions attributed, without verdict. Human-rights groups criticised aspects of the bans and prosecutions. {cite:wikipedia-raed-salah}

## Legacy
Salah made the "al-Aqsa is in danger" frame a fixture of Palestinian and wider Muslim discourse, and his movement shaped the civic-religious infrastructure of Arab towns inside Israel even after its banning. {cite:wikipedia-raed-salah}
