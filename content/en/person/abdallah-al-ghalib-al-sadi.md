## Summary
Son of [[person/muhammad-al-shaykh-al-sadi|Muhammad al-Shaykh]] and his Sa'di successor {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 245-251}.

## The challenge
He wrote to the Ottoman admiral: 'the sultan of the West will surely dispute Egypt's mahmal with you and come to you in Egypt' — a letter al-Salabi reads as resentment of the Ottoman caliphate and ambition for the imamate of all Muslims {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 245}.

## Crushing the revolts
After his father's murder in 964/1557 he crushed Mawlay Uthman's rising in the Sous, Mawlay Umar's in Debdou and Mawlay Abd al-Mu'min's in Marrakesh; he massacred three brothers who refused bay'a to his son Muhammad al-Mutawakkil, sending his brothers fleeing to Tlemcen and Algiers. He met [[person/hasan-ibn-khayr-al-din|Hasan b. Khayr al-Din]] inconclusively at Wadi al-Lin near Fez, and agreed to surrender Badis to Spain in exchange for defence of Morocco's shores {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 245-251}.
