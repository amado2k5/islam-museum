Hadith master of Basra on whose eminence consensus held; teacher of al-Bukhari and Ahmad; heard Ibn Uyayna, Hushaym, Ibn Mahdi; answered in the mihna, then affirmed the Qur'an uncreated before his death in 234.

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Ja'far, Abu al-Hasan the hafiz, Ibn al-Madini, calling him "one of the firm luminaries, and the hafiz of the age". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 138} He includes him only because [[person/abu-jafar-al-uqayli|al-Uqayli]] listed him among the weak, saying he leaned towards Ibn Abi Du'ad and the Jahmiyya while also saying his hadith was sound; al-Dhahabi comments: "what a wretched thing he did". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 138}

**The effect of the Mihna on narration from him.** Al-Uqayli reported from Abd Allah ibn Ahmad that his father [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] used to narrate from Ibn al-Madini, then stopped naming him, saying "a man told us", then left his hadith; al-Dhahabi replies that Ahmad's narration from him is in his *Musnad*. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 138} He notes that [[person/ibrahim-al-harbi|Ibrahim al-Harbi]] left him for his closeness to Ahmad ibn Abi Du'ad, who had been generous to him, and that [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] refrained from narrating from him in his Sahih for the same reason. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 138} Ibn Abi Hatim said that [[person/abu-zura-al-razi|Abu Zur'a]] stopped narrating from him because of his conduct in the Mihna, while his own father narrated from him because he had turned back from it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 138} Al-Dhahabi quotes his reply to someone who reproached him for giving in — "how light the sword seems to you!" — and his words: I feared being killed; had I been struck a single lash I would have died. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 141} Muhammad ibn Uthman ibn Abi Shayba reported hearing him two months before his death say that whoever says the Qur'an is created is an unbeliever, and hearing him on the pulpit declare unbelievers those who claimed this, denied the vision of God, or denied that God truly spoke to Moses. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 141} Al-Dhahabi judges that he was "fearful and cautious on the question of the Qur'an, though eager to show the good". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 139}

**Shi'ism at Basra.** He reports Ibn Ma'in saying that when Ali came to Baghdad he displayed the Sunna, and when he went to Basra he displayed Shi'ism; al-Dhahabi explains that he did so to win the Basrans over to love of Ali, since they were 'Uthmaniyya. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 139}

**His standing in the hidden defects of hadith.** Abu Hatim said he was a beacon among people in knowledge of hadith and its defects, and that Ahmad never named him but used his kunya out of respect. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 138} [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Ibn Uyayna]] said: they blame me for loving Ali; by God, I learned more from him than he learned from me; and he called him "the serpent of the valley". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 139} [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-mahdi|Ibn Mahdi]] said Ibn al-Madini was the most knowledgeable of men in hadith, and [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya al-Qattan]] said what Ibn Uyayna said. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 139} When he came to Baghdad he took the seat of honour, and when Yahya, Ahmad and al-Mu'ayti disagreed on something, he spoke on it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 139} Abu Dawud said Ibn al-Madini knew the variations of hadith better than Ahmad, and Salih Jazara said he was the most knowledgeable he had met of hadith and its defects. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 139} Al-Dhahabi says that he was the utmost in knowledge of the defects of prophetic hadith, with complete knowledge of criticism of men, perhaps unique in his time; that he was al-Qattan's student; and that he is said to have written about two hundred works. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 141}

**The report on "and fruit and abb".** Al-Athram reported that Ibn al-Madini narrated from al-Walid ibn Muslim Umar's report on the meaning of *abb*, with the wording "leave it to its Creator"; Ahmad said: this is false; it is "to the one who knows it". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 139} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 140}

## Al-Dhahabi's reply to al-Uqayli and the rule of solitary reports
Al-Dhahabi uses this entry to explain his method. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 140} He notes that [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] filled his Sahih with Ibn al-Madini's hadith and reports him saying he never felt small before anyone except Ali ibn al-Madini. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 140} Then he addresses al-Uqayli: if the hadith of Ali, his companion Muhammad, his teacher Abd al-Razzaq, [[person/uthman-ibn-abi-shayba|Uthman ibn Abi Shayba]], [[person/afan-ibn-muslim|Affan]] and their like were abandoned, the door would be shut and speech cut off; each of them is more trustworthy than al-Uqayli by many degrees. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 140} He holds that when a trustworthy hafiz reports hadiths no one else has, this raises him and shows his care, unless his error is shown, and that every Companion reported some sunna that no one else did. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 140} He sets the rule: a solitary report by a trustworthy, exact transmitter counts as sound but rare (*sahih gharib*); a solitary report by a merely truthful transmitter or one below him counts as denounced (*munkar*); and a transmitter who often reports what others do not support, in wording or chain, becomes abandoned. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 140} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 141} He adds that being trustworthy does not require being free of sin and error, and that the point of listing trustworthy men with a slight innovation or a few mistakes is to show that others outweigh them when they disagree; he ends: "so weigh things with justice and scruple". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 141}

## His death
Al-Bukhari said he died in Dhu al-Qa'da of the year 234 at Samarra. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 141}
