## In brief
Abdallah b. Amir b. Kurayz b. Rabi'a al-Qurashi al-Abshami, nephew of Uthman b. Affan; the historians mention him among those who wrote the treaty between Ta'if and a tribal quarter, and among men whose name matched their father's. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.901}

## His governorship and conquests
Al-Waqidi says he was among the leading men and nobles of Quraysh — generous, brave, fortunate. Uthman appointed him over Basra after Abu Musa al-Ash'ari in 29 AH and added Fars to his charge after Uthman b. Abi al-As; he conquered all of Khurasan, the marches of Fars, Sijistan, Kirman, and beyond until reaching the district of Ghazna. In his governorship Yazdajird, last king of Persia, was killed, and Ibn Amir entered ihram from Nishapur in thanksgiving to God; he came before Uthman, who reproached him for tempting him with ascetic display, and he brought great wealth which he divided among Quraysh and the Ansar. He was the first to establish the reservoirs at Arafa and channel the spring to them. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.901}

## In the civil war and his death
Uthman was killed while Ibn Amir held Basra, so he carried the funds in his charge to Mecca and joined Abu Talha and al-Zubayr; his part in the civil war is well documented. Al-Haytham b. Adi says he died in the eighties, while al-Tabari in the Dhayl gives 85 AH; al-Zuhri, Yahya b. Sa'id al-Ansari, Asim b. Ubaydallah, Muhammad b. Zayd b. al-Muhajir, Abd al-Rahman b. al-Qasim, Abdallah b. Abi Bakr b. Hazm, and others narrated from him. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.901}
