## Summary
Abu Sulayman Hamd ibn Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn al-Khattab al-Khattabi of Bust (319–388 AH) was a hadith scholar, jurist, man of letters, philologist and poet, and the author of [[work/maalim-al-sunan|Ma'alim al-Sunan]], the first known commentary on the [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan of Abu Dawud]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 1} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13}

## Lineage and home
His name was Hamd, and he descended from [[person/zayd-ibn-al-khattab|Zayd ibn al-Khattab]]; "al-Khattabi", with *a* and a doubled *t*, refers to his ancestor al-Khattab. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13} "Al-Busti" refers to [[place/bust|Bust]], a town in the land of [[place/kabul|Kabul]] in his biographer's words. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13} He was born there in Rajab 319 AH and died there in 388 AH. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 14}

## Teachers and students
- He learned language and letters from [[person/ghulam-thalab|Abu 'Umar al-Zahid]], Abu 'Ali Isma'il al-Saffar, Abu Ja'far al-Razzaz and other scholars of Iraq. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13}
- He learned law from [[person/al-qaffal-al-shashi|al-Qaffal al-Shashi]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13}
- He heard the Sunan of Abu Dawud from [[person/ibn-al-arabi-ahmad|Abu Sa'id ibn al-A'rabi]], and relates from him in his commentary. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 10} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 84}
- [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|Al-Hakim Abu 'Abd Allah Ibn al-Bayyi' al-Nisaburi]] and others transmitted from him. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13}

## Travels and standing
His biographer quotes [[person/al-samani|al-Sam'ani]]: he was an authority and truthful; he reached [[place/iraq|Iraq]] and [[place/hijaz|the Hijaz]], travelled about [[place/khurasan|Khurasan]] and went out to [[place/transoxiana|Transoxiana]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13}

## Works
The notice lists among his books: {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13-14}
- Gharib al-Hadith (Rare Words of Hadith), which the biographer calls supremely fine and eloquent.
- A'lam al-Sunan, a commentary on al-Bukhari's Sahih.
- [[work/maalim-al-sunan|Ma'alim al-Sunan]], a commentary on Abu Dawud's Sunan.
- Islah Ghalat al-Muhaddithin (Correcting the Errors of Hadith Transmitters).
- Sha'n al-Du'a (On Supplication).
- Al-'Uzla (On Solitude).

## His view of Abu Dawud's Sunan
He is quoted as saying that no book like it had been composed on the rulings of religion, that it won acceptance from everyone and became an arbiter between the groups of scholars and the ranks of jurists of every school, and that the people of Iraq, Egypt and the Maghrib rely on it. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 11}
