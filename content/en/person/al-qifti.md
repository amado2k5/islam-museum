## Overview
Jamal al-Din Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Yusuf ibn Ibrahim ibn Abd al-Wahid al-Shaybani al-Qifti, known as al-Qadi al-Akram (568–646/1172–1248), was a historian, scholar and vizier of Aleppo — author of Inbah al-Ruwat 'ala Anbah al-Nuhat and of the Tarikh al-Hukama, and the collector of a matchless library {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 872} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 325-326}.

## Upbringing and studies
He was born in 568/1172 at Qift in Upper Egypt (al-Idfawi gives 563) and received his early education in Cairo. In 583/1187 he went to Jerusalem, where his father had been appointed deputy to the Qadi al-Fadil, Salah al-Din's chancellor, and spent many years there as a student, already collecting material for his books {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 872} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 325}.

## In Aleppo
The disturbances following Salah al-Din's death forced him to move to Aleppo in 598/1201, where a friend of his father protected him and he returned to scholarship. The Atabeg al-Malik al-Zahir Ghazi put him over the diwan of finances — a post he accepted reluctantly — which brought him the title al-Qadi al-Akram. He resigned on al-Zahir's death in 613/1216; three years later al-Malik al-Aziz Muhammad restored him, and he held the office until he was dismissed at his own request in 628/1230. He then studied until al-Aziz appointed him vizier in 633/1236 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 872}.

He used his office to serve learning, sheltering Yaqut al-Hamawi, who had fled the Mongols, and helping him compile his great geographical dictionary {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 872}.

## Works and library
Of the twenty-six titles known for him only two survive: Ikhbar al-Ulama bi-Akhbar al-Hukama, the Tarikh al-Hukama — preserved in al-Zawzani's epitome — with 414 lives of physicians, philosophers and astronomers including quotations from Greek works lost in the original; and Inbah al-Ruwat with about a thousand biographies of scholars. Lost works include a history of Egypt to Salah al-Din's dynasty in six volumes and histories of the Seljuks, the Mirdasids, the Buyids and the Yemen {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 872} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 325}.

He was dignified and scrupulous, collected a library no one had matched, loved nothing of this world but books, and kept neither house nor wife. He died in Ramadan 646/1248 and bequeathed his books — worth fifty thousand dinars — to al-Malik al-Nasir of Aleppo {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 236} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 325-326}.
