## Summary
Alassane Dramane Ouattara — widely known as "ADO" — is the president of Cote d'Ivoire, an economist who moved from the International Monetary Fund and West African central banking into Ivorian politics and, after the violent 2010–2011 post-election crisis, into the presidency he has held since April 2011. {cite:wikipedia-alassane-ouattara}

## Life and formation
Born on 1 January 1942 in Dimbokro, he was trained as an economist in the United States (University of Pennsylvania) and built a career at the IMF — rising to deputy managing director in 1994 — and as governor of the Central Bank of West African States. He is Muslim, from the country's north, a fact that later became politically contested under the "ivoirite" doctrine of belonging. {cite:wikipedia-alassane-ouattara}

## Career
President Felix Houphouet-Boigny made him Cote d'Ivoire's first prime minister in 1990; he served until 1993. Barred from the 1995 and 2000 elections by nationality-dispute measures aimed at excluding him, he finally ran in 2010 and was declared winner by the electoral commission; incumbent Laurent Gbagbo refused to yield, and a months-long crisis killed around three thousand people before Ouattara's forces, backed by UN and French troops, arrested Gbagbo in April 2011. {cite:wikipedia-alassane-ouattara}

## Presidency
Ouattara's governments oversaw rapid economic growth, infrastructure building and cocoa-sector reform, and he won re-election in 2015, 2020 — a third term made possible by a contested constitutional change — and again in October 2025. Supporters point to stability and growth; critics cite the 2011 violence, term-limit manoeuvring and uneven reconciliation. The museum records both attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-alassane-ouattara}
