## Summary
Abd al-Malik's brother and right hand, then sultan of Morocco styled al-Dhahabi {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 265-270}.

## In the battle
With the chamberlain [[person/ridwan-al-ilj|Ridwan]] he concealed his brother's death at Wadi al-Makhazin and led the vanguard into the Portuguese rear; he was acclaimed sultan on the field itself, 30 Jumada II 986 {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 265-267}.

## The state
He continued his brother's programme: ministers held to office hours, a cipher alphabet for secret letters, the judiciary separated from the executive with his own mazalim council at the Qasba mosque, guarded posts every twenty kilometres, the Diwan council, an Ottoman-model army with merit-based commands, field hospitals, a gun foundry, the Larache and Salé fleets, and expansion into the western Sudan {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 268-270}.

## Facing Uluj Ali
When kapudan [[person/kilic-ali-pasha|Uluj Ali]] reached Algiers in Jumada II 989/June 1581 to annex Morocco, al-Mansur camped his troops at the Tensift, sealed his kingdom's passes, fortified the shores and sent an embassy to Istanbul — after a near-agreement with Spain trading Larache and other concessions — and the Ottomans backed off {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 271}.

## The shuttle embassies
Embassies ran between Istanbul and Fez between 997/1588 and 999/1590 — among them those of Ahmad ibn Wada', al-Shazimi and Abu al-Hasan al-Tamakrouti — and he received an Ottoman ambassador in 998/1589; yet the alliance to recover al-Andalus never came to be, the Porte absorbed by the Safavid and Habsburg wars and its duty to the Hejaz {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 271}.
