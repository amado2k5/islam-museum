## Summary
Abu al-Harith Mansur ibn Nuh, Mansur II, was a Samanid emir, lord of Bukhara and Transoxiana ([[polity/samanid-dynasty|the Samanids]]). He succeeded his father Nuh ibn Mansur when Nuh died in Rajab 387 AH (997 CE), and was seized and blinded in 389 AH (999 CE) after one year and seven months {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.7 p.487} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.8 p.3}.
## Accession and fall
Ibn al-Athir writes that the emirs, commanders and people pledged themselves to Mansur, who shared out what was left of the treasury, and that Bektuzun ran the affairs of his realm; the Turkic ruler Ilek Khan marched on Samarqand at the news of Nuh's death, and Faiq al-Khassa took Bukhara {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.7 p.487}. In 389 AH, he says, Bektuzun came to Mansur at Sarakhs, did not find the honour he hoped for, and complained to Faiq. The two agreed to depose him and set up his brother 'Abd al-Malik, a small boy; they summoned him on the pretext of a council, seized him and Bektuzun had him blinded {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.8 p.3}. Mahmud ibn Sebuktigin then rebuked them and moved to take Khurasan, and Ibn al-Athir later adds that 'Abd al-Malik, the last Samanid ruler, was held at Uzgand and died there {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.8 p.3} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.8 p.5}.

Hasan Ibrahim gives the same course: after Nuh's death in 387 AH Mansur tried to win hearts by giving to his supporters and commanders, Faiq and Bektuzun seized and blinded him after a rule of one year and seven months, and [[person/mahmud-ghaznawi|Mahmud of Ghazna]], angered that Bektuzun had been put over Khurasan, used the disorder to seize Nishapur and Bukhara {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.3 printed p.87-88, scan91-92}.
