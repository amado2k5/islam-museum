## Summary
Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq (12 August 1924 – 17 August 1988) was a Pakistani general who ruled Pakistan from his coup of 5 July 1977 until his death in an airplane crash in 1988 — as chief martial law administrator to 1985 and as the sixth president of Pakistan from 1978 — making him Pakistan's longest-serving de facto ruler. His political ideology is known as Ziaism: domestically a programme of Islamisation, abroad a central role in the Soviet–Afghan War. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-ar}

## Early life and education
Zia was born on 12 August 1924 in Jalandhar, Punjab, into a family of the Punjabi Arain tribe. His father, Muhammad Akbar Ali, worked at Army General Headquarters in Delhi and was noted for his religiousness, which earned him the clerical title of maulvi; Zia and his six siblings were taught the Qur'an from an early age. He was educated at Shimla and at St. Stephen's College, Delhi, graduating with distinction in history in 1943, and was commissioned from the Indian Military Academy at Dehradun. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-ur}

## Military service
He saw action in the Second World War in Burma and Malaya and in Indonesia, opted for Pakistan in 1947, and fought as a tank commander in the 1965 war with India. From 1967 to 1970 he headed a Pakistani military training mission to Jordan and proved instrumental in defeating the Black September insurgency against King Hussein, who came to view him favourably. Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto appointed him chief of army staff on 1 March 1976, superseding seven more senior lieutenant-generals. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en}

## Coup and presidency
After the disputed 1977 election and the civil disorder that followed, Zia deposed Bhutto in a coup on 5 July 1977 and declared martial law. Bhutto was controversially tried by the Supreme Court and executed less than two years later for authorising the murder of a political opponent. Zia assumed the presidency on 16 September 1978 while remaining army chief. He lifted martial law in 1985 after non-party elections and appointed Muhammad Khan Junejo prime minister, but accumulated further presidential powers through the Eighth Amendment, and in 1988 he dismissed Junejo's government. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-ar}

## Islamisation and domestic policy
Zia passed broad-ranging legislation as part of Pakistan's Islamisation, curbed civil liberties and heightened press censorship. Traditional madrassas received state sponsorship for the first time under his administration, growing from 893 to 2,801; most were Deobandi in orientation and they were funded through Zakat councils. The English article records that "Zia favoured the Deobandi doctrine" — a statement about his government's patronage within its Islamisation programme, recorded here as policy, not as an attestation of his personal school. He also escalated Pakistan's atomic bomb project and instituted industrialisation and deregulation; averaged over his rule, GDP growth was the highest in the country's history. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en}

## The Afghan war and foreign policy
Backed by the United States and Saudi Arabia, Zia coordinated the Afghan mujahideen against the Soviet occupation throughout the 1980s, culminating in the Soviet withdrawal of 1989 — but also in millions of refugees and a proliferation of heroin and weapons into Pakistan's North-West Frontier Province. He bolstered ties with China and the United States and emphasised Pakistan's role in the Islamic world, while relations with India worsened amid the Siachen conflict and accusations over the Khalistan movement. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-ar}

## Death
Zia was killed on 17 August 1988, together with several of his top military officials and two American diplomats, in a still-unexplained plane crash near Bahawalpur. He was buried at the Faisal Mosque in Islamabad. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en}

## Perspectives and criticism
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum. Zia remains a polarising figure in Pakistan's history, credited with preventing wider Soviet incursions into the region and with an era of economic prosperity, but decried for weakening democratic institutions and passing laws encouraging religious intolerance. He is also credited with promoting the early political career of Nawaz Sharif, who would be elected prime minister three times. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zia-ul-haq-en}
