## Overview
Majd al-Din Muhammad ibn Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim ibn 'Umar al-Firuzabadi, the Shafi'i lexicographer-scholar, was born — per al-Sakhawi — in Rabi' 729 AH at Kazerun and raised there; he memorised the Qur'an at seven, moved at eight to Shiraz taking letters and language from his father and its scholars, went on to Iraq entering Wasit then Baghdad, where he studied under its qadi al-Sharaf Abd Allah ibn Baktash, teacher at the Nizamiyya, and held lectureships and endowed posts there. {cite:shadharat-dhahab v.9 p.185}

His merits multiplied and his students with them — al-Safadi, Ibn 'Aqil, al-Isnawi and Ibn Hisham all transmitted from him; he came to Cairo and studied with its scholars, travelled the eastern and Syrian lands, entered Anatolia and India, met many learned men and carried much from them, his mashikha recording the Six Books, the Sunan of al-Bayhaqi, the Musnad of Ahmad, the Sahih of Ibn Hibban and the Musannaf of Ibn Abi Shayba among countless shaykhs. {cite:shadharat-dhahab v.9 p.185}
