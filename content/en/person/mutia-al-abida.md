## Summary
Muti'a al-Abida is among the women ascetics of [[place/basra|Basra]] in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]] (no. 607), her reports coming from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.715}

## Forty years of weeping
From a Basran companion of Muhammad ibn al-Husayn: she wept for forty years, and when reproached for weeping so much she said, "I shall not stop weeping until I know which of the two states I am in before God." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.715}

## In the cemetery
From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn: they visited her in the cemetery (al-jabban) of Basra and began to speak with her of good things, but could not make out much of what she said for her weeping, so they left her; he asked her how long she had been there in the cemetery, and she wept and said, "My son, for fifty-four years." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.715}

## For young visitors
Muti'a was a woman of Basra who lived for many years near the graves, remembering the next life, and wept much from awe of God.
