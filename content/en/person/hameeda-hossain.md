## Summary
Hameeda Hossain is a Bangladeshi historian and human-rights figure, one of the founding pillars of the country's rights and women's movements: she co-founded the Dhaka political weekly Forum in 1969, then the Ain o Salish Kendra legal-aid and human-rights centre, and wrote reference works on Bengal's textile history and women's rights. {cite:wikipedia-hameeda-hossain}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1936 in Hyderabad, Sindh (now Pakistan), into a Muslim Sindhi family of judges and lawyers, educated in Karachi and Hyderabad, then went to the United States for a bachelor's in literature and history at Wellesley College, followed by a doctorate in history at Oxford — her thesis on the East India Company's weavers later became her reference book The Company Weavers of Bengal on textile history (1750–1813). {cite:wikipedia-hameeda-hossain}

## The magazine Forum
After marrying the constitutional lawyer [[person/kamal-hossain|Kamal Hossain]] and moving to Dhaka, she co-founded with economist Rehman Sobhan and her husband in 1969 the English weekly Forum, which became the loudest liberal voice in East Pakistan, publishing on economic inequality, democracy and Bengali rights until the army silenced it with its March 1971 operation. {cite:wikipedia-hameeda-hossain}

## Rights and women's work
After independence she devoted herself to rehabilitating survivors of wartime sexual violence, founded in 1974 the Karika cooperative of Bangladeshi handicrafts, co-founded the legal human-rights centre Ain o Salish Kendra (ASK) and the Sramik Nirapotta Forum for worker protection, joined the international women's network DAWN and the Asia Pacific Forum on Women, Law and Development, and participated in the 1975 UN Women's Conference in Mexico City and in writing Bangladesh's CEDAW shadow report. {cite:wikipedia-hameeda-hossain} {cite:muslim500-hameeda-hossain}

## Writing
She authored and edited books and articles on human rights and women's issues in Bangladesh, in Islam and worldwide, including contributions on women's rights in Islam and works on textile and craft history; her published thesis remains a university reference on Bengal's economic history. {cite:wikipedia-hameeda-hossain}

## Recognition and legacy
She received the Bangladesh Development Initiative (BDI) Lifetime Achievement Award in 2021 and national and regional honours. Her career reads as half a century of connected rights work: from an opposition magazine to legal aid for the poor to crafts employing survivors to a historical archive linking weaving to women — she is also the wife and institution-building partner of Bangladesh's constitutional lawyer Kamal Hossain. {cite:wikipedia-hameeda-hossain}
