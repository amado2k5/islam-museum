## Summary
Abu'l-Hasan 'Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn 'Antar ibn Thabit, styled Muhadhdhib al-Din and known as Shamim al-Hilli, was a fine man of letters, expert in grammar, language and the Arabs' poetry, and a good poet himself. He studied at Baghdad under Abu Muhammad Ibn al-Khashshab and his tier, then travelled to Diyar Bakr and Syria, praising the great and taking their gifts. He made a book he named *al-Lamisa*, arranged in ten chapters, rivalling Abu Tammam's *Hamasa*. He was rich in merits — except that his tongue was foul and he fell often upon people's reputations. He was called Shamim because he would smell and find no scent. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
