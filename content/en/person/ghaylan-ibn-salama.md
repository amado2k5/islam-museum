## Summary
Ghaylan ibn Salama al-Thaqafi was among the first generation to learn the Arabic script. He accompanied Bishr, Sufyan and Abu Qays when they came to Ta'if on trade and learned writing from them, as al-Baladhuri recounts in his chapter on the script. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 660}

## Details
In his account "on the script," al-Baladhuri relates that three men of Tayyi' met at Baqqa — Muramir ibn Murra, Aslam ibn Sidra and 'Amir ibn Jadara — who devised the script, measuring the Arabic alphabet against the Syriac; people of al-Anbar learned it from them, and the people of al-Hira learned it from al-Anbar {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 659}. Bishr ibn 'Abd al-Malik, brother of Akidar the lord of Dumat al-Jandal and a Christian, learned the Arabic script from the people of al-Hira; coming to Mecca he was seen writing by Sufyan ibn Umayya ibn 'Abd Shams and Abu Qays ibn Manaf ibn Zuhra, who asked him to teach them, so he taught them the alphabet and then showed them the script until they wrote {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 660}. Bishr, Sufyan and Abu Qays then came to Ta'if on a trading journey, where Ghaylan ibn Salama al-Thaqafi attached himself to them and learned the script from them {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 660}. Bishr parted from them and went to the land of Mudar, where 'Amr ibn Zurara ibn 'Udas learned the script from him and was thereafter called 'Amr the Scribe; Bishr then went on to Syria where others learned it from him {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 660}.
