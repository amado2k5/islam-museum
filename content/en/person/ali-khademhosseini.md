# Ali Khademhosseini

Ali Khademhosseini (born 30 October 1975) is an Iranian-born Canadian-American biomedical engineer whose work in biomaterials and tissue engineering has made him one of the most cited researchers in his field. Neither an Arabic nor an Urdu edition of his article exists; this file's companion Arabic and Urdu texts are written from the same sources. {cite:wikipedia-khademhosseini-en} {cite:wikipedia-khademhosseini-fa} {cite:wikidata-khademhosseini}

## Early life and education

Born in Tehran, he moved with his family to Canada in 1987 and grew up in Toronto. He took his bachelor's (1999) and master's (2001) degrees in chemical engineering at the University of Toronto, working in the laboratories of Michael Sefton and Peter Zandstra, and his doctorate in bioengineering at MIT in 2005 under Robert Langer. {cite:wikipedia-khademhosseini-en}

## Career

He was a faculty member at Harvard Medical School and the Wyss Institute from 2005 to 2017, then a professor at UCLA, where he directed the Center for Minimally Invasive Therapeutics, and afterwards chief executive of the Terasaki Institute for Biomedical Innovation in Los Angeles; he has since co-founded a company in AI and science. His roughly one thousand papers include foundational work on GelMA hydrogels, organ-on-a-chip platforms with integrated sensors, surgical sealants and smart wound patches. He co-founded Obsidio, whose embolic technology was acquired by Boston Scientific in 2022, and founded the cultivated-meat company Omeat. The English infobox lists the Mustafa Prize among his awards. {cite:wikipedia-khademhosseini-en} {cite:wikipedia-khademhosseini-fa}
