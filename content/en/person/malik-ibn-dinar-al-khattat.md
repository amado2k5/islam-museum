## Overview
Malik ibn Dinar, Abu Yahya al-Basri the copyist, was one of the trustworthy Followers and a noted copyist of Qur'an codices; al-Dhahabi called him "a beacon among the righteous scholars". Born in the days of Ibn 'Abbas, he heard from Anas ibn Malik, al-Ahnaf ibn Qays, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Hasan, Ibn Sirin and al-Qasim ibn Muhammad. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.3167}

## Transmission and standing
Ibn Sa'd described him as trustworthy, a man of few reports who wrote codices; al-Nasa'i also accredited him. He died in or after 127 AH. His aphorisms on renunciation and wariness of the world remain among the best-known teachings handed down from the ascetics of Basra. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.3167-3168}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
Malik ibn Dinar's notice in *Hilyat al-Awliya* (volume 2, no. 200) appears under the name "Abu Yahya Malik ibn Dinar" among the knowing and the God-fearing, and transmits his reports and sayings through Basran chains. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 2/357}

Among the sayings transmitted from him: "The people of this world have left this world and nothing of it has left them," and "The affluent are not comforted like the remembrance of God." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 2/358}

He is mentioned as saying in his supplication: "O God, do not admit the house of Malik ibn Dinar into the Fire," and as having read in certain books and wisdom-sayings discourse on God's oneness and fear of Him, with reports of his illness in which he died. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 2/361} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 2/370-381}

The volume's index describes his speech as "aphoristic discourse on the truthful and the bearers of knowledge." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 2/399}
