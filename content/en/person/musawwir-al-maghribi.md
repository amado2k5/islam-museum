## Summary
Musawwir al-Maghribi was a dweller in the wastes who would not yield. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 533, sequence 534, on page 162. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/162}

## The monk who had not spoken
Kurz ibn Anbasa relates that Musawwir ibn Labib al-Maghribi said: I stood before a monk who was said to have spoken to no one for forty years and had not come down from his cell. I kept at him until he came near, and I pressed him to speak; he refused, and I said: by the majesty of the One for whose sake you left speech, speak to me. He swayed a little, like one fainting, then woke as if startled and said: ask, and be brief. I asked how long he had been in this matter. He said: one day. I asked how that was. He said: I heard people saying, tomorrow, today and the day after; I looked at my own case and found that I had not been given what they were given; then I saw that yesterday had escaped me, today is mine, and tomorrow I do not know whether I shall reach it. Then he lowered his head. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/162}
