Akram Zuaiter (1909 – 11 April 1996) was a Palestinian activist, writer and diplomat from Nablus, educated at the American University of Beirut, who worked on the newspapers Mir'at al-Sharq and al-Hayat and taught at al-Najah school.{cite:wikipedia-akram-zuaiter-en}{cite:wikipedia-akram-zuaiter-ar} A founding member of the Istiqlal Party, he was twice detained by the British in the 1930s and took part in the 1941 Rashid Ali revolt in Iraq.{cite:wikipedia-akram-zuaiter-en}

After 1948 he served Jordan as ambassador to Syria, Iran, Afghanistan and Lebanon, as foreign minister in 1966, as a senator and as chief of the Royal Court.{cite:wikipedia-akram-zuaiter-en}{cite:wikipedia-akram-zuaiter-ar} His books, above all al-Qadiyya al-Filastiniyya (1956) and the published documents of the Palestinian national movement, made him one of its chief chroniclers; he died at Amman on 11 April 1996.{cite:wikipedia-akram-zuaiter-ar}{cite:wikidata-akram-zuaiter}
