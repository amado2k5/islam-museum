## Summary
Amr ibn Shu'ayb ibn Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Amr ibn al-As, a Successor who narrated from his father from his grandfather Abdallah ibn Amr ibn al-As; he died in 118 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.550}

## His standing in hadith
The authors of the four Sunans, Ibn Khuzayma, Ibn Hibban in some cases, and al-Hakim all used his hadith as evidence; Abu Dawud related from Ahmad that the hadith scholars cite Amr ibn Shu'ayb when they choose to argue from a narration. The charge against him was that part of his "from his father from his grandfather" transmission came from a found scroll rather than audition, and copied scrolls invite corruption; Ibn Hibban nonetheless held him trustworthy in himself and argued he belongs among the reliable narrators, while his father-from-grandfather strand is judged like other Successors' interrupted reports: the mursal and cut are set aside and the sound reports stand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.550}

## How to read his transmission
Al-Dhahabi wrote that his hadith is to be examined: what arrives anomalous is dropped and the rest is narrated in the Sunans and legal chapters with its chain graded; great imams used him and none, so far as he knew, abandoned him. Caution is needed only for material carried uniquely by weak transmitters from him — al-Muthanna ibn al-Sabbah, Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah al-Arzami, Hajjaj ibn Arta'a, Ibn Lahi'a, Ishaq ibn Abi Farwa — unlike the transmissions of Husayn al-Mu'allim, Sulayman ibn Musa the jurist, and Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.551}

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to Amr ibn Shu'ayb ibn Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As al-Sahmi, Abu Ibrahim according to the sound view (also said Abu Abd Allah), calling him one of the scholars of his time; his symbol "[عو]" means the authors of the four Sunan cite him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 263} He narrated from his father, [[person/tawus-ibn-kaysan|Tawus]], Sulayman ibn Yasar, the Companion al-Rubayyi' bint Mu'awwidh, his aunt Zaynab bint Muhammad and [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]]. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 263} From him narrated his peers Makhul, Ata' and [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]], then [[person/ayyub-al-sakhtiyani|Ayyub]], [[person/qatada-ibn-diama|Qatada]], al-Awza'i, [[person/ibn-ishaq|Ibn Ishaq]] and many others. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 263} He died at Ta'if in the year 118. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 268}

## The chain "from his father, from his grandfather"
The debate about Amr turns on his narrating "from his father, from his grandfather": from his father Shu'ayb, from his father's grandfather [[person/abdullah-ibn-amr-ibn-al-as|Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As]]. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 266} Abu Zur'a said that what was held against him was how much he narrated from his father from his grandfather: critics said he heard only a few hadiths and took a written sheet (*sahifa*) that he had and narrated from it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 264} Ibn Ma'in said: when he narrates from his father from his grandfather, it is a book, and that is where his weakness comes from; when he narrates from Sa'id, Sulayman ibn Yasar or Urwa, he is trustworthy. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 265} [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ibn al-Madini]] said: what Ayyub and Ibn Jurayj narrate from him is sound; what he narrates from his father from his grandfather is a book he found, and is weak. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 265} Ibn Adi said he is trustworthy in himself, but his narration from his father from his grandfather is *mursal* if the grandfather meant is Muhammad ibn Abd Allah, who was not a Companion. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 266} Ibn Hibban wavered: he listed him among the weak, saying his narration from trustworthy men other than his father is a proof while that from his father from his grandfather is broken or *mursal*, then said the right course is to move him to *al-Thiqat* and leave aside only his *mursal* and broken reports. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 267} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 268}

Al-Dhahabi rejects the charge of a broken chain: al-Bukhari, Abu Dawud and others stated that Shu'ayb heard from his grandfather Abd Allah, and al-Bukhari said so explicitly in his entry on Shu'ayb — "there is no doubt about this". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 266} He adds that Shu'ayb's father Muhammad died early — it is said in the lifetime of his own father Abd Allah, who then raised his grandson Shu'ayb — so the pronoun in "his grandfather" refers to Shu'ayb. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 266} He points out that Shu'ayb heard from Mu'awiya, who died some years before Abd Allah, so his hearing from his grandfather cannot be denied. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 267} What remains open for him is whether the narration was taken, wholly or partly, from a found written sheet. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 268} He reports that al-Mughira paid no attention to Abd Allah ibn Amr's *sahifa* and would not care to own it for two dates or two coppers. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 266}

## Between trust and softening
Ibn Ma'in, Ibn Rahawayh and Salih Jazara declared him trustworthy, and al-Awza'i said: I never saw a Qurashi more accomplished than Amr ibn Shu'ayb. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 263} Ibn Rahawayh said: Amr ibn Shu'ayb from his father from his grandfather is like Ayyub from Nafi' from Ibn Umar. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 264} Al-Tirmidhi reported [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] saying that he saw Ahmad, Ali, Ishaq and al-Humaydi use his hadith as proof — "so who are people after them!" — on which al-Dhahabi comments that al-Bukhari still did not use him as proof in his own Sahih. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 264} Asked about him, Ibn Ma'in grew angry and said: what am I to say of him? The imams have narrated from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 264}

On the other side, [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said that the hadith people relied on him when they wished and left him when they wished; he also said: he has objectionable reports, and we write his hadith for comparison, but as a proof, no; and: sometimes we rely on him and sometimes the heart misgives about him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 264} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 265} [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] was asked whether he is a proof, and said: no, not even half a proof. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 264} [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya al-Qattan]] said his hadith was flimsy to them, and also: when a trustworthy man narrates from him, he is a proof. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 265} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 266} Abu Zur'a said that most of the objectionable reports from him come through al-Muthanna ibn al-Sabbah and Ibn Lahi'a, and that he is trustworthy in himself. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 265} Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' is reported as saying that nothing could be held against Qatada and Amr ibn Shu'ayb except that they narrated everything they heard. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 265}

**Al-Dhahabi's verdict.** Al-Dhahabi says: we do not say his hadith is of the highest class of sound hadith; "rather it is of the class of *hasan*". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 268}
