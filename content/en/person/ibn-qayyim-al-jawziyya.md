## Overview
The shaykh Shams al-Din Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya, a Hanbali of Damascus, author of *I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in* (Informing the Signatories) on behalf of the Lord of the Worlds. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.518}

## Death and works
Haji Khalifa gives his death as 751 AH. The conversion to the Gregorian calendar is derived. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.518} Among his works is [[work/ilam-al-muwaqqiin|I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in]]. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.518}

He is counted among the teachers of [[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.11}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lectures on Sharh Kitab al-Fawa'id

The Sheikh explains that the nisba al-Jawziyya comes from a city of that name, and that Ibn al-Qayyim's father was its custodian (qayyim), which is why he was known as Ibn al-Qayyim (the Sheikh's explanation; needs_source: no source given in the lecture). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep01 01:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep01 01:04:00}

The Sheikh holds that the choice fell on Ibn al-Qayyim's book al-Fawa'id because of its influence on the souls of many readers, the Sheikh among them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep02 00:25:00}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy says that neglecting the obligations loses the voluntary acts, which are the cause of the servant's love for his Lord, and he attributes to Ibn al-Qayyim a saying on this (needs_source: the attribution is as the sheikh gives it) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:10:30}. He recounts from Ibn al-Qayyim that the road back to the Paradise from which humanity was expelled passes through the valley of those who pray at night (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:11:00}.

He recounts that Ibn al-Qayyim described his teacher Ibn Taymiyya as one who subdued his desire until desire no longer ruled him (needs_source: the description is paraphrased) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 01:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:39:00}.

He recounts that when Ibn al-Qayyim was asked how one can know that a speech is true, he answered that truth has a radiance of its own (needs_source: the answer as the sheikh gives it) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:47:00}.

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Ibn al-Qayyim — Ibn Taymiyya's greatest student — as a creator whose rare gift was the craft of masterful authorship, distinguishing his surname: 'qayyim of the Jawziyya' means overseer of the Jawziyya school his father supervised {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:04:00}.
Of his life: born 7 Safar 691 AH into a learned family, he kept to Ibn Taymiyya until counted his heir and the authority on the school after him; Ibn Hajar described him as 'bold-hearted, broad of wisdom, learned and versed in the scholars' disagreements' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:02:30}.
He used his sojourns at Mecca for authorship, saying of Miftah Dar al-Sa'ada it was 'among the gifts God opened upon me in my retreat to Him at His house'; and he composed on his journeys Zad al-Ma'ad, Rawdat al-Muhibbin, Bada'i' al-Fawa'id and Tahdhib Sunan Abi Dawud — so how much did he write while settled {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:07:30}.
He was an avid collector of books: over a hundred references stand behind Ijtima' al-Juyush al-Islamiyya, and some thirty titles behind Ahkam Ahl al-Dhimma {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:09:30}.
Of his stands: he gave fatwas on Ibn Taymiyya's positions — among them that a triple divorce uttered in one sitting counts as one, now the settled view (the speaker notes) — and was imprisoned for denouncing the saddling of mounts to the Friend's tomb, as his student Ibn Rajab reports {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:12:30}.
Of his character: Ibn Kathir described him 'fine of character, envying none, harming none, hating none'; and of his wisdom 'whoever wrongs you then comes apologising, humility obliges you to accept his excuse even if hollow, leaving his conscience to God' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:16:00}.
Of his worship: Ibn Rajab said 'he was given to worship, night prayer and the longest salah; devoted to remembrance, passionate for God's love — I never saw broader in knowledge nor more knowing of the meanings of Qur'an and Sunna; he is not infallible, but I have seen none like him'; imprisoned with Ibn Taymiyya in the citadel he occupied himself with recitation and contemplation and was released only after his shaykh's death {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:18:30}.
After the dawn prayer he sat remembering God until sunrise, saying 'this is my morning provision; were I to miss it my powers would fail'; and his maxim 'through patience and certainty is leadership in religion attained' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:19:30}.
He derives from him a method of authorship: mastery of the subject first (the speaker advises reading fifty to seventy books before writing), then teaching it before composing, then fine division and indexing — contrasting him with Ibn al-Jawzi: Ibn al-Qayyim the scholar of precise writing, Ibn al-Jawzi nearer the preacher {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:23:30}.
He closes: Ibn al-Qayyim follows Ibn Taymiyya in method and principles yet is distinctive in his subjects — and among God's greatest gifts to a scholar is a student who immortalises his views {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep15 00:24:30}.
