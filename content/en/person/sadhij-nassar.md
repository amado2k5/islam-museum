Sadhij Nassar (1900–1970) was a Palestinian journalist and activist from Haifa who worked with her husband Najib Nassar on the newspaper al-Karmil, which he had founded in 1908.{cite:wikipedia-sadhij-nassar-en}{cite:wikipedia-sadhij-nassar-ar} From the 1920s she helped edit and run the paper, in 1926 she founded its Women's Section, and she edited al-Karmil from 1941 until the Mandate authorities closed it in 1944.{cite:wikipedia-sadhij-nassar-en}

In 1930 Nassar co-founded the Arab Women's Union in Haifa, and in October 1938 she was elected secretary of the bureau of the Eastern Women's Conference for the Defence of Palestine in Cairo.{cite:wikipedia-sadhij-nassar-en} Arrested late in 1938 on a charge of supplying arms to Palestinian fighters, she was the first Palestinian woman taken as a political prisoner under the Mandate and was held for eleven months in Bethlehem.{cite:wikipedia-sadhij-nassar-ar}{cite:wikipedia-sadhij-nassar-en} After the Nakba of 1948 she lived in Lebanon and then in Damascus, writing for the press in both countries, and she died at Damascus in 1970.{cite:wikipedia-sadhij-nassar-ar}{cite:wikidata-sadhij-nassar}
