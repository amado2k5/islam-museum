## Overview
Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Qudama was the preacher of Jamma'il and the father of Shaykh [[person/abu-umar-al-maqdisi|Abu Umar]] and of his brother al-Muwaffaq Ibn Qudama. Ibn Rajab mentions him in the Dhayl Tabaqat al-Hanabila at the close of the entry for his son Abu Umar {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 61}.

## Migration to Damascus
[[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]] quotes al-Diya' al-Maqdisi, Abu Umar's nephew, that the father took Abu Umar, al-Muwaffaq and their family to Damascus in 551 AH because the Franks had taken the Holy Land. They stayed at the mosque of Abu Salih outside the Eastern Gate for about two years, and then moved up to the mountain {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52}. Ibn Rajab also quotes Abu al-Faraj ibn al-Hanbali that his own father lodged them in the Abu Salih mosque, which proved unhealthy, so about forty of them died in a single month, and he advised them to move to the mountain {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52}.

## Character
Ibn Rajab calls him righteous, ascetic and devout, a man of reported miracles, spiritual states and devotions, who completed the Qur'an sixty-five times in one Ramadan. He had great gravity, and everyone who saw him kissed his hand {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 61}. He quotes Abu al-Faraj ibn al-Hanbali that he had a leading place in worship and goodness, and reports an extravagant tribute to him from the father of Ibn al-Hanbali {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 61}. He narrated hadith, and his sons Abu Umar and al-Muwaffaq narrated from him {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 61}.

## Birth and death
He was born in 491 AH and died in 558 AH, and was buried at the foot of Mount Qasiyun, with his son Abu Umar buried beside him {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 61}.
