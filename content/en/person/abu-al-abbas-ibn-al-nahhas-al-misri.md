## Summary
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Isa ibn al-Jarrah, Abu al-Abbas ibn al-Nahhas al-Rab'i of Egypt, the hafiz, has an entry in [[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]]'s [[work/tarikh-dimashq|Tarikh Dimashq]] because he heard hadith there. In Egypt he heard Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Sulayman, Abu Bakr ibn Zabban, Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Badr ibn al-Naffah and others; in Damascus [[person/ibn-jawsa|Abu al-Hasan ibn Jawsa]] and Makhul of Beirut; and he heard [[person/abu-arubah-al-harrani|Abu Aruba al-Harrani]], [[person/abd-allah-ibn-abi-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Bakr ibn Abi Dawud]], [[person/ibn-abi-hatim-al-razi|Ibn Abi Hatim]], Abu al-Abbas al-Daghuli and others; he settled in [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]] and died there. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 434} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 435} [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|Al-Hakim]], [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym al-Isbahani]], [[person/al-sulami|Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami]], Abu Hazim al-Abdawi, Abu Uthman al-Bahiri and others narrated from him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 435}

## His travels, as al-Hakim describes them
Ibn Asakir quotes al-Hakim: he wrote down hadith in his own country, the Hijaz, Syria, the two Iraqs, Khuzistan, Isfahan and the Jibal; he came to Abu Nu'aym in [[place/jurjan|Jurjan]] in 319, went down from there to Juwayn, then met the shaykhs of Nishapur and went out to Sarakhs to write from al-Daghuli; his first hearing in his own country was in 305; he stayed for a time with Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hatim and heard much from him, but everything he had heard in Iraq, the Hijaz and Syria was lost. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 436} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 437} He taught in Nishapur for years by dictation and reading, living there from 321 until Saturday, the last day of Dhu al-Qa'da 376, and he told al-Hakim he was eighty-five. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 437}

## The affair of al-Saffar's notebooks
Al-Hakim relates that he heard the shaykh Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Saffar of Isfahan praying in his mosque with his palms raised: Lord, You know that Abu al-Abbas al-Misri wronged me, betrayed me and kept back more than five hundred fascicles of my originals — let him have no benefit from them and no blessing in them. He explains the cause: Abu al-Abbas, who was al-Saffar's copyist, told him that Abu al-Abbas al-Asamm would not hand over al-Saffar's record of hearing in al-Thawri's al-Jami' unless he paid five dinars; al-Saffar sold something from his house and paid, Ibn al-Nahhas took the money and brought the book, and when the two shaykhs learned the truth they prayed against him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 437} Al-Hakim says he then went to the jurist Abu Muhammad Abd Allah ibn Hamid until Abu Bakr al-Sawi the copyist was put in Ibn al-Nahhas's place, so that people could benefit from what remained of al-Saffar's books; he tells the story, he says, so that students take warning and do not treat shaykhs lightly, for Abu al-Abbas's standing in this craft had been of the highest, yet his learning was lost and his end was bad. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 438}

## Al-Hakim's final judgement
In another report al-Hakim says: Abu al-Abbas al-Misri was a hafiz of long-standing travel and great zeal for learning; when he was needed, after his early records had been lost, he narrated from memory hadiths he said he knew; in most of his learned exchanges he aimed at the truth, and when we saw his books after his death we found nothing but good. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 438}
