## Summary
Maulana Nazar-ur-Rehman is a Pakistani Islamic preacher who since November 2018 has served as the amir — the head — of the Tablighi Jamaat in Pakistan, the Raiwind-based preaching network described by its adherents and observers as the largest Muslim grassroots movement in the world. {cite:wikipedia-nazar-ur-rehman} {cite:muslim500-nazar-ur-rehman}

## Life and education
He was born in 1929 (some accounts give 1926) in the village of Bilawal in Rawalpindi district, Punjab. His father, Hafiz Ghulam Mohiuddin, was a devotee of Pir Meher Ali Shah of Golra Sharif. Nazar-ur-Rehman studied at local madrasas and completed his daura of hadith in 1949–50 under Maulana Sultan Mahmud, a student of Mahmud Hasan Deobandi, at Kathiala Sheikhan in Gujarat district — a lineage that places him inside the Deobandi scholarly tradition. {cite:wikipedia-nazar-ur-rehman}

## Career
After his studies he served as imam of mosques in Bilawal, Buigarh, Taxila and Rawalpindi over the following decades while devoting himself to Tablighi work. Within the Jamaat he became deputy amir, and when Haji Abdul-Wahhab — the third amir — died in November 2018 at the age of ninety-six, Nazar-ur-Rehman, one of the three leaders Abdul-Wahhab had designated as possible successors, assumed the leadership. {cite:wikipedia-nazar-ur-rehman} {cite:muslim500-nazar-ur-rehman}

## Standing
The Tablighi Jamaat organises small itinerant groups who visit communities to encourage religious practice; it describes itself as strictly apolitical and rejects violence. Its annual ijtemas at Raiwind draw attendances reported in the hundreds of thousands to millions, and the Bishwa Ijtema in Bangladesh, held under the movement's umbrella, is among the world's largest Muslim gatherings. The Muslim 500 ranked Nazar-ur-Rehman 41st in its 2027 edition, reflecting the scale of the institution he leads. {cite:muslim500-nazar-ur-rehman}
