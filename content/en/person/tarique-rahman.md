## Summary
Tarique Rahman is a Bangladeshi politician, acting chairman of the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP) since 2018 and — after nearly seventeen years of exile in London — the returning leader widely reported as the frontrunner, and then reported prime minister-designate, of Bangladesh's February 2026 transition. {cite:wikipedia-tarique-rahman}

## Life and formation
Born on 20 November 1967 in Dhaka, he is the eldest son of President Ziaur Rahman — assassinated in 1981 — and Khaleda Zia, who twice served as prime minister. He studied at Dhaka institutions, ran a textiles business, and rose inside the BNP, becoming its senior joint secretary general in the 2000s and the party's principal organizer while his mother governed. {cite:wikipedia-tarique-rahman}

## Detention, exile and leadership
During the 2007–2008 military-backed caretaker government he was arrested on corruption charges — he says he was tortured in custody — and left for London in 2008 for medical treatment, remaining in self-imposed exile. Convicted in absentia in several cases, including one linked to the 2004 Dhaka grenade attack, he was later acquitted following the August 2024 ouster of Sheikh Hasina's government; supporters call the prosecutions politically motivated, a characterisation the museum records attributed. From London he ran the BNP as acting chairman from 2018, coordinating its long opposition campaign. {cite:wikipedia-tarique-rahman}

## Return
After Hasina's fall, Rahman negotiated the terms of return with interim leader Muhammad Yunus and landed in Dhaka on 25 December 2025 before a mass reception, pledging safety for citizens of all faiths. With the BNP frontrunner for the 12 February 2026 election and his party projected to lead the next government, he has been widely described as prime minister-designate; this record reflects reporting available at writing. {cite:wikipedia-tarique-rahman}
