## Overview
Sultan Mehmed II, known as the Conqueror, the seventh of the Ottoman rulers, son of [[person/murad-ii-uthmani|Murad Khan]]. He was born in 835 AH, came to the sultanate in 856 AH, and his reign lasted thirty-one years. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

## Birth and the first sultanate
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, calls Mehmed II the ultimate founder of the Ottoman Empire. It gives his birth in [[place/edirne|Edirne]] on 27 Rajab 835/30 March 1432, as the fourth son of [[person/murad-ii-uthmani|Murad II]] by a slave girl of the harem. In the spring of 846/1443 he was sent to govern the province of [[place/amasya|Amasya]] with his two tutors, called lalas. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

In Rabi' II 848/July 1444, aged twelve, he was recalled and declared sultan by his father, who abdicated in his favour to secure the succession against Orkhan, an Ottoman pretender in Constantinople. During this first sultanate, which lasted until Jumada II 850/August 1448, the grand vizier [[person/candarli-halil-pasha|Candarli Khalil]] and the sultan's lalas Zaghanos and Ibrahim fought for power. The article says Candarli, of learned background, favoured peace and compromise abroad, while Zaghanos, of the military faction, favoured conquest, above all of Constantinople. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

The sultan's youth and the rivalry of the two factions left the state unable to meet the crises of 848/1444, among them an uprising in Edirne and a crusader army crossing the Danube. Vassal rulers in the Balkans began to recover their independence. When the crusaders besieged Varna, Candarli called Murad back with the Anatolian army, and the Ottoman victory at [[event/battle-varna-848|Varna]] left it unclear who was truly sultan. By secretly urging the [[group/janissaries|Janissaries]] to rise, Candarli returned Murad to the throne in fact on 9 Jumada I 850/2 August 1446, and Mehmed was sent to govern [[place/manisa|Manisa]] with his lalas. The article adds that his supporters still treated him as holding supreme power. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

Murad took his son on his major Balkan campaigns, against the Hungarians at [[event/battle-kosovo-ii-852|Kosovo]] in 852/1448 and against the Albanians in the summer of 854/1450, and married him to Sitti Khatun, daughter of the Dhu l-Qadrid ruler. Mehmed received news of his father's death soon after and acceded for the second time on 16 Muharram 855/18 February 1451. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

## Preparing the conquest of Constantinople
According to the article, the [[polity/byzantine-empire|Byzantine]] emperor tried to extract concessions by threatening to release Orkhan, and the Karamanid Ibrahim invaded disputed lands. Candarli met these threats with territorial concessions to Byzantium and Serbia, so that the young sultan could make a first campaign in Anatolia against the Karamanid. Mehmed then reached an agreement with the Karamanid and returned quickly to Edirne. Under the influence of Zaghanos, the article says, he decided to end the shrunken Byzantine state and began preparations by building the Boghaz-Kesen castle on the Bosphorus. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

Agreements with [[polity/republic-of-venice|Venice]] on 13 Sha'ban 855/10 September 1451 and with Hungary on 25 Shawwal 855/20 November 1451 secured peace in the west. The sultan also hired Urban, a Hungarian expert, to make the most powerful cannon yet seen. At the council in Edirne that took the decision, Candarli pointed to the strength of the walls and the danger of a crusade, while Zaghanos and Shihab al-Din stressed the permanent threat Byzantium posed to Ottoman unity. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

## The siege and its aftermath
The article says the siege depended on time and on the artillery, since a long siege would let Hungarian and Venetian forces come to the city's aid. Thanks to the bombardment, it lasted under two months, from 26 Rabi' I 857/6 April 1453 to 20 Jumada I 857/29 May 1453. Its most dramatic moment was the failure of the Ottoman navy to stop provision ships entering the Golden Horn. Defeatist rumours spread among the besiegers and Candarli advised lifting the siege, while Zaghanos and [[person/aq-shams-al-din|Ak Shams al-Din]], the sultan's spiritual mentor, supported him. Zaghanos prepared the final assault, and the city fell by storm on 20 Jumada I 857/29 May 1453. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

This date differs from the Shadharat al-dhahab account further down this page, which names Jumada al-Akhira. The page [[event/conquest-constantinople-857|Conquest of Constantinople]] treats the event itself.

The article says that, as the city had been taken by force, the consequences of such a conquest were bound to follow, and that Mehmed regretted the ruin of the imperial city, declared it his capital at once and made its building and repopulation a main concern of his reign. The Genoese podesta of Pera surrendered the same day. On 1 June 1453 Mehmed granted a charter guaranteeing safety and communal privileges to the local non-Muslims as [[concept/dhimma|dhimmi]] subjects, and trading privileges to the Genoese merchants. His first acts after victory were to arrest Candarli and to execute Prince Orkhan, who had fought on the walls against him; Zaghanos became grand vizier. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005}

## Claims to legitimacy
The article describes Mehmed as taking on unprecedented charisma and as claiming to be the sole holder of the sword of the ghazi in the Islamic world. Ghaza, it says, became the legitimising principle of the Ottoman sultan even against Muslim rivals, and the Turcoman rulers of Anatolia, [[person/uzun-hasan|Uzun Hasan]] of the Ak Koyunlu and the [[polity/mamluk-sultanate|Mamluk]] sultan were declared to be acting against Islam's true interests by hindering his ghaza. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1005} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006}

By calling himself Kayser, heir to the Roman Empire as holder of its capital, the article says, Mehmed believed he could set aside the Byzantine imperial families and claim all the former eastern Roman lands, including the Balkans, the south coast of Crimea and Italy. His capture of Otranto in 885/1480 was taken as a prelude to the conquest of Italy, and Rome, symbolised as the Golden Apple, stayed the ultimate goal of Ottoman ghaza ideology. To justify taking the Serbian despotate he cited inheritance through the marriages of earlier sultans to Serbian princesses. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006}

## Campaigns in Europe and the seas
The article judges that Mehmed's wars were driven by a plan to control the Straits, the Black Sea and the Aegean, the Balkans up to the Danube, central Anatolia and the lands west of the Euphrates, with his main interest in the west. In the fourteen years after 1453 he campaigned against the Serbian despot, the Greek despots and Latin lords in the Morea and central Greece, the Bosnian king and Iskender Beg in Albania. He was less successful beyond the Danube: Wallachia renewed its submission in 866/1462, but Moldavia under [[person/stephen-iii-moldavia|Stephen the Great]] resisted fiercely in 881/1476. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006}

These campaigns brought him into conflict with [[polity/kingdom-of-hungary|Hungary]] and Venice. The article names his defeat at Belgrade in 860/1456 and the alliance of Venice and Hungary in 867/1463, which led to the Long War against Venice, lasting from 867/1463 to 884/1479. Hostilities with Hungary lasted until his death. The war cost Venice Euboea in 875/1470 and Shkodra, and ended in a peace on 2 Dhu l-Qa'da 883/25 January 1479 under which Venice paid an annual tribute of 10,000 gold pieces. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006}

On the Black Sea he took Amasra in 863/1459, Kaffa and Azak in 880/1475, and Anapa and Copa in 884/1479. With the conquest of the Greek empire of [[place/trebizond|Trebizond]] and the occupation of the Isfendiyarid principality in 865/1461, the article says, he made the Black Sea an Ottoman lake. His navy in 879/1474 had 92 galleys, 5 galliots, 59 horse transports and small boats, but he felt it could not match Venetian sea power. In 885/1480 the fleet of Gallipoli attacked [[place/rhodes|Rhodes]], where the siege failed, and the fleet of Vlore took [[place/otranto|Otranto]] on 4 Jumada II 885/11 August 1480. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006}

## Anatolia, the Ak Koyunlu and the Mamluks
Having kept Hungary and Venice quiet with peace talks in 873-8/1468-73, Mehmed turned to central Anatolia, where Karamanid dynastic quarrels let the rising Ak Koyunlu extend their influence. The article calls the rivalry with Uzun Hasan an extremely dangerous crisis, since he and Venice tried to unite the small states of the Levant in an anti-Ottoman coalition. Mehmed's victory at Bash-kent on 16 Rabi' I 878/11 August 1473 was a turning point and confirmed the annexation of Karamanid territory, though subduing the Turcoman tribes of the Taurus took six years more. It also brought open rivalry with the Mamluks, who claimed a protectorate over the Karamanids. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006}

The article reports a statement of the contemporary chronicler [[person/tursun-beg|Tursun Beg]] that his last campaign, under way when he died on 4 Rabi' I 886/3 May 1481, was aimed at the Mamluks rather than at the Hospitallers of Rhodes. This differs from the accounts given below, which have him setting out against Italy. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006}

## Centralised state and law
The article calls Mehmed II the true founder of the classical Ottoman system, one who organised the lands between the Danube and the Euphrates as a centralised domain that stayed the core of the empire for four centuries. By fortifying the Dardanelles and the Bosphorus he held a compact empire and took the titles sultan of the two continents and khakan of the two seas. He reduced or brought under central control the local landed families of the Balkans and Anatolia, who either became regular timar holders or were replaced by the sultan's slave-soldiers. His "land reform" of 883/1478 turned many freehold and endowed lands of such hereditary landlords into state lands. By emerging as the greatest ghazi, it adds, he also reduced the autonomy of the frontier lords. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1006} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1007}

For his bureaucracy he employed experts of many origins, Persians, Arabs, Greeks, Jews, Italians and Ottoman Turks. The scholars played a major role as before, the article says, but were only part of the machinery and could not interfere with the sultan's autocratic conduct of state affairs. It describes him as apparently the first Ottoman, perhaps the first Muslim ruler, to codify state law on the ruler's own law-making power, perhaps inspired by Turko-Mongol tradition. His two law codes dealt with state organisation, penal law and the relations between the state, the military class and the taxpaying subjects; the latter defined strictly what could be demanded of the subjects, and the codes remained the basis of Ottoman law until the 11th/17th century. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1007}

## Learning, religion and character
With the guidance of the astronomer [[person/ali-qushji|Ali Qushji]], Mehmed organised religious teaching and the ranks of the scholars. The article ties his achievement to his unprecedented charisma as Abu l-Fath, to talented leaders from East and West, to a rationalistic and practical education under the jurist [[person/molla-khosrow|Molla Khosrew]], to his faith in God's support and in the inspiration of his shaykh Ak Shams al-Din, and to his combining of Islamic and Roman imperial traditions. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1007}

He restored the Greek Orthodox Church with its traditional privileges as part of the imperial system in Muharram 858/January 1454, while banning Catholic organisations under the papacy from his lands. The article says the native urban population and the Orthodox Church do not seem to have resented the sultan, whom they called basileus or czar. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1007}

## Peasants, economy and interpretations
The article says the peasant masses, burdened by local landlords, probably welcomed a strong central power as protection; edicts claimed to defend the poor against the powerful, and the land surveys and codes aimed to keep peasant families on small holdings. It reads the abrogation of exploitative endowments and freeholds as more than a military reform, since it extended state ownership and protection over the producer. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1007}

Urban economy was regulated through bazaars, covered markets, weighing stations, customs and market rules, and through periodic reissue of silver coin with the old coin banned, which the article likens to taxing all cash held by individuals. Rice-growing lands became state property with labour under close state control. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1007}

The article reports that Mehmed held the ruler's organising of society to rest on his duty of ghaza, making Islam rule the world, and on his duty as imam to guide his subjects, whom his edicts call a trust from God. It also records the Marxist reading, held by the scholars it names, that these ideological claims served the exploitation of the direct producers by the Ottoman state and ruling classes. The museum reports both readings and gives no verdict. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1007}

## The conquest
Ibn al-Imad says that he besieged Constantinople for fifty days until he took it on Wednesday, 20 Jumada al-Akhira 857 AH. He prayed the Friday prayer in Hagia Sophia. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

Ibn al-Imad describes him as one of the greatest of the Ottoman sultans in holy war and the boldest of them. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

## Learning
He founded madrasas with eight doors in [[place/istanbul|Istanbul]], gave the students what met their needs and grades to rise through, and brought in great scholars, among them Ali al-Qushji and al-Kurani. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

He was among the pupils of [[person/aq-shams-al-din|Aq Shams al-Din]], and his son Abu Yazid succeeded him. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi}

He died in 886 AH, 1481 CE. The Gregorian date is from al-Salabi. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi}

## Accession and preparation
In al-Salabi: he took the throne on 16 Muharram 855 / 18 February 1451 at twenty-two, reorganised the administration, finances, army corps and soldiers' rolls, and prepared near a quarter-million fighters and over four hundred ships for the conquest. Among his teachers was [[person/ahmad-ibn-ismail-al-kurani|Mawla al-Kurani]], who in his boyhood beat him until he finished the Qur'an {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 87-92}.

## The end
Al-Salabi records that he died on Thursday 4 Rabi' I 886 / 3 May 1481 amid his army at Üsküdar, after more than thirty years of rule, aged fifty-two {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 157}.
## In al-Sakhawi's al-Daw' al-Lami'
[[person/al-sakhawi|Al-Sakhawi]] gives an entry for Muhammad son of Murad Bak son of Muhammad Bak son of Bayezid, lord of Rum, the son of Uthman: he succeeded his father in 855, made Constantinople his capital after conquering it, kept up the war against the Franks, sought out scholars and sent gifts to al-Kafiyaji, and built many madrasas, lodges and mosques. He died early in 886 on his way from Istanbul towards Bursa and was later moved to a tomb near his great mosque. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 10/47}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the last six episodes, 24 to 29, of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]] to Mehmed the Conqueror and the conquest of Constantinople, presenting it as a story of glory after a run of stories of collapse, one in which, he says, every element of victory came together {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:05:30}. What follows is his account.

### Upbringing
Al-Suwaidan introduces him as Sultan Mehmed Khan II son of Murad II, the seventh Ottoman sultan, called the Conqueror after he took Constantinople; he gives his birth as 833 AH and says he was the second of his brothers, preceded by a brother who fell in battle {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:06:00}.

He relates that his father [[person/murad-ii-uthmani|Murad II]] raised all his sons to be leaders who would carry the banner after him and entrusted Mehmed to tutors and scholars, and that the boy at first leaned towards play and luxury and would not respond to his teachers {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:07:00}. Murad asked for a teacher who could master him and was directed to [[person/ahmad-ibn-ismail-al-kurani|Ahmad ibn Isma'il al-Kurani]]; he handed him a rod and gave him leave to beat the boy if he disobeyed. When the prince laughed at this, al-Kurani beat him hard in that very sitting, after which he applied himself, completed the Qur'an in a short time, and read history, the branches of fiqh and other subjects with him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:08:30}. Al-Suwaidan says that as a boy he mastered three languages: Turkish, his mother tongue, Persian and Arabic {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:08:30}.

He recounts that Murad pushed him into posts of command from the age of twelve, making him governor of the province of Manisa, and then abdicated the whole sultanate to him when he was fourteen and withdrew into seclusion for worship and reflection, while still following the news closely {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:10:00}. He says Murad came out of retirement twice: once when Europe declared war on the Ottomans because the sultan was so young, when he led the Muslims to a crushing victory and then went back into seclusion, and once when the [[group/janissaries|Janissaries]] rebelled against the weak young sultan and he came out and disciplined them {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:11:00}. Al-Suwaidan's lesson is that forming a leader is not left to chance but needs planning, and that early experience of command is what makes leaders {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:11:30}.

He gives the greatest share in Mehmed's formation to the shaykh [[person/aq-shams-al-din|Aq Shams al-Din]], who from the age of seven planted in him the conviction that he was the commander meant by the Prophet's hadith that foretells the conquest of Constantinople and praises its commander and army, a hadith al-Suwaidan calls authentic and well known,, so that the first thing he did on taking power was to prepare the conquest {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:16:30}.

### His character, as al-Suwaidan describes it
He describes him as religious and God-fearing, fond of worship and retreat, honouring scholars, fluent in Arabic, one who rooted out corruption and enjoined right and forbade wrong, and who had studied mathematics and astronomy {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:01:30}. He relates that after the conquest Mehmed wished to set aside a period for worship away from government and asked his shaykh Aq Shams al-Din, who talked him out of it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:08:00}.

### The Conqueror at the siege
Al-Suwaidan stresses that he oversaw the preparations in person, inspecting the troops, the walls and the fortress and supervising the casting and testing of the cannon {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:12:00}. At the siege he led the army himself: he addressed his troops, watched a sea battle from the shore, rebuked his fleet commander and then rewarded and dismissed him, putting Hamza Pasha in his place, devised the plan to haul the ships overland into the Golden Horn, and personally led the last wave of Janissaries {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:11:00}. When the emperor answered that he would either keep his throne or be buried beneath his city's walls, Mehmed replied, in al-Suwaidan's telling, that soon he would have in Constantinople either a throne or a grave {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:03:00}.

He relates that Mehmed rode into the city on his horse while his commanders congratulated him, praising God and praying for mercy on the fallen; that he told his men they had become the conquerors of Constantinople foretold in the hadith; that he forbade killing and plunder and ordered gentleness and kindness towards the people; and that he then dismounted, faced the qibla and prostrated in thanks {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:17:00}. The siege and the conquest are told in detail on the page [[event/conquest-constantinople-857|Conquest of Constantinople]].

### After Constantinople
Al-Suwaidan says campaigning filled the rest of Mehmed's life: after Constantinople, which he counts the greatest of his conquests, he took southern Greece, northern Romania and Bosnia, attempted Italy and Rome, and died while setting out on that campaign {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:10:30}.
