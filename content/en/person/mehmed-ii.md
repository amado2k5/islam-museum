## Overview
Sultan Mehmed II, known as the Conqueror, the seventh of the Ottoman rulers, son of [[person/murad-ii-uthmani|Murad Khan]]. He was born in 835 AH, came to the sultanate in 856 AH, and his reign lasted thirty-one years. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

## The conquest
Ibn al-Imad says that he besieged Constantinople for fifty days until he took it on Wednesday, 20 Jumada al-Akhira 857 AH. He prayed the Friday prayer in Hagia Sophia. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

Ibn al-Imad describes him as one of the greatest of the Ottoman sultans in holy war and the boldest of them. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

## Learning
He founded madrasas with eight doors in [[place/istanbul|Istanbul]], gave the students what met their needs and grades to rise through, and brought in great scholars, among them Ali al-Qushji and al-Kurani. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

He was among the pupils of [[person/aq-shams-al-din|Aq Shams al-Din]], and his son Abu Yazid succeeded him. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi}

He died in 886 AH (1481 CE). The Gregorian date is from al-Salabi. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi}

## Accession and preparation
In al-Salabi: he took the throne on 16 Muharram 855 / 18 February 1451 at twenty-two, reorganised the administration, finances, army corps and soldiers' rolls, and prepared near a quarter-million fighters and over four hundred ships for the conquest. Among his teachers was [[person/ahmad-ibn-ismail-al-kurani|Mawla al-Kurani]], who in his boyhood beat him until he finished the Qur'an {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 87-92}.

## The end
Al-Salabi records that he died on Thursday 4 Rabi' I 886 / 3 May 1481 amid his army at Üsküdar, after more than thirty years of rule, aged fifty-two {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 157}.
## In al-Sakhawi's al-Daw' al-Lami'
[[person/al-sakhawi|Al-Sakhawi]] gives an entry for Muhammad son of Murad Bak son of Muhammad Bak son of Bayezid, lord of Rum, the son of Uthman: he succeeded his father in 855, made Constantinople his capital after conquering it, kept up the war against the Franks, sought out scholars and sent gifts to al-Kafiyaji, and built many madrasas, lodges and mosques. He died early in 886 on his way from Istanbul towards Bursa and was later moved to a tomb near his great mosque. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 10/47}
