## Birth and upbringing
Al-Hasan ibn 'Umar ibn al-Hasan ibn Habib, Badr al-Din Abu Muhammad wa-Abu Tahir, known as Ibn Habib al-Halabi, was a Shafi'i historian and chancery stylist. Born at Damascus in Sha'ban 710, he moved to Aleppo when his father was appointed its muhtasib, grew up there and took his nisba from the city {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/29} {cite:al-aalam 2/218-219}.

## Studies and travels
He heard from Ibrahim ibn Muslim, from his father 'Umar and from al-Fakhr ibn Khatib Birin, and heard at al-Hilla, Egypt and Alexandria; he travelled to Egypt and the Hijaz, moved about Syria and settled at Aleppo {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/29} {cite:al-aalam 2/218-219}. His entry records that he was at Tripoli in 758 {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/30}.

## Offices and standing
He served as deputy qadi and deputy secretary of the privy chancery, countersigned for the qadis, and in old age was dismissed {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/30}. Ibn Taghribirdi calls him the imam of his age in the crafts of insha' (official letter-writing) and shurut (notarial formulation), active in royal and religious correspondence {cite:nujum-zahira 11/189}.

## Works
His Durrat al-Aslak fi Dawlat al-Atrak recorded the annals of the Mamluk state from 648 to about 777, continued by his son Tahir; he also wrote Tadhkirat al-Nabih on Baybars and his sons, al-Najm al-Thaqib on the Prophet's biography, Nasim al-Saba, Juhaynat al-Akhbar on the names of caliphs and kings, and Kashf al-Marrut on Shafi'i fiqh {cite:al-aalam 2/218-219} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/29-30} {cite:nujum-zahira 11/189}.

## Pupils and death
Among those who transmitted from him were Abu Hamid Ibn Zahira, Ibn Qadi Shuhba, Ibn Zumayra, Sibt Ibn al-Jum'a and 'Ala' al-Din Ibn Khatib al-Nasiriyya {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/29-30}. He died at Aleppo in Rabi' II 779, aged about seventy {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/30} {cite:nujum-zahira 11/189} {cite:al-aalam 2/218-219}.
