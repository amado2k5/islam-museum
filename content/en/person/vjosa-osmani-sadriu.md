## Summary
Vjosa Osmani-Sadriu is the president of Kosovo, elected in April 2021 as the country's second woman head of state. A constitutional lawyer who first entered politics as an adviser to President Fatmir Sejdiu, she became the assembly's speaker before leading a reform mandate with Prime Minister Albin Kurti's Vetëvendosje movement. {cite:wikipedia-vjosa-osmani-sadriu}

## Life and formation
She was born on 17 May 1982 in Mitrovica, then in Yugoslavia, and grew up through the Kosovo war of 1998–99 — an experience she has cited as decisive for her public path. Educated at the University of Pristina and the University of Pittsburgh, where she earned master's and doctoral degrees in law, she taught constitutional law and published on international law and statehood. {cite:wikipedia-vjosa-osmani-sadriu}

## Political career
A member of the Democratic League of Kosovo (LDK), she served as chief of staff to President Sejdiu, entered parliament in 2010 and chaired its committee on European integration and later the foreign affairs committee. In 2019 she became the first woman speaker of the Kosovo assembly. Breaking with the LDK over the presidency question, she ran on a joint list with Vetëvendosje in the February 2021 election that swept the reformists to power. {cite:wikipedia-vjosa-osmani-sadriu}

## Presidency
On 4 April 2021 parliament elected her president for a five-year term. In office she has pressed Kosovo's Euro-Atlantic agenda — NATO and EU aspirations, the dialogue with Serbia — and has been a visible advocate internationally for recognition of Kosovo and for accountability for wartime crimes. {cite:wikipedia-vjosa-osmani-sadriu}

## Assessments
Supporters see her presidency as the face of a younger, anti-corruption Kosovo with a credible legal profile abroad; opponents in the older parties describe her rise as opportunistic and her rhetoric as sharper than the constitutional role allows.
