# Ali Hassan Mwinyi

Ali Hassan Mwinyi was a Tanzanian politician who served as the second president of Tanzania from 5 November 1985 to 23 November 1995, succeeding Julius Nyerere; before that he was president of Zanzibar and first vice-president of Tanzania. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en}

## Early life and education

Mwinyi was born on 8 May 1925 in the village of Kivure, Pwani Region, where he was also raised. He then moved to Zanzibar, where he was educated at Mangapwani Primary School and Mikindani Dole Secondary School, and from 1945 to 1964 he worked successively as a tutor, teacher and head teacher at various schools; on his return from England in 1962 he was appointed principal of the Zanzibar Teaching Training College, before deciding to enter national politics. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en}

## Presidency

Mwinyi became president of Zanzibar on 30 January 1984 and first vice-president of Tanzania the same day, then succeeded Nyerere as president on 5 November 1985, with the economy in a slump, goods scarce and foreign debt above three billion dollars. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en} He relaxed import restrictions and encouraged private enterprise, and in 1986 he made an agreement with the IMF for a $78 million standby loan, Tanzania's first foreign loan in over six years. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en} In his second term the transition towards multi-party politics began: in 1991 he appointed Chief Justice Francis Nyalali to lead a commission gauging support for the single-party system, supported its recommendation, and constitutional amendments were ratified in February 1992. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en} He was often referred to as Mzee Rukhsa, "everything goes", for pushing the liberalisation of the economy, morals, beliefs and values without breaking the law. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en}

## Honours and death

Mwinyi received the King Faisal Prize of Saudi Arabia for Service to Islam in 2022. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en} {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-ar} He was hospitalised for a chest illness in November 2023 and died of lung cancer at Mzena hospital in Dar es Salaam on 29 February 2024, at the age of 98. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en}

## Note on the record

No source names a religious school for Mwinyi: Wikidata gives his religion as Islam with no branch, a single structured value. The English text never calls him a Muslim; its religion content is the King Faisal Prize for Service to Islam — an honour, not an affiliation — and a category filing (Tanzanian Muslims), and the Urdu article makes no religion statement. {cite:wikidata-ali-hassan-mwinyi} {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en} {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-ar} {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-ur} His faith is therefore recorded as unknown.

Neither his predecessor Julius Nyerere nor his successor Benjamin Mkapa has a record in the museum, so no succession relation is written; the relation written is his life in Zanzibar, where he was educated, worked and later served as president. {cite:wikipedia-ali-hassan-mwinyi-en}
