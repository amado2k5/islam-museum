## Summary
Abu Kamil Shuja ibn Aslam was a mathematician among the Arabic authors of the tenth century CE, mentioned twice in the introduction of the editors of the 1939 Cairo edition of [[person/al-khwarizmi|al-Khwarizmi]]'s [[work/al-jabr-wal-muqabala|Book of al-Jabr and al-Muqabala]]. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.17} {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.24}

## His link with al-Khwarizmi
The editors place him "around 925 CE" among later authors who openly acknowledge al-Khwarizmi as one of their authorities, and take this as evidence of al-Khwarizmi's leading place in algebra. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.24} They quote Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddima* to the effect that the first to write on algebra was al-Khwarizmi, followed by Abu Kamil ibn Aslam. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.25}

## His problems
The editors note that an algebraic work of Abu Kamil contains problems of the kind Diophantus treated on rational right triangles, those whose sides are rational numbers, in which the sum of the area and one or two sides is known; they refer to Suter's study of his treatise on the pentagon and decagon in *Bibliotheca Mathematica*. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.17}

## Note
Abu Kamil's own books were not read here; this page draws on the introduction to the Cairo edition of the Algebra.
