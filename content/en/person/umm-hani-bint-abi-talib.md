## Summary
[[person/umm-hani-bint-abi-talib|Umm Hani]] bint [[person/abu-talib|Abi Talib]] ibn Abd al-Muttalib, [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]]'s cousin; it is said her name was Fakhita, or Fatima, or Hind — the first is best known {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2042}.

## Her marriage
She was wife of Hubayra ibn Amr al-Makhzumi {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2042}. Ibn al-Kalbi relates from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet asked Abu Talib for her hand, and Hubayra also asked, so Abu Talib married her to him — then Islam parted them {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2043}. In Ibn Sa'd's account, with a sound chain from al-Sha'bi, she made excuse when the Prophet asked for her, fearing she would fail a husband's rights; in another account she said: "I am a woman with small children, and I would not have them harm you" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2043}. In al-Suddi's account she was not of the emigrant women and so was not lawful to him — in that connection [[passage/quran-33-50|al-Ahzab 50]] is cited {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2043}.

## The conquest day
Abu Umar says Hubayra fled to Najran when Mecca was conquered, and spoke a poem apologising for his flight; when it reached him that Umm Hani had become Muslim he spoke verses on her {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2043}. She had Amr by him — by whom she took her kunya — Hubayra and others {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2043}.

## Narration
Umm Hani related hadiths from the Prophet in the Six Books and others; from her related her son Ja'da, her grandson Harun, her two clients Abu Murra and Abu Salih, her cousin [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Abdullah ibn Abbas]] and others {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2043}. Al-Tirmidhi and others mention that she outlived Ali {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2043}.
