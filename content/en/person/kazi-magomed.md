# Kazi-Magomed

Kazi-Magomed (1833–1902), whose name is also written Gazi-Muhammad, was the second son of Imam Shamil, named for his father's teacher and predecessor, the first Imam Ghazi Muhammad {cite:wikipedia-kazi-magomed-ru}. No encyclopaedia article on him exists in English; this entry is written from the Russian article and its Ukrainian and Turkish translations.

## Naib and defender of the Imamate

Born at Gimry in 1833, he was proclaimed his father's heir as a small child and confirmed in that designation at the Vedeno congress of January 1848 {cite:wikipedia-kazi-magomed-ru}. From 1850 he was naib of Karata; in the Crimean War he raided Kakheti with his father-in-law Daniyal-bek, and the Ottoman sultan made him a pasha in 1855 {cite:wikipedia-kazi-magomed-ru}. In 1859 he directed the defence of Vedeno and fought at the Andi Koysu before capitulating with his father at Gunib {cite:wikipedia-kazi-magomed-ru}.

## Exile and Ottoman service

He followed Shamil into exile at Kaluga, took the oath of Russian allegiance with him in 1866, and in 1871 travelled to Medina for his father's burial {cite:wikipedia-kazi-magomed-ru}. From Istanbul he entered Ottoman service instead of returning to Russia, commanded a cavalry brigade of Caucasian muhajirs in the war of 1877–1878, and after the war was sent in honourable exile to Medina with the rank of mushir; he died there in 1902 {cite:wikipedia-kazi-magomed-ru} {cite:wikidata-kazi-magomed}.
