## Summary

Muhammad Sa'id Ramadan al-Bouti (1929–2013) was a Sunni Ash'ari scholar of Kurdish origin, former dean of Damascus's Faculty of Sharia and among the most published and publicly known scholars of modern Syria. He was assassinated in a bombing at al-Iman Mosque in Damascus in March 2013.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-bouti}

## Life and formation

Born on Butan island near Cizre, Turkey, to a Kurdish scholarly family, he migrated as a child to Damascus, studied at al-Azhar and Damascus's Faculty of Sharia, took his doctorate from al-Azhar, then taught and became dean of the Faculty of Sharia at Damascus University.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-bouti}

## Works and method

His books include Kubra al-Yaqiniyyat al-Kawniyya, Fiqh al-Sira, Dawabit al-Maslaha fi al-Shari'a al-Islamiyya and Al-La Madhhabiyya. His method joined Ash'ari creed and traditional fiqh to responses against both Wahhabism and secularism, and his television programme and Umayyad Mosque lessons made him a public religious face.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-bouti}

## Controversy and death

His political positions stirred debate: he backed the Syrian authorities at major junctures and supported the government's discourse early in the 2011 crisis, dividing supporters and opponents — the museum records both without a verdict. He was killed by a bombing during his lesson at al-Iman Mosque on 21 March 2013.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-bouti}
