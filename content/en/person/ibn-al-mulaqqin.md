## Summary
Siraj al-Din Abu Hafs 'Umar ibn 'Ali ibn Ahmad al-Ansari, Andalusi by origin then Cairene — known as Ibn al-Mulaqqin — was among Egypt's hafizes and muhaddiths of the eighth Hijri century. Born at Cairo on Saturday 14 Rabi' I 723, he died on 6 Rabi' I 804 {cite:shadharat-dhahab 7/44-45}.
## Upbringing
His father Nur al-Din left al-Andalus for the land of the Turks, taught its people the Qur'an and prospered, then settled at Cairo and died while the boy was one year old. His mother's new husband — Sharaf al-Din 'Isa al-Maghribi, the Qur'an repetition-teacher of the Tulunid mosque — raised him, gave him his famous name, and taught him the Qur'an then the 'Umda {cite:shadharat-dhahab 7/44} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 dhayl p. 121}.
## Study and teaching
He heard Abu l-Fath Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, al-Mashtuli, Ibn al-Is'ardi and the circle of Ibn 'Abd al-Da'im, and graduated in hadith under Zayn al-Din al-Rahbi and 'Ala' al-Din Mughaltay. In 777 he travelled to Damascus, heard the last pupils of al-Fakhr Ibn al-Bukhari, gave fatwas and taught; Taj al-Din al-Subki wrote a commendation on his Sharh al-Minhaj {cite:shadharat-dhahab 7/44} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 dhayl pp. 121-122}.
## Ordeal and authorship
In 780 he was tried in the Barquq–Baraka affair, jailed in the stable and released through the intercession of Akmal al-Din al-Babarti and Siraj al-Din al-Bulqini, never entering the judicial deputyship again. He stayed home and composed near three hundred works; most burned shortly before his death, among them his twenty-volume Sharh on al-Bukhari {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 dhayl pp. 122-123}.
## Death
His state changed at the end and his son Nur al-Din 'Ali confined him until he died on 6 Rabi' I 804 at Cairo, buried beside his father in the hawsh of the Sufis outside Bab al-Nasr {cite:shadharat-dhahab 7/45}.
