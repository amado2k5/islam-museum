## Overview
Abu Umama al-Bahili was Sudayy ibn 'Ajlan ibn Wahb, known chiefly by his kunya. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says he knows of no disagreement over his name; the disagreement is over his lineage back to Bahila — that is, Malik ibn Ya'sur ibn Sa'd ibn Qays 'Aylan ibn Mudar — with one ancestor added or dropped, and some place him among Banu Sahm of Bahila; but no one disputes that he was of Bahila. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 348, 772}

## Transmission
He was one of those who narrated much from the Prophet, and most of his hadith is with the Syrians. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 772} A number of Successors narrated from him, among them Sulaym ibn 'Amir al-Khaba'iri, al-Qasim Abu 'Abd al-Rahman, Abu Ghalib Hazawwar, Shurahbil ibn Muslim and Muhammad ibn Ziyad. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 348}

## In the conquest of Syria
Al-Baladhuri records him as a commander of raiding parties in Syria: when [[person/yazid-ibn-abi-sufyan|Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan]] learned of a Byzantine force at al-'Araba in Palestine, he sent Abu Umama, who fell upon them and killed their leader. In Abu Mikhnaf's version, six Byzantine commanders encamped at al-'Araba with three thousand men; Abu Umama marched on them with a body of Muslims, routed them and killed one of the commanders, then pursued them to al-Dabiya, routed them again and took good spoils. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.151} Elders of Syria said the encounter at al-'Araba was the Muslims' first; see [[event/battle-of-al-araba|the Battle of al-'Araba]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.152}

## Residence and death
He lived in Egypt, then moved to [[place/homs|Hims]], settled there and died there. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 772} He died in 81 AH aged ninety-one; others say he died in 86. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 348, 772}

Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna said Abu Umama al-Bahili was the last of the Prophet's Companions left in Syria; Ibn 'Abd al-Barr comments that 'Abd Allah ibn Busr outlived him there and was the last Companion to die in Syria. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 348} In the Book of Kunyas, however, he calls Abu Umama himself the last Companion to die in Syria. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 772}
