## Overview
Baldwin IV, called the leper king, ruled the Frankish kingdom of Jerusalem in the years when [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din]] was gathering Syria under his rule. The Encyclopaedia of Islam has no article on him; Ibn al-Athir, the Muslim chronicler, never gives his name and speaks only of the "king of the Franks" who was a leper. This record therefore rests on two passages of Ibn al-Athir's chronicle and two passing mentions in the Encyclopaedia, and the identification of the chronicle's leper king with Baldwin IV is the museum's own, based on the dates and the title. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 68} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141}

## Succession
Ibn al-Athir, writing of Amalric, "king of the Franks" at the turn of the year, says he was the greatest of their kings in courage, judgement and cunning, and that he left a leper son unable to manage the kingdom. The Franks made him king in name only, and Raymond, count of Tripoli, took charge of the kingdom's affairs. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 68} The same chronicler adds that Raymond had been ransomed from Nur al-Din's prison and that the Franks gathered around him on his return. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 68} The event is recorded separately in [[event/death-of-amalric-569|the death of Amalric]].

## The leper king's death and the nephew
When Ibn al-Athir comes to the year 582/1186, he explains that the king of the Franks in Syria, who was a leper, had left the kingdom to a young nephew, his sister's son, and that [[person/raymond-iii-tripoli|Raymond of Tripoli]] looked after the boy and ran the kingdom, hoping for the crown himself. When the boy died the kingdom passed to his mother, which ended Raymond's hopes. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141} Her marriage to Guy is told under [[person/sibylla|Sibylla]] and [[person/guy-de-lusignan|Guy de Lusignan]]. The chronicler counts the resulting quarrel among the Franks as one of the greatest reasons the Muslims later recovered their lands. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 142}

## Jacob's Ford
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, in its article on the ford and bridge of Jacob, says that Baldwin IV built in 573/1178 a fortress entrusted to the Templars, the Castellet of the ford of Jacob, whose ruins still remain. Saladin took and destroyed it a year later, in 575/1179. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 582} The article on Saladin gives the same sequence from the Muslim side: the defeat of "King Baldwin", without a numeral, at Marj Uyun in Muharram 575/June 1179, and the destruction of Bayt al-Ahzan, or Jacob's Castle, in Rabi' I/August of that year. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 977} The battle is recorded separately in [[event/battle-of-jacobs-ford-1179|Jacob's Ford]].

## What is not known
No source read for this record gives his birth, his regnal years or his death, his parentage by name, or the name of his successor. Ibn al-Athir says only that a child nephew followed him. The museum has not read William of Tyre or other Frankish sources, and makes no claim about the leper king beyond what the passages above say.
