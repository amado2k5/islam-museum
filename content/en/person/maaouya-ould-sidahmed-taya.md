# Maaouya Ould Sid'Ahmed Taya

Maaouya Ould Sid'Ahmed Taya (born 28 November 1941) is a Mauritanian military officer and politician who was President of Mauritania from 1984 to 2005, the longest-serving holder of the office. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en} {cite:wikidata-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya}

## Rise

Taya was born at Atar, studied at a Franco-Arab school and then at a French military school, and fought in the Western Sahara War. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en} He became Chief of Army Staff in July 1980 and prime minister in April 1981 under President Mohamed Khouna Ould Haidalla, and on 12 December 1984 he took power in a bloodless military coup. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en}

## Presidency

Taya held the premiership alongside the presidency until 1992. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en} He pursued a strongly Arab nationalist line, established close co-operation with Saddam Hussein's Iraq, and won the 2003 elections amid opposition allegations of fraud; on 28 October 1999 Mauritania joined Egypt, Palestine and Jordan as the fourth Arab League member to officially recognise Israel. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en} The last years of his rule were marked by unrest in the military and hostility between the regime and the country's Islamists. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en}

## Overthrow and exile

While Taya was out of the country for the funeral of King Fahd of Saudi Arabia, soldiers seized power on 3 August 2005. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en} He left Niger for Banjul in the Gambia on 9 August and flew with his family to Qatar, arriving on 22 August 2005; the Arabic article likewise gives his residence as Doha. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en} {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-ar} In late 2013 he was appointed a teacher at the Ahmed Bin Mohammad Military School, a military academy in Qatar. {cite:wikipedia-maaouya-ould-sidahmed-taya-en}
