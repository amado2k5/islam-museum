## Summary

Raja Haji Fisabilillah (1727 – 18 June 1784), full name Raja Haji Fisabilillah ibni Daeng Chelak, was a Bugis-Malay warrior prince who served as the fourth Yang di-Pertuan Muda — the viceroy — of Riau from 1777 to 1784 {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. The Malay chronicle Tuhfat al-Nafis remembers him as a great hero {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}.

## Origins and kingmaking

He was born at Ulusungai in Riau in 1727, a son of the Bugis warrior Daeng Chelak and a younger brother of Raja Lumu, the future Sultan Salehuddin Shah of Selangor {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. From 1760 he worked to reassemble the fragmented Johor world: he coerced the rulers of Jambi and Indragiri and married their daughters, forced Sultan Mahmud II of Perak to give his niece in marriage to Raja Lumu in 1766, defeated Siak, and in 1771 acted as kingmaker by installing Syarif Abdurrahman Alkadrie as the first sultan of Pontianak {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. The Dutch feared his strength early — the former Dutch governor of Malacca, Thomas Schippers, was already planning against his bands in 1773 {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}.

## Viceroy of Riau

When his uncle Daeng Kemboja died around December 1777, the Bendahara Tun Abdul Majid appointed Raja Haji the fourth Yang di-Pertuan Muda of Riau, and the Dutch governor of Malacca acknowledged the appointment by letter on 28 December 1777 {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}.

## The Riau War and his death

Fearing a Bugis descent on Riau, the Dutch invaded in 1784 and opened the Riau War; after a three-month siege they withdrew without victory, only to find Malacca itself attacked by the combined Selangor and Bugis forces {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. Raja Haji led a series of raids on A Famosa, the Dutch fortress at Malacca, and was on the verge of victory — his men had surrounded the Dutch in one of the forts — when a shot from a distance killed him instantly, on 18 June 1784 at Teluk Ketapang {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. His nephew Raja Ibrahim was angered at how the war had drawn Selangor in {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}.

According to Munshi Abdullah, the Dutch recovered his body and were rumoured to have buried it in a pig farm; only after the English conquered Malacca did the royal family obtain its return, and he was given a proper Muslim burial at Bukit Kursi on Penyengat Island, in the reign of his son Raja Ja'far, the sixth Yang di-Pertuan Muda {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. His posthumous style was Marhum Teluk Ketapang, "the late one of Teluk Ketapang" {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-ms}. His grandson Raja Ali Haji became a renowned historian, poet and scholar {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}.

## Legacy

Indonesia posthumously proclaimed him a National Hero on 11 August 1997, and the airport of Tanjungpinang in the Riau Islands was renamed Raja Haji Fisabilillah Airport in 2008 {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. A mosque bearing his name was completed at Cyberjaya in Selangor on 24 February 2015, and on 20 September 2024 the Indonesian Navy patrol vessel KRI Raja Haji Fisabilillah was named after him {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}.

## Faith

No source read names a legal school or an order for Raja Haji himself. He was a prince of the Islamised Bugis-Malay world of Riau and Johor; his style Fisabilillah means "in the path of God", and after his body was recovered from its first burial his family had him reburied with a proper Muslim burial on Penyengat Island {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-en}. The Malay article's infobox files him as Sunni Islam {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-ms} and Wikidata carries no religion value {cite:wikidata-raja-haji-fisabilillah}. A name style, a Muslim burial and the Malay world's Shafi'i tradition are context, not a naming of the man's school, so the museum records his faith as unknown.

## Notes for researchers

His birth year is split: the English article and Wikidata give 1727, while the Indonesian article gives 1725 {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-id} {cite:wikidata-raja-haji-fisabilillah}; his birthplace is likewise Ulusungai in the English article, Kota Lama at Ulusungai in the Indonesian, and Linggi in the Malay article's infobox {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-ms}. The Malay article's infobox corrupts his reign and death to 1787 against its own lead, which agrees on 1784 {cite:wikipedia-raja-haji-fisabilillah-ms}. Wikidata's place-of-death value, Penyengat Island, is his burial island rather than the place he fell {cite:wikidata-raja-haji-fisabilillah}.
