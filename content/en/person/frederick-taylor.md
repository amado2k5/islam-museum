## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Frederick Taylor as the 'father of scientific management': an engineer who helped Henry Ford improve the assembly line, then in 1911 published what is counted as the first scientific study of modern management {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:28:00}.

## His theory
Al-Suwaidan explains that Taylor began with organising, moved on to planning, and set out the division of the four administrative operations: planning, organising, directing and controlling {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:29:00}.
His philosophy treats the worker as a machine that needs fuel to work, its fuel being money — what later became known as Theory X {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:30:00}.
Al-Suwaidan notes that many of those who developed management science were not specialists in it at all: Taylor was an engineer {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:27:30}.
