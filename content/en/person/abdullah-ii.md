## Summary
Abdullah II bin Al-Hussein is the king of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, having acceded to the throne on 7 February 1999. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-ar} He is a member of the Hashemites, Jordan's reigning royal family since 1921, is traditionally regarded as a 41st-generation direct descendant of the Prophet Muhammad, and is the longest-serving current Arab leader. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

The king is known for promoting interfaith dialogue and a moderate understanding of Islam, and he is custodian of the Muslim and Christian religious sites in Jerusalem, a position held by his dynasty since 1924. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-ar} The article describes him as a constitutional monarch heading a regime with wide executive and legislative powers, who liberalized the economy on taking the throne and responded to the unrest of 2011 by replacing the government and introducing reforms, while critics quoted in the article view the reforms as cosmetic and power as remaining centralized in the king's hands. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

## Early life and education
Abdullah was born on 30 January 1962 at Palestine Hospital in Al Abdali, Amman, to King Hussein and his British-born second wife Princess Muna Al-Hussein, and was named after his paternal great-grandfather Abdullah I, who founded modern Jordan. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} {cite:wikidata-abdullah-ii} His dynasty, the Hashemites, ruled Mecca for over 700 years from the 10th century until 1925 and have ruled Jordan since 1921; they are the oldest ruling dynasty in the Muslim world. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

As Hussein's eldest son he became heir apparent under the 1952 constitution, until political instability led Hussein in 1965 to appoint an adult heir instead, his uncle Prince Hassan. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} He began his schooling in 1966 at the Islamic Educational College in Amman, continued at St Edmund's School in England, and attended middle school at Eaglebrook School and high school at Deerfield Academy in the United States. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-ar}

## Military career
He began his military career in 1980 as a training officer in the Jordanian Armed Forces, attended the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst in England, and was commissioned as a second lieutenant in the British Army, serving a year in Britain and West Germany as a troop commander in the 13th/18th Royal Hussars. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

He was admitted to Pembroke College, Oxford, in 1982 for a one-year special-studies course in Middle Eastern affairs, joined the Royal Jordanian Army on his return, and in 1987 undertook advanced study in international affairs at Georgetown University's School of Foreign Service in Washington, D.C. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-ar} In 1994 he assumed command of Jordan's Special Forces and other elite units as a brigadier general, restructuring them into the Joint Special Operations Command two years later, and became a major general in 1998. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

## Accession
He joined his father on missions abroad, occasionally acted as his regent during the 1990s, led his father's delegation to Moscow in 1987, and hand-delivered a message to Muammar Gaddafi in 1998. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} After King Hussein's return from a six-month medical absence, Hussein publicly criticised his brother Hassan's management as regent, and on 24 January 1999, two weeks before his death, replaced Hassan with Abdullah as heir apparent. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

King Hussein died on 7 February 1999; hours later Abdullah appeared at an emergency session of parliament and swore the oath his father had taken almost fifty years earlier, and his investiture took place on 9 June 1999. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-ar}

## Reign and reforms
The king maintained his father's moderate pro-Western policy and support for the 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty, and in the early years of his reign reportedly went undercover to government institutions to see Jordan's challenges firsthand. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} In November 1999 he cracked down on the Hamas presence in Jordan, exiling four of its officials to Qatar and closing its Amman offices. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

He opposed the American intervention in Iraq, warning Vice President Dick Cheney in 2002 that a strike on Iraq would be disastrous, and tried in March 2003 to dissuade President George W. Bush from invading; failing that, he allowed American Patriot batteries in the Jordanian desert but did not allow the invasion to be launched from Jordan. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

The first parliamentary election of his reign was held in 2003 after a postponement criticised by the Islamic Action Front, and before it the king decreed an amendment giving women a six-seat quota in Parliament. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} In 2004 he coined the term "Shia Crescent" for a Shia-dominated region from Damascus to Tehran, later clarifying that he meant a shift in political, not sectarian, alignment. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

On 9 November 2005 Al-Qaeda in Iraq claimed responsibility for suicide bombings of three Amman hotels that killed 60 people and injured 115, the deadliest attack in Jordan's history; security was tightened, and according to the article no major terrorist attacks have been reported in the country since. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

When Arab Spring protests reached Jordan in 2011, Abdullah sacked Samir Rifai's government and pledged a democratic trajectory; a fuel-subsidy cut in November 2012 triggered large protests, and the regime responded with reforms including amending about a third of the constitution and establishing a Constitutional Court and an Independent Election Commission, while the 2016 election reintroduced a form of proportional representation and was considered fair and transparent by independent international observers. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

Jordan under his reign received waves of refugees: some 800,000 Iraqis reported in 2007, then more than a million Syrians, of whom the king said in January 2016 that Jordan was at the "boiling point". {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} After ISIL captured and burned to death the Jordanian pilot Muath Al-Kasasbeh in early 2015, the king ratified death sentences for two imprisoned Iraqi jihadists and Jordanian airstrikes on ISIL targets followed, drawing wide domestic support. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

In April 2021 he ordered the arrest of his half-brother Prince Hamzah and twenty other figures for what was called "sedition", announcing days later that the sedition was buried and Hamzah was "in his palace under my protection". {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

Economically, the king pursued aggressive liberalization and special economic zones and negotiated a free-trade agreement with the United States under which Jordanian exports there rose from $63 million in 2000 to over $1.4 billion in 2015, while Jordan's foreign debt reached $35.1 billion in 2016, 95 percent of GDP, a rise the article attributes to regional challenges. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

## Religious affairs and interfaith dialogue
In response to Islamophobic rhetoric after the 11 September attacks, Abdullah issued the Amman Message in November 2004, a detailed statement encouraging Muslim scholars of all sects to denounce terrorism, practice religious tolerance and represent the true nature of the Muslim faith; it was adopted unanimously at a conference he hosted in Amman in 2005 by 200 leading Islamic scholars, and stressed the validity of all eight schools of Islam, the forbidding of takfir and standards for issuing fatwas. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} He presented the Message to the United Nations General Assembly in 2010, where he proposed a World Interfaith Harmony Week, which was adopted and is celebrated annually in the first week of February. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

The Al-Aqsa compound has been under Hashemite custodianship since 1924; in 2013 an agreement with the Palestinian Authority confirmed the custodianship, in 2016 he funded the restoration of the Tomb of Jesus in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, and in 2017 he donated $1.4 million to the Jerusalem Islamic Waqf, while an independent report estimates the Hashemites' spending on administering and renovating Al-Aqsa since 1924 at over $1 billion. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} He led The 500 Most Influential Muslims' 2016 list, published by the Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Center, and was third on its 2018 list. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported by the sources, not as a verdict of the museum.

A March 2013 interview with The Atlantic sparked controversy when the king called the Muslim Brotherhood a "Masonic cult" and "wolves in sheep's clothing", described ousted Egyptian president Mohammad Morsi as a man with "no depth", and said Turkish prime minister Recep Tayyip Erdogan saw democracy as "a bus ride". {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

The 2021 Pandora Papers revealed, according to the article, Abdullah's hidden wealth through offshore entities, including an international property network valued in excess of $100 million held through British Virgin Islands companies; his lawyers denied any misuse of public funds or tax evasion, stating the properties were bought from his private wealth and held offshore for security and privacy, and a Royal Court statement after the 2022 Credit Suisse leak said the funds in one account resulted from selling his father's Airbus A340. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

In April 2021 the king described the affair of Prince Hamzah as a "sedition" that caused him "pain and anger", before declaring it over. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

## Writings
He published an autobiography, Our Last Best Chance: The Pursuit of Peace in a Time of Peril, in 2010, documenting the first decade of his rule, his childhood and behind-the-scenes accounts of encounters with political figures. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

## Personal life
He met Rania Al-Yassin at a dinner in January 1993 and they married on 10 June 1993; they have four children: Crown Prince Hussein, Princess Iman, Princess Salma and Prince Hashem. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} In 2004 he removed the crown prince title from his half-brother Prince Hamzah, and conferred it on his son Hussein on 2 July 2009. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} The museum does not publish family detail beyond what understanding his public life requires.

The article lists skydiving, motorcycling, water sports and collecting ancient weapons among his interests, notes his brief non-speaking appearance in a 1996 Star Trek: Voyager episode, and his work promoting Jordanian tourism as a television tour guide. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en}

## Impact and audience
The sources describe him as the longest-serving current Arab leader and a central figure in promoting interfaith dialogue and a moderate understanding of Islam; the citation for his 2018 Templeton Prize said he "has done more to seek religious harmony within Islam and between Islam and other religions than any other living political leader", as quoted in the article. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-ii-ar}
