## Overview
Uqba ibn Abi Muayt was a Qurashi whom the sources record as causing the Prophet specific, physical harm. He was one of two prisoners executed after Badr. The report itself records this fact without softening it.

## Life
The sources link Uqba to the most shameful scenes of the early Meccan years: he threw the entrails of a slaughtered camel on the Prophet while he was prostrating, and he strangled him with his garment at the Kaaba until Abu Bakr came. {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He was taken prisoner at Badr and executed on the way back. He was one of two prisoners singled out for execution, the other being [[person/al-nadr-ibn-al-harith|al-Nadr ibn al-Harith]], and the sources explain this by his record of harm. {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:watt-1953-mecca}.

## Legacy
The exhibit presents him dryly, as the sources do: a man who was repaid, after the battle, in kind for his documented acts. He is a difficult showcase: war, captivity and revenge in the first generation.

## For young visitors
Uqba harmed the Prophet many times among the people. After the first great battle, he was one of only two prisoners who were not pardoned.

## On the enmity towards the Prophet (The Sealed Nectar pp.88, 100)
He was among the most hostile people towards the Prophet. He threw the entrails of a camel on him while he was prostrating {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.88} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/543}, and he strangled him with his garment in the Hijr of the Kaaba until [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] pushed him away. ([[event/uqba-strangling-of-the-prophet|the strangling of the Prophet in the Hijr]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.100} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/544}.

## For Sprouts
Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, a Qurashi who harmed the Prophet in public again and again; after the first great battle he was one of only two captives not spared.
