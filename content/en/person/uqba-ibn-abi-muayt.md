## Brief
Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt was a Qurashi whom the sources record doing specific, physical harm to the Prophet — and one of two captives executed after Badr, a fact the tradition itself records without decoration.

## Life
The sources tie Uqba to the ugliest small scenes of the Meccan years: throwing the offal of a slaughtered camel on the Prophet as he prostrated, and throttling him with his cloak by the Kaaba until Abu Bakr intervened {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

Captured at Badr, he was put to death on the return march — one of two captives (the other, [[person/al-nadr-ibn-al-harith|al-Nadr ibn al-Harith]]) singled out for execution, which the sources justify by his record of harassment {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:watt-1953-mecca}.

## Legacy
The museum records him plainly, as the sources do: a man whose documented acts were paid back, after the battle, in kind. His case is a hard display case — war, captivity and revenge in the first generation.

## For young visitors
Uqba hurt the Prophet in public, again and again. After the first big battle he was one of only two prisoners who were not spared.
