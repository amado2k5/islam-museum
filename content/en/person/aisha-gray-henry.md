## Summary
Aisha Gray Henry is an American publisher and author who founded the non-profit press Fons Vitae, dedicated to English translations of Islamic spiritual and scholarly classics; The Muslim 500 lists her among influential figures in arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Fons Vitae
Founded in the 1990s, Fons Vitae ⁦(Latin for 'fountain of life')⁩ publishes scholarly English translations of foundational Islamic texts — most prominently al-Ghazali's Ihya Ulum al-Din in a multi-volume translation and Ibn Arabi's Fusus al-Hikam — used in universities and study circles worldwide. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Children's education
She authored and compiled books on the spiritual education of Muslim children, and directs the Ghazali Children's Project, which adapts the Ihya's teachings into illustrated books and curricula used by Muslim schools and families. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Method
The press works with academic translators and traditional scholars, pairing critical editions with accessible presentation; its catalogue has made key works of Islamic spirituality available to English readers outside specialist scholarship. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Assessment
She is credited with a publishing mission that joined academic rigour to devotional purpose, widening access to the Islamic intellectual tradition in the West; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}


## Fons Vitae publishing
Under her direction Fons Vitae has published more than a hundred translations and monographs — the Ghazali children's series, works of al-Ghazali, Ibn Arabi and others — making classical texts available in English for families and classrooms. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Ghazali Children's Project
The project's illustrated retellings of the Ihya for children — with workbooks, teacher manuals and school curricula — are used in Islamic schools across several countries and represent the press's most influential programme. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Institution building
She has served on the boards of Islamic educational and scholarly organisations and speaks regularly at conferences on Islamic publishing, schooling and interfaith education. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry} {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Origins
Born in Louisville, Kentucky, she embraced Islam as a young woman and pursued religious study before founding Fons Vitae in the 1990s — a press devoted to bringing Islamic classics into English for a new generation. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## The press's vision
Fons Vitae's editorial project — scholarly translations, spiritual classics and children's literature — built one of the most substantial English-language libraries of traditional Islamic texts outside academia. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}

## Standing
Within English-language Islamic publishing she is credited with a rare combination: building a press that served both scholars and families, and placing classical scholarship into Muslim homes that could not read the originals. {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry} {cite:muslim500-aisha-gray-henry}
