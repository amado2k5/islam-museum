## Summary
Taqiyya bint Ghayth ibn Ali al-Armanazi al-Suriyya, in the entry of [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']], was an accomplished poet of fine verse, born in Safar 505 AH (or, it is said, Muharram), originally from [[place/sur-lubnan|Tyre]]; her qasidas and short pieces formed a small diwan. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.178}

## With al-Silafi
She kept company for a time with the hafiz [[person/al-silafi|Abu Tahir Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Silafi al-Isfahani]] in the port of [[place/alexandria|Alexandria]], and he mentioned her in some of his notes with praise. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.178}

## Two poems for al-Malik al-Muzaffar
[[person/ibn-khallikan|Ibn Khallikan]] relates that the hafiz [[person/abd-al-azim-al-mundhiri|Zaki al-Din Abd al-Azim al-Mundhiri]] told him that Taqiyya composed a poem in praise of [[person/taqi-al-din-umar|al-Malik al-Muzaffar Taqi al-Din Umar]], nephew of Sultan Salah al-Din; it was a wine poem describing the paraphernalia of the drinking party, and he remarked, "The old lady knows these things from her youth." She then composed another poem describing war superbly and sent it to him, saying her knowledge of the one was like her knowledge of the other — meaning to clear herself of what had been imputed to her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.178}

## The lines on the veil
It is also related that she once stumbled and cut herself, and a slave girl of the house tore a strip from her own head-veil to bind the wound; Taqiyya composed two lines saying that, had she found a way, she would have given her own cheek in place of the girl's veil. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.178} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.179}

## Her death
She died at Alexandria early in Shawwal 579 AH; Kahhala draws her entry from Ibn Khallikan, Shadharat al-Dhahab, Mir'at al-Jinan, al-I'lam bi-Wafayat al-A'lam and al-Zirikli's al-A'lam. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.179}

## For young visitors
Taqiyya was a poet from the city of Tyre who studied with a great scholar in Alexandria and could write poems on any subject — even describing battles.
