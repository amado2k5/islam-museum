## Overview
Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Ahmad al-Siddiqi, Abu al-Hasan al-Bakri: an Egyptian exegete and Sufi of the Shafi'i school, born and deceased in Cairo between 899 and 952 AH (1493–1545 CE). He spent alternate years in Egypt and Mecca; he is said to have been the first of Egypt's scholars to make the pilgrimage in a mahfa litter, whereupon others followed him, and his fame spread through the lands despite his youth. {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.57}

His works include Tashil al-Sabil, a Qur'an commentary known as Tafsir al-Bakri; a commentary on al-Mujaddid's al-Ibab in fiqh; a commentary on al-Nawawi's Minhaj; and Tuhfat Wahib al-Mawahib on the Sufi stations and degrees. {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.57}
