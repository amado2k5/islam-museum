## Overview
Abd al-Salam ibn Mashish ibn Abi Bakr (Mansur) ibn Ali (or Ibrahim) al-Idrisi al-Hasani, Abu Muhammad, is described by al-Zirikli as a Moroccan ascetic {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 354}. He became famous for a text of his called «al-Salat al-Mashishiyya» (the Mashishi prayer), on which many have written commentaries; one of the commentaries is printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 354}.

## Birth and killing
He was born on Jabal al-Alam, by the port of Tetouan, and was killed there; al-Zirikli calls him a martyr {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 354}. He was killed by a group sent by a man called Ibn Abi al-Tawajin al-Kutami, whom al-Zirikli describes as a sorcerer who claimed prophecy, and he was buried at the summit of the mountain {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 354}. The entry gives the year of his death as 622 AH, 1225 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 354}.

## Writing about him
Al-Zirikli notes that Abu Muhammad Abd Allah ibn Muhammad al-Warraq (the entry itself adds a question mark after the name) wrote a treatise on the virtues of Ibn Mashish, a manuscript in the Rabat library {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 354}.
