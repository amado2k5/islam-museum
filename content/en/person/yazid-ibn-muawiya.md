## Overview
Yazid ibn Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan, the second caliph of the Umayyads. His mother was [[person/maysun-bint-bahdal|Maysun bint Bahdal]]. He was the son of [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] of the [[group/banu-umayya|Banu Umayya]] {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715}.

## In al-Baladhuri's Futuh al-Buldan
al-Baladhuri reports that when Mu'awiya died and his son Yazid succeeded, the army of Cyprus that his father had arranged closed its gate, and Yazid ordered their city demolished. Muhammad ibn Musfa transmits that he was bribed with great wealth to close it, and the people of Cyprus demolished their city and their mosques. He was also the one who imposed the land tax on the lands of Samaria in Jordan, at five dinars per head {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}. See [[event/second-raid-on-cyprus|the second raid on Cyprus]].

## In al-Mas'udi's Muruj al-Dhahab
al-Mas'udi reports that after his father's death he entered his house and did not appear to the people for three days. Then he came out, preached, and said that Mu'awiya had been a rope of God's ropes. He describes him as a man of mirth, of hawks, dogs and monkeys, and says that singing appeared in his days in Mecca and Medina {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 63-66}.

al-Mas'udi reports that when the people of Medina expelled his governor in the year 63 AH, he sent against them Muslim ibn Uqba al-Murri, who terrified and plundered it. He reports that the Ka'ba was demolished by the catapults of al-Husayn ibn Numayr eleven days before his death {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 66-69}.

These are al-Mas'udi's judgements and reports, and they need other sources for comparison.

## His officials in Khalifa ibn Khayyat
[[person/khalifa-ibn-khayyat|Khalifa ibn Khayyat]] closes his account of this reign by naming its governors and office-holders. They are given here in his order; lines the editor restored from later authors are marked as such. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 256}

- **Judgeship of Basra:** Abd al-Rahman ibn Udhayna al-Abdi (until the civil strife broke out) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 256}
- **Judgeship of Kufa:** [[person/shurayh-al-qadi|Shurayh]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 256}
- **Judgeship of Medina:** Abd Allah ibn Uthman al-Taymi (appointed by Amr ibn Sa'id) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 256}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"

[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] treats the pledge for Yazid in the episodes on "the revolt of al-Husayn" in his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]], and gives there his own view of Yazid's fitness, attributed to him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep18 00:02:30}.

### The pledge
He relates that when Mu'awiya sensed his death approaching he decided to appoint his son Yazid as caliph; most people pledged to him, and some leading figures opposed the appointment, among them [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn ibn Ali]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep18 00:12:00}. Al-Suwaidan describes the moves at the end of Mu'awiya's caliphate as an effort to impose a single candidate on the community, and holds that the community has the right to choose freely without pressure on its will {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep18 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep18 00:16:00}. He says four leading men refused this pledge, al-Husayn, [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr]], [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Abd Allah ibn Abbas]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Abd Allah ibn Umar]], and that Mu'awiya pressed them through his governor in Medina and they refused {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep18 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep18 00:18:00}. He says most of the community pledged to Yazid "by inducement and intimidation" {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:05:00}, and distinguishes Mu'awiya's caliphate, which he counts legitimate by everyone's pledge, from the imposition of Yazid {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep18 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:03:00}.

### His view of Yazid's fitness
Al-Suwaidan ascribes to al-Husayn, and adopts himself, the view that Yazid was not fit for the caliphate, lacking the conditions of probity and competence, and that the community had men more learned, more upright, more capable and more acceptable to the people, such as al-Husayn, Ibn Umar, Ibn al-Zubayr and Ibn Abbas {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:11:30}. He holds that the Umayyads wanted al-Husayn's pledge above all because it would give their rule legitimacy {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:12:00}. He argues that Yazid was not imam over those who had not pledged to him, so al-Husayn's rising does not count as rebellion against him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:15:00}. This is his reading; the museum gives no verdict on it.

### Yazid and the events in Kufa
He relates that Yazid, with rule settled for him in most lands, judged the governor of Kufa weak because he had declared he would touch no one who did not take up arms, and so joined Kufa to the governor of Basra, [[person/ubayd-allah-ibn-ziyad|Ibn Ziyad]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:02:30}. Al-Suwaidan holds that the Umayyad state met al-Husayn's candidacy with coercion and force, and that in his view the state was the aggressor because it violated the people's right to consultation {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:02:00}. He says that when news reached Yazid that al-Husayn had left Mecca, he wrote to Ibn Ziyad warning him that his time and his town had been tried with al-Husayn, and that by his conduct he would be set free or return to slavery {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:12:00}. He says Yazid would have wished al-Husayn to pledge to him and would have given him whatever he wanted {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:19:30}, and that Ibn Ziyad nearly accepted al-Husayn's offer to go to Yazid but for the objection of Shimr ibn Dhi al-Jawshan {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:15:30}.
