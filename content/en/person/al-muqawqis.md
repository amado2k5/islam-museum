## Overview
Al-Muqawqis, the ruler of Egypt at the Islamic conquest. Al-Baladhuri mentions him in the account of the peace with Amr ibn al-As and the attempt of the Byzantines to recover Alexandria. He took part in [[event/conquest-of-alexandria|the conquest of Alexandria]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.302-303}

## The peace that was refused
Al-Muqawqis made peace with Amr ibn al-As on terms that whoever of the Byzantines wished could leave, and whoever wished to stay could remain on a matter he named, and that two dinars be imposed on the Copts. This reached the king of the Byzantines, who was angered and sent armies, and they shut the gate of Alexandria. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.302-303}

## His three demands
Al-Muqawqis went out to Amr and asked three things. The first was that he should not give the Byzantines what he had given him, because they had deceived him. The second was that he should not break the covenant of the Copts, because the breach had not come from their side. The third was that, if he died, he should be buried in a church in Alexandria that he named. Amr granted his request. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.302-303}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Ibn Abd al-Hakam says that [[person/heraclius|Heraclius]] put al-Muqawqis in charge of Egypt, its war and the collection of its land tax, and that he resided at Alexandria. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 56} When the Prophet's letter came with [[person/hatib-ibn-abi-baltaa|Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a]], he placed it in an ivory casket and sealed it, met Hatib at night through an interpreter, said the Copts would not follow him, and asked that their talk be kept secret. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 66} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 67} In a report of Ibn Lahi'a from Yazid ibn Abi Habib, he sent the Prophet [[person/maria-al-qibtiyya|Mariya]] and her sister, a grey mule and a donkey, Coptic cloth, honey of Banha and money. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 69}

At the conquest he led the defence of [[event/siege-of-babylon-fortress-640|the fortress of Babylon]], withdrew to the Island, received [[event/ubada-embassy-to-al-muqawqis|Ubada ibn al-Samit's embassy]], offered the Muslims money to leave, and then made [[event/treaty-of-babylon|the peace]]. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 96} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 100} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 103} Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports that Heraclius wrote to rebuke him and that he refused to break the peace. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 104} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 105} At the end he asked Amr three things, the last that he be buried in Abu Yuhannis at Alexandria, and Amr said: "This is the easiest of them for us." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 107}
