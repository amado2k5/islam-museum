## Overview
Abu Ja'far al-Mansur, Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Abbas, the second of the Abbasid caliphs. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 422,426}

## Birth and qualities
Al-Suyuti says that he was born in 95 AH and that his mother was Salama the Berber, a concubine. He describes him as the greatest in dignity, courage and firmness of the Abbasids, a gatherer of wealth, and calls him Abu al-Dawaniq for his calling the governors and craftsmen to account for dawaniq and grains. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 422,426} Al-Mas'udi says that he was born in the year in which al-Hajjaj died. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 295-296}

## His works
He built al-Rusafa in 151 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 422,426} Al-Mas'udi says that fifty thousand men worked each day on the building of Baghdad. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 295-296} Al-Baladhuri says that he returned the people of Cyprus to the old peace of Mu'awiya, and granted his brother Sulayman ibn Ali Baalbek and its villages after the confiscation of the wealth of the Umayyads. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Abu Hanifa
Al-Suyuti says that he appointed [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] to the judgeship, then imprisoned him, and he died a few days later. It is said that he killed him by poison because he issued a fatwa for rebellion against him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 422,426} The phrase "it is said" keeps the matter open to dispute.

## The firsts attributed to him by al-Mas'udi and al-Suyuti
- The first caliph to bring astrologers near and to act by the rules of the stars. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 668-669}
- The first caliph in whose time books were translated from foreign languages into Arabic, among them Kalila wa Dimna and Euclid. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 668-669}
- The first caliph to use his clients in offices and to set them before the Arabs. Al-Mas'udi attributes to that the fall of the might of the Arabs. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 668-669}
- In his days Muhammad ibn Ishaq wrote the book of the campaigns and the biography. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 668-669}

## Death
Al-Mas'udi says that he died at Bir Maymun on his way to the Sacred Sanctuary, and that he left six hundred thousand thousand dirhams and fourteen thousand thousand dinars. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 295-296}

## For Sprouts
Abu Ja'far al-Mansur was the second Abbasid caliph. He built Baghdad — al-Mas'udi says fifty thousand men worked on it each day — and in his days Ibn Ishaq wrote the sira and books were translated from other languages into Arabic. He died on the road to the Sanctuary.

## For Explorers
Abu Ja'far Abd Allah ibn Muhammad al-Mansur, second Abbasid caliph, was born in 95 AH to Salama the Berber. Al-Suyuti describes him as the most dignified, brave and resolute of the Abbasids, nicknamed Abu al-Dawaniq for counting workmen to the last grain. He built Baghdad — al-Mas'udi says fifty thousand men each day — and al-Rusafa in 151 AH, and returned Cyprus to Mu'awiya's old treaty. Al-Mas'udi's 'firsts' for him: the first caliph to court astrologers, the first to have books translated from foreign tongues into Arabic — Kalila wa-Dimna and Euclid — the first to employ his clients over the Arabs, and in his days Ibn Ishaq composed the sira. He died at Bi'r Maymun on the road to the Sanctuary.

## For Scholars
Abu Ja'far Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Abbas al-Mansur, second Abbasid caliph, was born in 95 AH — in the year of al-Hajjaj's death, on al-Mas'udi's account — to Salama the Berber, an umm walad. Al-Suyuti calls him the most dignified, brave and resolute of the Abbasids and a gatherer of wealth, nicknamed Abu al-Dawaniq for reckoning workmen to the last grains. His works: Baghdad — fifty thousand men daily per al-Mas'udi — al-Rusafa in 151 AH, Cyprus returned to Mu'awiya's old treaty in al-Baladhuri, and grants to his brother Sulayman after the Umayyad confiscations. His account carries the Abu Hanifa case: al-Suyuti says he had him beaten over the judgeship then jailed until he died — 'it was said' by poison for a fatwa of rebellion, the 'it was said' keeping it contested. Al-Mas'udi's firsts attach to him: courting astrologers, translating foreign books (Kalila wa-Dimna, Euclid), preferring clients over Arabs, and Ibn Ishaq's sira composed in his reign. He died at Bi'r Maymun on the road to the Sanctuary leaving a great treasury on al-Mas'udi's count.
