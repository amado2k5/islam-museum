## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah al-Abbasi al-Alawi is, in the judgement of the book's editor, the author and narrator of [[work/sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq|Sirat al-Hadi ila al-Haqq]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.7} The opening page describes him as one of the noble young men of [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi's]] days and among the martyrs at Najran, carried alive from the battle to [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]], where he died and where his tomb is well known and visited. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.15}

## Descent
The opening page traces his descent to al-Abbas, son of the Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib, and calls him one of the ahl al-bayt; the intermediate names of the chain, as printed, cannot be checked from the machine-read text, so only the two ends are recorded here: Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah, and his remote ancestor [[person/al-abbas-ibn-ali|al-Abbas ibn Ali]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.15}

## His emigration to al-Hadi
His father, [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]], was among the first to receive al-Hadi's call before he left for Yemen. When he decided to emigrate to him he told his son and ordered him to follow, and Ali was then a boy on whom no obligation before God had yet fallen, that is, one who had not yet come of age. He went to al-Hadi in Dhu al-Hijja 285 AH, entered his service in Yemen and stayed with him until his own death. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.8}

## His part in the biography
Ali gave the biography two kinds of material: a large part, most of which he relates from his father, from [[person/muhammad-ibn-sulayman-al-kufi|Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Kufi]] and from others who were with al-Hadi, and a part consisting of events he witnessed and shared in. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.8} The opening page says the biography contains many of his stands. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.16} The book records verses by which al-Hadi mourned him at his grave in Khiwan, calling him an Abbasid of chosen forebears and as firm as a mountain. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.16}

## The standpoint of the source
The book is a Zaydi source written by followers of the imam; the language that honours Ali and his father is the book's own and not a judgement of the museum.
