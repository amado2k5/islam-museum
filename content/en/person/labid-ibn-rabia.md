## Overview
[[person/labid-ibn-rabia|Labid ibn Rabi'a al-Kilabi al-Ja'fari]], Abu Aqil, "the well-known poet". {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1297} Al-Marzubani in his dictionary describes him as a brave knight, a poet and generous, who composed poetry for a long time in the pre-Islamic period and then became Muslim. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1298}

## His Islam and his poetry
When [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] wrote to his governor in Kufa to ask Labid and al-Aghlab al-Ajli what they had composed of poetry in Islam, Labid said: "God has replaced poetry for me with the chapters of the Cow and the Family of Imran." Umar increased his stipend. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1298} It is said that he composed in Islam only one verse, and there is disagreement about which. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1298} It is established that the Prophet said that the truest word a poet ever said was a word of Labid's. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1298}

## The attribution of verses
Abu Umar says that a verse some attribute to him is not his, but belongs to Qurada ibn Nafatha; and that his poem beginning "Surely all things other than God are vain" shows in some of its verses that he composed it in Islam. Ibn Hajar objects that it is the poem he recited to Quraysh before his Islam, in the well-known story of [[person/uthman-ibn-mazun|Uthman ibn Maz'un]] in the biography. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1298}

## His elegy for his brother Arbad
Ibn Ishaq relates that [[person/arbad-ibn-qays|Arbad ibn Qays]] was Labid's brother on their mother's side, and gives six elegies by him after [[event/death-of-arbad-ibn-qays|his death by lightning]] on his return from the delegation of the Banu Amir.
