## Summary
Ravil Ismagilovich Gaynutdin is a Russian Tatar religious leader who has served as mufti of the Republic of Tatarstan since 1992 and as chairman of the Council of Muftis of Russia since 1996, making him one of the most visible figures of organised Muslim life in the Russian Federation. {cite:wikipedia-ravil-gaynutdin}

## Life and formation
He was born on 25 August 1959 in the village of Shaberdy in Tatarstan, then part of the Soviet Union, into an ethnic Tatar Muslim family. He studied at the Mir-i Arab madrasa in Bukhara — the only officially permitted Islamic seminary in the USSR — and later at the imam faculty of the Islamic University in Tripoli, Libya, a formation typical of the generation that rebuilt Russian Islam after the Soviet collapse. {cite:wikipedia-ravil-gaynutdin}

## Career
Gaynutdin worked as imam of Moscow's Cathedral Mosque in the late Soviet years and rose through the structures of the Central Spiritual Administration of Muslims. In 1992 he became mufti of Tatarstan, and in 1996 he was elected chairman of the Council of Muftis of Russia, the largest of the country's centralised Muslim bodies; he has been re-elected repeatedly since. In these roles he presided over the expansion of mosque construction, Islamic educational institutions and publishing in post-Soviet Russia, and he represents the community in state forums and interfaith councils alongside the Russian Orthodox Church and other recognised faiths. He also chairs the Spiritual Board of Muslims of the Russian Federation structures associated with the Council and sits on state advisory councils on religious cooperation. {cite:wikipedia-ravil-gaynutdin}

## Contributions and legacy
Gaynutdin has received Russian state orders and Tatar honours for interfaith and public service, and has published books and translations on Islamic topics in Russian. Observers describe his Council as favouring close cooperation with the state, and competing muftiates — notably the Moscow-based Spiritual Assembly — have at times rivalled its claim to speak for all Russian Muslims; the museum records that institutional contest as fact, without verdict. His long tenure nonetheless made the Council of Muftis a principal public face of Russian Islam at home and abroad. {cite:wikipedia-ravil-gaynutdin}
