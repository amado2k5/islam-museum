## Summary

Sheikh Abdulaziz bin Abdullah Al ash-Sheikh ⁦(30 November 1943 – 23 September 2025)⁩ was the third Grand Mufti of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, holding the office from 14 May 1999 until his death — a quarter-century as the head of the kingdom's religious establishment, chairing the Council of Senior Religious Scholars and the Permanent Committee for Islamic Research and Issuing Fatwas, the two bodies through which Saudi official Islam speaks. {cite:wikipedia-abdulaziz-al-sheikh} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 230}

## Life and formation

Born in Mecca into the Al al-Sheikh family — the scholarly dynasty descending from Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, the eighteenth-century reformer whose compact with the Al Saud bound his heirs to the Saudi religious establishment — he lost his father at eight and his eyesight at about twenty, pursuing his religious formation regardless. He graduated from Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University in Riyadh, and in 1969–70 took up his first leadership role at the Sheikh Muhammad bin Ibrahim Mosque in the Dukhna quarter of Riyadh. {cite:wikipedia-abdulaziz-al-sheikh}

## Rise through the establishment

He served in the kingdom's religious judiciary and as imam and khatib of major Riyadh mosques, taught at Imam university, and joined the fatwa-issuing apparatus — the Permanent Committee for Islamic Research and Issuing Fatwas — rising through the hierarchy of the Council of Senior Scholars. On 14 May 1999, after the death of Sheikh Ibn Baz, he was appointed Grand Mufti by royal decree. {cite:wikipedia-abdulaziz-al-sheikh}

## The muftiate

As Grand Mufti he was the kingdom's highest religious authority: head of the Council of Senior Scholars, chairman of the Permanent Committee, and the public voice of official fatwa on matters from worship and medicine to politics and war. His pronouncements across the twenty-six years are the ordinary output of a muftiate — rulings on prayer, finance, organ donation, extremism — of which the record keeps the office's shape rather than a catalogue. His tenure coincided with the post-2001 scrutiny of religious discourse and with the reform era of King Salman and Crown Prince Muhammad bin Salman, during which the office's powers were progressively drawn closer to the state. {cite:wikipedia-abdulaziz-al-sheikh}

## Death and succession

He died in Riyadh on 23 September 2025, aged 81, and was succeeded as Grand Mufti by Sheikh Saleh al-Fawzan. {cite:wikipedia-abdulaziz-al-sheikh}

## Assessment

Al al-Sheikh's quarter-century reads as the late form of an office founded in 1953: the blind jurist of the dynasty, administrating fatwa through councils rather than personal charisma. Muslim 500's obituary describes him as a leading voice of the global Salafi movement — the institutional face of Hanbali-Athari Saudi Islam for a generation. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 230} {cite:wikipedia-abdulaziz-al-sheikh}
