## Summary
Mostafa Mahmoud was an Egyptian physician and writer. The page introducing him in the first printing of his book [[work/allah-wal-insan|Allah wa-l-Insan]] (1957) says he graduated from the Qasr al-Aini Faculty of Medicine in December 1953, specialising in chest diseases, began writing short stories in 1947 in the weekly al-Risala, and then worked for Akhir Sa'a, Akhbar al-Yawm, al-Tahrir and Rose al-Yusuf. {cite:mm-allah-wal-insan-1957 p. 5} He is the author of [[work/rihlati-min-al-shakk-ila-al-iman|Rihlati min al-Shakk ila al-Iman]] ("My Journey from Doubt to Faith"), in which he narrates his passage from doubt to faith. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 5} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 14}

## Upbringing and formation
In al-Qur'an: Muhawala li-Fahm 'Asri he relates that his first meeting with the Qur'an was at the age of four in the Qur'an school of Shaykh Mahmud, that he grew up in a family where "each member was left to himself", and that he failed his first school year three times without being scolded. {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 4} In Rihlati he relates that in early adolescence he began asking: who created God? and that he now thinks his motive was pride in his own mind rather than a search for truth. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 5} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 6} As a boy he immersed himself in the Tanta municipal library, reading Shibli Shumayyil and Salama Musa and discovering Freud and Darwin, and kept a small laboratory in his room. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 7} He then entered medical school, studying in English, and learnt "the scientific outlook" that recognises only what the senses perceive. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 8} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 9}

## Journalism and literature
The introduction says his first book, Akl 'Aysh, appeared in the Golden Book series with short tales and popular sketches, and that he "discussed the problem of religions freely in Rose al-Yusuf", which had a wide effect on readers. {cite:mm-allah-wal-insan-1957 p. 5} At the end of Rihlati is a list of twenty-six of his books, among them collections of stories, novels, plays, studies and essays, including Allah wa-l-Insan, Lughz al-Mawt, Lughz al-Hayat, Einstein and Relativity, al-'Ankabut and al-Khuruj min al-Tabut, the play al-Shaytan Yaskun fi Baytina written in April 1973, and al-Madina, on his journeys to Europe between 1956 and 1968. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 91}

The list of his books at the end of the fifth edition of *Muhammad: Muhawala li-Fahm al-Sira al-Nabawiyya* gives fifty-four titles and records that his novel *Rajul taht al-sifr* won the State Prize for 1970 and that his complete works were published in Beirut in 1972 {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 98}. His reflective books include [[work/lughz-al-mawt|Lughz al-Mawt]] (Dar al-Awda, 1972) {cite:mm-lughz-al-mawt-awda-1972 p. 6}, and on the Prophet's life he wrote [[work/muhammad-muhawala-li-fahm-al-sira|Muhammad: An Attempt to Understand the Sira]] {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 3}.

## The journey from doubt to faith (in his own account)
He says it took "thirty years of drowning in books" and thousands of nights of solitude and reflection. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 6} He describes stages: first imagining God as "the energy immanent in the universe" in a materialist way; then falling into "Indian pantheism", Spinoza's philosophy and Bergson's idea, practising yoga and being taken with reincarnation, which appeared in his novels al-'Ankabut and al-Khuruj min al-Tabut. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 9} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 10} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 11} He says science itself, by showing a single fabric and single laws across living things, led him to one transcendent Creator, and "gave me the complete Islamic idea of God". {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 12} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 13} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 14}

## His views (the writer's views)
:::perspectives
- **Mostafa Mahmoud on material civilisation (opinion).** Following [[person/muhammad-asad|Muhammad Asad]], he reads the one-eyed "false messiah" (al-Masikh al-Dajjal) as a symbol of modern material civilisation, which sees with the single eye of matter and has lost the eye of the spirit, and holds that Muslims lost confidence in their heritage before its glitter. {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 80} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 81} {cite:mm-rihla-min-al-shakk-maarif p. 82}
- **His view of evolution (opinion).** In [[work/hiwar-ma-sadiqi-al-mulhid|Hiwar ma'a Sadiqi al-Mulhid]] he holds that the Qur'an tells of human creation "in stages and phases", not all at once, and that Darwin "was right" about anatomical resemblance and about evolution, but "wrong" to explain ascent by material factors alone and to posit a single origin for all species. {cite:mm-hiwar-ma-sadiqi-al-mulhid-awda-1986 p. 124} {cite:mm-hiwar-ma-sadiqi-al-mulhid-awda-1986 p. 126} {cite:mm-hiwar-ma-sadiqi-al-mulhid-awda-1986 p. 129} This is set out in the entry [[question/is-human-evolution-compatible-with-islam|evolution and Islam]].
- **His view of the Qur'an and science (opinion).** In [[work/al-quran-muhawala-li-fahm-asri|al-Qur'an: Muhawala li-Fahm 'Asri]] he gives examples he regards as Qur'anic anticipations of science, such as fingerprints, mountains, iron and the genome, matters of the disputed [[concept/al-ijaz-al-ilmi|"scientific miracle"]] question. {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 1} {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 2} {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 3}
- **Academic view and scholars' criticism of him:** not yet documented with a defined source in the museum.

## For young visitors
Mostafa Mahmoud was a doctor who loved science and writing. He wrote many stories and books, and told how he doubted as a boy and how thinking and science later led him to faith in God.
