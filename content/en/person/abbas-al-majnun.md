## Summary
al-Abbas, known as al-Majnun, was pained with longing and withdrawn from people, watchful for his Beloved and set apart from his own kind. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 508, sequence 509, on page 145. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/145}

## Ibn al-Mubarak on Mount Lebanon
Ibn al-Mubarak relates that he climbed Mount Lebanon and saw a man in a woolen robe with torn sleeves, on which was written: not to be sold or bought. The man wore a hair cloth, a cloak of contentment, and a turban of trust in God. When he saw Ibn al-Mubarak he hid behind a tree, and Ibn al-Mubarak adjured him by God until he appeared. Ibn al-Mubarak said: you servants endure solitude and suffer the loneliness of these deserts. He laughed, put his sleeve on his head, and recited verses of longing for his Beloved, asking Paradise not for its own delights but to see Him. He then vanished. Ibn al-Mubarak watched that spot for a year without seeing him. He met Abu Sulayman al-Darani, described him, and al-Darani wept and said: alas for my longing to see him once more. He said: that is al-Abbas al-Majnun, who eats two meals a month of tree fruit or plants of the earth, and has worshipped for sixty years. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/145}
