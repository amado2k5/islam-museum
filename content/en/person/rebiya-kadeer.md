## Summary
Rebiya Kadeer is a Uyghur businesswoman-turned-activist who went from being one of China's richest women to one of its most famous political prisoners, and then to the presidency of the World Uyghur Congress in exile — the public face of the Uyghur cause for a decade. {cite:wikipedia-rebiya-kadeer}

## Life and formation
Born in 1946 in Altay in the Xinjiang region of northwest China, Kadeer began in poverty — at times a laundress — and built a trading and real-estate fortune across the post-Mao reform decades, becoming one of China's wealthiest self-made women and a member of a national consultative political body. {cite:wikipedia-rebiya-kadeer}

## Imprisonment and exile
Her philanthropy and advocacy for Uyghur women drew state suspicion after her husband, the scholar Sidik Haji Rouzi, emigrated and criticised Beijing on US-funded radio. Arrested in August 1999 while passing information to a visiting US congressional research delegation, she was sentenced in 2000 to eight years for "providing state secrets to foreigners" — material consisting of publicly available newspapers. Released to the United States in March 2005 on medical parole under international pressure, she settled near Washington, DC. {cite:wikipedia-rebiya-kadeer}

## World Uyghur Congress
In November 2006 the World Uyghur Congress — the Munich-based umbrella of exile Uyghur organisations, which China designates a separatist body — elected her president; she served until November 2017, succeeded by Dolkun Isa. From exile she campaigned on behalf of Uyghurs facing detention and assimilation policies in Xinjiang; Beijing blamed her for the July 2009 Urumqi unrest, a charge she denied, and later sentenced several of her children in China in cases critics call retaliatory. The museum records the competing claims attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-rebiya-kadeer}

## Legacy
Kadeer's trajectory — insider entrepreneur, prisoner, exile leader — made her the best-known Uyghur internationally and kept the Xinjiang issue before Western governments long before the mass-detention revelations of the late 2010s. {cite:wikipedia-rebiya-kadeer}
