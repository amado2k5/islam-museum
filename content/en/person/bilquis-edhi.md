# Bilquis Edhi

Bilquis Bano Edhi (14 August 1947 – 15 April 2022) was a Pakistani nurse and philanthropist who, with her husband Abdul Sattar Edhi, co-chaired the Edhi Foundation and was one of the most active philanthropists in Pakistan; she is credited with helping save the lives of more than 16,000 children, and was called "The Mother of Pakistan". She received the 1986 Ramon Magsaysay Award for Public Service (shared with her husband), the Mother Teresa Memorial International Award for Social Justice (2015) and Pakistan's Hilal-i-Imtiaz. {cite:wikipedia-bilquis-edhi-en} {cite:wikipedia-bilquis-edhi-ar} {cite:wikipedia-bilquis-edhi-ur} {cite:wikidata-bilquis-edhi}

## Biography

Bilquis was born at Bantva, Gujarat, in British India — so the English article's infobox and biography, the Arabic article, and the Urdu article's text agree; Wikidata and the Urdu infobox say Karachi, whither her family moved after Partition — and as a teenager who had not enjoyed school she joined the small Edhi dispensary in Karachi's Mithadar district as a nurse in 1965. Its founder, Abdul Sattar Edhi, recognised her enthusiasm through her six months' training in midwifery and basic healthcare and put her in charge of the nursing department; the two were married — the biography says she was nineteen, and the infobox dates the marriage to 1965. She took over the management of the jhoola project — the baby cradles her husband had begun in 1952 — and established more than 300 cradles across Pakistan where parents can leave unwanted infants rather than kill them; the cradles carry the message "Do not kill, leave the baby to live in the cradle", and over ninety per cent of the surrendered infants are girls. Among the children in her care was Geeta, a deaf-mute Indian girl stranded in Pakistan, whom she sheltered for years until her repatriation to India. In 2022 she was named among The Muslim 500. {cite:wikipedia-bilquis-edhi-en} {cite:wikipedia-bilquis-edhi-ur}

## Death

Bilquis Edhi died at the Aga Khan Hospital, Karachi, on 15 April 2022, aged 74, of congestive heart failure after a prolonged illness. Her funeral prayers were offered at New Memon Masjid and she was buried at Mewa Shah Graveyard, Karachi, on 16 April 2022; the Government of Sindh issued an official obituary and announced a day of mourning. {cite:wikipedia-bilquis-edhi-en} {cite:wikipedia-bilquis-edhi-ar}
