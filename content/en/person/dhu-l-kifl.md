## Overview
Dhu'l-Kifl is a Qur'anic figure named twice — among the patient with Job, Ishmael and Idris, and among 'the righteous' with Ishmael, Elisha, Jonah and Lot. Ibn Kathir counts him among the prophets in the commentators' reading {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## In classical tafsir
Al-Tha'labi records that Dhu'l-Kifl was Bishr son of Ayyub, sent as messenger to the Romans' land after his father, who undertook to suffice his people in judgment and govern them justly — hence the name; he dwelt in Syria until he died aged ninety-five {cite:qisas-anbiya-thaalabi-edition}. Ibn Kathir lists him among the righteous, and reports the naming came from his undertaking to suffice the people {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## In modern scholarship
Dhu'l-Kifl's identity is not fixed to a single figure in the early Islamic sources; academic theories identify him with Ezekiel or other biblical figures — John Walker argued the Ezekiel identification in The Muslim World {cite:walker-dhulkifl-mw-1926}, and the Encyclopaedia of Islam sums up that the identity remains unsettled {cite:vajda-ei2-dhulkifl}.
