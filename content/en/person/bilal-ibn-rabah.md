## Brief
Bilal ibn Rabah was an Abyssinian-born slave in Mecca and one of the earliest Muslims. He was tortured for his faith until [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] ransomed him. He became the muezzin of the community, the first voice to call the Muslims to prayer.

## Life
Born in Mecca to an enslaved Abyssinian mother around 580 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}, Bilal embraced Islam while still owned by [[person/umayya-ibn-khalaf|Umayya ibn Khalaf]], who used to throw him onto the sands of Mecca. Tradition relates that under the torture he kept saying "One, one" until [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] bought him and freed him {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

In Medina the Prophet chose him as muezzin. He took part in [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], and after the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest of Mecca]] he is recorded calling the adhan from the roof of the Kaaba itself {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. After the Prophet's death he served in Syria. The sources differ on whether he died there or in Medina, around 640 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Bilal stands where three histories meet: slavery and freedom, Africa and Arabia, and the sound of Islam. Every adhan in the world is remembered as descending from his voice.

## In the secret call (The Sealed Nectar p. 76)
The book mentions him among the early Muslims after the first group, and names him Bilal ibn Rabah the Abyssinian ([[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|the secret call]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}.

## His torture in Mecca (The Sealed Nectar p. 90)
Umayya ibn Khalaf would put a rope around his neck and hand him over to boys, who would drag him around the mountains of Mecca. He would throw him onto the sands of Mecca at the height of noon, place a rock on his chest, and say to him: "You will stay like this until you die or disbelieve in Muhammad." Bilal would say: "One, one," until [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] bought him and freed him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 90}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 6)

The Sheikh relates that the Prophet told Bilal he had never entered Paradise without hearing the sound of his sandals there, and asked what he did, and Bilal answered that he never committed a sin without following it with a plea for forgiveness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:21:30}. He adds that Bilal would pray after every ablution, and the Sheikh then recommends reading Surat Yusuf, saying that it brings light and certainty into the chest and calms blood pressure (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:22:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 7)

The Sheikh repeats that the Prophet heard the sound of Bilal's sandals in Paradise, then moves to the day of the conquest of Makkah and says that Ali asked to give the call to prayer from the top of the Kaaba {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:30:00}. He says the Prophet wished to set a rule and did not permit it, saying that only Bilal would give the call, and he notes that Abd Allah ibn Umm Maktum also gave the call to prayer in Madina {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:31:00}. He says Bilal climbed the Kaaba and gave the call and the mountains of Makkah heard it, and that after the Prophet was taken, Bilal stood to call the adhan and his voice caught at the mention of the Messenger's name as he looked toward the grave {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:31:00}. He says Bilal asked Abu Bakr's leave and set out for Homs, after which the Sheikh prays for the lands of the Muslims, asking God to restore them as they were, to give victory to Islam and to honour the Muslims {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:31:30}.

He relates that Bilal gave the maghrib call in Madina, and as soon as he said Allahu akbar the people of Madina left their work and their market, as though history had returned {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:34:00}. He says that sobbing and weeping were heard in Madina at the mention of the Prophet's name in the call, and that the Companions were deeply attached to him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:34:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy recounts that Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf offered Bilal ibn Rabah marriage into his family by giving him his sister, and that Bilal said he would seek God's guidance (istikhara) before answering (needs_source: the exchange is garbled in the automatic transcript) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:18:30}.

He recounts that the Companions asked Bilal to give the call to prayer at al-Aqsa; when he did, it moved everyone present to tears and brought the Prophet to their minds (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:19:30}. He recounts that the Prophet told Bilal that he had heard Bilal's footsteps ahead of him in Paradise (needs_source: parts of the saying are unclear in the transcription) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 01:12:30}.

The sheikh says Islam made Khabbab ibn al-Aratt equal, in everything, to the son of Umar ibn al-Khattab (needs_source: the list of names is as the sheikh gives it) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:29:00}.

## For Scholars
Bilal ibn Rabah was an Abyssinian-born slave in Mecca (c. 580 CE) and among the first Muslims; he became Muslim while owned by Umayya ibn Khalaf, who tortured him on the midday sands while he said 'One, One', until Abu Bakr bought and freed him — as Ibn Ishaq and al-Raheeq record. The Prophet chose him as the community's muezzin, the first to call to prayer; he witnessed Badr and is remembered calling the adhan on the Ka'ba at the conquest. After the Prophet's death he served in Syria, and the sources differ on whether he died there or in Medina c. 640 CE. Historians read him where three histories meet — slavery and freedom, Africa and Arabia, and the sound of Islam; every adhan in the world is traced in memory to his voice.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh holds that a great man is great by his character, not his seat of office, giving the example of Bilal the Abyssinian, from whose tongue the words of the call to prayer came so that hearts trembled, who was pointed out only for his piety, and for whom it is honour enough that the Prophet commended him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:02:00}

## In Abd al-Rahman Mansour's programme 'Ahalina'
[[person/abd-al-rahman-mansour|Abd al-Rahman Mansour]] mentions Bilal in his programme [[work/ahaleena|'Ahalina']] while forbidding fathers to despise a child for his looks or colour: Bilal, he says, was an Abyssinian and a black slave, and yet the Prophet heard the sound of his sandals in Paradise; he joins this to the teaching that the noblest of people before God are the most God-fearing, not the fairest or the most handsome. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep05 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep05 00:06:00}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the third episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] introduces Bilal as a riddle before naming him: a slave before his Islam in a society that wronged slaves and the poor, freed by [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]] and so counted among Abu Bakr's good deeds {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:01:00}; black of face and white of heart, one of the first to accept Islam, who bore persecution as no one else did {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:01:30}. He says that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], whenever he saw him, called him "our master", and that every Muslim recalls him each time the call to prayer is heard {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:02:00}. He gives his kunya as Abu Abd al-Karim and calls him "the first muezzin", and says he grew up enslaved in Mecca, his father Rabah and his mother Hamama being among the clients and slaves of [[person/umayya-ibn-khalaf|Umayya ibn Khalaf]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:03:00}. He describes pre-Islamic Mecca as a society that ate carrion and blood, buried its daughters alive and oppressed the weak, women and slaves, and says Bilal was among the first to accept Islam when the Prophet was sent {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:03:30}.

He relates from al-Hakim, with a chain he calls sound, through [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]], that the first to declare Islam openly were seven: the Prophet, Abu Bakr, [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar]] and his mother [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya]], [[person/suhayb-ibn-sinan|Suhayb]], Bilal and al-Miqdad; God protected the Prophet through his uncle and Abu Bakr through his clan, while the polytheists put the rest in iron armour and roasted them in the sun until they gave them what they wanted, all except Bilal {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:04:30}. He says Bilal's people held him cheap and handed him to boys who dragged him through the ravines of Mecca with a rope of palm fibre round his neck while he repeated "One, One" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:05:30}, and that Umayya ibn Khalaf would throw him on the sand in the midday heat, lay a rock on his chest and give him the choice of disbelief or death {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:06:00}; this is the same picture of his torture that al-Raheeq al-Makhtum gives {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.90}. He relates that the Prophet passed him while he was being tortured, was pained and wished he had money to buy him; Abu Bakr heard of it, went to them and bought him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:06:30}. Umayya asked five ounces of gold, Abu Bakr paid and freed him, and when Umayya said he would have sold him even for one ounce, Abu Bakr replied that he would have bought him even for a hundred; he attributes the story to Abu Nu'aym's Hilya {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:07:00}. He attributes to al-Bukhari that Umar used to say Abu Bakr was their master and had freed their master, meaning Bilal {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:08:00}, and says that when the polytheists claimed Abu Bakr had bought him to repay an earlier favour, the Qur'an was revealed praising Abu Bakr's sincerity in spending only to seek the face of his Lord {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:08:00}.

He relates from Sahih Muslim, through [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]], that six of them were with the Prophet, among them Sa'd, Bilal, Ibn Mas'ud and a man of [[group/hudhayl|Hudhayl]], and when the polytheists asked that they be driven away, the verse was revealed telling the Prophet to keep himself patiently with those who call on their Lord morning and evening {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:09:00}. He relates from Sahih Muslim, through A'idh ibn Amr, that [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] passed by [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman]], Suhayb and Bilal, who remarked that God's swords had not yet taken their due from him; Abu Bakr objected to their saying this of the chief of Quraysh and then told the Prophet, who said that perhaps he had angered them, and that if he had angered them he had angered his Lord; Abu Bakr asked their pardon and they forgave him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:09:30}. He relates that [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr]] taunted Bilal over his black mother and Bilal complained to the Prophet, who told Abu Dharr that something of the Jahiliyya remained in him; Abu Dharr laid his cheek in the dust and asked Bilal to tread on it or forgive him, and Bilal forgave {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:11:00}. As an instance of his wisdom he relates that when a man boasted of his ancestors before Bilal and then asked about his father, Bilal answered that his father was the one to whom the angels bowed, meaning [[person/adam|Adam]], for all people come from Adam and Adam from dust {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:12:30}.

He says Bilal was given the glad tidings of Paradise twice, and relates from al-Tirmidhi's chapter of merits, grading it sound, that Paradise longs for three: [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], Ammar and Bilal {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:13:00}. He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari, through [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir]], that the Prophet saw himself enter Paradise, saw al-Rumaysa' the wife of [[person/abu-talha-al-ansari|Abu Talha]], and heard footsteps that he was told were Bilal's {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:13:30}; and from Sahih al-Bukhari, through [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], that at the dawn prayer the Prophet asked Bilal which deed of his gave him most hope, since he had heard the sound of Bilal's sandals in Paradise, and Bilal answered that he never purified himself at any hour of night or day without praying with that purification whatever was written for him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:14:00}.

He says Bilal emigrated to [[place/medina|Medina]] with the others and waited there for months, longing for the Prophet, until he arrived with Abu Bakr {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:15:30}. He relates that Abu Bakr and Bilal fell ill with fever in Medina: Abu Bakr would recite a line about how near death is, and Bilal, when the fever left him, would raise his voice with two lines of longing for Mecca naming its idhkhir and jalil plants and Shama and Tafil, which he explains as two mountains of Mecca, with idhkhir a plant that grows there {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:16:00}. He draws from this that a believer longs for his homeland whatever harm he met there, citing the Prophet's words on leaving Mecca that it was the dearest of God's lands to him and that he left it only because its people drove him out {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:17:00}. He relates that when Bilal wished to marry, the Prophet told him to propose to the sister of [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], whom he describes as a Qurashi of high lineage from [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]], the Prophet's maternal kin, and a rich man, "a billionaire in today's terms" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:17:30}; Bilal told him that if he agreed, praise be to God, and if he refused, God is greater, and Ibn Awf welcomed the muezzin of the Messenger of God {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:18:30}. As an instance of his honesty he relates that a man asked Bilal to speak for him in a marriage proposal, and Bilal went with him and testified to the woman's family that he was a man of bad character, citing the verse that forbids concealing testimony {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:19:00}.

He tells the story of the call to prayer: the Muslims in Medina disagreed over a signal for the time of prayer, between a bell like the Christians' and a horn like the Jews'; Abdullah ibn Zayd ibn Tha'laba saw in a dream a man in two green garments carrying a bell, who taught him the adhan; the Prophet said it was a true vision and told him to teach it to Bilal, whose voice was finer; and when Bilal called it, Umar came out trailing his cloak, saying he had seen the same {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:19:30}. He draws the lesson that it is good for a muezzin to have a fine voice {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:21:00}. He says Bilal took part in all the campaigns and killed Umayya ibn Khalaf at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:22:00}, and that on the day of [[event/conquest-of-mecca|the conquest]] the Prophet entered the Ka'ba with [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama ibn Zayd]], Bilal and then [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Abdullah ibn Umar]], prayed inside, and then told Bilal to climb on top of it and call the adhan while the people of Mecca listened {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:22:00}.

He relates that when the Prophet died and the time of prayer came before his burial, Abu Bakr asked Bilal to call the adhan; when he reached the testimony to the Prophet he wept and could not finish, Medina shook with weeping, and he then asked Abu Bakr to take another muezzin, saying he would call for no one after the Messenger of God {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:23:00}. He relates that Bilal asked Abu Bakr's leave to join the army in Syria and Abu Bakr refused, whereupon Bilal told him that if he had bought him for himself he should keep him, and if he had bought him for God he should let him go, and he was given leave {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:24:00}. He took part in the conquest of Syria and Palestine in Umar's time; when Umar received the key of [[place/al-aqsa-mosque|al-Aqsa]] he asked Bilal to call the adhan in memory of the Prophet's days, and Bilal called it and wept and the Companions wept; and he lived in Syria until past the age of sixty {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:24:30}. He ends with Bilal's death: his wife wept, lamenting his distress, and he told her to cry out for joy instead, for tomorrow he would meet the beloved ones, Muhammad and his companions {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep03 00:25:00}.

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Ahl al-Janna'
In the tenth episode of his programme [[work/ahl-al-jannah|'Ahl al-Janna']], on the merciful and the harsh, [[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] relates that Abu Bakr saw Umayya ibn Khalaf torturing Bilal with a rock on his belly and asked him to have mercy; Umayya told him to have mercy himself, so Abu Bakr bought Bilal, freed him and lifted the rock from his belly. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep10 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep10 00:05:30} He says Umayya then remarked that he would have sold him for a single uqiya, and Abu Bakr replied that he would have paid more had he asked (needs_source: the reports differ on the price). {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep10 00:05:30}
