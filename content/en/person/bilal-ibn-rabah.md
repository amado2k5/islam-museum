## Brief
Bilal ibn Rabah was an Abyssinian-born slave in Mecca and one of the first Muslims. Tortured for his faith and ransomed by Abu Bakr, he became the community's muezzin — the first voice ever to call Muslims to prayer.

## Life
Born in Mecca to an enslaved Abyssinian mother about 580 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}, Bilal embraced Islam while still owned by [[person/umayya-ibn-khalaf|Umayya ibn Khalaf]], who had him dragged over the burning sands. Tradition tells that he answered the torture with "One, One" — until [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] bought and freed him {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

In Medina the Prophet chose him as muezzin. He fought at [[event/battle-of-badr]], and after the [[event/conquest-of-mecca]] he is remembered calling the adhan from the roof of the Kaaba itself {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. After the Prophet's death he served in Syria; the sources differ on whether he died there or in Medina, around 640 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Bilal stands at the meeting point of three histories: slavery and freedom, Africa and Arabia, and the soundscape of Islam — every adhan in the world descends, in memory, from his voice.

## For young visitors
Bilal was enslaved and tortured for what he believed, and would not stop saying "One." He became the first person ever to call the Muslim prayer — and his call is still heard today.
