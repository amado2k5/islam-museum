# Messali Hadj

## Summary

Ahmed Ben Messali Hadj (1898–1974) was an Algerian nationalist politician, often called the father of Algerian nationalism, who founded the first modern movements for Algerian independence.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}

## Early life

He was born in Tlemcen on 16 May 1898 into a Kouloughli family of modest means; his father was a shoemaker of Turkish origin and his mother came from a family whose father was a qadi and a member of the Darqawiyya brotherhood.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-fr} The French article records that as a boy he was a pupil at the madrasa of the Derkaoua zaouïa of Cheikh Benyelles and kept frequenting the zaouïa as a young man; the English article says he received a religious education influenced by the Darqawiyya order, while the Arabic article states of him personally that he was of the Darqawiyya order — a difference between the sources that the record's faith note preserves rather than resolves.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-fr}{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-ar} He served in the French army from 1918 to 1921.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}

## Paris and the Étoile nord-africaine

Messali went to Paris in October 1923 — the Arabic article says October 1925 — and was recruited by Abdelkader Hadj Ali to the colonial commission of the French Communist Party in 1925.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-ar} In 1926 he founded the Étoile nord-africaine, and at Brussels in 1927 he set out its demands, including abolition of the Indigénat, trade-union rights and education for all.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}

## The PPA and the road to 1954

After exile in Geneva, where he met Shakib Arslan and reoriented from Marxism towards pan-Arabism, he founded the Parti du peuple algérien in March 1937.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en} A Vichy court sentenced him in 1941 to sixteen years of hard labour, and he was confined in southern Algeria and then Brazzaville; his arrest and deportation formed part of the background to the Sétif uprising of May 1945.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en} He went on to lead the MTLD, dissociated himself from the armed struggle in 1954, and founded the Mouvement national algérien to rival the FLN.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-fr}

## Death

He died on 3 June 1974 — in Paris according to the English and Arabic articles, at Gouvieux in the Oise according to the French — and was buried at Tlemcen, in the cemetery of Shaykh al-Sanusi according to the Arabic article.{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-en}{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-fr}{cite:wikipedia-messali-hadj-ar}
