## Summary
Al-Sinaki, clerk of works of the Damascus Umayyad Mosque, an accomplished, extremely humble littérateur with poetry; appointed supervisor at the mint, he was asked to hold a sealed chest in trust until the depositor returned from the Hijaz — knowing nothing of its contents. Then word was written to the amir Taybars the vizier's deputy that the chest held dies for striking false coin; his house was raided, the chest found, and his defence was not accepted. He was disgraced through Damascus and expelled. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 546-547}

## Exile
From the road he sent a companion: 'My land, though it wronged me, is dear; I am but musk — away from your soil, I fade.' {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 546-547}
