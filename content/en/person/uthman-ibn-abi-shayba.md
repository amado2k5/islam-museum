## Overview
Uthman ibn Abi Shayba al-Kufi was a traditionist who died in Kufa in the year 239 AH, as al-Mas'udi records among the deaths of that year. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 512}

Ibn Kathir, quoting al-Tabari, lists his death in the same year and describes him as the author of the well-known Tafsir and Musnad, a description that points to his fame in hadith and exegesis. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 718}

He came from a family of traditionists: he transmitted from his father and from his uncle Abu Bakr, as Ibn Kathir reports in another passage. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya p. 44}

Tarikh Baghdad preserves his report that he visited Abu Khalid al-Ahmar as he lay dying, and heard him say that his soul's departure was dearer to him than its remaining in his body. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib p. 27}

Uthman also judged Sufyan ibn Husayn, tutor to al-Mahdi, to be trustworthy but unsteady in hadith and few in what he related. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib p. 154}

## In Mizan al-I'tidal
[[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] marks his entry in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] with "sahh", meaning that his reliability is the accepted view, and calls him Abu al-Hasan, one of the eminent hadith imams like his brother Abu Bakr. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 35} He quotes Abd Allah ibn Ahmad as having read to his father hadiths narrated by Uthman — among them one from Jabir about the Prophet attending the polytheists' gatherings — which Ahmad strongly rejected, calling them forged or as good as forged; Ahmad said he preferred his brother Abu Bakr to Uthman, although Ibn Ma'in preferred Uthman. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 35} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 36} On one wording of that hadith al-Dhahabi comments that it means he had recently seen idols being touched, not that he touched them. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 36}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: trustworthy (al-Dhahabi, Ahmad in one report, Ibn Ma'in)**: al-Dhahabi says Uthman needs no corroboration, and it is not held against him that he is alone in some hadiths given how much he narrated, though he may err; al-Bukhari and Muslim relied on him in their Sahihs, Abu Ya'la, al-Baghawi and others narrated from him; asked about him, Ahmad said "I know only good of him" and praised him, and Yahya said: trustworthy and reliable. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 37}
- **Sunni: objection to some of his hadith (Ahmad in his son's report, al-Azdi)**: Ahmad rejected hadiths he narrated and said: we thought he was imagining these hadiths; al-Azdi said: I saw our colleagues mention that Uthman narrated hadiths in which he is not corroborated. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 36} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 37}
:::

**Misreadings of the Qur'an.** Al-Dhahabi notes it was said that Uthman did not know the Qur'an by heart, and quotes [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib]]'s al-Jami' that no hadith scholar is reported to have made more misreadings of the Qur'an than he, with reports of his reading verses from Surat al-Fil and elsewhere wrongly. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 37} Al-Dhahabi comments that it may have been a slip of the tongue, for he certainly knew Surat al-Fil, and people transmitted his commentary from him; he adds that he seems to have been a jester, and perhaps he repented. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 37} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 38}

**Death.** Al-Dhahabi relates from Ibrahim ibn Abi Talib that he visited him and Uthman wished for the death of Ishaq ibn Rahawayh so that Jarir's hadith would be his alone; returning from the pilgrimage he found Uthman dying; al-Dhahabi says he outlived Ishaq by five months and died in Muharram 239 AH. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 38}
