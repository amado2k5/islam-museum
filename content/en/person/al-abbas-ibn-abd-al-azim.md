## Overview
Al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Azim ibn Isma'il ibn Tawba al-Anbari al-Basri, Abu al-Fadl, is described by al-Dhahabi as the memoriser, the proof and the imam {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2121}. He travelled widely and was deeply versed in the transmitted reports {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2121}. The sign printed with his entry shows that al-Bukhari cited him as a suspended report and that the other compilers recorded his hadith by direct hearing {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2121}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan, Mu'adh ibn Hisham, Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Umar ibn Yunus, Yazid ibn Harun, al-Nadr ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Razzaq, Abu Asim al-Nabil and a great many others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2121}. Baqi ibn Makhlad, Abu Hatim, Abdan al-Ahwazi, Ibn Khuzayma, Umar ibn Bujayr, Zakariyya al-Saji and others narrated from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2121} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2122}.

## What the critics said
Al-Nasa'i said he was trustworthy and reliable {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2122}. Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna al-Simsar said he was one of the leading Muslims, and another said he was among the most sensible people of his time and a man of merit {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2122}.

## His death
Al-Dhahabi says he died in 246 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2122}.
