## Summary
Martin Lings (24 January 1909 – 12 May 2005), who took the name Abu Bakr Siraj al-Din, was an English writer and scholar. Raised a Protestant and, in the account of the English Wikipedia, an unbeliever in his youth, he embraced Islam in Egypt at the end of the 1930s, worked for the British Museum and British Library, and wrote *Muhammad: His Life Based on the Earliest Sources* (1983), which became a widely read English biography. {cite:livingislam-lings-obituaries-2005 Times and Guardian} {cite:encyclopedia-com-lings-contemporary-authors notice} {cite:wikipedia-martin-lings-en Early life}

## Background
He was born at Burnage, Lancashire. He read English at Magdalen College, Oxford, where, according to the Contemporary Authors notice and the Guardian obituary, C. S. Lewis was among his teachers and friends (BA 1932). He taught Anglo-Saxon and Middle English at the University of Kaunas in Lithuania, for four years according to the notice, from 1935 to 1939 according to the obituaries. The English Wikipedia says he discovered the writings of René Guénon and Frithjof Schuon at Oxford. {cite:encyclopedia-com-lings-contemporary-authors notice} {cite:livingislam-lings-obituaries-2005 Guardian} {cite:wikipedia-martin-lings-en Early life}

## The path to Islam
The sources give different accounts of the step. The Times obituary says he embraced Islam in 1939, after a trip to Egypt and meetings with Shadhili Sufis; the Guardian dates it to about 1940, when he went to Egypt; the Contemporary Authors notice places it in Cairo after he arrived in 1939, as assistant to Guénon, and after learning Arabic. The English Wikipedia adds that he met Schuon at Basel in 1938 and joined the branch of the Alawi order that Schuon led. The museum states the range, 1939 to about 1940, and records no more exact date. {cite:livingislam-lings-obituaries-2005 Times and Guardian} {cite:encyclopedia-com-lings-contemporary-authors notice} {cite:wikipedia-martin-lings-en Conversion}

## After embracing Islam
In [[place/cairo|Cairo]] he taught English literature at the university, staging a Shakespeare production each year, and married Lesley Smalley in 1944; he left Egypt in 1952 after anti-British disturbances. He wrote *The Book of Certainty* under the name Abu Bakr Siraj al-Din (1952). Back in England he took a degree in Arabic (1954) and a doctorate (1959) at the School of Oriental and African Studies, on the Sufi Ahmad al-Alawi, published as *A Sufi Saint of the Twentieth Century*; he worked at the British Museum from 1955 and was Keeper of Oriental Printed Books and Manuscripts from 1970 to 1973, then at the British Library. His [[work/muhammad-his-life-based-on-the-earliest-sources|*Muhammad: His Life Based on the Earliest Sources*]] (1983) won prizes from the Pakistani and Egyptian governments; the Times obituary mentions an award from President Mubarak and the Guardian a decoration from Zia ul-Haq. His books on Shakespeare, Qur'anic calligraphy and Sufism followed, and he later became shaykh of a branch of the Shadhiliyya. {cite:livingislam-lings-obituaries-2005 Times and Guardian} {cite:encyclopedia-com-lings-contemporary-authors notice} {cite:wikipedia-martin-lings-en Career}

## Difficulties and reactions
The sources read do not report how his family or friends reacted to his conversion. They record that he left Egypt in 1952 after anti-British disturbances there, ending a stay of some twelve years. He died at his home in Kent ten days after addressing a crowd of about three thousand people at Wembley, according to the Guardian obituary. {cite:wikipedia-martin-lings-en Career} {cite:livingislam-lings-obituaries-2005 Guardian}

## Criticism and debate
The English Wikipedia records a public debate between Lings and the Salafi scholar Abu Bilal Mustafa al-Kanadi over details of his biography of Muhammad, reported in the Saudi Gazette. The sources read do not give the arguments of either side, so the museum records only that the debate took place. He remained a follower of Frithjof Schuon, who has no record in the museum yet; no judgement on that association is made here. {cite:wikipedia-martin-lings-en Controversy}
