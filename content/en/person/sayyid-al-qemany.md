## Summary

Sayyid al-Qemany (1947–2022) was an Egyptian writer and researcher, one of the most prominent secular voices writing in Arabic on the origins of Islamic history, whose contentious readings brought him court cases, media battles and years living outside Egypt.{cite:wikipedia-sayyid-al-qemany}

## Life

He was born in al-Bahariya Oasis, graduated from the Faculty of Arts, and worked in writing and research. After threats and an attack on his home following the storm over his book Rabb hadha al-Zaman he left Egypt and continued writing abroad until his death.{cite:wikipedia-sayyid-al-qemany}

## Books and method

Al-Qemany presents early Islamic history as political history before religious history: Al-Hizb al-Hashimi (The Hashemite Party and the Founding of the Islamic state) reads the rise of the first state as political struggle, and Al-Ustura wal-Turath (Myth and Heritage) examines prophetic hadith critically. The religious mainstream rejected these theses and many scholars replied to them.{cite:wikipedia-sayyid-al-qemany}

## Controversy

He received Egypt's State Encouragement Award in 2009, prompting a press and al-Azhar campaign against the award; he died in the United States in 2022. Admirers call him courageous for opening closed files, opponents call him a distorter of history — the museum records both without a verdict.{cite:wikipedia-sayyid-al-qemany}
