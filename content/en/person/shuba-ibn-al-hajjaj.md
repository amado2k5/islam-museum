## Overview
Shu'ba ibn al-Hajjaj ibn al-Ward al-'Ataki, Abu Bistam, a client of the Azd tribe, was born and raised in Wasit and settled in Basra as a youth, becoming its leading scholar and elder. His precision of memory and extreme caution in transmitting reports earned him the title "commander of the faithful in hadith". Born in 80 or 82 AH, he died in 160 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.1980-1981}

## Teachers and transmission
He saw al-Hasan al-Basri and took questions from him, and transmitted from Anas ibn Malik, al-Hakam ibn 'Utayba, Qatada ibn Di'ama, 'Amr ibn Dinar, Yahya ibn Abi Kathir, Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani and Mansur ibn al-Mu'tamir among many others. Every leading scholar of his generation narrated from him — Sufyan al-Thawri, Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna, Yahya al-Qattan, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Waki' and Hushaym — and even Harun al-Rashid narrated from him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.1980-1981}

## Standing in hadith
Sufyan declared "Shu'ba is the commander of the faithful in hadith"; 'Ali ibn al-Madini estimated his corpus at some two thousand reports, while al-Dhahabi believed he transmitted considerably more, and ranked him alongside al-Awza'i, Ma'mar and al-Thawri in volume of narration. He was exacting about whom he narrated from: when his companions asked him to relate only from trustworthy men, he told them "Rise" — and ended the session. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.1980-1982}

## Self-denial
He told the hadith students "Woe to you, keep to the market — I am a dependent of my two brothers", and never ate a dirham of his own earnings. He rebuked a man who bought a shirt for eight dirhams when a cheaper one plus charity would have been better. He entered Baghdad twice, in the days of al-Mansur and al-Mahdi. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.1981-1982}
