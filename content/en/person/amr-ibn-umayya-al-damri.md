Amr ibn Umayya ibn Khuwaylid al-Damri al-Kinani, a prominent Companion, a horseman of the desert people and one of the Muslims' eyes; he witnessed the Sira's events after his Islam and was among those present at the tragedy of the Well of Ma'una, with al-Mundhir ibn Muhammad in the Muslims' pasture-herd. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When the two saw the birds circling they came and saw their companions struck down in their blood, and fought; al-Mundhir was killed and Amr captured. When he told Amir ibn al-Tufayl he was of Mudar, Amir cut off his forelock and freed him for a slave-vow that had been on his mother; Amr returned to Medina bearing the news of the Reciters' martyrdom to the Prophet. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Amr ibn Umayya was the Prophet's eye and a trusted diplomatic envoy — the one the Prophet chose as ambassador to Ashama the Negus, king of Abyssinia, carrying the letters of the call and the marriage of Umm Habiba; he died in Medina late in Mu'awiya's caliphate. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
