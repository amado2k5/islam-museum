Amr ibn Umayya ibn Khuwaylid al-Damri al-Kinani, a prominent Companion and a horseman of the desert, one of the Muslims' eyes. He witnessed the events of the Sira after his Islam, and he was among those who attended the tragedy of Bi'r Ma'una with al-Mundhir ibn Muhammad, in the Muslims' grazing herd. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When the two saw the birds hovering, they came and saw their companions felled in their blood, and they fought. Al-Mundhir was killed and Amr was taken prisoner. When Amir ibn al-Tufayl learned that he was of Mudar, he cut his forelock and set him free, to fulfil a vow to free a slave that his mother had made. Amr returned to Medina carrying to the Prophet the news of the martyrdom of the reciters. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Amr ibn Umayya was the Prophet's eye and a trusted diplomatic envoy. He was the one the Prophet chose as ambassador to Ashama the Negus, king of Abyssinia, carrying the letters of invitation and the matter of the marriage of Umm Habiba. He died in Medina late in the caliphate of Mu'awiya. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## His mission to Mecca to kill Abu Sufyan
Ibn Hisham relates, in a passage that Ibn Ishaq did not mention among the lists of expeditions, that after the killing of Khubayb ibn Adi the Prophet sent him to Mecca to kill Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, together with Jabbar ibn Sakhr. He was recognised in Mecca and fled. On the way he killed a Qurayshi who had glimpsed their hiding-place in a cave, took down Khubayb's wooden post and threw it into a ravine. On his way back he killed a one-eyed old man of Banu al-Dil, a spy for Quraysh, and took another man prisoner; see [[event/mission-of-amr-ibn-umayya-against-abu-sufyan|the report]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/282} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/283} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/284}
