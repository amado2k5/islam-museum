## Overview
Ibrahim ibn al-Abbas al-Suli, a poet. Al-Mas'udi gives his death as 246 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.492-495}

## On a hadith that never wearies
Al-Mas'udi considers the best thing said about a hadith that never wearies to be his line: "I am never bored except at meeting a narrator of fine hadith, who adds to my learning." {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.255}

## The account of dismissal
Al-Mas'udi relates that an official dismissed him over a conspiracy, and refused his oath, saying that the sultan's oath was not an oath to him, because he was a Rafidi. He replied with words on rejection, brought out the register, and the conspiracy was unravelled. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.492-495}

## From his sayings
He says, as al-Mas'udi relays: "The companions of the sultan are like a people who climbed a mountain and then fell from it: the nearest of them to destruction is the furthest from the climb." {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.492-495}
