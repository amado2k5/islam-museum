## Overview
Sa'd ibn Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Bakr ibn Maslah, Abu al-Sa'adat Sa'd al-Din, of Nablus by origin, Hanafi of Jerusalem, resident of Cairo, known as Ibn al-Dayri, is the forefather of the Khalidiyya family of Palestine; he died in Egypt in 867 AH (1463 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.87}

Born at Jerusalem — his nisba goes to the village of al-Dayr in Marda in the Nablus hills — he moved to Egypt and held the Hanafi judgeship from 842 AH for twenty-five years until his eyesight failed and he retired. His books include al-Habs fi-l-Tuhma; al-Siham al-Mariqa fi Kabid al-Zanadiqa; a six-volume completion of al-Suruji's Sharh al-Hidaya left unfinished; a commentary on the creed ascribed to al-Nasafi; and al-Nu'maniyya, a long poem with prose benefits. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.87}
