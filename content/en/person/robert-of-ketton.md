## Summary
Robert of Ketton was an English monk best known as the first to render the meaning of the Qur'an into Latin, in 1143, and he also worked on Arabic astronomy. Carlo Nallino calls him "Robertus Retinensis" or "Cestrensis", and says he was English by birth, alive in 1141 and 1143, and died at [[place/pamplona|Pamplona]] in Spain. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 59}

## His translation of al-Battani's Zij
Rudolph of Bruges, in his translation of Ptolemy's Planisphaerium (1143 or 1144), wrote that the "most fitting translation" of [[work/al-zij-al-sabi|al-Battani's Zij]] by the industry of "my Robert" had enriched the treasury of Latin. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 58} Nothing of this version survives, so it is not known whether it covered the whole work or a part. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 59}

## His canons for the meridian of London
Robert composed "Canons on the motions of the celestial bodies for the meridian of the city of London" from [[person/al-battani|al-Battani]]'s tables, from which Nallino infers that Robert had the whole of al-Battani's work, chapters and tables, in hand. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 43} {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 59}
