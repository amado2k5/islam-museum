## Summary
Ata ibn al-Sa'ib ibn Zayd al-Thaqafi, Abu Zayd of Kufa, one of the scholars of the Successors, as [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] describes him in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]]. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 70} He narrated from Abd Allah ibn Abi Awfa, [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], his father and others; [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]] and [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]] narrated from him; and late in life he changed and his memory deteriorated. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 70} He is not [[person/ata-ibn-al-saib|Ata ibn al-Sa'ib al-Khashl]], client of Banu Layth, whom al-Baladhuri mentions at the conquest of Balkh.

## Reciter and worshipper
Al-Dhahabi says he was one of the skilled Qur'an reciters, having recited to [[person/abu-abd-al-rahman-al-sulami-al-muqri|Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami]]. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} Ahmad said he completed the Qur'an every night, and Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash said that whenever he saw Ata ibn al-Sa'ib and Dirar ibn Murra he saw the trace of weeping on their cheeks. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash reported Ata saying that [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] stroked his head and prayed for blessing on him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} Al-Dhahabi states that he lived until 136, so that he must have approached a hundred years. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i said of him: he is one of the last survivors. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71}

## Confusion: early and late hearers
Ata is a well-known example in the books of men of a transmitter who became confused (*mukhtalit*). {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 70} [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said: whoever heard from him early has sound reports, and whoever heard from him late has nothing; he also said: trustworthy, trustworthy, a righteous man. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} [[person/al-bukhari|Al-Bukhari]] said: his early hadiths are sound; Yahya ibn Sa'id said that [[person/hammad-ibn-zayd|Hammad ibn Zayd]] heard from him before he changed. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} Abu Hatim said: his place is truthfulness before he became confused; [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]] said: trustworthy in his early hadith, but he changed; the narration of Shu'ba, al-Thawri and Hammad ibn Zayd from him is good. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]], however, said: he is not a proof, and his hadith is weak except what comes through Shu'ba and Sufyan. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71} Al-Dhahabi notes that [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya al-Qattan]] narrated from him, and that he was the earliest-dying of al-Qattan's teachers. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 71}

**Evidence of the confusion.** [[person/ibn-ulayyah|Ibn Ulayya]] said that Ata came to Basra and, when they questioned him, he would be confused, and when they asked "from whom?" he would say: our shaykhs Maysara, Zadhan and so-and-so. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 72} Wuhayb said he asked him how much he had taken from Abida, and Ata said forty hadiths, while [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ali ibn al-Madini]] said he narrated not a word from Abida — which shows he had become confused. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 72} [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Sufyan ibn Uyayna]] said he had heard from him early, then Ata visited them and he heard him relate some of what he had heard before, mixing it up, so he kept clear of him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 72} Among his objectionable reports al-Dhahabi cites one about a man whose false oath was expiated by saying "there is no god but God". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 72} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 73}
