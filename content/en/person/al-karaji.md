## Between arithmetic and engineering
Abu Bakr al-Karaji was a mathematician and engineer whose principal mathematical works were associated with Baghdad; TDV discusses Fakhr al-Mulk's Buyid-era patronage and al-Fakhri {cite:tdv-kereci pp. 277–278}. MacTutor describes algebraic work followed by later engineering interests after leaving the city {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography}. No certain birth or death date is supplied: TDV places death after410/1019, while the biographical sources discuss differing estimates and interpretations {cite:tdv-kereci pp. 277–278} {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography}.

## Name and identity
Al-Karaji and al-Karkhi are not merely alternative spellings: one points toward Karaj, the other toward Baghdad's Karkh district; MacTutor presents the debate without establishing a certain birthplace {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography}. Neither name nor political patronage supplies a religion. Ataie-Ashtiani and Simmons explicitly identify the named author as Muslim, the basis used here {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 p. 762}. Sunni is the separately labelled museum default for the Abbasid/Baghdad milieu, not personal testimony about a legal school; the biographies read name no contrary personal affiliation {cite:tdv-kereci pp. 277–278} {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography}. Buyid patronage is not converted into personal school evidence.

## Arithmetic of expressions
The [[invention/karaji-polynomial-arithmetic|polynomial-arithmetic page]] explains extending addition, subtraction and multiplication to algebraic expressions, with rules for powers and reciprocals {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography}. This is not invention of all algebra or modern notation: division was narrower than general polynomial division, root extraction had restrictions and geometrical demonstrations remained {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography} {cite:tdv-kereci pp. 277–278}. The page preserves differing historical assessments of independence from geometry rather than choosing a slogan in place of that disagreement {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography}.

## Levelling and water routes
His [[invention/karaji-qanat-surveying|levelling and tunnel-alignment manual]] joins levels and geometrical explanation within an older qanat tradition, not invention of every conduit or well {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.762–768}. Folio 26r introduces author-devised levels beside a frame/plumb drawing;29r,42r and 45v were also inspected in a limited reading, not a complete book edition {cite:karaji-inbat-ljs399 ff.26r,29r,42r,45v}. Penn dates the copy1674 against1675 in the 2020 captions: a copy date, not the invention year {cite:karaji-inbat-ljs399 catalogue} {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.766,769}. That article has now been read in full, with its proposed priority and modern water interpretations remaining the authors' assessments, not Darcy's law or a safety standard {cite:karaji-ataie-simmons-2020 pp.761–769}.
