## Summary
Ikrisha bint al-Atrash, as [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] describes her in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']], was one of the women of eloquence, clear speech and forceful argument; he notes that Balaghat al-Nisa' calls her "bint al-Atash". {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.329}

## Before Mu'awiya
She came in to [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]] with a staff in her hand, greeted him as caliph and sat. He said, "So now I have become Commander of the Faithful?" She said, "Yes — since Ali is not alive." {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.329}

## Her speech at Siffin
Mu'awiya reminded her that at [[event/battle-of-siffin|Siffin]] she had stood between the lines with a sword-belt slung on her, preaching, and he recited her speech: it opens with [[passage/quran-5-105|al-Ma'ida 105]], says Paradise is a home its dwellers never leave, so buy it with a home whose bliss does not last; warns against leaving the fight to others, which would undo the bond of Islam; calls that day "the lesser Badr and the second Aqaba"; urges the Muhajirun and Ansar to fight with clear sight; and describes the Syrian army in harsh terms. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.329} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.330} Mu'awiya said, "Were it not for God's decree, and His wish to give us this rule, the two armies would have turned upon me." {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.330}

## The alms
He asked what had moved her. She said God had returned their alms to them and their wealth among them, except by right; they had lost that, so no poor man of theirs was relieved and no broken one set; if it was his doing, then someone like him should not take traitors for helpers or wrongdoers for officials. He said matters weighed on him more pressing than theirs — seas bursting and frontiers breaking open; she replied that God had not prescribed a right for them that harmed others. He said, "Alas, people of Iraq — Ibn Abi Talib roused you, and you cannot be withstood," and ordered her alms returned, gave her redress and sent her back with honour. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.330}

## The sources of the story
The stories of these 'women who came before Mu'awiya' are told in the adab literature; [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] takes them from Ibn Tayfur's Balaghat al-Nisa', al-Iqd al-Farid, Subh al-A'sha, Ibn Asakir's history and Nihayat al-Arab. They voice the positions their speakers took in the civil war, and the museum gives them as attributed reports, without a verdict. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.330}

## For young visitors
Ikrisha was a brave woman who asked the caliph Mu'awiya to let her people's alms go to their own poor, and he agreed.
