## Summary
The Prophet of Islam, Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib (c. 570–632 CE). The source approaches him here through his shama'il, his physical and moral qualities: the beauty of his form and the perfection of his character, as his contemporaries described them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.479-487}.

## The beauty of his form
[[person/umm-mabad|Umm Ma'bad al-Khuza'iyya]] described the Prophet to her husband when he passed her tent as an emigrant. She said: bright and radiant, with a clear face and a fine form. No corpulence marred him and no smallness of head belittled him. In his eyes there was a deep blackness, in his eyelashes length, in his voice a huskiness, and in his neck a gleam. When he was silent, dignity crowned him; when he spoke, splendour did. He was the most handsome and most striking of people from afar, and the finest and sweetest from near {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.479}.

Ali ibn Abi Talib described him: he was neither overly tall nor short, but of middle height among the people. His hair was curly, his body well-proportioned, his complexion white with a reddish tinge, his eyes deep black, and his eyelashes long. He was broad at the joints and across the upper back. Between his shoulders was the seal of prophethood, for he is the Seal of the Prophets. He was the most generous of people with his hand, the most courageous in heart, the most truthful in speech, and the most faithful to his word. Whoever saw him suddenly stood in awe of him, and whoever knew him closely loved him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.479-480; jami-al-tirmidhi}.

Anas ibn Malik said: he was radiant in complexion, neither pale-white nor dark. I never touched silk or brocade softer than his palm, and I never smelled musk or anything more fragrant than his scent {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.480, 483; sahih-al-bukhari}. Al-Bara' said: he was the most handsome of people in face and the finest of them in character {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.481; sahih-al-bukhari}.

## The perfection of the soul and the nobility of character
He was distinguished by eloquence of tongue and power of speech. He was given comprehensive words and singled out for remarkable wisdom, and he knew the tongues of the Arabs, addressing each tribe in its own language {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.484}. Forbearance, pardon when he had the power to punish, and patience under hardship were his qualities. A'isha said: he was never offered a choice between two matters without choosing the easier, so long as it was not a sin; and he never took revenge for himself, unless the sanctity of God was violated, in which case he avenged it for God {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.484; sahih-al-bukhari}. He was the most generous of people, and most generous in Ramadan, when Jibril met him to study the Qur'an with him. He was more generous with good than the driving wind, and he was never asked for anything and said no {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.484; sahih-al-bukhari}. He was the bravest of people. Ali said: when battle grew fierce and eyes reddened, we sheltered behind him, and none was nearer the enemy than he {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.484}. He was the most modest of people, more modest than a virgin in her chamber, and when he disliked something it was visible in his face {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.485; sahih-al-bukhari}. He was the most just, the most chaste, the most truthful in speech and the greatest in trustworthiness. His interlocutors and even his enemies acknowledged this of him, and before his prophethood he was called al-Amin, the Trustworthy {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.485}. He was the humblest of people: he would mend his own sandal, sew his own garment, work with his hands in his house and serve himself, and he disliked standing out above his companions {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.485-486}.

[[person/hind-ibn-abi-hala|Hind ibn Abi Hala]] transmitted of him that he was often troubled in thought and constantly reflective, long in silence, and did not speak except when needed. Most of his laughter was a smile, and he neither sat nor stood without remembrance of God {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.486}. Al-Mubarakpuri concluded that these traits are only brief marks of his perfection, and that the full reality of the glories he possessed cannot be grasped. His Lord had already praised his character in [[passage/quran-68-4|al-Qalam 4]], describing him as being of a tremendous character {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.487}.

## For young visitors
People described the Prophet as beautiful in appearance and beautiful in character: forbearing, generous, brave, humble and trustworthy. He never took revenge for himself, and he never said "no" to someone who asked. The Qur'an later addressed him as being of a tremendous character.

## Noble lineage (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 48–49)
*The Sealed Nectar* divides the Prophet's lineage into three parts. The first part, on which the biographers and genealogists agree, runs up to Adnan: Muhammad ibn [[person/abdullah-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|Abdullah]] ibn [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] ibn [[person/hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]] ibn [[person/abd-manaf-ibn-qusayy|Abd Manaf]] ibn [[person/qusayy-ibn-kilab|Qusayy]] ibn Kilab ibn Murra ibn Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy ibn Ghalib ibn [[person/fihr-ibn-malik|Fihr]] ibn Malik ibn al-Nadr ibn Kinana ibn Khuzayma ibn Mudrika ibn Ilyas ibn Mudar ibn Nizar ibn Ma'add ibn Adnan. The second part, above Adnan up to Ibrahim, is disputed. The third part, above Ibrahim up to Adam, contains what is not sound {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.48} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/1-4}. The details are in [[concept/nasab-al-nabi|the Prophet's lineage and its three parts]], and his family is in [[group/banu-hashim|Banu Hashim]]. It is related that the Prophet said: "I am the son of the two sacrificed ones," meaning Isma'il and his father Abdullah {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/151-155}.

## Birth and early upbringing (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 54–58)
He was born in the valley of Banu Hashim in Mecca, on the morning of Monday, 9 Rabi' al-Awwal, in the first year of the event of the Elephant. The author equates this with 20 or 22 April 571 CE ([[event/year-of-the-elephant-and-birth|the birth]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.54} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/62} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 1/38-39}. His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib named him Muhammad and had him circumcised on his seventh day {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.54} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/159-160}. He was nursed first by [[person/thuwayba|Thuwayba]] and then by [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima al-Sa'diyya]] in the desert of [[group/banu-sad-ibn-bakr|Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr]]. His foster-brothers were there, along with his cousin [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-al-harith|Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith]] and his uncle [[person/hamza|Hamza]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55} {cite:zad-al-maad 1/19}. In his fourth or fifth year the [[event/shaqq-al-sadr|incident of the opening of his chest]] took place, and Halima returned him to his mother {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.56-57} {cite:sahih-muslim 1/92}. When he reached six, his mother died at al-Abwa' on the return from Yathrib ([[event/death-of-amina|the death of Amina]]). His grandfather [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] took charge of him and was tender toward him. The grandfather died when he was eight years, two months and ten days old, and his uncle [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]] took him in ([[event/death-of-abd-al-muttalib|the death of Abd al-Muttalib]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.57-58} {cite:talqih-fuhum-ahl-al-athr p.7}.

## Youth before prophethood (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 58–61)
When he was twelve, his uncle Abu Talib took him on a trading journey to Syria. At Busra he met the monk [[person/bahira|Bahira]], who recognized him by his description and advised that he be sent back ([[event/bahira-encounter|the meeting with Bahira]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.58-59} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/180-183}. At fifteen he took part in the [[event/harb-al-fijar|War of the Fijar]], gathering arrows for his uncles. After it he attended the [[event/hilf-al-fudul|Pact of al-Fudul]] in the house of Ibn Jud'an, and he said after the mission began that if he were called to it in Islam he would answer {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.59-60} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/184-187} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/133-135}. He had no particular occupation in his early youth, though the accounts agree that he tended sheep, in Banu Sa'd and in Mecca for its people for small wages {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.60} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/166} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p.52}. At twenty-five he went out as a trader to Syria with the goods of [[person/khadija|Khadija]] ([[event/trade-journey-to-syria-for-khadija|the Syria journey]]), and he married her two months after his return ([[event/marriage-to-khadija|the marriage]]). All his children were from her except Ibrahim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.60-61} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/187-191}. When he was thirty-five, the Quraysh rebuilt the Ka'ba, and they made him the arbiter in placing the Black Stone ([[event/rebuilding-of-the-kaaba|the rebuilding of the Ka'ba]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.61-62} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/192-197}.

## His character before prophethood in general (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 62–64)
The author sums up his image before prophethood. In his upbringing he combined the best qualities found among people. He had sound thought and a firm judgement of a high order. He used his long silence to deepen his reflection and to search out the truth. With his fertile mind and pure nature he looked at the affairs of his people, rejected their superstitions and kept away from them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.62-63}. He did not drink wine, did not eat what was sacrificed on the idol stones, and did not attend the festivals or celebrations of the idols. From his earliest upbringing he recoiled from these deities, so that nothing was more hateful to him, and he could not bear to hear oaths sworn by al-Lat and al-Uzza. The author supports this from his conversation with Bahira {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.63} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/128}.

He relates from Ibn al-Athir a report that the Prophet had intended none of the things the people of ignorance did except twice, and God prevented each time. Once he wished to spend a night in Mecca as young men do, and he heard the sound of a lute at a wedding and sat down to listen, and God struck his ear so that he fell asleep. The same happened on another night. After that he never intended any evil. The author notes that the report's authenticity was disputed: al-Hakim and al-Dhahabi authenticated it, while Ibn Kathir weakened it {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.63} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya 2/287}. Among his people he was distinguished by sweet qualities and noble character: the most honourable in manliness, the finest in disposition, the most respected in giving protection, the greatest in forbearance, the truest in speech, the gentlest in temperament, the purest in soul, the most generous in goodness, the most dutiful in deeds, the most faithful to covenants, and the most trustworthy. His people named him [[concept/al-amin|al-Amin]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.63-64}. As Khadija said, he bore the burdens of others, earned for the destitute, hosted guests and helped in the calamities of truth {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.64} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/3}.

## Under the shade of prophethood: the beginning of revelation (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 65–73)
As he approached forty, solitude became dear to him. For three years before his commission he would withdraw to the cave of [[place/ghar-hira|Hira]] during the month of Ramadan {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.65} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 1/47}. When he completed forty years, true dreams came to him for six months. Then the angel came to him in Ramadan with the first verses of Surat al-Alaq ([[event/first-revelation|the beginning of revelation]]). The author judges that this was the night of Monday, 21 Ramadan, corresponding to 10 August 610 CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.66-67} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/2-3}. He returned to [[person/khadija|Khadija]], who reassured him, and she took him to [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.67}. Then, in the author's preferred view, the revelation paused for some days. Then the first verses of Surat al-Muddaththir came down, with the command to stand up and warn ([[event/second-revelation-al-muddaththir|the descent of al-Muddaththir]]). He stood up and remained standing in the call for more than twenty years {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.69-70} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.73}. The author digresses on the [[concept/maratib-al-wahy|stages of revelation]], drawing on Ibn al-Qayyim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.70} {cite:zad-al-maad 1/18}.

## The secret call (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 74–77)
The author divides his call into a Meccan phase and a Medinan phase. The first stage of the Meccan phase was three years of secret call ([[concept/marahil-al-dawa|stages of the call]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.74}. He offered Islam first to those closest to him. On the first day, [[person/khadija|Khadija]], [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd]], [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] embraced Islam. Then came those whom Abu Bakr called to it, and then a group whom Ibn Hisham counted at more than forty ([[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|the secret call]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.75-76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. He would gather with them and guide them in secret, and they would pray in the mountain passes while concealed ([[concept/al-salat-qabl-al-isra|the prayer before the Night Journey]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.76-77} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/247}.

## For Sprouts
The Prophet of Islam. Muslims believe the Qur'an was revealed to him, and he founded the first Muslim community in Medina.

## For Explorers
Muhammad, the Prophet of Islam, was born in Mecca around the year 570. Muslims believe the Qur'an was revealed to him. He lived in Mecca, then moved to Medina, where the first Muslim community took shape. He died in 632. People in Mecca knew him as trustworthy before revelation began.

## For Scholars
Muhammad ibn Abdullah was born in Mecca around 570 CE and died in 632 CE. Muslims believe he is the Prophet to whom the Qur'an was revealed. The people of Mecca knew him for honesty and trust before his mission, and he traded and worked among his people. Revelation came when he was forty, and he called Quraysh to the oneness of God; the harm he suffered grew until he migrated to Medina in 622, where he founded the first Muslim community. His life is told in the sira and the hadith, and the museum gives each report with its source and its degree of reliability.
