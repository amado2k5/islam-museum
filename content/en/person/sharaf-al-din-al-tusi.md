## Summary

Sharaf al-Din al-Muzaffar al-Tusi was a twelfth-century mathematician and astronomer associated with teaching, cubic equations and the [[invention/tusi-linear-astrolabe|linear astrolabe]]. He is not Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, and a shared geographical surname does not transfer Maragha observatory employment to him. Ahmet Ozel emphasizes that the biographical evidence is sparse and scattered through accounts of other scholars {cite:tdv-sharaf-tusi-ozel biography-and-works}.

## A life recovered through teaching

The sources summarized in the biography place him in Damascus, Aleppo and Mosul, with a visit to Hamadan. They connect him with Abu al-Fadl and Kamal al-Din ibn Yunus, who reported studying Euclid's Elements and Ptolemy's Almagest with him. Ibn Yunus's testimony places his teacher in Mosul before the1180 date reported in the article. These fragments do not make a continuous daily itinerary or identify an exact birthplace within the Tus region {cite:tdv-sharaf-tusi-ozel biography} {cite:sharaf-tusi-mactutor biography}.

MacTutor presents an approximate1135 birth and1213 death, whereas Ozel warns that a later claim of being alive in1209 arose from misreading an erased manuscript date. We therefore do not impose an exact death year or turn1209 into the staff's manufacture date. Chronological uncertainty remains visible instead of becoming a falsely precise lifespan {cite:sharaf-tusi-mactutor chronology} {cite:tdv-sharaf-tusi-ozel biography}.

## Algebra and geometry

## The staff and its reception

The biographies credit him with the linear astrolabe and describe Ibn Yunus's modification and al-Marrakushi's exposition.
