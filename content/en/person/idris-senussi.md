# Idris of Libya

## Summary

Idris (Muhammad Idris bin Muhammad al-Mahdi as-Senussi; 12 March 1889 – 25 May 1983) was King of Libya from 24 December 1951 until his overthrow on 1 September 1969, and before that head of the Senussi order and Emir of Cyrenaica and Tripolitania.{cite:wikipedia-idris-senussi-en}

## Early life

He was born at Jaghbub, the headquarters of the Senussi movement — the English and French texts give 12 March 1889, while some sources, and the Arabic article, give 1890 — a son of Muhammad al-Mahdi as-Senussi and a grandson of Muhammad ibn Ali as-Senussi, founder of the Senussi Sufi order.{cite:wikipedia-idris-senussi-en}

## Head of the order

In 1916 he succeeded his cousin Ahmed Sharif as chief of the Senussi order, ended the order's attacks on British forces in Egypt, and negotiated the settlements of al-Zuwaytina and Acroma with Italy; in 1920 the Accord of al-Rajma recognised him as Emir of Cyrenaica, and in 1922 the Tripolitanians offered him their emirate too.{cite:wikipedia-idris-senussi-en} As Italy reconquered Libya he withdrew into long exile in Egypt.{cite:wikipedia-idris-senussi-en}

## King

After the Second World War the United Nations resolved on Libyan independence, and Idris was proclaimed king of the federal United Kingdom of Libya on 24 December 1951.{cite:wikipedia-idris-senussi-en} He banned political parties, made the kingdom a unitary state in 1963, and presided over the first oil boom. While he was in Turkey for medical treatment, army officers under Muammar Gaddafi deposed him on 1 September 1969.{cite:wikipedia-idris-senussi-en}

## Death

He lived in exile in Cairo, where he died on 25 May 1983; he was buried in Al-Baqi Cemetery in Medina.{cite:wikipedia-idris-senussi-en}
