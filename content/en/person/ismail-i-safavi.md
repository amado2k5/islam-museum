## Summary
Shah Ismail, founder of [[polity/safavid-iran|Safavid Iran]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 177}.

## Origins in al-Salabi
Al-Salabi traces the Safavids to Shaykh [[person/safi-al-din-ardabili|Safi al-Din al-Ardabili]] (650–735 AH), Ismail's great ancestor, whose Sufi propagandists spread the call through Iran, Ottoman provinces, Iraq and Syria, propagating descent from Ali ibn Abi Talib; the book claims Safi al-Din practised dissimulation, appearing a Shafi'i {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 177-178}.

## Chaldiran
Contending with [[person/selim-i-ottoman|Selim I]], Ismail received Selim's embassy bearing a dervish's rag, beads, bowl and staff — a reminder of his lineage. When the shah asked for truce Selim dishonoured and killed his envoy, then crushed him at [[event/battle-chaldiran-920|Chaldiran]] in August 1514; Ismail fled and Selim entered his capital Tabriz, sequestering his treasure and the Qizilbash, making it his base of operations {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 181}.

## After the defeat
The book says defeat made Ismail readier to league with the Portuguese: he confirmed their hold on Hormuz in exchange for help against Bahrain and Qatif, in a project said to divide the Arab East into spheres {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 182-183}.
