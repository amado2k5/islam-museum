## Summary
Muniba Mazari is a Pakistani artist, television anchor and motivational speaker whom admirers call the 'Iron Lady of Pakistan'. Paralysed from the waist down after a car crash in 2007, she rebuilt her life around painting, broadcasting and public speaking, becoming the first wheelchair-using model in Pakistan and, in 2015, a UN Women Goodwill Ambassador. {cite:wikipedia-muniba-mazari}

## Early life and the accident
Born in 1987 in Rahim Yar Khan, in Pakistan's Punjab province, she was married young and, at around twenty-one, was seriously injured when the car she was travelling in left the road. She sustained multiple spinal fractures and spent two years in hospital, where doctors told her she would not walk again. She has described this period — she began painting from her hospital bed — as the beginning of her second life. {cite:wikipedia-muniba-mazari}

## Art and media work
Painting became both therapy and profession. Working in acrylics, she developed a body of portrait work that she has exhibited and sold, and she presents herself as a 'wheelchair-bound artist' rather than hiding the chair. She moved into broadcasting as an anchor for Pakistan Television, and in 2015 became the first wheelchair-using model in the country when she appeared in a campaign for the fashion brand Toni & Guy / Toni&Guy-linked work in Pakistan, later also modelling for other campaigns. {cite:wikipedia-muniba-mazari}

## Public speaking and advocacy
Her TEDx and conference talks — built around gratitude, acceptance and resisting self-pity — reached a global audience through social media and made her one of Pakistan's best-known speakers. Appointed a UN Women National Goodwill Ambassador for Pakistan in 2015, she campaigns for gender equality, the inclusion of persons with disabilities, and the education and rights of women and girls. She was named in the BBC's 100 Women list in 2015 and in Forbes' 30 Under 30 for Asia in 2016. {cite:wikipedia-muniba-mazari}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles her as a figure of resilience whose public advocacy links disability rights to the wider question of women's place in Pakistani public life. Admirers present her story as proof that a life can be rebuilt after catastrophe; her own framing is plainer — that she is a woman who paints, speaks and works, and happens to do it from a wheelchair. {cite:muslim500-muniba-mazari}
