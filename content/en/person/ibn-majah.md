## Overview
Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Qazwini, hadith scholar of Qazvin, author of the [[work/sunan-ibn-majah|Sunan]]. Most later scholars count it the sixth of the six books. He died in 273 AH. {cite:sunan-ibn-majah}

## The journey and the book
He travelled in search of hadith to Iraq, the Hijaz, Syria and Egypt. His book is distinguished by hadiths on asceticism and tribulations, and by material he alone transmits, not found in the two Sahihs. {cite:sunan-ibn-majah}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam names Ibn Majah, who died in 886, as a Sunni scholar of hadith born in Qazvin in Iran. His Kitab al-sunan contains roughly four thousand sayings and ranks among the six canonical Sunni hadith collections. The Dictionary notes that some admire it greatly while others fault it for including numerous doubtful, or weak, reports. It dates the book's acceptance as an authoritative text to around the start of the 1100s, with general recognition coming only about a hundred years afterwards; al-Nawawi, who died in 1277, and Ibn Khaldun, who died in 1405, among others, judged it not trustworthy enough to carry authority. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e949}
