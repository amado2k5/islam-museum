## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Ziyad, Abu Abd Allah al-Iskandarani, the Maliki jurist known as Ibn al-Mawwaz. Ibn Asakir describes him as an author on the school of [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik ibn Anas]] who came to Damascus {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 197}. Quoting Ibn Yunus, he adds that Ibn al-Mawwaz was a client of Qays and had written works of law on Malik's school {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 198}.

## Life and legal training
The hafiz Abu al-Abbas al-Walid ibn Bakr al-Andalusi lists him in his book naming the jurists among Malik's companions. He says that Ibn al-Mawwaz lived in Alexandria, studied law under Ibn al-Majishun and Ibn Abd al-Hakam, relied on [[person/asbagh-ibn-al-faraj|Asbagh]], stood higher than Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam and Muhammad ibn Sahnun, and followed the manner of Muhammad ibn Abdus in elaborating cases {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 197}. Al-Walid adds that the practice in Egypt rests on Ibn al-Mawwaz's opinion, while in Qayrawan and al-Andalus it rests on that of [[person/sahnun-ibn-said|Sahnun]]; that he sometimes followed Asbagh ibn al-Faraj in faulting Ibn al-Qasim and said so openly in his books; and that he belongs with Asbagh's circle because his books turn on Asbagh {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 198}.

Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi, in his book on the generations of Malik's jurists, says that he was in Alexandria, studied under Ibn al-Majishun and Ibn Abd al-Hakam and relied on Asbagh, then fled from Alexandria to Syria and stayed in one of its forts until he died, and that Egypt relies on his word {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 198}. The phrase printed in al-Shirazi's text just before this, "he was sought in the litter", printed in Arabic as «وطلب في المحفة», is unclear in the text held here, so it is not interpreted.

## Coming to Damascus
Ibn Asakir records that he came to Damascus with Ahmad ibn Tulun in 269 AH, when [[person/ahmad-ibn-tulun|Ibn Tulun]] came to depose al-Muwaffaq {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 197}. His son Abu al-Qasim Bakr ibn Muhammad ibn Ibrahim and Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Abi Matar al-Iskandarani narrated from him {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 197}.

## Death and the differing reports
Ibn Asakir quotes Ibn Yunus: Ali ibn Abi Matar al-Iskandarani wrote to him that Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Mawwaz died in Damascus in 269 AH, and Ibn Abi Matar was the last to narrate from him {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 198}. That year corresponds to 882 or 883 CE by the museum's derived conversion, close to the year on the card. Al-Shirazi, in the same entry, says he died in 281 AH {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 51 p. 198}. The entry does not decide between the two reports.
