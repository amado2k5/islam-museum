## Summary

Habash al-Hasib al-Marwazi was an astronomer and mathematician from Marw whose career connects Baghdad and later Samarra. Francois Charette explicitly identifies him as a Muslim astronomer. ## A changing scientific programme

Charette interprets four periods: formation in Baghdad around815–829 within Indian and Persian traditions, contact with the mumtahan programme around829–834, independent observations after Ma'mun's death, and the later Samarra period. These are interpretive phases, not certain administrative boundaries. Proximity to the observational group does not prove formal membership: Habash does not present himself as one of its protagonists {cite:habash-charette-bea four career periods and mumtahan discussion}.

He encountered traditions associated with al-Fazari and al-Khwarizmi and composed early non-Ptolemaic zijs, later becoming known for a Ptolemaic handbook with revised observations, parameters and computation. TDV records Ibn Yunus criticizing some planetary latitudes and Ibn Iraq and al-Biruni studying the work. Influence is documented alongside criticism; it does not mean every value remained correct or every method travelled unchanged {cite:habash-charette-bea zijs and reception} {cite:tdv-habash-fazlioglu reception}.

## Instruments and writings

Charette discusses a melon-shaped astrolabe using azimuthal equidistant mapping, probably invented by Habash; probability is not certainty. He also describes a universal stellar timekeeping plate surviving in a unique incomplete witness and David King's proposed relationship with a later European instrument. The proposal remains attributed, not converted into proven transmission {cite:habash-charette-bea instrumentation discussion}.

Scholarship assigns treatises on the celestial globe, spherical astrolabe and armillary sphere, and the Book of Bodies and Distances, which recounts scientific activity under Ma'mun, including a meridian-degree measurement. Reporting an expedition does not make Habash its leader or author of every measurement. Lost works on sundials, crescent visibility, planispheric astrolabes and critical observational reports are also listed; titles are not inspected contents {cite:habash-charette-bea other works} {cite:tdv-habash-fazlioglu works6–8 and final list}.
