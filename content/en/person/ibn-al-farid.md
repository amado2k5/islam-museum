## Overview
Ibn al-Farid, the poet of divine love, titled Sultan of the Lovers, Proof of the People of Unity and Bearer of the Poets' Banner; he grew up in Egypt devoting himself to worship, roaming Mount Muqattam and the ruins of Qarafa, then cut himself off at Mecca some fifteen years, and returned to teach in the khitaba corner of al-Azhar mosque — al-Malik al-Kamil himself would come down to visit him. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 149-153}

## His diwan
His is the famous diwan acknowledged by friend and foe alike, containing the Ta'iyya ode whose commentary many undertook — al-Siraj al-Hindi, al-Shams al-Bisati, al-Jalal al-Qazwini, al-Farghani, al-Qashani and al-Qaysari; his finest verse was said to be the Fa'iyya "Qalbi yuhaddithuni bi-annaka mutlifi". He died in Egypt and was buried at al-Muqattam. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 149-153}
