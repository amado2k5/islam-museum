## Summary
Iman Mohamed Abdulmajid, known simply as Iman, is a Somali-American model, actress and entrepreneur born in Mogadishu in 1955. One of the defining faces of late twentieth-century fashion, she later founded a pioneering cosmetics company, and The Muslim 500 lists her among influential Muslim cultural figures. {cite:muslim500-iman} {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## Early life and discovery
The daughter of a Somali diplomat and a physician mother, she was educated in Egypt and at the University of Nairobi, where she was studying political science when photographer Peter Beard discovered her in 1975 and brought her to New York. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## Modelling career
She became a muse to designers including Yves Saint Laurent, Calvin Klein, Donna Karan and Versace — Saint Laurent devoted an entire couture collection to her — and appeared on covers of Vogue and Harper's Bazaar through the late 1970s and 1980s, breaking barriers for models of colour. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## Business and philanthropy
In 1994 she founded IMAN Cosmetics, among the first major lines designed for women of colour, later expanding into fashion and home brands. She has served as a CARE Global Advocate, worked with Save the Children and UNICEF on Somalia and refugee causes, and keeps the legacy of her late husband David Bowie, whom she married in 1992. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## Assessment
Iman is credited with widening fashion's idea of beauty and with building an early inclusive beauty business; commentary also notes her advocacy for African and Somali causes. The museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-iman} {cite:muslim500-iman}


## IMAN Cosmetics
IMAN Cosmetics was among the first mainstream beauty lines formulated for women of colour, building a lasting niche before the industry's later 'inclusive' turn; she has described founding it after repeatedly being told a market for darker skin tones did not exist. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## Advocacy
Her humanitarian work spans decades: CARE Global Advocate for refugee and maternal-health programmes, front-line campaigning with Save the Children and UNICEF on Somalia, and public advocacy for diversity on runways and in casting — she co-founded the Diversity Coalition with fellow models. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## Bowie and legacy
Married to David Bowie from 1992 until his death in 2016, she has carefully stewarded both her business legacy and his estate, appearing publicly to accept honours on his behalf while keeping her own philanthropic profile distinct. {cite:wikipedia-iman} {cite:muslim500-iman}

## Career spans
Her modelling years ran from a 1975 debut through covers and campaigns for nearly every major house, and her 1989 Vogue cover — a rare solo cover for a model of African descent at the time — is regularly cited in histories of fashion's colour line. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## IMAN Cosmetics
Founded in 1994, her cosmetics company addressed a documented gap — products for women of colour ignored by mainstream lines — and grew into a multimillion-dollar brand distributed across the United States and abroad. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## Philanthropy and advocacy
She has worked with the Children's Defense Fund, Keep a Child Alive and CARE — serving as CARE's first global advocate — and used her platform for refugee and Somali causes across decades. {cite:wikipedia-iman} {cite:muslim500-iman}

## Modelling's colour line
Iman's career in modelling — beginning with her 1975 discovery by photographer Peter Beard and running through covers and campaigns for nearly every major house — unfolded in an era when high fashion's runways and editorial pages were almost uniformly white. Her presence, sustained across two decades and culminating in the 1989 Vogue cover, is regularly cited in histories of the industry's racial order as one of the landmarks of its slow change. {cite:wikipedia-iman}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists her among the most influential Muslims in arts and entertainment, citing her modelling career, her cosmetics company's landmark role in addressing the market's neglect of women of colour, and her decades of humanitarian advocacy on refugee and Somali causes. {cite:muslim500-iman}
