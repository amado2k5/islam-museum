## Brief
Hind bint Utba — daughter of the Meccan elder [[person/utba-ibn-rabia|Utba]] and wife of [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] — is the sira's most vivid figure of Meccan defiance. She converted at the conquest and became the mother of the first Umayyad caliph.

## Life
At [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] the sources show her at the front of the Meccan line, urging the fighters on with song. After the battle the sira attributes to her the mutilation of [[person/hamza|Hamza]]'s body — told in the sources with varying detail {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

At the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest of Mecca]] she converted. The tradition preserves the women's pledge as a genuine exchange: Hind answering the Prophet's conditions with pointed retorts before giving her word {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

Her son [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] would rule the first Islamic empire.

## Legacy
Later tradition named her "eater of the liver" for Uhud — the museum records the act as the sources tell it, and the conversion they equally record. She is one of the clearest cases in the sira of a person crossing the war's whole arc.

## For young visitors
Hind fought fiercely against the Muslims, then made peace with them — and her son became the ruler of the whole Islamic world.
