## Summary
Gulchehra Hoja is a Uyghur-American journalist born in Urumqi in 1973, a leading reporter on the Uyghur people for Radio Free Asia; The Muslim 500 lists her among influential media figures. {cite:muslim500-gulchehra-hoja} {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}

## Early career
Trained in Urumqi, she became a presenter on Xinjiang Television, where she created and hosted China's first Uyghur-language children's programme, before her growing unease with state-media constraints pushed her to leave. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}

## Exile and RFA
She emigrated to the United States in 2001 and joined Radio Free Asia's Uyghur service, where her reporting — including the first detailed international accounts of the internment camps established in Xinjiang from 2017 — brought the Uyghur crisis to world attention. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}

## Cost
More than two dozen of her relatives in Xinjiang were detained in connection with her work, a reprisal documented by press-freedom organisations; she has testified before the US Congress and received international journalism honours. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}

## Assessment
She is regarded as a defining journalistic voice on the Uyghur issue and an example of reporting conducted at personal cost; commentary on the politics of her employer is reported attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja} {cite:muslim500-gulchehra-hoja}


## RFA Uyghur service
At Radio Free Asia she anchors Uyghur-language programmes that for two decades have been among the few free-media sources reporting on Xinjiang — work press-freedom organisations describe as uniquely exposed and consequential. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}

## Family and reprisal
Her reporting has come at the cost her colleagues and press organisations document publicly: family members in Xinjiang detained in reprisal, a fact she has testified to in Washington and described in her memoir. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}

## A Stone Is Most Precious Where It Belongs
Her 2023 memoir recounts her path from Urumqi television star to exile journalist, and was shortlisted for awards and reviewed as a landmark account of Uyghur life under pressure. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja} {cite:muslim500-gulchehra-hoja}

## Origins
Born in Urumqi in 1973, she began as a celebrated television presenter in Xinjiang before relocating to the United States in 2001 and joining Radio Free Asia's Uyghur service — a path that took her from state broadcasting to free-media journalism. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}

## The memoir
Her 2023 memoir A Stone Is Most Precious Where It Belongs recounts her Uyghur childhood, her television career and her exile — a book shortlisted for awards and reviewed as a landmark account of Uyghur life under pressure. {cite:wikipedia-gulchehra-hoja}
