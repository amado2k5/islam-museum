## Summary
Abu 'Abdallah Muhammad ibn Khafif al-Shirazi, shaykh of the province of Fars and a man of states and stations; al-Sulami said he is today the shaykh of shaykhs and the era's landmark — none older in years nor more complete in state remained to the people; he held fast to Book and Sunna, a faqih on al-Shafi'i's madhhab; he was of amirs' sons yet renounced the world until he said: I used to gather rags from the middens, wash them and mend from them what I wore. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 76-77}

## His teacher
He travelled to Shaykh Abu'l-Hasan al-Ash'ari and took from him — among his noblest pupils; Ahmad ibn Yahya said: I see tasawwuf sealed with Abu 'Abdallah Ibn Khafif; al-Subki called him shaykh of shaykhs, firm of foot in knowledge and religion; he prayed over him nearly a hundred times. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 76-77}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 660 of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]]. He is the last of the Sufis treated before the book turns to the ascetics of Abu Nu'aym's own town, Isfahan. Abu Nu'aym says that he met the great masters and kept company with [[person/ruwaym-ibn-ahmad|Ruwaym]], Abu al-Abbas ibn Ata, Tahir al-Maqdisi and Abu Amr al-Dimashqi, that he was the shaykh of his time in spiritual state and in knowledge, and that he died in 371 AH. Abu Nu'aym was his contemporary and transmits from him by licence and by letter. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/385}

**A hadith Abu Nu'aym flags.** From what Ibn Khafif wrote to him he quotes a hadith with a chain that runs through Shu'ba. He then says that its reliable transmitters could not have carried such a report, suggests that Ibn Khafif's own teacher had one hadith slip into another, and gives the other version, from Ibn Mas'ud, which he takes to be the original. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/385-386}

**Definitions.** The notice gathers his answers on the terms of the Sufis. Intoxication is the boiling of the heart when the Beloved is remembered. Fear is the heart's unrest at what it knows of the might of the One worshipped. Discipline is breaking the self through service and keeping it from slackness. Piety is avoiding whatever takes one far from God. Trust is being satisfied with God's guarantee and dropping all suspicion of His decree. Asceticism is the heart's letting go of means and the hand's shaking off possessions. Contentment is being satisfied with bare sufficiency. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/386}

**On remembrance.** Asked about remembrance of God, he said that the One remembered is one, remembrance varies, and the hearts of those who remember stand at different levels. He divided it into outward remembrance, which is saying "there is no god but God", praise and recitation of the Qur'an, and inward remembrance, which is rousing the heart to knowledge of God, His names, attributes and acts. He then ranked remembrance by the state of the one who remembers: the fearful according to the threat, the hopeful according to the promise, the watchful according to their knowledge that God sees them, and those who trust according to what has been shown them of His sufficiency. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/386-387}
