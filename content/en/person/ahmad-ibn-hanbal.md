## Summary
Imam Ahmad authored no book of fiqh; the most he wrote on it was a letter on prayer, written to an imam who prayed behind him and erred in his prayer {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 124}. He disliked writing down books containing branching questions and opinion, holding that engaging with transmitted reports came first {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 123}. His companions wrote down more than thirty volumes of his words and fatwas {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 124}.

## The Musnad
The book mentions that the Musnad contains thirty thousand hadiths {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 104}. It transmits from his son Abdullah a report saying his father gathered it from more than seven hundred thousand hadiths, and that whatever the Muslims differed on of the Messenger of God's hadith is referred back to it {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 104}. The book's margin discusses the attribution of this report to him {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 104}.

## The creeds
In its first division the book presents the creeds transmitted from Imam Ahmad {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 47}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy recounts that Ahmad ibn Hanbal prayed for al-Shafi'i after every prayer (needs_source: a report the sheikh gives) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:18:00}. He recounts that critics faulted al-Shafi'i for visiting Ahmad often, and that al-Shafi'i answered that the favour lies in both visits {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:18:30}.

He recounts that when Ahmad fell ill, al-Shafi'i feared for Ahmad's life and for the knowledge that would be lost with him; al-Shafi'i himself fell ill from that fear, and then Ahmad forced himself to go and visit him (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:19:00}.

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds, as his own opinion, that the community should study its own history, and he names Ahmad ibn Hanbal's ordeal among the matters to be studied; the word is unclear in the automatic transcript (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 01:16:00}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh lists Ahmad ibn Hanbal among great orphans, and relates that al-Shafi'i used to visit him and be visited by him; that al-Shafi'i fell ill from worry when Ahmad fell ill, and when Ahmad heard of it he forced himself to visit him, and al-Shafi'i recited verses saying the beloved fell ill and he visited him and fell ill from fear for him, then the beloved came and he was cured by the sight of him (needs_source: the report and the verses as the Sheikh tells them). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep17 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep17 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep17 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep17 00:07:30}

## His biography in the introduction to Fada'il al-Sahaba
Wasi Allah Abbas, editor of [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]], gives his lineage: Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal ibn Hilal ibn Asad ibn Idris ibn Abd Allah ibn Hayyan ibn Abd Allah ibn Anas ibn Awf ibn Qasit ibn Mazin ibn Shayban al-Shaybani {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.25}. He was born in Rabi' I 164 AH, as his sons [[person/salih-ibn-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Salih]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Abd Allah]] reported from him; his father died when he was small and his mother raised him, taking away his clothes when he wanted to set out early for hadith and saying "Wait until people call the dawn prayer, or until morning" {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.25}.

### Study and travel
Ahmad said the first person he wrote hadith from was Abu Yusuf, that he began seeking hadith at sixteen, that he first heard from Hushaym in 179, and that Hushaym died when he was twenty {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.25}. He travelled to [[place/kufa|Kufa]], [[place/basra|Basra]], [[place/mecca|Mecca]], [[place/medina|Medina]], Yemen, Syria and the Jazira; his first journey was to Basra in 186, and he entered it five times {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.26-27}. His son Salih relates that he went on pilgrimage with [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]], intending to go on afterwards to [[person/abd-al-razzaq-al-sanani|Abd al-Razzaq]] in Sanaa; they met Abd al-Razzaq in the circumambulation, and Yahya proposed hearing from him in Mecca to save a month's journey each way, but Ahmad refused to spoil his intention and went on until he heard from him in [[place/sanaa|Sanaa]] {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.26}. His money ran out there; he refused Abd al-Razzaq's dirhams as gift or loan, and made drawstrings and broke his fast on what they earned {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.26}. Salih also reports him saying: had I had fifty dirhams I would have gone to Rayy, to Jarir ibn Abd al-Hamid {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.27}.

### Breadth of transmission and praise
Salih reports: my father said, "I wrote a million hadiths in my own hand, besides what was written for me"; Abd Allah said: my father wrote a million hadiths and left out two hundred thousand from people he would not narrate from {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.27}. The editor quotes [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] as saying that when he left Baghdad he left no one there more scrupulous, more God-fearing or more learned in law than Ahmad, and that Ahmad was an imam in eight things: hadith, law, language, the Qur'an, poverty, asceticism, scrupulousness and the Sunna {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.27-28}. Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam said that knowledge ended with four — Ahmad, Ali ibn al-Madini, Yahya ibn Ma'in and Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba — and that Ahmad was the best jurist of them {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.28}. Ibrahim al-Harbi said that in Ahmad it was as though God had gathered the knowledge of the first and the last {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.28}.

### The Mihna
The editor regards his stand during the controversy over the createdness of the Qur'an in the [[event/mihna-of-the-quran|Mihna]] as the most prominent fact of his life; he says it appeared under Harun al-Rashid and grew under al-Ma'mun, that many scholars gave in out of *taqiyya*, and that Ahmad held firm and was flogged {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.28-29}. He quotes one of the floggers as saying he gave him eighty lashes that would have brought down an elephant {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.29}.

### His death
He died on Friday 12 Rabi' I 241 AH, aged seventy-seven {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.29}.

### His works
From Ibn al-Nadim the editor lists: *al-Ilal wa-Ma'rifat al-Rijal*, the *Tafsir*, *al-Nasikh wa-l-Mansukh*, *al-Zuhd*, *al-Masa'il*, *al-Fada'il*, *al-Fara'id*, *al-Manasik*, *al-Iman*, *al-Ashriba*, *Ta'at al-Rasul*, *al-Radd ala al-Jahmiyya* and the *Musnad*, which Ibn al-Nadim says holds over forty thousand hadiths {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.29-30}. From al-Khatib he adds the *Tarikh*, *Hadith Shu'ba*, *al-Muqaddam wa-l-Mu'akhkhar fi Kitab Allah*, *Jawabat al-Qur'an* and *Hadith al-Shuyukh*; from Brockelmann *Kitab al-Sunna*, *al-Salat* and *Usul al-Sunna*; from Sezgin *al-Wuquf wa-l-Wasaya*, *Ahkam al-Nisa'*, *al-Tarajjul*, his answer on the createdness of the Qur'an and others {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.30-31}. He also names works not listed by his biographers, among them a *Kitab al-Fitan* in the Zahiriyya, *Fada'il Ahl al-Bayt* mentioned by al-Hakim, a *Musnad Ahl al-Bayt* in the Taymuriyya library, and *al-Asma' wa-l-Kuna* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.32}. A footnote explains that the *Masa'il* are parts transmitted by Abu Dawud, Ishaq al-Kawsaj, Abd Allah, al-Baghawi, Ibn Hani', Hanbal, al-Maymuni, al-Marrudhi, Harb and al-Harbi, which al-Khallal gathered in *al-Jami' li-Ulum al-Imam Ahmad* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.29-30}.

### His definition of a Companion
The editor quotes Ahmad, when the people of Badr were mentioned before him, as saying that the best of people after them are the Companions of the Messenger of God: everyone who accompanied him for a year, a month, a day or an hour, or saw him, is one of his Companions, with companionship in proportion to how long he accompanied him {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.11}.
