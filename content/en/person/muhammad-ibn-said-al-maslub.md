## Summary
Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Maslub ("the crucified"), a Syrian of [[place/damascus|Damascus]] and a companion of [[person/makhul-al-dimashqi|Makhul]], whom [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] calls "ruined, accused of heresy and crucified". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 561} He narrated from [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]], Ubada ibn Nusayy and others, and from him narrated Ibn Ajlan, [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|al-Thawri]], Marwan al-Fazari, Abu Mu'awiya, al-Muharibi and others. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 561} His symbol "[ت، ق]" means al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Maja cite him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 561}

## A name with a hundred faces
Al-Dhahabi states that transmitters altered his name in many ways to hide him and disguise his weakness: Muhammad ibn Hassan (after his grandfather), Muhammad ibn Abi Qays, Muhammad ibn Abi Hassan, Muhammad ibn Abi Sahl, Muhammad ibn al-Tabari, Muhammad client of Banu Hashim, Muhammad al-Urdunni, Muhammad al-Shami, Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Shami and more, "until the rent grows wide". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 561} [[person/al-bukhari|Al-Bukhari]] said: al-Maslub is called Ibn al-Tabari; [[person/abu-jafar-al-uqayli|al-Uqayli]] claimed he was Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Shumayla, and was mistaken. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562} He reports Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Sawada saying: they turned his name into more than a hundred names, which I have gathered in a book. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 563} This is a prominent example of what is called [[concept/tadlis|tadlis]] of teachers.

He quotes [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]]: whoever disguises a liar bears the sin, because he preferred that a false saying be taken into the religious law. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 563} Al-Dhahabi distinguishes those who disguised him from those who did not: Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Umawi and Sa'id ibn Abi Hilal named him plainly and did not disguise him, while Marwan ibn Mu'awiya and Abd al-Rahman ibn Salman named him after his grandfather. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 563}

## The critics' sayings
[[person/al-nasai|Al-Nasa'i]] said: neither trustworthy nor reliable, and he counted him among the liars known for forging hadith, with [[person/ibn-abi-yahya-al-aslami|Ibn Abi Yahya]] in Medina, [[person/al-waqidi|al-Waqidi]] in Baghdad and [[person/muqatil-ibn-sulayman|Muqatil ibn Sulayman]] in Khurasan. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 561} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562} Abu Ahmad al-Hakim said: he forged hadith; al-Thawri said: a liar; [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said: he was a liar, and: he forged hadith deliberately. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562} Abu Zur'a al-Dimashqi reported, from Muhammad ibn Khalid from his father, hearing him say: "there is no harm, if a saying is fine, in making up a chain for it." {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562} [[person/al-daraqutni|Al-Daraqutni]] and others said: abandoned. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562}

## His crucifixion
Abd Allah ibn Ahmad reported from his father that Abu Ja'far [[person/al-mansur|al-Mansur]] crucified him for heresy. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562} [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]], however, said in Abbas's report: Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Shami is denounced in hadith, "and it is not as they say, that he was crucified for heresy". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562} At the start of the entry al-Dhahabi writes: "and he was crucified, and God knows best". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 561}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Thawri, Ahmad, al-Nasa'i, Abu Ahmad al-Hakim and al-Daraqutni**: a liar who forged hadith deliberately; al-Mansur crucified him for heresy. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 561} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562}
- **Sunni: Yahya ibn Ma'in in Abbas's report**: denounced in hadith, but not crucified for heresy. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 562}
