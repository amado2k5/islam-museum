## Overview
Abd Allah ibn Kathir, kunya Abu Sa'id, also said to be Abu Bakr, one of the readers of Mecca of the second generation. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.73}

He was a client of Amr ibn Alqama al-Kinani. He was called "al-Darani" because he was a perfumer, and perfumers are called al-Darani in the Hijaz; it is also said "al-Dari al-Lakhmi", because the Banu al-Dari ascribe themselves to Hani ibn Lakhm, among them Tamim al-Dari. It is said that he was of the Persians whom Khosrow sent to Yemen. He died in Mecca in 120 AH and was buried there, and the leadership of the readers passed to him. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.73}

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

He is Abu Ma'bad 'Abd Allah ibn Kathir ibn 'Amr ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Zadhan ibn Firuzan ibn Hurmuz al-Dari al-Makki, the shaykh and imam of Mecca in the reading. He read on Abu al-Sa'ib [[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-saib|'Abd Allah ibn al-Sa'ib]] al-Makhzumi, on Mujahid ibn Jabr and on Dirbas, the client of Ibn 'Abbas; Ibn al-Sa'ib read on Ubayy ibn Ka'b and 'Umar, Mujahid on Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn al-Sa'ib, and Ibn 'Abbas on Ubayy and Zayd, and the chain then reaches the Prophet. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 130} Ibn al-Jazari says he died in 120 AH without doubt, was born in 45, and had no rival for the leadership of the reading in Mecca; he quotes Ibn Mujahid that he remained the imam agreed upon there until his death, and al-Asma'i's report that Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala' said he completed a reading with Ibn Kathir after completing one with Mujahid, and that Ibn Kathir knew more Arabic than Mujahid and was fluent and eloquent. Among the Companions he met were 'Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, Abu Ayyub al-Ansari and Anas ibn Malik. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 130} His two transmitters are [[person/al-bazzi|al-Bazzi]] and [[person/qunbul|Qunbul]], and Ibn al-Jazari counts seventy-three lines of transmission for his reading. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 130}
