## Overview
Abu Ali al-Fudayl ibn Iyad, born in Khurasan. He came to Kufa and heard from al-Mansur ibn al-Mu'tamir, then devoted himself to worship and moved to Mecca, where he stayed until he died in 187 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.341-342}

## With al-Rashid
Al-Mas'udi relates that he entered upon [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]] with his head covered, and told him that he had taken on a great affair, and al-Rashid wept. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.341-342}

He asked to be excused from the "badra" (the purse of money). When Sufyan ibn Uyayna blamed him, he said: "If it were good for them, it would be good for me." {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.341-342}

## In al-Dhahabi's Siyar
Al-Dhahabi narrates the account of his audience with Harun al-Rashid and his counsel to him, including what Salim ibn 'Abdallah, Muhammad ibn Ka'b and Raja' ibn Haywa had advised 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz upon his accession; Harun wept until he fainted, and al-Fudayl refused the thousand dinars offered him, saying "I am pointing you to the path of salvation and you reward me with this?" — whereupon Harun said: "This is the master of the Muslims." {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.3045}
