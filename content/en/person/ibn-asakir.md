## Overview
Abu al-Qasim 'Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn Hibat Allah al-Dimashqi, known as Ibn 'Asakir, the hadith master of Syria and author of the eighty-volume *Tarikh Dimashq*. Ibn Shuhba called him the glory of the Shafi'is and the imam and standard-bearer of the hadith folk of his age. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 719-720}

He was born in Damascus at the opening of 499 AH (September–October 1105) into the Banu 'Asakir, a great Sunni house of scholars and judges, and died on 11 Rajab 571 (25 January 1176); he was buried in the Bab al-Saghir cemetery beside his father and family. The Gregorian conversion is derived. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 71, 82} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 745-746}

His elder brother al-Sa'in first had him heard hadith at the Umayyad Mosque in 505. He entered Baghdad in 520 and stayed five years, keeping to the Nizamiyya; he made the hajj in 521 and returned to Damascus in 525, the year Taj al-Mulk Buri was assassinated. In 529 he set out on a second journey through Azerbaijan, Jurjan, Isfahan, Khurasan and Transoxiana of about four years, meeting al-Sam'ani at Nishapur, and on his return began dictating hadith in 533 with his shaykhs' leave. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 71-79} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 746}

He heard from about thirteen hundred shaykhs and eighty women, completed the Qur'an every Friday and every day in Ramadan, declined offices offered to him, and al-Sam'ani said he joined knowledge of the texts and the isnads, travelling and striving until he gathered what no one else had gathered. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 719-720}

When Nur al-Din took Damascus in 549 he built for him the first dar al-hadith, known as the dar al-Sunna, and pressed him to finish the Tarikh, which stood at fifty-seven volumes in 549, seventy in 562, and settled at eighty volumes of eight hundred parts. Among his other works are *Fadl 'Asqalan* and the *Arba'in fi al-ijtihad fi iqamat al-jihad*, written after Ascalon fell in 548, and *Tabyin Kadhib al-Muftari* in defence of al-Ash'ari. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 746-747} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 75}
