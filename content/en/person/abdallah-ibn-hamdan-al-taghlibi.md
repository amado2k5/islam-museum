## Overview
Abdallah ibn Hamdan ibn Hamdun al-Taghlibi al-Adawi is described by al-Zirikli as an amir and one of the foremost commanders of the Abbasid age. His entry in al-A'lam is headed by his kunya, Abu al-Hayja', and ends with his death in 317 AH, 929 CE; al-Zirikli gives no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 57}.

## His posts
Al-Muktafi billah appointed him to Mosul and its districts in 293 AH, and he stayed until al-Muqtadir dismissed him in 301 AH; he came to Baghdad, al-Muqtadir robed him in honour and reinstated him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 57}. He was then arrested in 303 AH with his brother al-Husayn and released in 305 AH; in 308 AH he was given the Khurasan road and al-Dinawar, which he administered while in Baghdad. He also farmed the taxes and estates of Mosul and the neighbouring lands {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 57}.

## His killing
One of al-Muqtadir's men killed him during the turmoil of al-Muqtadir's deposition and the pledge of allegiance to al-Qahir {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 57}.
