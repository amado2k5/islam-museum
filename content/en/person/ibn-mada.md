Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Lakhmi al-Qurtubi, better known as Ibn Mada', was a grammarian and jurist of Cordoba from a family of noble local standing.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en} His birth year is disputed between 1116 and 1119; the Arabic article heads his years 513–592 AH although 513 AH corresponds to 1119 rather than the 1116 it is paired with, and the conflict is preserved in his record.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-ar}{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en} As a youth he studied religion, geometry and medicine, then moved to Seville to study grammar and syntax from the works of Sibawayh, and later travelled to Ceuta to study historiography and prophetic tradition with Qadi Ayyad.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en}

He became an authority in Zahiri fiqh — the madhhab of the Almohad state — and the Almohads appointed him judge first at Fes and then at Béjaïa, until in 580 AH the caliph Abu Yaqub Yusuf made him chief judge of the whole caliphate, an office he kept under al-Mansur until his death.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-ar}{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en} In that office he assisted the Almohad authorities in banning religious books written by non-Zahiris and oversaw their outright burning under al-Mansur; the Dutch arabist Kees Versteegh has described his adherence to Zahirism as fanatical.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en}

His assault on the grammarians filled three books — al-Mashriq, Tanzih al-Qur'an and al-Radd 'ala al-Nuhat (Refutation of the Grammarians) — of which only the last survives.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-ar} He attacked the grammar of the eastern, above all Basran, linguists as complicated, casuistic, obscure and artificial, and called instead for the abolition of grammatical governance and linguistic analogy and for a simple grammar built on the true facts of the language as its native speakers understand it.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en} The revolt was his jurisprudence carried into language: as the Zahiris deny legal causality, so he denied linguistic causality, holding that speech, like all things, is predetermined by God rather than chosen by the speaker; he is considered the first linguist to address dependency in grammar in the modern sense.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en}

He died at Seville in Jumada al-awwal 592 AH, corresponding to 1196, approaching eighty and still chief judge of the caliphate.{cite:wikipedia-ibn-mada-en} Wikidata agrees with both articles on 1196, though the item's short description once rendered the year 1195 by a conversion slip.{cite:wikidata-ibn-mada}
