## In al-Sakhawi's al-Daw' al-Lami'
[[person/al-sakhawi|Al-Sakhawi]] gives an entry for Ahmad ibn Ali ibn Ahmad, Shihab al-Din al-Qalqashandi the Shafi'i: born in 756, he wrote in the chancery and served as deputy judge; his works include Subh al-A'sha in four volumes. He died on Saturday 10 Jumada al-Akhira 821. {cite:daw-lami-jil-1992 2/8}

## In the preface of Subh al-a'sha
Al-Qalqashandi says in the preface of [[work/subh-al-asha|Subh al-a'sha]] that he took up his post in the chancery "at the noble sultanic gates" around 791 AH, and then composed a maqama arguing that writing is the craft fitting for a man of learning and preferring chancery writing to the writing of accounts; someone whose judgement he trusted urged him to follow it with a full work serving as its commentary, and that became Subh al-a'sha. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v1 p.8} {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v1 p.9} He describes himself modestly as attached to the people of this craft "though I be but an adoptive member". {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v1 p.9} In the book's conclusion he testifies to conditions in his own day, saying that the relaying of messenger pigeons had lapsed in his time and that the beacons for warning of the Mongols had become "traces that have faded". {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v14 p.394} {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v14 p.400} See [[concept/pigeon-post|the pigeon post]] and [[concept/mamluk-beacons|the beacons and crop-burnings]].
