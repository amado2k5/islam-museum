Abu Salama Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud ibn Amir al-Ashja'i al-Ghatafani, an obeyed chief among Banu Ashja' and a knight of the Arabs' craftiest men. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He came with his people Ghatafan in the Confederates' army besieging Medina; God opened his heart to Islam at the siege's height, and he slipped secretly to the Prophet, declared his Islam and said: "Messenger of God, my people do not know of my Islam — command me as you wish". The Prophet told him: "you are but one man among us — so deceive them off us if you can, for war is deception". {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He went with wisdom and cunning to Banu Qurayza — their drinking-companion in the Jahiliyya — warning them of the outcome of Quraysh's and Ghatafan's siege, counselling them not to fight alongside them until they took hostages from their nobles; then he went to Quraysh and Ghatafan and told them Qurayza had repented of breaking the pact and had promised Muhammad to hand over hostages from your own chiefs to be beheaded — so he created between them a rift and a great suspicion that was among the greatest causes of the alliance's failure and dispersal. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## In Suwar Min Hayat al-Sahaba by Abd al-Rahman Rafat al-Basha
[[person/abd-al-rahman-rafat-al-basha|Al-Basha]] opens his chapter on him in [[work/suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba|Suwar Min Hayat al-Sahaba]] with a hadith attributed to the Prophet: "in you are two traits God and His Messenger love — forbearance and clemency." He describes Nu'aym as a youth of keen heart and bright intellect, quick-witted and glib, a man of pleasure who sought his enjoyments among the Jews of Yathrib — especially Banu Qurayza — frequenting them often and closely attached to them. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.400}
He recounts that the Jewish chiefs of Banu Nadir stirred Quraysh to war against the Muslims and bound them by pact, then hired Ghatafan from Najd; Ghatafan's leader Uyayna ibn Hisn marched out with his army, and in the van of his men was Nu'aym. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.401} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.402}
On a night of the siege — which lasted some twenty days, the hypocrites and the sick of heart having slipped away from the Prophet — Nu'aym lay awake asking himself: what brought you from those distant places in Najd to war on this man? He saw that the war was for no known cause, and resolved to withdraw from his people's army. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.404} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.405} He slipped from his people's camp in the dark of night to the Prophet, bore witness that there is no god but God and that he is His Messenger and that what he brought is true, told him his people did not know of his Islam, and the Prophet said: "you are but one man among us — go to your people and weaken them if you can, for war is deception." {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.405} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.406}
Nu'aym then went to Banu Qurayza, who in pre-Islamic days had counted him a companion and boon-companion, and counselled them not to fight alongside Quraysh and Ghatafan until they had taken a group of their nobles as hostages; then he went to Abu Sufyan and told him Banu Qurayza had repented of opposing Muhammad and sent to offer that many nobles of Quraysh and Ghatafan be handed over to him to be beheaded, after which they would join him against them — and Abu Sufyan warned them that if the Jews asked for hostages they should hand over no one. He then carried the like message to Ghatafan. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.406} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.407} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.408}
Abu Sufyan sent his son to test Banu Qurayza; they demanded seventy of the Muslims' nobles as hostages, and when he returned they said: "damned be the sons of apes and swine — by God, had they asked us for a single fine ewe we would not have handed it over." {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.408} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.409}
Nu'aym succeeded in tearing the confederates' ranks apart and sundering their word, and God sent upon Quraysh and their allies a violent scudding wind that tore up their tents, overturned their cooking-pots and quenched their fires; they departed in the dark of night. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.409} Thereafter Nu'aym was charged with affairs and bore the banners; when Mecca was conquered Abu Sufyan stood reviewing the Muslim armies, saw a man carrying Ghatafan's banner and asked "who is this?" — "Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud" — and said: "evil is what he did to us on the day of the Trench; by God he was one of the fiercest enemies of Muhammad — and here he is carrying his people's banner before him and marching against us under its standard." {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.409} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.410}
