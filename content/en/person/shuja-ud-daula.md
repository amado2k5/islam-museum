## Summary

Shuja-ud-Daula (19 January 1732 – 26 January 1775) was the third Nawab of Awadh and Grand Vizier of the Mughal Empire, from 5 October 1754 until his death, a son of Safdar Jang {cite:wikipedia-shuja-ud-daula-en}.

## Panipat, Buxar and Allahabad

He despised the Maratha-backed vizier Imad-ul-Mulk, backed the exiled Prince Ali Gauhar — the future Shah Alam II — against the usurpers at Delhi, and rose to Grand Vizier of the empire {cite:wikipedia-shuja-ud-daula-en}. At the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761 he fought beside Ahmad Shah Durrani and helped cut the Marathas' supply lines {cite:wikipedia-shuja-ud-daula-en}. In 1764 he allied with Shah Alam II and Mir Qasim against the East India Company and was defeated at Buxar; the Treaty of Allahabad of 1765 restored his state at the price of territory and a heavy indemnity, and began Awadh's subordination to the Company {cite:wikipedia-shuja-ud-daula-en}. He ruled from Faizabad, which he developed into his capital, died there on 26 January 1775, and was buried at Gulab Bari {cite:wikipedia-shuja-ud-daula-en} {cite:wikidata-shuja-ud-daula}.

## Faith

His faith is recorded as Shia Twelver, documented. The Arabic article gives his religion as Shia Muslim {cite:wikipedia-shuja-ud-daula-ar}, and the English Oudh State article states, citing the Encyclopaedia of Islam, that the Nawabs of Oudh were Shia Muslims who promoted Shia as the state religion {cite:wikipedia-oudh-state-en}; his own English article's body describes his Awadh as a Shi'a kingdom whose Kashmiri Shi'a migrants looked to the state as the sword-arm of the Shi'as in India {cite:wikipedia-shuja-ud-daula-en}. Under the founder's mapping of 1 November 2026 these attestations, in the Twelver context of his Nishapuri house and court, settle shia-twelver; Wikidata's religion value and the articles' category filings are structured data and were not counted.
