## Summary
DJ Khaled, born Khaled Mohammed Khaled in New Orleans, Louisiana, on 26 November 1975, is an American DJ, rapper and record producer of Palestinian descent. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en} He first gained recognition as a radio host on Miami's 99 Jamz in the 1990s, and became known for albums built around guest artists, among them Major Key (2016), which debuted atop the Billboard 200. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en} In 2014 he described himself as a devout Muslim. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en}

## Career
Khaled was born in New Orleans to Palestinian immigrant parents — his father from Al-Mazra'a ash-Sharqiya and his mother from Ramallah — and built his reputation in Miami, first as a radio host on WEDR (99 Jamz) and then as DJ and producer for Fat Joe's Terror Squad collective. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en} His debut album Listennn... the Album appeared in 2006, followed by We the Best (2007), We Global (2008) and Victory (2010), the latter supported by the triple-platinum single "All I Do Is Win". {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en} We the Best Forever (2011) brought him mainstream prominence with "I'm on One"; after Kiss the Ring (2012), Suffering from Success (2013) and I Changed a Lot (2015), his ninth album Major Key (2016) debuted atop the Billboard 200 and was nominated for Best Rap Album at the Grammy Awards. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en} In 2021 he released Khaled Khaled, his twelfth studio album, named after his legal name. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en}

## Works
His albums include Listennn... the Album (2006), We the Best (2007), We Global (2008), Victory (2010), We the Best Forever (2011), Kiss the Ring (2012), Suffering from Success (2013), I Changed a Lot (2015), Major Key (2016) and Khaled Khaled (2021). {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**His faith as the sources state it.** The article states that in 2014 Khaled described himself as a devout Muslim, in his Larry King interview, and that in December 2022 he visited Mecca with Mike Tyson to participate in the Umrah pilgrimage, citing The New Arab. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en} The Arabic article repeats both points; the Urdu stub files him among the Muslims of Florida and of Louisiana. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-ar} {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-ur} A self-description of devotion and a pilgrimage attest identity and practice, not a school: no source names a madhhab for him, and Wikidata gives only Islam generically. {cite:wikidata-dj-khaled} His record therefore carries his faith as unknown, with the attestations recorded verbatim. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en}

**Criticism over Gaza.** The article reports that on the song "Make Them Pay" from Drake's 2026 album Iceman, Drake criticizes Khaled for not speaking out on the Gaza war, despite his Palestinian roots. {cite:wikipedia-dj-khaled-en}
