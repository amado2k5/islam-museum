## Summary
Ons Jabeur is a Tunisian professional tennis player, born in Ksar Hellal on 28 August 1994. She reached a career-high singles ranking of world No. 2 on 27 June 2022, the highest ranking achieved by any African or Arab player in WTA or ATP history, and has contested three major singles finals. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en} {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-ar} {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-ur} {cite:wikidata-ons-jabeur}

She has won five WTA Tour singles titles, including the 2022 Madrid Open, a WTA 1000 event, and is the first African and Arab woman to reach a major singles final. In Tunisia she is nicknamed the "Minister of Happiness". {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

## Early life
Jabeur was born in Ksar Hellal, a small town in Tunisia, to Samira and Ridha Jabeur, and grew up in the larger nearby coastal city of Sousse; she has two older brothers and an older sister. Her mother, who played tennis recreationally, introduced her to the sport at the age of three. She trained under Nabil Mlika for ten years from the ages of four to thirteen, moved at twelve to the capital, Tunis, to train at the Lycée Sportif El Menzah, and later trained in Belgium and France from the age of sixteen. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

## Junior career and the early professional years
Jabeur began on the ITF Junior Circuit in 2007, reached the French Open girls' singles finals of 2010 and 2011 and won the latter, becoming the first African or Arab to win a junior major since 1964 and the first Arab girl to win a junior major singles title. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

She turned professional in 2010 and spent nearly a decade playing primarily at ITF level, making her WTA Tour main-draw debut at Doha in February 2012 and remaining in the top 200 for years without entering the top 100, until a runner-up finish at the 2018 Kremlin Cup, reached as a qualifier, took her back into the top 100. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

## The breakthrough: a major quarterfinal and a first title
At the 2020 Australian Open, Jabeur became the first Arab woman to reach a major quarterfinal, defeating Caroline Wozniacki in the last match of Wozniacki's career on the way. In June 2021 she won the Birmingham Classic to become the first Arab woman to win a WTA Tour title, and in October 2021 she became the first Arab player to reach the top 10 in either ranking's history. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

## 2022: Madrid, two major finals and world No. 2
In May 2022 Jabeur won the Madrid Open, becoming the first African player to win a WTA 1000 title, reached the Rome final the following week, won the German Open in Berlin, and rose to world No. 2 on 27 June 2022. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

At Wimbledon she became the first African woman, and the first Arab or North African player, in the Open Era to reach a major singles final, losing it to Elena Rybakina in three sets; two months later she reached the US Open final and lost to Iga Świątek. She ended her best season ranked No. 2 and made her debut at the WTA Finals. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

## 2023–2025: a second Wimbledon final, decline and a break
Jabeur won the Charleston Open in April 2023 and the Ningbo Open in September for five career titles, and reached a second consecutive Wimbledon final, defeating four major champions on the way, before losing to Markéta Vondroušová in what she described as the most painful loss of her career. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

Injury and form struggles followed — a shoulder injury ended her 2024 season early — and in July 2025 she announced an indefinite break from professional tennis, saying she had not felt happy on court for some time and that it was time to take a step back. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

## Personal life and faith as the sources report them
Jabeur is married to Karim Kamoun, a Russian-Tunisian former fencer who has been her fitness coach since mid-2017. On 10 November 2025 she announced her pregnancy and that she was expecting her first child, and later that month it was announced that she and her longtime coach Issam Jellali would join the team of Turkish player Zeynep Sönmez for the 2026 season, she as a mentor and Jellali as head coach. {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}

## Reach
Jabeur's public significance lies in her standing as the highest-ranked tennis player in African and Arab history and the first African and Arab woman to play a major singles final; her achievements are credited with raising the profile of tennis across the African continent, she won the Arab Woman of the Year award in 2019, and in Tunisia she is a beloved national figure known as the "Minister of Happiness". {cite:wikipedia-ons-jabeur-en}
