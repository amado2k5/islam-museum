## Summary

Radwa Ashour (1946–2014) was an Egyptian novelist and critic, professor of English literature at Ain Shams University, who built a school of Arabic historical fiction joining document, memory and women.{cite:wikipedia-radwa-ashour}

## Life

Born in Cairo, she graduated in English literature from Cairo University and took a doctorate in African-American literature from the University of Massachusetts, teaching at Ain Shams. She married the Palestinian poet Mourid Barghouti; their son is the poet Tamim al-Barghouti.{cite:wikipedia-radwa-ashour}

## Works

Her best-known work is the Granada Trilogy (1994–1995) on al-Andalus after the fall; Al-Tanturiyya (2010) tells the Palestinian Nakba through a woman; Atyaf (Specters, 1999) is a semi-autobiography of her generation. She also wrote short fiction, translations and criticism.{cite:wikipedia-radwa-ashour}

## Method and standing

She wrote history from the side of the defeated — Moriscos, Palestinians, women — in precise layered prose. Arab and Western critics celebrated her, her work was translated, and she won literary honours including the International Prize for Arabic Fiction's lifetime achievement award.{cite:wikipedia-radwa-ashour}
