## Brief
Al-Nadr ibn al-Harith was a Qurashi who had traveled to al-Hira and returned with the Persians' tales of kings — offering his storytelling against the Prophet's recitation. Captured at Badr, he was executed on the march home.

## Life
Where the Prophet recited, al-Nadr recited the Persian cycles — Rustam and Isfandiyar — to hold Quraysh's audiences. The tafsir tradition links him to the verses about those who buy "idle tales" to lead people astray {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

At Badr he fought on the Meccan side and was captured; like [[person/uqba-ibn-abi-muayt|Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt]], he was one of two prisoners put to death on the return — the tradition says at the Prophet's order, for his years of mockery {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:watt-1953-mecca}.

## Legacy
He is the story's rival narrator: two men telling Arabia's two stories in the same marketplace, until battle decided between them. The museum displays his fate without editorializing — the early war was fought in words before it was fought in blood.

## For young visitors
Al-Nadr told Persian adventure stories to compete with the Prophet's recitation. After the battle he was one of two prisoners who were not spared.
