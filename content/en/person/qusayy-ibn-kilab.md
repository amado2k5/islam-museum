## Summary
His name was Zayd ibn Kilab; he was called Qusayy ('far away') because he grew up far from Mecca. He gathered scattered Quraysh — hence 'al-Mujammi' (the Gatherer) — took the rule of Mecca and the Ka'ba from Khuza'a, and was the first to institute for Quraysh the Dar al-Nadwa, the hijaba, the siqaya, the rifada, the ifada and the liwa {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/109-125} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## His place in the lineage
His sons were Abd Manaf, Abd al-Dar, Abd al-Uzza and Abd; they divided the offices — the siqaya, rifada, liwa and ifada to Abd Manaf's line, the hijaba, sidana and nadwa to Abd al-Dar's — and after him Quraysh became the clans of the hollows, the outskirts and the far quarters {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/125-132}.

## For younger visitors
Qusayy ibn Kilab is one of the Prophet's ancestors in the part of the lineage the genealogists agree on, from Adnan down to Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib.
