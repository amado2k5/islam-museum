## Overview
Qusayy ibn Kilab: the gatherer of Quraysh in Mecca, taker of the House's rule from Khuza'a around the year 440 CE, founder of the Dar al-Nadwa, and holder of the five offices.

## Life
His father died while he was in his mother's lap; his mother married a man of Banu Udhra — Rabi'a ibn Haram — who carried her to his country on the edge of Syria; when Qusayy grew up he returned to Mecca and asked [[person/hulayl-ibn-hubshiyya|Hulayl ibn Hubshiyya]] for his daughter Hubba's hand, and he married her to him {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}.

Then he prevailed over the rule of Mecca and the House after a war with Khuza'a and the arbitration of Ya'mar ibn Awf, in the mid-fifth century CE, year 440 {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab}. He gathered his people from their dwellings into Mecca and apportioned it in quarters among them, founded the [[building/dar-al-nadwa|Dar al-Nadwa]] {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}, and held the offices of leadership, the banner, the guardianship of the House, the siqaya and the rifada {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## The legacy
Qusayy bequeathed what he held of Quraysh's affairs to his son Abd al-Dar, his eldest, telling him: I will surely join you to the people even should they outrank you {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}. After him Banu Abd Manaf contested Banu Abd al-Dar, and the offices were divided: the siqaya and rifada to Banu Abd Manaf, the Dar al-Nadwa, banner and guardianship to Banu Abd al-Dar {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
Qusayy is the Prophet's distant ancestor; he gathered his people Quraysh in Mecca, became its ruler, and set the order of serving the pilgrims.
