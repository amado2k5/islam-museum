Abu al-Walid Hassan ibn Thabit ibn al-Mundhir al-Najjari al-Ansari, the poet of the Messenger of God and the poet of Islam, a master of rhyme who defended the call. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Prophet set up a pulpit for him in the Prophet's Mosque, from which he would defend the Messenger of God and satirise the polytheists. The Prophet told him: "Satirise them, and the Holy Spirit is with you." His verse is a record of the Prophet's biography, of the campaigns of the Ansar, and of laments for their martyrs, such as Hamza and the martyrs of Mu'ta. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

He was caught up in the slander of the Ifk and received the prescribed punishment. He later repented and regretted it. In praise of A'isha he composed his famous poem, which describes her as chaste and discreet, untouched by suspicion, and rising hungry from the flesh of the heedless. A'isha forgave him and honoured him. He lived to a great age and died in Medina late in the reign of Mu'awiya, at about a hundred and twenty years old. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## The delegation of Banu Tamim and the poems of the Year of Delegations
Ibn Ishaq relates that Hassan was absent when the [[event/delegation-of-banu-tamim|delegation of Banu Tamim]] boasted before the Prophet. The Prophet sent for him. Hassan said that his messenger had told him he was summoned to answer the poet of Banu Tamim, and on the way he composed some verses. Then the Prophet told him to stand and answer al-Zibriqan, and he recited [[work/hassan-reply-to-zibriqan|his poem "Verily the tufts of Fihr and their brothers"]]. Al-Aqra' ibn Habis ruled that the poet of the Muslims was more eloquent than theirs {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/209} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/212}. Ibn Hisham relates [[work/hassan-second-reply-to-zibriqan|another reply]] by him to other verses by al-Zibriqan {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/211}. At the end of the chapter on Bara'a, Ibn Ishaq gives [[work/hassan-poem-enumerating-the-maghazi|a poem of his enumerating the days of the Ansar with the Prophet]], and Ibn Hisham says it is narrated for his son Abd al-Rahman. Then come [[work/hassan-poem-kunna-muluk-al-nas|the poem "We were the kings of the people before Muhammad"]] and [[work/hassan-poem-qawmi-ulaika|the poem "My people, those are the noble ones, if you ask"]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/199} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/201} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/202}.

## His reply to Asma bint Marwan
Ibn Ishaq relates that Asma bint Marwan composed verses disparaging Islam and its people and inciting against the Prophet. Hassan answered her with verses in which he places the Banu Wa'il, Waqif and Khatma below the Banu Khazraj, and describes her killing. See [[work/hassan-reply-to-asma-bint-marwan|the reply]] and [[event/killing-of-asma-bint-marwan|the account]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/286}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 7)

The Sheikh says that Hassan ibn Thabit was a poet, and that the Prophet used to set a chair for him and tell him to speak, since the Holy Spirit supports him (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:22:00}.

He says that Jibril supports him with inspiration (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:22:30}. The Sheikh plays on the sound of names, saying that the foreign poet's name ends on the pattern of Iblis while Hassan is on the pattern of wudu', and that the distance between them is great {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:22:30}.
