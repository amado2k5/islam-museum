Abu al-Walid Hassan ibn Thabit ibn al-Mundhir al-Najjari al-Ansari, the Messenger of God's poet and Islam's poet, the master of rhyme who defended the call. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Prophet would set him a pulpit in the Prophet's Mosque, on which he stood defending the Messenger of God and satirising the polytheists, the Prophet telling him: "satirise them — the Holy Spirit is with you". His verse is the record of the Prophet's Sira, of the Ansar's campaigns, and the elegy of their martyrs such as Hamza and the martyrs of Mu'ta. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

He was caught in the affair of the Ifk and was flogged the hadd; then repented, regretted, and in praise of A'isha spoke his famous poem: "chaste, self-contained, she suspects no suspicion, and rises hungering from the meat of the heedless" — and A'isha pardoned him and used to honour him. He lived long and died in Medina late in Mu'awiya's reign at about a hundred twenty years. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
