## Overview
Yahya ibn Ma'in ibn 'Awn ibn Ziyad al-Ghatafani al-Murri, Abu Zakariyya of Baghdad, was the master of narrator criticism — the science of wounding and accrediting — and the foremost authority on the transmitters of his age. He died in 233 AH, leaving thirty book-chests and twenty cases of writings. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.4202}

## Method and standing
It was said of him that his word judged and settled disputes between quarrelling critics. Among his companions 'Ali ibn al-Madini was the most knowledgeable, Sulayman al-Shadhakuni the most retentive of the chapters, and Yahya himself the most prolific writer. His verdicts "trustworthy" and "weak" became the accepted currency of the hadith collections. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.4202-4204}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 1)
The Sheikh tells an anecdote of a hadith narrator who was questioned by two men: had he narrated from a certain person? He said, no, by God; had he heard from him? He said, no, by God; then the two men identified themselves, one saying that he was Ahmad (the context suggests Ibn Hanbal) and that the other was Yahya ibn Main (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:53:00}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] relates from the introduction to [[work/al-majruhin-min-al-muhaddithin|«al-Majruhin»]] of Ibn Hibban, calling it a story well known among hadith students, that Yahya ibn Ma'in and [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] entered a mosque in Kufa where a preacher was speaking; as they stood to pray they heard him say "Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Yahya ibn Ma'in told us", and then give a long hadith about a bird created from every word of the declaration of God's oneness. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:26:00} Al-Heweny says it is an obvious lie that anyone who had once caught the scent of hadith would know. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:27:30} Yahya shortened his prayer and called the man, who came expecting a reward; Yahya asked who had told him this, he said Ahmad and Yahya, and Yahya said: "I am Yahya ibn Ma'in and this is Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and we never heard it." {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:28:00} The man said he had heard that Yahya ibn Ma'in was a fool and only now confirmed it, and that he had written from seventeen Ahmad ibn Hanbals and seventeen Yahya ibn Ma'ins; Ahmad put his sleeve over his mouth and said, "Let him go." {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:29:30}

In an episode on concealment in chains (tadlis) he says that when Yahya ibn Ma'in was ill, visitors asked him, "Abu Zakariya, what do you desire?", and he said, "An empty house and a high chain"; al-Heweny cites it as evidence of the hadith scholars' eagerness for [[concept/isnad|short chains]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:14:30}
