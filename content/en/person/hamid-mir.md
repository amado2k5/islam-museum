## Summary
Hamid Mir is a Pakistani journalist and television host born in Lahore in 1966, among the most influential broadcast journalists in Pakistan; The Muslim 500 lists him among influential media figures. {cite:muslim500-hamid-mir} {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## Career
The son of the journalist Waris Mir, he began in newspapers in the 1980s, founded and edited Urdu dailies, then joined Geo TV at its launch in 2002 as host of Capital Talk, which became Pakistan's most-watched political talk show. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## Interviews
His interviews include Osama bin Laden ⁦(three meetings before September 2001)⁩, General Pervez Musharraf, John Kerry, Colin Powell and Benazir Bhutto — whose final interview he conducted days before her assassination. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## Pressure
He has been banned, taken off air, and survived a 2014 shooting in Karachi widely attributed by press groups to his journalism; he received the CPJ International Press Freedom Award and remains a columnist for Jang. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## Assessment
He is credited with defining confrontational prime-time political journalism in Pakistan; critics on many sides dispute his positions, a debate the museum reports attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir} {cite:muslim500-hamid-mir}


## Capital Talk
His flagship Geo programme Capital Talk ⁦(2002-)⁩ is among the longest-running and most-watched political talk shows in Pakistani television, the platform for his interviews with heads of government, military figures and opposition leaders. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## The bin Laden interviews
He was the only journalist to interview Osama bin Laden three times — 1997, 1998 and after 9/11 — scoops that made him internationally known and drew both acclaim and criticism. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## Persecution and defiance
He survived a 2014 assassination attempt in Karachi and earlier bans under Musharraf; press-freedom organisations cite his case as emblematic of the risks facing Pakistani journalism. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir} {cite:muslim500-hamid-mir}

## Origins
Born in 1966 in Lahore to a family of journalists — his father Waris Mir a noted columnist — he began in print before moving to television, where his Capital Talk made him the best-known political interviewer in Pakistan. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## The interview record
His interviews span three decades — heads of government, military figures, opposition leaders and Osama bin Laden — a body of encounters that press coverage treats as defining Pakistani political television. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## Persecution
His 2014 assassination attempt in Karachi and earlier bans under Musharraf made him a symbol of the risks facing Pakistani journalism — a case press-freedom organisations cite as emblematic of the country's media environment. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir} {cite:muslim500-hamid-mir}

## Political television's defining interviewer
Hamid Mir's Capital Talk ⁦(2002-)⁩ is among the longest-running and most-watched political talk shows in Pakistani television: the platform for his interviews with heads of government, military figures, opposition leaders and Osama bin Laden — the only journalist to interview bin Laden three times. His 2014 assassination attempt in Karachi and earlier bans under Musharraf made him a symbol of the risks facing Pakistani journalism, and press-freedom organisations cite his case as emblematic of the country's media environment. {cite:wikipedia-hamid-mir}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists him among the most influential Muslims in media, citing his three decades of political interviewing, the unique bin Laden interview record and his survival of persecution as a symbol of Pakistani press freedom. {cite:muslim500-hamid-mir}



## Standing in the field

Within political television, Hamid Mir stands as Pakistan’s best-known political interviewer, host of Capital Talk since 2002 — a position that explains the scale of public attention the career has attracted. The Muslim 500's listing places Hamid Mir among the most influential Muslims of the present generation in this field, reflecting both the size of the audience the work reaches and the extent to which the figure has come to represent it beyond specialist circles. {cite:muslim500-hamid-mir}
