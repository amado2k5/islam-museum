## Summary
Lady Abdullah Haroon (1886–1966), whose real name was Nusrat Khanum, was a social and political activist of Sindh before the independence of Pakistan. Born in Iran, she settled in Karachi, where in 1914 she married the businessman and politician Abdullah Haroon. She founded the Anjuman-i-Khawateen to promote the education and social and economic well-being of the women of Sindh, supported the Khilafat Movement, was elected president of the All India Women Muslim League in 1943, and served as vice-president of the All Pakistan Women's Association. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en}

## Life and marriage
Nusrat Khanum was born in 1886 in Iran and later settled in Karachi, where in 1914 she married Abdullah Haroon, after which she was known as Lady Abdullah Haroon. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en} His article's infobox likewise names his spouse as Lady Abdullah Haroon, and the two Wikidata items carry reciprocal spouse claims. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-haroon-en} {cite:wikidata-lady-abdullah-haroon} {cite:wikidata-abdullah-haroon} The couple's children included Yusuf Haroon, Mahmoud Haroon and Zeenat Haroon Rashid. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-haroon-en}

## Work for women's education
She was deeply interested in educating the women of Sindh, and started a school at her own home. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en} She founded a women's organisation, the Anjuman-i-Khawateen, to promote the social and economic well-being of women in Sindh. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en} The Urdu article adds that she founded the Muslim Girls Students Federation and the Women National Guard. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-ur}

## Political work
Earlier in her political career, in 1919, she actively participated in politics as a vigorous supporter of the Khilafat Movement in Sindh. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en} She was able to raise political consciousness among Sindhi women under the banner of the Muslim League, and was very helpful in gathering support for the party during the Indian provincial elections of 1946. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en} The Urdu article describes her as the first president of the Muslim League's women's wing. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-ur} She was elected president of the All India Women Muslim League in 1943, and was vice-president of the All Pakistan Women's Association, founded by Begum Ra'ana Liaquat Ali Khan. {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en} The English article quotes the judgement that she "was a prominent woman of Sindh... a devoted worker of the Pakistan movement... closely associated with the Quaid-i-Azam from whom she always received guidance". {cite:wikipedia-lady-abdullah-haroon-en}
