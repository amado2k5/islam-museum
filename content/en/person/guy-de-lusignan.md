## Overview
Guy de Lusignan was a Frankish knight who rose to the crown of Jerusalem through marriage and was the king whom [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din]] captured at [[event/battle-of-hattin-583|Hattin]]. The Encyclopaedia of Islam has no article on him, and the Muslim chronicler Ibn al-Athir writes his name as "Kay". This record therefore follows Ibn al-Athir's account of the years 582 to 584 and the passing mentions in the Encyclopaedia's articles on Cyprus. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 341}

## Marriage and the crown
Ibn al-Athir says that the king of the Franks in Syria, who suffered from leprosy, left the kingdom to a young nephew, and that when the boy died the kingdom passed to his mother. She then fell in love with a man among the Franks who had lately come to Syria, called Kay, and married him. She transferred the kingdom to him and had the crown placed on his head. According to the chronicle she summoned the patriarch, priests, monks, the Hospitallers, the Templars and the barons, told them she had given the kingdom back to him, and had them bear witness; they obeyed him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141}

The chronicler counts this succession among the causes of the split that followed. [[person/raymond-iii-tripoli|Raymond, count of Tripoli]], who had governed for the boy and hoped for the crown, was angered, broke with the new king and wrote to Saladin. Ibn al-Athir judges that the quarrel among the Franks was one of the greatest reasons the Muslims were later able to recover their lands and Jerusalem. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 141-142} The event is recorded separately in [[event/frank-split-raymond-582|the Frankish schism]].

## Hattin
In 583/1187 the Frankish forces gathered under their king, with the count of Tripoli now rejoined to them, and marched out from Acre to Sepphoris. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 145} The Encyclopaedia of Islam dates the battle to Saturday 24 Rabi' II 583. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 977} As Ibn al-Athir tells it, thirst and heat wore the Franks down; the survivors climbed a hill near Hattin, where only the king's tent could be pitched, and the king was left there with about a hundred and fifty of their best knights. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 146} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 147} When the king's tent fell, the Muslims took the king, his brother, [[person/raynald-de-chatillon|Raynald, lord of Kerak]], the lord of Jubayl, the son of Humphrey and the master of the Templars. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 148}

Ibn al-Athir then describes what followed in Saladin's tent. Saladin seated the king beside him and gave him iced water, since thirst had nearly destroyed him. The king drank and passed the cup to Raynald, and Saladin said that this man had not drunk with his protection. He then rebuked Raynald and killed him with his own hand, while the king trembled until Saladin calmed him and assured him of his safety. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 148} Saladin ordered the king and a group of notable prisoners sent to Damascus. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 149}

## Captivity and release
After the surrender of Jerusalem Saladin released the queen, who asked leave to join her husband; the chronicle says he was then held in the castle of Nablus, and she went to him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 157} Later, while Saladin waited at Marj Uyun and worried about the Franks gathering at Tyre, he heard that the king he had captured and then released after the conquest of Jerusalem had reconciled with the Marquis after a quarrel, and that they had assembled a vast host. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 180} Ibn al-Athir names the Marquis only by his title; he is the figure known as [[person/conrad-de-montferrat|Conrad of Montferrat]], whose arrival at Acre and flight to Tyre the chronicle tells earlier. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 152}

## Cyprus
The Encyclopaedia of Islam places Guy among the Crusader lords from Syria who joined King Richard when he landed on Cyprus in May 1191, calling him a claimant to the crown of Jerusalem. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 341} It adds that Richard sold the island to the Templars, and that in May 1192 he resold it to Guy de Lusignan, described as husband of the heiress to the crown of Jerusalem. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 341} The article on Famagusta notes that the town passed to Guy in 1192. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 239} His brother Aimery succeeded him as lord of Cyprus in 1194, and in 1197 took the title of king. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 341}

## Dates and doubts
No source read for this record gives his birth or death. Wikidata lists a birth around 1150 (with a second, circa 1129) and death on 18 July 1194; these are marked as tradition pending a recorded review. The identification of "Kay" with Guy rests on the marriage Ibn al-Athir describes and on the Encyclopaedia's Cyprus articles, not on a source that links the two names directly.
