## Overview
Al-Mughira ibn Abdallah ibn Mu'arrid al-Asadi, Abu Mu'arrid, known as al-Aqayshar, is described by al-Zirikli as a satirical poet of high rank, from the Bedouin country around Kufa, who used to go often to al-Hira. The entry gives his death as about 80 AH, about 700 CE, and gives no birth year {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 275}.

## His life
He was born in the Jahiliyya, grew up in the first days of Islam and lived a long life. He was an Uthmani, one of the partisans of Uthman ibn Affan, and he reached the time of the state of Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 275}. He was nicknamed al-Aqayshar because his face was red and peeling, and he grew angry when called by that name {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 275}.

## His poetry and standing
Al-Zirikli reports al-Marzubani as saying that he was one of the libertines of Kufa and of its poets, that he satirised Abd al-Malik and lamented Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 275}. Al-Amidi knew him as the man of the drink, because of a line in a well-known poem of his about wine {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 275}. His reports are many, and al-Zirikli says some of them are strange {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 275}.

## His death
He was killed near Kufa by being choked with smoke {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 275}.
