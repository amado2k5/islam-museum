## Overview
Ibn Shanabudh, a reciter of the Qur'an who is known for an important incident in 323 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.604}

## The incident
Al-Suyuti says that he was made to repent of reciting the irregular readings, and a record was written against him in the presence of the vizier [[person/ibn-muqla|Ibn Muqla]]. He relates that Ibn Shanabudh prayed against the vizier that God should cut off his hand, and the vizier's hand was cut off, and he tasted humiliation. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.604} He died in the days of al-Radi. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.607}

His full name and date of death need an additional source.

## In Ibn al-Jazari's account

Ibn al-Jazari says that Ibn Shannabudh died in Safar 328 AH on the correct view, and was a famous imam and great master, reliable, exact and righteous, who travelled through the lands in search of the readings and gathered what no one else had; he read on [[person/qunbul|Qunbul]]. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 129} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 132} He held that reading by a sound chain was permitted even where it differed from the written text, a council was held over it, the question is disputed, and Ibn al-Jazari says nobody counted this a flaw in his narration or a stain on his probity. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 132}
