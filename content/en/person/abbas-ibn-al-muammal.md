## Summary
Abu al-Walid al-Abbas ibn al-Mu'ammal al-Sufi endured his trial and was given his due; his rest was in weeping and sorrow, and his refuge in the graves. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 528, sequence 529, on page 159. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/159}

## Imprisonment and a dream
Zayd al-Khabari relates from Abu al-Walid al-Abbas ibn al-Mu'ammal al-Sufi, who had enjoined good upon Harun and was imprisoned for a long time. He said: a comer came to me in a dream and said: how many joys will the grieving have tomorrow in the Resurrection, to fill the first of their grief in the world? I woke in fear, and soon God relieved me and brought me out of that prison, and my companions and family rejoiced. In the dream the same comer came to me and said: rejoice, grieving one, in lasting delight tomorrow before their King. I said: by God, grief is for the good of the hereafter, not for the world. After that he was always weeping, following a funeral, visiting the sick, or keeping to the cemetery, and he was very sorrowful. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/159} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/160}
