Ahmed Balafrej (1908–1990) was a Moroccan politician and diplomat, born at Rabat in 1908 — the English biography and the French infobox give 5 September, the Arabic biography 8 September — to a family of Andalusian origin.{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-en}{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-fr}{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-ar} In 1934 he took part, with Allal al-Fassi and Mohamed Hassan Ouazzani, in founding the Moroccan Action Committee, the first national movement of the cities' young activists, and then in founding the Istiqlal Party, whose secretary-general he was from 1944 to 1960.{cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fassi-fr}{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-ar} After four months of prison without trial, the French exiled him to Corsica in May 1944, his health alone sparing him a threatened deportation to Madagascar.{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-fr} In October 1953 he defended the cause of an independent Morocco before the General Assembly of the United Nations, which adopted a resolution calling for Morocco's self-determination, and he remained singularly close to Sultan Mohammed V, whose return from exile in November 1955 the royal family owed in part to his diplomacy.{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-fr}

After independence, Balafrej was Morocco's first foreign minister, from 28 October 1956 to 16 April 1958, and then prime minister from 12 May 1958 to December 1958, keeping the foreign ministry in his own government.{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-en}{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-ar} He returned to office in 1963 as the personal representative of Hassan II and minister of foreign affairs, from 5 January to 13 November of that year.{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-en} He died at Rabat on 14 April 1990.{cite:wikipedia-ahmed-balafrej-en}{cite:wikidata-ahmed-balafrej}
