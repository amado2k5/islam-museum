## Summary
Muhammad Jusuf Kalla is an Indonesian politician and businessman, the only figure in the country's history to serve as vice president twice under two different presidents: with Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono (2004–2009) and then with Joko Widodo (2014–2019). He is known for a businessman's pragmatism carried into politics, for brokering domestic conflict settlements, and for his long chairmanship of the Indonesian Red Cross. {cite:wikipedia-jusuf-kalla}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1942 in Watampone, South Sulawesi, into a Bugis merchant family, studied economics at Hasanuddin University in Makassar where he also led a local Islamic student movement, then managed the family business (NV Hadji Kalla), expanding it into a group working in automotive distribution, construction and energy. {cite:wikipedia-jusuf-kalla}

## Political career
He entered government as Minister of Industry and Trade in the late 1990s, then served as Coordinating Minister for People's Welfare under Megawati, in which capacity he emerged as a mediator in ending communal violence in Poso (Central Sulawesi) and Maluku, and prepared the ground for the Aceh peace process crowned by the 2005 Helsinki agreement. He was elected vice president twice on two different tickets, chaired the Golkar party (2004–2009), and led the Indonesian Red Cross for successive terms, directing responses to disasters from the 2004 tsunami onward. {cite:wikipedia-jusuf-kalla} {cite:muslim500-jusuf-kalla}

## Mediation and humanitarian work
He is credited with the standing of a "deal maker" in Indonesian politics: a practical negotiator seeking workable settlements rather than principled stands, visible in the local peace agreements and in the government's disaster responses through the Red Cross, which he turned into the country's largest relief organisation. {cite:wikipedia-jusuf-kalla}

## Debate
He has faced repeated criticism from journalists and opponents over potential conflicts between his family's businesses and his offices, and over statements some call blunt to the point of provocation on questions of minorities and free expression; the Indonesian press documents these controversies and the museum reports them attributed. {cite:wikipedia-jusuf-kalla}

## Legacy
Kalla remains the emblem of Indonesia's pragmatic politician: a businessman turned peace broker turned vice president to two ideologically opposed presidents, and the humanitarian head of the largest relief body in the world's most populous Muslim country. Historians of Indonesian politics read his path as the post-Suharto model of leadership built on consensus and the deal rather than ideology. {cite:wikipedia-jusuf-kalla}
