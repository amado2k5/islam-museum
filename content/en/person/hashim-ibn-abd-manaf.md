## Summary
Hashim ibn [[person/abd-manaf-ibn-qusayy|Abd Manaf]], whose name was Amr, and after whom the Prophet's family is called the Hashimite family. He took charge of the siqaya and the rifada when the Banu Abd Manaf and the Banu Abd al-Dar reconciled by dividing the offices between them. He was wealthy and of great standing. He was the first to serve tharid, a dish of bread and broth, to the pilgrims in Mecca, and the first to establish the two journeys of winter and summer for Quraysh {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/129-137}.

## His name
His name was Amr. He was called Hashim only because he crumbled bread into tharid for his people. A poet in the book says that Amr crumbled the bread for his people, at a time when the people of Mecca were in a lean and hungry year {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49}.

## The siqaya and the rifada
When the Banu Abd Manaf and the Banu Abd al-Dar divided the offices, the Banu Abd Manaf resorted to casting lots, and the siqaya and the rifada fell to Hashim, who held them for the rest of his life {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/129-132}. See [[event/partition-of-meccan-offices|The division of the offices]] and [[concept/manasib-quraysh|The offices of Quraysh]].

## The two journeys
He was the first to establish the two journeys for Quraysh, the journey of winter and the journey of summer, as the poet quoted by the author mentions {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49}.

## Marriage in Yathrib and death at Gaza
Hashim went to the Levant as a merchant. When he came to Medina he married [[person/salma-bint-amr|Salma bint Amr]], of the Banu Adi ibn [[group/banu-al-najjar|al-Najjar]], and stayed with her. Then he set out again for the Levant while she was with her own family, pregnant with Abd al-Muttalib. Hashim died at [[place/gaza|Gaza]] in the land of Palestine {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/137} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/24}. Salma bore [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] in 497 CE, and named him Shayba because of the white in his hair. She raised him in her father's house in Yathrib, and no one in his family in Mecca was aware of him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49}.

## His children
Hashim had four sons, Asad, Abu Sayfi, Nadla and Abd al-Muttalib, and five daughters, al-Shifa, Khalida, Da'ifa, Ruqayya and Janna {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/107}.

## On lineage
The house of Hashim is the one from which God chose the Prophet. The author quotes the hadith in Muslim about God choosing the Banu Hashim from Quraysh {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} {cite:sahih-muslim 2/245}. Hashim is the third ancestor in the agreed-upon part of the Prophet's lineage {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.48}.

## Notes for researchers
The date of 497 CE for the birth of Abd al-Muttalib is the author's dating, taken from Rahma lil-Alamin. It has no second source.

## For young visitors
Hashim was the great ancestor of the Prophet, after whom his clan Banu Hashim is named. He fed the pilgrims tharid, and he died in Gaza before his son Abd al-Muttalib was born.
