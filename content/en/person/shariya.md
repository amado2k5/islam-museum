## Summary
Shariya, in the entry of [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']] drawn from al-Aghani and al-Wafi bi-l-Wafayat, was a singer born among the slave-born women of [[place/basra|Basra]] and one of the finest singers of her time. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.284} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.287}

## With Ibrahim ibn al-Mahdi
A Hashimite woman of Basra, descended from Ja'far ibn Sulayman, bought her, educated her and taught her singing; she then passed to [[person/ibrahim-ibn-al-mahdi|Ibrahim ibn al-Mahdi]] and learned all or most of his repertoire from him, which is why some ranked her above [[person/arib-al-mamuniyya|Arib]]. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.284} It is related that her mistress first offered her to [[person/ishaq-al-mawsili|Ishaq al-Mawsili]], who bid three hundred dinars; Ibrahim bought her and had his singing girls train her for a year, and when Ishaq heard her afterwards he said she would be cheap at three thousand dinars — and was astonished to learn she was the same girl. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.284} In [[person/ibn-al-mutazz|Ibn al-Mu'tazz]]'s version Ibrahim lacked the price and borrowed it from [[person/ali-ibn-hisham-al-marwazi|Ali ibn Hisham]]; he kept her seven years, raised her like a daughter, would make her repeat one passage of a melody more than a thousand times until she had it, and called her "my daughter". {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.285} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.286}

## In al-Mu'tasim's household and after
[[person/al-mutasim|Al-Mu'tasim]] bought her for 5,500 dinars and she remained his until his death; Sahl al-Ahwal relates that he was offered seventy thousand dinars for her and refused to sell. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.286} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.287} After al-Mu'tasim's death she led the singers until late in the caliphate of [[person/al-wathiq|al-Wathiq]], who called her "my lady"; it is said she took up the lute only under [[person/al-mutawakkil|al-Mutawakkil]], when hostility between her and Arib had set in. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.287}

## For young visitors
Shariya was a singer from Basra trained by a music-loving prince, Ibrahim ibn al-Mahdi, who would have her repeat a melody hundreds of times until it was perfect.
