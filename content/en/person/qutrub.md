## Summary
Abu 'Ali Muhammad ibn al-Mustanir ibn Ahmad al-Basri, the philologist famous as Qutrub, took adab from Sibawayh and a circle of Basran scholars. He used to arrive at Sibawayh's before the other pupils, and Sibawayh said to him one day: "you are nothing but a night-moth (qutrub)" — and the nickname stuck. He was among the circle of Abu 'Ukayf al-'Ajli's sons. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His works
Among his books: Ma'ani al-Qur'an, al-Ishtiqaq, al-Qawafi, al-Nawadir, al-Azmina, al-Farq, al-Aswat, al-Sifat, al-'Ilal, al-Addad, Khalq al-faras, Khalq al-insan, Gharib al-hadith, al-Hamz, Fa'ala wa-af'ala, and the Refutation of those who deny the Qur'an's mutashabih — and he was the first to compose a book of the mithlath in the language. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
