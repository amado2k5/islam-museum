## Summary
Ismail ibn Musa Menk is a Zimbabwean Muslim preacher and scholar, known as Mufti Menk, born on 27 June 1975 in Salisbury, Rhodesia — now Harare, Zimbabwe. He is the Grand Mufti of Zimbabwe and head of the fatwa department of the Council of Islamic Scholars of Zimbabwe. He is known worldwide for short motivational lectures in a plain speaking style that spread through digital platforms, and the Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre in Jordan has listed him in its annual publication The 500 Most Influential Muslims every year since 2010, including the 2026 edition, according to the article. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en} {cite:wikidata-ismail-ibn-musa-menk}

## Early life and education
He was born to Indo-Zimbabwean Muslim parents of Gujarati origin. He began his studies with his father, memorising the Qur'an — the Arabic article says by the age of nine — and learning Arabic and Urdu by the age of twelve; he went to St. John's College in Harare for senior school. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en} {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-ar}

He then studied jurisprudence and Sharia at the Islamic University of Madinah, and specialised afterwards in the Hanafi school at Darul Uloom Kantharia in Gujarat, India. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en} {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-ar}

## Work and preaching
Menk is the Grand Mufti of Zimbabwe and heads the fatwa department of the Council of Islamic Scholars of Zimbabwe (Majlisul Ulama). The Arabic article adds that he directs Dar al-Ummah, an Islamic educational centre serving Zimbabwe's Muslim population, and is an imam of a Harare mosque; it describes him as known in East Africa in particular, and teaching on a global scale. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en} {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-ar}

His fame rests chiefly on short lectures and lessons in English, broadcast through YouTube and social platforms, addressing faith, daily life and motivation, and reaching a wide audience inside Africa and beyond. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}

## His stated positions
Menk opposes terrorism and has pledged his aid in curbing religious extremism in the Maldives. On 31 March 2018 he urged Muslims to avoid Muslim–Christian violence, arguing that Muslims and Christians are brothers and sisters from one father, the prophet Adam. The article reports that he blames Western media for misleading the world that Muslims are terrorists, and quotes him, via Gulf News, that everyone on earth is part of one family with one maker, so no one has the right to force a belief on another. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}

## Books
He published a collection of his sayings as Motivational Moments in 2018, and a second edition, Motivational Moments 2, in 2019. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}

## Awards and recognition
- He has been listed in The 500 Most Influential Muslims, published by the Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre (Jordan), every year since 2010, under "Preachers & Spiritual Guides", including the 2026 edition. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}
- He received an honorary doctorate of social guidance from Aldersgate College, Philippines, and its partner Aldersgate College – Dublin, Ireland, on 16 April 2016. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}
- He received the Global Leadership Award in Social Guidance at the KSBEA 2015 Awards, awarded by the Cochin Herald. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported in the sources, not as the museum's judgement on any of them. The English and Arabic articles report these positions; the original statements have not all yet been reviewed.

**The Singapore ban:** On 31 October 2017 Singapore banned Menk from its borders, believing he expresses views incompatible with its multicultural laws and policies. The Ministry of Home Affairs said the rejection of his short-term work pass stemmed from his "segregationist and divisive teachings", and the Straits Times reported his assertion that it is blasphemous for Muslims to greet believers of other faiths during festivals such as Christmas or Diwali. The Majlisul Ulama Zimbabwe, Menk's own institution, expressed "regret and dismay" at the ban, called him an "asset to multi-cultural, multi-religious Zimbabwe", and said viewers should listen to his sermons in full, not edited clips of a few minutes, to see the moderate path he has chosen. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en} {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-ar}

**The Denmark ban:** In November 2018 the Danish government banned Menk from entering its borders for two years. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}

**His remarks on homosexuality and the cancelled British tour:** The Huffington Post reported that Menk denounced gay people in a YouTube video in offensive terms and said they are "worse than animals". In 2013 he was due to visit six British universities — Oxford, Leeds, Leicester, Liverpool, Cardiff and Glasgow — but the speaking tour was cancelled after student unions and university officials expressed concern about his views. Liverpool University stated that it is not the role of a university to censor people's views, but to provide a neutral, open environment where they can be debated and challenged, and that its diversity and equality policies inform its decisions on such events. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en} {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-ar}

## Personal life
The articles record only his family origin: his parents were Indian Muslims from Gujarat who settled in Zimbabwe, and his father was his first teacher. The museum publishes no family detail beyond what understanding his work requires. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en} {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-ar}

## Reach and audience
Menk's impact rests on digital media rather than books: short English lectures and clips circulated by social platforms in Africa, Asia, Europe and North America. Follower numbers are platform figures that change constantly, and the museum does not state them as facts; his YouTube channel stood at about 6.3 million subscribers as observed in October 2026. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-ibn-musa-menk-en}
