## Overview
Baha' al-Din al-'Amili, known as al-Bahai, was Muhammad b. Husayn, born in 953/1547 and dead in 1030/1621, an author of works in Arabic and Persian on many subjects. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 458}

## Career
He came from Jabal 'Amila in Syria, migrated to Persia and obtained an honoured place at the court of Shah 'Abbas. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 458}

## Works
His best-known work is the anthology al-Kashkul ("the beggar's bowl"), often printed in the East. He also wrote an exposition of Shi'i fiqh in Persian, the Jami'-i 'Abbasi, and works on astronomy and mathematics. As a Persian poet he is known for the mathnawi Nan u-halwa, which Ethe considered a kind of introduction to the Mathnawi of Jalal al-Din Rumi, and a second mathnawi, Shir u-shakar. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 458}
