## Overview
Isma'il son of Ibrahim and [[person/hajar|Hagar]]: settled in Mecca as a suckling infant, married from Jurhum, helped his father build the House, and is the uppermost ancestor of the Adnanite Arabs.

## Life
His father settled him as a suckling with his mother in Mecca's valley; when he grew up and learned Arabic from [[group/jurhum|Jurhum]], they married him to a woman of theirs, whom he divorced at his father's signal, then he married the daughter of Mudad ibn Amr, Jurhum's chief {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

God gave him by her twelve sons: Nabit, Qaydar, Adba'il, Mibsham, Mishma', Duma, Misha, Hadad, Yatma, Yatur, Nafis and Qaydman — from whom branched twelve tribes that dwelt at Mecca then spread across the Peninsula {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab}.

## The legacy
He held the leadership of Mecca and the rule of the House all his life, and died at 137 years in the Genesis account {cite:sifr-al-takwin}. His sons kept an honoured station for what their father had of building the House {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. From his son Qaydar came Adnan, and from his line the Adnanite Arabs kept their genealogies {cite:tarikh-al-tabari}.

## For young visitors
Isma'il, Ibrahim's son, grew up in Mecca, married from the tribe of Jurhum, built the Ka'ba with his father, and many Arab tribes trace their descent to him.
