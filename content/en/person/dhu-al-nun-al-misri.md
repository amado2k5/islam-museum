## Overview
Dhu al-Nun al-Misri, a Sufi whom al-Suyuti describes as the first to speak in Egypt about the ordering of spiritual states and the stations of those close to God. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.543}

## Disputes about him
Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam, the leading figure in Egypt, disputed with him. He held that Dhu al-Nun had introduced a science that the early scholars had not spoken of, and he accused him of heresy. He was brought before [[person/al-mutawakkil|al-Mutawakkil]], who, when he heard him speak, loved him and honoured him, to the point that he would say: "When the righteous are mentioned, welcome to Dhu al-Nun." {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.543}

## In Ibn al-Nadim
Ibn al-Nadim names him Abu al-Fayd Dhu al-Nun ibn Ibrahim and says that he was a Sufi. He had a role in alchemy and wrote books, among them al-Rukn al-Akbar and al-Thiqa fi al-San'a. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.503-504}

The data does not give his birth or death dates; an additional source is needed.

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
His notice in [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (no. 456) is one of the longest in the ninth volume: it begins on page 331 and runs almost to the end of the volume. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] names him Abu al-Fayd Dhu al-Nun ibn Ibrahim al-Misri and describes him as a man of firm expressions and fine allusions. What has been read here is its opening, to page 339. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/331-332}

**His prayers.** The notice opens with long rhymed prayers related from him by his Egyptian and Syrian students. In one he asks God to make him one of those who grew uneasy in the company of the ignorant, boarded the ship of discernment and set sail on the wind of certainty. In another he approaches God through God's own favours to him, counting the stages of his making from dust to the womb, to infancy, to full reason; he then admits that he cannot count the favours, let alone give thanks for them, and that thanks is itself a favour from God. In it he says that he calls on his Lord in company as lords are called on, and in solitude as loved ones are called on. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/332-336}

**A letter to a sick man.** One of his brothers wrote to him in illness asking for his prayers. He answered that illness is something the pure of heart find company in, that whoever does not count affliction a favour is not among the wise, and he urged on him a modesty that would keep him from complaining. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/336-337}

**Before al-Mutawakkil.** Ibrahim ibn Yahya tells that when Dhu al-Nun was brought to the caliph al-Mutawakkil he was lodged in a house in the charge of Zurafa. When he met the caliph, who was mounted, and was told to greet him first, he said that the report handed down is that the rider greets the one on foot. Al-Mutawakkil smiled, greeted him first and dismounted. He asked: are you the ascetic of the people of Egypt? Dhu al-Nun said: so they say. He then preached to him with a long description of servants who worship God with a pure inner self, whose bodies are of this world and whose hearts are of heaven. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/337-338}

**Sayings.** Yusuf ibn al-Husayn relates from him that knowledge of God is reached by three things: looking at affairs and how God has arranged them, at destinies and how He has measured them, and at creatures and how He has made them. Abd al-Hakam ibn Ahmad relates that he read an inscription in Syriac on a gate in Egypt, and when he worked it out it meant: the planners plan, and destiny laughs. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/339}

The rest of the notice, from page 340 to its end, has not yet been mined.

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
After the prayers the notice turns to anecdotes and sayings, on pages 340–343 of the ninth volume. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/340}

**Generosity in religion.** On the mountains of Antioch a woman in a woollen robe met him, named him, and said that the Beloved had opened a way between her and his heart. She asked what generosity is. He said giving and bestowal. She asked what generosity in religion is. He said hastening to obey the Lord. She asked whether, when one hastens to obey, something good is loved from the Lord; he said yes, ten for one. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/340}

**Weeping.** On a journey a woman met him and asked where he was from; he said he was a stranger. She asked whether the friendship of God could ever bring the sorrows of exile, since He is the companion of strangers and the helper of the weak. He wept, and when she asked why, he said that a remedy had reached a wound that had already festered. She said that the truthful do not weep as a rule, but that weeping is rest for the heart, and that nothing the heart hides is more fitting than a sigh; when the tear falls, the heart is relieved. Then she herself wept and cried: my Lord, how long will You leave me in a house where I find no one to help me weep all my life? Then she went away. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/341}

**Sayings.** "How many an obedient one feels at home, how many a sinner feels estranged, how many a lover is humble, and every hopeful one is a seeker." The wise, he said, acknowledges his own fault and feels the faults of others, gives what he has, and excuses before being asked to excuse. He named three signs of contentment: leaving choice before the decree, the loss of bitterness after it, and love rising in the midst of trial. He named three signs of longing: loving death with ease, hating life with comfort, and lasting sorrow with sufficiency. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/341-342}

**Signs of election and of turning away.** Asked the sign of the chosen, he said: one who sets aside ease, gives full effort in obedience, and loves to lose rank. The sign of God turning toward a servant is seeing him patient, grateful and mindful; the sign of God turning away is seeing him heedless, fearful and turned from the remembrance of God. The sign of intimacy with God is that He makes you at home with His creatures and makes you feel estranged from yourself, and when He makes you feel estranged from creatures He makes you at home with Himself. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/343}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (second continuation)
Abu Nu'aym continues Dhu al-Nun's anecdotes and sayings on pages 344–349 of the ninth volume. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/344-349}}

**On the bank of the Nile.** Dhu al-Nun walked along the Nile shore in Egypt and met a girl praying; she called on God as the One near the tongues of speakers, near the hearts of those who remember, and at the edges of the proud, and said that He knew what she had done, O hope of the hopeful. Then she cried out and fell fainting. On another occasion he spent a night among the crops in the Nile fields, and a dark-skinned woman came toward him among the crops, and when she saw him she wept and went away. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/344}}

**The root of sin.** Asked the cause of sin, he said that it begins with a glance, from the glance comes a passing thought, and if the thought is met with a return to God it goes away; if it is not remembered it mingles with whisperings, and desire is born of it, and all of this is a hidden disease. He said that if you speak a request that does not gain what God does not want, and His knowledge of what you want should spare you asking. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/345}}

**The servants of the Levant coast.** He reports from a worshipper on the shore of the Levant sea that God has servants who knew Him with certainty and turned to Him in works, bore misfortunes for the rewards they hoped for, kept company with the world in sorrow, and did not look on it with a desire for it, taking from it only the provision of a traveller. They feared the night raid and so hurried; they hoped for deliverance and so resolved. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/346}}

**Refused prayer.** He related that he heard al-Hasan say he did not fear for them that their prayers would be refused, but that they would be refused the ability to pray. He said that a pure nature needs only the scent of greatness, and that wisdom is a pointer to it. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/347}}

**In the wilderness of the Children of Israel.** Sa'id ibn Uthman relates that he was with Dhu al-Nun in the desert of the Children of Israel when a woman approached. Dhu al-Nun said that no one sets foot in that place except a truthful one. When he greeted her she said: what has a man to do with mixing with women? He said: I am your brother Dhu al-Nun, and I am beyond suspicion. She welcomed him, and asked by what means he had come to know God so that he knew Him. He answered that he saw a Beloved: when he drew near, the Beloved drew him near; when he called, the Beloved answered; when he slackened, the Beloved urged him on; when he obeyed, the Beloved increased him; and when he disobeyed, the Beloved bore with him patiently. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/348-349}}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (third continuation)
Abu Nu'aym continues Dhu al-Nun's sayings on pages 350–355 of the ninth volume. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/350-355}}

**The marks of people of certainty.** He describes a company who crossed the valleys of doubt by certainty, sought help in the obligations through knowledge, proved the corruption of their hopes by gnosis, fled from the loneliness of heedlessness, clothed themselves in knowledge to guard against ignorance, passed through the darkness of the night, refuted the arguments of innovators by following the Sunna, and hastened to leave what is disliked before the chance is lost. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/350}}

**Keeping company with the knower.** He said that keeping company with the knower is like keeping company with God: he bears with you and forbears toward you, imitating God's beautiful qualities. Asked about the dhimmi and the hanif, he said the hanif is more fit for forbearance, pardon and patience. Asked how he was in the morning, he said: I am in weariness; if my weariness leaves me, death is seeking me. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/351}}

**Seeking wisdom.** He said that whoever has reached the road to the next world should ask wise men often and consult them, and that the first thing to ask about is the intellect, since all things are reached only by the intellect; if you want to serve God, understand first and then serve. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/352}}

**Withdrawal from people and from the self.** A man of Basra asked him when withdrawal from creatures would be sound for him; he said: when you are strong in withdrawing from your own self. Asked when his seeking of asceticism would be sound, he said: when you are ascetic toward your own self. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/352}}

**The party of God.** He said that God tests them until their minds, joints and hearts have no room for love of anyone else; they belong wholly to Him, and He belongs to them in this world and the next; He is pleased with them and they with Him; He loves them and they love Him. When a verse about the party of God was recited, Dhu al-Nun cried out and asked where these people are and how one reaches them. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/353}}

**Signs of the next world in God.** Yusuf ibn al-Husayn asked him the sign of the next world in God. He said there are three: clarity, mutual help and fidelity; clarity in religion, mutual help in sharing, and fidelity in affliction. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/354}}

**An old man in the mountains of Syria.** He tells that he was travelling among the mountains of Syria when he found an old man on a hillock, his eyebrows fallen over his eyes from age. He greeted him, and a short exchange followed; the old man recited verses on the love of God, and Dhu al-Nun left with a heavy heart. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/355}}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (fourth continuation)
Abu Nu'aym continues Dhu al-Nun's sayings and anecdotes on pages 356–361 of the ninth volume. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/356-361}}

**Bad manners.** Asked for the sign of bad manners, he said: much disputing. He related from Ja'far ibn Muhammad that the base man is one who does not mind what is said of him or what he says. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/356}}

**A visit to a woman worshipper.** He went in to a woman who worshipped, and asked how she was in the morning. She said that she had risen from the world, hurrying toward departure and preparing for the terror of the day of crossing; she acknowledged her shortfall in thanking God for His favours and her inability to count them. In her words was the point that a self which sells the next world for this one has no claim to a condition. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/356-357}}

**Exhortations and reproach of the self.** On pages 357–358 comes a long form of reproach and address to God: to whom has God guaranteed victory in this world and the next, so that he should seek help from one as helpless as himself? Who has God undertaken to provide for in sickness and health, so that he should seek provision from others by disobeying Him? Who knows his own worlds and yet, from ignorance, prefers the passing to the lasting? These are questions put to the heedless self. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/357-358}}

**Drawing hearts.** He said that heedless hearts are gathered by intellect, that eyes are drawn by a beautiful voice, that favour is won by success, that life is made sweet by the company of the righteous, and that all good is gathered in the righteous companion. He said that whoever longs for intimacy with God finds rest in Him, and whoever finds rest in Him has his night, his day, his secret and his public life in delight. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/359-360}}

**The house of peace.** He said that God has servants whom He settled in the house of peace; they keep their bellies clear of forbidden food, shut their eyes on the sights of sin, and bind their limbs from idle talk and from action at the wrong time. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/360}}

**Joy and grief in the knower.** Asked which of the two states dominates the knower's heart, joy or grief and sorrows, he said that in his understanding there is no state pointed to apart from another, and no cause apart from another. He gave the image of the knower in this world as a man crowned with the crown of honour and seated on a throne in a house, with a sword hung above his head by a thread. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/361}}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (fifth continuation)
Abu Nu'aym continues Dhu al-Nun's sayings and anecdotes on pages 362–368 of the ninth volume. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/362-368}}

**Triads.** He lists three marks of completeness: giving up wandering from land to land, little pleasure in praise when tested, and a purity of the self in secret and in public. Three marks of certainty: few disputes with people in companionship, no self-praise in giving, and freedom from condemning them in refusal or calamity. Three signs of trust: breaking attachments, giving up flattery, and practising patience. Three signs of submission: meeting the decree with contentment, patience in affliction, and thanks in ease. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/362-363}}

**When do I know my Lord?** Abd Allah ibn Sahl asked him when he would know his Lord. He answered: when He is your companion and you see no other as your intimate. When will I love my Lord? When what displeases Him is more bitter to you than patience. When will I long for my Lord? When you make the next world your lasting place and do not take this world as your home and dwelling. He also said that it is written in the Torah: cursed is the one who trusts a man like himself. When people of hadith came to ask him about passing thoughts and whisperings, he said he would not speak on that, since such talk takes the heart away from prayer and other duties. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/363}}

**The descriptions of love.** The lovers of God, he said, have their hearts split open, so they see by the light of hearts toward the majesty of God; their bodies are of this world, their spirits are veiled, and their intellects are of heaven. They move among the ranks of the angels as if seeing them, and they witness the sovereignty of affairs with certainty. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/366}}

**The barefoot man in the desert.** He relates that Dhu al-Nun saw in the desert a man walking barefoot, saying that love is wounded at the heart and has no rest. Asked, the man answered that the medicine had shifted the wound, and that the wound had sent forth the medicine; when he had wandered in the spirit of the holy places, the medicine became his and was with him. Dhu al-Nun then kept his company, and they set out without provision and travelled three days. The man said: I am hungry. Dhu al-Nun swore that he would not ask him anything: if God wills, He feeds; if He wills, He withholds; the story points to leaving off requesting. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/364-365}}

**What is the sign of love for God?** The man then asked Dhu al-Nun the sign of love for God. Dhu al-Nun was silent for an hour and could not find an answer, so that the man thought his soul had left his body; then he spoke and described it. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/365-366}}

**Warning against the Arabized Nabataean.** Dhu al-Nun said he takes refuge in God from the Nabataean once he has become Arab. He reports that in the desert he saw a place with an inscription warning against freed slaves, rash youths, soldiers who make a show of devotion and Nabataeans who pass as Arabs. He was a slender man. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/367}}

**Prayer and verse.** Dhu al-Nun closes with a supplication in the sense of the opening of the Qur'an, asking for guidance and steadfastness, and with a verse saying he dies with his longing not dead, and not quenched from the strife of desire toward God. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/368}}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (sixth continuation)
Abu Nu'aym continues Dhu al-Nun's sayings and anecdotes on pages 369–375 of the ninth volume. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/369-375}}

**Humility.** He said that among the noblest humility is that a person does not look at himself apart from God. When a man asked him for something, he said that the One who has undertaken your provision is not one to be suspected by you. He prayed: my Lord, all things have entered Your mercy, and only one whom doubt has led to deny You is left outside it. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/369}}

**The people of Paradise.** He says that tomorrow the people of Paradise will dwell with the maidens in the high chambers, eat what their souls desire, and be looked on by God, who will lift the veil from the hidden things for them. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/370}}

**Counsel to a seeker.** A man came asking for an admonition; Dhu al-Nun was brief and prayed for him, and recited verses urging a seeker of knowledge to let his tears fall and to stand when the striver stands, answering the call. Then the man went off crying out: O help of the upright, help me. The narrator said: be gentle with yourself; perhaps He will glance at you one glance and forgive you. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/371}}

**The ascetic who finds no intimacy in creatures.** He relates that the ascetics whose hearts are full of longing to meet God find no intimacy in creatures and do not seek provision from those who are provided for; among people they are lowly and humbled, and before God they are great. Asked how one reaches Sam'dun, he said: set your resolve straight by casting away harm, and ask the One who governs by His management. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/372}}

**The mark of a lover.** He said to someone who professes love for God: beware of humbling yourself to other than God. A sign of one who loves God is that he has no need of anyone other than God. Asked about the perfection of the intellect, he said: that you do what you are commanded and leave off straining after what you have been spared; then you are perfect in intellect. The perfection of knowledge is to cling to God in your states, not to your deeds. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/373}}

**Servants who saw and acted.** He said that God has servants who saw and then looked, understood and then knew, knew and then acted, acted and then benefited; the veil was lifted between them and Him, and they looked with the sight of their hearts at what is stored for them in the hidden of the unseen; they cut off every veil, and He was their aim and their goal. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/374}}

**At the Kaaba.** He tells that he was performing the circumambulation of the Kaaba when he saw a man clinging to its curtains, weeping and saying: I kept my affliction from anyone but You, I confided my secret to You, I was occupied with You over what is other than You. He then recited verses on the sweetness of union that increases longing. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/375}}
