## Overview
Dhu al-Nun al-Misri, a Sufi whom al-Suyuti describes as the first to speak in Egypt about the ordering of spiritual states and the stations of those close to God. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.543}

## Disputes about him
Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam, the leading figure in Egypt, disputed with him. He held that Dhu al-Nun had introduced a science that the early scholars had not spoken of, and he accused him of heresy. He was brought before [[person/al-mutawakkil|al-Mutawakkil]], who, when he heard him speak, loved him and honoured him, to the point that he would say: "When the righteous are mentioned, welcome to Dhu al-Nun." {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.543}

## In Ibn al-Nadim
Ibn al-Nadim names him Abu al-Fayd Dhu al-Nun ibn Ibrahim and says that he was a Sufi. He had a role in alchemy and wrote books, among them al-Rukn al-Akbar and al-Thiqa fi al-San'a. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.503-504}

The data does not give his birth or death dates; an additional source is needed.

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
His notice in [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (no. 456) is one of the longest in the ninth volume: it begins on page 331 and runs almost to the end of the volume. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] names him Abu al-Fayd Dhu al-Nun ibn Ibrahim al-Misri and describes him as a man of firm expressions and fine allusions. What has been read here is its opening, to page 339. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/331-332}

**His prayers.** The notice opens with long rhymed prayers related from him by his Egyptian and Syrian students. In one he asks God to make him one of those who grew uneasy in the company of the ignorant, boarded the ship of discernment and set sail on the wind of certainty. In another he approaches God through God's own favours to him, counting the stages of his making from dust to the womb, to infancy, to full reason; he then admits that he cannot count the favours, let alone give thanks for them, and that thanks is itself a favour from God. In it he says that he calls on his Lord in company as lords are called on, and in solitude as loved ones are called on. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/332-336}

**A letter to a sick man.** One of his brothers wrote to him in illness asking for his prayers. He answered that illness is something the pure of heart find company in, that whoever does not count affliction a favour is not among the wise, and he urged on him a modesty that would keep him from complaining. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/336-337}

**Before al-Mutawakkil.** Ibrahim ibn Yahya tells that when Dhu al-Nun was brought to the caliph al-Mutawakkil he was lodged in a house in the charge of Zurafa. When he met the caliph, who was mounted, and was told to greet him first, he said that the report handed down is that the rider greets the one on foot. Al-Mutawakkil smiled, greeted him first and dismounted. He asked: are you the ascetic of the people of Egypt? Dhu al-Nun said: so they say. He then preached to him with a long description of servants who worship God with a pure inner self, whose bodies are of this world and whose hearts are of heaven. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/337-338}

**Sayings.** Yusuf ibn al-Husayn relates from him that knowledge of God is reached by three things: looking at affairs and how God has arranged them, at destinies and how He has measured them, and at creatures and how He has made them. Abd al-Hakam ibn Ahmad relates that he read an inscription in Syriac on a gate in Egypt, and when he worked it out it meant: the planners plan, and destiny laughs. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/339}

The rest of the notice, from page 340 to its end, has not yet been mined.

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
After the prayers the notice turns to anecdotes and sayings, on pages 340–343 of the ninth volume. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/340}

**Generosity in religion.** On the mountains of Antioch a woman in a woollen robe met him, named him, and said that the Beloved had opened a way between her and his heart. She asked what generosity is. He said giving and bestowal. She asked what generosity in religion is. He said hastening to obey the Lord. She asked whether, when one hastens to obey, something good is loved from the Lord; he said yes, ten for one. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/340}

**Weeping.** On a journey a woman met him and asked where he was from; he said he was a stranger. She asked whether the friendship of God could ever bring the sorrows of exile, since He is the companion of strangers and the helper of the weak. He wept, and when she asked why, he said that a remedy had reached a wound that had already festered. She said that the truthful do not weep as a rule, but that weeping is rest for the heart, and that nothing the heart hides is more fitting than a sigh; when the tear falls, the heart is relieved. Then she herself wept and cried: my Lord, how long will You leave me in a house where I find no one to help me weep all my life? Then she went away. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/341}

**Sayings.** "How many an obedient one feels at home, how many a sinner feels estranged, how many a lover is humble, and every hopeful one is a seeker." The wise, he said, acknowledges his own fault and feels the faults of others, gives what he has, and excuses before being asked to excuse. He named three signs of contentment: leaving choice before the decree, the loss of bitterness after it, and love rising in the midst of trial. He named three signs of longing: loving death with ease, hating life with comfort, and lasting sorrow with sufficiency. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/341-342}

**Signs of election and of turning away.** Asked the sign of the chosen, he said: one who sets aside ease, gives full effort in obedience, and loves to lose rank. The sign of God turning toward a servant is seeing him patient, grateful and mindful; the sign of God turning away is seeing him heedless, fearful and turned from the remembrance of God. The sign of intimacy with God is that He makes you at home with His creatures and makes you feel estranged from yourself, and when He makes you feel estranged from creatures He makes you at home with Himself. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/343}
