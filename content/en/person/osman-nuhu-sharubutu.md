# Osman Nuhu Sharubutu

Osman Nuhu Sharubutu is a living Ghanaian scholar, Grand Mufti and National Chief Imam of Ghana since 1993. He was born at Old Fadama (Old Zango), Accra; the Arabic article gives 23 April 1919, the English article carries both dates — 23 April 1919 or 23 April 1923 — Wikidata carries the two competing values side by side, and the Arabic article's categories file him under births of 1341 AH, corresponding to 1922–1923, so the year is disputed.{cite:wikipedia-sharubutu-ar}{cite:wikipedia-sharubutu-en}{cite:wikidata-sharubutu}

## Upbringing and education

His father was Nuhu Sharubutu and his mother Hajja Ayishatu Abbass. He began his education at home, taught by his father by day and his mother by night, and was then sent to Kumasi, where Abdullah Dan Tano taught him Arabic grammar, Arabic literature, Islamic jurisprudence and hadith. After his studies he devoted himself to teaching Muslim youth who wished to become Islamic thinkers and scholars, and he continued to pursue knowledge even while teaching.{cite:wikipedia-sharubutu-en}{cite:wikipedia-sharubutu-ur}

## Office and works

Sharubutu was appointed Deputy Regional Chief Imam in 1974, after discussions among tribal chiefs, Islamic scholars and religious personalities, deputising for his cousin Imam Muhammed Mukhtar Abbas, who had succeeded his own father; in 1993 he became National Chief Imam of Ghana. He is the founder of the Dr. Sheikh Osmanu Nuhu Sharubutu Education Trust Fund (SONSETFund) and of the Islamic Peace and Security Council of Ghana (IPASEC), and a member of the National Peace Council. In July 2021 he led the first Friday prayer at the Ghana National Mosque at Kanda, Accra. He is known for appeals to peace and unity in communal disputes and for an interfaith posture that led former president John Kufuor to say he deserved the Nobel Peace Prize. His wife, Hajia Rahmatu, died on 27 March 2024.{cite:wikipedia-sharubutu-en}
