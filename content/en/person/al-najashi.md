## Overview
Ahmad ibn Ali ibn Ahmad ibn al-Abbas al-Najashi al-Asadi, Abu al-Abbas, was a historian of the [[group/imamiyya|Imami]] school; the Imamis are a Shi'i community whose view differs from the Sunni frame this museum takes as its default, and al-Zirikli describes him in those terms. He was known as Ibn al-Kufi and also called al-Sayrafi, and he was from Baghdad. Al-Zirikli dates his birth to 372 AH and his death to 450 AH, that is 982 and 1058 CE as printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 172}.

## Life and death
Al-Zirikli says no more of his life than that he was a man of Baghdad and that he died at Mutayrabad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 172}.

## His works
Al-Zirikli lists his [[work/rijal-al-najashi|book of Rijal]], printed, on the biographies of Shi'i scholars and their works, in which the author included himself, his lineage and his books; at the start of its second part he calls it a catalogue of the names of the Shi'a's authors and of the works of theirs that he came across. He also wrote a book on Kufa, its monuments and merits, and a book on the lineages, days and poetry of the Banu Nasr ibn Qu'ayn, who were his ancestors {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 172}.
