## Summary
Andrew Tate — a four-time world kickboxing champion who rebuilt himself as one of the internet's most-watched and most-criticised influencers — announced his conversion to Islam in October 2022, an announcement reported internationally and later seen in his public prayer and statements. {cite:muslim500-andrew-tate} {cite:wikipedia-andrew-tate}

## Career
Born in 1986 in Washington, D.C., and raised in Britain, he won multiple kickboxing world titles, appeared on the reality show Big Brother ⁦(2016)⁩, and then built online businesses — including the subscription platforms Hustlers University and The Real World — selling self-improvement content to a young male audience. Platforms repeatedly suspended his accounts over policy violations before partial reinstatements. {cite:muslim500-andrew-tate} {cite:wikipedia-andrew-tate}

## Legal proceedings
Romanian prosecutors charged Tate and his brother in 2023 with human trafficking and forming an organised crime group — charges they deny — and UK courts have issued an arrest warrant on separate allegations, with civil claims also pending. Proceedings were ongoing at the time of writing. The museum records charges as allegations, reports them attributed, and does not rule. {cite:muslim500-andrew-tate} {cite:wikipedia-andrew-tate}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him under Celebrities and Sports as an example of a mass-following Muslim convert whose public conduct divides audiences: some Muslims welcome his conversion and outreach to young men, while others — and critics broadly — point to the misogynistic content for which he became famous and to his legal cases. Positions are recorded attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-andrew-tate}
