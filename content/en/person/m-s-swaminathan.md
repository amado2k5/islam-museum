Mankombu Sambasivan Swaminathan (7 August 1925 – 28 September 2023) was an Indian geneticist, plant breeder, administrator and humanitarian who was a global leader of the green revolution and is regarded as the main architect of India's Green Revolution.{cite:wikipedia-m-s-swaminathan-en} Born at Kumbakonam in the Madras Presidency, he turned from zoology to agriculture in the shadow of the Bengal famine, took his doctorate at Cambridge, and from 1954 built his career at the Indian Agricultural Research Institute in New Delhi, where his work on high-yielding wheat and rice varieties helped move India from food imports to self-sufficiency.{cite:wikipedia-m-s-swaminathan-en}

Swaminathan directed the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (1972–1980) and then the International Rice Research Institute in the Philippines (1982–1988), the first Asian to head it.{cite:wikipedia-m-s-swaminathan-en} His leadership at IRRI was instrumental in his being awarded the first World Food Prize in 1987, and the prize money set up the M. S. Swaminathan Research Foundation in Chennai.{cite:wikipedia-m-s-swaminathan-en} He sat as a nominated member of the Rajya Sabha from 2007 to 2013, received the Padma Shri (1967), Padma Bhushan (1972) and Padma Vibhushan (1989), and was awarded the Bharat Ratna in 2024, after his death at Chennai on 28 September 2023.{cite:wikipedia-m-s-swaminathan-en}{cite:wikidata-m-s-swaminathan}
