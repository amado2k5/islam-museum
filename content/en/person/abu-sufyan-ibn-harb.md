## Brief
Abu Sufyan ibn Harb led Mecca through its open war against the Muslims — commander at Uhud and the Trench — then submitted on the eve of the conquest and died a Muslim elder. His son would found the Umayyad caliphate.

## Life
A merchant prince of [[group/banu-umayya|Banu Umayya]], Abu Sufyan led the caravan whose escape precipitated [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], then commanded the Meccan army at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] (625) and the confederate coalition at [[event/battle-of-the-trench|the Trench]] (627) {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

When Mecca could no longer resist, he went out to the Muslim camp and was received: the tradition records the Prophet declaring safe whoever entered Abu Sufyan's house. He fought with the Muslims at [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]] weeks later — and, tradition says, lost his second eye at Ta'if {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

Under the Rashidun he held governorship and saw his children placed in the new order: [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] in Syria, [[person/umm-habiba-bint-abi-sufyan|Umm Habiba]] married to the Prophet himself. He died around 653 CE, very old {cite:tabari-donner-1993}.

:::perspectives
Sunni tradition counts Abu Sufyan a companion: a late convert honored by the Prophet's amnesty, who then served the community.

Historians read his career as Meccan realpolitik — the able leader of the opposition who converted when resistance was impossible and whose family then rose inside the new state.

In Twelver Shia tradition he is remembered through Mu'awiya's war against Ali, and his late conversion is regarded with deep suspicion.
:::

## For young visitors
Abu Sufyan fought the Muslims for six years — then made peace, joined them, and lived to see his son become a caliph.
