## Summary

Hasan Hanafi (1935–2021) was an Egyptian philosopher and professor at Cairo University, one of the most controversial contemporary Arab philosophers, author of the 'Islamic Left' project and the science of 'Occidentalism'.{cite:wikipedia-hasan-hanafi}

## Life and formation

Born in Cairo, he graduated in philosophy from Cairo University, completed his studies at the Sorbonne in Paris, and returned to teach philosophy at Cairo University, where he taught generations and attached his name to the debate on heritage and renewal in Arab thought.{cite:wikipedia-hasan-hanafi}

## Intellectual projects

He launched Al-Turath wa al-Tajdid (Heritage and Renewal), a project to reread Islamic heritage through phenomenological and hermeneutic tools; then the 'Islamic Left', a thesis joining faith to social justice and liberation; then 'Occidentalism', a proposed science studying the West as the East was studied by Orientalism. His books include Min al-'Aqida ila al-Thawra and Muqaddima fi 'Ilm al-Istighrab.{cite:wikipedia-hasan-hanafi}

## Controversy and assessment

His projects were welcomed by the left, criticised by Islamists who saw in them a departure from religious constants, and criticised by liberals for their ideological framing. He died in 2021 leaving a large body of work still under debate — the museum records the descriptions without a verdict.{cite:wikipedia-hasan-hanafi}
