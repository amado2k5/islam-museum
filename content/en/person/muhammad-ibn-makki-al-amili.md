## Overview
Al-Zirikli opens the entry by calling him an Imami jurist, that is, of the Imami (Twelver) Shi'a: Muhammad ibn Makki ibn Muhammad ibn Hamid al-Amili al-Nabati al-Jizzini, Shams al-Din, called al-Shahid al-Awwal, "the First Martyr" {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 95}. His family came from al-Nabatiyya in the land of Amil, and he lived in Jizzin in Lebanon {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 95}. The entry gives 734 and 786 AH (1333 and 1384 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 95}.

## His travels
He travelled to Iraq, the Hijaz, Egypt, Damascus and Palestine, and took from their scholars {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 95}.

## His trial and death
The entry says that in the days of Sultan Barquq he was accused of laxity in belief, was imprisoned in the citadel of Damascus for a year, and was then beheaded, which is why he is called al-Shahid al-Awwal {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 95}.

## His works
Among his books are "al-Luma'a al-Dimashqiyya", "al-Risala al-Alfiyya" and "al-Risala al-Naqliyya", all printed; "al-Durus al-shar'iyya", in manuscript in the Chester Beatty library and, in two parts, in the Hakim library at Najaf; and "al-Bayan"; al-Zirikli says all of them are on Shi'i jurisprudence {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 95}.
