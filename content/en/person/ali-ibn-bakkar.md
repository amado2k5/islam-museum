## Summary
Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Bakkar was one of the ascetics of the frontier. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 452 in the ninth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], calling him a steadfast frontier guard and a fighter who returned to the charge. He records that Ali lived at [[place/massisa|al-Massisa]] as a frontier guard and kept company with [[person/ibn-adham-ibrahim|Ibrahim ibn Adham]], [[person/abu-ishaq-al-fazari|Abu Ishaq al-Fazari]] and Makhlad ibn al-Husayn. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/317}

## Reports
Abd Allah ibn Khubayq relates that Ali asked him where he lived, in a year a little after 200 AH, and on hearing "Antioch" advised him to keep to his house and go out only for what he needed. This date is the only indication of time in the notice, and the units of the year cannot be read in the scanned text. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/318}

A narrator tells that the servant girl would spread his bed, and he would touch it and say that it was soft and cool, then swear not to lie on it that night; he would pray the dawn prayer with the ablution of the night prayer. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/318}

Abu Bakr al-Maqabiri came in while he was cleaning barley for his horse and asked whether he had no one to do that for him. Ali told him that on one campaign the Muslims were routed and his horse failed him, and that since that day he had bound himself to let no one else see to its feed. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/318}

When the greetings of Hudhayfa al-Mar'ashi were brought to him, he returned them and praised Hudhayfa as a man who had eaten only what is lawful for thirty years. He then said that he would rather not meet him, because he feared he would put on a show for him, adorn himself for someone other than God, and so fall in God's sight. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/318-319}

Asked what it means to think well of God at death, he said: that God will not put you and the wicked in one abode. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/318}

## Transmission
Abu Nu'aym then gives a number of hadiths that Ali ibn Bakkar transmitted, most of them from Abu Ishaq al-Fazari from his teachers; they are related from him by Muhammad ibn Baraka al-Halabi and others. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/319-322}
