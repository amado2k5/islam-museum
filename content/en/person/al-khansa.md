## Overview
Tumadir daughter of Amr ibn al-Harith ibn al-Sharid al-Sulamiyya, titled al-Khansa. A poet of the Banu Sulaym, known before Islam for her lament over her two brothers Sakhr and Mu'awiya. She and her sons embraced Islam, and her four sons were martyred on the day of [[event/battle-of-al-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]]. The museum's data say that she sent them off to the battle. {cite:nisa-hawl-alrasul p.288-289}

Al-Mas'udi says that al-Asma'i quoted her to al-Rashid in the report of the horse race at al-Raqqa. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 350}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy relates that al-Khansa' urged her four sons to fight and seek martyrdom, and that when news came that they had been killed she praised God who had honoured her by their deaths and hoped He would gather her with them in the abode of His mercy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep02 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep02 00:20:00}

## In Kahhala's A'lam al-Nisa'
Kahhala names her Tumadir bint Amr ibn al-Harith ibn al-Sharid, a famous poet and Companion who came to the Prophet with her people of Banu Sulaym and embraced Islam; he admired her poetry, asked her to recite and would say, "Go on, Khunas." {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.364} He reports that Jarir, asked who was the greatest poet, said "I am — but for al-Khansa'", and that al-Nabigha told her at the fair of Ukaz that had al-A'sha not just recited to him he would have called her the greatest poet of jinn and men. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.364} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.365} He gives her exhortation to her four sons on the eve of al-Qadisiyya, their rajaz verses and their deaths one after another, her words on hearing the news — "Praise be to God who honoured me with their death" — and that Umar ibn al-Khattab paid her her four sons' stipends, two hundred dirhams each, until his death. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.373} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.374} He says she died in the desert at the start of Uthman's caliphate in 24 AH, and closes the entry with some thirty sources. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.375} Kahhala notes that Ibn al-Jawzi gives the exhortation in *Sifat al-Safwa* for Khansa' bint Amr al-Nakha'iyya, a devout woman of the desert, and that he placed it under the poet. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.375}
