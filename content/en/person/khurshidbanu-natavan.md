# Khurshidbanu Natavan

## Summary
Khurshidbanu Natavan (1832–1897) was an Azerbaijani poet and philanthropist, the daughter and sole heir of Mehdigulu Khan, the last khan of Karabakh, known to the public as "the khan's daughter" (Khan qizi). {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} She is considered one of Azerbaijan's best lyrical poets, writing ghazals in Azerbaijani and Persian; she founded the Majlis-i Uns literary society at Shusha, laid the town's first water main, and was famed for the Karabakh horses she bred and showed across Europe. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-az}

## Early life and education
She was born at Shusha — the leads of all four articles and Wikidata give 6 August 1832, while the Life sections of the English and Arabic articles give 5 August, and the museum follows the majority reading while disclosing the split — the only child of Mehdigulu Khan (1763–1845) and Badir Jahan Begum, named after her grandmother Khurshid Begum, daughter of Javad Khan. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ar} {cite:wikidata-khurshidbanu-natavan} She was raised by her aunt Gawhar agha, who taught her music, poetry and painting; she was educated at home, learned eastern languages, and read Firdowsi, Nizami, Saadi, Hafiz, Navoi and Fuzuli. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} The Azerbaijani article adds that as a girl she memorised Qur'an verses and religious rulings alongside secular subjects — a detail of her education, recorded here in its place. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-az}

## Inheritance and family
Her father died when she was fourteen, leaving her vast lands — 1,315 households, 41 nomadic territories and seven villages — and in 1861 she inherited nine more villages from her mother. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} She probably married the Kumyk noble Khasay Utsmiev in 1847; their children were Mehdigulu Khan Vafa and Khanbike Khanum (1856–1921). In 1866 she married Seyyid Huseyn Agamirov, a commoner of Shusha, with whom she had five children. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} In 1858 she met Alexandre Dumas at Baku and exchanged gifts with him, and Dumas wrote about the meeting. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru}

## The Majlis-i Uns and her poetry
She founded and sponsored the first literary societies in Shusha and in Azerbaijan as a whole, above all the Majlis-i Uns ("Society of Friends"), which gathered the leading poetic and musical figures of Karabakh — the English article dates its founding to 1864, while the Russian article says she organised and headed it in 1872, and the museum records both readings. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} She began writing poetry in the 1850s under the signature "Khurshid", most of which is lost; from 1870 she took the pen name Natavan — Persian for "powerless, helpless". {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-az} Humanism, kindness, friendship and love are the main themes of her ghazals and ruba'yat; many are sentimental romantic poems expressing the feelings of a woman unhappy in her family life who lost a son young, and many are still sung as folk songs. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} Her full divan has not survived; Salman Mumtaz published the first collection of her poems in 1928. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru}

## Philanthropy
She was closely engaged in the social and cultural development of Karabakh: under her, Shusha gained a palace, a mosque, trade houses, a theatre, summer and winter club buildings and a real school, as the Russian article lists them — works of patronage and building, recorded here in their place in her life. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} Her most famous deed was the water main first laid to Shusha in 1872 from the Isa spring at Sarybaba, ten kilometres away, at a cost of one hundred thousand roubles; it solved the town's water problem and is still known as the "Khan gizi spring" and the "Natavan springs". {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} She also did much for the Karabakh horse breed: her horses were shown at the Exposition Universelle in Paris (1867), the Moscow agricultural exhibition (1869) and Tbilisi (1882), winning gold medals and certificates of honour. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en}

## Death and tomb
She died at Shusha — the English, Azerbaijani and Arabic articles and Wikidata give 2 October 1897, while the Russian article gives "September – 8 October 1897", and the museum follows the majority reading at debated status, disclosing the split — and was buried in the Imarat cemetery at Aghdam. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} {cite:wikidata-khurshidbanu-natavan} After the occupation of the Aghdam district in the First Karabakh War her tomb was damaged, and in January 2021, after the district returned to Azerbaijan, the photojournalist Reza Deghati reported the cemetery destroyed and her tomb looted and her bones missing. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} Her Shusha bust by Hayat Abdullayeva (1982), which had been carried off to Baku bullet-pocked after the city's occupation, was returned to Shusha in January 2021; monuments to her also stand at Baku (1960), Waterloo in Belgium and Évian in France. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru}

## Perspectives and criticism
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**Her faith.** No source names a school for Natavan personally. The Russian article states, in its Private life section, that she belonged to the former ruling family of the Karabakh Khanate — the Javanshirs — "who were Shia Muslims": the predicate attaches to her family, and under the museum's rule family background is not attestation, so the Shia-mapping rule does not engage. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} Everything else is context: the Qur'an memorisation of her childhood is education, and the mosque she built in Shusha is patronage. {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-az} {cite:wikipedia-khurshidbanu-natavan-ru} Wikidata carries no religion value for her. {cite:wikidata-khurshidbanu-natavan} Her faith is therefore recorded as unknown, with the Russian article's family statement preserved in full in her data record: two independent sources naming a school for Natavan herself would change it.
