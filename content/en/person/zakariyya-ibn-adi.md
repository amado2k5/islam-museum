## Overview
Zakariyya ibn Adi ibn Zurayq, also said to be Ibn al-Salt, al-Taymi, a client of the tribe, of Kufa, Abu Yahya, is called by al-Dhahabi the imam, the firm memoriser; he settled in Baghdad and was the brother of Yusuf ibn Adi who settled in Egypt, their father Adi having been a dhimmi who became Muslim {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}. The sign printed with his entry shows that al-Bukhari and al-Tirmidhi recorded his hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Hammad ibn Zayd, Sharik, Abu al-Ahwas, Hushaym, Ibn al-Mubarak, Yazid ibn Zuray', Ubayd Allah ibn Amr al-Raqqi and their generation {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}. Ishaq ibn Rahawayh, Ishaq al-Kawsaj, Abd ibn Humayd, Abu Muhammad al-Darimi, Hajjaj ibn al-Sha'ir, Mu'awiya ibn Salih al-Dimashqi and Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari, outside the Sahih and in it through an intermediary, narrated from him, with many others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}.

## What the scholars said
Al-Ijli called him a reliable Kufan, a righteous and austere man; al-Mundhir ibn Shadhan said he had seen no better memoriser; Abd al-Rahman ibn Kharash said reliable and scrupulous; Abu Awf al-Bazuri said he had written from no one better {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}. It is told that when Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Yahya came and asked for the book of Ubayd Allah ibn Amr, he told them to take it so he could dictate all of it, and that he distinguished the wording of the companions of al-A'mash when he narrated from them {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}. It is said that when his grave was dug he said, O God, I long for You; that he came back after a month from a post over a village at thirty dirhams a month because he did not find himself working to the value of the pay; and that he refused a man's eye ointment because the man was one who heard hadith from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}. Al-Dhahabi says Abu Nu'aym al-Kufi attacked him without proof, saying what had he to do with hadith, he knew the Torah better {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}. Ibn Sa'd said he was among the clients of Taym Allah, a righteous and reliable man {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}.

## His death
Ibn Sa'd says he died in Jumada I 211 AH; Isma'il ibn Abi al-Harith and others say he died on the second of Jumada II 212 AH in Baghdad {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1722}.
