## Overview
Abd al-Malik ibn Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz was an Umayyad prince, son of the caliph [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]]. Al-Zirikli says he lived at his father's side and died shortly before him, being one of the people dearest to him; he gives his death as 101 AH, 719 CE, with no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 161}.

## Life
Al-Zirikli quotes Ibn Abd al-Hakam as saying that God aided Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz with three helpers, one of them his son Abd al-Malik, who supported him in the right and strengthened him in his task {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 161}. When Umar took office, Abd al-Malik told his father that he had postponed matters that he, Abd al-Malik, had thought he would hurry if he held office for part of a day, and wished he would act on them even if the cooking pots boiled over with the two of them; his father accused him of the haste of youth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 161}. The three died one after another at Dayr Sim'an near al-Ma'arra, and Umar grieved and wished for death {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 161}.

## Writings about him
Al-Zirikli notes that Ibn Rajab ([[person/ibn-rajab|the Hanbali scholar]]) wrote a treatise, "Sirat Abd al-Malik ibn Umar", in manuscript, which he saw in the Saudi Library in Riyadh, no. 54 / 86 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 161}.
