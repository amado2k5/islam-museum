## Overview
Khidr ibn Jalal al-Din ibn Ahmad, al-Mawla al-Rumi al-Hanafi, known as Hizir Bey, was the first man appointed qadi of Constantinople after its conquest. Born and raised at the town of Sivri Hisar, his fame rose until Sultan Mehmed the Conqueror summoned him to Bursa and gave him his grandfather's madrasa there, and on taking Constantinople made him its judge. He died in 863 AH (1459 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.306}

He was deeply read in Arabic, Turkish and Persian letters and composed verse in all three. Of his Arabic poetry, Jawahir al-'Aqa'id is a nuniyya on tawhid sent to the Sultan with two lines, the second 'O Sultan, my verse, hastened in a night or two', whence it is called 'the haste of two nights'. His writings include glosses on al-Taftazani's Hashiya on the Kashshaf and an urjuza on prosody. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.306}
