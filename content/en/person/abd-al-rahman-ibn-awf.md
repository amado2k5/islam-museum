## Summary
Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf al-Zuhri al-Qurashi was among the earliest Muslims during the [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|secret call]] in Mecca, according to al-Raheeq al-Makhtum. He was one of the eight of the first vanguard who embraced Islam through the call of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}.

## In al-Raheeq al-Makhtum
After his own conversion, Abu Bakr was active in the call. He began calling those among his people whom he trusted, who visited him and sat with him. Through his call, Uthman ibn Affan, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, both of Banu Zuhra, and Talha ibn Ubaydullah embraced Islam. These eight, together with the first four, formed the first vanguard and the forerunners of Islam {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. He belongs to [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## Not covered here
This chapter of the book mentions nothing beyond his early conversion. The rest of his story is completed in its own places in the book and in other sources.

## For young visitors
Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf was among the first to believe in the Prophet. His friend Abu Bakr called him, and he embraced Islam. He was one of the eight who went ahead of others into Islam.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy recounts that Umar set up a council of six, and that Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf was to choose between Uthman and Ali (needs_source: the order is as the sheikh gives it) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:42:00}. He recounts that Abd al-Rahman asked Uthman to govern the people by the Qur'an, the Sunna and the conduct of the two predecessors, and Uthman agreed, while Ali refused (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:51:30}. He recounts that Talha, al-Zubayr and Sa'd withdrew, leaving Abd al-Rahman with Ali and Uthman; he then raised Uthman's hand, and Uthman accepted the conditions of the community (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:52:00}.

He recounts that Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf offered his sister in marriage to Bilal ibn Rabah (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:18:30}. He recounts that Abd al-Rahman threw his cloak over the man who attacked Umar, and that the man then killed himself (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:46:30}.

The sheikh lists him among the Companions as a merchant (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:53:00}. He recounts that after his death his wealth was divided among his heirs, and that its start was in Medina, while the rest of the account is garbled in the automatic transcript (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:55:30}.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the fifth episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] presents Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf as one of the senior Companions and early believers, who became Muslim when the Muslims could be counted on the fingers, and one of the fighting heroes of the [[group/muhajirun|Muhajirun]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:00:30}. He sees him as "an honourable model of the Muslim businessman": a great merchant who owned millions yet was never made proud by his wealth, but was humble and generous, the world beneath his feet and the hereafter in his heart, spending in secret and in public {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:01:30}. He names him Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf al-Zuhri, of [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]], the Prophet's maternal kin, and one of [[concept/ashara-mubashshara|the Ten promised Paradise]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:02:00}, and gives the hadith of the Ten as Ibn Awf himself narrated it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:02:30}.

He says the Prophet first offered Islam to his friend [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], who accepted at once without hesitation, and Abu Bakr then invited the men of good character and honour among his friends {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:03:00}; six of the Ten, he says, became Muslim through him within a single week {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:03:30}. This agrees with al-Raheeq al-Makhtum, where Ibn Awf embraces Islam at Abu Bakr's invitation {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}. He relates that Abu Bakr brought him to the Prophet, at whose hands he became Muslim; the Prophet asked his name, he said Abd Amr, and the Prophet changed it to Abd al-Rahman {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:04:00}. He says Ibn Awf went on [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|the first emigration to Abyssinia]] when the Prophet permitted it, telling them there was a king there under whom no one was wronged; he came back after some months because he could not bear to be parted from the Prophet, suffered much harm, and then emigrated to Medina, so that for Anwar he is "the man of the two emigrations" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:05:00}.

He relates that in Medina the Prophet joined him in brotherhood with [[person/sad-ibn-al-rabi|Sa'd ibn al-Rabi']] of the Ansar, who offered to split his wealth with him in half and to divorce one of his two wives so that Ibn Awf could marry her once her waiting period was over {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:06:30}; he comments that the restraint of the Muhajirun matched the generosity of the Ansar {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:07:30}. Ibn Awf prayed that God bless Sa'd's wealth and family, said he was a merchant and asked to be shown the market, and worked there until he came back with dried curd and ghee, which became his capital {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:08:00}. Days later the Prophet saw a trace of yellow scent on him and asked about it; Ibn Awf said he had married for a date-stone's weight of gold, which Anwar estimates at about three grams, and the Prophet told him to give a wedding feast, even if only with one sheep; he draws the lesson that the Companions kept dowries light {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:08:30}. He relates from al-Tabarani and al-Haythami, whose narrators he says are those of the Sahih, through [[person/busra-bint-safwan|Busra bint Safwan]], that the Prophet asked her who was seeking the hand of [[person/umm-kulthum-bint-uqba|Umm Kulthum bint Uqba]]; she named several, Ibn Awf among them, and he advised marrying her to Ibn Awf, as one of the best of the Muslims {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:12:00}.

He says the Prophet prayed behind no human being except two men, Abu Bakr and Ibn Awf {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:10:00}, and relates from Sahih Muslim and the Sunan of al-Nasa'i, through Amr ibn Wahb al-Thaqafi, that they were with [[person/al-mughira-ibn-shuba|al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba]] when he was asked whether anyone besides Abu Bakr had ever led the Prophet in prayer; he recounted that the Prophet made ablution, wiped over his socks and his turban and prayed one rak'a of the dawn prayer behind Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, after which the two of them made up what they had missed {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:10:30}. He relates from Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, graded hasan sahih as he says, through [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Sa'id ibn Zayd]], that Ibn Awf was with the Prophet and others of the Ten on Hira when the Prophet struck the mountain with his foot and bade it be still, for on it stood only a prophet, a siddiq or a martyr {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:11:00}.

He reports from [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]]'s Siyar, through Talha son of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, that the people of Medina were all dependent on Ibn Awf: a third he lent to, a third he paid debts for, and a third he gave gifts to {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:13:30}; and from [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]] that he left a bequest to the veterans of Badr, of whom a hundred were alive after his death, each receiving four hundred dinars, and that [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] was among them and took his share; he explains a dinar as a gold pound {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:14:30}. He relates from [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]]'s Mustadrak, which he calls sound by Muslim's criteria, through [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], a hadith that the best of people are those best to the Prophet's family after him, and says that Ibn Awf sold a garden for four hundred thousand and divided it among the Prophet's wives {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:15:00}. When the Prophet called for donations for the Army of Hardship, he says, Ibn Awf gave two hundred ounces of gold and placed them in the Prophet's lap, to the people's astonishment {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:15:30}; and he reports from al-Dhahabi's Siyar that in the Prophet's time Ibn Awf gave away half his wealth, then forty thousand dinars, then equipped five hundred horses and five hundred mounts for God's cause, and in a single day gave away thirty-one slaves, possibly meaning that he freed them {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:16:00}.

He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari, through [[person/sad-ibn-ibrahim-al-zuhri|Sa'd ibn Ibrahim]] from his father, that Ibn Awf could not be told apart from his slaves, so plain were his clothes {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:17:00}, and that when food was brought to him while he was fasting he wept, recalling that [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]], a better man than he, was killed and shrouded in a cloak that left his feet bare when it covered his head, and [[person/hamza|Hamza]] likewise, and fearing that their own good deeds had been rewarded early in this world; then he left the food {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:17:30}. He cites a hadith that he attributes to Ahmad and Ibn Maja, that plainness of appearance is part of faith, explains it as modesty in dress, and warns against extravagance in clothing while allowing good clothes {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:21:30}; and he relates from al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim, graded hasan by al-Albani as he says, that whoever gives up fine clothing out of humility before God while able to afford it will be called by God on the Day of Resurrection before all creation to choose whichever robe of faith he wishes {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:22:00}.

He reports from al-Dhahabi's Siyar, through Abd al-Rahman ibn Azhar, that when Uthman fell ill he had his client [[person/humran-ibn-aban|Humran]] write the succession after him to Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf; when Ibn Awf learned of it he stood between the pulpit and the grave and prayed that, if Uthman had named him, God would take him before Uthman, and he died about six months before him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:18:30}. He compares this with Abu Bakr's naming of Umar, says that Uthman ruled what would today be about sixty countries, and draws the lesson of Ibn Awf's indifference to power {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:19:00}. He reports al-Dhahabi as counting among Ibn Awf's finest acts that he withdrew himself from the office at [[event/shura-council-of-uthman|the Shura]] and chose for the community the man indicated by those who loose and bind, whereas a lover of power would have taken it for himself or given it to his cousin [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:20:30}. He relates from al-Hakim, with a chain he says al-Busiri graded sound, through Ibrahim son of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, that his father fainted in his illness so deeply that he was thought dead; when he came round he said that two harsh men had taken him away to be judged, until a man met them and told them to bring him back, for he was among those for whom happiness and forgiveness were written while still in their mothers' wombs, and his sons would have the enjoyment of him for as long as God willed {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:22:30}. He says Ibn Awf died in Uthman's time, in 32 AH, that Uthman led the funeral prayer over him and that he was buried in [[place/baqi-al-gharqad|al-Baqi']] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:24:30}, and that [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] said at his funeral that he had known the clear water of this world and gone before its dregs {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:24:30}.
