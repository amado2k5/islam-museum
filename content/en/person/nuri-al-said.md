# Nuri al-Said

Nuri al-Said (December 1888 – 15 July 1958) was an Iraqi politician who dominated the political life of Hashemite Iraq, serving fourteen terms as prime minister between 1930 and 1958. Born in Baghdad, he had been an Ottoman officer, a figure of the Arab Revolt and a founder of the Iraqi army before becoming the monarchy's indispensable statesman. {cite:wikipedia-nuri-al-said-en} {cite:wikipedia-nuri-al-said-ar}

## Career
Nuri's governments pursued close alignment with Britain, negotiated the fifty-fifty oil profit-sharing agreement of the early 1950s, created the Iraqi Development Board and brought Iraq into the Baghdad Pact of 1955. To his detractors his blend of Iraqi nationalism, conservatism, pro-Western policy and anti-communism failed to adapt to a changing society, and by the late 1950s he was deeply unpopular with several segments of it. {cite:wikipedia-nuri-al-said-en}

## Death
When the coup of 14 July 1958 overthrew the monarchy, Nuri attempted to flee the country. He was captured in Baghdad and killed on 15 July 1958. {cite:wikipedia-nuri-al-said-en} {cite:wikipedia-nuri-al-said-ar} {cite:wikidata-nuri-al-said}
