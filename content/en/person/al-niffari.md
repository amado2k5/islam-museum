## Summary

Muhammad ibn Abd al-Jabbar ibn al-Hasan al-Niffari was an Iraqi Sufi, author of [[work/al-mawaqif-wal-mukhatabat|al-Mawaqif wa-l-Mukhatabat]] {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}. The source used here is Mostafa Mahmoud's [[work/raaytu-allah|Ra'aytu Allah]], a modern writer who admires al-Niffari, so what is reported here is that book's account.

## What is known of his life

Mostafa Mahmoud writes that the details of al-Niffari's life are "unknown", and that no more is known of him than that he lived in the fourth century after the Hijra in the town of Niffar in Iraq, loved solitary retreat and spent most of his life in worship and contemplation {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}. He calls him "the knowing imam" and "the Sufi" {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 13} {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}.

## His book

According to the same source, al-Niffari's book is "a collection of scraps" he left at his death, gathered by his followers, which speak of the spirit, the body and the self and explain tawhid, Islam and the Qur'an "in a language of great depth" {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}. Among the sayings Mostafa Mahmoud quotes is that God "is the proof of things and is not proved by them" {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 13}.

## His style and doctrine, as Mostafa Mahmoud sees them

Mostafa Mahmoud holds that al-Niffari has no doctrine of his own but walks the Sufi path, standing out by "an astonishing power of expression and a burning language" {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 148}. He sums up his doctrine of divine knowledge as transcendence and crossing over: leaving self and body while "keeping to servanthood all the time" {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 156}.

:::perspectives
- **Mostafa Mahmoud's view.** Al-Niffari's "my Lord said to me" and "He stayed me" are Sufi language, not "a claim to prophethood", and the man was "committed to the Qur'an in letter and meaning and to the Sunna of Muhammad in conduct and following" {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}.

## For young visitors

Al-Niffari lived more than a thousand years ago in Iraq. He loved being alone to think, and he wrote short, deep sayings that his students gathered after he died {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}.
