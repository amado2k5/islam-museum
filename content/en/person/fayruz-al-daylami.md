## Overview
Fayruz al-Daylami of the Abna, the Persian families whom Khosrow sent to Yemen with the son of Dhu Yazan, under the command of Wahriz. He embraced Islam, and Qays ibn Hubayra al-Muradi won him over to kill al-Aswad al-Ansi. He was among those who entered upon al-Aswad by stealth at dawn in his house at Sana'a. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Who killed al-Aswad
Opinions differ on the killer. Some said that Qays slaughtered him and cut off his head. Others said that Fayruz killed him and that Qays gave him the credit. The source reports that when the two claimed it in Medina, Umar said: "This lion killed him", meaning Fayruz. Some scholars mention that the Prophet said, five days before his death: "God has killed al-Aswad al-Ansi; a righteous man, Fayruz ibn al-Daylami, killed him." See [[event/killing-of-al-aswad-al-ansi|the killing of al-Aswad al-Ansi]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Abd al-Rahman Ra'fat al-Basha's Suwar min Hayat al-Sahaba
[[person/abd-al-rahman-rafat-al-basha|Al-Basha]] in [[work/suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba|Suwar min Hayat al-Sahaba]] tells the [[event/killing-of-al-aswad-al-ansi|killing of al-Aswad al-Ansi]] in Fayruz's own voice. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.450} He explains that power in Yemen then lay with the Abna' — descendants of Persians who had settled in Yemen, with Arab mothers — headed by Fayruz, and that their chief [[person/badhan|Badhan]] had accepted Islam and been confirmed by the Prophet in his rule until he died shortly before al-Aswad appeared. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.449} He describes [[person/al-aswad-al-ansi|al-Aswad]] as a soothsayer and conjurer, huge and eloquent, who showed himself only veiled; Madhhij were the first to follow him, and he fell on Sana'a, killed its governor Shahr ibn Badhan and married his widow Azad. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.448} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.449}
In Fayruz's narration, he and his cousin Dadhawayh went to al-Aswad's army commander [[person/qays-ibn-makshuh|Qays ibn Abd Yaghuth]], who had come to fear his tyranny, and the three pledged to strike from within; they enlisted Fayruz's cousin Azad, who showed them a remote room in the palace whose wall they could breach; they entered at night, Fayruz brought the blade down on his neck, and Azad sent the guards away by telling them the prophet was receiving revelation. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.451} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.452} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.453} At dawn Fayruz gave the call to prayer from the palace wall, bearing witness that al-Aswad was a liar — the agreed signal — and threw the head down to his followers, who broke. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.454} Al-Basha closes with the messengers reaching Medina after the Prophet had died, and with the report that he had already told his Companions of al-Aswad's death, saying, "A blessed man of a blessed household killed him." {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.454}
