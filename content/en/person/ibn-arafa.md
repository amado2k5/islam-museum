## Overview
Muhammad ibn Muhammad Ibn Arafa al-Warghami, Abu Abdallah, was the imam, scholar and preacher of Tunis in his time, born and died there. The printed text gives his life as 716 AH, 1316 CE, to 803 AH, and puts 1400 CE beside it {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}. His name points to Warghama, a village in Ifriqiya {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.

## His offices
He took the imamate of the Great Mosque in 750 AH, was advanced to its preaching in 772 and to the giving of legal opinions in 773 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.

## His books
Al-Zirikli names "al-Mukhtasar al-Kabir", printed, on Maliki jurisprudence; "al-Mukhtasar al-Shamil", in manuscript, on the oneness of God; "Mukhtasar al-Fara'id", in manuscript; "al-Mabsut", on jurisprudence, in seven volumes, of which al-Sakhawi said that it is extremely obscure; "al-Turuq al-Wadiha fi 'Amal al-Munasaha", in manuscript; and "al-Hudud", printed, on legal definitions. Muhammad ibn Qasim al-Rassa' wrote "al-Hidaya al-Kafiya", printed, on his life and his rulings {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.

## The year of his death
Al-Zirikli says the sources agree on 803 AH, but that the author of "'Unwan al-Arib" relates that a trustworthy man told him the inscription on his tomb says he died on 20 Jumada II 800 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}. The museum records both without choosing between them.
