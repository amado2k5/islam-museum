# Ali Daei

## Summary

Ali Daei is an Iranian former footballer and manager who was born in Ardabil on 21 March 1969.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-ar}
A striker who captained Iran, he scored 108 international goals, a world record that stood until 2021, and was the first man to reach a hundred international goals.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
With Bayern Munich and Hertha Berlin he became the first Asian player to play, and to score, in the UEFA Champions League.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}

## Early life

Daei was born in Ardabil to an Iranian Azerbaijani family.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
He studied materials engineering at Sharif University of Technology in Tehran, graduating while his football career was already under way.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}

## Club career

Daei began with Esteghlal Ardabil in his home city, then played for Taxirani and Bank Tejarat in Tehran before joining Persepolis in 1994.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
After a season at Al Sadd in Qatar he moved to Germany with Arminia Bielefeld in 1997, alongside his compatriot Karim Bagheri, and then to Bayern Munich, with whom he won the Bundesliga in 1998–99 although his playing time was limited.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
At Hertha Berlin he scored twice against Chelsea in the Champions League in September 1999 and once against AC Milan at the San Siro.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
He returned to Iran via Al Shabab in the Emirates, playing again for Persepolis and for Saba Battery, and finished his career as player-manager of Saipa, retiring in May 2007 after winning the league title.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}

## International career

Daei captained Iran from 2000 to 2006 and played at the 1998 and 2006 World Cups.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
His 108 goals in 148 internationals made him the most prolific scorer in international football history until Cristiano Ronaldo passed the mark in 2021; he had become the first man to score a hundred international goals in November 2004.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
The Arabic article gives the older figures of 149 caps and 109 goals; the English article's corrected totals are followed here.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-ar}{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
He was a UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador from 2001, served on FIFA's Football Committee from 2007 to 2013, and was inducted into the Asian Football Hall of Fame in 2014.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}

## Managerial career

Daei managed Saipa, then the Iran national team in 2008–09, and later Persepolis, Rah Ahan, Saba Qom and Naft Tehran, winning the Hazfi Cup with Persepolis and again with Naft Tehran in 2017, before a final spell at Saipa ended in 2019.{cite:wikipedia-ali-daei-en}
