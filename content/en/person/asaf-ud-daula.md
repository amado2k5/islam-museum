## Summary

Asaf-ud-Daula (23 September 1748 – 21 September 1797) was the fourth Nawab of Awadh, reigning from 26 January 1775 to 21 September 1797, a son of Shuja-ud-Daula and Bahu Begum {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ud-daula-en}.

## Lucknow and the Imambara

He succeeded at Faizabad with British support against his younger brother's mutiny, and in 1775 moved his capital to Lucknow, escaping the dominance of the begums at Faizabad and making the city his court's seat {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ud-daula-en}. There he built on a famed scale — the Daulat Khana palace, the Rumi Darwaza gateway, and above all the Bara Imambara, begun in 1784 as famine relief work for his subjects and counted among the largest arched halls in the world {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ud-daula-en}. He was a patron of Urdu and Persian letters and a poet in both languages, with a diwan to his name; the Delhi poets Sauda and Mir came to his Lucknow court {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ud-daula-ur}. He died at Lucknow on 21 September 1797 and was buried in the central hall of his own Imambara {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ud-daula-ar}.

## Faith

His faith is recorded as Shia Twelver, documented. The Arabic article gives his religion as Shia Muslim {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ud-daula-ar}, and the English Oudh State article states, citing the Encyclopaedia of Islam, that the Nawabs of Oudh were Shia Muslims who promoted Shia as the state religion {cite:wikipedia-oudh-state-en}; his own documented practice matches the Twelver context — he founded the Bara Imambara, a Husainiya for the Muharram rites, and was buried in it {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ud-daula-ar}. Under the founder's mapping of 1 November 2026 these attestations settle shia-twelver; Wikidata's religion value and the article's category filings are structured data and were not counted.
