## Summary
Nasreddine Dinet, born Alphonse Étienne Dinet (Paris 1861 – Paris, 24 December 1929), was a French painter of Algerian life. He first visited Bou Saâda in 1884, learned Arabic and came to live in the oasis town, announced his conversion to Islam in a private letter in 1908, completed it formally in 1913 and took the name Nasr ad-Din, and made the pilgrimage to Mecca in 1929. {cite:aljazeera-dinet-french-orientalist-2019 film page} {cite:dawn-french-orientalist-exhibition-2024 article} {cite:wikipedia-nasreddine-dinet-en Biography}

## Background
He was born in Paris to a well-to-do family; his father was a prominent advocate for Algeria, and he began in law, as the family tradition required, before an art exhibition turned him to painting. He studied at the Lycée Henry IV, the École des Beaux-Arts and the Académie Julian, co-founded the Société des Peintres Orientalistes Français in 1887 and won a silver medal at the Exposition Universelle of 1889. {cite:dawn-french-orientalist-exhibition-2024 article} {cite:wikipedia-nasreddine-dinet-en Biography}

## The path to Islam
In 1884, aged 22, he went to Bou Saâda with an entomologist friend. He returned again and again; by his fourth visit, in 1888, he had learned Arabic and travelled with Sliman Ben Ibrahim, a guide who became a lifelong friend and collaborator, and in the 1900s he was fully bilingual. He bought a house in the Arab quarter of Bou Saâda in 1903, partly, Al Jazeera says, to irritate the colonial administration; Dawn dates his permanent settlement to 1901. In 1908 he announced his conversion in a personal letter, and in 1913 he converted formally and took the name Nasreddine. The sources read do not describe the reasoning behind the step. {cite:aljazeera-dinet-french-orientalist-2019 film page} {cite:dawn-french-orientalist-exhibition-2024 article} {cite:wikipedia-nasreddine-dinet-en Biography}

## After embracing Islam
He kept painting Algerian landscapes, shepherds and town scenes, and in the hot months photographed scenes that he painted later in Paris. He also translated Arabic literature into French, among it an epic of Antar ibn Shaddad in 1898. In 1929, aged 68, he made the pilgrimage to Mecca with his wife and died in Paris a few months later, on 24 December. At his request he was buried in the Muslim cemetery of Bou Saâda; about five thousand people attended the funeral, according to the English Wikipedia, and Dawn adds that the French government later recognised him as a builder of friendly relations between France and Algeria. {cite:dawn-french-orientalist-exhibition-2024 article} {cite:aljazeera-dinet-french-orientalist-2019 film page} {cite:wikipedia-nasreddine-dinet-en Biography}

## Difficulties and reactions
Al Jazeera's film page, citing his biographer Nacer Lemdjed, says that after the announcement of 1908 fellow artists and the ruling class attacked him, and that he was excluded from the exhibitions that had been his main income. The sources read do not report how his own family reacted. {cite:aljazeera-dinet-french-orientalist-2019 film page}

## Criticism and debate
The English Wikipedia describes his painting, set beside modernists such as Henri Matisse, as conservative and highly ethnographic, a judgement about style rather than about his faith. His own place in the colonial setting is read in different ways in the sources: Al Jazeera presents his house in the Arab quarter as partly a gesture against the colonial administration, while Dawn records the later French recognition of him as a friend of both countries. The museum records both readings. {cite:wikipedia-nasreddine-dinet-en Biography} {cite:aljazeera-dinet-french-orientalist-2019 film page} {cite:dawn-french-orientalist-exhibition-2024 article}
