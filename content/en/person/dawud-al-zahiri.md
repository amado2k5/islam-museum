## Overview
Dawud ibn Ali al-Isbahani, known as Dawud al-Zahiri, founder of the [[group/zahiri-madhhab|Zahiri school]]. {cite:duha-islam p.567}

## Life
He was born in Kufa around 200 AH, grew up in Baghdad, and died there in 270 AH. {cite:duha-islam p.567}

## His school
Ahmad Amin notes that he first studied the school of al-Shafi'i and defended it, and wrote in praise of it. He then became independent and founded a school known as the Zahiri school, which many people followed, especially in Persia and al-Andalus. {cite:duha-islam p.567}

He rejected analogy (qiyas) and held to the apparent meaning of the Book and the Sunna, allowing analogy only where the cause was stated explicitly. He attacked the analogists and showed, according to the Book, the errors in legal rulings to which analogy had led them. {cite:duha-islam p.568}

## In al-Fihrist of Ibn al-Nadim
Abu Sulayman Dawud ibn Ali ibn Dawud ibn Khalaf al-Isbahani, the first to adopt the apparent meaning of texts. He took the Book and the Sunna and set aside all other sources, including opinion and analogy, according to al-Mas'udi. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.579}

## In al-Fihrist of Ibn al-Nadim: Life
Al-Mas'udi describes him as a virtuous, truthful and pious man, and says that he died in Baghdad in 270 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.579} Al-Shirazi mentions that he was born in 202 AH. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981}

## In al-Fihrist of Ibn al-Nadim: Teachers and students
He studied under [[person/ishaq-ibn-rahwayh|Ishaq ibn Rahwayh]] and [[person/abu-thawr-al-kalbi|Abu Thawr]]. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981} Abu Ja'far al-Tabari read jurisprudence under him. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.303}

## In al-Fihrist of Ibn al-Nadim: Works
Ibn al-Nadim lists among his books al-Idah, al-Ifsah, al-Da'wa, al-Bayyinat, al-Usul and al-Hayd. He then wrote on every chapter of jurisprudence, from purification to the etiquette of the judge. His Book of Marriage reached a thousand folios, and his Book of Obligatory Fasting six hundred. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.303}
