## Overview

Ibn Dhakwan, Abu 'Amr ibn Ahmad ibn Bashr ibn Dhakwan al-Qurashi al-Fihri al-Dimashqi, is one of the two transmitters of the reading of [[person/abdallah-ibn-amir-al-yahsibi|'Abd Allah ibn 'Amir al-Yahsubi]], with [[person/hisham-ibn-ammar|Hisham ibn 'Ammar]]. He was born on 'Ashura' 173 AH and died in Shawwal 202 AH. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 155} He was the shaykh of the readings in al-Sham and imam of the Umayyad Mosque, to whom the headship of teaching passed after Ayyub ibn Tamim. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 155}

## His name

The machine-read text of al-Nashr prints his first name as 'Abd al-Rahman, both in the text and in the index of names, while other references give 'Abd Allah. This was not checked against the page image, so the record keeps to his kunya and lineage. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 154}

## Chain and contemporaries' testimony

Ibn Dhakwan read on Abu Sulayman Ayyub ibn Tamim, whose chain reaches Yahya ibn al-Harith al-Dhimari and through him Ibn 'Amir. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 154} Ibn al-Jazari quotes Abu Zur'a al-Dimashqi as saying that in Ibn Dhakwan's time there was nobody in Iraq, the Hijaz, al-Sham, Egypt or Khurasan whom he thought a better reciter. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 155} Two principal lines of his transmission pass through al-Akhfash and al-Suri. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 152}
