Ali-beg Firdus (1862 or 1864 – 30 June 1910) was a Bosnian Muslim politician from Livno, a landowner of the captain family that governed the town from 1750 to 1835.{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-en}{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-bs} Educated at the Livno ruždija and otherwise self-taught — deepening his Turkish, Persian and Arabic on journeys through the Ottoman Empire — he sat on Livno's municipal council from 1885 to 1894 and was the town's mayor from 1895 to 1900, resigning over his disagreement with government policy.{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-hr} From 1900 he sat on the provincial waqf commission, and he rose to lead the landowners' wing of the Movement of Muslims for Religious and Educational Autonomy, negotiating with the provincial government for the community's waqf and schools.{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-bs}{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-hr}

On his written invitation, Bosnian Muslim representatives met on 3 December 1906 and founded the Muslim People's Organisation (MNO), electing Firdus its first president.{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-en}{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-bs} At the 1910 elections the party took all the mandates reserved for Muslims, and Firdus became the first president of the Bosnian Diet when it convened on 15 June 1910; he died in office at Sarajevo a fortnight later, on 30 June 1910, and Safvet-beg Bašagić succeeded him in the chair.{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-hr}{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-cs}{cite:wikidata-ali-beg-firdus} A street in Sarajevo's Švrakino Selo neighbourhood bears his name.{cite:wikipedia-ali-beg-firdus-en}
