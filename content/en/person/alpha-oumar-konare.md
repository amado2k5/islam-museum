# Alpha Oumar Konaré

Alpha Oumar Konaré (born 2 February 1946) is a Malian politician, professor, historian and archaeologist who served as President of Mali for two five-year terms from 1992 to 2002 and chaired the African Union Commission from 2003 to 2008. {cite:wikipedia-alpha-oumar-konare-en} {cite:wikidata-alpha-oumar-konare}

## Scholarly career

Konaré was born in Kayes, the fourth son of a Fula homemaker, and went to primary school there before studying in Bamako, Dakar and Katibougou, then reading history at the École Normale Supérieure of Bamako, at the University of Warsaw and later in Geneva. {cite:wikipedia-alpha-oumar-konare-en} He taught at lycées in Kayes, Markala and Bamako, worked on historic patrimony and ethnography at the Ministry of Youth, Sports, Arts, and Culture, and became a professor in the History and Geography department at the École Normale Supérieure of Bamako. {cite:wikipedia-alpha-oumar-konare-en} Between 1989 and 1992 he was president of ICOM, the International Council of Museums, and he served as a consultant for UNESCO and the UNDP. {cite:wikipedia-alpha-oumar-konare-en}

## Presidency

Konaré led the ADEMA party and won the presidency in 1992, in the democratic opening that followed the fall of Moussa Traoré and a transitional government, and was re-elected for a second term ending in 2002. {cite:wikipedia-alpha-oumar-konare-en} Shortly before leaving office, on 29 May 2002, he pardoned the imprisoned former president Moussa Traoré and his wife, for the sake of national reconciliation. {cite:wikipedia-moussa-traore-en}

## After the presidency

From 2003 to 2008 Konaré chaired the African Union Commission, the executive arm of the African Union. {cite:wikipedia-alpha-oumar-konare-en} {cite:wikipedia-alpha-oumar-konare-ar}
