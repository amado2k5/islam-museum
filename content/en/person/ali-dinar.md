## Overview

Ali Dinar (Muhammad Ali Dinar) was the last sultan of Darfur of the Keira dynasty.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-fr} He took the throne in 1891, in the middle of the Mahdist period, spent years as a subordinate — at times a prisoner — of the Mahdist state at Omdurman, then fled home in 1898 and restored his ancestors' sultanate as an independent power.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en} He ruled for eighteen years, building a functioning administration and judiciary, until his declaration for the Ottoman Empire in the First World War brought a British invasion in which he was killed in action on 6 November 1916; the sultanate was then incorporated into Anglo-Egyptian Sudan.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}

## Early life and accession

He was born at the village of al-Shawaya, west of Nyala, in 1856 by the Arabic and French articles — the English article gives about 1865 and Wikidata only the 1860s; the variants are recorded in his record.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-fr}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en} His father was Zakariya ibn Muhammad Fadl, himself of the sultans' line.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar} During the Mahdist conquest of Darfur the "shadow sultan" Abu l-Khayrat held the Keira claim in hiding; when he was killed by rebellious slaves, Ali Dinar ascended the throne, in 1891 by the English article's dating.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en} Some sources allege that he incited Abu l-Khayrat's killing; the article records that he denied this, and the museum repeats the point only as that attributed allegation.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}

## Under the Mahdist state

Mahdist expeditions took al-Fashir, and Ali Dinar surrendered to them there on 13 October 1891.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en} He was imprisoned — for drinking wine, the English article says — and saved by the intercession of the amir Mahmud w. Ahmad, then taken to Omdurman and presented as a prisoner to the Khalifa Abdallahi, before whom he pleaded for his life.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en} The Arabic article tells the same years from the other side: he came to al-Fashir in 1891, visited Omdurman in 1892, gave his allegiance to the Khalifa, spread the Mahdist call in Darfur, and served in the Mahdist forces under Ibrahim al-Khalil against the Ali Jili movement.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar} The English article records that he was allowed back to Darfur in June 1894 and sent back to Omdurman in January 1895 under suspicion of intending rebellion, and that in 1896 he served Ibrahim al-Khalil in the Nuba Mountains, until the collapse of the Mahdist state let him escape.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}

## The restored sultanate

Just before, or during, the battle of Omdurman in September 1898, Ali Dinar fled back to Darfur with many notables of the old sultanate, reasserted its authority and, by the English article's account, rooted out Mahdist influence, built a functioning judiciary and regained the people's confidence through efficient administration.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en} He opened diplomatic ties with the Sanusiyya, Wadai, Dar Sila and Dar al-Kuti, and the French article adds that he took part in the Wadai war against the French, backing Muhammad Daoud Mourra ibn Yousouf.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-fr} The Arabic article preserves the works of piety Darfur tradition credits to his reign: a factory at al-Fashir producing the Kiswa of the Kaaba, which he sent to Mecca every year for some twenty years; the digging of the Abyar Ali wells at Dhu al-Hulayfa, the miqat of the pilgrims from Medina, and the renovation of its mosque; and his palace at al-Fashir, built between 1871 and 1912, which today houses Sudan's second museum.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar}

## War and death

In 1915 Ali Dinar declared his support for the Ottoman Empire in the First World War.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-fr} The British government answered with the invasion of Darfur, and he was killed in action on 6 November 1916 — at the battle the Arabic article calls al-Baranjiyya — after which the sultanate passed into Anglo-Egyptian Sudan.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar} His sword was taken in the campaign and is exhibited at the British Museum.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-fr}

## Positions and criticism

This section reports what the sources attribute to Ali Dinar and how they frame him; it is not the museum's own judgement.

**His confession.** No fetched source names a school of law or theology for Ali Dinar; his record's faith is unknown, as its faith note sets out.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar} What is attested is allegiance and piety of a political kind: allegiance to the Khalifa Abdallahi and zeal for the Mahdist call in the Arabic telling, a campaign to root out Mahdist influence in the English one — two framings the museum keeps side by side, each attributed to its source.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}

**The throne and the war.** The English article's biography (Theobald's, in its references) frames his reign as a restoration ended by empire: an efficient ruler undone by the 1915 Ottoman declaration, while the Arabic article frames his end as death "in defence of the homeland and its independence".{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-ar} The allegation that he incited his cousin's killing in 1891 remains, in the English article's own presentation, an allegation he denied.{cite:source/wikipedia-ali-dinar-en}
