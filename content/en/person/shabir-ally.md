## Summary
Shabir Ally is a Guyanese-Canadian Islamic preacher and debater, born in Guyana around 1953. He is president of the Islamic Information & Dawah Centre International in Toronto and prepares and presents the weekly television programme *Let the Quran Speak*. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en} {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-ar} The programme's YouTube channel (@QuranSpeaks) had 419,000 subscribers in the founder's channel harvest of October 2026; the English Wikipedia article's own channel figures, updated 4 August 2026, give 417,000 subscribers and 55.4 million views. {cite:youtube-let-the-quran-speak} {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en}

## Early life and education
Shabir Ally was born in Guyana, on the Caribbean coast of South America, around 1953, and moved to Canada with his family in 1978; his Indo-Guyanese family has ancestral roots in Gujarat, India. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en} {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-ar} He received his B.A. in religious studies from Laurentian University, with a specialisation in Biblical literature, and his M.A. and Ph.D. from the University of Toronto; his PhD thesis, completed in 2013, was on the exegesis of the Qur'an and treated al-Suyuti's *al-Durr al-manthur*. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en} He also holds a Bachelor of Science degree with a major in physics. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en}

## Career and debates
As of 2020, Ally was President of the Islamic Information & Dawah Centre International in Toronto, and he performs dawah addressed to both Muslim and non-Muslim audiences. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en} He has debated many figures from the Western Christian community, among them William Lane Craig, Michael Licona, John Dominic Crossan, James White, Jay Smith, David Wood and Nabeel Qureshi. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-ar}

## Views
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

Ally is known for a contextual interpretation of Qur'anic verses, and for justifying them by similar expressions within the Christian Bible. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en} The article attributes to him the views that covering the hair is not mandatory for women in Islam, that stoning adulterers is not a part of Islam, that the classification of hadith by many traditional scholars and the idea of scholarly consensus (ijma) as a source of law are open to question, that celebrating Christmas and Halloween is not prohibited for Muslims, and that the theory of evolution is not incompatible with Islam; each view is sourced in the article to his own recorded answers. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en} The article notes that, despite his conservative upbringing, many of his views differ significantly from the traditional conservative mainstream. {cite:wikipedia-shabir-ally-en}
