## Overview
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Raysuni al-Hasani al-Idrisi al-Arusi, Abu al-Abbas, was a rebel leader of standing and one of the opponents of French colonial rule in the far Maghrib, as al-Zirikli puts it. He came from the village of Zinat among the Bani Arus. Europeans call him al-Raisuli, and his men call him al-Sharif al-Raysuni. Al-A'lam gives his years, with a question mark on the first, as about 1270 AH, 1854 CE, to 1343 AH, 1925 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 220}.

## His rise and imprisonment
Al-Zirikli says his reports are many and that in summary he rose in the days of Mulay Hasan ibn Muhammad, and crowds of the Bani Arus and of his maternal kin, the Bani Mansur, gathered round him. The government of Marrakesh fought him and failed, then used a ruse; he fell into Sultan Hasan's hands and was jailed in irons at the port of Essaouira for three years. When the sultan died, his successor Abd al-Aziz ibn al-Hasan pardoned him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 220}.

## Revolt, then the governorship of Tangier
The state was in disorder, and Abd al-Aziz, still young, was being enticed by the French and others with gifts. Al-Raysuni came out of his seclusion and called for a general revolt against the Makhzen government and against the Europeans. He grew strong in the mountains of the Bani Arus and took the countryside around Tangier which, in al-Zirikli's words, was under French authority in 1904; the Friday sermon was said in his name from the pulpits of Tazrut and its surroundings. The sultan sought a reconciliation that ended with his appointment as Abd al-Aziz's deputy at Tangier. He restored security there and in its outskirts and held near independence, ruling in the sultan's name while the sultan had no power over him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 220}.

French sources, al-Zirikli reports, say the Spaniards supplied him with money and arms so that he would not threaten Tetouan. Foreign ambitions circled Tangier and Abd al-Aziz was asked to remove al-Raysuni, which he did; al-Raysuni withdrew to Zinat in revolt. The sultan fought him, his village was burned, and battles followed for two years {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 220}.

## Between Abd al-Aziz, Abd al-Hafiz and the Spaniards
Strife arose between the brothers Abd al-Aziz and Abd al-Hafiz, and al-Raysuni went to Abd al-Hafiz with congratulations and became one of his men. When the Spaniards widened their occupation of some western districts, entered Tetouan in 1331 AH and made for the area of Larache, al-Raysuni rose against them with crowds of tribesmen near Tetouan and was victorious, and entered Chefchaouen as its conqueror. They offered peace, concluded in September 1915, 1333 AH, on the terms that the mountains be al-Raysuni's and the coasts the Spaniards'. The peace did not last; the fighting resumed and extended to 1921 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 220}.

## His quarrel with Ibn Abd al-Karim al-Khattabi
When the revolt of Amir Muhammad ibn Abd al-Karim al-Khattabi arose in the Rif, the Spaniards offered al-Raysuni pledges and promises, and he made peace with them. Ibn Abd al-Karim called on him to join the jihad and he declined. Al-Zirikli relays a saying of his to the effect that, when Ibn Abd al-Karim was a boy, the boy's father asked him for help to send his son to Madrid to study, and he did so, and that now Ibn Abd al-Karim was his enemy and was inciting the tribes against him. Ibn Abd al-Karim's anger grew because al-Raysuni not only sat out the struggle but began calling the tribes to side with the Spaniards {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 220}.

## Capture and death
Ibn Abd al-Karim sent a force that attacked him at Tazrut, and after a battle of two days al-Raysuni was captured, ill and nearly seventy. He was taken with his family to the town of Tamasint in the Rif and died there {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 220}.
