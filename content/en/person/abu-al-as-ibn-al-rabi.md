## Overview
[[person/abu-al-as-ibn-al-rabi|Abu al-As ibn al-Rabi']] ibn Abd al-Uzza ibn Abd Shams al-Abshami; his mother was Hala bint Khuwaylid, and his title was "the puppy of al-Batha'". Al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar says that he was called "the trustworthy" before Islam. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1714} His name is disputed: it is said Luqayt (as Mus'ab al-Zubayri, al-Fallas, al-Ala'i and al-Hakim said, and al-Baladhuri preferred), and it is said Hashim, and it is said Muhashsham; Ibn Mandah relates that it was said Yasir, and Ibn Hajar thinks this a corruption of "Yasim". {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1714}

## Before Islam
Before the mission, according to Zubayr on the authority of his uncle Mus'ab, he was a brother to the Prophet in the claim of some scholars, and he often visited his house, and he was married to the Prophet's eldest daughter [[person/zaynab-bint-muhammad|Zaynab]]; he did not accept Islam until after the Hijra. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1714} Ibn Ishaq said that he was among the men of Mecca counted for wealth, trustworthiness and trade. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1715}

## Badr and his captivity
Ibn Hajar relates from Ibn Ishaq that he fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] with the polytheists and was captured with those who were captured. When the people of Mecca sent to ransom their captives, Zaynab sent a necklace that Khadija had given her when she brought her to Abu al-As. The Prophet was very tender toward her, and said to the Muslims: "If you see fit to release her captive and return to her her necklace," and they did, on condition that he send her to Medina, and he did. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1715} Ibn Ishaq says that the man who captured him at Badr was Abd Allah ibn Jubayr ibn al-Nu'man; al-Waqidi relates that it was Khirash ibn al-Simma and that his brother Amr ibn al-Rabi' came to ransom him. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1715}

## The second captivity and his acceptance of Islam
In Ibn Ishaq's report he came later with a caravan of Quraysh, and the Muslims captured him and took what he had; Zaynab gave him protection, so he returned to Mecca, paid back the deposits to their owners, then emigrated to Medina as a Muslim, and the Prophet returned his daughter to him. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1715} Musa ibn Uqba relates that the man who captured him the second time was Abu Basir al-Thaqafi and those Muslims with him, when they stayed on the coast cutting the road on Quraysh's merchants during the truce between al-Hudaybiyya and the conquest. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1715}
