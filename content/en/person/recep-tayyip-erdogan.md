## Summary
Recep Tayyip Erdoğan is a Turkish politician who has been president of Turkey since 28 August 2014, having previously served as prime minister from 2003 to 2014 and as mayor of Istanbul from 1994 to 1998. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-ar}

He has been Turkey's de facto leader since 2003 and its longest-serving modern leader. He began in Islamist party politics, then co-founded the Justice and Development Party (AKP) in 2001, presenting it as a conservative democratic party, and led it to victory in the 2002, 2007 and 2011 elections before becoming the country's first directly elected president in 2014. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} Under him Turkey moved from a parliamentary to an executive presidential system and became a widely intervening regional actor, while opponents and international organisations described his country's course as democratic backsliding and suppression of dissent. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Early life and education
Erdoğan was born on 26 February 1954 in Kasımpaşa, Beyoğlu, Istanbul, to a modest conservative Muslim family originally from Adjara in Georgia; his father Ahmet Erdoğan was a coastguard captain, the family spent summers in Güneysu, Rize, where its roots lay, and returned to Istanbul for good when he was thirteen. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

As a teenager he sold postcards, water and simit on the street, and played semi-professional football for a local club; the stadium of Kasımpaşa S.K. is now named after him. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} He graduated from Kasımpaşa Piyale Primary School in 1965 and from Istanbul İmam Hatip High School in 1973, a religious vocational school where a quarter of the curriculum covers the Quran, the life of the Prophet Muhammad and Arabic, and where his classmates began calling him hoca. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} His official biography says he then studied business administration at the Aksaray School of Economics and Commercial Sciences, now part of Marmara University, but the authenticity and status of his degree have been disputed in controversy over his eligibility for the presidency. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

Erdoğan is a member of the Community of İskenderpaşa, a Turkish Sufi community of the Naqshbandi order. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Early political career
Erdoğan entered politics in 1976 through the National Turkish Student Union, then chaired the Beyoğlu and Istanbul youth branches of the Islamist National Salvation Party until the 1980 military coup dissolved all parties, after which he worked as a consultant and senior executive in the private sector. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} In 1983 he followed Necmettin Erbakan's followers into the new Welfare Party, becoming its Beyoğlu district chair in 1984 and Istanbul chair in 1985. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

In the 1994 local elections he won the Istanbul mayoralty with 25.19% of the vote in an upset, and governed pragmatically: hundreds of kilometres of new water pipes, more than fifty bridges and viaducts, modern recycling facilities, cleaner buses, a major share of the municipality's two-billion-dollar debt repaid and four billion dollars invested in the city, earning him a UN-Habitat award by unanimous vote of an international jury. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

In December 1997, in Siirt, he recited a modified version of Ziya Gökalp's "Soldier's Prayer", whose added verses compare mosques to barracks, minarets to bayonets and the faithful to soldiers; he was convicted of inciting religious hatred under article 312/2 of the penal code, sentenced to ten months reduced to four, forced to give up the mayoralty, imprisoned from 24 March to 27 July 1999 and banned from politics. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-ar} On the day he went to prison he released a poetry album, This Song Doesn't End Here, which became Turkey's best-selling album of 1999 with over a million copies sold. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

When the Virtue Party was banned in 2001 the movement split: Erbakan's followers founded the Felicity Party, while the reformers founded the AKP under Abdullah Gül and Erdoğan, presenting it as a broad conservative democratic party respecting Islamic norms without an explicit religious programme; it won 34% of the vote in the 2002 elections. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Premiership (2003–2014)
Because Erdoğan's political ban was still in force, Gül became prime minister first; after legal changes backed also by the opposition CHP, Erdoğan won the Siirt by-election and became prime minister in March 2003. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-ar}

He inherited an economy recovering from the 2001 crisis and backed Finance Minister Ali Babacan's investor-friendly policies: real GDP grew 64% between 2002 and 2012, central bank reserves rose from $26.5 billion to $92.2 billion in 2011, and Turkey's IMF debt fell from $23.5 billion to $0.9 billion by 2012, though the current-account deficit widened in the same period from $600 million to some $58 billion. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

EU accession negotiations formally opened on 3 October 2005, and European Voice had named him "European of the Year 2004" for his reforms, before the talks stalled in 2009–2010 over Cyprus. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} Education spending rose to the largest share of the budget, compulsory schooling went from eight to twelve years, the number of universities nearly doubled from 98 in 2002 to 186 in 2012, airports rose from 26 to 50 in a decade, and high-speed railways ran for the first time in Turkish history from 2009. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

On minorities, the death penalty was abolished in all circumstances in 2004, human-rights and ombudsman institutions were created in 2012, laws returned confiscated properties worth some $2 billion to non-Muslim foundations, and the Jewish community celebrated Hanukkah publicly in 2015 for the first time in modern Turkish history. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

On the Kurdish issue, his 2009 initiative sought to end the PKK conflict that had cost more than 40,000 lives, allowing Kurdish in broadcasting and campaigns; on 23 November 2011 he apologised on behalf of the state for the Dersim massacre; and in late 2012 his government began peace negotiations with the PKK that ended three years later. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

On 17 December 2013 a corruption scandal estimated at US$100 billion led to the arrests of his close allies and incriminated Erdoğan himself; he responded that it was a Gülenist plot inside the judiciary, and the cases were dissolved in September 2014. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Presidency (2014–present)
Erdoğan won the election of 10 August 2014 with 51.79% and became the twelfth president and the first elected by direct popular vote, announcing that he would not keep the tradition of presidential neutrality and would play an active role in government, with Ahmet Davutoğlu as his prime minister. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} The April 2017 referendum approved amendments abolishing the prime ministry and making the presidency an executive office with broad powers; he was re-elected in 2018 with 52.59% and again in the 2023 runoff with 52.18% against Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu, and on 8 March 2024 he declared he would retire when his term ends in 2028. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

On 15 July 2016 a military faction attempted a coup; his government reasserted control the next day, and Erdoğan and senior officials blamed the exiled cleric Fethullah Gülen, repeatedly demanding his extradition from the United States. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} A state of emergency declared on 20 July 2016 was extended until 2018, and by March 2018 more than 50,000 people had been arrested and over 160,000 dismissed in wide purges that included independent media. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

His new presidential complex in Ankara, the Ak Saray, drew heavy criticism: almost 1,000 rooms at a cost of $350 million, built on protected Atatürk Forest Farm land despite court orders halting construction, leading opponents to brand it the "Kaç-Ak Saray", the illegal palace. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

In economic policy he pressed the central bank to cut interest rates despite high inflation, promoting the theory that high interest rates cause inflation — an idea the article notes is universally rejected by economists — and from 2018 Turkey entered a currency-and-debt crisis with a sharply depreciating lira and very high inflation. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

The February 2023 earthquake in south-central Turkey killed more than 50,000 people; the disaster agency AFAD, centralised under his control since 2017, faced intense scrutiny for a slow and disorganised response, and the article notes that his government had issued "amnesties" legalising substandard buildings for fines, a policy he had boasted about in 2019 speeches. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Projects and initiatives
Erdoğan co-founded the United Nations Alliance of Civilizations initiative, which seeks to galvanise international action against extremism through intercultural and inter-religious dialogue. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

During the 2011 drought his government contributed over $201 million to humanitarian relief in Somalia, re-opened Turkey's embassy in Mogadishu among the first governments to resume formal relations after the civil war, and launched development and infrastructure projects there. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

In July 2020, after the Council of State annulled the 1934 cabinet decision that had made Hagia Sophia a museum, Erdoğan ordered its reclassification as a mosque, a decision condemned by the Turkish opposition, UNESCO, the World Council of Churches, the Holy See and many international leaders, and defended by its proponents on the ground that Sultan Mehmed II's waqf had designated the site a mosque. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Foreign policy
Foreign policy during his premiership was associated with Ahmet Davutoğlu, his advisor and then foreign minister, and rested on the principle of "don't make enemies, make friends" and a "zero problems" policy towards neighbours. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

On Syria, relations collapsed after 2011, Turkey backed the opposition, Turkish forces operated against the Islamic State and the Syrian Democratic Forces, and Turkey became the main backer of the Syrian transitional government led by Ahmed al-Sharaa. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

During Russia's invasion of Ukraine he acted as mediator: he closed the Bosphorus to Russian naval reinforcements, brokered with the United Nations the July 2022 grain-export deal, mediated the September 2022 prisoner exchange that freed 215 Ukrainian soldiers, and in February 2025 offered Turkey as the "ideal host" for talks between Russia, Ukraine and the United States. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

In May 2022 he temporarily blocked Finland and Sweden from joining NATO, accusing both of tolerating groups Turkey classifies as terrorist organisations, before ratifying Finland's accession in April 2023 and Sweden's in January 2024. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

In June 2017 he called the blockade of Qatar "inhumane and against Islamic values". {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} In September 2017 he condemned the persecution of Muslims in Myanmar as a "genocide" against the Muslim minority. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} In October 2023 he said Hamas was not a terrorist organisation but a liberation group, and condemned Israel as a "terrorist state" committing genocide against the Palestinians, while critics accused him of hypocrisy because Turkey itself was heavily bombing Kurdish areas at the same time. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported in the sources, not as the museum's verdict on them. The English and Arabic articles report these positions; the original statements of all parties have not yet been reviewed in full.

**His supporters:** Early in his premiership Erdoğan was praised as a role model for emerging Middle Eastern nations for reform packages that expanded religious freedoms and minority rights as part of EU accession negotiations, and he has been described as having "long championed Islamist causes". {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} His supporters argue that the post-coup purges were necessary because Gülen-linked schools cheated on entrance exams and Gülen followers then entered the judiciary. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

**His critics:** The English article states in its lead that Turkey has experienced democratic backsliding and suppression of dissent under Erdoğan, and that his presidency has been marked by a shift towards authoritarianism. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} The 2013 Gezi Park protests grew from a small sit-in defending an Istanbul park into the largest protests in a decade after the police response with tear gas. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} Reporters Without Borders documented press freedom falling from a rank around 100 in his first term to 153 of 179 countries in 2021, and called him an "enemy of press freedom" after more than 200 journalists were arrested and over 120 media outlets closed following the coup attempt. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} His government temporarily blocked Twitter in 2014 and blocked Wikipedia entirely from April 2017 until the ban was lifted in January 2020 after Turkey's top court ruled it unconstitutional. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} Between 2014 and 2019, 128,872 investigations were launched for insulting the president and 27,717 criminal cases opened. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

In 2025 he oversaw the arrest of Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu and his subsequent disqualification from the 2028 presidential election, for which İmamoğlu was considered the opposition front-runner, leading to ongoing widespread protests. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en} On the Armenian question he continued Turkey's policy of denial, while issuing on 23 April 2014 a statement in nine languages offering condolences for the mass killings as a "shared pain" of Turks and Armenians without recognising a genocide. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Personal life
Erdoğan married Emine Erdoğan (née Gülbaran) on 4 July 1978; they have two sons and two daughters. This record notes no family detail beyond what understanding his public career requires. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}

## Impact and audience
Erdoğan is the dominant figure of Turkish politics for over two decades: he built and led the AKP, changed the system of government, and widened Turkey's regional presence from the Balkans to the Horn of Africa, while assessments of his legacy remain sharply divided between those who see him as the builder of a strong modern Turkey and those who see his era as a retreat from secular democracy. {cite:wikipedia-recep-tayyip-erdogan-en}
