## Summary
Aaidh ibn Abdullah al-Qarni is a Saudi preacher, writer and poet born in 1959 in the Asir region, internationally known for La Tahzan ⁦(Don't Be Sad)⁩, among the best-selling Arabic books of its era and translated into many languages; he has published more than eighty books across religious studies, self-help and poetry. {cite:wikipedia-aaidh-al-qarni} {cite:muslim500-aaidh-al-qarni}

## Life and upbringing
Born in the village of al-Qarn in Asir in the south-west of the kingdom, he memorised the Qur'an young and joined Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University in Riyadh, taking a bachelor's at the Faculty of Usul al-Din, a master's in hadith in 1408 AH and a doctorate in 1423 AH on al-Qurtubi's al-Mufhim, then taught hadith at the university for seven years. {cite:wikipedia-aaidh-al-qarni}

## La Tahzan and the works
He published La Tahzan at the start of the millennium as a book of spiritual consolation gathering verses, hadith, poetry and stories against sorrow and anxiety; his sources describe millions of copies sold in more than thirty languages, followed by works in the same register — You Can Be the Happiest Woman in the World, That You Become the Happiest of People, and La Takhaf in 2026 — alongside poetry collections and a simplified tafsir. {cite:muslim500-aaidh-al-qarni} {cite:wikipedia-aaidh-al-qarni}

## Method of writing
Al-Qarni's style joins religious instruction to literature: he draws heavily on pre-Islamic and Islamic poetry and on his own verse, addressing the non-specialist reader in accessible language; La Tahzan thus became a reference of what is described as modern "Islamic psychology" literature, followed by works on everyday faith. {cite:muslim500-aaidh-al-qarni} {cite:wikipedia-aaidh-al-qarni}

## Preaching and turn
He rose in the 1980s–1990s among the Sahwa generation of preachers, recording thousands of lessons and sermons and hosting television programmes on tafsir, hadith and the Prophet's biography. In 2019 he announced in a televised interview his apology for his "hardline" Sahwa-era discourse and his alignment with the state's new direction — a move the Muslim 500 profile documents and other sources describe as drawing mixed reactions in religious circles. {cite:muslim500-aaidh-al-qarni}

## Digital reach
He commands an exceptional digital audience, estimated by his sources at more than thirty-three million followers across his accounts, and continues to publish: in 2026 appeared La Takhaf in the same register, aimed at turning contemporary anxiety into spiritual calm. {cite:muslim500-aaidh-al-qarni}

## Legacy and debate
La Tahzan is read as a phenomenon of modern Islamic publishing: a consolation text crossing languages that has accompanied readers through illness and loss. The author also faced plagiarism accusations, one decided against him in a Saudi ruling in 2012, his defence pleading common scholarly practice; the museum reports the episode and the response attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-aaidh-al-qarni}
## Hadith formation
His higher studies centre on hadith: a master's in its sciences in 1408 AH and a doctorate in 1423 AH on al-Qurtubi's al-Mufhim, an important commentary on Sahih Muslim, and he taught hadith at Imam Muhammad ibn Saud University for seven years before devoting himself to preaching and writing. {cite:wikipedia-aaidh-al-qarni}

## The poet-preacher
Al-Qarni has published poetry collections and well-known verse of praise and Islamic sentiment, and many count him among the most successful of his Saudi generation in joining poetry to preaching, his style described as carrying the spirit of the diwan into the book of general reading. {cite:wikipedia-aaidh-al-qarni}
