# Fuad Masum

Muhammad Fuad Masum (born 1 July 1938) is an Iraqi Kurdish politician who served as president of Iraq from 24 July 2014 to 2 October 2018, the second non-Arab to hold the office. He was born at Koya (Koy Sanjaq), the son of Mullah Masum Khider, a former head of the Association of Muslim Scholars in Kurdistan. {cite:wikipedia-fuad-masum-en} {cite:wikipedia-fuad-masum-ar}

## Education and career
Masum studied at religious schools in Kurdistan until the age of eighteen, then read law and Sharia at Baghdad University and completed his higher education at Al-Azhar in Cairo, taking a doctorate in Islamic philosophy there in 1975. He taught at Basrah University, moved through the Iraqi Communist Party and the Kurdistan Democratic Party before joining the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, and served as the first prime minister of the PUK-administered part of the Kurdistan Region in 1992–1994. After 2003 he sat in the Iraqi parliament and acted as its speaker in 2010. {cite:wikipedia-fuad-masum-en} {cite:wikipedia-fuad-masum-ar}

## Presidency
Elected president after the 2014 parliamentary election, Masum succeeded his old comrade Jalal Talabani — both men's articles attest the succession at both ends — and in August 2014 charged Haider al-Abadi with forming a government. Barham Salih succeeded him in October 2018. {cite:wikipedia-fuad-masum-en} {cite:wikipedia-jalal-talabani-en}
