## Summary

Tariq al-Bishri (1933–2021) was an Egyptian judge, historian and legal thinker who joined high judicial standing at the Council of State with books that became references for understanding the Egyptian national movement and Muslim-Coptic relations.{cite:wikipedia-tariq-al-bishri}

## Life

He graduated in law and served in the administrative judiciary, becoming first deputy head of the Council of State, sat on constitutional and legal committees, and in his last years chaired the constitutional amendments committee after the January 2011 revolution.{cite:wikipedia-tariq-al-bishri}

## Works

His book Al-Muslimun wal-Aqbat fi Itar al-Jama'a al-Wataniyya (Muslims and Copts within the National Community, 1980) is a reference study of Muslim-Coptic relations in the modern era. He also wrote Al-Haraka al-Siyasiyya fi Misr 1945–1952 and Al-Dimuqratiyya wal-Nasiriyya, and studies on sharia, law and national history.{cite:wikipedia-tariq-al-bishri}

## Method and standing

His thought moved from the left toward an Islamic frame from inside national history, making him a model of the thinker joining law, history and ideas. Islamists and liberals alike respected him for his precision and independence.{cite:wikipedia-tariq-al-bishri}
