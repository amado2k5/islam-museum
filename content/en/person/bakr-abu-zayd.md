## Summary

Bakr Abu Zayd (1946–2008) was a Saudi Hanbali jurist, member of the Council of Senior Scholars and the International Islamic Fiqh Academy, best known for Hilyat Talib al-'Ilm, which became a guide for students of knowledge across the Muslim world.{cite:wikipedia-bakr-abu-zayd}

## Life

Born in Riyadh, he studied sharia at Imam Muhammad ibn Saud University, served as a judge at Riyadh's Grand Court, then as a member of the Council of Senior Scholars and the OIC's International Islamic Fiqh Academy.{cite:wikipedia-bakr-abu-zayd}

## Works

His books include Hilyat Talib al-'Ilm (1989) on the student's manners and the aims of knowledge, Fiqh al-Nawazil on contemporary cases, Al-Ta'alum criticising pretension to knowledge, and Al-Ma'ida wal-Tasnif on the science of compilation.{cite:wikipedia-bakr-abu-zayd}

## Method and standing

He joined Hanbali legal theory to concern for teaching methods and scholarly etiquette, and his lessons on creed and fiqh books trained younger students. He died in 2008; his Hilya remains among the most-read books of scholarly manners.{cite:wikipedia-bakr-abu-zayd}
