## Summary
Naif al-Mutawa created THE 99, a comic-book series of 99 young heroes whose powers are drawn from the traditional names of God in Islam — the first Islamic-themed superhero universe to reach a global market. {cite:muslim500-naif-al-mutawa} {cite:wikipedia-naif-al-mutawa}

## Formation
Born in Kuwait in 1971, he studied clinical psychology in the United States — a doctorate from Long Island University — along with business training at Wharton and Columbia, and worked on trauma programmes before turning to media. {cite:wikipedia-naif-al-mutawa}

## Career
Launched through his company Teshkeel Media in 2006, THE 99 ran in comics and an animated television series and crossed over with DC Comics' Justice League; al-Mutawa said the aim was to give Muslim children tolerant heroes rooted in their own culture. The series won praise internationally — including public commendation by US President Barack Obama — and drew criticism from some religious conservatives and regulators. He also teaches clinical psychology at Kuwait University and founded Tuhoon, a Kuwaiti mental-health platform. {cite:muslim500-naif-al-mutawa} {cite:wikipedia-naif-al-mutawa}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him under Media as the entrepreneur who brought Islamic values into global pop culture. THE 99's portrayal of figures inspired by the divine attributes was challenged by conservative critics in the Gulf, while defenders saw it as carefully non-figurative; the museum records the dispute attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-naif-al-mutawa}
