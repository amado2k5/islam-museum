## Overview
Mika'il is the angel charged with provision, rain and vegetation in the view of the majority of exegetes; his name is mentioned in the Qur'an once with Jibril in the verse of enmity [[passage/quran-2-98|Al-Baqara 98]], and Jibril at [[person/jibril|his page]] is his partner in bearing revelation and the Most High Assembly.

## The name and its meaning
"Mika'il" with tanwin on the mīm, a fatha on the kāf and a sukūn on the alif; it is also said "Mīkāl" in another reading. The scholars differed on its derivation: some said it is non-Arabic or Aramaic in origin, and some said from "maka" meaning strength; no settled Arabic derivation is found in the tradition as in "Jibril" {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## In the Qur'an
- He is mentioned by name in only one verse: {whoever is an enemy to God and His angels and His messengers and Jibril and Mīkāl} [[passage/quran-2-98|Al-Baqara 98]]; and it is read "Mīkā'īl", "Mīkāl" and "Mīkā'īl" in the readings.
- No act of his is known in the Qur'an by name; rather his charge is known from books of tafsir and hadith: in the exegetes' view he is the angel of provision and rain {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## In hadith and reports
- No independent hadith mentioning him by name is found in the two Sahihs and the Sunan in the well-known survey; rather he is known by his report in books of tafsir and Shama'il {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997}.
- In books of tafsir it is mentioned that God entrusted to him the clouds, the rain and vegetation, so he withholds what He wills and sends what He wills; he is "the angel who sends the clouds" in Ibn Kathir's view and others {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
- It is related in an athar that he said to the heaven and the earth: take whatever you wish of provision with God — its chain is not sound with the hadith scholars; the principle in assigning his charges is what comes in books of tafsir attributed to the Salaf {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1} {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2}.

## With the exegetes
Ibn Kathir details at [[passage/quran-2-98|Al-Baqara 98]] that God gathered in the verse enmity toward Him with enmity toward His angels and messengers, and named Jibril and Mika'il explicitly because they are among the greatest of the angels in His sight, and they are the two sent with revelation and rain {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}. He mentions in the stories of the prophets that they are partners in the "Most High Assembly" that listened to Iblis {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## In books of Shama'il and sira
The Shama'il mention in describing the great angels that Mika'il is of the people of heaven who praise God, and some of them mention that he is the one charged with rain in the transmitted books of tafsir {cite:shamail-kushak-2012} {cite:shamail-bajuri-awwama-2001}.

## Notes for researchers
- His mention in the Qur'an is little: a name in one verse only; and his charge of provision and rain is from books of tafsir, not from a direct Qur'anic text.
- His relation to Jibril: his partner in revelation and the Most High Assembly in the exegetes' view, and Ibn Kathir counts them among the greatest of the angels {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
- What is related about his meeting the Prophet in human form in some books of tafsir is related without a connected chain, and it is not established in the two Sahihs {cite:sharh-mushkil-al-athar-arnaout}.
