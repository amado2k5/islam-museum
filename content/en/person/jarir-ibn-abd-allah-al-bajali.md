## Overview
[[person/jarir-ibn-abd-allah-al-bajali|Jarir ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Shalil al-Bajali]], Abu Amr, of the Bajila. He embraced Islam in the year the Prophet died, lived in Kufa until the caliphate of Ali, and then moved to Qarqisiya. {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p.625}

## His beauty
He was at the peak of beauty and height, and Umar ibn al-Khattab called him "the Joseph of this community" for his beauty. {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p.625}

## Al-Qadisiyya
He challenged Mihran on the day of al-Qadisiyya and killed him. {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p.625}

## Dhu al-Khalasa
A tyrant of Dhu al-Khalasa asked him to build a house for the Khath'am called "the Yemeni Ka'ba". He went out to it and burned it, and the Prophet prayed for him to be steadfast and guided. {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p.625}

## The pledge
He pledged allegiance to the Prophet that he would give sincere counsel to every Muslim and would separate himself from the polytheist. {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p.625}

## Death
He died in the year fifty-six, and it is said fifty-four. {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym p.625}

## For young visitors
Jarir ibn Abdallah al-Bajali was handsome and tall. He embraced Islam shortly before the Prophet died, burned a house that was worshipped in Yemen, and fought at al-Qadisiyya.

## Notes for researchers
- The text implies that Umar called him "Joseph of this community"; the statement is reported in summary, and its wording should be checked in the edition relied on.
- The account of Dhu al-Khalasa and the burning of the house is reported from Abu Nu'aym through his chains, and we have not yet checked it against a second source.

## For Scholars
Jarir ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Shalil al-Bajali, Abu Amr, of Bajila, entered Islam in the year the Prophet died, lived at Kufa until Ali's caliphate, then moved to Qarqisiya. He was the utmost in beauty and height, and Umar ibn al-Khattab called him 'the Yusuf of this nation' for his beauty. He fought a duel with Mihran at al-Qadisiyya and killed him. And he was tasked with the idol-house of Dhu al-Khalasa — a house for Khat'am called 'the Yemeni Kaaba' — rode to it and burned it, and the Prophet prayed for his steadfastness and guidance.

## In Ahmad ibn Hanbal's Fada'il al-Sahaba
In the chapter on Jarir in [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]], [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] narrates from Qays ibn Abi Hazim, from Jarir, that the Prophet said to him, "Will you not relieve me of Dhu al-Khalasa?" — a shrine of Khath'am called the Yemeni Ka'ba. He set out with a hundred and fifty horsemen of Ahmas; he told the Prophet he could not sit firm on a horse, and the Prophet struck his chest until he saw the marks of his fingers and said, "O God, make him firm and make him a guide and rightly guided"; he went, broke and burned it, and sent word to the Prophet with the good news — a sound chain according to the editor {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1127-1128}. From Tariq ibn Shihab he narrates that when the delegation of Bajila came to the Prophet he said, "Register the Bajalis and begin with the Ahmasis", and prayed for them five times — sound according to the editor {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1128-1129}.
