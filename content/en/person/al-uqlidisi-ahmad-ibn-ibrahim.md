## Overview
Abu al-Hasan Ahmad ibn Ibrahim, called al-Uqlidisi, a mathematician. He probably lived in Damascus, and his nickname is linked with the book attributed to Euclid. He died around 370 AH (980 CE). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.255}

## Book and witness
The sources connect Kitab al-fusul with Damascus341/952–953, distinguishing composition from the surviving1157 copy {cite:tdv-oklidisi pp. 25–26} {cite:uqlidisi-mactutor Biography}. Copying Euclid is a possible explanation of the nisba, not an established occupation; the death estimate above is not a certain date, and MacTutor stresses limited personal evidence {cite:uqlidisi-saidan-dsb opening biography} {cite:uqlidisi-mactutor Biography}. The earlier encyclopaedia page was not independently read for this enrichment; its original paragraph remains attributed to its source and author.

## Identity and contribution
MacTutor explicitly quotes Saidan identifying the named scholar as Muslim, the basis here rather than his name or country {cite:uqlidisi-mactutor Biography}. Sunni is separately the labelled museum default for the Islamic Damascus milieu, not personal school testimony; the biographies read name no contrary personal affiliation {cite:tdv-oklidisi pp. 25–26} {cite:uqlidisi-mactutor Biography}.

The [[invention/decimal-fractions-al-uqlidisi|decimal-fraction page]] explains the units sign, repeated halving and Saidan's/Rashed's disagreement over completeness {cite:uqlidisi-saidan-dsb decimal-fraction discussion} {cite:uqlidisi-mactutor Biography}. It claims neither universal numeral/decimal-point priority nor direct readership by al-Samawal, al-Kashi or Stevin {cite:uqlidisi-mactutor Biography}. Paper adaptations and learners' questions also occur in the book; these support an inference of teaching experience, not appointment at a named school {cite:uqlidisi-saidan-dsb four parts}.
