Samad Mir (1893 – 9 January 1959) was a Kashmiri Sufi poet, born at Alam Sahib Narwara in Srinagar to Khaliq Mir and Noor Khaliq Mir, dervish followers from Nambalhar in Budgam; his father, also a Sufi poet, died within a year of his birth.{cite:wikipedia-samad-mir-en}{cite:wikipedia-samad-mir-ar} Avtar Mota's portrait, drawn on Moti Lal Saqi's Sahitya Akademi monograph, gives the birth instead as 1894 at Sekidafar.{cite:chinar-shade-samad-mir} Mir worked as a labourer at the Hari Niwas Palace in Srinagar, married and raised three sons and a daughter, and in adulthood returned to his ancestral village.{cite:wikipedia-samad-mir-en}

Illiterate, he composed orally and his verses were written down by Ali Shah of Wagar; more than two hundred poems survive, collected by Moti Lal Saqi in the Kulyaat-e-Samad Mir, which the Jammu and Kashmir Academy of Art, Culture and Languages has published in four revised editions.{cite:wikipedia-samad-mir-en}{cite:wikipedia-samad-mir-ks} His narrative poem Aknandun ("The Only Son") is his best-known work, and his verse is distinguished among Kashmiri Sufi poets by its weaving of Sanskrit and Hindi words into diction shaped by Islamic mysticism.{cite:wikipedia-samad-mir-en}{cite:chinar-shade-samad-mir} He died at Nambalhar on 9 January 1959 and was buried at Agar, where a shrine now stands over his grave.{cite:wikipedia-samad-mir-en}{cite:wikidata-samad-mir}
