## Overview
Rexford Newcomb was an American architectural historian, described by Wikidata as living from 1886 to 1968. {cite:wikidata} In 1929 he was professor of architectural history at the University of Illinois, and in that year he wrote the monograph [[work/decorative-tiles-of-north-africa|The Decorative Tiles of North Africa]], No. 7 in a series published by the Associated Tile Manufacturers of New York. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929}

This record is a short stub. The museum has read only that one monograph by him, and the Encyclopaedia of Islam names an S. Newcomb, an astronomer, but not him.

## The monograph
The monograph surveys the architecture and architectural ceramics of Tunisia, Algeria and Morocco. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p. 16} It is a short trade monograph that leans on earlier French and Italian writers, and the museum's source note warns that some of its dates are garbled, so they are recorded as his and not converted. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p. 16} Its description of the minaret of [[building/jami-qayrawan|the Great Mosque of Kairouan]] and its account of the lustre tiles of the mihrab are summarised on that building's page. {cite:newcomb-decorative-tiles-north-africa-1929 p. 16}
