## Summary
Bisan Owda is a Palestinian journalist and filmmaker from Gaza who became, in her twenties, one of the most recognised Palestinian reporting voices in the world through short videos from inside the war, crowned in 2024 by the highest American journalism honours. {cite:wikipedia-bisan-owda}

## Early life and work
Born in 1997 or 1998 and raised in Beit Hanoun in the northern Gaza Strip, she worked in civil-society fields before the war: a member of UN Women's Youth Gender Innovation Agora Forum, she cooperated with the European Union on climate change and was named an EU Goodwill Ambassador, and she worked with the UN Population Fund. She presented "Hakawatia" on Roya TV exploring Palestinian history and culture, and appeared in educational Palestinian-dialect videos for the Easy Languages channel. {cite:wikipedia-bisan-owda}

## Reporting the war
When the Gaza war began in October 2023, Owda began publishing near-daily video updates documenting the experience of Palestinian civilians: the flight from Beit Hanoun, sheltering around al-Shifa Hospital, life in tents without clean water or sufficient food. She became known for opening her reports with "It's Bisan from Gaza and I'm still alive"; most of her videos are in English for a global audience, and her AJ+ series passed forty million views across platforms and was carried by major international networks. {cite:wikipedia-bisan-owda}

## Awards and recognition
In 2024 her series "It's Bisan from Gaza and I'm Still Alive" won the News & Documentary Emmy for Outstanding Hard News Feature Story: Short Form, a Peabody Award in the News category and an Edward R. Murrow Award for News Series; the Muslim 500 lists her among influential Muslim figures. {cite:wikipedia-bisan-owda} {cite:muslim500-bisan-owda}

## Controversy
A pro-Israel American organisation objected to her Emmy nomination, alleging a link to a left-wing Palestinian political organisation; Owda denied the claim, and the president of the National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences said its two juries had found no evidence of any affiliation, and the nomination proceeded to the win. Her TikTok account was also temporarily suspended in January 2026 before being restored after public outcry. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-bisan-owda}

## Style and place in contemporary journalism
Owda's work is read as a model of what researchers call first-person witness journalism: the report does not merely relay news but documents the journalist's own life as part of the event, so that a phone camera stands in for a newsroom and the reporter's declared location — "I'm still alive" — becomes part of the text rather than a footnote. The approach drew the attention of journalism scholars and the broad public alike, and analytical pieces have discussed it as a shift of credibility from the media institution to the field witness. {cite:wikipedia-bisan-owda}
## The awards in context
Her Emmy win in the news-and-documentary category was an event professional media discussed at length, combining institutional recognition with a wave of objection from parties that saw the nomination as politicised, to which the academy's leadership replied that its juries had examined the claims and found nothing to substantiate them. The museum's record confines itself to the sequence as the sources document it: the awards, the objection campaign, the denial and the academy's decision. {cite:wikipedia-bisan-owda}
