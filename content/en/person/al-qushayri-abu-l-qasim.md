## Overview
Abu al-Qasim Abd al-Karim ibn Hawazin al-Qushayri of Nishapur, the ascetic Sufi shaykh of Khurasan, teacher of the community and head of the Path, author of the Risala and of Lata'if al-isharat; he joined the revealed Law to interior truth and was an exemplar imam — exegete, traditionist, jurist, theologian, grammarian, scribe and poet {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 171-173} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 pp. 564-565}.

## Upbringing and the Path
He was born in Rabi' I 376 in the Ustuwa district of Nishapur, his father of the tribe of Qushayr and his mother of Sulaym; his father died while he was a child, so he read letters under Abu al-Qasim al-Yamani, then entered Nishapur on a tax errand and fell into Abu Ali al-Daqqaq's net, married his daughter Fatima, took law from Abu Bakr al-Tusi and kalam from Ibn Furak and Abu Ishaq al-Isfarayini until he stood at the head of the Ash'aris, and succeeded his shaykh at his madrasa in 405/1015 {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 171-173} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 564}.

## The ordeal and the writings
He composed his great tafsir before 410/1019 and held his first hadith dictation sessions in 437/1046 after a Pilgrimage in which he heard hadith at Baghdad and the Hijaz; when the pulpits of Khurasan were charged with cursing the Ash'aris and he was singled out for exile and prison, he wrote the 'Complaint of the people of the Sunna', reached Baghdad where al-Qa'im honoured him, was at Qazvin in Tughril Beg's company, and withdrew to Tus with his family {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 564} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 174-176} {cite:tabaqat-shafiiyya-subki pp. 269-276}.

## Return and death
When the Arslanian era dawned in 455/1063 and Nizam al-Mulk restored the balance he returned to Nishapur, where he lived ten honoured years and died on Sunday 16 Rabi' II 465 — Rajab in Ibn al-Jawzi's account — buried in his madrasa beside Abu Ali al-Daqqaq {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 175-176} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5 p. 564} {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 pp. 646-647}.

## In the introduction to the Dar al-Minhaj edition of the Risala
The editor of [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]] gives his name as Abu al-Qasim 'Abd al-Karim ibn Hawazin ibn 'Abd al-Malik ibn Talha ibn Muhammad al-Qushayri al-Ustuwa'i al-Naysaburi al-Shafi'i: al-Qushayri after the Banu Qushayr ibn Ka'b, his mother being of the Banu Sulaym; al-Ustuwa'i after Ustuwa, a district of Nishapur; his title Zayn al-Islam, and most often called "the Master" (al-Ustadh). {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 16} He was born in Rabi' al-Awwal 376 AH and grew up an orphan; he read literature with Abu al-Qasim al-Alyamani, then attached himself to [[person/abu-ali-al-daqqaq|Abu 'Ali al-Daqqaq]] in Nishapur, studied law with Abu Bakr al-Tusi and the two sciences of principles with Ibn Furak and then Abu Ishaq al-Isfara'ini, and read the books of al-Baqillani. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 16} {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 17} {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 18} The editor quotes Ibn al-Athir that al-Kunduri, vizier of Tughril Beg, had the Ash'aris cursed along with the Rafida, so that al-Qushayri, Imam al-Haramayn and others left their lands, and says al-Qushayri wrote on this his "Complaint of Ahl al-Sunna Relating the Trial That Befell Them" around 445 AH. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 23} {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 24} Among his books he lists Lata'if al-Isharat, al-Tahbir fi al-Tadhkir, Tartib al-Suluk, Nahw al-Qulub and al-Taysir fi 'Ilm al-Tafsir. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 35} {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 36} He died on the morning of Sunday 16 Rabi' al-Akhir 465 AH, as the editor reports from Ibn al-Subki, and was buried in his school at the feet of his shaykh al-Daqqaq; al-Muntazam gives Rajab as the month of his death. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 37} See [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]].
