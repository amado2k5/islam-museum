## Overview
Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Zakariya al-Razi, philosopher and physician, author of the "al-Mansuri" on medicine. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 547}

## In al-Mas'udi
Al-Mas'udi saw a book of his which mentions the doctrines of the Harranian Sabians, and distinguishes them from other Sabians, the Kaymariyun. Al-Mas'udi says that it contains matters too long to mention and which are ugly in the view of many people. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 547}

He says that al-Razi refuted al-Kindi's treatise on refuting the craft of gold, and wrote books on the mineral stones and the manner of their work. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 620}

## In Ibn al-Nadim
Ibn al-Nadim relates from him that he held that a philosopher's knowledge is not sound unless the science of alchemy is sound, and that the ancient philosophers used to practise it, and he called Jabir ibn Hayyan "our master". {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 494,500}

Ibn al-Nadim describes him, in the art of physicians, as unique in his age, combining the knowledge of the ancients especially medicine, and he wrote "al-Mansuri", and had a friendship with Mansur ibn Isma'il. He was generous and kind to the poor, and became blind at the end of his life. He mentions from the index of his books "al-Hawi" and "The Doubts on Galen". {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 415-417}
