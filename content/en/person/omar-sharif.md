## Summary

Omar Sharif (10 April 1932 – 10 July 2015), born Michel Yusef Dimitri Chalhoub in Alexandria, was an Egyptian actor described as his country's greatest male film star and 'the first Egyptian and Arab to conquer Hollywood'; he began in Egyptian cinema in the early 1950s, rose internationally as Sherif Ali in Lawrence of Arabia (1962) and in the title role of Doctor Zhivago (1965), acted in over 100 films in fifty years, and won three Golden Globe Awards and a César Award {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-en} {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-ar}.

## Life

Chalhoub was born in Alexandria into a Melkite Greek Catholic family of Syro-Lebanese descent and studied at Victoria College and Cairo University; the director Youssef Chahine cast him opposite Faten Hamama in The Blazing Sun (1954), and in order to marry her in 1955 he converted to Islam and changed his name legally to Omar Sharif — the Arabic article says in its own words that he 'converted to Islam in order to marry her' — and they had one son, Tarek, before divorcing in 1974 {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-en} {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-ar}. He later lived largely in Europe and died on 10 July 2015 at Bahman Hospital in Helwan, Cairo, a date the Arabic article pairs with 23 Ramadan 1436, as it pairs his birth with 4 Dhu al-Hijja 1350 {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-ar} {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-en}. No text names a legal school for him, and his later statements complicate the picture — in 2004 he said 'I believe in everything and nothing... I believe that God is Justice', and in 2010 'I believe in God and I believe in religion, but religion should belong to you' — so his faith is recorded as unknown with the whole trail preserved {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-en} {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-ar}.

## Work

From Egyptian cinema he moved to international productions: Lawrence of Arabia (1962), which brought him an Academy Award nomination and Golden Globes, Doctor Zhivago (1965), Funny Girl (1968), historical roles from Genghis Khan to Che Guevara, and late work including Monsieur Ibrahim (2003), which won him the César Award for Best Actor, and Hassan and Morcos (2008); he was also a world-class contract bridge player who wrote on the game {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-en} {cite:wikipedia-omar-sharif-ar}.
