## Summary
Abu Jalnak Ahmad al-Halabi, the renowned Aleppan poet — captured by the Tatars at Aleppo; when they asked him about the Muslim army he magnified it, so they killed him, in 700 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 795}.

## Details
Among the dead of 700 Ibn al-'Imad records "Abu Jalnak Ahmad al-Halabi, the famous poet": the Tatars took him prisoner at Aleppo and asked him about the Muslim army, whereupon he magnified and multiplied it in their eyes, so they killed him {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 795}. He perished in the Tatar capture of Aleppo; the editor notes he could find no biography of him in the sources at hand, so what we know of him rests on this one notice in Shadharat al-Dhahab {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 795}. The author quotes two of his lines — "and you are like longing, you neither spare nor leave ... what excuse will you bring before the blamers?" — a remnant of the verse of a man whom calamity killed while he was magnifying the courage of his own people {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 795}.
