## Summary
Ahmad Akansus was a Moroccan historian and man of letters of the Ida u Kansus, a Berber tribe of Sus. He served the Sharifian court, and his general history of Islam gives much space to the history of Morocco and the 'Alawi dynasty.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), article signed E. Lévi-Provençal, gives Ahmad as a Moroccan historian and man of letters from the Berber tribe of Ida u Kansus, which lived in Sus in southern Morocco, where he was born in 1211/1797. He studied at Fez, obtained a post as secretary at the Sharifian court, and was promoted vizier in 1236/1820. Mawlay Sulayman entrusted him with official missions, but he lost his post on the sultan's death in 1238/1822. He retired to Marrakesh, wrote poetical and historical works, and became a prominent representative of the Tijaniyya. He died blind, at an advanced age, on 29 Muharram 1294/14 February 1877 at Marrakesh. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 337}

His major work, al-Jaysh al-'aramram al-khumasi fi dawlat Awlad Mawlana 'Ali al-Sijilmasi, is a general history of Islam with pride of place given to Morocco and, in particular, to the 'Alawi dynasty up to 1282/1865. Its chief merit is that it is the first chronicle of the reigns of 'Abd al-Rahman b. Hisham and Muhammad b. 'Abd al-Rahman, and it was used by al-Nasiri. For the earlier period it copies most of all the chronicles of al-Ifrani and al-Zayyani. A limited number of copies were lithographed at Fez in 1336/1918. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 337}
