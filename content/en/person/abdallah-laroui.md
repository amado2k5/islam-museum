## Summary
Abdallah Laroui is a Moroccan historian counted among the founders of historical criticism in contemporary Arab thought, his name bound to the “Concepts” series deconstructing the terms of Arab political culture. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-laroui}

## Life and upbringing
Born in 1933 in Azemmour, Morocco, he studied history in Morocco and at the Sorbonne in Paris, working as a university professor and diplomat before devoting himself to writing. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-laroui}

## Career and works
His books include Contemporary Arab Ideology (1967), a critique of nationalist, salafist and liberal currents; The History of the Maghrib as an academic reference; the series The Concept of the State, The Concept of Freedom and The Concept of Reason; and novels and diaries. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-laroui}

## Method and contribution
Laroui calls for secularisation and historicism: reading ideas in their temporal context rather than seeking an eternal essence of identity. He is celebrated as a pioneer in renewing the Arab mind, and his secular positions are criticised by Islamists and nationalists alike; the museum attributes these descriptions to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-abdallah-laroui}
