## Overview
Harim ibn Hayyan al-Abdi, a commander of the Rashidun period. Al-Baladhuri reports in "The Conquests of the Lands" that Uthman ibn Abi al-As sent him to the fortress of Shubayr, and that he took it by storm after a siege and fighting. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Another report, which al-Baladhuri transmits from "some", says that he took the fortress of al-Satwaj by storm. The name of the fortress is therefore a point of disagreement in the source. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Ahmad Farid's lessons 'The Boat'
In [[work/the-boat-ahmad-farid|'The Boat']] [[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] quotes Harim ibn Hayyan on God's love: when a servant turns his heart towards God, God turns the hearts of the believers towards him until He grants him their affection. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep08 00:02:00} He cites the saying in his lesson on the love God places for believers among people, after the hadith of Gabriel's call. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep08 00:01:00}

## In Ahmad ibn Hanbal's Kitab al-Zuhd
[[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] gives Harim a chapter of [[work/kitab-al-zuhd-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Kitab al-Zuhd]] titled "Reports of Harim ibn Hayyan". {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 286} In it he relates from Matar al-Warraq that Harim spent a night with Humama, a Companion of the Prophet, who wept all night remembering a night whose morning will scatter the graves; and Humama spent a night with Harim, who wept remembering a night whose morning will scatter the stars; and that the two would go to the perfume market and ask God for paradise, then to the blacksmiths and seek refuge from the Fire. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 286}

Among his sayings reported by al-Hasan: "I have seen nothing like the Fire, whose fugitive sleeps, nor like paradise, whose seeker sleeps"; and among those reported by Qatada, that no servant turns his heart to God without God turning the believers' hearts to him. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 287} Abu Imran al-Jawni relates that he warned against "the sinful scholar", and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] wrote asking what he meant; he answered that it is a leader who speaks with knowledge and acts sinfully, so that people are confused. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 287}

Malik ibn Dinar relates that when he was appointed governor he lit a fire between himself and his people when they came, saying: do you want to cast me into a greater fire than this? {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 288} Al-Hasan relates that he went on pilgrimage with Abd Allah ibn Amir and wished he were a plant eaten by the camel rather than face the reckoning; al-Hasan says, "By God, he was the wiser of the two and the more knowing of God." {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 288} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 289} At his death he asked that his debt be paid by selling his armour and commended the closing verses of Surat al-Nahl; al-Hasan relates that he died on campaign on a hot day and a cloud came and watered his grave alone. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 286} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 289}
