## Overview
Sultan al-Zahir Rukn al-Din Baybars al-Bunduqdari. On the day of Ayn Jalut he was at the head of the army of [[person/qutuz|al-Muzaffar Qutuz]], and he pursued the Tatars as far as Aleppo. Ibn al-Imad says that Qutuz promised him Aleppo and then went back on his promise, and estrangement began between them. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.299}

Al-Suyuti says that he agreed with a group of the amirs to kill Qutuz, and they killed him on the road in 658 AH, and he took the sultanate and was titled al-Malik al-Zahir. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.720}

He established the caliphate in Egypt in Rajab 659 AH, and al-Mustansir was given the allegiance. In 674 AH he sent an army to Nubia, which took its king captive, and the jizya was imposed on the people of Dongola. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.723}

Ibn al-Imad says he was given the sultanate on the seventeenth of Dhu al-Qa'da 658 AH, witnessed the battle of al-Mansura, and died in Damascus on the twenty-eighth of Muharram 676 AH, having taken forty fortresses from the Franks. He says that if it were not for his oppression and tyranny at times he would be counted among the just kings; this is Ibn al-Imad's judgement. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.358}

## According to al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that the al-Zahiriyya madrasa, founded by al-Zahir Rukn al-Din Baybars al-Bunduqdari, stands at Bab al-Dhahab in the eastern great palace of the Fatimids, on the site of its mihrab. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1032}

He also says that Baybars was among the amirs who left Cairo for Syria after Aqtay's killing, and that they went to al-Malik al-Nasir Yusuf ibn al-Aziz, lord of Syria, who received them. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1081}
