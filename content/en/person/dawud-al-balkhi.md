## Summary
Dawud al-Balkhi was one of the early shaykhs of the East. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] names him in [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (notice 461) together with [[person/ibn-adham-ibrahim|Ibrahim ibn Adham]], [[person/shaqiq-al-balkhi|Shaqiq al-Balkhi]] and Hatim al-Asamm. He says that the others have already been treated, that Dawud never became as widely known as they did, and that in everything that reached him he found only one story about him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/44}

## Ibrahim ibn Adham's story
Ibrahim ibn Adham tells that he travelled between Kufa and Mecca with a man who, after praying two units, would say some words under his breath, and a bowl of bread in broth and a jug of water would be there on his right; he ate and fed Ibrahim. Ibrahim mentioned this to a shaykh known for marvels, who said: that is my brother Dawud. The shaykh described his state until those present wept, and said that he lived beyond the river of Balkh in a village he named. He asked what Dawud had taught him, and Ibrahim said: God's greatest name. The story ends with the shaykh making himself known as al-Khidr and telling him that the ascetics have taken contentment with God as their garment. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/44-45}

## Abu Nu'aym's comment
Abu Nu'aym says that he saw this story transmitted with a chain from Ibrahim ibn Adham, and did not want to leave the book without a mention of Dawud. The whole notice rests on this one story; it gives no lineage and no date. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/45}
