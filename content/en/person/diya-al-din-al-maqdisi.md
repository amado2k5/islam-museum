## Summary
Diya' al-Din Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahid ibn Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Isma'il ibn Mansur al-Sa'di al-Maqdisi al-Salihi, the great hafiz, of whom [[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]] says: the hadith scholar of his age, unique in his time, whose fame makes lengthy praise unnecessary. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 236} He was the nephew, through his mother, of [[person/abu-umar-al-maqdisi|Shaykh Abu Umar]] and [[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|al-Muwaffaq Ibn Qudama]], and Ibn Rajab draws most of his reports on the Maqdisi household from him. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 134} Shadharat al-Dhahab calls him the hadith scholar of Syria, the shaykh of the Sunna and the authority on criticism and vindication of narrators. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 231-233}

## Birth
His own handwriting gives his birth as 5 Jumada II 569; [[person/ibn-al-najjar|Ibn al-Najjar]], however, says he asked him and was told: in Jumada I of that year. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 236} The year is agreed; the month differs between his note and his answer.

## Travels and hearing
He heard in Damascus from Abu al-Majd al-Banyasi, al-Khadir ibn Tawus and Ahmad ibn al-Mawazini; in Egypt from al-Busiri and Fatima bint Sa'd al-Khayr; and much in Baghdad from [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]], Ibn al-Ma'tush, Ibn Sukayna and Ibn al-Akhdar. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 237} In [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]] he heard from Abu Ja'far al-Saydalani and his generation, in [[place/hamadan|Hamadan]] from Abd al-Baqi ibn Uthman, in [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]] from al-Mu'ayyad al-Tusi, in [[place/herat|Herat]] from Abu Rawh and in [[place/marw|Marw]] from Abu al-Muzaffar ibn al-Sam'ani. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 237} He travelled twice to Isfahan and heard there more than can be described, stayed for a time in Herat and Marw, held licences from al-Silafi and Shuhda, and is said to have written from more than five hundred shaykhs and to have acquired many original manuscripts. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 237}

## Praise from the scholars
Ibn al-Najjar said: I wrote from him in Baghdad, Nishapur and Damascus; he is a hafiz, exact, firm, trustworthy, truthful, noble, a proof, learned in hadith and in the states of narrators, scrupulous, God-fearing, ascetic, devout, careful to eat only what is lawful, a fighter in God's path; my eyes have not seen his like in integrity, chastity and his fine way of seeking knowledge. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 237} Umar ibn al-Hajib said: our shaykh was the shaykh of his time and without peer in knowledge, memory, reliability and religion, one of the godly scholars, extremely careful in transmission, assiduous in worship, withdrawn from people. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 237} Ibn al-Hajib asked [[person/al-zaki-al-barzali|al-Zaki al-Birzali]] about him and was told: trustworthy, a mountain, a hafiz, devout. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 237}

[[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] reports that [[person/jamal-al-din-al-mizzi|the hafiz al-Mizzi]] used to say: al-Diya' knew more about hadith and narrators than the hafiz [[person/abd-al-ghani-al-maqdisi|Abd al-Ghani]], and there was no one like him in his time. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 238} Al-Dhahabi himself calls him the imam, scholar, hafiz and proof, the hadith scholar of Syria and the shaykh of the Sunna, who wrote, authenticated, weakened, criticised and vindicated, and was the one referred to in this field. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 238} The sharif Abu al-Abbas al-Husayni counted him among the imams of the field, knowing narrators and their states and hadith sound and unsound, scrupulous, devout and free of affectation. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 238}

## The Diya'iyya school
Al-Dhahabi says he built a school at the door of the Muzaffari mosque on the slope of Qasyun, helped by some benefactors, and endowed it with his books and fascicles. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 238} Another writer says he built it for hadith scholars and visiting strangers despite poverty, building one part and then waiting until he had gathered enough to build more, working on it with his own hands and accepting nothing from anyone for it out of scrupulousness. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 238} He kept to the mountain of [[place/al-salihiyya|al-Salihiyya]]. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 238}

## Works
Ibn Rajab lists his works. First is al-Ahkam, about twenty fascicles in three volumes, left unfinished; then al-Ahadith al-Mukhtara, hadiths fit to be used as proof outside the two Sahihs, drawn from his own hearing, of which he wrote ninety fascicles without completing it — some imams said it was better than al-Hakim's Sahih. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 238} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 239} Others include the Virtues of Deeds, the Virtues of Syria, the Merits of the People of Hadith, the Description of Paradise, the Description of Hell, the Singular and the Rare Reports of the Sahih, the Hijra to Abyssinia, the Virtues of the Qur'an, the Narrators from al-Bukhari, the Proofs of Prophethood, the Prohibition of Reviling the Companions, and the Command to Follow the Sunnas and Avoid Innovations. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 239} He wrote a book on why the Maqdisis emigrated to Damascus and the wonders of their shaykhs in about ten fascicles, and gave each of their great scholars a biography in many fascicles, among them his uncle al-Muwaffaq's in two and the hafiz Abd al-Ghani's. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 239} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 136} On the criticism of chains he wrote al-Irshad on difficult cases of *mursal* transmission in the chain, corrections to the hafiz Abd al-Ghani and to Ibn Asakir's al-Mashyakha al-Nubl, and an index of the opening phrases of Ibn al-Jawzi's al-Mawdu'at. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 239}

## His students and death
Those who narrated from him include [[person/ibn-naqta|Ibn Nuqta]] in his Istidrak, Ibn al-Najjar in his history, al-Birzali, Umar ibn al-Hajib, his nephew [[person/al-fakhr-ibn-al-bukhari|al-Fakhr Ibn al-Bukhari]], the judge Taqi al-Din Sulayman and a great many others. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 239} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 240} He died on Monday 18 Jumada II 643 on the slope of Qasyun and was buried there. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 240}
