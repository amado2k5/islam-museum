## Summary
Mohamed Bazoum was elected president of Niger in 2021 in what was widely described as the country's first peaceful democratic transition between elected leaders; two years later he was overthrown by his own presidential guard in the July 2023 coup and detained in the presidential palace. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bazoum} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bazoum}

## Formation
Born in 1960 at Bilabrin in southeastern Niger, into the Ouled Slimane Arab community of the Diffa region, Bazoum studied philosophy at the University of Dakar and worked as a teacher before entering politics. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bazoum}

## Career
A co-founder and later president of the Nigerien Party for Democracy and Socialism (PNDS-Tarayya), he served as minister of foreign affairs and of the interior under President Mahamadou Issoufou before winning the 2020–21 election. His presidency pursued security co-operation against jihadist insurgencies in the Sahel until the coup of 26 July 2023 placed him under house arrest; regional bodies and Western governments demanded his restoration, unsuccessfully. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bazoum} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bazoum}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 recorded him as the emblematic case of democratic transfer in the Sahel; after the coup his continued detention became a focus of regional diplomacy and of international human-rights attention. The museum reports the facts and positions of the parties attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-bazoum} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-bazoum}
