## Overview
The shaykh Aq Shams al-Din, Muhammad ibn Hamza al-Dimashqi al-Rumi, born in 792 AH (1389 CE) and died around 1459 CE. He travelled with his father to the lands of Rum and sought the sciences. His lineage goes back to Abu Bakr. He was the teacher and tutor of Mehmed the Conqueror. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.113-114}

## Upbringing and learning
He memorised the Qur'an at the age of seven, and studied at Amasya, then Aleppo, then Ankara. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.113-114}

## The Conqueror's teacher
He taught the Conqueror the Qur'an, the Sunna, jurisprudence, Arabic, Persian, Turkish, mathematics, astronomy, history and the arts of war. He supervised him while he was a prince at Manisa, training in the administration of a province. Al-Salabi says that he persuaded the young prince that he was the one meant by the Prophet's hadith about the conquest of Constantinople. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.113-114}

## At the siege of Constantinople
Al-Salabi relates that during the siege the amirs and viziers gathered before the sultan and expressed their despair. The sultan sent his vizier to the shaykh's tent, and the shaykh wrote a message that the servant plans and God decrees. The war council decided to continue. He says that the Conqueror said on the taking of the city that he was delighted by the presence of such a man. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.113-114}
