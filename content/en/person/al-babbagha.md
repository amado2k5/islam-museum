## Summary
ʿAbd al-Wahid ibn Nasr ibn ʿAli, Abu al-Faraj al-Makhzumi, the poet known as al-Babbagha ("the Parrot"), was a cultivated man of letters and a skilled epistolary writer. He died in Shaʿban 398 AH. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.241-243}

## Appeal from prison
One of his best-known verses is his poem addressed to ʿAmid al-Juyush when he was imprisoned: "I asked my time whom I should appeal to, and it said: appeal to ʿAmid al-Juyush." In it he says: "My house was seized, my slaves fled, my clothes wore out, and I sold my furnishings. I used to be nicknamed al-Babbagha, but time has torn my finery, and my food was pure rice; and now here I am, content with sāl al-shīsh." {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.242}

## Ibn Hilal al-Sabi's letter
Abu Ishaq [[person/ibrahim-ibn-hilal-al-sabi|Ibrahim ibn Hilal al-Sabi]] wrote to him from prison, after he had visited him in his confinement, complaining to him of his cage. Al-Babbagha replied in verse, saying: "Though I was once titled al-Babbagha, many have been given the title of injustice and not of justice." {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.242-243}
