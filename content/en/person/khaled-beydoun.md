## Summary
Khaled A. Beydoun is an Egyptian-American law professor and a leading academic authority on Islamophobia — the subject of his best-known book, 'American Islamophobia: Understanding the Roots and Rise of Fear' (2018). He teaches at Arizona State University's law school. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-beydoun}

## Formation and scholarship
Born in 1980 and raised in Detroit's Arab-American community, Beydoun trained as a lawyer and worked in civil-rights litigation before moving into academia, teaching at law schools including Wayne State, Arkansas and Arizona State. His scholarship examines how Islamophobia operates as a system — in law, national-security policy and political discourse — rather than merely as individual prejudice, an argument 'American Islamophobia' set out for a general readership. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-beydoun}

## Public voice
Beydoun writes and comments widely for general audiences — essays, opinion journalism and a large social-media presence — on anti-Muslim discrimination, US foreign policy and Arab and Muslim American life. A subsequent book, 'The New Crusades', extended the argument across the War on Terror era. {cite:muslim500-khaled-beydoun}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as the most visible academic interpreter of anti-Muslim discrimination for the American public — a scholar whose framing of Islamophobia as structural rather than incidental has shaped both advocacy and public debate. {cite:muslim500-khaled-beydoun}
