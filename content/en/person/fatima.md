## Brief
Fatima, the youngest daughter of the Prophet Muhammad by [[person/khadija|Khadija]], the wife of [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]], and the mother of [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|al-Hasan]] and [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn]]. Through her two sons the Prophet's lineage continues.

## Life
She was born in Mecca at some point between about 605 and 615 CE; the sources differ {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. She is recorded as a girl washing from her father the harm that the opponents in Mecca used to throw on him. She migrated to Medina at the [[event/hijra|hijra]] and married Ali around 624 CE. Their household lived in hardship and patience {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

After the Prophet's death in June 632 CE, the early sources record her grief and her dispute with the new leadership over an inheritance she claimed from her father's estate at Fadak. She died a few months after him, between three and six months according to differing reports, and was buried in Medina in an unmarked grave, as the tradition relates {cite:madelung-1997}.

## Legacy
Every lineage traced back to the Prophet, including the sharifs and honoured notables across the Islamic world, goes back to Fatima and Ali. Her name is borne by towns, mosques, a whole Fatimid dynasty and millions of women.

## For young visitors
Fatima was the daughter of the Prophet. Her two sons were the Prophet's only grandchildren who grew up, so everyone who today says he is a descendant of the Prophet is a descendant of hers.

## On the marriage to Khadija (The Sealed Nectar p. 61)
The Sealed Nectar, in its chapter on the marriage, says that Fatima was among the children of [[person/khadija|Khadija]], the fifth in order after al-Qasim, Zaynab, Ruqayya and Umm Kulthum. All of the Prophet's daughters died during his lifetime except Fatima, who came after him six months later and then joined him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 61} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/190-191} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p. 60}.
