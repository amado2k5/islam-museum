## Summary
Abu Ishaq al-Heweny (also spelled al-Huwayni), born Hijazi Muhammad Yusuf Sharif, was an Egyptian hadith scholar and preacher, born in the village of Huwayn in Kafr el-Sheikh Governorate on 1 Dhu al-Qa'da 1375 AH / 10 June 1956, and died in Doha on 17 Ramadan 1446 AH / 17 March 2025. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikidata-abu-ishaq-al-heweny}

He is regarded as one of Egypt's most prominent contemporary hadith scholars. He studied with Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani and was shaped by his books and editions; he wrote and edited works on hadith science and gave widely followed lessons and television programmes. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

## Upbringing and education
He was born in the village of Huwayn, about thirteen kilometres from the capital of Kafr el-Sheikh Governorate, into a middle-class farming family; his father named him Hijazi on returning from the Hajj. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

He began seeking knowledge young: he took the Qur'an from his uncle Abd al-Hay Zayyan and attended the lessons of Shaykh Muhammad Najib al-Muti'i in Shafi'i jurisprudence, the principles of jurisprudence and the principles of hadith. For his final school year he moved to Cairo, stayed with his brother, and attended the lectures of Abd al-Hamid Kishk. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

He joined the Faculty of Al-Alsun at Ain Shams University, in the Spanish department, ranking first in his class every year except the last, graduated with excellence and won a scholarship to continue in Spain, where he did not stay long. He worked in a small grocery by day and spent his nights reading al-Albani's books, and he said that studying al-Albani's «Al-Silsila al-Da'ifa» for two years at university benefited him more than all his other years of study. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

## His studies and teachers
As a boy he loved Arabic language and literature, reading al-Manfaluti, al-Rafi'i, Mahmud Shakir and others, before turning to the religious sciences and to hadith and the Sunna collections in particular. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

His teachers included Muhammad Najib al-Muti'i, from whom he took the principles of jurisprudence and of hadith and part of «Sahih al-Bukhari» and al-Nawawi's «Al-Majmu'» in Shafi'i law; Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani, the teacher closest to his heart, to whom he made two famous journeys in Jordan, in Muharram 1407 AH and Dhu al-Hijja 1410 AH, asking him some two hundred questions on the hidden defects of hadith alone; Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz, whose lessons he attended in Jeddah; Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymin, whose lessons he attended at the Sacred Mosque; Ibn Jibrin; Abd Allah ibn Qa'ud; and his uncle Abd al-Hay Zayyan for the Qur'an and its recitation. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Career and preaching
He preached two Fridays in each Hijri month at the Shaykh al-Islam Mosque in his home town and gave a weekly lecture at the Ibn Taymiyya Mosque in Kafr el-Sheikh city. He presented a weekly live programme, «Fadfada», on the Al-Nas channel, appeared on Al-Hekma and Al-Rahma, and supervised the Al-Nada channel, where he presented «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'». {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported from the sources, not as the museum's verdict on them.

The sources describe him as one of the most prominent Salafi shaykhs in Egypt; the Arabic article describes his creed as Athari and records his work of criticising weak and fabricated hadiths and answering opponents, including his book «Tali'at Samt al-La'ali'», a reply to Muhammad al-Ghazali. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

The English article reports that some claimed he said "a woman's face is like her genitals", and that the words were taken out of context, as he was speaking about hijab and what should be covered. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

The Arabic article records praise from a number of scholars: his teacher al-Albani told him "What has been authenticated for you has not been authenticated for another" and called him "our virtuous friend" in his books; Abd al-Aziz al-Rajihi recommended him to a questioner asking where to study in Egypt; and Mashhur Hasan Al Salman testified that he was among the firmly grounded scholars of hadith. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Books
He wrote and edited many works, some printed and some still in manuscript. The best known include «Fasl al-Khitab bi-Naqd al-Mughni 'an al-Hifz wa'l-Kitab» (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, 1405 AH/1985), «Al-Inshirah fi Adab al-Nikah» (1407 AH/1987), «Al-Nafila fi'l-Ahadith al-Da'ifa wa'l-Batila» (1408 AH/1988), «Kashf al-Makhbu' bi-Thubut Hadith al-Tasmiya 'ind al-Wudu'», «Nahi al-Suhba 'an al-Nuzul bi'l-Rukba», «Ghawth al-Makdud bi-Takhrij Muntaqa Ibn al-Jarud», «Badhl al-Ihsan bi-Taqrib Sunan al-Nasa'i», «Tali'at Samt al-La'ali' fi'l-Radd 'ala Muhammad al-Ghazali», and editions of «Al-Ba'th» by Abd Allah ibn Abi Dawud, «Al-Arba'un al-Sughra» by al-Bayhaqi, and «Kitab al-Samt wa Adab al-Lisan» by Ibn Abi'l-Dunya. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Personal life
He married twice and had twelve sons; among his known sons is Hatem, who announced his death. The museum publishes no family detail beyond what understanding his work requires. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Death
He died in Doha on the evening of Monday 17 Ramadan 1446 AH / 17 March 2025, after a health crisis that took him to hospital; Wikidata gives the cause of death as cerebral haemorrhage. Ahmad Isa al-Ma'sarawi led his funeral prayer after the afternoon prayer the next day at the Imam Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab Mosque in Doha, and he was buried at Mesaimeer Cemetery. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikidata-abu-ishaq-al-heweny}

## Impact and audience
Wikidata records 731,000 subscribers for his YouTube channel on 7 March 2021 — a dated platform figure the museum records only with its date. His lessons and books continue to circulate through his official website and the platforms after his death. {cite:wikidata-abu-ishaq-al-heweny} The channel is published under the handle @alhuwayni; its own description names it his official channel, and it was verified in a channel harvest checked on 25 October 2026. {cite:youtube-alhuwayni}

## What he relates about himself in Madrasat al-Hayat
In his lesson series [[work/madrassat-al-hayat|Madrasat al-Hayat]], for the most part a commentary on Ibn al-Jawzi's [[work/sayd-al-khatir|Sayd al-Khatir]], al-Heweny says that he opened this commentary every Ramadan, that he had begun it about four years or a little more before the first lesson in the archive, and that he selects particular reflections from the book rather than reading it all. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:02:30} In a lesson from the Book of Knowledge of Sahih al-Bukhari he says that on Mondays he used to explain Sayd al-Khatir one week and a book of hadith terminology the next, and tells how a man who had come from Beheira stood up in the middle of the lesson to say he had understood nothing. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:28:30}

Of his beginnings he says that at the end of secondary school and the start of university, in '74 and '75, he taught a circle made up mostly of pupils in the fifth and sixth years of primary school and in preparatory school, and that these youngsters, and later university students, responded faster than their fathers. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:31:00} He mentions that he was treated for a time in Germany, that while there he saw an attack on al-Bukhari and nearly cut his trip short to answer it, and that on his return he gave episodes on the al-Nas channel and wrote a treatise in reply. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:52:00} He says that during his treatment he learned from the doctors how insulin works in the cell, and explains it to his audience as a witness to God's blessings in the body. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:22:30}

In the lesson on evasion he relates that he was barred from teaching for six months, then confined to his own district of Kafr el-Sheikh Governorate, which has thirteen districts, and then to a single mosque he could not leave, and that the general council of al-Jam'iyya al-Shar'iyya wrote to its branches that a mosque would be handed to the Ministry of Awqaf if he preached or taught in it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:45:30} He says that he and a group of shaykhs have met regularly for more than twenty-five years, and that in a call to the al-Hekma channel he answered what newspapers and programmes had attributed to him about restoring slave-girls. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:38:30} All of this is his own account of what happened to him. He mentions that he owns the two surviving volumes of Ibn Aqil's al-Funun. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:46:30}

In the lesson from the Book of Knowledge he sets out his method for explaining any hadith along seven lines: the chapter heading, its pillars and its link with what comes before and after; introducing the men of the chain; the lessons of hadith science drawn from the chain, applied rather than theoretical; al-Bukhari's repetitions of the hadith and how the chain or wording differs; who else narrates it, above all Muslim; the additions in other collections; and finally the explanation, drawing many lessons from few words. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:10:00} He says he is very fond of Sahih Muslim for its craft of chains and because Muslim does not cut up the texts, and that the chapter headings printed in it are al-Nawawi's, not Muslim's. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:44:30}

## What he tells of himself in «Difaf al-Kawthar»
In the episodes of [[work/dhifaf-al-kawtar|«Difaf al-Kawthar»]], a filmed programme of short episodes that opens with a sung anthem and introduces al-Heweny as "the muhaddith", he tells a few things about himself. The clearest is that he made his first Hajj in 1410 AH with Shaykh [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani]]; he says it was al-Albani's last Hajj, that he rode with him by car from Jedda to Mecca sitting right behind him, that he had asked leave to be the only one putting questions on the way, and that he was recording. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:23:00}

He says he is working on a critical edition (tahqiq) of a collection of hadiths that speak of the deeds most beloved to God, to read them to people so that each can take from them what he is able to do. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep05 00:18:00} He says he does not know how to use a computer, not even its power button, nor how to use it for scholarly indexes, and that many parents are like him in this. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:15:00} He says he has loved [[person/abu-hazim-al-araj|Abu Hazim Salama ibn Dinar]] for a long time, for the weight of his words and the scholars' agreement in praising him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:02:00}

In several episodes he teaches at a board in front of the viewers, drawing chains of transmission and marking the narrator dropped from them. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:20:30} He says questions reach him about mosques where four or five parts (juz') of the Qur'an are recited in the night prayer, and that he has not seen this himself. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:24:30} In an episode on polygamy he says he has stopped many men who came to him wanting to take a second wife, that many women turn to him to prevent it, and he describes himself as among the strongest defenders of women's rights; this is his own account of himself. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:20:00}
