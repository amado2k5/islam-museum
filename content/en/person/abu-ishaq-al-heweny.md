## Summary
Abu Ishaq al-Heweny (also spelled al-Huwayni), born Hijazi Muhammad Yusuf Sharif, was an Egyptian hadith scholar and preacher, born in the village of Huwayn in Kafr el-Sheikh Governorate on 1 Dhu al-Qa'da 1375 AH / 10 June 1956, and died in Doha on 17 Ramadan 1446 AH / 17 March 2025. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikidata-abu-ishaq-al-heweny}

He is regarded as one of Egypt's most prominent contemporary hadith scholars. He studied with Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani and was shaped by his books and editions; he wrote and edited works on hadith science and gave widely followed lessons and television programmes. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

## Upbringing and education
He was born in the village of Huwayn, about thirteen kilometres from the capital of Kafr el-Sheikh Governorate, into a middle-class farming family; his father named him Hijazi on returning from the Hajj. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

He began seeking knowledge young: he took the Qur'an from his uncle Abd al-Hay Zayyan and attended the lessons of Shaykh Muhammad Najib al-Muti'i in Shafi'i jurisprudence, the principles of jurisprudence and the principles of hadith. For his final school year he moved to Cairo, stayed with his brother, and attended the lectures of Abd al-Hamid Kishk. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

He joined the Faculty of Al-Alsun at Ain Shams University, in the Spanish department, ranking first in his class every year except the last, graduated with excellence and won a scholarship to continue in Spain, where he did not stay long. He worked in a small grocery by day and spent his nights reading al-Albani's books, and he said that studying al-Albani's «Al-Silsila al-Da'ifa» for two years at university benefited him more than all his other years of study. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

## His studies and teachers
As a boy he loved Arabic language and literature, reading al-Manfaluti, al-Rafi'i, Mahmud Shakir and others, before turning to the religious sciences and to hadith and the Sunna collections in particular. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

His teachers included Muhammad Najib al-Muti'i, from whom he took the principles of jurisprudence and of hadith and part of «Sahih al-Bukhari» and al-Nawawi's «Al-Majmu'» in Shafi'i law; Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani, the teacher closest to his heart, to whom he made two famous journeys in Jordan, in Muharram 1407 AH and Dhu al-Hijja 1410 AH, asking him some two hundred questions on the hidden defects of hadith alone; Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz, whose lessons he attended in Jeddah; Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymin, whose lessons he attended at the Sacred Mosque; Ibn Jibrin; Abd Allah ibn Qa'ud; and his uncle Abd al-Hay Zayyan for the Qur'an and its recitation. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Career and preaching
He preached two Fridays in each Hijri month at the Shaykh al-Islam Mosque in his home town and gave a weekly lecture at the Ibn Taymiyya Mosque in Kafr el-Sheikh city. He presented a weekly live programme, «Fadfada», on the Al-Nas channel, appeared on Al-Hekma and Al-Rahma, and supervised the Al-Nada channel, where he presented «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'». {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported from the sources, not as the museum's verdict on them.

The sources describe him as one of the most prominent Salafi shaykhs in Egypt; the Arabic article describes his creed as Athari and records his work of criticising weak and fabricated hadiths and answering opponents, including his book «Tali'at Samt al-La'ali'», a reply to Muhammad al-Ghazali. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

The English article reports that some claimed he said "a woman's face is like her genitals", and that the words were taken out of context, as he was speaking about hijab and what should be covered. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-en}

The Arabic article records praise from a number of scholars: his teacher al-Albani told him "What has been authenticated for you has not been authenticated for another" and called him "our virtuous friend" in his books; Abd al-Aziz al-Rajihi recommended him to a questioner asking where to study in Egypt; and Mashhur Hasan Al Salman testified that he was among the firmly grounded scholars of hadith. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Books
He wrote and edited many works, some printed and some still in manuscript. The best known include «Fasl al-Khitab bi-Naqd al-Mughni 'an al-Hifz wa'l-Kitab» (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, 1405 AH/1985), «Al-Inshirah fi Adab al-Nikah» (1407 AH/1987), «Al-Nafila fi'l-Ahadith al-Da'ifa wa'l-Batila» (1408 AH/1988), «Kashf al-Makhbu' bi-Thubut Hadith al-Tasmiya 'ind al-Wudu'», «Nahi al-Suhba 'an al-Nuzul bi'l-Rukba», «Ghawth al-Makdud bi-Takhrij Muntaqa Ibn al-Jarud», «Badhl al-Ihsan bi-Taqrib Sunan al-Nasa'i», «Tali'at Samt al-La'ali' fi'l-Radd 'ala Muhammad al-Ghazali», and editions of «Al-Ba'th» by Abd Allah ibn Abi Dawud, «Al-Arba'un al-Sughra» by al-Bayhaqi, and «Kitab al-Samt wa Adab al-Lisan» by Ibn Abi'l-Dunya. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Personal life
He married twice and had twelve sons; among his known sons is Hatem, who announced his death. The museum publishes no family detail beyond what understanding his work requires. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar}

## Death
He died in Doha on the evening of Monday 17 Ramadan 1446 AH / 17 March 2025, after a health crisis that took him to hospital; Wikidata gives the cause of death as cerebral haemorrhage. Ahmad Isa al-Ma'sarawi led his funeral prayer after the afternoon prayer the next day at the Imam Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab Mosque in Doha, and he was buried at Mesaimeer Cemetery. {cite:wikipedia-abu-ishaq-al-heweny-ar} {cite:wikidata-abu-ishaq-al-heweny}

## Impact and audience
Wikidata records 731,000 subscribers for his YouTube channel on 7 March 2021 — a dated platform figure the museum records only with its date. His lessons and books continue to circulate through his official website and the platforms after his death. {cite:wikidata-abu-ishaq-al-heweny} The channel is published under the handle @alhuwayni; its own description names it his official channel, and it was verified in a channel harvest checked on 25 October 2026. {cite:youtube-alhuwayni}

## What he relates about himself in Madrasat al-Hayat
In his lesson series [[work/madrassat-al-hayat|Madrasat al-Hayat]], for the most part a commentary on Ibn al-Jawzi's [[work/sayd-al-khatir|Sayd al-Khatir]], al-Heweny says that he opened this commentary every Ramadan, that he had begun it about four years or a little more before the first lesson in the archive, and that he selects particular reflections from the book rather than reading it all. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:02:30} In a lesson from the Book of Knowledge of Sahih al-Bukhari he says that on Mondays he used to explain Sayd al-Khatir one week and a book of hadith terminology the next, and tells how a man who had come from Beheira stood up in the middle of the lesson to say he had understood nothing. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:28:30}

Of his beginnings he says that at the end of secondary school and the start of university, in '74 and '75, he taught a circle made up mostly of pupils in the fifth and sixth years of primary school and in preparatory school, and that these youngsters, and later university students, responded faster than their fathers. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:31:00} He mentions that he was treated for a time in Germany, that while there he saw an attack on al-Bukhari and nearly cut his trip short to answer it, and that on his return he gave episodes on the al-Nas channel and wrote a treatise in reply. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:52:00} He says that during his treatment he learned from the doctors how insulin works in the cell, and explains it to his audience as a witness to God's blessings in the body. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:22:30}

In the lesson on evasion he relates that he was barred from teaching for six months, then confined to his own district of Kafr el-Sheikh Governorate, which has thirteen districts, and then to a single mosque he could not leave, and that the general council of al-Jam'iyya al-Shar'iyya wrote to its branches that a mosque would be handed to the Ministry of Awqaf if he preached or taught in it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:45:30} He says that he and a group of shaykhs have met regularly for more than twenty-five years, and that in a call to the al-Hekma channel he answered what newspapers and programmes had attributed to him about restoring slave-girls. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:38:30} All of this is his own account of what happened to him. He mentions that he owns the two surviving volumes of Ibn Aqil's al-Funun. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:46:30}

In the lesson from the Book of Knowledge he sets out his method for explaining any hadith along seven lines: the chapter heading, its pillars and its link with what comes before and after; introducing the men of the chain; the lessons of hadith science drawn from the chain, applied rather than theoretical; al-Bukhari's repetitions of the hadith and how the chain or wording differs; who else narrates it, above all Muslim; the additions in other collections; and finally the explanation, drawing many lessons from few words. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:10:00} He says he is very fond of Sahih Muslim for its craft of chains and because Muslim does not cut up the texts, and that the chapter headings printed in it are al-Nawawi's, not Muslim's. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:44:30}

## What he tells of himself in «Difaf al-Kawthar»
In the episodes of [[work/dhifaf-al-kawtar|«Difaf al-Kawthar»]], a filmed programme of short episodes that opens with a sung anthem and introduces al-Heweny as "the muhaddith", he tells a few things about himself. The clearest is that he made his first Hajj in 1410 AH with Shaykh [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani]]; he says it was al-Albani's last Hajj, that he rode with him by car from Jedda to Mecca sitting right behind him, that he had asked leave to be the only one putting questions on the way, and that he was recording. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:23:00}

He says he is working on a critical edition (tahqiq) of a collection of hadiths that speak of the deeds most beloved to God, to read them to people so that each can take from them what he is able to do. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep05 00:18:00} He says he does not know how to use a computer, not even its power button, nor how to use it for scholarly indexes, and that many parents are like him in this. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:15:00} He says he has loved [[person/abu-hazim-al-araj|Abu Hazim Salama ibn Dinar]] for a long time, for the weight of his words and the scholars' agreement in praising him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep16 00:02:00}

In several episodes he teaches at a board in front of the viewers, drawing chains of transmission and marking the narrator dropped from them. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:20:30} He says questions reach him about mosques where four or five parts (juz') of the Qur'an are recited in the night prayer, and that he has not seen this himself. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep07 00:24:30} In an episode on polygamy he says he has stopped many men who came to him wanting to take a second wife, that many women turn to him to prevent it, and he describes himself as among the strongest defenders of women's rights; this is his own account of himself. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:20:00}

## His life as he tells it in «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'»
In the thirty episodes of [[work/asdaf-al-loaloa|«Asdaf al-Lu'lu'»]], a filmed programme recorded at his home before Ramadan to be broadcast on its nights, al-Heweny tells his life through books: what he read, what he bought, what he wrote, and who shaped him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:08:30} He says his love of books began early, that he was writing rhymed lines from the sixth year of primary school, and that his elder full brother, Dr Rizq, taught the brothers to read and made them love it; Dr Rizq had a small library in which he saw, for the first time, seventy or a hundred books standing together. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:10:30} As a boy he read the writers of the day, among them Tawfiq al-Hakim, Yusuf al-Siba'i, Yusuf Idris and Anis Mansur; he says the first writer who made him weep was Mustafa Lutfi al-Manfaluti, in his second preparatory year, that he came to love [[person/mustafa-sadiq-al-rafii|Mustafa Sadiq al-Rafi'i]] through Mahmud Shakir, and that he read [[person/yahya-haqqi|Yahya Haqqi]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:25:00} Then he turned to classical poetry: [[person/mahmud-sami-al-barudi|Mahmud Sami al-Barudi]], whom he called "the Mutanabbi of the modern age", [[person/al-mutanabbi|al-Mutanabbi]] himself, who overwhelmed him as a poet though he does not like his character, then the vagabond poets, up to al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani, whom he ranks highest. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:10:30} He says he wrote eight diwans of poetry, which Dr Rizq still keeps, that he wrote poetry until his third secondary year and half of his first college year before hadith drew him away, and that this literary reading shaped his style in writing and speaking. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:26:30}

He says his brother's library held two books of religious learning: «Tafsir al-Khazin», in old loose quires, which he came upon in 1974 and which was the first tafsir he read, and «Musnad al-Imam Ahmad» edited by [[person/ahmad-muhammad-shakir|Ahmad Shakir]], whose chains he could not follow then; that first-edition copy is in his library today. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:22:30} The first thing he ever wrote was a tract he called «al-Durra al-Saniyya»: he set out to write his own will and it grew into a booklet, which he calls research rather than authorship because it rested on al-Albani's «Ahkam al-Jana'iz»; he dates it to 1979, the year he graduated. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep02 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:10:30}

In his third secondary year, in deep anxiety over the exams, he stayed with his brother in Cairo, who was studying medicine at al-Azhar, and prayed the Friday prayer at the nearby mosque of [[person/abdul-hamid-kishk|Shaykh Abd al-Hamid Kishk]]; it was the first time he had heard a preacher of such eloquence. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:12:30} At the book stalls after the sermon he found al-Albani's «Sifat Salat al-Nabi»; he had only a shilling in his pocket, so its abridgement was the first book he bought with his own money, and the first bound volume he bought was al-Shawkani's [[work/al-fawaid-al-majmua-fi-al-ahadith-al-mawdua|«al-Fawa'id al-Majmu'a»]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:19:30} He says he scored 79 per cent in the secondary-school certificate in 1975, after thinking he had failed the mechanics paper and neglecting the rest; he had wanted medicine like his brothers, then sat the police college tests and was put off by the cursing he heard there, so he chose the Faculty of Al-Alsun. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:11:00} He was first placed in the Arabic department; he spoke to the vice-dean, Dr Abd Allah Khurshid, who moved him to Spanish. He loved Spanish for the memory of al-Andalus, hoped to become a member of its language academy, and his classmates called him "ratón", the library mouse. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:21:30} His heart turned to religious learning at the end of his first university year. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep03 00:23:00}

He tells how he first let his beard grow in the two months he spent with his brother in Cairo, moved by Kishk's sermons, and how his brother made him shave it; his father, he says, belonged to the Shadhili order; he let it grow again in Spain, on a short scholarship that the Spanish cultural centre gave to top students, and there in the hotel he memorised a large part of the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:25:30} He lived in the university city near Abbasiyya Square while the faculty was in Hilmiyyat al-Zaytun; he summarised his Spanish subjects in a hundred-page notebook, revised the Qur'an he was memorising on foot when the buses were full, and hid his religious books from his brother, who wanted him to become a teaching assistant or a simultaneous interpreter. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:19:00}

After graduating he was not appointed a teaching assistant and refused work as a tourist guide, so he worked in a grocery; his brother then entered him, without telling him, for the radio's exam for Spanish-language announcers, and he says the day of his exam was the day the Iraqi reactor was bombed; later he was nominated as simultaneous interpreter in the office of the governor of Kafr el-Sheikh, but by then he had let his beard grow and wore a long shirt, and he did not go back to government work. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep05 00:29:30} He says he was conscripted in January 1980 into the infantry, then, with an officer's help, transferred to the Armed Forces Training Authority in Cairo, so that every afternoon he went to the library of Shaykh Hamid Ibrahim; he spent a year and four months there, his whole service, and calls it the "golden phase" of his studies. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep09 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep09 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep09 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep09 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep10 00:19:30} The first phase, before it, had been the lessons of [[person/muhammad-najib-al-mutii|Shaykh Muhammad Najib al-Muti'i]], while he was a university student. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep08 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep08 00:10:00}

He says he was imprisoned in the round-up that preceded Sadat's assassination and left Abu Za'bal prison with long hair, and that in prison he memorised the suras Al Imran, al-Ma'ida, al-A'raf and al-An'am in four days. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep06 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep06 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep15 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep15 00:18:30} Then the landlord of the house he shared with a friend grew afraid of his books, so he hid about ten cartons of them behind crates of aged cheese in the store-room of the grocery where he worked, and lived there four months reading little; what he loved to read then was al-Mu'allimi's «al-Tankil». {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep06 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep06 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep07 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep07 00:13:00} Then he found a forty-square-metre flat in Manshiyat Nasr, which he says was a palace to him, and there he began his first real scholarly work. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep07 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep07 00:25:00} He says he was later held in administrative detention after he had settled in Kafr el-Sheikh, and that he held study circles in hadith science in prison. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep15 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep15 00:15:00}

He bought the first edition of Ibn Adi's «al-Kamil» the day after his wedding, for a hundred pounds of the wedding gift money, and wrote in it: Monday 25/6/1405, corresponding to 18/3/1985. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep04 00:03:30} He began teaching [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]] at the mosque of Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri on 30 Safar 1406, corresponding to 13/11/1985. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:01:30} He settled in his home province of Kafr el-Sheikh in 1987, after years in Cairo divided between his job, editing and takhrij. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep14 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep14 00:19:00} The first sermon he remembers was in 1979, about a month after he graduated, in a neighbouring village called al-Raghama, where a man interrupted him wanting his lunch; he finished his sermon on the love of God and His Messenger. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep20 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep20 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep20 00:14:30} He says he gave about thirty-seven sermons on Surat Yusuf and spent seventy Fridays on Surat al-Qasas without finishing it, and that he began his preaching with the Prophet's stories, gathering fifty of them into the book «Sahih al-Qissa al-Nabawiyya». {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep17 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep21 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep20 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep20 00:22:00}

Among the teachers he heard in Cairo he names Shaykh Jamil Ghazi and Shaykh Abd al-Rashid Saqr. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep16 00:15:30} He recounts the mistakes of his early zeal in his village: praying the sunna before Maghrib against local habit, the sitting of rest, and moving his finger in the tashahhud until al-Albani corrected him in a gathering; he says he should have taught people before surprising them, and that he won the worshippers back by putting perfume in their hands before the prayer. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep16 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep16 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep16 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep16 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep16 00:26:00} He says his first wish was to be a pure hadith scholar shut away in the library with manuscripts and hadith books, that people's questions drove him to legal theory and then to fiqh, and he admits that before hadith science he was "blind". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep20 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep20 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep22 00:16:30} He names tawhid, following the Sunna (ittiba') and purification of the soul (tazkiya) as the scholarly foundations on which he built his preaching. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep22 00:20:00}

His home library is six rooms arranged by discipline and used by him alone, so he knows where every book stands and guides his children to one by counting steps; he writes in each book the day, place ## His works as he describes them in «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'»
The programme's introduction says his works exceed a hundred and forty; the interviewer relies on an index of them compiled by Abu Amr Ahmad al-Wakil and says about thirty-five are printed and the unfinished projects approach a hundred. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep01 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:21:30} Al-Heweny says about sixty ajza' among them grew out of his takhrij without being planned, and that he once counted the hadiths he had worked on and found nearly nine thousand. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:16:00}

His first published book was «Fasl al-Khitab bi-Naqd al-Mughni an al-Hifz wa-l-Kitab», a critique of the book of Umar ibn Badr al-Mawsili that he found as a supplement to the al-Azhar magazine; he says it was the book that made al-Albani know him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep07 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep07 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:10:30} He sold it, with his edition of al-Nasa'i's «Khasa'is Ali», to Muhammad Ali Baydun of Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya on Wednesday 16 Rajab 1404, 18 April 1984, for three hundred pounds, while he was still working in the grocery. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:19:30} «Fasl al-Khitab» came out wrongly attributed to Ibn Qudama because its title recalls [[work/al-mughni|«al-Mughni»]], and the «Khasa'is» was printed from the old text without his corrections to the chains, which he calls "my first scholarly fall". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:23:30} He has a longer version of the same book, «Jannat al-Murtab», and then «Ghanimat al-Iyab», in which he retracts his errors, among them his grading of the hadith on Zamzam water as hasan, which he now holds weak; he says he was not courteous to the imams in «Jannat al-Murtab» because early in life he was pleased with himself. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep25 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep25 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:11:30}

The dearest of his books to him is «Badhl al-Ihsan bi-Taqrib Sunan al-Nasa'i Abi Abd al-Rahman», a takhrij of [[work/sunan-al-nasai|Sunan al-Nasa'i]] that he began in Shaykh Hamid Ibrahim's library. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:11:00} Another is «Ghawth al-Makdud», which al-Albani praised; he says Dar al-Kitab al-Arabi printed it with many errors because it never sent him proofs. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep12 00:28:30} Another is «Tanbih al-Hajid ila ma Waqa'a min al-Nazar fi Kutub al-Amajid», on errors in scholars' books, corrected with minimal comment;
