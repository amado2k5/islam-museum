## Summary
Qamaruzzaman Azmi is an Indian-British scholar of the Barelvi school, born on 23 March 1946 in Azamgarh, Uttar Pradesh, and president of the World Islamic Mission, the organisation gathering Barelvi Sunni Muslims worldwide; he was listed in The Muslim 500 for successive years for his institution-building. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi} {cite:muslim500-preachers-2024-list}

## Life and upbringing
Raised in a scholarly Sufi family, he learnt the foundations of the Islamic sciences, Urdu and Persian from his father and grandfather, then entered the Anwarul Uloom madrasa, Al-Jamiatul-Ashrafia in Mubarakpur at twelve, and Nadwatul Ulama in Lucknow where he took the aalim degree, returning to Ashrafia for his graduation ceremony. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi}

## The Barelvi school
He belongs to the Barelvi movement, the Sufi Sunni Hanafi school founded in late nineteenth-century northern India by Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi; he is a successor in the Qadiri order of Mustafa Raza Khan Qadri and a standard-bearer of the "Ahl al-Sunna wa'l-Jama'a" line among Indian diasporas in the West. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi}

## Teaching and the Mission
In 1966 his teacher Abdul Aziz Muradabadi sent him to Faizabad as a religious instructor, where at eighteen he founded the Al-Jamiatul-Islamia university. In 1973 an agreement in Mecca founded the international Sufi organisation later named the World Islamic Mission, which he joined as joint secretary-general, moving to Bradford, Britain, in 1974; he later became president of the Manchester-headquartered body active across Europe, America, Africa and Asia. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi}

## Scholarship and preaching
He counts among the Barelvi school's preachers in the West, speaking and writing within Maturidi creed, Hanafi fiqh and the Qadiri tariqa, a disciple of Mustafa Raza Khan Qadri. He has patronised mosques and institutes in several countries and is credited with spreading the Barelvi Sunni discourse among South Asian diasporas in Europe. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi}

## Patronage and honours
He is patron-in-chief of the international Sunni Dawat-e-Islami movement and holds the Mufti-e-Azam gold medal per his documentation, remaining after decades in Bradford a recognised figure for the Pakistani and Indian community mosques of Britain, Europe and North America. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi}

## Legacy
His mark lies in the institution more than the book: a university founded in Faizabad as a teenager, a transcontinental organisation he came to lead, and half a century of preaching among migrants seeking a Sunni Sufi authority in the West; The Muslim 500 files him among Preachers & Spiritual Guides. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi} {cite:muslim500-preachers-2024-list}
## Nadwa and Ashrafia
Azmi is a graduate of two of the oldest Sunni madrasas of northern India: Al-Jamiatul-Ashrafia in Mubarakpur, Azamgarh, and Darul Uloom Nadwatul Ulama in Lucknow, which granted him the aalim degree — a scholar formed in the Indian scholastic tradition before he carried it to the West. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi}

## Five decades of institutions
His documentation credits him with more than fifty years spent founding organisations, mosques, colleges and universities across different countries — the service for which, per the source, he earned repeated listing in The Muslim 500 from 2011 onward. {cite:wikipedia-qamaruzzaman-azmi}
