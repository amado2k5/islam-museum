## Overview
The imam and hadith master Taqi al-Din Abu 'Amr 'Uthman b. 'Abd al-Rahman al-Shahrazuri, known as Ibn al-Salah — shaykh al-Islam and the leading hadith scholar of his age. Born in 577/1181 at the village of Sharakhan near Shahrazur, he died at Damascus in 643/1245. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 185} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 959}

He studied fiqh under his father Abu al-Qasim Salah al-Din at Shahrazur, then moved to Mosul where he repeated the whole of the 'Muhadhdhab' before his beard had grown and served as repeater under al-Imad Ibn Yunus. A little over twenty, he journeyed to Baghdad, Dubays and Hamadan, then to Khurasan where he acquired the science of hadith at Nishapur and Marw, and he entered al-Sham in 617 or earlier, hearing from Muwaffaq al-Din Ibn Qudama and others. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 185-186}

He taught at the Salahiyya madrasa in Jerusalem; when al-Malik al-Mu'azzam demolished its walls he moved to Damascus and taught at the Rawahiyya, took the mashyakha of the al-Ashrafiyya dar al-hadith in 630, then Sitt al-Sham's madrasa, discharging all three posts at once. Ibn Khallikan kept his company in 632 and collected his fatwas. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 186} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 229-230}

He dictated a session called *al-Ahadith al-Kulliyya* (The Comprehensive Hadiths), gathering the comprehensive hadiths on which, it is said, the religion turns — twenty-six hadiths which [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] took and built his *Forty* on. See [[work/ahadith-kulliyya-ibn-al-salah|al-Ahadith al-Kulliyya]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

His works include the [[work/muqaddimat-ibn-al-salah|Muqaddima]], the founding textbook of hadith terminology, [[work/adab-al-mufti-wal-mustafti|Adab al-Mufti wa al-Mustafti]], the [[work/tabaqat-al-fuqaha-al-shafiiyya|Tabaqat al-Fuqaha' al-Shafi'iyya]] which al-Nawawi abridged, and Siyanat Sahih Muslim; when 'the Shaykh' is named without qualification in hadith science, it is he who is meant, as al-'Iraqi noted in his Alfiyya. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 959}

## From his book on the hadith sciences
In the preface he names the book Ma'rifat Anwa' Ilm al-Hadith. He divides it into sixty-five types and says the types could be multiplied without limit. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 6} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 8}

The book gives his definition of the sound hadith: one whose chain is connected, through upright and precise transmitters on the authority of others like them, to its end, and which is neither anomalous nor defective. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 11} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 12}

It also states two views on which he was opposed:
- that in his age one could not affirm the soundness of a hadith that the imams had not declared sound;
- that what the two Shaykhs agree on is certainly sound. On this point he says he first inclined to the opposite view and then abandoned it.

See [[question/tashih-al-hadith-fi-al-usur-al-mutaakhkhira|the question of authentication]] and [[question/ifadat-ma-ittafaqa-alayhi-al-shaykhan-al-qat|the question of certainty]]. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 28}

His travels show in the chains at the end of the book:
- at Baghdad he read to Abu Hafs Umar ibn Muhammad ibn al-Mu'ammar;
- at Nishapur he read to Abu al-Hasan al-Mu'ayyad ibn Muhammad ibn Ali al-Muqri, once at the head of the grave of Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj;
- also at Nishapur, to Zaynab bint Abi al-Qasim al-Sha'ri and to Abu al-Fath Mansur ibn Abd al-Mun'im al-Furawi.
