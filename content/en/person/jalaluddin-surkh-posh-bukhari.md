Sayyid Jalaluddin Husayn al-Naqvi al-Bukhari, known as Surkh-Posh — "clad in red", for the red cloak he habitually wore — was a Sufi saint born at Bukhara in 595 AH, his family name taken from the city of his birth.{cite:wikipedia-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-en} Fleeing the Mongol invasion, he reached Multan in 1237, where he was initiated into the Suhrawardi order, appointed a deputy of Bahauddin Zakariya, and sent to preach at Bhakkar and then at Uch.{cite:khan-constructing-islam-on-the-indus-2016} The sources divide over his birth year in Christian reckoning: the family tradition's 595 AH is rendered 1199 by Wikidata, Khan gives 1198, and older reference works give 1192, while the dates carried in some lists (1244–1350) match no biography at all — 1244 is in fact the year of his settlement at Uch.{cite:wikidata-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari}{cite:khan-constructing-islam-on-the-indus-2016}

He settled at Uch in 1244 and made it a Suhrawardi centre for Sindh and the southern Punjab; he is counted among the pioneers of the order in the subcontinent and the founder of its Jalali branch, named after him.{cite:wikipedia-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-en} His life was spent travelling as a missionary, and tribes such as the Soomro, Samma, Chadhar and Sial are said to have accepted Islam through him.{cite:wikipedia-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-en} Uch remained the seat of his Bukhari sayyid descendants, and his tomb and mosque there have stood on UNESCO's tentative World Heritage list since 2004.{cite:wikipedia-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-en}

His youngest son, Ahmad Kabir, was his chosen successor, and through Ahmad Kabir his grandson Makhdum-i Jahaniyan Jahangasht became the most eminent Suhrawardi shaykh of the Uch line in the following century.{cite:wikipedia-jahaniyan-jahangasht-en} He died at Uch; the family biography tradition gives 19 Jumada al-awwal 690 AH, 20 May 1291, and Wikidata agrees on 1291.{cite:wikidata-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari}
