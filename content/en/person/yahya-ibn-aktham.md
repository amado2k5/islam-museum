## Overview
Yahya ibn Aktham, judge and boon-companion of [[person/abd-allah-al-mamun|al-Ma'mun]], and a transmitter from him in hadith. al-Suyuti reports that al-Ma'mun said to him that if they were to delve into medicine, Galen would be in their knowledge, and in jurisprudence Ali ibn Abi Talib would be in his learning {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.497,503,515}.

## Al-Mas'udi's account
al-Mas'udi says that he was from Marw, of the Banu Tamim. He studied jurisprudence in the manner of the Basrans. He wrote works, among them the book "al-Tanbih", and debates with Ahmad ibn Abi Da'ud. He was attached to al-Ma'mun and was his companion. al-Ma'mun became angry with him in Egypt in the year 215 AH and sent him back to Iraq. He died at al-Rabadha in the year 243 AH. al-Mas'udi also transmits reports that disparage his conduct {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.415-417} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.513}.

## Ibn Khaldun's defence
Ibn Khaldun defended him and described those reports as slander, and said that they rest on the weak reports of storytellers. He transmitted the praise of Isma'il al-Qadi for him, and the denial of Ibn Hanbal of what was said against him. Ibn Hibban in al-Thiqat said that most of what is told of him is not sound {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p.30-31}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] mentions Yahya ibn Aktham in the reflection on perspicacity, saying he belonged to a later generation, that of Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Ibn Ma'in. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:23:00} He relates that Sufyan ibn Uyayna once complained that he had sat with men who had seen the Companions and now had to sit with these people; a youth answered that the misfortune of those who had seen the Prophet in having Sufyan was greater than Sufyan's misfortune in having them. Sufyan fell silent, recited verses of Abu Nuwas on the merit of silence, and asked who the youth was; told it was Yahya ibn Aktham, he said he was fit to sit with those, meaning rulers. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:26:00}

He relates that al-Ma'mun loved him and saw signs of intelligence in him, and appointed him judge of Basra at seventeen. The people of Basra, among whom were men older and more learned, did not like it, and one of them asked his age to belittle him; he answered: my age is the age of Attab ibn Asid when the Prophet appointed him over Mecca. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:27:00}
