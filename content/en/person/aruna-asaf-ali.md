# Aruna Asaf Ali

Aruna Asaf Ali, born Aruna Ganguly (16 July 1909 – 29 July 1996), was an Indian educator, political activist and publisher, widely remembered for hoisting the Indian national flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay during the Quit India movement in 1942 {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. After independence she remained active in politics and became Delhi's first mayor {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}.

## Early life

She was born on 16 July 1909 in Kalka, in Punjab, into a Bengali Brahmin family {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. Her father, Upendranath Ganguly, was a restaurant owner from Barisal in eastern Bengal; her mother, Ambalika Devi, was the daughter of Trailokyanath Sanyal, a renowned Brahmo leader who wrote many Brahmo hymns {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. She was educated at Sacred Heart Convent in Lahore and at All Saints' College in Nainital, and afterwards taught at the Gokhale Memorial School in Calcutta {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}.

She met Asaf Ali, a Congress leader, in Allahabad, and they married in 1928 despite parental opposition on grounds of religion and age — he was a Muslim and her senior by more than twenty years {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. Her guardian uncle declared her dead to him and performed her shraddh {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}.

## The independence movement

She joined the Indian National Congress after her marriage and marched in the Salt Satyagraha processions {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. Arrested in 1931 on a vagrancy charge, she was not released under the Gandhi–Irwin Pact until a public agitation, and her co-prisoners' refusal to leave without her, forced the issue {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. In 1932 she hunger-struck in Tihar Jail against the treatment of political prisoners {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}.

On 9 August 1942, after the government arrested the Congress leadership, she presided over the remainder of the Quit India session and hoisted the Congress flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan as police fired on the assembly {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. Dubbed the heroine of the 1942 movement, she went underground with a price of 5,000 rupees on her capture, edited the Congress monthly Inquilab with Ram Manohar Lohia, and emerged only when the warrant was withdrawn in 1946 {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. Gandhi criticised her support of the Royal Indian Navy mutiny, which she saw as a unifying factor of Hindus and Muslims at the peak of the Pakistan movement {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}.

## After independence

She moved from the Congress Socialist Party to the Socialist Party in 1948, and in the early 1950s joined the Communist Party of India, leaving it in 1956 {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. In 1954 she helped form the National Federation of Indian Women {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. In 1958 she was elected the first mayor of Delhi {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}. With Edatata Narayanan she founded the Link publishing house, which published the daily Patriot and the weekly Link {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}.

She received the International Lenin Peace Prize in 1964, the Jawaharlal Nehru Award in 1991 and the Padma Vibhushan in 1992, and the Bharat Ratna posthumously in 1997 {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}.

She died in New Delhi on 29 July 1996, aged 87 {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}{cite:wikidata-aruna-asaf-ali}.

## Faith, as the sources attest it

Her own articles give her family's background — a Bengali Brahmin family, a grandfather who led the Brahmo Samaj — and file her by category among the Brahmos, but nowhere state her own religion, and record no conversion {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-en}{cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-ur}. The Arabic and Urdu editions leave the infobox religion field empty {cite:wikipedia-aruna-asaf-ali-ar}, and Wikidata records no religion for her {cite:wikidata-aruna-asaf-ali}. One passage does name her religion: the English article on her husband says of their marriage that "Asaf Ali was a Muslim while Aruna was a Hindu" {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}, a sentence the Urdu article on him repeats in translation {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-ur}. Under the museum's two-source rule, one attestation and its translation are a single source family, and family background and category filings are not personal attestations; her faith is therefore left unknown in the data record, with this evidence preserved and flagged for a founder ruling.
