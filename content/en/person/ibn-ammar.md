## Overview
Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Ammar, the poet and vizier of Seville, given the title "Dhu al-Wizaratayn" (the holder of two vizierates). He was the vizier and boon companion of [[person/al-mutamid-ibn-abbad|al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad]]. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 73}

Al-Maqqari relays a story that al-Mu'tamid rode out on the river with Ibn Ammar, and the wind had stirred the water. He said to him: "Complete the verse: the wind made of the water a coat of mail." Ibn Ammar thought at length, and a washerwoman said: "What a coat of mail for battle, if it froze!" Al-Mu'tamid married her, and she was al-Rumaykiyya, the mother of his children. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 211-212}

## His death
Al-Maqqari says that Ibn Ammar composed his *lamiyya* ode on al-Mu'tamid and al-Rumaykiyya, and that it stirred al-Mu'tamid against him until he killed him with a mace. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 211-212}

Al-Maqqari relays from al-Hajjari that his lavish praise of those who made excuses for themselves, and his generosity to them, show that he was devoted to the cup. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 73, 211-212}
