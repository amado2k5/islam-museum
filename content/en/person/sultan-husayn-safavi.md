## Overview
Sultan Husayn was the last of the Safavid shahs to rule from Isfahan. Born in 1079/1668 as the eldest son of Shah Sulayman, he succeeded his father in 1105/1694 at the age of twenty-six. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 pp. 635-636} Isfahan fell to the Afghans in his reign, in 1135/1722. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 826} The museum's data file gives his death in 1726.

## Character and court
He grew up in the harem, in the practice begun under Shah Abbas I, and came to the throne ignorant of affairs of state and of the world generally; by nature he was quiet and studious and in his early years inclined to austerity. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635} The article's author calls him weak in character: the court eunuchs took control of government and fierce rivalry grew between them and the mujtahids and mullas. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 635} The article on the Safavids records the derisive nickname "Mulla Husayn", and says the political weight of the Twelver religious scholars rose in his reign, with growing persecution of Sufis. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 826}

## Revolts and the fall of Isfahan
Trouble began in Baluchistan and was put down by Giorgi XI, king of Kartli; then the Ghalzay chief Mir Ways rose at Kandahar and stayed virtually independent until his death in 1126/1715. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 636} His successor Mahmud entered Persia, defeated the royal forces near Gulnabad and besieged the capital; after many thousands of its people had died of disease and starvation it capitulated in October 1722. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 636} Mahmud received the insignia of royalty from Shah Sultan Husayn and entered the city.

## Captivity and death
After his deposition Sultan Husayn was kept in confinement at Isfahan. In February 1725 Mahmud, in a fit of insanity, killed most of the Safavid princes and wounded the ex-shah as he tried to ward off a blow aimed at one of them. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 636} Ashraf, son of Abd al-Aziz, then took the throne, and in the late autumn of 1726, after a rude message from the Ottoman commander Ahmad Pasha that he would restore Sultan Husayn, he sent emissaries to Isfahan who put the ex-shah to death. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 636}
