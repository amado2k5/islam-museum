Tewhida Ben Sheikh (1909–2010) was the first modern Tunisian and North African woman to become a physician, and a pioneer of women's medicine, in particular contraception and abortion access.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-en} She was born on 2 January 1909 — the French biography's lead and the English narrative give Tunis, while the English infobox and Wikidata give Ras Jebel, her family's home town — into an elite, socially conservative family; widowed early, her mother raised five children alone and was persuaded by her daughter's teachers and by Dr Etienne Burnet of the Pasteur Institute of Tunis to let her study in France.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-fr}{cite:wikidata-tawhida-ben-cheikh}{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-en} Educated at the first public school for Muslim girls in Tunis, she became in 1928 the first Tunisian Muslim girl to take the baccalauréat, then earned her medical degree at Paris in 1936 and defended her doctorate the following year, the first Tunisian woman physician and a pioneer in paediatrics.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-fr}{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-ar}

On her return to Tunis she opened a private practice near Bab Menara, local doctors honouring her with a dinner, and went on to head the maternity services of the Charles-Nicolle hospital (1955–1964) and then of the Aziza Othmana hospital until her retirement in 1977.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-en}{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-ar} She created the family-planning service at Charles-Nicolle in 1963, opened a clinic in 1968, and in 1970 directed the national family and population office, fighting for the right to abortion, legalised in 1973.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-fr}{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-ar} Active in the national movement, she was a member of the Muslim Union of Tunisian Women founded by Bchira Ben Mrad, vice-president of the Tunisian Red Crescent, editor in 1937 of the women's magazine Leïla, and founder of the Society for Social Aid, a home for orphans, a women's home and the Qammata society for the care of poor mothers and infants.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-fr}{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-en}{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-ar}

She married a dentist in 1943 and had two sons and a daughter, the historian and archaeologist Zeineb Benzina among them; she was a niece of the nationalist leader Tahar Ben Ammar.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-fr} She died at Tunis on 6 December 2010, aged 101.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-ar}{cite:wikidata-tawhida-ben-cheikh} A health centre at Montreuil near Paris was named after her in 2011, Tunisian Post issued a stamp with her likeness in 2012, and in March 2020 the Central Bank of Tunisia put her portrait on the new ten-dinar banknote.{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-en}{cite:wikipedia-tawhida-ben-cheikh-fr}
