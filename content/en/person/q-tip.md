## Summary
Q-Tip, born Jonathan William Davis in Manhattan, New York City, on 10 April 1970, is an American rapper and record producer, nicknamed "the Abstract", the lead MC and main producer of A Tribe Called Quest. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en} He converted to Islam in the mid-1990s, changing his name to Kamaal Ibn John Fareed. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en} In 2024 he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of A Tribe Called Quest. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en}

## Career
He began his career in the late 1980s as MC and main producer of A Tribe Called Quest, the influential alternative hip-hop group whose jazz-influenced sound he shaped. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en} In the mid-1990s he co-founded the production team The Ummah, and after the group first disbanded he released his gold-certified solo debut Amplified (1999), followed by the Grammy-nominated The Renaissance (2008) and the experimental Kamaal the Abstract (2009). {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en} He won a Grammy Award as a featured artist on the Chemical Brothers' "Galvanize" (2006), acted in films including Poetic Justice (1993) and Prison Song (2001), which he co-wrote and led, and has hosted the Apple Music radio show Abstract Radio since 2015. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en} In 2016 he was named artistic director for hip-hop culture at the Kennedy Center, and in 2018 he became an instructor of a jazz and hip-hop course at New York University's Clive Davis Institute. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en}

## Works
His solo albums are Amplified (1999), The Renaissance (2008) — his highest-charting album, at number 11 on the Billboard 200, and nominated for the Grammy Award for Best Rap Album — and Kamaal the Abstract (2009), recorded with a live band and shelved by his label for years before release. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en} With A Tribe Called Quest he released the group's albums from 1990 to their final album in 2016. {cite:wikipedia-q-tip-en}
