Abu'l-Fadl Muhammad ibn Husayn Bayhaqi was born about 995 at Harethabad, a village in the Bayhaq district of Khurasan, into a Persian family, and was educated at Nishapur. In 1020/1 he joined the Ghaznavid chancery, the divan al-risala, as a pupil and assistant of its chief secretary, Abu Nasr Mushkan, under whom he served for nineteen years, until Mushkan's death in 1039/40 {cite:wikipedia-abul-fadl-bayhaqi-en}.

## Rise, fall and imprisonment

After Mushkan's death Bayhaqi endured the harsh management of the next chief, Abu Sahl Zawzani, and under Sultan Abd al-Rashid (r. 1049-1052) himself became chief secretary. On that sultan's fall he was dismissed and imprisoned - by one account jailed by the qadi of Ghazni over an unpaid obligatory payment to a wife, by another the victim of his enemies' plots - and his possessions were seized. He was released after the usurper Toghrul was killed in 1052, retired to Ghazni, and (it is reported) was recalled once, in 1058, to draft a peace treaty between the Ghaznavids and the Seljuqs {cite:wikipedia-abul-fadl-bayhaqi-en}.

## The Tarikh-i Bayhaqi

In retirement he spent twenty-two years writing his great history, the Tarikh-i Bayhaqi, also known as the Tarikh-i Mas'udi. It originally ran to thirty volumes, of which only about five and a half survive; the surviving part covers chiefly the reign of Mas'ud I (1030-1041), which Bayhaqi had lived through at court. The work is prized for its unusual frankness about sultans and viziers, its precision, and its elegant Persian prose, and is the fullest narrative source for the Ghaznavid court of its time {cite:wikipedia-abul-fadl-bayhaqi-en}{cite:wikipedia-abul-fadl-bayhaqi-fa}.

## Death, and a namesake to avoid

Bayhaqi died at Ghazni on 21 September 1077, and his tomb is at Harethabad {cite:wikipedia-abul-fadl-bayhaqi-en}{cite:wikidata-abul-fadl-bayhaqi}. He is not Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn al-Husayn al-Bayhaqi (994-1066), the famous hadith scholar who has his own record in this museum: the two men share only the Bayhaq nisba and near-identical lifetimes. A third Bayhaqi, Abu'l-Hasan Ali ibn Zayd (Ibn Funduq), later wrote a history of Bayhaq and is one of this man's biographers.
