## Summary
Mohamed Ramadan is an Egyptian actor and singer and arguably the biggest working pop-culture star in the Arab world: a performer who moves between television dramas that dominate Ramadan-season ratings and singles that accumulate hundreds of millions of views. The Muslim 500 lists him in Arts & Culture; Wikipedia records his position as one of the most prominent figures in MENA entertainment. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-ramadan} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-ramadan}

## Rise
Born in 1988 in Giza and raised partly in Qena in Upper Egypt — the origin of his nickname "Eagle of Upper Egypt" — Ramadan started in small film and television roles in the 2000s before breaking through in series and films in the early 2010s. By mid-decade he was carrying prime-time television: the Ramadan-season serials that gather the Arab world's largest audiences — a run of top-rated serials and films that made him, by audience share, the leading star of Egyptian television. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-ramadan} {cite:muslim500-mohamed-ramadan}

## The singer
Parallel to acting he built a music career that became the louder half of his fame: singles like Bum Bum, Ya Habibi, Mafia and Babylon — the last filmed at Versailles — drew hundreds of millions of streams and took Arabic-language pop into collaborations with international artists, including the track Habibi with Sia. His persona — extravagant, self-confident, self-named "Number One" — is itself the product: a star brand built on spectacle. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-ramadan} {cite:muslim500-mohamed-ramadan}

## Controversy and reach
His career has also attracted controversy — a widely publicised photograph with Israeli celebrities in Dubai in 2020 drew sharp criticism, and his flamboyant style divides opinion — but his numbers are unmatched in Egyptian entertainment. The Muslim 500 cites him as one of the most prominent pop-culture figures in MENA; the museum reports the criticism and the stardom attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-ramadan} {cite:muslim500-mohamed-ramadan}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Ramadan represents the new Arab superstar economy: an actor-singer whose fame is measured in billions of streams and whose brand — brash, self-made, unashamed — speaks to a generation for whom he is Egyptian entertainment's loudest export. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-ramadan} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-ramadan}

## The man behind the brand
Ramadan's public persona is a deliberate construction: the staged wealth, the "Number One" slogan, the confrontational social media are a brand engineered for attention in a crowded Egyptian entertainment market. The brand has a cost — he has been fined, criticised by the actors' syndicate, and debated as a symbol of celebrity excess — but it is also a genuine performance skill: the audience that buys the persona buys the voice and the presence that built it. The Muslim 500 lists him for that reach; the museum records both the reach and the debates around the construction. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-ramadan} {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-ramadan}

## The celebrity as brand
Ramadan's record is the museum's example of the celebrity who treats himself as a product: the Egyptian actor who understood that in a crowded entertainment market the only thing that mattered was being talked about, and who built a persona — the staged wealth, the slogan, the confrontational media — that guaranteed he would be. The construction is the story; the market is the context; and the result — a Muslim star whose name is a brand before it is a person — is the profile his Muslim 500 entry records. The museum notes the mechanism and leaves the debates to those who have them. {cite:muslim500-mohamed-ramadan}
