## Summary
Dr Hashima Hasan is an Indian-American theoretical nuclear physicist who became a senior programme scientist at NASA headquarters, where she has managed flagship astrophysics missions including work associated with the James Webb Space Telescope and the NuSTAR and IXPE X-ray observatories. The Muslim 500 places her in Science & Technology as a key programme scientist behind the James Webb Space Telescope. {cite:muslim500-hashima-hasan} {cite:wikipedia-hashima-hasan}

## Formation in India
Hasan was born in Lucknow, India, and educated in physics at the University of Lucknow before taking her doctorate in theoretical nuclear physics at the University of Oxford. She returned to India to research and teach — including work with the Physical Research Laboratory and the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research — and spent years in India's space and nuclear-science establishment, including a period at the Space Applications Centre and as a senior scientist at the Nuclear Science Centre in New Delhi. {cite:wikipedia-hashima-hasan}

## To NASA
She moved to the United States in the mid-1990s, first to research positions and then to NASA headquarters in Washington, where she became a programme scientist in astrophysics — the NASA role that shepherds a space mission from design through launch to operations. Over two decades she has been associated with some of the agency's most visible instruments: the Nuclear Spectroscopic Telescope Array ⁦(NuSTAR)⁩, the Imaging X-ray Polarimetry Explorer ⁦(IXPE)⁩, the NASA/W. M. Keck Observatory partnership, and the James Webb Space Telescope, alongside leading astrophysics education and public outreach. {cite:wikipedia-hashima-hasan} {cite:muslim500-hashima-hasan}

## The programme scientist's craft
A programme scientist does not fly the telescope; she keeps the science case alive through budgets, reviews and crises. The Muslim 500's description of Hasan as a "key program scientist" behind the James Webb Space Telescope points to exactly this invisible labour: holding the line for a decades-long, multi-billion-dollar observatory through its delays and cost overruns until it opened its mirrors in 2022. {cite:muslim500-hashima-hasan} {cite:wikipedia-hashima-hasan}

## Recognition
Her service has been recognised within NASA through performance and achievement awards, and she is a frequent public speaker on astrophysics and on women in science — a visible Muslim woman in a field where both are rare. {cite:wikipedia-hashima-hasan} {cite:muslim500-hashima-hasan}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Hasan is the scientist-administrator at the summit of space science: a physicist from Lucknow who helps run the instruments through which humanity now photographs the early universe — an Indian Muslim woman carrying one of NASA's largest scientific portfolios. {cite:muslim500-hashima-hasan} {cite:wikipedia-hashima-hasan}

## From Lucknow to orbit
The distance Hasan travelled is also the distance India's space programme travelled: from a post-colonial institute in Lucknow studying molecules between the stars, to managing missions that put Indian telescopes and satellites into orbit. AstroSat, launched in 2015 as India's first dedicated space observatory, is the emblem of that arc — and Hasan's name is attached to the managerial layer that made it possible: budgets, reviews, international agreements. In the museum's record she represents the administrator-scientist, the figure whose instruments are committees and contracts but whose outputs fly. {cite:wikipedia-hashima-hasan} {cite:muslim500-hashima-hasan}

## The administrator's instrument
Hasan's record is the museum's reminder that big science runs on administration as much as on brilliance: the instruments that fly, the budgets that get approved, the collaborations that get signed — these are the products of a managerial layer whose work is invisible in the final result. Her career is the profile of that layer: the scientist who understood that the mission's success was as much her responsibility as the physicist's, and who took the administrative job that made it possible. {cite:muslim500-hashima-hasan}
