## Overview
Abdullah Bahauddin ibn Mahmud Shihab al-Din ibn Abdullah al-Alusi was a Baghdadi jurist and one of the Shafi'i judges. Al-Zirikli lists him in al-A'lam under the heading al-Alusi and gives his years as 1248 to 1291 AH, 1832 to 1874 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 136}.

## Life
He graduated under his father, rose above state office and devoted himself to teaching {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 136}. He then fell ill and turned to Sufism, sold his books and property and set out for Istanbul; highwaymen stopped him and he returned to his city empty-handed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 136}. He was forced into government work and was appointed judge of Basra for two years; fever wasted his body, he went back to Baghdad and died there {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 136}.

## Works
He wrote books as occasions arose. Al-Zirikli names al-Mitnan on the sciences of logic and rhetoric; al-Wadih on grammar; al-Ta'attuf ala al-Ta'arruf on the two foundations and Sufism, in manuscript in the hand of his son Mahmud Shukri al-Alusi in the public endowments library in Baghdad; and Tarsilana, in manuscript, in a small volume of what his son Mahmud Shukri collected {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 136}. A book in praise of [[person/abdul-ghani-al-jamil|Abd al-Ghani al-Jamil]], titled al-Rawd al-Khamil, is attributed to him in al-Zirikli's entry on Abd al-Ghani ibn Jamil {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 33}.
