Osman Nuri Hadžić (28 June 1869 – 23 December 1937) was a Bosnian writer and intellectual from Mostar, educated in his home town's mekteb, ruždija and madrasa, then at the Sarajevo School of Sharia Judges, with law studies in Vienna and a degree from Zagreb.{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-en} His earliest stories, written with his friend Ivan Miličević under the joint pen name Aziz Osman, appeared in literary journals while he was still young; with Miličević he also wrote the novel Bez svrhe (Without Purpose, 1897), a critique of madrasa education.{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-en} He served at the Sarajevo district court and in the Provincial Government, and became professor and then director of the School of Sharia Judges.{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-en}

While still a student he had published Islam i kultura (Islam and Culture, 1894), a reply to an anti-Muslim pamphlet arguing that Muslims had contributed to human culture and learning across the centuries and that education was a religious duty for Muslims of both sexes; he expanded the argument late in life into Muhammed i Koran (Muhammed and the Quran, 1931), regarded as one of the most comprehensive Bosnian-language works on the culture and history of Islam.{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-en} On 1 May 1900 he co-launched the journal Behar with Safvet-beg Bašagić and Edhem Mulabdić, resigning six years later, and he returned to print in 1925 with a Gajret series on the woman question in Islam.{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-en}{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-bs} He died at Belgrade on 23 December 1937, a member of the State Council of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia; one section of the English article gives the day as 22 December.{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-bs}{cite:wikidata-osman-nuri-hadzic}{cite:wikipedia-osman-nuri-hadzic-en}
