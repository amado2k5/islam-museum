## Overview
Ali ibn Rasul, the Turkman founder of the [[polity/rasulid-dynasty|Rasulid state]] in Yemen, according to Ibn Khaldun. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## His career
He was chamberlain of the court of al-Mas'ud Yusuf ibn al-Kamil, and he was appointed by him as his deputy over Yemen when he went on pilgrimage ill. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

He deposed Musa al-Ashraf ibn al-Mas'ud and seized the rule of Yemen. He adopted the call of al-Kamil in Egypt, and sent his two brothers as hostages for his obedience. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## His death
He died in 629 AH (about 1232 CE; the conversion is derived), and his son al-Mansur Umar succeeded him. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## Notes for researchers
- The information is from Ibn Khaldun alone; it needs an additional source.
