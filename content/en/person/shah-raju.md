## Summary
Mark Zebrowski writes that before [[person/abul-hasan-qutb-shah|Abul Hasan]] became sultan of Golconda in 1672 he lived at Gulbarga, site of the shrine of the fifteenth-century Sufi Gesudaraz, as a follower of the famous saint Shah Raju, Gesudaraz's direct descendant; he spent fourteen years of his youth as Shah Raju's pupil at the shrine. Shah Raju went with his pupil to Hyderabad and had great influence at court. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 186} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 188}

## Influence
Shah Raju wrote in Urdu and popularised the composing of marthiyas, dirges on the martyrdom of [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|Hasan]] and [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|Husain]], sons of Ali. He and his son [[person/akbar-shah-husaini|Akbar Shah Husaini]] are still, the author notes, greatly venerated by the Muslims of the Deccan. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 186}

## Portraits
Two portraits of him by royal artists survive: one signed by Rahim Khan and an equestrian portrait signed by Rasul Khan, which for the author reflect Bijapuri taste within the [[concept/golconda-painting|Golconda]] workshop under Abul Hasan. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 13} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 186}
