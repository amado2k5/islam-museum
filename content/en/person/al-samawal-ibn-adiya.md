## Overview
Al-Samawal ibn Adiya is a proverb among the people of the Jahiliyya for keeping faith. Al-Mubarakfuri's "Al-Rahiq al-Makhtum" says that covenant was for them a debt they held fast to, and for it they made light of killing their children and ruining their homes. The story of [[person/hani-ibn-masud|Hani ibn Mas'ud al-Shaybani]], al-Samawal ibn Adiya and [[person/hajib-ibn-zurara|Hajib ibn Zurara al-Tamimi]] is enough to know this. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.47} See [[concept/jahili-virtues|the virtues of the people of the Jahiliyya]].

## What is not set out here
Al-Rahiq mentions only briefly the story of al-Samawal and the armour of Imru' al-Qays and the killing of his son before his eyes. His accounts in al-Mas'udi are in the data of this entry.

## For young visitors
Al-Samawal is a man proverbially praised for keeping faith. The story says he preferred that his son be killed rather than betray a trust he held.
