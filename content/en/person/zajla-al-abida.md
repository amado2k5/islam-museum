## Summary
Zajla al-Abida was a client (mawla) of [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]]; Ibn al-Jawzi places her in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]] among the women ascetics of the Basra section (no. 604), but her reports show that she lived in Syria: she went out to the coast to wash the clothes of the frontier garrisons, and [[person/said-ibn-abd-al-aziz-al-tanukhi|Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz]] said, "There is none in Syria or Iraq better than Zajla." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.715}

## Days for hastening
From Ahmad ibn Sahl al-Azdi: a group of Qur'an readers visited her and urged her to be gentle with herself; she said, "What have I to do with gentleness to it? These are days for hastening: whoever misses something today will not catch it tomorrow." She swore she would pray to God as long as her limbs carried her, fast for Him all her days and weep for Him as long as her eyes held water, then asked, "Which of you gives his servant an order and is pleased for him to fall short in it?" {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.715}

## The visit of al-Khawwas
From Abbad ibn Abbad Abu Utba al-Khawwas: they visited her when she had fasted until her skin darkened, wept until her eyes weakened and prayed until she could only pray seated; they spoke of God's pardon to lighten things for her, but she gasped and said that her knowledge of herself had wounded her heart, and that she wished God had not created her; then she turned back to her prayer. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.715} From Kulayb ibn Isa: she never raised her eyes to the sky. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.715}

## For young visitors
Zajla was a woman of Syria who prayed and fasted a great deal, and went down to the seashore to wash the clothes of the soldiers guarding the frontier.
