## Summary
Fahmy Howeidy is among the most influential newspaper columnists in the modern Arab world: for decades his columns — first in Al-Ahram, then in Al-Shorouk and pan-Arab papers — have argued public questions from an explicitly Islamic frame of reference. {cite:muslim500-fahmy-howeidy} {cite:wikipedia-fahmy-howeidy}

## Formation
Born in 1937, he joined Al-Ahram young and worked under the editorship of Mohamed Hassanein Heikal, eventually rising to deputy editor-in-chief of the paper that was then the Arab world's flagship daily. {cite:wikipedia-fahmy-howeidy}

## Career
His books and columns cover Islamist movements, Arab politics and — distinctively — Muslim minority communities from China to Europe, based on decades of field visits and reportage. After leaving Al-Ahram he continued as a syndicated columnist, including at Al-Shorouk in Cairo and papers in the Gulf. {cite:muslim500-fahmy-howeidy} {cite:wikipedia-fahmy-howeidy}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him under Media as the dean of Islamically framed Arab commentary. His positions — sympathetic to Islamist currents, critical of governments — have made him admired in Islamist circles and criticised by secular and official voices; the museum records the positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-fahmy-howeidy}
