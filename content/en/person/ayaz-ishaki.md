# Ayaz İshaki

Muhammed Ayaz İshaki (Ğayaz İsxaqi; 22 February 1878 – 22 July 1954) was a leading figure of the Tatar national movement: author, journalist, publisher and politician, whose standing in Tatar literature has been compared to Pushkin's and Tolstoy's among Russians. He was born at the village of Yaushirma near Kazan, to Mishar Tatar parents, was taught at home by his father and then in a madrasa, and completed the Russian-Tatar teachers' school in 1898–1902.{cite:wikipedia-ayaz-ishaki-en}

In Kazan from 1904 he moved among the socialists, was arrested in 1907 and imprisoned near Arkhangelsk, and after the February Revolution threw himself into the campaign for Tatar cultural autonomy: he was secretary of state of the short-lived Idel-Ural State of 1918. Harried by the Soviet authorities, he emigrated in 1920, published the journal Milli Yul ("The Way of the Nation") in Berlin from 1928, and travelled through Poland, Germany, Japan, China and Finland organising the Tatar diaspora before settling in Turkey in 1939.{cite:wikipedia-ayaz-ishaki-en}{cite:wikipedia-ayaz-ishaki-ru}

His best-known works are the dystopian story "Extinction in 200 Years" (1904) and the play "Zuleiha" (1917), on the forced baptism of his people. His daughter was the Turkologist Saadet Çağatay. İshaki died at Ankara on 22 July 1954 and was buried in the Edirnekapı graveyard in Istanbul.{cite:wikipedia-ayaz-ishaki-en}{cite:wikipedia-ayaz-ishaki-ru}

The English and Russian articles agree on his career; Wikidata fixes his birth in the Julian form, 10 February 1878, and his death at Ankara. Wikidata gives his religion as Islam in general, but no text names a school for him personally — his father's home teaching, his madrasa schooling and his Jadid alignment are instruction, education and movement, not attestation — so his faith is recorded as unknown.
