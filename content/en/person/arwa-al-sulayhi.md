## Summary
Arwa bint Ahmad ibn Ja'far al-Sulayhiyya was a resolute and capable Yemeni queen, styled al-Hurra al-Kamila ("the perfect free lady") and "the younger Bilqis", and the last of the Sulayhid rulers, as [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] presents her after al-Zirikli's *al-A'lam* and an article by Ali Ibrahim Abduh. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31}

## Upbringing and marriage
She was born in Haraz in Yemen and grew up in the care of [[person/asma-bint-shihab-al-sulayhiyya|Asma' bint Shihab al-Sulayhiyya]], whose son al-Mukarram Ahmad ibn Ali al-Sulayhi married her; when he was struck by paralysis he entrusted affairs to her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31}

## Her rule
She took a fortress at Dhu Jibla where she stayed some months each year, and managed the kingdom and its wars until al-Mukarram died in 484 AH and was succeeded by his cousin Saba' ibn Ahmad; she remained in power — petitions were brought to her, the ministers met in her presence, and she ruled from behind a screen. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31} The Friday prayer in Yemen named her: first [[person/al-mustansir-fatimid|the Fatimid al-Mustansir]], then the Sulayhid, then al-Hurra. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31} When Saba' died in 492 AH and Sulayhid power weakened, she fortified herself in Dhu Jibla, took control of the districts and forts around it, appointed ministers and officials, and her reign lasted forty years more. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31}

## Death and legacy
She died at Dhu Jibla and was buried in its mosque, leaving many works, public fountains and endowments. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31}

## For young visitors
Arwa was a queen who ruled Yemen for many years from her fortress in the town of Dhu Jibla. Ministers met at her court, and she built mosques and set up many endowments.
