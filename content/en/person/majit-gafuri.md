# Majit Gafuri

Majit Gafuri (Ğəbdilməjit Nurğəniy olı Ğafurov; 20 July 1880 – 28 October 1934) was a Bashkir and Tatar poet, writer and playwright, one of the leaders of the democratic trend in Tatar literature and one of the founders of national children's literature. He was born in the village of Zilim-Karanovo in Ufa Governorate, fifth child of a Tatar-speaking teacher (khalfa); the Russian article's genealogy of his family also names mullah ancestors.{cite:wikipedia-majit-gafuri-en}{cite:wikipedia-majit-gafuri-ru}

Orphaned in 1893, he studied at a neighbouring village madrasa and from 1898 at the Räsüliä madrasa in Troitsk, working in his spare time in gold mines and teaching Kazakh children on the steppe; in 1905 he studied at the Möxämmädiä madrasa in Kazan and in 1906 at the Ğäliä madrasa in Ufa, where he stayed after finishing in 1908. His first poem appeared in 1902 and his first book, "The Siberian Railway, or the State of the Nation", in 1904 at Orenburg.{cite:wikipedia-majit-gafuri-en}{cite:wikipedia-majit-gafuri-ru}

After the death of Gabdulla Tukay in 1913, Gafuri took up the mantle of the leading democratic voice in Tatar literature. He wrote poetry and plays celebrating workers and the struggle against tsardom during the civil war, helped organise Bashkortostan's local newspapers, and published the semi-autobiographical "Black-Faced" (1927) and "In the Poet's Gold Mines" (1931), as well as fables for children from Kazakh folklore. He was named People's Poet of the Bashkir ASSR in 1923. He died at Ufa on 28 October 1934 and was buried there.{cite:wikipedia-majit-gafuri-en}{cite:wikipedia-majit-gafuri-ru}
