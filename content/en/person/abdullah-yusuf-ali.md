Abdullah Yusuf Ali (14 April 1872 – 10 December 1953) was an Indian-British scholar best known for his English translation of the Qur'an. Born in Bombay into a Dawoodi Bohra family, he was educated at the Anjuman Himayat-ul-Islam school and Wilson College in Bombay, memorised the Qur'an as a boy, and took a first-class degree from the University of Bombay before reading law at St John's College, Cambridge; he entered the Indian Civil Service and was called to the Bar at Lincoln's Inn.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-yusuf-ali-en}

He received the CBE in 1917 for his support of the British war effort.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-yusuf-ali-en} Recruited by Muhammad Iqbal, he served twice as principal of Islamia College, Lahore, from 1925 to 1927 and again from 1935 to 1937.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-yusuf-ali-en} His 'The Holy Qur'an: Text, Translation and Commentary' (1934–1938) remains one of the two most widely used English versions of the Qur'an.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-yusuf-ali-en} The biographical tradition records that during his Lahore years he adopted Sunni Islam of the Hanafi school; recent scholarship, reading his commentary as Ismaili in inspiration, disputes that he ever left his ancestral Bohra doctrine, and the question is recorded here as unsettled.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-yusuf-ali-en}{cite:ijlts-alhitari-yusuf-ali-2022} He died in London on 10 December 1953 and was buried in the Muslim section of Brookwood Cemetery.{cite:wikipedia-abdullah-yusuf-ali-en}{cite:wikidata-abdullah-yusuf-ali}
