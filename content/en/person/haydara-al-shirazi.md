## Summary
Burhan al-Din Haydara al-Shirazi, later of Rum — a master of semantics, rhetoric and Arabic — died in 820 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 153}.

## Details
Ibn al-'Imad cites al-Suyuti: Burhan al-Din Haydara al-Shirazi, later known as "al-Rumi", was a polymath in ma'ani, bayan and Arabic; he studied under Sa'd al-Din al-Taftazani and wrote a commentary on al-Qazwini's al-Idah described as "interwoven" — merging the base text with glosses and clarifications {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 153}. He moved to the lands of Rum and settled there, hence the nisba; among his pupils was the author's own teacher, the 'allama Muhyi al-Din al-Kafiji, famed for works in Arabic, fiqh and tafsir {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 153}. Haydara thus formed a link between the Khwarazmian-Transoxanian school of rhetoric and the scholars of ninth-century Rum, transmitting that tradition westward where it benefited its people {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 153}.
