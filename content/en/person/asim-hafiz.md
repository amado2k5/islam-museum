## Summary
Asim Hafiz OBE was appointed in 2005 as the first Muslim chaplain to the British armed forces, and went on to serve as Islamic Religious Adviser to the Chief of the Defence Staff and the service chiefs — the senior religious voice inside the UK military on Muslim affairs. {cite:muslim500-asim-hafiz} {cite:wikipedia-asim-hafiz}

## Formation
He trained for about a decade as an Islamic scholar, completing memorisation of the Qur'an and graduating as an alim in 1999, before working in mosque and community posts in Britain. {cite:muslim500-asim-hafiz} {cite:wikipedia-asim-hafiz}

## Career
As chaplain and then religious adviser he provided pastoral care to Muslim service personnel, advised the chain of command on religious and cultural questions, and deployed to Afghanistan between 2010 and 2012 in support of stabilisation and conflict-resolution work. He was appointed OBE in 2014 for services to defence. He has also headed the Avicenna Foundation, which offers university scholarships, and has argued publicly — against criticism from parts of his own community — that military service is fully compatible with Muslim faith. {cite:muslim500-asim-hafiz} {cite:wikipedia-asim-hafiz}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him under Social Issues as the figure who normalised Muslim chaplaincy in a Western military — a precedent followed elsewhere. His public claim that there is no contradiction between Muslim faith and service in the British armed forces has been contested by some British Muslims; the museum records the position attributed. {cite:muslim500-asim-hafiz}
