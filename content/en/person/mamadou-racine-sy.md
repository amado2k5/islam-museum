# Mamadou Racine Sy

## Summary
Mamadou Racine Sy (c. 1838 – 24 February 1902) was a Toucouleur officer of the Senegalese tirailleurs and the first Black African to reach the rank of captain in the French army. Raised at Souïma near Podor with a Quranic formation — he himself taught Quranic studies as a young man — he enlisted in 1860 against his father's wish, rose through the ranks in the colonial campaigns of Senegal and the French Sudan, and was made captain by an exceptional presidential decree in 1883. He ended his career as a post commander and colonial intermediary, and died at Kita in 1902. {cite:wikipedia-mamadou-racine-fr} {cite:wikidata-mamadou-racine-sy}

His birth year is not certain: the French article gives 1838 or 28 March 1842 and says his birth date and place are not known with certainty; the record follows c. 1838, the year given by Wikidata and by the titles of his two book-length biographies, and marks it debated. {cite:wikipedia-mamadou-racine-fr} {cite:wikidata-mamadou-racine-sy}

## Youth and formation
He was a Toucouleur, born at Souïma near Podor in the Fouta, the son of Elimane Racine, the village chief, who was on good terms with the French and influential among the Toucouleurs, and of Seynabou Rabi Bâ, of the Fulani and Torodo aristocracy of Thilogne and Guede Ouro. As a child he was trained in the Muslim religion and became an instructor in Quranic studies. {cite:wikipedia-mamadou-racine-fr}

## Military career
He entered the Senegalese tirailleurs on 4 October 1860, against his father's will; his father sent ten young men to buy him back, without success. He distinguished himself by bravery in combat, took part in the campaigns against Seku Amadu in Senegal and against Samori Ture in the Sudan, and was present at the battles of Koundian and Ségou. He was made corporal in 1865, sergeant on 9 August 1866 and second lieutenant "à titre indigène" on 15 May 1868 — the youngest African to receive the epaulette — and became a knight of the Légion d'honneur in November 1869, the first Black man to receive that distinction. In 1872, at Bakel, he led the prayer at the mosque dressed in a boubou, and was reprimanded for it. In 1878 he was allowed to travel to Paris for the Universal Exhibition. {cite:wikipedia-mamadou-racine-fr}

French law limited the promotion of indigenous soldiers to lieutenant, and his rise stalled for want of a vacancy; he was promoted lieutenant on 17 March 1880. Wounded at the taking of Daba in Bélédougou on 16 January 1883, he was proposed for captain by several officers, including Colonel Borgnis-Desbordes, against strong resistance in the army, where many whites refused to have a Black man command white non-commissioned officers. Exceptionally, President Jules Grévy signed a decree on 19 October 1883 making Mamadou Racine the first African captain of the French colonial marine infantry; he was first posted to the staff of Haut-Sénégal, so that no whites were under his command. {cite:wikipedia-mamadou-racine-fr}

In March 1886 he carried to Colonel Frey the treaty of Kéniébakoro by which Samori Ture recognised French sovereignty over the left bank of the Niger, and he became for the colonial authorities a guide, interpreter and diplomat with France's new ally. He commanded the post of Nyamina in 1889 and then that of Goumbo in 1896, the year of his retirement. Archinard named him chief of Kayes right bank and its dependent territories on 31 October 1890, and General de Trentinian, lieutenant-governor of the Sudan, named him fama ("king") of Bambouck on 27 March 1899 — an honorary post that placed him as an intermediary between the colonial administrators and the colonised populations. He had learned early to read and write French and adopted French customs. {cite:wikipedia-mamadou-racine-fr}

## Death and memory
He died on 24 February 1902 at Kita, during a recruitment mission for the railway that the governor-general had entrusted to him because of his prestige and influence; in 2011 his grave was transferred to the cemetery reserved for French soldiers. In France such colonial auxiliaries have largely remained anonymous, while in Senegal his name is associated with the portrait of a collaborator in the service of the colonisers, in contrast to the heroic figures of African resistance; the French article records both memories without settling them. {cite:wikipedia-mamadou-racine-fr}

## Faith
In this museum his faith is recorded as unknown.
