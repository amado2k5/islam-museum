# Kunta-Haji Kishiev

Kunta-Haji Kishiev (birth year contested, between 1800 and 1830 in the reference literature – 19/31 May 1867) was a Chechen Sufi sheikh, founder of Haji-Muridiyya, the Chechen branch of the Qadiriyya order, and a preacher of non-violence during the Caucasian War {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-en} {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-ru}.

## Formation and teaching

Born at Isti-Su (Melcha-Khi) to Kishi and Khedi, he received a religious education and studied under the Naqshbandi sheikh Gezi-Haji of Zandak {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-en} {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-ru}. On his hajj in the late 1850s he visited the tomb of Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani in Baghdad and became a devoted follower of the Qadiriyya, whose teaching he preached on his return {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-en} {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-ru}. He taught his murids — some five thousand by the early 1860s — the daily prayers and the loud circular zikr, and rejected further war against Russia {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-en}.

## Arrest and death

Summoned before Imam Shamil at Vedeno, he was examined and, by the differing accounts, condemned or cleared {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-ru}. After his arrest in January 1864, some three thousand of his followers, armed only with daggers, tried to free him at Shali and were dispersed with heavy loss {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-en}. He died in exile at Ustyuzhna on 19 (31) May 1867 {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-ru}; his followers hold that he did not die {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-ru}.

## Faith

Two independent texts attest Kunta-Haji's Qadiriyya affiliation personally: the English article calls him a follower of the Qadiriyya Sufi order and records his devotion to it after Baghdad {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-en}, and the Russian article names him founder of Haji-Muridiyya, a variety of the Qadiriyya {cite:wikipedia-kunta-haji-kishiev-ru}. His faith segment is therefore recorded as sufi, documented, and his membership of the Qadiriyya is written as a relation. The "Sunni" of the English infobox stands alone — Wikidata's value is Islam in general {cite:wikidata-kunta-haji-kishiev} — so no Sunni segment is recorded.
