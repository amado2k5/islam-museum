## Overview
Abu Abd Allah al-Bata'ihi, surnamed al-Ma'mun, was vizier of the Fatimid caliph al-Amir bi-Ahkam Allah in Egypt. Orphaned poor — his father had been one of al-Afdal's spies in Iraq — he attached himself to a building-worker then carried loads in the Great Market, until al-Afdal noticed him and raised him from among the servants step by step to the vizierate; he was generous and broad-minded, highly guarded and attentive to people's affairs. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.9}

## The Lawata campaign and his execution
When a large body of Lawata came from the Maghreb into the districts of Egypt, plundering and devastating, al-Ma'mun gathered the army of Egypt, marched against them, routed them, killed and captured many and fixed an annual tribute on them, returning in triumph. In Ramadan 519 AH al-Amir seized him and crucified him and his brothers; the cause was that he had suborned Ja'far, the caliph's brother, to kill al-Amir and take the caliphate, and Abu al-Hasan ibn Abi Usama informed the caliph. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.9 p.224-225}
