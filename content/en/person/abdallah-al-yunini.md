## Overview
Abu 'Uthman 'Abdallah ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Ja'far al-Yunini, the great ascetic "Lion of Syria"; from Yunin, a village of Baalbek — an awe-inspiring, tall, sharp-tempered shaykh of complete courage, commanding right and forbidding wrong, constant in jihad and dhikr, a man of strivings and marvels. The amir Amjad, lord of Baalbek, would visit him and be humbled: "Amjad, you are unjust"; his bow was said to weigh eighty ratls and no raiding host set out in Syria without him — when the Mujahid Asad al-Din gifted him a horse at Homs he mounted and worked wonders among the foe. He never rose for anyone out of reverence for God, never saved a dinar or dirham, and wore only raw wool and a sheepskin cap. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## Death
He died in 617 on a Saturday after the Friday prayer, asleep with his rosary in hand, past eighty; his grave at Baalbek is visited. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}
