## Summary
Waleed bin Ibrahim Al Ibrahim is a Saudi media magnate born in 1954, founder and chairman of the Middle East Broadcasting Center ⁦(MBC)⁩, the largest media conglomerate in the Arab world; The Muslim 500 lists him among influential media figures. {cite:muslim500-waleed-al-ibrahim} {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Founding MBC
Brother-in-law of King Fahd and son of a Saudi business family, he launched MBC1 from London in 1991 as the first pan-Arab privately owned satellite channel, offering entertainment and news outside state broadcasting monopolies. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Building the group
He expanded MBC into a network — Al Arabiya news channel ⁦(2003)⁩, MBC2 movies, MBC3 children's, Al Hadath news and the Shahid streaming platform — relocating operations to Dubai in the 2000s and to Riyadh in the 2020s as Saudi Arabia's media capital. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Influence and scrutiny
MBC shaped Arab popular culture for three decades — Ramadan drama, talent formats and twenty-four-hour news — while its closeness to Saudi state interests is regularly debated in media commentary; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Assessment
He is credited with creating the Arab satellite television industry as a private business and with the dominant entertainment network of the region; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim} {cite:muslim500-waleed-al-ibrahim}


## MBC empire
Under his chairmanship MBC grew from a single London-based channel into a multi-channel broadcaster — news, entertainment, children's and regional channels — headquartered since 2022 in Riyadh, with tens of millions of viewers daily. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Saudi media politics
His relationship with the Saudi state — including the 2017-18 Ritz-Carlton detention and subsequent share transfer — has been documented in press coverage as a defining episode in the kingdom's media consolidation. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Shahid and streaming
MBC's Shahid platform, built under his tenure, became the largest Arabic streaming service, carrying Ramadan drama slates that shape viewing across the Arab world. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim} {cite:muslim500-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Origins
Born in Saudi Arabia into a family of broadcast pioneers — his brothers founded early Saudi private media — he launched MBC in London in 1991 as the first pan-Arab private satellite broadcaster. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## Building an empire
MBC's growth under his chairmanship — news, entertainment, children's and regional channels — made it the Arab world's largest private broadcaster, reaching tens of millions of viewers daily across the Middle East and diaspora. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## The Saudi relationship
His navigation of Saudi media politics — including the 2017-18 Ritz-Carlton detention and the state's later stake — is documented as a defining story of the relationship between media capital and political power in the kingdom. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim} {cite:muslim500-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## The MBC empire
Waleed Al-Ibrahim's founding of MBC in London in 1991 created the first pan-Arab private satellite broadcaster: a single channel that under his chairmanship grew into a multi-channel group — news, entertainment, children's and regional channels — reaching tens of millions of viewers daily across the Middle East and diaspora. MBC's Shahid platform, built under his tenure, became the largest Arabic streaming service, and his navigation of Saudi media politics — including the 2017-18 Ritz-Carlton detention — is documented as a defining story of media capital and political power in the kingdom. {cite:wikipedia-waleed-al-ibrahim}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists him among the most influential Muslims in media, citing his role as the founder and chairman of MBC and his position as the Arab world's most-powerful private broadcaster. {cite:muslim500-waleed-al-ibrahim}
