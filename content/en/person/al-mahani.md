## Overview
Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Isa al-Mahani, one of the scholars of numbers and geometers, according to Ibn al-Nadim. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 375,371}

His works include a commentary on the fifth treatise of Euclid's book, a treatise on the thrones of the stars, a treatise on proportion, and a book on twenty-six figures of the first treatise of Euclid. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 375,371}

## Biography and date limits

This extension preserves the earlier Fihrist account and attribution. MacTutor places birth about820 in Mahan, Kerman; observations853–866 quoted by Ibn Yunus provide firmer life evidence. TDV places him in Baghdad around860 and questions the267/880 death pairing; Dold-Samplonius writes about880. An observation date is not an invention date, and no Hijri birth year is manufactured {cite:mahani-mactutor biography} {cite:tdv-mahani-fazlioglu biography} {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb biography}.

MacTutor explicitly identifies an Islamic mathematician, an individual basis rather than a name-based inference. The separately declared Sunni museum frame is not a personal denominational document; the accounts consulted record no contrary tradition. Neither a city's location nor a proof's quality establishes personal belief {cite:mahani-mactutor summary} {cite:tdv-mahani-fazlioglu biography}.

## Observations and algebraic reduction

The reported observations concern conjunctions and solar and lunar eclipses. Dold-Samplonius describes three successive lunar eclipses beginning about half an hour later than al-Mahani's astrolabe-based calculations. This is a report from lost writings, not a museum recomputation or proof of perfect accuracy {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb observations}.

The [[work/mahani-sphere-cylinder-commentary|Sphere and Cylinder commentary]] is associated with his [[invention/mahani-sphere-division-cubic|cubic formulation of sphere division]] at a prescribed volume ratio. He did not solve the equation. The sources report al-Khazin's later conic solution and Khayyam's appreciation of the attempt. The later solution is not credited to al-Mahani, and al-Khazin is not confused with al-Khazini {cite:tdv-mahani-fazlioglu algebra-and-work5} {cite:mahani-mactutor cubic}.

## Ratio, Euclid and further works

Dold-Samplonius describes comparing magnitudes through the greatest-common-measure process and its quotient sequences, rather than only Euclid's equimultiple definition. She mentions Thabit ibn Qurra and a parallel development by al-Nayrizi; in her account neither established the connection to Euclid's definition, which Ibn al-Haytham later supplied. This is the scholar's description, not an original proof inspected here {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb ratio}.

She lists commentaries on books V, X and XIII and a lost work on26 propositions of book I not needing reductio ad absurdum. TDV also lists an azimuth treatise, a work on stellar latitudes and a shorter approach to the parabola's area after Thabit. These are biographical subjects, not extra invention records counted without separate research {cite:tdv-mahani-fazlioglu works-and-parabola} {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb Euclid}.

Al-Mahani revised Menelaus, and al-Harawi revised and completed his work. The sources report that al-Tusi did not value their additions and instead used Ibn Iraq's work. That judgement belongs to its author, not the museum's verdict on all al-Mahani's achievements. Some revisions are lost; the evidence here comes from biographies and work lists, not manuscript inspection {cite:tdv-mahani-fazlioglu work7} {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb Menelaus}.

## The ratio treatise and reception

The [[work/mahani-risala-fi-nisba|Treatise on Ratio]] record links his [[invention/mahani-successive-quotient-ratios|successive-quotient formulation]] to proportionality, retaining uncertainty over whether DSB's three reported texts differ. MacTutor reports al-Nayrizi adopting his concepts, not a documented shared teaching session. Neither the modern17/7 example nor invention of the inherited Euclidean process is attributed to him; nor is the equivalence proof Dold-Samplonius assigns later to Ibn al-Haytham {cite:tdv-mahani-fazlioglu work1} {cite:nayrizi-mactutor ratio} {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb ratio}.
