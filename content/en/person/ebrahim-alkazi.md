Ebrahim Alkazi (18 October 1925 – 4 August 2020) was an Indian theatre director and teacher, born at Pune to a wealthy businessman from Unaizah in present-day Saudi Arabia and a Kuwaiti mother; the Arabic article styles him Ibrahim Hamad al-Qadi and calls him the father of Indian theatre.{cite:wikipedia-ebrahim-alkazi-en}{cite:wikipedia-ebrahim-alkazi-ar} Educated at Pune and at St Xavier's College, Bombay, he trained at the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art in London, won the BBC Broadcasting Award in 1950, and on returning to India ran the Theatre Group and then the Theatre Unit in Bombay before founding a school of dramatic arts in 1956.{cite:wikipedia-ebrahim-alkazi-en}

In 1962 he became director of the National School of Drama in New Delhi, a post he held until 1977; there he created the Repertory Company in 1964, trained a generation of Indian actors and directors, and staged landmark productions of Tughlaq, Ashadh Ka Ek Din and Andha Yug, often in open-air settings and Delhi monuments.{cite:wikipedia-ebrahim-alkazi-en} With his wife Roshen he founded the Art Heritage Gallery in Delhi, and his honours include the Padma Shri (1966), the Padma Bhushan (1991), the Padma Vibhushan (2010) and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship.{cite:wikipedia-ebrahim-alkazi-en}{cite:wikidata-ebrahim-alkazi} He died at New Delhi on 4 August 2020; the Arabic article's infobox gives Mumbai as the place of death.{cite:wikipedia-ebrahim-alkazi-ar}
