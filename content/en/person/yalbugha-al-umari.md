## Overview
Yalbugha ibn Abd Allah al-Umari al-Khassaki was a mamluk of [[person/al-nasir-hasan|Sultan al-Nasir Hasan]] who rose to command of a thousand, then led the revolt in which his master was killed, and ruled Egypt as regent for several years. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/438} Ibn Hajar calls him the famous great amir and says he was given the title Nizam al-Mulk. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/438}

## Rise under Hasan
Ibn Hajar says Hasan first gave him command of a thousand after the arrest of [[person/sarghitmish|Sarghitmish]] in 759, and made him amir majlis late in the same year. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/438} Ibn Taghribirdi adds that Hasan gave him the fief of an exiled amir, Jurji al-Idrisi, and the post of amir majlis. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/307} Within a few years he was the man everyone pointed to, and the sultan came to feel his weight. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/311}

## The revolt against Hasan
In 762, Ibn Taghribirdi says, reports reached Hasan that Yalbugha disapproved of the large grants to women and of the power of the eunuchs, and the sultan decided to arrest him. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/311} Yalbugha armed his mamluks, called on his fellow mamluks with promises of rank and fiefs, and warned them of what lay ahead if the sultan prevailed, so that many joined him. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/312} Hasan was beaten and fled to the Citadel by night; Yalbugha followed him there and had him killed. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/313} The Encyclopaedia of Islam describes the opposition faction as headed, against mamluk loyalty to the founder of the household, by Yalbugha. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 343}

## Regent under al-Mansur and al-Ashraf Shaban
Yalbugha put Hasan's nephew al-Mansur Muhammad on the throne and became the manager of the realm together with his fellow mamluk Taybugha al-Tawil, each promising not to oppose the other. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/3-4} Ibn Hajar says he held the post of atabek. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/438} In 762 he led the army to Syria against the governor of Damascus, Baydamur, who had rebelled after the news of Hasan's death; the rebels surrendered under oath and were then arrested and sent to Alexandria. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/4-5} He married Hasan's widow Tulubiya. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/6} In Shaban 764 the amirs deposed al-Mansur and made al-Ashraf Shaban sultan, with Yalbugha controlling the state. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/6}

The Encyclopaedia of Islam says Yalbugha and Taybugha shared the regency until Taybugha was removed in Jumada II 767/March 1366, after which Yalbugha held sole power. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 163} In Muharram 767/October 1365 a crusading force under Peter I of Cyprus briefly occupied Alexandria; Yalbugha and the sultan marched to relieve it, and he then began building a war fleet. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 pp. 163-164} Ibn Taghribirdi tells how he summoned carpenters from Syria to cut timber in the mountains near Antioch for the ships. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/30} Ibn Hajar adds that he confiscated money from the Christian monasteries, that he cancelled the tax on pilgrims at Mecca, and that his charity to students of knowledge was large. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/438-439}

He also had a large household: Ibn Hajar says he bought many mamluks, that they numbered about three thousand, and that he lived at al-Kabsh near the Qanatir al-Siba'. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/438} He favoured the Hanafi school and paid generously those who followed it. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/439} A special large turban cap, the kalluta Yalbughawiyya, was named for him. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 662} The future sultan Barquq was brought up in his household, the Yalbughawiyya. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 343}

## His fall
The Encyclopaedia of Islam says Shaban, resenting Yalbugha's domination, colluded with the regent's mutinous mamluks in Rabi II 768/December 1366. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 164} Ibn Taghribirdi's account runs as follows. Ibn Hajar says that, while the sultan was at Tarana, Yalbugha's own mamluks, who had agreed with Shaban to strike, moved against him; he sensed it, fled to Cairo and gathered the boats on the river. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/439} Ibn Taghribirdi adds that he set up Anuk, a nephew of Shaban, as sultan on the middle island, so that the people called Anuk the sultan of the island. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/37} The sultan's men found boats and crossed; Yalbugha's emirs deserted him one by one. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/38-39} He prayed the afternoon prayer, handed his sword to his chamberlain Taybugha and went home to al-Kabsh, where he was taken and brought to the Citadel. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/39} That night his own mamluks took him out and killed him; the head was recognised by a mole behind the ear. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/39-40} The dawadar Tashtamur washed and buried him in the tomb Tashtamur had built in the desert near that of Khawand Tughay. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/40} Ibn Taghribirdi observes that God repaid him in kind, since he died at the hands of his mamluks as he had killed his master. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/40} The Encyclopaedia of Islam dates his death to Rabi II 768/December 1366. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 343}
