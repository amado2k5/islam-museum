## Summary
Mohammad Rashid Qabbani served for eighteen years as Grand Mufti of the Lebanese Republic, the head of Dar al-Fatwa and the senior authority of Lebanon's Sunni community — a tenure that spanned the country's post-war reconstruction, the 2005 upheaval and the spillover of the Syrian war. {cite:muslim500-mohammad-rashid-qabbani} {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-rashid-qabbani}

## Formation
Born in Beirut in 1942, Qabbani studied Islamic law at Al-Azhar University in Cairo, where he earned his doctorate, before returning to Lebanon to teach and serve in the fatwa apparatus he would eventually lead. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-rashid-qabbani}

## The muftiate
Elected mufti in 1996, he presided over Dar al-Fatwa, the body that supervises Sunni religious courts, mosques and charitable endowments in Lebanon. Throughout, he presented himself as a voice against sectarian polarisation, denouncing violence and calling for Muslim-Christian coexistence even as Lebanon's politics grew steadily more confessional. {cite:muslim500-mohammad-rashid-qabbani}

## Standing and criticism
The Muslim 500 describes him as Lebanon's leading Sunni scholar, noting his regular public stands against sectarianism and violence. His later years in office were not free of controversy: the internal elections and finances of Dar al-Fatwa were disputed in Lebanese courts and press, and his sharp statements on political questions drew both support and criticism. The museum records these disputes attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-mohammad-rashid-qabbani} {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-rashid-qabbani}
