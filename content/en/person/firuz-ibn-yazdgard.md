## Summary
Firuz ibn Yazdgard was the son of the last Sasanian king of Persia. Al-Baladhuri reports that, as they allege, he fell in among the Turks, who married him to one of their own, and he remained among them — this after his father Yazdgard was killed at [[place/marw|Marw]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 443}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri narrates the end of Yazdgard ibn Shahriyar: Nizek advanced with the Turks and camped at al-Janabid, where they fought him until the Turks broke even; then fortune turned and his companions were killed and his army plundered, so he came to the city of Marw which would not open to him {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 442}. He dismounted and walked until he entered a miller's house at al-Righab; it is told that Mahawayh sent men who killed him there, or else that the miller, seeing his crown and coveting it, dropped a millstone on him — the account varies {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 442}. The report closes: "And Firuz ibn Yazdgard fell in, as they allege, among the Turks, who gave him a wife, and he settled among them" {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 443}. The son of the last Sasanian king thus remained married among the Turks, as the tradition records without further detail {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 443}.
