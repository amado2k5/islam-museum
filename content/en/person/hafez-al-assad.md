# Hafez al-Assad

Hafez al-Assad (6 October 1930 – 10 June 2000) was a Syrian military officer and politician who served as president of Syria from 1971 until his death, having previously been prime minister (1970–1971), defence minister, and commander of the Syrian Air Force. He led the Ba'ath Party's Syrian regional command from 1970, and the party ruled Syria under his family until its overthrow in December 2024, under his son Bashar al-Assad. {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-en} {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-ar}

## Early life
He was born in Qardaha, in the coastal mountains of north-west Syria, into a poor Alawite peasant family belonging to the Kalbiyya tribe. He was the first of his family to attend high school, in Latakia, where he faced anti-Alawite prejudice, and he joined the Ba'ath Party as a student organiser before entering the military academy at Homs and becoming a fighter pilot. {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-en} {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-ar}

## Rise to power
Assad took part in the 1963 coup that brought the Syrian Ba'ath to power and was appointed commander of the air force. In the further coup of February 1966 he became defence minister. On 13 November 1970 he carried out the coup the Ba'ath called the "Corrective Movement", ousting the regime of Salah Jadid and Nureddin al-Atassi; he served as prime minister and then, from March 1971, as president, the first Alawite Ba'athist president in Syrian history, as the Arabic article puts it. {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-en} {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-ar}

## Rule
In power, Assad abandoned Jadid's policy of exporting revolution, built alliances with the Soviet Union and the Eastern Bloc during the Cold War, kept working ties with some Western European and Gulf states, and presented Syria as the defender of the Palestinians while his practical support for the PLO in Lebanon remained limited. Domestically, the security and intelligence sectors passed under near-monopoly Alawite control around his person, staffed by kinsmen and clients, while Sunni officers held senior posts without independent power — a structure the English article documents from the scholarship of Batatu and Hinnebusch. In February 1982 his forces crushed the Muslim Brotherhood uprising in Hama, destroying much of the city. {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-en}

## Death and burial
Assad, a diabetic who had suffered a heart attack in 1983, died of a heart attack in Damascus on 10 June 2000, while on the telephone with the Lebanese prime minister, Salim al-Huss. After an acting presidency of Abdul Halim Khaddam, his son Bashar al-Assad succeeded him as president. Hafez was buried beside his elder son Bassel (who had died in 1994) in the family mausoleum at Qardaha. In December 2024, after the fall of his son's regime, rebels burned the tomb; in 2025 his remains were exhumed and taken away, and their resting place is unknown. {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-en} {cite:wikidata-hafez-al-assad}

## Religion
Assad's Alawite affiliation is attested in full by the sources: the English article records his birth into an Alawite family of the Kalbiyya tribe and treats his presidency throughout as that of an Alawite ruler; the Arabic article calls him the first Alawite Ba'athist president in Syrian history; Wikidata's religion value is the Alawites item itself, and the Arabic infobox gives Islam in general. The museum's faith vocabulary has no Alawite value, and a distinct named tradition is not relabelled with another tradition's value, so his faith is recorded as unknown with this attestation preserved entire in his data record, and the question flagged for the museum's founder. {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-en} {cite:wikipedia-hafez-al-assad-ar} {cite:wikidata-hafez-al-assad}
