## Summary
Ramzan Akhmadovich Kadyrov has headed the Chechen Republic, a republic of the Russian Federation, since 2007. Son of the mufti-turned-president Akhmad Kadyrov, he governs a restituted Grozny in close alliance with Moscow while human rights organisations and Western governments accuse his security apparatus of grave abuses — accusations he and his government deny. {cite:wikipedia-ramzan-kadyrov} {cite:muslim500-ramzan-kadyrov}

## Life and formation
He was born on 5 October 1976 in the village of Tsentaroy (Akhmat-Yurt). During the first Chechen war his father was a senior mufti on the separatist side; during the second war the family changed sides to Moscow, and Akhmad Kadyrov became the republic's president, assassinated in May 2004. Ramzan, who had led his father's security detail, consolidated power through the presidential guard and was appointed head of the republic in 2007. {cite:wikipedia-ramzan-kadyrov}

## Rule
Under his rule Grozny was rebuilt from ruins with federal funds, large mosques were raised — including the Grozny central mosque, among Europe's largest — and public life was reshaped around an officially promoted Islam mixing Sufi tradition and state loyalty. Militancy in the republic subsided compared to the 2000s. Chechen units under his patronage have fought in Russia's wars, including in Ukraine and Syria. {cite:wikipedia-ramzan-kadyrov} {cite:muslim500-ramzan-kadyrov}

## Contested record
United Nations experts, Russian rights groups such as Memorial (later dissolved in Russia), and Western governments have documented allegations of enforced disappearances, torture, extrajudicial killings and persecution of gay men and dissenters under his administration; the European Union, United States and others have imposed sanctions on him and associates. Kadyrov and Chechen officials reject the accusations as fabrications, and supporters credit him with ending war and restoring order. The museum records the allegations attributed to their sources and the denials alongside them, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-ramzan-kadyrov}

## Faith and image
He presents publicly as a Sunni Muslim of Chechnya's Sufi tradition, sponsors pilgrimage and Islamic institutions, and styles himself a defender of Muslim causes — while also building an extravagant personal image of sport, animals and spectacle documented in the press. {cite:wikipedia-ramzan-kadyrov} {cite:muslim500-ramzan-kadyrov}
