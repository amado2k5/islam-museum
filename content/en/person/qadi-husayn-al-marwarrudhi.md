## Overview
Abu Ali al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Marwarrudhi was a Shafi'i jurist known simply as al-Qadi, the author of the Ta'liqa in fiqh {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 134}. Ibn Khallikan calls him a great imam with unusual views within the school {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 134}. He says he died in 462 AH at Marw al-Rudh, which corresponds to 1069 CE by the museum's conversion {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 135}.

## Standing in the school
Ibn Khallikan notes that whenever Imam al-Haramayn in Nihayat al-Matlab, or al-Ghazali in al-Wasit and al-Basit, writes "the Qadi said", it is he who is meant and no one else {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 134}.

## Teacher, works and students
He took his fiqh from Abu Bakr al-Qaffal al-Marwazi and wrote on legal theory, on substantive law and on points of disagreement {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 134}. He continued throughout to judge between people, teach and give legal opinions {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 134}. A number of notable scholars took fiqh from him, among them Abu Muhammad al-Husayn ibn Mas'ud al-Farra' al-Baghawi, author of al-Tahdhib and Sharh al-Sunna {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 134}.

## Death
He died in 462 AH at Marw al-Rudh; Ibn Khallikan refers readers to his earlier account of Marw al-Rudh under the letter alif {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 135}.
