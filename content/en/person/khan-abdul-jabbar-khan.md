# Khan Abdul Jabbar Khan

Khan Abdul Jabbar Khan (1883 – 9 May 1958), popularly known as Dr. Khan Sahib, was a Pashtun physician and politician from Utmanzai in Charsadda, and the elder brother of Abdul Ghaffar Khan {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. He led the government of the North-West Frontier Province, opposed the Partition of India alongside his brother and the Khudai Khidmatgars, and after independence became the first Chief Minister of West Pakistan {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. Wikidata gives his birth year as 1882 against the article texts' 1883 {cite:wikidata-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan}; the museum follows the texts and records the conflict in its data.

## Early life and medicine

He was born at Utmanzai to a Muhammadzai Pashtun family, his father Bahram Khan a local landlord, eight years before his brother Abdul Ghaffar {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. After the Edwards Mission High School in Peshawar he studied at Grant Medical College, Bombay, and completed his training at St Thomas' Hospital in London {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. He served in France during the First World War, then joined the Indian Medical Service at Mardan, resigning his commission in 1921 after refusing a posting to Waziristan during the operations against his fellow Pashtun tribes {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}.

## The independence movement

In 1935 he was elected to the Central Legislative Assembly in New Delhi for the North-West Frontier Province {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. In the 1937 provincial elections his Frontier National Congress emerged as the largest party and he led the province's government {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. With his brother and the Khudai Khidmatgars he boycotted the July 1947 referendum on the province's future, because it offered no option of independence or of joining Afghanistan {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-ar}.

## In Pakistan

He was jailed under Abdul Qayyum Khan Kashmiri's provincial government and later released with his brother and other activists {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. In 1954 he joined Muhammad Ali Bogra's central cabinet as Minister for Communications, a decision that split him from Bacha Khan {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. In October 1955 he became the first Chief Minister of West Pakistan under the One Unit scheme, formed the Republican Party the same month, and resigned in March 1957 {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}.

## Assassination

He was assassinated at about 8:30 on the morning of 9 May 1958 in the garden of his son Sadullah Khan's house at 16 Aikman Road, Lahore, by Atta Mohammad, a dismissed land-revenue clerk; some sources attribute the order to the Khaksar leader Allama Mashraqi {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-ar}. His body was taken to Utmanzai and buried beside his wife Mary {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}. New Delhi's Khan Market, established in 1951 for Partition refugees from the Frontier, is named in his honour {cite:wikipedia-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan-en}.

## Faith, as the sources attest it

No fetched text names a school for Dr. Khan Sahib himself, and Wikidata carries no religion statement for him {cite:wikidata-khan-abdul-jabbar-khan}. His brother's article describes the family both were born into as a Sunni Muslim family, but a family's description is not the man's own attestation, so the museum's data record leaves his school unknown.
