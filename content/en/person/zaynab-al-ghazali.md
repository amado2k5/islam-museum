## Summary

Zaynab al-Ghazali (1917–2005) was an Egyptian preacher and founder of the Muslim Women's Association, among the most prominent women in the history of contemporary Islamic movements, and a political prisoner of the 1960s.{cite:wikipedia-zaynab-al-ghazali}

## Life

Born in Mit Ghamr, Dakahlia, she worked in the women's movement then founded the Muslim Women's Association in 1936, joining preaching, education and charity; invited to join the Muslim Brotherhood, she backed Hassan al-Banna at first without formal membership.{cite:wikipedia-zaynab-al-ghazali}

## Activity and imprisonment

In the 1960s she drew closer to the Brotherhood organisation, was arrested in 1965 and sentenced to hard labour; her memoir Days of My Life recounts torture and endurance in Nasser's prisons until her release in 1971.{cite:wikipedia-zaynab-al-ghazali}

## Books and standing

Her books include Days of My Life, Nazarat fi Kitab Allah and Knights of the Word. She is remembered as a pioneer of organised Islamic women's work and an icon of Islamist women prisoners; assessments differ over her organisational ties to the Brotherhood.{cite:wikipedia-zaynab-al-ghazali}
