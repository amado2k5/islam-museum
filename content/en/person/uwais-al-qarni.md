## Overview
Uways ibn 'Amir ibn Jaz' ibn Malik al-Qarni, of Banu Qarn ibn Radman ibn Najiya ibn Murad, was one of the foremost ascetics and worshippers and a chief of the Successors; his origin was Yemen, dwelling in the wastes and sands, and he died in 37 AH (657 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.32}

He lived through the Prophet's lifetime without seeing him; he came as a delegate to 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, then settled at Kufa, was present at the battle of Siffin with 'Ali, and many hold he was killed in it. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.32}

## In Ibn al-Mubarak's Kitab al-Zuhd
[[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Ibn al-Mubarak]] devotes a chapter of his [[work/kitab-al-zuhd-ibn-al-mubarak|Kitab al-Zuhd]] to Uways and al-Sunabihi. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 293} It contains the report of Usayr ibn Jabir that Uways used to sit with them in a circle, and when he spoke his words reached their hearts as no one else's did; that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] asked a delegation whether there was a man from Qaran among them; and that when a man told Uways the Commander of the Faithful had mentioned him, Uways made him promise to tell no one. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 293} It also reports from Amr ibn Murra that when Umar met him and his standing became known, he fled and was not seen again until he died. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 293}

[[person/yahya-ibn-muhammad-ibn-saaid|Ibn Sa'id]] adds that the chains of the hadith of Uways "are all sound, transmitted by trustworthy men from trustworthy men", and that Usayr is called Usayr ibn Jabir by the people of Basra and Yusayr ibn Amr by the people of Kufa. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 294} The editor notes that Muslim transmitted the hadith of Uways briefly through Abu Nadra and at length through Zurara ibn Awfa from Usayr. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 293}
