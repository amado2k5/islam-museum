## Overview
Abu Dharr al-Harawi is Abd ibn Ahmad al-Ansari al-Maliki, known as Ibn al-Sammak. He heard hadith in Herat, Sarakhs, Balkh, Merv, Basra, Baghdad, Damascus and Egypt, then settled in Mecca. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.72-73}

## His travels and transmission
He compiled a dictionary of his teachers. He came to Baghdad and transmitted there, then made the pilgrimage and settled, and he lived in al-Sarawat, and he used to perform the pilgrimage every year and transmit and then return. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.72-73}

## His standing
Al-Maqqari describes him as trustworthy, exact, pious, ascetic and generous, and says that he became the chief of the shaykhs of the Sanctuary and was referred to in Sufism. He relates that most of the sound copies of the Sahih of al-Bukhari in the Maghrib go back to his transmission through al-Baji, or to the transmission of Ibn Sukkara through his chain. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.72-73}

## His school
Al-Maqqari says that the reason he adopted the Maliki school was that he saw al-Daraqutni attending on the judge Abu Bakr ibn al-Tayyib and kissing his face, and saying: "This is the imam of the Muslims." So he followed his school. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.72-73}

## Death
He died in 435 AH, and it is said 434 AH. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.72-73}
