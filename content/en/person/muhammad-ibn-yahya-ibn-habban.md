## Summary
Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Habban ibn Munqidh ibn Amr ibn Malik ibn Khansa' ibn Mabdhul ibn Amr ibn Ghanm ibn Mazin, Abu Abd Allah al-Ansari al-Madani, a trustworthy Successor prolific in hadith who held a teaching circle in the Prophet's mosque where he gave legal opinions; he died in Medina in 121 AH aged seventy-four {cite:tahdhib-al-kamal-mizzi p.551}.

## Lineage and teachers
His lineage runs to Mazin, ancestor of the Banu al-Najjar of Khazraj. He narrated from Anas ibn Malik, Dhakwan Abu Salih al-Samman, Rafi' ibn Khadij, Abbad ibn Tamim, Abd al-Rahman ibn Hurmuz al-A'raj, his father Yahya ibn Habban, his uncle Wasi' ibn Habban, and others {cite:tahdhib-al-kamal-mizzi p.551}.

## Students and standing
Among his narrators were Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn Shihab al-Zuhri — his contemporary — Hisham ibn Urwa, Malik ibn Anas, al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Muhammad ibn Ishaq ibn Yasar, Ubayd Allah ibn Umar al-Umari, Rabi'a ibn Abi Abd al-Rahman and Rabi'a ibn Uthman al-Taymi. Al-Nasa'i declared him trustworthy, and Ibn Hibban listed him in Kitab al-Thiqat {cite:tahdhib-al-kamal-mizzi p.551}.

## Death
Al-Waqidi said: he had a circle in the Prophet's mosque and gave legal opinions; he was trustworthy and prolific in hadith, and died in Medina in 121 AH aged seventy-four {cite:tahdhib-al-kamal-mizzi p.552}. All the major collections narrate from him {cite:tahdhib-al-kamal-mizzi p.551}.

## For younger visitors
Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Habban was a Medinese scholar among the Successors who sat in the Prophet's mosque teaching and answering questions, and the great scholars — Imam Malik and al-Zuhri among them — took hadith from him.
