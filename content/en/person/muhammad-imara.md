## Summary

Muhammad Imara (1931–2020) was a prolific Egyptian Islamic thinker, author and editor, member of the Islamic Research Academy, who combined writing on Islamic thought with editing heritage texts.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-imara}

## Life

Born in Sharqiyya, he graduated in Arabic from al-Azhar University, worked in teaching, journalism and research, was elected to the Islamic Research Academy, and edited Islamic cultural journals.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-imara}

## Works

His authored and edited books exceed two hundred; best known are Tayarat al-Fikr al-Islami (Currents of Islamic Thought), Al-Islam wa Usul al-Hukm (Islam and the Foundations of Governance) and Al-Mu'tazila, plus critical editions of the complete works of Muhammad Abduh, Abd al-Rahman al-Kawakibi and Rashid Rida's journal al-Manar.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-imara}

## Method and standing

Imara championed the Islamic reform school of the Abduh–Rida line, and fought intellectual battles against both secularism and takfir, becoming a centrist voice in Egyptian debate; critics noted his output sometimes came at the cost of depth — the museum records both.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-imara}
