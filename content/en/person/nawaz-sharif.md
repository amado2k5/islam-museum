## Overview
Mian Muhammad Nawaz Sharif (born 25 December 1949) is a Pakistani politician and businessman who served three times as prime minister of Pakistan — 1990–1993, 1997–1999 and 2013–2017 — the longest-serving prime minister in the country's history, with more than nine years in total, although every term ended in his removal. For decades his name has stood at the centre of Pakistan's public stage and on its screens: the privatiser and motorway-builder of the 1990s, exiled after the 1999 coup, returned to win in 2013, removed by the courts in 2017, and returned from exile again in 2023. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en} {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-ar}

He is the leader of the Pakistan Muslim League (N) and has been a member of the National Assembly since 29 February 2024. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

## Early life and education
Nawaz Sharif was born in Lahore, Punjab, into a Punjabi-speaking Kashmiri family; his father, Muhammad Sharif, was an industrialist who founded the Ittefaq and Sharif groups. Nawaz studied business at Government College and law at the University of Punjab. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en} {cite:wikidata-nawaz-sharif}

## Beginnings and career
Sharif entered politics in 1981, when President Zia ul-Haq appointed him finance minister of Punjab province; he was elected chief minister of Punjab in 1985 and re-elected after the end of martial law in 1988. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

In November 1990 he became Pakistan's twelfth prime minister, heading the conservative Islami Jamhuri Ittihad coalition. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

## Terms in office
His first government built its economy on privatisation and economic liberalisation, reversing the Bhutto-era nationalisations, notably for banks and industries, and legalised foreign-exchange dealing through private money changers. His term ended in 1993 when President Ghulam Ishaq Khan dissolved the National Assembly, and Sharif led the opposition until 1996. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

He returned as prime minister in February 1997. Under his government Pakistan carried out its first nuclear tests, Chagai-I and Chagai-II, on 28 and 30 May 1998 in response to India's tests, and built Pakistan's first major motorway, the M2. On 12 October 1999 General Pervez Musharraf's coup removed him; he was imprisoned and tried, then went into exile in Saudi Arabia under a deal described as lasting ten years. An attempted return in 2007 ended in deportation; he later returned for good and led his party to victory in 2013. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

In his third term he campaigned on ending power cuts and building motorways and major power projects. On 28 July 2017 the Supreme Court disqualified him from holding public office in the Panama Papers case, finding that he had been dishonest, and he stood down as prime minister. In 2018 an accountability court sentenced him to ten years in prison; in 2019 he went to London for medical treatment on bail and was declared an absconder when he did not return. He returned to Pakistan in October 2023, after four years of exile, and the Islamabad High Court acquitted him in the Avenfield and Al-Azizia cases. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section presents differing views as reported in the sources; it is not the museum's verdict. Sharif is a living figure still at the centre of Pakistani politics, so the museum presents the facts attributed to their sources.

**His supporters** credit him with economic liberalisation and privatisation, with building the motorway network beginning with the M2, with the 1998 nuclear tests, and with repeated democratic returns despite coup and exile. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

**His critics** raised corruption charges and questions about his family's industrial wealth; an accountability court convicted him in 2018 before his later acquittal, and the opposition challenged his 2024 victory in NA-130 — the article reports that an examination by the Pattan Development Organisation and an investigation by Geo TV found manipulated vote counts in his favour, claims the sources carry and the museum does not adopt. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

## Personal life
Sharif belongs to a long-established Lahore industrial family whose businesses his father Muhammad Sharif founded. The museum publishes no family detail beyond what understanding his public career needs. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}

## Impact and audience
Across four decades of television, newspapers, exiles and returns, Sharif remained the most prominent face of conservative civilian politics in Pakistan, his name linked in public memory both to development and ambition and to a permanent contest with the military establishment and the judiciary — every one of his three terms ended early. {cite:wikipedia-nawaz-sharif-en}
