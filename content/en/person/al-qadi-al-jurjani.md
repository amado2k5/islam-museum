## Overview
The qadi Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Jurjani was a Shafi'i jurist, man of letters and poet, and Ibn Khallikan gives him an entry in Wafayat al-A'yan {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 278}.

## Life
Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi mentions him in Tabaqat al-Fuqaha' and says he has a diwan of verse {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 278}. Ibn Khallikan quotes al-Tha'alibi in Yatimat al-Dahr as calling him the peerless man of his age, who joined the handwriting of Ibn Muqla to the prose of al-Jahiz and the verse of al-Buhturi, and who as a youth roamed Iraq, Syria and other lands, taking a rich share of the sciences and letters {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 279}. Ibn Khallikan also gives another report, not attributed by name, that his brother Muhammad brought him to Nishapur in 337 AH while he was still a boy below puberty, that the two heard from all the shaykhs, and that he was upright in his judgeship and truthful {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 281}.

## Poetry and works
Ibn Khallikan mentions long, well-known verses of his on self-respect, in which he answers those who called him reserved by saying that he was a man who held back from a place of humiliation {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 278}. Among the verses quoted, he says that people advise him to reach wealth through submission and do not know that submission is itself poverty, that two things stand between him and money and forbid him wealth, namely his proud soul and fate {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 279}, and that when told to roam the earth because provision is wide, he answered that the place of provision is narrow {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 279}. He wrote verses on al-Sahib ibn Abbad, among them lines congratulating him on his recovery {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 280}, and verses saying that he never tasted the sweetness of life until the house and the book became his companions, and that nothing is dearer to him than knowledge {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 280}. Ibn Khallikan describes his verse as abundant and easy in manner, and names his book al-Wasata bayna al-Mutanabbi wa-khusumih, in which he shows great learning and wide reading {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 280-281}.

## Death
Ibn Khallikan quotes al-Hakim Abu Abd Allah Ibn al-Bayyi' in Ta'rikh al-Naysaburiyyin that he died at the end of Safar 366 AH in Nishapur aged seventy-six {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 281}. He gives another report, from someone other than al-Hakim, that he died at Rayy while chief judge in 392 AH, that his coffin was carried to Jurjan and that he was buried there, and then says that what al-Hakim relates is better established and sounder {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 281}. Ibn Khallikan describes Jurjan as a great city in the direction of Khurasan {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 281}. The two reports lie twenty-six years apart; the museum records both and gives no verdict {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 3 p. 281}.
