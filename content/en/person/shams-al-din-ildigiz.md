## Overview
Shams al-Din Eldiguz was a Turkish mamluk from the Qipchaq lands, bought by the Seljuk sultan Mas'ud ibn Muhammad. He began in the sultan's kitchen, then rose through the ranks of the court in Iraq until he was granted the province of Arran in northern Azerbaijan, and he married the sultan's widowed sister-in-law. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 p.90}

## Expansion and the founding of the state
Eldiguz steadily widened his power, seizing most of Azerbaijan together with the Jibal, Hamadan, Isfahan and Ray, until his authority stretched from Tiflis to Makran; in this way he founded the [[polity/ildigizid-atabegs|Atabegate of Azerbaijan]] in 531 AH (1136 CE). His son Muhammad was the effective ruler of Iraq in addition to the governorship of Azerbaijan, and succeeded his father on his death in 568 AH (1172 CE). {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 p.90}

## End of the dynasty
These far-flung territories shrank back to Azerbaijan under the atabeg Abu Bakr Muhammad, who made Tabriz his capital, and they remained under the family until Jalal al-Din Mingburnu, the last Khwarazmshah, took them from Uzbek Pahlavan, its last amir, in 622 AH (1225 CE). {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 p.90}
