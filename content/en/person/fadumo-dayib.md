## Summary

Fadumo Qasim Dayib ⁦(born 1972)⁩ is a Somali public-health expert, development practitioner and social-change activist who in 2016 became the first woman in Somalia's history to stand for the presidency — a candidacy she undertook knowing she had little chance of winning, she said, because someone had to break the precedent. {cite:wikipedia-fadumo-dayib} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 187}

## Early life

She was born in Thika, Kenya, to Somali parents: her mother had lost eleven children to preventable disease before travelling to Kenya, where she met Dayib's father, a truck driver; the family settled in Thika and two more siblings were born there. Her parents were illiterate — her mother an enterprising roadside tea-seller — and Dayib herself did not learn to read and write until she was fourteen. {cite:wikipedia-fadumo-dayib}

The family was expelled from Nairobi in 1989 and deported to Mogadishu, where her mother was briefly imprisoned. In 1990 her mother sold what remained to send the children to safety as refugees in Finland, where Dayib has lived since. {cite:wikipedia-fadumo-dayib}

## Education

In Finland she trained as a critical-care nurse, then took two master's degrees — in public health and related fields at the University of Eastern Finland — and won a fellowship to the Harvard Kennedy School for a degree in public administration. She is a doctoral candidate at the University of Helsinki researching UN Security Council Resolution 1325 on women, peace and security. {cite:wikipedia-fadumo-dayib}

## Career

She worked in the private sector on refugee employment, then for the United Nations: setting up maternal-health clinics in Somalia in 2005 — evacuated when security collapsed — and running HIV-prevention offices and training health workers for the UN in Fiji and Liberia. In 2017 she became, per the sources, the first Somali woman to hold a country-director post in the UN in Somalia. {cite:wikipedia-fadumo-dayib}

## The candidacy

She declared in 2014 her intention to run in what was billed as Somalia's first democratic election since 1967. In the indirect poll of November 2016 she stood as the only woman among eighteen candidates, facing death threats for her decision. Her platform, as reported: anti-corruption measures, an end to female genital mutilation, dismantling the '4.5' clan-based quota system of governance, and opening dialogue with the armed insurgency to stop the killing of Somalis. She said her real chance was small — corruption and the clan system — but that a woman had to stand. {cite:wikipedia-fadumo-dayib}

## Assessment

Dayib's listing reflects the layered work she represents: refugee to nurse to UN official to presidential candidate, carrying a public-health frame into politics. She continues, per the sources, to live in Nairobi and to advocate for women's participation in peacebuilding. {cite:wikipedia-fadumo-dayib} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 187}
