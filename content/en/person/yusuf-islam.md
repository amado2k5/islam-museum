## Overview
Yusuf Islam — born Steven Demetre Georgiou in Marylebone, London, on 21 July 1948, famous to the world as Cat Stevens — is the most widely known convert to Islam of the pop age: the singer of "Matthew and Son", "Wild World", "Peace Train", "Father and Son" and "Morning Has Broken", who sold tens of millions of records, then read a Qur'an given him by his brother and took the shahada in December 1977 {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-islam-en}.

## The life before Islam
His father Stavros was a Greek Cypriot who ran a restaurant in the West End, his mother Ingrid Swedish; the boy grew up above the shop in Soho, went to a Catholic school and taught himself guitar on the house piano. He broke through at eighteen with "I Love My Dog" (1966), survived a year of tuberculosis in 1968 that pulled him back from the teen-idol machine, and remade himself as a confessional songwriter: Tea for the Tillerman (1970), Teaser and the Firecat (1971) and Catch Bull at Four (1972) made him one of the best-selling musicians on earth {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-islam-en}.

## The turn
Two events of 1976-77 turned him. Swimming off Malibu he was carried out by the current and, in his own account, called on God to save him and was carried back to shore — the moment he later named as his vow {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-islam-en}. Then his elder brother David, back from Jerusalem, gave him a Qur'an as a birthday gift; reading it, he said, he recognised the book that explained everything he had been looking for through Buddhism, Zen, numerology and the rest. He entered Islam in 1977 and took the name Yusuf — the Joseph of the Qur'an who was thrown in the river and drawn out again, the parallel he chose for himself {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-islam-en}.

## After the shahada
He auctioned his guitars, left the charts, married Fauzia Mubarak Ali in 1979 and raised five children in London; he founded the Islamia Primary School in Brent (1983) — the first Muslim school in Britain to win state funding — and later a girls' secondary school and the charity Small Kindness {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-islam-en}. From the mid-1990s he recorded again, first voice-and-drum Islamic albums (The Life of the Last Prophet, A Is for Allah), then fully instrumented records: An Other Cup (2006), Roadsinger (2009), Tell 'Em I'm Gone (2014), The Laughing Apple (2017) and King of a Land (2023), inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2014 {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-islam-en}.

## The storms
The record also keeps the hard chapters: the 1989 remarks on the Salman Rushdie affair that he has spent decades explaining as a student's ill-judged citation of the classical penalty; the 2004 refusal of entry to the United States when his name matched a watch-list spelling, which ended with him received as a guest of honour years later; and the libel suits he won against British papers that had printed he supported terrorism {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-islam-en}.

## For young visitors
A very famous pop singer named Cat Stevens almost drowned in the sea, was given a Qur'an by his brother, became a Muslim named Yusuf, built a Muslim school in London — and after many years started singing again.
