## Overview
Amr ibn Marzuq al-Bahili, a client of the tribe, of Basra, Abu Uthman, is called by al-Dhahabi the shaykh and imam, chain-bearer of Basra, and was born a little after the year 130 {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}. The sign printed with his entry shows that al-Bukhari recorded him paired with another narrator and that Abu Dawud recorded him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Shu'ba, Hammad ibn Salama, Abd al-Rahman al-Mas'udi, Hammad ibn Zayd and others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}. Al-Bukhari narrated from him in his Sahih paired with another, Abu Dawud in his Sunan, where he is among his senior shaykhs, and Harb al-Kirmani, Abu Zur'a, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Asim, Abu Muslim al-Kajji, Abu Khalifa al-Jumahi and many others did too {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}. It is said he held three thousand hadith from Shu'ba {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}.

## What the critics said
Al-Qawariri says that Yahya al-Qattan did not approve of Amr ibn Marzuq {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}. Ali ibn al-Madini said they abandoned the hadith of the two Fahds and the two Amrs, among whom he meant Amr ibn Marzuq {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}. On the other side Yahya ibn Ma'in praised him, saying reliable and trusted, a man of the raids, the Qur'an and merit; Ahmad ibn Hanbal, told that Ali had weakened him, said he was a righteous man, that Affan approved of him, and that he was a man of the raids and of good; and Abu Hatim said he was reliable and among the devout, and that he found no one among Shu'ba's students with better hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}. Muslim ibn Ibrahim reports that the books of Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi passed to Amr ibn Marzuq, who went raiding by sea {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}.

## Reports about him
Al-Nasa'i records in the Kuna that Bundar heard Amr asked whether he had married a thousand women, and he answered more than a thousand {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}. Muhammad ibn Isa ibn Abi Qamash saw his head and beard red with henna {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}.

## His death
He died at Basra in Safar 224 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2972}.
