Fuat Sezgin was born on 24 October 1924 at Bitlis and came to scholarship through Istanbul University's Institute of Oriental Studies, where the German orientalist Hellmut Ritter turned him toward the history of science in the Islamic world{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-en}{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-tr}. His 1950 doctorate, Buhari'nin Kaynakları, argued against the European consensus that al-Bukhari's hadith collection rested on oral transmission reaching back only a few generations: the written sources, he showed, went back to the seventh century{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-en}. Appointed associate professor in 1954, he was dismissed in the purge that followed the 1960 coup — one of the academics remembered as the "147s" — and left Turkey with a single suitcase{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-tr}.

Sezgin rebuilt his career at Frankfurt, becoming professor in 1965, and in 1982 founded the Institute for the History of the Arab-Islamic Sciences, adding a museum of more than eight hundred replicas of historical instruments, tools and maps; a sister museum opened beside Gülhane Park in Istanbul in 2008{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-en}. His seventeen-volume Geschichte des arabischen Schrifttums carried Brockelmann's history of the Arabic written tradition across the whole field of Islamic learning{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-en}{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-de}. He died at Istanbul on 30 June 2018{cite:wikipedia-sezgin-tr}{cite:wikidata-sezgin}.
