## Summary
Abu Ali Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ya'qub Miskawayh al-Razi was a moral philosopher and historian, author of [[work/tajarib-al-umam|Tajarib al-umam]] and Tahdhib al-akhlaq. He lived in the Buyid age, was boon companion of the vizier [[person/al-muhallabi-al-wazir|al-Muhallabi]], and keeper of the libraries of [[person/ibn-al-umayd|Ibn al-Amid]] and [[person/adud-al-dawla|Adud al-Dawla]]. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.12} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.32}

## Little recorded of his life
The editor of Tajarib al-umam, Abu al-Qasim Imami, says the early sources give no detailed account of Miskawayh's life, only scattered passages, and that his contemporary [[person/abu-sulayman-al-sijistani|Abu Sulayman al-Mantiqi al-Sijistani]] promised in Siwan al-hikma a separate treatise on what he had heard of his life and seen of his conduct; that treatise has not come down. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.7}

## Birth and death
The editor says Miskawayh lived about a hundred years, from 320 AH "most probably" to 9 Safar 421 AH, the latter as Yaqut reports from Yahya ibn Manda. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.11} He notes that Margoliouth provisionally put his birth at 330 AH "or a little earlier", Izzat moved it back to 325 and Badawi to 320, "if not earlier". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.11} The editor argues from Miskawayh's statements that al-Muhallabi told him most of what happened in his time "through long companionship and frequent sittings", and that at Mu'izz al-Dawla's gatherings "I was his boon companion at the time"; al-Muhallabi became Mu'izz al-Dawla's secretary in 339 and died in 352, so in the editor's view Miskawayh cannot have been born after 320. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.11} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.12} He also mentions verses in which Miskawayh complains of old age and of reaching extreme old age. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.12}

## His name
The editor discusses the form of the name and holds that "Miskawayh" was Ahmad's own byname, not his father's, relying on Yaqut's heading "Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ya'qub, called Miskawayh" with "called" read in the nominative as Margoliouth did, and on al-Rudhrawari, Ibn Abi Usaybi'a, al-Qifti and Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, who call him "Miskawayh" without "Ibn". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.15}

## His titles
His biographers describe him as sage, theologian, philosopher, moralist, historian, mathematician, geometer, philologist, man of letters, poet and secretary; among his bynames were "al-Khazin" (the librarian) and "al-Nadim" (the companion), and he was called "the Third Teacher", said to be for his part in rebuilding the practical branch of Greek philosophy, moral philosophy. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.19}

## In Buyid service
Miskawayh kept the libraries of Ibn al-Amid, of his son Abu al-Fath and of Adud al-Dawla, and his companionship with Ibn al-Amid lasted seven years, as he himself says. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.32} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.315} Among the events of 355 AH he relates that Ibn al-Amid's library was in his charge when the Khurasani ghazis plundered his house at Rayy, that it alone of all his stores was spared, and that Ibn al-Amid told him: "I bear witness that you are a man of good fortune". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.262} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.263} He says he was with Rukn al-Dawla when he made his way out of his palace that day. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.264} See [[event/khurasani-ghazis-at-rayy-355|the Khurasani ghazis at Rayy]].

## His works
The editor lists his works, among them: Tartib al-sa'adat wa-manazil al-ulum, on the ranks of happiness and of the sciences after the school of Aristotle; al-Fawz al-asghar, also called "the answer to the three questions"; al-Hawamil wa-l-shawamil, his answers to Abu Hayyan al-Tawhidi's questions; Tahdhib al-akhlaq, which Nasir al-Din al-Tusi rendered into Persian as Akhlaq-i Nasiri; and Jawidan khirad. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.19} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.20} He also wrote a book on "the states of the sages and the qualities of the former prophets". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.28}

## History in his view
In the preface to Tajarib al-umam he makes the aim of history that what a person retains of past events should become "as if experiences of his own", because "the affairs of the world are alike, and its conditions correspond". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.48} He says that most of what he relates after 340 AH is seen and witnessed, or verified reports from Ibn al-Amid and al-Muhallabi. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.34}
