## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Yahya ibn Abd al-Wahid al-Hafsi al-Hintati, Abu Ishaq, was in al-Zirikli's words the commander of the faithful in Tunis and the lands of Ifriqiya. Al-Zirikli gives his death as 682 AH, 1283 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 79–80}.

## Career
Before he took power he lived in al-Andalus; news reached him of the death of his brother al-Mustansir, Muhammad ibn Yahya, ruler of Tunis and its surroundings. He crossed the sea, reached Tlemcen and took Bejaia, then overcame the garrison of Tunis, which had sworn allegiance to Yahya ibn al-Mustansir, styled al-Wathiq bi-llah. When al-Wathiq learned how strong his uncle had become he stood down, Ibrahim entered Tunis and the oath of allegiance was completed in 678 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 79–80}. Sensing danger from his deposed nephew, he killed him and three of his sons {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 79–80}.

In his days the rebel Ibn Abi Umara, that is Ahmad ibn Marzuq, appeared and grew strong. Ibrahim marched out against him but was let down before the encounter when his entourage turned on him; he withdrew to Bejaia and handed his office to a son called Abu Faris, his governor of Bejaia, who took the title al-Mu'tamad. The son advanced to fight the rebel and the rebel killed him in 682 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 79–80}. When the news reached Ibrahim he fled to Tlemcen, but followers of Ibn Abi Umara overtook him and brought him to Bejaia and sent word to their leader, who ordered his killing; he was killed in Bejaia {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 pp. 79–80}.
