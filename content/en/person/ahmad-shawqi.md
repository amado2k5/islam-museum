## Summary
Egyptian poet (1868–1932), titled 'Prince of Poets' (Amir al-Shu'ara'), among the greatest Arabic poets of the modern age. His verse is collected in Al-Shawqiyyat; he founded Arabic verse drama with plays such as The Fall of Cleopatra, Majnun Laila, Ali Bey al-Kabir and Cambyses, and is known for panegyrics, patriotic poems and his laments for al-Andalus. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shawqi}

## Life and career

Ahmad Shawqi was born in Cairo in 1868 into a family mixing Egyptian, Turkish, Kurdish and Greek stock, raised close to the Khedive's court, studied law in Egypt and was sent by Khedive Abbas II to study in France. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shawqi}

Returning from Paris he was appointed to the Khedival diwan as court poet, then the British deposed and exiled him to Spain in 1914 after he refused cooperation with the occupation; in exile he wrote some of his most famous poems. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shawqi}

## Poetry and drama

He issued Al-Shawqiyyat in four volumes gathering his panegyrics, patriotic, social and elegiac verse and his historical and Islamic poems; among the most famous is his ode on al-Andalus and his poems of the 1919 Egyptian revolution. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shawqi}

He created the form of Arabic verse drama with The Fall of Cleopatra, Majnun Laila, Cambyses, Antara, Ali Bey al-Kabir and Princess Dalal, and also wrote historical prose works and the epistolary Aswaq al-Dhahab. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shawqi}

## Legacy

In 1927 the Arab poets gathered at an Egyptian ceremony and proclaimed him 'Prince of Poets', a title that has stayed since; his Nile-side palace later became a museum bearing his name. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shawqi}

He died in Cairo in 1932; with Hafez Ibrahim he marks modern classical poetry in Egypt, influenced generations of poets, and his plays are still taught and his poems still sung. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shawqi}
