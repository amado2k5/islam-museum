## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Ali ibn Uthman ibn Ya'qub al-Marini, Abu Salim, the sultan al-Musta'in billah, was one of the Marinid kings of the far Maghrib, of the Banu Abd al-Haqq. The entry gives his death as 762 AH, 1361 CE, and gives no birth year {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}.

## Coming to the throne
His brother Abu Inan Faris had sent him to al-Andalus, where he stayed until Abu Inan died and the allegiance was pledged to Abu Inan's young son Abu Bakr al-Sa'id billah. Abu Salim then sailed to the coast of Ghumara and called on the people of the Maghrib to pledge to him, and they came to him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}. A vizier named Hasan ibn Umar al-Fududi was running Abu Bakr's kingdom; he deposed his master and received Abu Salim with a pledge, in 760 AH, and Abu Salim settled in New Fez {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}.

## His reign
Among his men was the famous historian Ibn Khaldun, whom he made keeper of his signature and of his secret correspondence {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}. Distrusting Hasan al-Fududi, he gave him Marrakesh to send him far away; al-Fududi left Marrakesh and joined Tadla in revolt with a band of the Banu Jusham, and the sultan sent men who brought him back, paraded him and then killed him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}. The sultan marched on Tlemcen, took it and subdued the Banu Zayyan, then decided to live in the old kasbah of Fez and left one of his viziers, Umar ibn Abdallah al-Fududi, in charge of New Fez {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}.

## His overthrow and death
Umar al-Fududi bore grudges against the sultan, and agreed with the commander of the Christian soldiers, named Garcia son of Anatole, to depose him. They took a Marinid simpleton named Tashfin, one of the sons of the sultan Ali ibn Uthman, and dressed him in the insignia of kingship. Umar declared revolt against Abu Salim and allegiance to Tashfin, the soldiers plundered the treasury and disorder spread through the city {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}. Abu Salim came to enter and could not, and his men dispersed; he changed his clothes and took refuge in the valley of Warigha, where some of al-Fududi's men recognised him and seized him. Al-Fududi ordered him killed, and his head was brought to him in a nosebag {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}.

## Ibn al-Khatib's verdict
Al-Zirikli quotes Lisan al-Din Ibn al-Khatib: the sultan Abu Salim was the remnant of the Marinid house and the last of his people in gentleness, modesty and distance from evil. His reign, the entry says, lasted two years, three months and five days {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 36}.
