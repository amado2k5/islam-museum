## In brief
Yahya b. Sa'id b. Qays b. Amr b. Sahl al-Najjari al-Ansari of Medina, Abu Sa'id — also said Abu Nasr — the judge; al-Hakim says he was judge of the Prophet's sanctuary-city and its mufti in his age, a pupil of the Seven Fuqaha and the scholar of the Medinans of his day. Born before 70 in Ibn al-Zubayr's time; his grandfather Qays b. Amr was a Companion with a hadith in the Sunan on the two rak'as of dawn. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.4169}

## His transmission
He heard from Anas b. Malik, al-Sa'ib b. Yazid, Abu Umama b. Sahl, Abdallah b. Amir b. Rabi'a, and Yusuf b. Abdallah b. Salam, and sat with the Seven Fuqaha and those after them; al-Zuhri narrated from him despite his seniority, as did Malik, Shu'ba, al-Thawri, al-Awza'i, Hammad b. Salama, Hammad b. Zayd, al-Layth b. Sa'd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn Uluyya, Ibn Uyayna, the judge Abu Yusuf, and many more. He is the source of the hadith "actions are by intentions", reported from him — it is said — by nearly two hundred transmitters. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.4169-4170}

## His judgeship and death
Abu Ja'far al-Mansur appointed him judge of Medina at al-Hashimiyya; in the anecdote related by Sulayman b. Bilal, Yahya enlisted him over his household and taught him how to sit between two litigants. Al-Shirazi reports through Hammad b. Zayd that Ayyub al-Sikhtiyani said none more learned in fiqh than Yahya b. Sa'id was left behind. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981} He died at al-Hashimiyya in 143 AH while judge there for Abu Ja'far, as Ibn Sa'd states through al-Waqidi. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.4170}

The death year is fixed by Ibn Sa'd through al-Waqidi; he lived in [[place/medina|Medina]].
