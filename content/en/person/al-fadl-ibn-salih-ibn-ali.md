## Overview
Al-Fadl ibn Salih ibn Ali ibn Abdallah ibn al-Abbas al-Hashimi al-Abbasi, Abu al-Abbas, was an Abbasid amir. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 122 AH, 740 CE, and his death as 172 AH, which he renders as 788 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 149}.

## Command and governorships
[[person/al-mansur|Al-Mansur]] appointed him to lead the pilgrimage in 138 AH. He was named governor of Egypt for [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] at the end of 168 AH while he was in Iraq; al-Mahdi died early in 169 AH before al-Fadl set out, and al-Mahdi's son [[person/al-hadi-musa|al-Hadi]] confirmed him. He went to Egypt, whose affairs were unsettled, brought the rebels under control and killed their leader Dihya ibn Musab al-Umawi; he had barely settled when the post arrived with his dismissal, so his term lasted less than a year {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 149}.

He also governed Damascus, where he restored the gates of its congregational mosque and the dome in the mosque courtyard {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 149}.

## Character
Al-Zirikli describes him as one of the bravest amirs and as an eloquent poet and man of letters {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 149}.
