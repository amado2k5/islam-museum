## Summary
Adibul Hasan Rizvi is a Pakistani urologist who founded in 1970 the Sindh Institute of Urology and Transplantation (SIUT) in Karachi and turned it into the country's largest healthcare institution treating patients free of cost or at symbolic charge — an emblem of charitable medicine in the Muslim world. {cite:wikipedia-adibul-hasan-rizvi}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1938 in Jabalpur in British India (now Madhya Pradesh), and his family migrated to Pakistan after Partition. He studied medicine at Dow Medical College in Karachi, then specialised in surgery and urology in Britain, returning to Pakistan despite offers of work in the West. {cite:wikipedia-adibul-hasan-rizvi}

## Founding SIUT
He began in 1970 with a small eight-bed urology unit inside a government hospital in Karachi and, over five decades, built it into an integrated medical complex of hundreds of beds, dialysis units and one of Asia's largest organ-transplant programmes, with branches in other cities — all under one fixed principle: no patient is turned away for inability to pay, and treatment is free for those in need. The institute has performed millions of dialysis sessions and thousands of transplants, and is credited with the largest free dialysis service in the world. {cite:wikipedia-adibul-hasan-rizvi} {cite:muslim500-adibul-hasan-rizvi}

## Philosophy and expansion
In interviews Rizvi describes his model as resting on the conviction that health is a right, not a commodity; SIUT is funded by local and international donations and partial government support without abandoning free care for the poor. The institute has trained generations of urologists, anaesthetists and nurses and runs schools and community health-awareness programmes. {cite:wikipedia-adibul-hasan-rizvi}

## Recognition
He has received Pakistan's top civil honours including the Hilal-i-Imtiaz and Sitara-i-Imtiaz, the 1998 Ramon Magsaysay Award for public service, the Sheikh Hamdan Award for Medical Sciences in the Middle East, and honorary doctorates; Pakistani and international press describe him as the "doctor of the poor". {cite:wikipedia-adibul-hasan-rizvi}

## Legacy
Rizvi transformed himself from surgeon into a whole national institution: the SIUT model is now studied in public-health administration in developing countries, and world medical literature holds that he proved high-quality medicine possible without profit in a resource-poor country. He still leads the institute he built, into advanced age, and remains the most present Pakistani physician in the charitable public memory. {cite:wikipedia-adibul-hasan-rizvi}
