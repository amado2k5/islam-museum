## Overview
Izz al-Din Aybak al-Turkmani, whose title was al-Malik al-Mu'izz, deputy of [[person/shajar-al-durr|Shajar al-Durr]] and then sultan of Egypt. According to al-Suyuti he became independent in the sultanate in Rabi' al-Akhir 648 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.709-710}

## Rule
He then had the soldiers swear allegiance to al-Malik al-Ashraf, who was eight years old, and Aybak remained his atabeg. He then abolished the child's name in 652 AH and became independent. Shajar al-Durr, his wife, killed him in 655 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.709-710}

## According to al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that Aybak al-Turkmani was the first of the Mamluks to rule the kingdom of Egypt, and that he became independent in 650 AH. This is two years later than al-Suyuti's date for his independence {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1080}.

He says that Aqtay al-Jamdar, one of the senior Bahri emirs, rivalled him and sent to him to leave the Citadel and hand it over {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1080}.

He says that the Bahri Mamluks stayed in Syria after Aybak was killed, until they returned to Egypt in the days of Qutuz {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1081}.
