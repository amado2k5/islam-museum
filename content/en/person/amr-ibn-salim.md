## Summary
A man of [[group/banu-kab-ibn-khuzaa|Banu Ka'b]] of [[group/khuzaa|Khuza'a]] who stood before the Prophet in [[place/medina|Medina]]'s mosque adjuring him by the fathers' pact after [[event/khuzaa-bakr-clash-al-watir|the aggression at al-Watir]]; his verses — in Ibn Ishaq's words — were "among what stirred the conquest of Mecca" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/36}.

## The appeal
Amr ibn Salim stood in the mosque reciting the verses of his famous [[work/amr-ibn-salim-istighatha|appeal]], among them: "O Lord, I adjure Muhammad / by our fathers' and his father's old pact", asking aid for Banu Ka'b and describing how they were surprised at al-Watir and killed bowing and prostrating {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/36}.

The Prophet answered him: "you are aided, Amr ibn Salim" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/37}. In al-Isti'ab's account: "I am not aided if I do not aid Banu Ka'b ibn Abd Manaf and Abd al-Wulud as I would aid them" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/37}. It is related that when Banu Ka'b afterwards entered the mosque they saw a cloud moving, and were told: it is for Banu Ka'b {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/37}.

## For young visitors
He was a poet of a tribe attacked at night while at peace. He came to Medina and stood reciting verse begging for aid, and the Prophet answered him with a single word: "aided".
