Zinda Kaul (August 1884 – 1965), known to generations of students as Masterji, was a Kashmiri poet, writer and teacher. Born at Habbakadal in Srinagar into a Kashmiri Pandit family, he taught in schools for years, worked as a clerk, and retired in 1939 as a translator in the Kashmir publicity office.{cite:wikipedia-zinda-kaul-en} Braj B. Kachru's profile styles him Pandit Zinda Koul and records the same arc from classroom to clerkship.{cite:koshur-zinda-koul}

He composed first in Persian, Hindi and Urdu — his first poem was recited at a Sanatan Dharm Sabha meeting in Srinagar in 1896 — and turned to Kashmiri only in 1942.{cite:wikipedia-zinda-kaul-en} His devotional collection Sumran won the Sahitya Akademi Award in 1956, making him the first Kashmiri poet so honoured, and he translated the mystic poet Nand Ram Parmanand into English in three volumes.{cite:wikipedia-zinda-kaul-en}{cite:saarang-zinda-koul} He died at Jammu in the winter of 1965.{cite:wikipedia-zinda-kaul-en}
