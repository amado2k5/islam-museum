## Overview
Abu 'Abdallah Muhammad b. 'Abdallah b. Muhammad b. Hamduya al-Dabbi al-Tahmani of Nishapur, the hafiz known as Ibn al-Bayyi'; he learned fiqh on al-Shafi'i's madhhab then sought hadith until it mastered him and he was famed for it — he heard it from a company too numerous to count, his mu'jam of shaykhs nearing two thousand men. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His works
He wrote in its sciences what reached fifteen hundred parts; among his singular works, al-Mustadrak 'ala'l-sahihayn, Tarikh 'ulama' Naysabur, al-Madkhal ila 'ilm al-sahih and Fada'il al-imam al-Shafi'i. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Quest and journeys
Born on Monday 3 Rabi' I 321 at Nishapur, he sought knowledge from childhood under his father and uncle: first hearing in 330, mustamli to Ibn Hibban in 334, the journey to Iraq in 341 a few months after Isma'il al-Saffar's death, the hajj and the crossing of Khurasan and Transoxiana — about a thousand shaykhs at Nishapur alone and a thousand more elsewhere. He kept the special company of Imam Abu Bakr al-Sibghi, who left him the management of his school Dar al-Sunna and its endowments. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 122-128}

## Standing and death
'Abd al-Ghafir al-Farisi called him the imam of the hadith folk of his age and its truest connoisseur; al-Daraqutni, al-Bayhaqi, al-Qushayri and Abu Ya'la al-Khalili transmitted from him. His Mustadrak drew criticism, yet al-Dhahabi counted him shaykh al-Islam and the hafiz of the community. He died on Tuesday 3 Safar 405, the qadi Abu Bakr al-Jiri praying over him. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 123-131} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 pp. 90-91}

## Critics' praise, as al-Wadi'i gathers it
In his introduction to the *Mustadrak*, [[person/muqbil-ibn-hadi-al-wadii|Muqbil al-Wadi'i]] collects what the imams said of him. Al-Dhahabi, in *Siyar A'lam al-Nubala'*, calls him the imam, the hafiz, the critic, the learned master, the shaykh of the hadith scholars, and says that he compiled, extracted, impugned, vouched, authenticated and identified defects, "one of the oceans of knowledge, with a slight Shi'i leaning in him". {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 6}

He quotes al-Khalil ibn 'Abd Allah: al-Hakim made two journeys to Iraq and the Hijaz, the second in 368; he debated [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]], who was satisfied with him; and he was trustworthy and widely learned, his works approaching five hundred parts. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 6} Al-Hakim told al-Khalil that every town of Khurasan and Transoxiana had a history written by one of its scholars except Nishapur, which led him to write the *History of the Naysaburis*; and he compiled for Abu 'Ali ibn Simjur a book on the days, wives and hadiths of the Prophet called *al-Iklil*. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 6-7}

Abu Hazim al-'Abdawi reports al-Hakim as saying: "I drank Zamzam water and asked God to grant me skill in compiling." {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 7} When Sa'd ibn 'Ali al-Zanjani was asked about four contemporary hadith masters, al-Daraqutni in Baghdad, 'Abd al-Ghani in Egypt, Ibn Manda in Isfahan and al-Hakim in Nishapur, he said that al-Hakim was the best compiler of them. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 7}

## What was held against him
Al-Wadi'i lists among the charges the weakening of his memory in old age, and Shi'i leanings. Al-Khatib said he inclined to Shi'ism; al-Dhahabi quotes Abu Isma'il al-Harawi calling him a Rafidi and rejects it: he was not a Rafidi but had Shi'i leanings, and in *al-Mizan*, "the man is no Rafidi, only a Shi'i". {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 8} Al-Dhahabi also quotes Ibn Tahir as saying that he professed the Sunni position on the precedence and caliphate of the first caliphs, and was hostile to Mu'awiya. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 8} On the criticism of his book, see [[work/al-mustadrak-ala-al-sahihayn|*al-Mustadrak 'ala al-Sahihayn*]].

## Dictating the Mustadrak
Al-Hakim dictated the preface of the *Mustadrak* on Monday 7 Muharram 373 AH. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 39}
