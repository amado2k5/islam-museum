## Overview
Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Ali ibn Qays ibn al-Mukhtar al-Kurdani, known as Ibn Wahshiyya, from Janbula and Qaysin. Ibn al-Nadim counts him among the eloquent Nabateans who spoke the language of the Chaldeans. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.497}

## His books
Ibn al-Nadim attributes to him in the craft of alchemy the *Great Book of Principles*, the *Small Book of Principles*, *al-Mudarraja* (The Ladder), *al-Madhakirat fi al-San'a* (Discussions on the Craft), and a book containing twenty books. He says that Ibn Wahshiyya had a share in magic, sleight of hand and incantations. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.504-505}

## The pens
Ibn Wahshiyya mentions the copy of pens with which books of the craft and of magic are written. Ibn al-Nadim read it in his own hand, and read a copy of it in the hand of Abu al-Hasan ibn al-Kufi. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.504-505}

## On the reports of magicians
The *Fihrist* in its reports on magicians says that he claimed to be a magician who performed the works of talismans. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.433} The description rests on a single source, and those with expertise are the ones who should weigh its value.
