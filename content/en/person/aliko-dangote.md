## Summary
Aliko Dangote is a Nigerian industrialist, founder and chairman of the Dangote Group — West Africa's largest conglomerate — and, by most rankings, Africa's richest man for more than a decade running. His empire's logic is substitution: manufacture in Nigeria what Nigerians import. {cite:wikipedia-aliko-dangote}

## Formation
Born in 1957 in Kano, in northern Nigeria, into a merchant family — his great-grandfather was a commodities trader — Dangote studied business at Al-Azhar University in Cairo, then built a trading firm importing cement, sugar, rice and flour. From the 1990s he moved the group upstream into manufacturing the same goods. {cite:wikipedia-aliko-dangote}

## The Dangote Group
Dangote Cement became the largest cement producer in Africa, with plants across the continent; the group's sugar refinery is among the largest in the world; and its salt, flour, packaging and fertiliser businesses supply West African markets. Its defining project, the Dangote Refinery near Lagos — a 650,000-barrel-per-day plant that began producing fuels in 2024 — is the largest single-train refinery in the world and aims to end Nigeria's decades of importing refined petroleum. {cite:muslim500-aliko-dangote}

## Philanthropy and public roles
Through the Aliko Dangote Foundation he is among Africa's largest private donors to health and education — polio eradication, nutrition, university endowments — and has chaired national business councils and served on international advisory boards. {cite:wikipedia-aliko-dangote}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as the emblematic industrialist of the African Muslim world: a Kano trader's descendant who built, rather than traded, the infrastructure of a national economy — and whose refinery redrew the energy map of West Africa. {cite:muslim500-aliko-dangote}
