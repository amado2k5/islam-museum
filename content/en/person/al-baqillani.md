## Summary
Qadi Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn al-Tayyib ibn Muhammad ibn Ja'far al-Baqillani al-Basri, one of the best-known theologians of the Ash'ari school and one of its great systematisers; he settled in Baghdad and died there on 23 Dhu'l-Qa'da 403 AH (5 June 1013) {cite:wafayat-ayan}.

## Ibn Khallikan's notice
Ibn Khallikan describes al-Baqillani as a defender of the Ash'ari doctrine and path who settled in Baghdad and wrote many famous works in kalam and other fields; he was unmatched in his age, headship of the school devolved on him, he was noted for acute inference and quick reply, he transmitted hadith, and he was famous for the length of his disputations {cite:wafayat-ayan}.

## Death
He died on a Saturday evening and was buried on Sunday, seven days before the end of Dhu'l-Qa'da 403 AH in Baghdad, first in his house in Darb al-Majus and later reburied in the Bab Harb cemetery {cite:wafayat-ayan}.

## The theologian and diplomat
McCarthy's entry in the Encyclopaedia of Islam records that the date of al-Baqillani's birth is unknown, that he was born in Basra and spent most of his adult life in Baghdad, that visits to Shiraz and to the Byzantine court are mentioned — in 369/980 he carried Adud al-Dawla's reply to a Byzantine embassy by his own hand — and that for a time he exercised the office of qadi outside the capital {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 233, 1018}. The qadi Iyad listed fifty-two of his works, of which six are extant: the I'jaz al-Qur'an, a classic on its subject; the Tamhid, the earliest complete manual of theological polemic; al-Insaf; the Manaqib on the Imamate; the Intisar on the textual integrity of the Qur'an; and al-Bayan on the apologetic miracle {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1018}.

## For young visitors
Al-Baqillani was a major fourth-century theologian of the Ash'ari school who wrote many books and died in Baghdad in 403 AH.
