## In brief
Mutarrif b. Abdallah b. al-Shikhkhir, the imam Abu Abdallah al-Amiri al-Harashi of Basra; he was a chief in learning and practice, a figure of standing in Islam with presence in men's hearts. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.64}

## Transmission and standing
He narrated from his father and from Ali, Ammar, Imran b. Husayn, A'isha, Iyad b. Himar, and Abdallah b. Mughaffal al-Muzani; his brother Yazid Abu al-Ala', Thabit b. Aslam al-Bunani, Sa'id al-Jariri, Qatada, Ghaylan b. Jarir, Muhammad b. Wasi', and a group narrated from him. Ibn Sa'd called him "reliable, distinguished, pious, intelligent, and mannered", and Ahmad b. Hanbal said that in Basra only Mutarrif b. al-Shikhkhir and Ibn Sirin escaped the trial of Ibn al-Ash'ath. Ghaylan b. Jarir relates that a man lied about him and Mutarrif prayed "O God, if it is a lie, then raise him dead from his place". {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.64-65}

## His death
He was a chiefly man of great rank who wore fine clothes, rode horses, and entered upon rulers; he died in 95 AH. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.65}
