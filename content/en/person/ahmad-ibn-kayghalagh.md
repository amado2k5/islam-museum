## Overview
Ahmad ibn Ibrahim ibn Kayghalagh, Abu al-Abbas, was an emir of the Abbasid era, of Turkish origin, born and raised in Baghdad, who rose to the rank of commanders {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}. The entry gives his years as about 258 to after 323 AH, about 872 to 935 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}.

## Commands and governorships
He served with Muhammad ibn Sulayman in the fighting against the Qaramita in Syria in 283 AH, which al-Zirikli places in the reign of al-Muktafi {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}. He came to Egypt in 292 and 302 with some of al-Muktafi's armies to put down revolts there, was emir of Damascus and Jordan in 300 and settled in Baghdad in 303 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}. Al-Muqtadir gave him the emirate of Egypt in a year the entry prints as 300; he stayed about seven months, unrest rose against him and he was removed, and he was given Isfahan in 319 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}. The Abbasid al-Qahir returned him to Egypt in 321; he entered it in 322 and his emirate lasted about twenty-one months, until Muhammad ibn Tughj opposed him and he handed the emirate over without a fight; he was dismissed in 323 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}.

## Letters
Al-Zirikli quotes al-Tha'alibi in «al-Yatima»: Ahmad ibn Kayghalagh was of the sons of the emirs of Syria, a poet and littérateur, and al-Tha'alibi cites some delicate verses of his {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}.
