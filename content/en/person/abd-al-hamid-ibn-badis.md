## Overview
Abd al-Hamid ibn Muhammad al-Mustafa ibn Badis was an Algerian scholar and leader of the reform movement. He was born in Constantine in 1889 CE (1307 AH) and died in 1940 CE (1359 AH). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba j.3 p.53}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam calls Ibn Badis, who died in 1940, an Islamic reformer and national leader who headed the Association of Algerian Ulama. He was educated in the traditional way in Tunis, it says, and went back to Algeria as a teacher. He founded a weekly newspaper, The Critic, which spread Salafi ideas and attacked Sufi practices it labelled "un-Islamic". In 1931 he and other clerics formed the Association, which stood for an Algerian nation rooted in its Arab and Islamic heritage, for the renewal and reform of Islam, and against the Sufi orders and the assimilationists. The Dictionary adds that he put the decline of Muslims down to weakness from within, division, tyranny and the taking up of non-Islamic customs, and stressed education as the means of cleansing Islam of popular accretions and bettering the individual, as the first move toward a revival of society overall. Offering a modernist reading of the Quran, it says, his chief contribution was to tie reform and education to the advancement of Algerian nationalism. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e933}
