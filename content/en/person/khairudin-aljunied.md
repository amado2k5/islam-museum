## Summary
Syed Muhd Khairudin Aljunied is a Singaporean historian specialising in the intellectual and social history of the Malay world — Muslim Southeast Asia. He is a tenured associate professor at the National University of Singapore and a senior fellow at Georgetown University's Alwaleed Bin Talal Center for Muslim-Christian Understanding, where he previously held the Malaysia Chair of Islam in Southeast Asia. {cite:georgetown-khairudin-aljunied}

## Education
Aljunied took his BA and MA in history at the National University of Singapore, completing the MA in 2003, and earned his doctorate at the School of Oriental and African Studies in London in 2008. His doctoral training in London joined a family heritage — he is descended from the Aljunied family of Arab traders and scholars prominent in Singapore's history — with the modern discipline of area studies. {cite:georgetown-khairudin-aljunied}

## Career
He joined the NUS history department as assistant professor in 2008 and was tenured as associate professor in 2013. Alongside NUS he held a Fulbright visiting professorship at Columbia University in 2013, was appointed professor and Malaysia Chair of Islam in Southeast Asia at Georgetown's School of Foreign Service for 2017–2018, remaining a senior fellow there, and held visiting professorships at the University of Brunei Darussalam (2021–2022) and the University of Malaya (2022–2023). He co-edits the Routledge book series on Islam and Muslims in Southeast Asia. {cite:georgetown-khairudin-aljunied}

## Contributions and legacy
Aljunied has authored or edited more than a dozen books. Early works examined colonial Malaya — Colonialism, Violence and Muslims in Southeast Asia (2009) on the Maria Hertogh controversy, and Radicals (2015) on protest in colonial Malaya. He then turned to Muslim thought itself: Muslim Cosmopolitanism (2017), Hamka and Islam (2018), Islam in Malaysia: An Entwined History (2019), Shapers of Islam in Southeast Asia (2022) and Contemplating Sufism (2024). His work recovers Southeast Asian Muslim thinkers as cosmopolitan intellectuals rather than marginal figures of a "periphery", and he is listed in the Muslim 500 in the scholars category. {cite:georgetown-khairudin-aljunied}
