## Summary

Mirza Muhammad Tahir Nasrabadi (b. 1027 AH / 1618 CE, alive in 1088 AH / 1677–78 CE) was a litterateur and poet of Nasrabad, Isfahan, author of the Tazkirat al-Shu'ara known as the Tazkira-ye Nasrabadi, a biographical dictionary of some one thousand poets of the Safavid age, dedicated to Shah Suleiman I. Poets of Iran, Transoxiana and India corresponded with him and sent him their verse, and Saib Tabrizi was the most famous of his associates. The date of his death is unrecorded.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-tahir-nasrabadi-fa}{cite:wikipedia-tazkera-ye-taher-e-nasrabadi-en}

## A life among poets

Nasrabadi came from a noted and respected family of Nasrabad, a district of Isfahan, and received some education there. He travelled — to Mecca, to the shrine cities and to Mashhad — and built a wide correspondence: poets across Iran, Transoxiana, the Ottoman lands and India sent him their poems, and he was an associate of the leading poet of the age, Saib Tabrizi. His own pen produced more than the famous Tazkira: a treatise called Mir'at al-Kamal, a selection of Saib's verse, a selection from the Shahnameh, and a love mesnevi, Gul-i Rana.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-tahir-nasrabadi-fa}

## The Tazkira

The Tazkirat al-Shu'ara, the Tazkira-ye Nasrabadi, is one of the major records of Persian literary life under the later Safavids: brief biographies of around one thousand poets, among them rulers and princes of Iran and India, and even the author's own father and grandfather. The book maps the Persianate literary world in three parts — Iran, Turan and India — and was dedicated to the reigning shah, Suleiman I. Modern scholarship has kept it in print: critical editions appeared in Tehran, an English translation by Arash Karimpour was published in 2024, and an Urdu translation exists.{cite:wikipedia-tazkera-ye-taher-e-nasrabadi-en}

Of his end, the sources say only that he was alive in 1088 AH (1677–78 CE); no death date is recorded, and the dates 1630–1700 sometimes attached to him are attested for no article read.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-tahir-nasrabadi-fa}{cite:wikidata-muhammad-tahir-nasrabadi}
