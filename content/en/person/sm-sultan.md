## Summary
SM Sultan, full name Sheikh Mohammed Sultan, was a Bengali painter, born on 10 August 1923 at Machimdia in Narail and died on 10 October 1994 at Jessore. His fame rests on his striking depictions of exaggeratedly muscular Bengali peasants engaged in the activities of their everyday lives. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

His early works were influenced by western techniques and forms, particularly impressionism, while in his later works, particularly those exhibited in 1976, scholars cited in the article find a constant drive to decolonise his techniques and forms. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

## Early life and education
He was born in Machimdia village, in what was then Jessore District, British India, now Narail District, Bangladesh. After five years of primary education at Victoria Collegiate School in Narail, he went to work for his father, a mason. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

Even as a child he felt a strong artistic urge, seized every opportunity to draw with charcoal, and developed his talent depicting the buildings his father worked on. He wanted to study art in Calcutta but his family lacked the means, until he secured financial support from the local zamindar and went to Calcutta in 1938. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

In that period he joined Allama Mashriqi's Khaksar movement in British India, a movement which, as a cited scholar describes it, sought to organise the self and Muslim sociality to lay the ground for decolonisation. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

The poet and art critic Hasan Shahid Suhrawardy restyled him S. M. Sultan, offered him accommodation in his home and the use of his library. Sultan did not meet the admissions requirements of the Government School of Art, but in 1941 got in with Suhrawardy's help, as Suhrawardy sat on the school's governing body. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

## Career
He left art school after three years, in 1944, and travelled around India earning his living by drawing portraits of Allied soldiers encamped along his route. His first solo exhibition was in Shimla in 1946, followed after Partition by solo exhibitions in Lahore in 1948 and Karachi in 1949. None of his artworks from this period survived, mainly due to his own indifference to preserving his work. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

Officially selected by the government in Karachi, he visited the United States in the early 1950s under an international arts programme and exhibited in New York, Washington, Boston, Chicago and at Michigan University, then travelled to England and joined the annual open-air group exhibition at Victoria Embankment Gardens, Hampstead, London. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

In 1953 he returned to his native Narail and settled in an abandoned building overlooking the Chitra River, living close to the land and far from the outside art world for the next twenty-three years, with a reputation as a whimsical recluse and a Bohemian. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

## Works
The themes of his paintings are nature and rural life. In the 1970s and 1980s he did some of his best work: agricultural labourers ploughing, planting, threshing and fishing took centre stage, with exaggeratedly muscular physiques that made visible the inner strength of the sturdy, hard-working peasants, the backbone of Bangladesh. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

His named works include First Plantation (1975), Char Dakhal (1976), Harvesting (1986) and Fishing-3 (1991), and his works are held in major Bangladeshi collections including the Bangladesh National Museum, the National Art Gallery, the S.M. Sultan Memorial Museum and the Bengal Foundation. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

In 1976 the Bangladesh Shilpakala Academy put on an individual exhibition of his work, his first major exhibition and his first in Dhaka. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

His paintings never included urban elements or anything produced by modern technology, which he considered imported, and he had little interest in abstract art, though he broke with the artistic conventions of the past. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

## Recognition
He received the Ekushey Padak in 1982 for his achievement in fine arts, the Bangladesh Charu Shilpi Sangsad Award in 1986, and the Independence Day Award in 1993. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

He established the Kurigram Fine Arts Institute at Narail in 1969 and another art school at Jessore in 1973, now named Charupeeth. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

Professor Lala Rukh Selim of the University of Dhaka described him as one of the four pioneers of Bangladeshi modernism, along with Zainul Abedin, Safiuddin Ahmed and Quamrul Hassan, and in 1989 Tareque Masud directed the documentary Adam Surat (The Inner Strength) on his life. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

## Personal life and death
He remained a confirmed bachelor and settled in Narail with an adopted family and pets; this museum publishes no detail of his life beyond what understanding his work requires. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}

He died on 10 October 1994 at Jessore, as the English article reports, and was buried at Narail. {cite:wikipedia-sm-sultan-en}
