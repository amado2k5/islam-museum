## Summary
G. Willow Wilson is an American novelist, memoirist and comics writer born in New Jersey in 1982, a prominent Muslim voice in American popular culture; The Muslim 500 lists her among influential figures in arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-g-willow-wilson} {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Formation
She embraced Islam as a young woman, moved to Cairo to teach and write, and chronicled the experience in the memoir The Butterfly Mosque ⁦(2010)⁩, an account of conversion, marriage and life in Egypt before the revolution. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Fiction
Her debut novel Alif the Unseen ⁦(2012)⁩ — a techno-fantasy of hackers and jinn in an unnamed Gulf city — won the World Fantasy Award; later novels include The Bird King ⁦(2019)⁩, set at the fall of Muslim Granada. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Comics
With artist Adrian Alphona and editors at Marvel she co-created Kamala Khan, the Pakistani-American teenager who became Ms. Marvel — the first Muslim American hero to headline a Marvel comic, later adapted for screen — and wrote the series to multiple Hugo-recognised storylines. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Assessment
She is credited with putting a believing Muslim heroine at the centre of American superhero comics and with a body of fiction joining Islamic ideas to genre storytelling; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson} {cite:muslim500-g-willow-wilson}


## Ms. Marvel
Her run writing Ms. Marvel ⁦(2014-)⁩ created Kamala Khan — a Pakistani-American Muslim teenager from Jersey City — as a mainstream superhero; the comic won a Hugo Award and anchored the character's later film and streaming appearances. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Books
Her novels Alif the Unseen ⁦(World Fantasy Award winner)⁩, The Bird King ⁦(2019)⁩ and the memoir The Butterfly Mosque trace religion, fantasy and history; the memoir recounts her conversion and years in Egypt. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Comics work
Beyond Ms. Marvel she has written for Wonder Woman and other major comics, and her career is cited in publishing discussions of diversity and representation in mainstream superhero publishing. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson} {cite:muslim500-g-willow-wilson}

## Origins
Born in New Jersey in 1982, she studied history at Boston University and embraced Islam as a young woman, spending years in Cairo teaching English and writing before her comics and fiction career took shape. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Cairo and conversion
Her memoir The Butterfly Mosque recounts her conversion, her marriage to an Egyptian and her years in Cairo — a narrative profiled as among the most-read conversion memoirs in English. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## Standing
She occupies a rare position: simultaneously a bestselling prose novelist, a major superhero writer, and one of the most visible Muslim converts in American publishing — a profile that made her a fixture of both literary and pop-culture coverage. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson} {cite:muslim500-g-willow-wilson}

## The Ms. Marvel moment
G. Willow Wilson's creation of Kamala Khan — a Pakistani-American Muslim teenager from Jersey City — as a mainstream superhero was a landmark in publishing: a Muslim character anchoring a major Marvel title, written by a Muslim author, winning a Hugo Award and spawning film and streaming adaptations. The character's success is regularly cited in discussions of representation in superhero publishing, and her own position — bestselling prose novelist, major superhero writer and one of the most visible Muslim converts in American publishing — made her a fixture of both literary and pop-culture coverage. {cite:wikipedia-g-willow-wilson}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists her among the most influential Muslims in arts and literature, citing her creation of Ms. Marvel, her World Fantasy Award-winning novel Alif the Unseen and her position as a leading Muslim voice in American publishing. {cite:muslim500-g-willow-wilson}
