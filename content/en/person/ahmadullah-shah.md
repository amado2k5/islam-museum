# Ahmadullah Shah

Ahmadullah Shah (1787–1858), known as the Maulvi of Faizabad, was an Indian scholar and one of the leading figures of the Indian Rebellion of 1857 in Awadh. His birth name was Sikandar Shah; he was also known by the names Danka Shah and Nakkar Shah {cite:wikipedia-ahmadullah-shah-en}. He was born in 1787 at Faizabad in the Oudh State, into a family originally from Gopamau village in Hardoi district, and received a traditional Islamic education {cite:wikipedia-ahmadullah-shah-en}{cite:wikidata-ahmadullah-shah}.

Before the rebellion he had travelled widely — to Hyderabad, Madras, Calcutta and Delhi, and on Hajj to Mecca — and is credited with distributing pamphlets calling for jihad, among them the tract known as Fateh Islam {cite:wikipedia-ahmadullah-shah-en}. When the rising of 1857 came, he fought alongside Fazl-e-Haq Khairabadi and Khan Bahadur Khan, leading the resistance in the region around Lucknow; the British never managed to capture him alive {cite:wikipedia-ahmadullah-shah-en}.

On 5 June 1858 he was killed at Shahjahanpur by Raja Jagannath Singh of Powayan, who presented his severed head to the British in return for the announced reward of 50,000 silver pieces {cite:wikipedia-ahmadullah-shah-en}{cite:wikidata-ahmadullah-shah}.

The English article states that "the Maulvi was a Sunni Muslim", and the Urdu article likewise says he belonged to Ahl al-Sunna {cite:wikipedia-ahmadullah-shah-en}{cite:wikipedia-ahmadullah-shah-ur}; because the Urdu article follows the English one's structure and citations so closely, the museum treats the two as a single attestation family and records no school for him, keeping the statement in his entry's note.
