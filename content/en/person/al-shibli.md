## Summary
Abu Bakr Dulaf b. Jahdar al-Shibli, Khurasani by origin, Baghdadi by birth and upbringing; he kept company with Shaykh Abu'l-Qasim al-Junayd and the righteous of that age and judged among them. In his early career he had governed a town, then renounced the world; when Ramadan entered he would turn wholly to worship, saying: 'This is a month my Lord has magnified.' {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] places his notice (no. 646) near the end of the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]]. The rhymed opening calls him the one drawn away and love-struck, the one seized and drunk. Abu Nu'aym heard many of the reports from men who had heard al-Shibli himself. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/366-367}

**In the hospital.** Al-Shibli was taken into the hospital for treatment, and the vizier Ali ibn Isa visited him. One of those present reminded him of his own saying that every truthful saint without a miracle is a liar, and asked what his miracle was. He answered that his miracle is that his thought when sober, set beside his thought when drunk, never departs from agreement with God. To visitors there he said that he had been put in the hospital many times and given many medicines, and it had only increased his madness. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/367-368}

**With al-Junayd.** Khayr al-Nassaj tells that al-Shibli, in a state of intoxication, burst in on [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]] at home while al-Junayd's wife had her head uncovered. Al-Junayd told her not to trouble herself, because "he is not there". Al-Shibli recited verses on union and rejection and left. Abu Nu'aym also relates that al-Junayd once told him that people are drowned away from God, while he is drowned in God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/367} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/371}

**The rumour of his death.** He fell gravely ill and word spread that he had died. Leading companions of al-Junayd hurried to his house, among them Ibn Ata and [[person/jafar-al-khuldi|Ja'far al-Khuldi]]. Told that they had come to his funeral, he sat up and said: dead men have come to the funeral of a living man. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/368}

**Sayings.** He said that one who is veiled from God by creatures is not like one who is veiled from creatures by God. Asked about asceticism, he said it is turning the heart from things to the Lord of things. He said that God has not veiled Himself from His creatures; creatures are veiled from Him by love of this world. Hearing a warning about the quiet of graves, he said: you are the graves; whoever turns from God is in woe, and whoever turns to Him is in joy. Asked about pure tawhid, he answered that whoever puts it in words or points to it has not reached it, and whoever is silent about it is ignorant of it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/367} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/370} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/374}

**Shaving his beard.** A son of his died, and the boy's mother cut off her hair in grief. Al-Shibli had his beard shaved. Asked why, he said: she cut her hair for one who is lost; how should I not shave my beard for One who is present? {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/370}

**With Ibn Mujahid.** He came upon [[person/ibn-mujahid|Abu Bakr ibn Mujahid]] in the vizier Ali ibn Isa's company. Ibn Mujahid asked him about his burning of clothes, bread and food that people could use, and where that stood in knowledge and the law. Al-Shibli answered with a Qur'anic verse from the story of Solomon and the horses. Ibn Mujahid fell silent and told the vizier: it is as if I had never read it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/373-374}

**Verse.** The notice is full of lines he recited or quoted in his circle, most of them on love and separation. One set is his answer to a man who asked what he would wear for the feast when his clothes were worn out. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/372-373}

**His death.** A man present at his death relates that when his tongue was held and his brow sweated, he signalled for the ablution of prayer. The man washed him and forgot to run his fingers through the beard. Al-Shibli took his hand and passed the fingers through his beard himself, and the man wept that he kept this practice at such an hour. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/371}

## Additions from the notice and corrections

**Continuation of the hospital and the Junayd episode (correction).** When the vizier Ali ibn Isa visited him and asked what his Lord had done, the vizier answered: in heaven He decrees and executes. Al-Shibli said: I asked about the Lord you worship, not the Lord you do not worship, meaning the caliph al-Muqtadir; Ali told one of those present to debate him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/367} After the Junayd exchange, before he left, he struck Junayd on the head; then, when he turned to go, he struck Junayd's feet, said "that is it", and fell fainting. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/367}

**Continuation of the rumour of his death.** When told that they had come to his funeral, he sat up and said: dead men have come to the funeral of a living man; then he said to them: I think I have left among you someone who can carry my body. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/368}

**More sayings.** He said: the disciple has no lapse, the knower has no knowledge, knowledge has no attachment, the lover has no rest, the truthful has no claim, the fearful has no shelter, and creatures have no escape from God. He said: a glance is unbelief, a passing thought is polytheism, and a sign is deceit; a glance is deprivation, a passing thought is abandonment, and a sign is desertion. He said: whoever is cut off is joined, and whoever is joined is cut off. Asked about the verse on prayer, he explained it as: call upon God without heedlessness, and He answers without delay. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/368}

**Love, tawhid and knowledge (continued).** He said: whoever loves God before God loves him is a polytheist. He said: creation strayed in knowledge, knowledge strayed in the name, and the name strayed in the essence. He said: whoever knows God, everything submits to him, because it shows the mark of His dominion. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/369} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/370}

**A student's question.** A student asked where he should seek God; he said: your mother bore you! Does one seek who holds the heavens on a finger and the earths on a finger? God is not veiled from creatures; creatures are veiled from Him by love of the world. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/370}

**Secrets, jealousy and drunkenness.** He said: keeping secrets is guarding them from seeing what is other. Jealousy is of two kinds: human jealousy, and divine jealousy over time, lest it be lost on anything other than God. He said: how much people need a drunkenness that frees them from watching themselves, their deeds and their states. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/371} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/372}

**Love and generosity (continued).** Abu al-Fath ibn Shafi' visited him in the hospital and was heard crying out that people know he is in love but do not know with whom. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/373} A beggar stood at his circle calling "O God, O Generous"; al-Shibli wept and said: how can I describe the Truth as generous when a creature asks with his hand? He then recited verses on generosity. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/373}

**Trust, fear, hope and tawhid.** Trust is that He carries you in what He lays on you; fear is that you fear He will hand you over to yourself; hope is that you hope He will not cut you off short of it. On bare tawhid: whoever answers about it by expression is a heretic, whoever points to it is a dualist, whoever gestures to it is a likener, whoever speaks of it is heedless, whoever keeps silent about it is ignorant, whoever thinks it is present is far from it, and whoever is in ecstasy about it has lost it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/374}

**Lowliness and honour.** He said: I looked at the lowliness of every lowly man and his lowliness increased mine over them, and I looked at the honour of every honoured man and my honour increased over them; their honour is a lowliness in my honour. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/374}

**A dream.** One of his companions saw him in a dream and asked who of his companions was happiest in his company. He said: the greatest of them in respect for God's sacred things, the most constant in remembering God, the most upright in God's right, the swiftest to seek His pleasure, the best knower of their own shortcoming, and the greatest in honouring the sacred of His servants. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/375}

**Ibn Mujahid (correction).** Al-Shibli went to Ibn Mujahid's house, did not find him there, and was told that he was with the vizier Ali ibn Isa; he went to the vizier's house and asked permission; Ibn Mujahid said to the vizier: today I will show you a wonder from al-Shibli; then Ibn Mujahid let him in and asked about burning clothes and food. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/373} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/374}
