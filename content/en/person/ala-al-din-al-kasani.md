## Summary

Ala' al-Din Abu Bakr ibn Mas'ud ibn Ahmad al-Kasani, called "King of the Scholars", was the author of the Hanafi law book [[work/badai-al-sanai|Bada'i' al-Sana'i' fi Tartib al-Shara'i']]. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74} This account is from the introduction by the editors of the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya edition, who refer to al-Qurashi's al-Jawahir al-Mudiyya and others and relate reports from Ibn al-Adim. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75}

## His nisba

His nisba is to [[place/kasan|Kasan]], a town beyond al-Shash, also called Kashan, as the editors note from al-Ansab and Mu'jam al-Buldan. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74}

## Teacher and marriage

He studied law with [[person/ala-al-din-al-samarqandi|Ala' al-Din Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Ahmad al-Samarqandi]] and read with him most of his works, such as [[work/tuhfat-al-fuqaha|al-Tuhfa]] in law and books of legal theory. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74} His teacher married him to his learned daughter [[person/fatima-al-samarqandi|Fatima]]; it is related that she had memorised al-Tuhfa and that several rulers of Rum sought her hand and her father refused; al-Kasani stayed close to his teacher, excelled in legal theory and positive law, and wrote al-Bada'i' as a commentary on al-Tuhfa; he showed it to his teacher, who was delighted and married his daughter to him, making the book her dower; and the jurists said: he explained his Tuhfa and married his daughter. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74}

## Envoy to Aleppo

The editors relate that in the land of Rum he debated a jurist on [[concept/taswib-al-mujtahidin|whether two differing mujtahids are both right]]; the jurist said the report from Abu Hanifa is that every mujtahid is right, and al-Kasani said the sound report is that one is right and the other mistaken, the truth being on one side, and that the jurist's view is the Mu'tazila's; words passed between them, al-Kasani raised his whip against the jurist, the matter reached the ruler, and the vizier advised sending him as an envoy to [[person/nur-al-din-mahmud-ibn-zangi|Nur al-Din Mahmud]] at Aleppo rather than dismissing him, since he was a great and respected man. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75}

## Teaching at the Halawiyya

Al-Radi al-Sarakhsi, author of al-Muhit, had been given the teaching post at the [[building/al-halawiyah-madrasa|Halawiyya]] madrasa in Aleppo and then removed; Nur al-Din appointed al-Kasani in his place at the jurists' request, and they used to spread his prayer-rug and sit around it every day until he arrived. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75} Ibn al-Adim relates that when he came to Damascus the jurists asked him to debate a question, and he said he would not debate any question on which the Hanafi masters disagreed; they named many questions, and for each he said that such and such of the Hanafis held it, until the gathering broke up. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75} Besides al-Bada'i' he wrote al-Sultan al-Mubin fi Usul al-Din. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75}

## With his wife in Aleppo

Ibn al-Adim relates that al-Kasani resolved to return home at his wife's urging; Nur al-Din asked him to stay, and he explained that he would not go against her; they sent a eunuch to speak to her for the ruler, but she veiled herself from him and sent word to her husband that it was not lawful for the eunuch to look at her; they then sent a woman, and she agreed, and stayed in Aleppo until her death. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76} Al-Kasani visited her grave every Friday night until he died. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76}

## His death

Ibn al-Adim relates from Diya' al-Din Muhammad ibn Khamis al-Hanafi that he was with al-Kasani at his death; al-Kasani began reciting Surat Ibrahim and, on reaching the verse [[passage/quran-14-27|Ibrahim 27]], his soul departed. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76} From Khalifa ibn Sulayman: he died on Sunday after the noon prayer, 10 Rajab 587 AH; Iftikhar al-Din al-Hashimi took over the teaching at the Halawiyya after him; and he was buried beside his wife inside the shrine of Abraham the Friend of God outside [[place/aleppo|Aleppo]], where visitors know their grave as "the grave of the woman and her husband". {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76} He left a son, whose upbringing al-Malik al-Zahir took in hand. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.77}
