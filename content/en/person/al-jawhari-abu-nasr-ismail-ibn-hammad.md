## Summary
Abu Nasr Isma'il ibn Hammad al-Jawhari al-Farabi was a philologist from Farab and the author of the dictionary [[work/al-sihah-jawhari|al-Sihah, Taj al-Lugha wa-Sihah al-Arabiyya]]. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba} {cite:sihah-attar-1979-v1 p.1} Bakri Shaykh Amin quotes Yaqut al-Hamawi's *Mu'jam al-Udaba'*: "al-Jawhari was one of the wonders of the age in intelligence and acumen; his origin was in the land of the Turks, from Farab, and he was a master of language and letters". {cite:sihah-attar-1979-scan-b p.21} The *Mawsu'at A'lam al-Ulama' wa'l-Udaba'* says that the reason for his name al-Jawhari is not known. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## Studies and travels
Al-Jawhari learned Arabic from [[person/abu-ali-al-farisi|Abu Ali al-Farisi]] (d. 356 AH) and [[person/al-sirafi|Abu Sa'id al-Sirafi]] (d. 368 AH), completed his learning with the scholars of the Hijaz and with the Arabs in their own lands, travelling among Rabi'a and Mudar, returned to Khurasan, and then moved to [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]], where he settled as a teacher, author and master of calligraphy. {cite:sihah-attar-1979-scan-b p.21} {cite:sihah-attar-1979-scan-b p.22} His maternal uncle was Abu Ibrahim [[person/ishaq-ibn-ibrahim-al-farabi|Ishaq ibn Ibrahim al-Farabi]], author of Diwan al-Adab; Attar quotes Yaqut as having seen a copy of Diwan al-Adab in al-Jawhari's hand recording that he read it with Abu Ibrahim at Farab, and considers the statement inexact, since al-Farabi wrote his book in Zabid and died there. {cite:sihah-attar-1979-scan-b p.7}

## Al-Sihah
Al-Jawhari compiled al-Sihah in Nishapur for Abu Mansur Abd al-Rahim al-Bayshaki, a man of letters, preacher and legal theorist. {cite:sihah-attar-1979-scan-b p.22} He arranged it by the last radical of the root and then by the first; its editor Ahmad Abd al-Ghafur Attar regards it as heading a school of lexicography followed by Lisan al-Arab and al-Qamus, and holds that al-Jawhari originated his method without having seen al-Bandaniji's Kitab al-Taqfiya; see [[question/did-al-jawhari-originate-the-method-of-al-sihah|Did al-Jawhari originate the arrangement of al-Sihah?]]. {cite:sihah-attar-1979-v1 p.21} {cite:sihah-attar-1979-v1 p.36} Attar quotes al-Jawhari's own introduction, where he says he arranged it "in an order in which I have had no predecessor". {cite:sihah-attar-1979-v1 p.30}

## His death
The biographies relate, as Bakri Shaykh Amin reports, that al-Jawhari was seized by delusions, climbed onto the roof of the old mosque of Nishapur and said: "I have done in this world something in which no one preceded me, and I shall do for the next something in which no one preceded me", then tied the two leaves of a door to his sides, claimed he would fly, threw himself off and died. {cite:sihah-attar-1979-scan-b p.22} The year of his death is disputed: the *Mawsu'a* and the Dar al-Hadith edition give 398 AH; Bakri Shaykh Amin says researchers prefer 393 AH, the year Attar also uses; and Khalil al-Atiyya gives 400 AH. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba} {cite:sihah-tamir-dar-al-hadith-2009 p.1} {cite:sihah-attar-1979-scan-b p.22} {cite:sihah-attar-1979-v1 p.20} {cite:sihah-attar-1979-v1 p.24}
