## Overview
Sulayman ibn Abdallah ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib was a Hashimite of the line of al-Hasan and a brother of Idris {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}. Al-Safadi in al-Wafi says he was among those who went out with al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan at Fakhkh when al-Husayn rose against al-Hadi, and that he was killed; he then reports a different account from al-Bayhaqi {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}. In this entry al-Safadi gives no year of birth or death.

## Two accounts of his fate
Al-Safadi says al-Bayhaqi reports that he was known as Sulayman of the Maghrib and claims he was not killed at Fakhkh but entered the Maghrib while seeking power; flight forced him to hire himself out to a boatman at sea and to a camel driver on land, and the Abbasid governors sought him while the Berbers defended him {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}. Al-Safadi quotes verses he composed on them: he calls them a western band who rallied to him and attached themselves to the Berbers, who kept the Prophet and his law in his house, and says it did not harm them that Hashim cut him off while they, though not of his stock, kept faith with him; in further verses he praises God for the guidance that his ancestor brought people, and calls himself and his people that ancestor's sons and kin {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}.

## In Tlemcen and his descendants
Al-Safadi says his affairs ended with his coming to Tlemcen, where the sons of his brother Idris were and where the leadership lay with them; they honoured him until he died {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}. A quarrel later arose between the two families and Sulayman's descendants were sent out to the Central Maghrib, as the sense of the sentence is best read {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}. His best-known son was Hamza ibn Sulayman, after whom Suq Hamza in the Maghrib is named; his sons inherited rule there until Jawhar al-Mu'izzi came to them, took every notable among them to al-Mu'izz and removed them from their rule. Remnants stayed in the mountains and outlying districts, famous and honoured among the Berber tribes {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}. Al-Safadi adds that he is the father of Muhammad, who entered the Maghrib {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 p. 242}.
