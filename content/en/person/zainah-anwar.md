## Summary
Zainah Anwar is a Malaysian activist for women's rights and the law who co-founded in 1988 Sisters in Islam (SIS), the first Muslim women's organisation in the world to fight explicitly from within the Islamic frame for equality in family law, and co-founded the global Musawah movement, launched in 2009 to coordinate reform of family law in Muslim countries. She is counted among the founders of contemporary Islamic feminism. {cite:wikipedia-zainah-anwar}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1954 in Johor Bahru, Malaysia, into a legal family — her father was among the framers of independent Malaysia's constitution — and studied journalism and international relations, working as a journalist in Malaysian newspapers and as a researcher at the Institute of Strategic and International Studies (ISIS Malaysia) before dedicating herself to civil work. {cite:wikipedia-zainah-anwar}

## Sisters in Islam
In 1988 she and a group of professional women founded SIS around a central question: why is it always that "Islam says so" is the answer to injustice against women? The organisation focused on re-reading family-law texts from an Islamic feminist perspective, published widely circulated study booklets including Are Men and Women Equal Before Allah? and Polygamy Is Not a God-Given Right to Men, and shifted the debate on women's rights in Islam from the margins of Malaysian society to its centre. She led the organisation as executive director and then chair for many years. {cite:wikipedia-zainah-anwar} {cite:muslim500-zainah-anwar}

## The Musawah movement
In 2009 she launched in Kuala Lumpur the global Musawah movement: a knowledge-and-action framework gathering scholars and activists from fifty countries to re-read family laws on the basis of justice and equality, through research, publications, conferences and legislative advocacy; she chairs its international advisory board and directs its knowledge-building programme on Islamic family laws. {cite:wikipedia-zainah-anwar}

## Debate
Islamic factions and conservatives object to her discourse, accusing her of distorting the Sharia under the banner of reform, while her supporters describe her as saving the debate from a double falsification — neither submission to traditional text nor cancellation of Islam; she has published answers and books including Islamic Family Law Reform: The Battle for Equality, and these positions are reported attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-zainah-anwar}

## Recognition and legacy
She has received wide international recognition from rights bodies and councils, and her books and Musawah's publications are presented at university conferences on Islam and gender. Her legacy is a generation of activists and researchers moving the debate inside Islam rather than confronting it from outside — the Sisters in Islam methodology becoming a template replicated by Muslim women's organisations worldwide. {cite:wikipedia-zainah-anwar}
