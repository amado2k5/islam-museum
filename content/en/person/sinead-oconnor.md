## Summary
Sinéad O'Connor was one of the defining voices of late-twentieth-century popular music — and, from 2018, a Muslim convert who took the name Shuhada' Sadaqat while continuing to record and perform. {cite:muslim500-sinead-oconnor} {cite:wikipedia-sinead-oconnor}

## Career
Born in Dublin in 1966, she rose with her 1987 debut The Lion and the Cobra and reached worldwide fame with Nothing Compares 2 U ⁦(1990)⁩. Her career was marked as much by protest — against abuse in the Catholic Church, for which she both suffered and was later vindicated in public memory — as by ten studio albums. {cite:muslim500-sinead-oconnor} {cite:wikipedia-sinead-oconnor}

## Conversion and last years
In October 2018 she announced her conversion to Islam, studied with teachers in Ireland and returned to the stage performing in hijab, saying she found in Islam 'a home'. She died in London on 26 July 2023, weeks after the suicide of her son; tributes across Ireland and the Muslim world marked both losses. {cite:muslim500-sinead-oconnor} {cite:wikipedia-sinead-oconnor}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 records her in its obituaries as the most famous Western convert-musician of her generation — an artist whose spiritual journey was public, contested and unscripted. {cite:muslim500-sinead-oconnor}
