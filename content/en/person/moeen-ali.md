## Summary
Moeen Munir Ali is an English cricketer, born in Sparkhill, Birmingham, on 18 June 1987, who played for England between 2014 and 2024 and was formerly vice-captain of England in limited-overs cricket. {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-en} {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-ur} {cite:wikidata-moeen-ali}

He made his debuts in all three formats in 2014, was part of the England teams that won the 2019 Cricket World Cup and the 2022 T20 World Cup, and announced his international retirement on 8 September 2024. {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-en}

## Early life
Ali was born in Sparkhill, Birmingham. He is of Kashmiri and English descent: his grandfather migrated to England from Mirpur, Kashmir, while his grandmother, Betty Cox, was a white Briton. He can understand Urdu and Punjabi. His father worked as a taxi driver and as a psychiatric nurse, he grew up on the same street as his cousin the cricketer Kabir Ali and other cricketers, and his brothers Kadeer and Omar are also cricketers. {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-en}

## Domestic career
He signed for Warwickshire aged fifteen, made his first-class debut in 2005, captained England Under-19s at the 2006 Under-19 World Cup, moved to Worcestershire in 2007 and stayed until 2022, was county cricket's MVP and PCA Player of the Year in 2013, captained the Worcestershire Rapids to the Vitality Blast title in 2018, then returned to Warwickshire; the article in its current form says he represents Yorkshire. He also played in multiple Twenty20 leagues, including Royal Challengers Bangalore and Chennai Super Kings in the Indian Premier League, winning the IPL with Chennai in 2021 and 2023. {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-en}

## International career
He debuted in all three formats in 2014. He played 68 Tests, scoring 3,094 runs and taking 204 wickets — the third-best England spinner in Tests, behind Derek Underwood and Graeme Swann — 138 One Day Internationals and 92 Twenty20 Internationals, finishing with 6,678 international runs and 366 wickets in 298 appearances. He first retired from Tests in 2021, came out of Test retirement for the 2023 Ashes after Jack Leach's injury and retired from Tests finally at the end of that series, and retired from all international cricket in September 2024, saying it was time for the next generation, while intending to continue franchise cricket and to coach. {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-en}

## Faith and public work as the sources report them
The sources report Ali's public practice of Islam without naming a school for him. BBC News reported him in 2017 under the headline "I want people to think Muslims are not all bad"; press reporting records that he performed Hajj at twenty-two and a later Umrah in preference to Indian Premier League riches, and that he asked Chennai Super Kings to remove an alcohol brand's logo from his jersey, which the club accepted. In a 2014 Test against India he wore "Save Gaza" and "Free Palestine" wristbands; the ECB regarded his stance as humanitarian, not political, but the match referee overruled the permission. He is an ambassador for StreetChance, which provides free cricket coaching in deprived areas, and a global brand ambassador for Orphans in Need from 2015. {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-en}

## Honours and personal life
He published his autobiography Moeen in 2018, was appointed OBE in the 2022 Birthday Honours for services to cricket, and received an honorary doctorate from Coventry University in November 2024. The museum records only what is needed on his personal life: he is married to Firoza Hossain and they have a daughter and a son. {cite:wikipedia-moeen-ali-en}
