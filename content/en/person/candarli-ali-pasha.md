Çandarlı Ali Pasha was the eldest son of [[person/candarli-kara-halil-pasha|Çandarlı Kara Halil Hayreddin Pasha]], the first man to bear the title of grand vizier in the Ottoman state; neither the year nor the place of his birth is known to the sources.{cite:wikipedia-candarli-ali-pasha-tr}{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en}{cite:wikidata-candarli-ali-pasha} He received a medrese education and entered the ilmiye class, served as kadı and then as kazasker before 1386, and when his father died unexpectedly on 22 January 1387 he succeeded him as vizier and then as grand vizier.{cite:wikipedia-candarli-ali-pasha-tr}{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en}

In 1388 Ali Pasha led thirty thousand men into Bulgaria, taking Tarnovo and Shumen, and in 1389 he fought at Kosovo, where Murad I was killed and Bayezid I was raised to the throne with his support.{cite:wikipedia-candarli-ali-pasha-tr}{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en} He served Bayezid for the whole reign — at the blockade of Constantinople and at Nicopolis in 1396 beside the prince Süleyman — and before the Battle of Ankara in 1402 he advised a war of attrition against Timur rather than a pitched battle; the sultan rejected the advice.{cite:wikipedia-candarli-ali-pasha-tr}{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en}

After Bayezid's capture he fled with Süleyman Çelebi to Rumelia, secured his proclamation at Edirne, and on his behalf brokered the Treaty of Gallipoli of 1403 with Byzantium, Venice, Genoa and the other Christian powers, administering the state in the prince's name through the Interregnum like a ruler in fact.{cite:wikipedia-candarli-ali-pasha-tr}{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en} Institutional reforms are credited to him — codifying the kadıs' fees so they would not take bribes, and founding the corps of palace pages (iç oğlan) — and among his endowments was the completion of the Yeşil Mosque at Iznik, begun by his father.{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en}{cite:wikipedia-candarli-ali-pasha-tr} The later Ottoman chroniclers' portrait of him as a drunkard who corrupted his sultans is flagged by the English biography, following the Encyclopaedia of Islam, as the propaganda of his rivals, the partisans of the victorious Mehmed I.{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en}

Ali Pasha died at Ankara on 18 December 1406 (Wikidata gives 27 December); his body was carried to Iznik and buried in his father's tomb at the Yeşil Mosque.{cite:wikipedia-candarli-ali-pasha-tr}{cite:wikipedia-candarlizade-ali-pasha-en}{cite:wikidata-candarli-ali-pasha}
