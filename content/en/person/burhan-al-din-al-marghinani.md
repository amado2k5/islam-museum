## Summary

Burhan al-Din Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Abi Bakr ibn Abd al-Jalil ibn al-Khalil ibn Abi Bakr al-Marghinani was a Hanafi jurist of Transoxiana and the author of [[work/al-hidayah-marghinani|al-Hidaya]]. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.16} This account follows the biography written by al-Hidaya's editor Sa'id Bakdash, which summarises the biographical dictionaries and modern studies. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.14} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.16}

## Name and origins

The editor records the nisba al-Siddiqi, tracing descent to Abu Bakr al-Siddiq; he was known as al-Farghani after [[place/farghana|Farghana]], the wide province of Transoxiana; al-Marghinani after Marghinan, one of its best-known towns, today Margilan; and al-Rishdani after Rishdan, a village of Marghinan. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.16}

## Birth, death and burial

He was born after the afternoon prayer on Monday 8 Rajab 511 AH, as al-Laknawi reports from writings of some of his ancestors; the editor calls al-Zirikli's date of 530 AH in al-A'lam a clear error. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.16} He died on the night of Tuesday 14 Dhu al-Hijja 593 AH. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.16} He was buried in [[place/samarqand|Samarqand]]; the editor quotes al-Qurashi's introduction to al-Jawahir al-Mudiyya: in Samarqand there is a quarter called "the Turbat al-Muhammadin", where some four hundred authors and muftis are buried, each named Muhammad; when the author of al-Hidaya died they carried him there but were prevented from burying him in it, and he was buried close by. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.17}

## Travel and teachers

He made the pilgrimage in 544 AH and met scholars in the two Sanctuaries. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.22} He compiled his own mashyakha listing his teachers with notices of them in the manner of the hadith scholars, which al-Qurashi used in al-Jawahir al-Mudiyya, and al-Sighnaqi compiled another for him. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.22} The teachers identified so far number thirty-two. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.22} They include his father Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Jalil; his maternal grandfather the judge Umar ibn Habib al-Zarandramishi; Ali ibn Muhammad al-Isbijabi (d. 535 AH), commentator of al-Tahawi's Mukhtasar; [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sadr-al-shahid|al-Sadr al-Shahid Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz ibn Maza]] (d. 536 AH); and [[person/umar-al-nasafi|Najm al-Din Umar ibn Muhammad al-Nasafi]] (d. 537 AH). {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.23} He read the Sunan of al-Tirmidhi with Sā'id ibn As'ad al-Marghinani, received a licence from al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Marghinani of Bukhara, and Ibn al-Shihna names [[person/qadi-khan|Qadi Khan]] al-Uzjandi (d. 592 AH) among his teachers in law. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.23} He is quoted as saying "how many great shaykhs I met and did not ask", from Ta'lim al-Muta'allim by his student [[person/al-zarnuji|al-Zarnuji]]. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.22}

## Students and sons

Among those he trained, and who transmitted al-Hidaya from him, was Shams al-A'imma Muhammad ibn Abd al-Sattar al-Kardari (d. 642 AH). {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.23} His three sons studied law with him: Muhammad; Umar, who excelled in law and gave fatwas; and Imad al-Din, who became a reference for fatwas and wrote an Adab al-Qadi. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.23} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.24}

## Works

The editor notes that al-Baghdadi counted ten books by him in Hadiyyat al-Arifin and that he himself found twelve: [[work/bidayat-al-mubtadi|Bidayat al-Mubtadi]]; [[work/al-tajnis-wal-mazid|al-Tajnis wa-l-Mazid]]; al-Ziyadat; a commentary on Muhammad ibn al-Hasan's al-Jami' al-Kabir; Uddat al-Nasik fi Idda min al-Manasik; Fara'id al-Uthmani; [[work/kifayat-al-muntahi|Kifayat al-Muntahi]]; Mukhtarat al-Nawazil; a dictionary of his teachers; Muntaqa al-Marfu', whose attribution some doubt; Nashr al-Madhhab; and al-Hidaya. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.24} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.26} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.28} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.29} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.31} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.33} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.34} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.35} He quotes al-Laknawi in al-Fawa'id al-Bahiyya: "all his works are accepted and relied on, especially al-Hidaya". {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.24}

## What was said of him

The editor gathers what biographers said of him, including his own remark that he surpassed his peers because he never paused in his studies, as in Ta'lim al-Muta'allim and al-Fawa'id al-Bahiyya, and the words of Ibn al-Mustawfi in Tarikh Irbil, al-Dhahabi in Siyar A'lam al-Nubala', al-Kaki, al-Itqani, al-Babarti, Ibn Hajar, Ibn al-Humam, Ibn Abidin and al-Laknawi. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.17} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.18} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.20} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.21} Drawing these together, the editor describes him as a jurist, legal theorist, hadith scholar, Qur'an commentator and man of letters, a mujtahid within the school who weighed its reports, and father of three sons who were jurists. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.8}
