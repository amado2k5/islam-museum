## Overview
Ala' al-Din Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Ansari, known as Ibn al-Shatir, a timekeeping engineer (muwaqqit) in Damascus. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 570-571}

Haji Khalifa says of him that he studied the astronomical instruments closely and found that they did not serve every task at every latitude. He judged that the error entered them because of difficulty in setting them or because some parts moved against others and their lines crowded together. He therefore devised an instrument he called *al-Rub' al-Tamm* (The Complete Quadrant), which yields every task in every climate. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 570-571}

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives a short entry for Ali ibn Ibrahim ibn Muhammad of Damascus, Abu al-Hasan Ibn al-Shatir: born in Rabi' al-Awwal 704, he excelled in astronomy. The entry gives no date of death. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/8}

## Timekeeping and the lunar model: documented extension

David A. King explicitly identifies Ibn al-Shatir as Muslim and describes his head timekeeping position at Damascus's Umayyad Mosque. This supplies individual Muslim identity; the record's Sunni frame is the museum's declared default, not testimony to a particular personal legal school. Earlier fields, prose and attribution remain intact; identity is not inferred from a name or city {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea opening}.

Muammer Dizer describes childhood bereavement, learning ivory inlay from a relative, study in Cairo and Alexandria, and return to Damascus after time in Aleppo. This account explains the combination of craft and scholarship, but establishes neither a lunar discovery night nor a particular instrument used to determine a parameter {cite:tdv-ibn-shatir-dizer biography}.

Dates are not silently reconciled: the earlier text reports Ibn Hajar's Rabi al-Awwal704AH, Dizer gives Shaban705AH/March1306, and King circa1305. Attributed alternatives remain; we neither change the original nor make a new calendar conversion. King places death circa1375, while Roberts's opening gives1375/6 within his older chronology {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/8} {cite:tdv-ibn-shatir-dizer biography} {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea heading} {cite:roberts-shatir-1957 p428}.

The [[invention/ibn-al-shatir-lunar-model|double-epicycle lunar model]] is distinct from the complete quadrant: a mathematical construction reducing exaggerated Ptolemaic distance variation while remaining Earth-centred. Roberts studied it in [[work/nihayat-sul-ibn-shatir|Nihayat al-sul]] using Marsh139, copied768AH/1366. The museum read his article, not the manuscript; copying does not date the invention {cite:roberts-shatir-1957 pp428,431–432}.

King distinguishes the lost early handbook and observational work with no known copy from the surviving theoretical treatise and new handbook. He also describes a monumental sundial, portable devices and quadrant/astrolabe writings, distinguishing original sundial fragments from a later copy. Every book title is not a new instrument, nor does an instrument's survival make the missing observational text available {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea planetary-works-and-instruments}.

Finally the Copernican comparison remains source-bound: Roberts1957 leaves transmission open; King later treats some details as evidence of transfer. This record provides no documented route and does not reduce Ibn al-Shatir to a preface to someone else. Centuries of handbook and instrument-manual reception likewise do not establish adoption of lunar theory by every reader {cite:roberts-shatir-1957 p432} {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea reception}.

## The polar-axis sundial

The [[invention/shatir-polar-sundial|monumental horizontal sundial]] differs from the lunar model and complete quadrant. King describes two-by-one-metre marble, a pole-directed gnomon and curves for equal hours and sunrise, sunset, midday and afternoon references, attributing design and construction to Ibn al-Shatir. This is not invention of every sundial; Cam's survey documents ancient and Islamic antecedents {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea sundial} {cite:tdv-gunes-saati-cam historical-survey}.

Dizer dates the device773AH/1371–1372 and names al-Tantawi in making the later copy. King describes a late-nineteenth-century copy on the minaret and original fragments in a Damascus museum garden. These are source reports, not a current visit. The small Aleppo dial and ambiguous1343 instrument report must not be conflated with this dated object {cite:tdv-ibn-shatir-dizer instruments} {cite:king-ibn-shatir-bea sundial-and-contemporary-report}.

## The complete quadrant and its teaching programme

The [[invention/shatir-complete-quadrant|complete-quadrant record]] separately describes a sine-quadrant improvement,not a second count of the sundial or lunar model. Dizer names complete and Alai types and says their examples have not survived,unlike some astrolabes. The universal-usefulness statement in the original prose above therefore remains an attributed claim,not a museum accuracy trial {cite:tdv-ibn-shatir-dizer instruments-and-work6}.

Osama Fathi Imam's introduction to [[work/al-naf-al-amm-shatir|the General Benefit]] describes200chapters and100questions/answers concerning spherical astronomy,time,qibla,shadows and heights. The public introduction was read,not the complete edition/manuscripts;the modern editor and supervisor Ahmad Fouad Basha are not instrument makers. The context photograph shows a different sine quadrant,and the invention article's geometry is independently derived modern paper explanation,not a recovered original grid {cite:fathi-perfect-quadrant-2015 introduction}.
