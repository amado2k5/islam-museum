## Overview
Abu al-Hasan al-Bahili al-Basri is called by al-Dhahabi the great scholar and shaykh of the theologians, and a pupil of Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari; he is a theologian of the Ash'ari school within Sunni Islam {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1397}. Al-Dhahabi says he excelled in the rational sciences and was alert, quick-witted, gentle, upright and devout {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1397}.

## His circle
Ibn al-Baqillani said that he, Abu Ishaq al-Isfara'ini and Abu Bakr ibn Furak were together in al-Bahili's lessons, which he gave once each week with a curtain drawn between them and him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1397}. He adds that al-Bahili was so absorbed in God that he was like a madman or one distracted, and did not know how far the lesson had gone until they reminded him; and that he explained the curtain by saying that he saw people as heedless, and so would be seen by them through the eye with which he saw them, so that he even screened himself from his slave-girl {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1397}.

## What his pupils said of him
Al-Ustadh al-Isfara'ini said that beside his shaykh al-Bahili he was like a drop in the sea, and that he had heard al-Bahili say he was like a drop beside the sea that was Shaykh al-Ash'ari {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1397}.
