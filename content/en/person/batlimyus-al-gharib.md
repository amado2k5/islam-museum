## Summary
Ptolemy the Foreigner (Batlimyus al-Gharib) was, for Ibn al-Nadim, a man who attended Aristotle and spread his virtues; his books included a "book of the reports of Aristotle, his death and the ordering of his works." {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 357}

## Details
In the Fihrist's section on the natural philosophers whose dates and ranks are unknown, Ibn al-Nadim writes of Ptolemy the Foreigner: "he used to attend upon Aristotle and publish his virtues; among his books is the book of the reports of Aristotle, his death and the ordering of his books." {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 357}

In Ibn al-Nadim's presentation the man is thus one of Aristotle's companions and chroniclers: he kept his company, spread his fame, and gathered in a book the reports about him, his death and the ordering of his works — among the earliest biographers and bibliographers of Aristotle known to the Arabic bibliographical tradition. Ibn al-Nadim lists him alongside Artemidorus the dream-book author, Gregory bishop of Nyssa, Theon the partisan of Plato and other ancient sages. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 357}
