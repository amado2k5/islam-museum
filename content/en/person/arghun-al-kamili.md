## Summary
[[person/sheila-blair|Sheila Blair]] notes that later sources singled out three people as the main followers of [[person/yaqut-al-mustasimi|Yaqut]] in Iraq: [[person/ahmad-al-suhrawardi|Ahmad al-Suhrawardi]], Arghun al-Kamili and Nasrallah Tabib. Arghun was active over the first half of the fourteenth century and is best known for Qur'an manuscripts in [[concept/rayhan-script|rayhan]]. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 333}

## In Syed Muhammad Salim
Tarikh-i Khatt o Khattatin lists him first among five pupils of Yaqut who became famous and spread "Yaqut's manner", saying he was Iranian by origin and lived in [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]], that the inscriptions of a Baghdad madrasa are in his hand, and that works by him are found in Tehran, Istanbul and the Anjuman Taraqqi-e Urdu (Hind). {cite:salim-tarikh-khatt-o-khattatin-2001 p. 123}

## Teamwork
Blair points out that Ibn al-Wahid in Cairo worked with a team, as his Baghdad contemporaries Ahmad al-Suhrawardi and Arghun al-Kamili did, though his team was larger: luxury Qur'ans of the period were the joint work of calligrapher, illuminator and outliner. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 388}
