## Overview
Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Abi Bakr ibn Muhammad, Abu Bakr, Jalal al-Din, is entered in al-A'lam as Ibn al-Nusaybi: a judge and Shafi'i jurist of the people of Aleppo {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 315}. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 851 AH, 1447 CE, and his death as 916 AH, 1510 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 315}. The index of al-A'lam has two references to this entry, one under Ibn al-Nusaybi the judge {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 32} and one under Ibn Hibatullah the Shafi'i {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 70}; the name Ibn Hibatullah does not occur in the text of the entry itself, so its link to the subject rests on that cross-reference alone.

## Upbringing and judgeship
He was born in Aleppo, grew up and studied in Cairo, and acted as deputy judge in Cairo, Damascus and Aleppo {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 315}.

## His works
Al-Zirikli lists his al-Ibhaj, four volumes on Shafi'i law, written as a commentary on al-Minhaj, and a large miscellany of literature {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 315}.

## His father
His father was Umar ibn Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Abi Bakr ibn al-Nusaybi, Abu Hafs, a learned Shafi'i, born and died in Aleppo, who died in 873 AH, 1469 CE; he acted as deputy judge, taught, visited Cairo and compiled a thabat, and al-Zirikli notes that he was the father of Jalal al-Din Muhammad ibn Umar {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 63}.
