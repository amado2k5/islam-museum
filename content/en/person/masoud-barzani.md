## Summary
Masoud Barzani is the dominant Kurdish politician of his generation in Iraq: president of the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) since 1979 and the first elected president of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, serving from 2005 until his resignation in November 2017 after the independence referendum he had championed. {cite:wikipedia-masoud-barzani}

## Life and formation
He was born on 16 August 1946 in Mahabad, in Iranian Kurdistan, where his father Mustafa Barzani was helping lead the short-lived Republic of Mahabad — Masoud was reportedly born the day the republic's flag was raised. After its collapse the family spent years in Soviet exile before returning to Iraq, and the teenage Barzani joined the peshmerga during the Kurdish revolts of the 1960s, foregoing formal higher education for guerrilla politics. {cite:wikipedia-masoud-barzani}

## Career
When Mustafa Barzani died in 1979, Masoud succeeded him at the head of the KDP. Through the 1980s the party fought Saddam Hussein's campaigns, including the Anfal operations in which tens of thousands of Kurds, many of them Barzanis, were killed. After the 1991 Gulf War uprising and the no-fly zone, the KDP governed part of liberated Kurdistan — fighting a civil war with Jalal Talabani's PUK in the mid-1990s before a US-brokered peace. Following the 2003 invasion the Kurdish parties joined Iraq's new order, and Barzani was elected regional president in 2005, re-elected in 2009. {cite:wikipedia-masoud-barzani}

## Referendum and resignation
After Kurdish forces and international allies pushed armed militants out of much of northern Iraq in 2014–2017, Barzani held an independence referendum on 25 September 2017 that passed overwhelmingly; Baghdad rejected it, federal forces retook Kirkuk and other disputed territories, and Barzani resigned the presidency on 1 November 2017 while retaining the KDP leadership and decisive behind-the-scenes influence. {cite:wikipedia-masoud-barzani}

## Legacy
Supporters credit him with turning Iraqi Kurdistan into a stable, oil-producing autonomous region and keeping the independence dream alive; critics cite extended rule, patronage and the referendum's fallout. The museum records both assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-masoud-barzani}
