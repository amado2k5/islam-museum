## Summary
Hussam Abu Safiya is a Palestinian paediatrician best known as the director of Kamal Adwan Hospital in northern Gaza during the months of siege, and since December 2024 as a detainee held by the Israeli authorities without any announced charge, according to human-rights organisations. {cite:wikipedia-hussam-abu-safiya}

## Early life and training
He was born on 21 November 1973 in the Jabalia refugee camp in the northern Gaza Strip, and trained in medicine abroad — at Ahmet Yassawi University — before specialising in paediatrics and neonatology, earning a master's degree and passing the Palestinian Board examinations. He then worked as a doctor for the Gaza Health Ministry. {cite:wikipedia-hussam-abu-safiya}

## Career
He rose within Kamal Adwan Hospital in Beit Lahia, one of the principal hospitals of northern Gaza, to head its paediatric department, and was appointed the hospital's director in February 2024, succeeding Ahmed al-Kahlout. Under his directorship the hospital was among the last functioning facilities in the north, and Abu Safiya appeared in videos and appeals describing shortages of fuel, medicine and food and the reception of casualties amid the collapse of the health system, becoming a public face of the sector's crisis. {cite:wikipedia-hussam-abu-safiya} {cite:muslim500-hussam-abu-safiya}

## Detention and the dispute around it
On 27 December 2024 the Israeli military carried out a forced evacuation of the hospital's staff and patients; footage showed Abu Safiya walking towards an Israeli tank before he was detained along with hundreds of others. The sources attribute to the Israeli authorities the claim that the hospital had been used by militants and that the doctor held a rank in an armed group; the doctor, his family and his advocates deny any militant affiliation or activity, and Gaza's Health Ministry and rights organisations describe him as detained in his capacity as a physician. He has been classified an "unlawful combatant" and no formal charge has been filed; Physicians for Human Rights-Israel has documented a deterioration in his health in custody, and Israel's Supreme Court has rejected petitions for his release. The museum reports these positions attributed to their holders, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-hussam-abu-safiya}

## Standing and legacy
The Muslim 500 lists him among the prominent Muslims of the present day, and his case has become — for his supporters and for international rights groups — emblematic of the situation of medical workers in conflict; calls continue for his release or for a trial on stated charges. {cite:muslim500-hussam-abu-safiya} {cite:wikipedia-hussam-abu-safiya}

## Notes on the record
His detention sits in a wider context the sources document: hundreds of health workers were detained during the Gaza war, and many of them — like Abu Safiya — were classified "unlawful combatants" under Israeli law, a designation whose legality and application rights organisations contest, since, according to Physicians for Human Rights-Israel, it leaves detainees without trial or full legal representation. In the months before his detention, press reports recorded in the cited sources say Abu Safiya lost a son in the war and continued directing the hospital regardless — a detail that has made his biography, for those who follow it, a story about medical professionalism in wartime and not only a legal dispute. {cite:wikipedia-hussam-abu-safiya}
## International attention
His case is followed by several parties inside and outside Palestine: lawyers and human-rights organisations demand an independent medical examination and either his release or a trial, international newspapers have published reports on his condition, and his family and supporters run campaigns for his freedom.
