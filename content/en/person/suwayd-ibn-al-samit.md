## Overview
Suwayd ibn al-Samit, from Yathrib, of the Banu Amr ibn Awf of the Aws. He was called "al-Kamil" (the Accomplished). al-Samhudi cites him {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.233}.

## Before Islam
The source says that in the pre-Islamic period he combined writing, archery and swimming. He was called "al-Kamil" for his strength and his poetry {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.233}.

## His meeting with the Prophet
He came to Mecca, and the Prophet invited him to Islam. He neither drew away from it nor answered it. He then returned to Yathrib, and was killed shortly afterwards, on the day of Bu'ath {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.233}.

al-Samhudi transmits from Ibn Ishaq that men of his people used to say: "We see that he was killed while he was a Muslim." This is the view of his people; the data does not link his Islam to any other evidence {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.233}.

## In al-Raheeq al-Makhtum — Safi al-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri
He came to Mecca for the pilgrimage or the lesser pilgrimage. The Prophet offered him Islam, and he said, “Perhaps what you have is like what I have.” The Prophet offered him the wisdom of Luqman. The Prophet said that he had something better than that, and he recited the Qur'an to him. Suwayd became Muslim and said, “This is indeed a good saying.” ([[event/offering-islam-to-the-tribes|the offer of Islam to the tribes]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.131} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.132} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/424-425}.
