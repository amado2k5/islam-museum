## Overview
Ubayy ibn Khalaf was one of the men of Quraysh who fought the Muslims at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]]; Ibn Ishaq tells of his death in the course of the Prophet's withdrawal into the glen. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 7}

## His threat in Mecca
Ibn Ishaq relates from Salih ibn Ibrahim ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf that Ubayy ibn Khalaf used to meet the Prophet in Mecca and say: "Muhammad, I have al-'Awdh, a horse I feed a measure of millet every day; I will kill you on it." The Prophet would reply: "Rather, I will kill you, God willing." {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 7}

## The day of Uhud
When the Prophet had been brought up into the glen, Ubayy caught up with him, saying, "Muhammad! May I not survive if you survive." The people said, "Messenger of God, shall one of us turn on him?" He said, "Leave him." When he drew near, the Prophet took the spear from al-Harith ibn al-Simma and shook himself so that those around scattered from him as flies scatter from a camel's back when it shakes; then he faced Ubayy and struck him in the neck, so that he reeled from his horse again and again. Ibn Hisham glosses the "flies" (*sha'ra'*) as a stinging fly, and "reeled" as tumbling and rolling from his horse. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 7}

## His death
He went back to Quraysh with only a small scratch on his neck, but the blood congested and he said, "By God, Muhammad has killed me!" They said, "You have lost your nerve; there is nothing wrong with you." He said, "He told me in Mecca, 'I will kill you'; by God, had he spat on me he would have killed me." He died at Sarif as they were carrying him back to Mecca. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 7} Ibn Ishaq then gives verses by [[person/hassan-ibn-thabit|Hassan ibn Thabit]] on his killing. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 8}

## In al-Suhayli's commentary
Al-Suhayli dwells on the wording, noting al-Qutabi's reading "as the hairs (*shu'r*) scatter", explained as the plural of *sha'ra'*, a fly smaller than the gadfly, and that a version other than Ibn Ishaq's has "he hurled the spear at him". {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 32}
