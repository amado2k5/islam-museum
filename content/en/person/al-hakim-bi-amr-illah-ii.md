## Overview
Al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah II, Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn al-Mustakfi, the caliph in Cairo between 741 and 753 AH. His father appointed him heir to the caliphate when he died at Qus. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.739}

## The oath of allegiance
Al-Suyuti says that when the sultan was dying, he directed that the affair should return to the heir apparent Ahmad. Al-Mansur Abu Bakr ibn al-Nasir held a council on Thursday the eleventh of Dhu al-Hijja 741 AH, attended by the caliph Ibrahim and the judges. Ibn Jama'a said that al-Mustakfi had bequeathed the caliphate to his son Ahmad and had forty upright men witness it at Qus, so Ibrahim was deposed and Ahmad given the allegiance. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.739}

He took the title of his grandfather al-Hakim. Ibn Fadl Allah describes him as the imam of their age, who revived the forms of the caliphate and united the Banu his father's kin. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.743}

## His death
He died in the plague in the middle of 753 AH. The general plague had been in his days in 749 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.753}
