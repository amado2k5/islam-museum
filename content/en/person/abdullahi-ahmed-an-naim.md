## Summary
Abdullahi Ahmed An-Na’im is a Sudanese jurist at Emory University, intellectual heir of his teacher Mahmoud Mohamed Taha, executed in Sudan in 1985. He calls for internal reform of jurisprudence grounding rights in the Meccan texts. {cite:wikipedia-abdullahi-ahmed-an-naim}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Sudan in 1946, he studied law in Khartoum, Cambridge and Edinburgh, and was shaped by the Republican movement led by Mahmoud Mohamed Taha, whose project he carried after the execution. {cite:wikipedia-abdullahi-ahmed-an-naim}

## Career and works
He has taught law at Emory University for years and works with rights organisations. His books include Toward an Islamic Reformation, proposing a method to shift rights authority to the Meccan verses, and Islam and the Secular State, arguing that state neutrality protects rather than abolishes religion. {cite:wikipedia-abdullahi-ahmed-an-naim}

## Method and contribution
He distinguishes the sharia as historical human jurisprudence from religion itself, holding that imposing sharia by state force contradicts religious freedom. He is celebrated in international human-rights literature and criticised by Islamic currents that see his project as concession; the museum attributes these positions to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-abdullahi-ahmed-an-naim}
