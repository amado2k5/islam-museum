## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ali al-Subki, Taj al-Din Abu Nasr, son of Taqi al-Din: he came to Damascus with his father in 739, read with al-Mizzi and attended al-Dhahabi. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/425} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/426} His works include Jam' al-Jawami', al-Ashbah wa-l-Naza'ir and the greater, middle and lesser Tabaqat; he became chief judge of Damascus and preacher of the Umayyad Mosque, and was tried and dismissed more than once before being restored. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/426} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/427} He died on 7 Dhu al-Hijja 771 AH. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/427} The editor's footnote gives his birth as 728. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/425}

## Offices and ordeals
Shams al-Din Ibn al-Naqib licensed him to give fatwas and teach before he was twenty; he taught in most of the madrasas of Damascus, was appointed by his father to the Dar al-Hadith al-Ashrafiyya, then held the tawqi' al-dast in 754 and the preachership of the mosque {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/425} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/426-427}. In Safar 756 the decree appointing him to the Shafi'i judgeship of Damascus arrived, on his father's footing and in his father's lifetime {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya s.a. 756 p. 263}. He was dismissed in Sha'ban 759, replaced by his brother Abu al-Baqa', and restored at the beginning of Shawwal {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/425}. In 769 he refused to confirm the guardians' receipts for the minors' fund; the regent Amir Ali dismissed him, imprisoned him and installed Siraj al-Din al-Bulqini, but he was released and restored after al-Bulqini left for Cairo {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/425-426} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 745}. He died in Damascus on the night of Tuesday 7 Dhu al-Hijja 771 {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/426-427}.
