## Overview
Zayd ibn Haritha, freedman of the Prophet, whom he adopted for a time, and one of the earliest to embrace Islam. He is the only Companion named in the Qur'an. He was killed as commander in the expedition of Mu'ta in 629 CE.

## Life
Taken captive in childhood in a raid by a tribe, he was sold in Mecca and given as a gift to [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] in the house of [[person/khadija|Khadija]]. When his father found him and came to take him back, the tradition says Zayd chose to stay. The Prophet freed him and adopted him publicly, so that he was called Zayd ibn Muhammad {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He was among the first believers. He married and divorced, and remained in the Prophet's closest circle. The Qur'an mentions "Zayd", the only Companion so named, in the context of the end of that adoption {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. At [[event/battle-of-mutah|Mu'ta]] in September 629 CE the Prophet first placed him in command, and he was killed. He was followed in command by [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far ibn Abi Talib]] and then by Abdullah ibn Rawaha {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

## Origins and capture in the Encyclopaedia of Islam
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, entry on Zayd ibn Haritha al-Kalbi, also called Zayd al-hibb, "the beloved", describes him as a slave who later became a freedman and adoptive son of [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]]. It says the details of his early life are disputed and sets out several accounts {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}.

In one account he was sold into slavery in Mecca by members of his own tribe. In the account of al-Waqidi, his father died among the tribe of his wife, [[group/tayy|Tayyi']], and left him some camels. Bedouin who had hired the camels from the youth sold him treacherously in the market of [[place/ukaz|Ukaz]], and the buyer was [[person/hakim-ibn-hizam|Hakim ibn Hizam]], who was trading for his paternal aunt [[person/khadija|Khadija]] {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}.

In the account of al-Kalbi, Zayd and his mother were visiting her tribe when horsemen of al-Kayn ibn Jasr attacked them. They sold Zayd at Ukaz to Hakim, who bought him for Khadija, and after her marriage to Muhammad she gave Zayd to him. In this version his father was still alive and vowed to search for his son. Fellow tribesmen found Zayd in Mecca, and he chose to remain with Muhammad {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}.

The article adds that Zayd's descendants passed on a different account, still current in the fourth/tenth century. In it Zayd grew up in his mother's family and stayed with his grandfather after she died. Horsemen of [[group/banu-fazara|Fazara]] sold him at Ukaz to Khadija's paternal cousin [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraka ibn Nawfal]]. A fellow tribesman recognised him, but he chose to stay with Muhammad. In this account his father, and after a while his brother Jabala, embraced Islam, and the article notes that, as the Companion dictionaries attest, the family secured Companion status for both of them {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}.

## Marriages
According to the article, Zayd's earliest marriage was to Umm Ayman, whose name was Baraka, Muhammad's dry nurse and freedwoman. It took place in Mecca, probably not long after Muhammad's call. She bore [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama]] eight or ten years before the Hijra {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}.

His other marriages took place in Medina after the Hijra. He married Muhammad's cousin [[person/zaynab-bint-jahsh|Zaynab bint Jahsh]], whose mother was Muhammad's paternal aunt. Several years later he married, one after another, three women of Quraysh, one of them a daughter of Muhammad's paternal uncle [[person/abu-lahab|Abu Lahab]]. At some stage he was also married to a woman of the Khazraj of Medina {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}.

## Adoption and the Qur'an
The article reports that Zaynab married Muhammad shortly after her marriage to Zayd. This gave rise to a claim by the so-called Hypocrites that Muhammad was acting against his own ruling, which forbade marrying the former wife of one's son. The article states that, as a result, a verse, [[passage/quran-33-5|sura 33, verse 5]], was revealed that ended the state of adoption and returned Zayd to his Kalbi affiliation. On this matter the article observes that Zayd is the only Companion whose name appears in the Qur'an, in [[passage/quran-33-37|sura 33, verse 37]] {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}. For the concept of adoption in Arab custom and its end, see [[concept/al-tabanni|al-tabanni]].

## Death at Mu'ta
The article describes Zayd as a fine archer who led several military expeditions. It says he was killed in the [[event/battle-of-mutah|battle of Mu'ta]] in 8/629, aged fifty or fifty-five {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 494}.

## Legacy
His life touches great themes: slavery and freedom, the chosen family, and how the first community changed its customs. Tradition remembers him simply as "the beloved of the Messenger of God" and his freedman.

## For young visitors
Zayd was a slave whom the Prophet freed and loved like a son. He could have returned to his family, but he chose his new one, and his name is in the Qur'an.

## His expeditions in Ibn Hisham's Sira
Ibn Ishaq counts among his expeditions raids toward al-Qardah, al-Jumum and al-Taraf. He also counts the [[event/raid-of-zayd-on-judham|raid on Judham]], in which the Prophet returned what had been taken to Rifa'a ibn Zayd and his people, and the [[event/raid-of-zayd-on-banu-fazara-and-umm-qirfa|raid on Banu Fazara at Wadi al-Qura]]. In that raid he was wounded and swore not to wash his head until he had attacked them again. He returned to them, and the Prophet ordered the killing of Umm Qirfa {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/260} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/265}.

## In the secret call (The Sealed Nectar, pp.75, 77)
The book counts him among the four who embraced Islam on the first day of the call, and names him as the Prophet's freedman Zayd ibn Haritha ibn Sharahbil al-Kalbi {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 1/50}. The footnote notes that he had been captured and enslaved. Khadija owned him and gave him to the Prophet. When his father and uncle came to take him away, the Prophet chose to keep him over them. He was adopted in the Arab manner and was called Zayd ibn Muhammad until Islam abolished adoption {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75}. A report from him describes Gabriel teaching ablution at the first of what was revealed ([[concept/al-salat-qabl-al-isra|the prayer before the Night Journey]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.77} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul-al-najdi p.88}.

## In Ta'if (The Sealed Nectar, pp.125-126)
He was with the Prophet on his journey to [[event/journey-to-taif|al-Ta'if]], and he protected him with his own body from the stones until he was wounded on the head {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.125}.

## The expedition to Madyan
Ibn Hisham relates, on the authority of Fatima bint al-Husayn, that the Prophet sent Zayd toward Madyan with Dumayra, a freedman of Ali, and a brother of his. They took captives from the people of a settlement by the water. The captives were sold and separated from one another, and the Prophet said: "Do not sell them except together." See [[event/sariyya-of-zayd-to-madyan|the sariyya]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/284}.

## For Sprouts
Zayd ibn Haritha was one of the people dearest to the Prophet and among the first to believe in him. Taken as a small boy, he was bought by Khadija, given to the Prophet, freed and adopted for a time until the Qur'an ended adoption. When offered the choice, Zayd refused to return to his family and stayed with the Prophet; he was martyred at Mu'ta in 629 CE.

## For Explorers
Zayd ibn Haritha al-Kalbi was captured as a child and bought by Khadija before her marriage to the Prophet, who freed him and called him his son until the Qur'an ended adoptive naming. When his family finally found him and asked for him back, Zayd chose to stay with the Prophet. He was among the earliest Muslims and one of the Prophet's most trusted: the Prophet married him to Zaynab bint Jahsh and then to Umm Ayman, mother of his son Usama. He led expeditions and was the first of the three named commanders at Mu'ta in 8 AH (629 CE), where he fell. He is the only companion named in the Qur'an, in the story of Zaynab.

## For Scholars
Zayd ibn Haritha ibn Sharahil al-Kalbi, the Prophet's freedman, was among the very first Muslims. Captured as a boy in a raid and sold in Mecca, he was bought by Khadija and given to the Prophet, who freed him; when his father and uncle came to ransom him, Zayd refused to leave, saying he would choose no one over the Prophet, who then proclaimed his adoption at the Ka'ba — he was known as Zayd ibn Muhammad until al-Ahzab restored lineage naming and ended the dai' adoption. The Prophet married him to Zaynab bint Jahsh; they separated, and the Prophet married Zaynab in the verse that lifted the stigma of marrying an adopted son's former wife — Zayd is the only companion named in the Qur'an, in [[passage/quran-33-37|al-Ahzab 37]]. He then married Umm Ayman, mother of Usama ibn Zayd, the Prophet's last army commander. Zayd led raids and was the first of the three commanders named for Mu'ta in 8 AH, where he was martyred; the sira treasures his childhood story and his loyalty when offered return.
