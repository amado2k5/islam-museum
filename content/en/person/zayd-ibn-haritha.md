## Brief
Zayd ibn Haritha was a freedman of the Prophet, once adopted by him, and one of the earliest Muslims. He is the only Companion named in the Qur'an, and he died commanding the expedition to Mu'ta in 629 CE.

## Life
Captive in a tribal raid as a boy, Zayd was sold in Mecca and given to [[person/muhammad]] in the household of [[person/khadija]]. When his own father traced him and came to take him home, tradition tells that Zayd chose to stay — whereupon the Prophet freed him and adopted him publicly, so that he was called Zayd ibn Muhammad {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

Among the earliest believers, he married, divorced, and remained in the Prophet's closest circle; the Qur'an's reference to "Zayd" (33:37) — the only Companion named — is bound up with the ending of that adoption {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. At [[event/battle-of-mutah]] in September 629 he held the army's first command and was killed, followed in command by [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib]] and Abd Allah ibn Rawaha {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

## Legacy
His life touches big themes: slavery and freedom, chosen family, and how the early community changed its own customs. The tradition remembers him simply: "the beloved freedman."

## For young visitors
Zayd was a slave whom the Prophet set free, then loved like a son. He could have gone home, but he chose his new family — and his name is in the Qur'an.
