## Overview
Jalal al-Din Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr al-Suyuti (849–911 AH), a memoriser from Egypt who wrote prolifically, and author of "History of the Caliphs". {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.773-781}

## Upbringing
He says in "History of the Caliphs" that his father was imam to the second caliph al-Mustakfi and wrote out for him the deed of the caliphate, and that he grew up in the house of the caliph. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.773-781}

In his biography in "Shadharat al-Dhahab", his father died when he was five years and seven months old. He finished the Qur'an before the age of eight, then memorised "Umdat al-Ahkam", the "Minhaj" of al-Nawawi and the "Alfiyya" of Ibn Malik. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.468-470}

He began learning in 864 AH, and read with a group including al-Shams al-Surami and al-Taqi al-Shamni, and attended al-'Ala al-Bulqini and al-Sharaf al-Manawi most closely. His pupil al-Shadhili relates that his teachers numbered 151 persons, and that his works exceeded five hundred. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.468-470}

## His sources for the History of the Caliphs
For events he relied on "al-Masalik" and its appendix, then on "Inba' al-Ghumr" by Ibn Hajar up to 850 AH. He consulted the "History of Baghdad" of al-Khatib and the "History of Damascus" of Ibn Asakir and others. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.773-781}

## His last years
When he reached forty he devoted himself to worship, gave up issuing fatwas and teaching, and stayed in the garden of al-Miqyas. He returned the gifts of the amirs, among them a thousand dinars from al-Ghawri. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.468-470}

Shadharat al-Dhahab relays a report from his servant about a miracle; it is a narrative attributed to its narrator, and we have not verified it.

Among his works is "al-Itqan fi Ulum al-Qur'an". {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan}

## In «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'» by Abu Ishaq al-Heweny
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] mentions that he gathered cases against the position al-Suyuti took in «al-La'ali' al-Masnu'a», where, answering Ibn al-Jawzi, he required a liar in the chain before judging a hadith fabricated; al-Heweny holds that a hadith may be fabricated while the liar in it is unknown. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep08 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep08 00:07:00} He says he had manuscripts of «Jam' al-Jawami'», which is al-Suyuti's «al-Jami' al-Kabir», that al-Suyuti ordered its hadiths by their first word only, and that al-Albani's rearrangement of «al-Jami' al-Saghir» made it the easiest book in which a student can find a hadith quickly. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:17:30} He says the Islamic Research Academy issued a part of «al-Jami' al-Kabir» every month, which spared him the manuscripts. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:19:30}

So he decided to place his own takhrijs, close to nine thousand hadiths, beside the hadiths of «al-Jami' al-Kabir»; he rearranged it, and the letter alif alone came to twenty-eight volumes, of which he finished up to the fourth or fifth, working on the section of sayings rather than deeds. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:24:30} He calls this project «al-Suhub al-Hawami'», and hopes that if he does not finish it some of his children or students will fold all his works into it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep26 00:27:00} He mentions that al-Suyuti abridged «Tarjuman al-Qur'an» into «al-Durr al-Manthur», which he owns in Dr al-Turki's edition. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep29 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep29 00:02:00}

## His completion of Tafsir al-Jalalayn
Al-Suyuti completed the commentary of [[person/jalal-al-din-al-mahalli|Jalal al-Din al-Mahalli]] from the start of Surat al-Baqara to the end of al-Isra' in al-Mahalli's manner, making the book [[work/tafsir-al-jalalayn|Tafsir al-Jalalayn]]. {cite:tafsir-jalalayn-lithograph-1278 p.4} In his closing note he says he began on 1 Ramadan 870 AH, finished on 10 Shawwal of the same year and finished the fair copy on 6 Safar 871; see [[event/al-suyuti-completes-tafsir-al-jalalayn-870|al-Suyuti completes Tafsir al-Jalalayn]]. {cite:tafsir-jalalayn-lithograph-1278 p.370} He says it had never crossed his mind to attempt it, and asks anyone who finds an error in it to tell him. {cite:tafsir-jalalayn-lithograph-1278 p.370} Muhammad Muhammad Tamir lists among his works on the Qur'anic sciences and exegesis al-Itqan fi Ulum al-Qur'an, al-Durr al-Manthur fi al-Tafsir bi'l-Ma'thur and Lubab al-Nuqul fi Asbab al-Nuzul, and reports from his student al-Dawudi that his works exceeded five hundred, and from Ibn Iyas that they reached six hundred. {cite:tafsir-jalalayn-tamir-maktabat-al-iman p.5}
