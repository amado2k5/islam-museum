İlber Ortaylı was a Turkish historian of Crimean Tatar origin, born on 21 May 1947 in a refugee camp at Bregenz, Austria, where his parents had fled Stalin's deportation of the Crimean Tatars; the family immigrated to Turkey when he was two{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-en}{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-tr}. He studied in Istanbul and Ankara and at the University of Vienna, took his master's degree at the University of Chicago under Halil İnalcık, and received his doctorate from Ankara University in 1978 with a thesis on local administration in the Tanzimat period{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-en}.

Ortaylı taught at Ankara University, resigned in 1982 in protest at the academic policy of the post-coup government, and returned as a professor in 1989; he later taught at Galatasaray and Bilkent universities{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-en}. Appointed director of the Topkapı Palace Museum in 2005, he served until his retirement in 2012, and through his newspaper columns, television programmes and popular history books became Turkey's best-known public historian{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-tr}{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-ar}. He died at Istanbul on 13 March 2026, after weeks of intensive care following complications from prostate surgery, diabetes and chronic kidney failure, and was buried in the Fatih Mosque graveyard after a memorial ceremony at Galatasaray University and the religious service at Fatih Mosque{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-en}{cite:wikipedia-ortayli-tr}.
