# Mahmoud Jibril

Mahmoud Jibril el-Warfally (28 May 1952 – 5 April 2020) was a Libyan politician and strategic-planning specialist. He chaired the executive board of the National Transitional Council during the 2011 Libyan civil war and was recognised internationally as Libya's interim prime minister from March to October 2011; he later founded and led the National Forces Alliance. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-ar}

## Early life and education
He was born on 28 May 1952 at Bayda in Libya; the date is cited in the English article to the Library of Congress authority record and the birthplace to the University of Pittsburgh and the Daily Telegraph, while the Arabic article gives the year only. He graduated in economics and political science from Cairo University in 1975, then took a master's in political science (1980) and a doctorate in strategic planning (1984) from the University of Pittsburgh, where he taught strategic planning for several years. He led the team that drafted the Unified Arab Training manual and organised and ran leadership-training programmes for senior management in many Arab countries. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-ar} {cite:wikidata-mahmoud-jibril}

## Under the Gaddafi regime
From 2007 to early 2011 he served inside the Gaddafi-era state as head of the National Planning Council and of the National Economic Development Board of Libya. The English article describes him as a protégé and close friend of Saif al-Islam Gaddafi in that period, promoting privatisation and liberalisation policies. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-en}

## The 2011 war and the National Transitional Council
When the regime fell into civil war, he joined the National Transitional Council (NTC) at its formation and chaired its executive board from 5 March to 23 October 2011, also serving as its head of international affairs and, in effect, foreign minister. Foreign governments and media increasingly treated him as Libya's interim prime minister, and his administration was recognised by the majority of UN member states as the "sole legitimate representative" of Libya. The Arabic article credits his contacts with the governments of France, the United States and the Emirates with a major role in bringing down the regime. He stepped down when Tripoli fell and the NTC moved to form a new government. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-ar}

## National Forces Alliance
In 2012 he founded the National Forces Alliance, a broad coalition that won the largest share of party-list seats in Libya's July 2012 General National Congress election, and he led the alliance from 14 March 2012 until his death. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-en}

## Death
On 21 March 2020 he suffered a cardiac arrest and was admitted to the Ganzouri Specialized Hospital in Cairo. He tested positive for COVID-19 three days later and died in Cairo on 5 April 2020, aged 67. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-en} {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-jibril-ar}
