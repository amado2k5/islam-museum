## Summary
Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith ibn Abd al-Muttalib al-Hashimi was the Prophet's cousin and his foster-brother among [[group/banu-sad-ibn-bakr|Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr]], at [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima's]] house {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55} {cite:zad-al-maad 1/19}.

## In the birth chapter of The Sealed Nectar
When the book lists the Prophet's foster-brothers in Banu Sa'd, after the sons of al-Harith ibn Abd al-Uzza it mentions his cousin Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith ibn Abd al-Muttalib {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55} {cite:zad-al-maad 1/19}. He is the son of [[person/al-harith-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Harith]], the eldest of the sons of [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52}.

## What is not covered here
This chapter does not deal with his early opposition to the call, his conversion shortly before the conquest of Mecca, or his poetry. What the data record says about these comes from other sources, and it will be completed when the museum reaches those points in the book.

## In Khalid Muhammad Khalid's Rijal Hawl al-Rasul
[[person/khalid-muhammad-khalid|Khalid Muhammad Khalid]] relates that Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith, the Prophet's cousin, had fought him and satirised him; when he resolved to embrace Islam he feared someone in the army would take revenge first, so he disguised himself and, holding his son Ja'far by the hand, threw himself before the Prophet, who turned away; then both declared the shahada, and he said "No reproach, Messenger of God", and the Prophet answered "No reproach, Abu Sufyan", entrusted him to Ali to teach him ablution and the Sunna, and had it announced that the Messenger of God was pleased with him. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.545} He notes that Abu Sufyan had fought at Badr with Quraysh, and was the one who told Abu Lahab at Zamzam of Quraysh's defeat, saying they had met "white men on piebald horses between heaven and earth". {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.545} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.546} At Hunayn he was among those who stood firm when the army broke, holding the reins of the Prophet's mount with his left hand and striking with his sword in his right; when the dust settled the Prophet said, "Who is this? My brother Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith?" {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.547} The chapter ends with his digging his own grave three days before his death and telling his family: "Do not weep for me; I have not stained myself with a sin since I became Muslim." {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.548}

## For young visitors
Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith was the Prophet's cousin. He was nursed beside him at Halima's in the desert, and so became his foster-brother.
