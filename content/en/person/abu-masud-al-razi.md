## Summary
Ahmad ibn al-Furat ibn Khalid, Abu Mas'ud al-Dabbi al-Razi, the hafiz: [[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]] opens his entry in [[work/tarikh-dimashq|Tarikh Dimashq]] with "one of the trustworthy imams and firm hafizes". {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 150} He heard in [[place/damascus|Damascus]] and elsewhere from Hisham ibn Isma'il al-Attar, Abu al-Yaman al-Hakam ibn Nafi', Abu Salih the secretary of al-Layth, Abd Allah ibn Numayr, Abu Usama, [[person/abd-al-razzaq-al-sanani|Abd al-Razzaq]], [[person/abu-dawud-al-tayalisi|Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi]], Yazid ibn Harun, Abu Nu'aym and many others. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 150} [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] narrates from him in his Sunan, as do Abu Khalifa, Ja'far al-Firyabi, [[person/ibn-abi-asim|Ibn Abi Asim]] and a number of the people of [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]]. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 151}

## Travels and settling in Isfahan
Ibn Asakir quotes al-Khatib that he travelled widely, hearing and collecting in Basra, Kufa, the Hijaz, Yemen, Syria, Egypt and the Jazira, met the scholars of his age, and came to [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]] in the lifetime of [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]], exchanging hadith with its hafizes in Ahmad's presence; Ahmad honoured him and gave him precedence. He then settled in Isfahan for the rest of his life, and he is not known to have narrated in Baghdad except in learned exchange. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 156} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 157} Abu Nu'aym says he first came to Isfahan before going to Iraq, then travelled and returned to settle there, teaching for forty-five years and compiling a Musnad and other books. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 156}

## His memory
Ibn Asakir relates that in a session where others produced five hadiths on a topic, Abu Mas'ud produced a sixth, and Ahmad ibn Hanbal prodded him in the chest in admiration. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 153} Ahmad asked a man from Isfahan: is Abu Mas'ud among you? Told yes, he said: I know no one today who knows the Prophet's connected hadiths better. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 153} Abu Aruba said Abu Mas'ud ranks with Ibn Abi Shayba in memory. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 153} He is quoted as saying: I wrote from 1,750 men and included 310 in my compilation; I wrote down 1,500,000 hadiths and took from them 300,000 on Qur'an commentary, law and other matters. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 154} It is reported that he arrived in Isfahan without his books and dictated thousands of hadiths from memory, and when the books arrived and were compared they differed only in a few places. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 154} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 155} He told against himself that once he memorised thirty hadiths in Yazid ibn Harun's session, and when he got home to note them down his maid told him the flour had run out — and he forgot twenty-seven. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 155} He is also quoted as saying: I would love to be killed for my love of Abu Bakr and Umar. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 155}

## Death
Abu Nu'aym said he died in Sha'ban 258 AH, that the judge Ibrahim ibn Ahmad al-Khattabi led his funeral prayer and that he was buried in the Mudhaqan cemetery; the nephew of Rusta also gives 258, while Abd Allah ibn Ja'far ibn Faris gives 268. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 156} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 157} Ibn Asakir closes with a dream seen by Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Banna, which he relates with its chain. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 157}
