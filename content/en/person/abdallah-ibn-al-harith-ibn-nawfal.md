## Overview
Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal al-Hashimi, Abu Muhammad al-Qurashi al-Madani, nicknamed Babba, is called by al-Dhahabi the chief and the emir {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. He says he was born in the Prophet's lifetime, and Ibn Sa'd calls him reliable, a Successor, whose mother brought him to the Prophet, who put some of his own saliva in his mouth and prayed for him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. Al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar says his mother was Hind, sister of Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan, and al-Dhahabi adds that she was also the sister of the Mother of the Believers Umm Habiba {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. He notes that his father al-Harith was governor of Mecca for Umar and died in Uthman's time {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Umar, Uthman, Ali, Ubayy ibn Ka'b, al-Abbas, Safwan ibn Umayya, Hakim ibn Hizam, Umm Hani bint Abi Talib, Ka'b al-Ahbar and others; he sent one report in mursal form and was present at al-Jabiya with Umar {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. His sons Ishaq and Abd Allah narrated from him, as did al-Zuhri, Abu al-Tayyah Yazid ibn Humayd, Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad, Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr, Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and others; he narrated several hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}.

## Governor of Basra
When Yazid died and Ubayd Allah ibn Ziyad fled to Syria, the leading men of Basra agreed to make him their emir, then wrote their allegiance to Ibn al-Zubayr, who confirmed him over them and later dismissed him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. In the revolt of Ibn al-Ash'ath he fled to Syria for fear of al-Hajjaj {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. Al-Dhahabi says he was one of the chiefs of Banu Hashim, fit for the caliphate because of his knowledge and eminence {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}.

## His death
It is said he died in Oman in 84 AH, while Abu Ubayd says 83 {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. Al-Dhahabi adds that he lived somewhat over seventy years, close to eighty {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}. The same page of the book also carries a separate entry under the same name for the nephew of the Companion Muhmiya ibn Jaz' al-Zubaydi, an Egyptian narrator who is a different man and is not the subject here {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2363}.
