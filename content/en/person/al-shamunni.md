## Summary
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Hasan ibn Ali al-Shumunni, Abu al-Abbas Taqi al-Din — of Constantine origin, born in Alexandria, raised in Cairo, a Hanafi — was a hadith scholar, Qur'an commentator and grammarian who died in Cairo in 872 AH (1468 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.229} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} He wrote [[work/muzil-al-khafa-an-alfaz-al-shifa|Muzil al-Khafa' an Alfaz al-Shifa']], the gloss printed beneath Qadi Iyad's [[work/al-shifa-bi-tarif-huquq-al-mustafa|al-Shifa]]. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.2}

## Early life and change of school
He was born in [[place/alexandria|Alexandria]] in the last ten days of Ramadan 801 AH and grew up in [[place/cairo|Cairo]]; the name al-Shumunni refers to a farm or village in the Maghrib. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} He first studied as a Maliki and then became a Hanafi, reportedly because [[person/al-bisati|al-Bisati]] had preferred a less able fellow student over him. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6}

## Learning and teaching
He excelled in jurisprudence, both kinds of usul, Arabic, rhetoric (ma'ani and bayan), logic, morphology, geometry, astronomy and arithmetic, heard hadith from a number of teachers, and studied parts of the commentary on al-Iraqi's *Alfiyya* with the biographer's own teacher, staying close to him. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} The biography tells how that teacher admired his instant answer on why the hadith "let them create a grain" moves as it does from the larger to the smaller: making fine things is harder, and the command is a challenge of incapacity, so descending from the greater to the lesser fits. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} He taught the rational sciences without notebook or gloss; two learned Persians once found him teaching the *Mutawwal* from memory, debated him and could not stump him, and he told them he had taught it twelve times without consulting it. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} So many benefited that most of the learned men of Egypt, of every school, became his students. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.7}

## Character
The biography calls him a learned imam, a Sunni of solid religion associated with Sufism, dignified and cheerful, a lover of hadith and its people, who lived in a retreat in the Jamaliyya quarter. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.7} Qanibay al-Jarkasi appointed him preacher of his funerary complex and shaykh of its Sufis, and he moved there. He did not bend in religious matters: he returned the gift of a young man of a notable family who asked his licence to teach and refused the licence, and he declined the Hanafi chief judgeship even when the confidential secretary came to tell him that the sultan would come down to him himself if he did not accept. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.7}

## Works
His works include *al-Munsif min al-Kalam ala Mughni Ibn Hisham*, a gloss on *Mughni al-Labib* abridged from al-Damamini's gloss with additions; *Muzil al-Khafa' an Alfaz al-Shifa'*, notes on the exact reading of the words of *al-Shifa*, abridged from Burhan al-Halabi's commentary with supplements; and *Kamal al-Diraya fi Sharh al-Niqaya* on Hanafi law. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.6} {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.229} The colophon of *Muzil al-Khafa'* says he finished it in the last ten days of Dhu al-Qa'da 847 AH. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.696}

## Death
He died on the night of Sunday 17 Dhu al-Hijja 872 AH in his lodging in the funerary complex where he preached; the prayer was said for him at its gate and he was buried there, leaving two sons, a daughter and a thousand dinars. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.7}

## For young visitors
Al-Shumunni was a scholar from Alexandria who lived in Cairo. He knew difficult books by heart and taught them without notes, and he wrote a short guide that explains how to read the words of the book *al-Shifa*, to make it easier for students.

## Research notes
- His biography in the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya printing of *al-Shifa* (pp. 6–7) is, by its footnote, taken from *al-Badr al-Tali'* as drawn from *al-Daw' al-Lami'*; the original biographer speaks in the first person ("our shaykh", "I attended many of his lessons").
