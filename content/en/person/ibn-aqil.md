## Overview
Abu al-Wafa' Ali ibn Aqil al-Baghdadi, shaykh of the Hanbalis. Al-Suyuti relates that he washed the caliph al-Mustazhir when he died in 512 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 661}

## Repentance
He had studied kalam according to the Mu'tazili doctrine. When al-Sharif Abu Ja'far and other shaykhs objected to what they found in his own hand in the books about the doctrines of innovators, he wrote on the tenth of Muharram 465 AH a deed of repentance, in which he disowned the doctrines of the innovators, the Mu'tazila, and the company of their adherents. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.275-276}

Ibn al-Jawzi says that a large number of witnesses testified to this. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.275-276}

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] holds up Ibn Aqil al-Hanbali as an example of guarding one's time: he says Ibn Aqil wasted nothing of his life, that his student [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] took this trait from him, and he quotes him as choosing to swallow ka'k soaked in water rather than bread, because of the difference in chewing between them. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:44:30}

He says Ibn Aqil had a book in eight hundred volumes, al-Funun; that Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali says in Dhayl Tabaqat al-Hanabila that he saw a volume of it numbered beyond four hundred; and that al-Heweny owns the two volumes that survive, which he finds hard reading because Ibn Aqil wrote down whatever came to his mind. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:46:30}
