## Summary
Ali al-Sallabi is a Libyan historian and writer who joined a large historical project to political work in his country. He authored dozens of volumes on the Prophet’s biography and Islamic states for the general Arabic reader. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sallabi}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Libya in 1963, he studied history and sharia, was influenced by Libya’s Islamic current, and spent years in exile before the 2011 revolution. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sallabi}

## Career and works
His works include an extended sira, a series on the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the volumes The Ottoman State and The Seljuk State, plus books on the jurisprudence of balances and national reconciliation. Politically he was associated with Yusuf al-Qaradawi and played a mediating role after the Libyan revolution. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sallabi}

## Method and contribution
His compendious style presents events narratively with moral and political lessons. His histories are read as useful popularisations but criticised by some specialists for padding and weak academic documentation, and his political role in Libya is contested by opponents; the museum reports these descriptions attributed. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sallabi}
