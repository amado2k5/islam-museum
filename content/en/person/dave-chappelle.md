## Summary
David Khari Webber Chappelle (born 24 August 1973) is an American stand-up comedian and actor, born in Washington, D.C., who converted to Islam when he was seventeen. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} {cite:wikidata-dave-chappelle} The Arabic article dates his conversion to 1991, and he told Time magazine in 2005 that he does not normally talk about his religion publicly because he does not want people to associate him and his flaws with "this beautiful thing". {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-ar} {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

He co-created and starred in the sketch series Chappelle's Show on Comedy Central (2003–2006), then abruptly left during production of its third season despite a contract offer of about $55 million, and later returned with stand-up tours and a series of Netflix specials. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} He received the Mark Twain Prize for American Humor in 2019, America's highest comedy honour, and has won six Grammy Awards for Best Comedy Album. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

## Career
Chappelle was born in Washington, D.C. and grew up in Silver Spring, Maryland; his father was a professor of vocal performance and dean of students at Antioch College, and his mother a professor and a Unitarian Universalist minister. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} He graduated in 1991 from the Duke Ellington School of the Arts in Washington, where he studied theatre arts, then moved to New York to work as a comedian, and his 1992 appearance on Def Comedy Jam began his rise. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-ar}

His first starring role was the 1998 comedy Half Baked, which he co-wrote; earlier film appearances included Robin Hood: Men in Tights (1993) and The Nutty Professor (1996). {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} He recorded his first hour-long special, Killin' Them Softly, for HBO in 2000. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

Chappelle's Show debuted in 2003, parodying aspects of American culture from racial stereotypes to politics and pop culture, with musical performances mostly by hip-hop and soul artists. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} In May 2005, during production of the third season, Chappelle abruptly travelled to South Africa; he told Time he was unhappy with the show's direction and needed reflection under tremendous stress, and later told Oprah Winfrey that burnout, loss of creative control and an uncomfortable work environment were among his reasons for leaving. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

After years of infrequent appearances he returned to full-time touring in 2013, hosted Saturday Night Live after the 2016 election — winning an Emmy for it — and in 2016 signed a Netflix deal of $20 million per special, releasing six specials under it and more afterwards. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} In 2020 he released the short special 8:46 on the killing of George Floyd, and his company later set up a comedy club in Yellow Springs, Ohio, the village where he lives with his family. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

## Works
His best-known works include the sketch series Chappelle's Show (2003–2006); the documentary Dave Chappelle's Block Party (2005), in which he hosted a free concert in Brooklyn with musicians including Kanye West, Mos Def and The Roots; and the films Undercover Brother (2002), Chi-Raq (2015) and A Star Is Born (2018). {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} His Netflix specials include The Age of Spin, Deep in the Heart of Texas, Equanimity and The Bird Revelation (2017), Sticks & Stones (2019), The Closer (2021) and The Dreamer (2023). {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out positions and responses as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

The 2019 special Sticks & Stones drew criticism for jokes about the LGBTQ community and the #MeToo movement, yet won the Grammy Award for Best Comedy Album. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} The Closer (2021) drew a wider response: advocacy groups condemned it as transphobic, Netflix employees staged a walkout demanding its removal, while Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos defended keeping it on creative-freedom grounds, and Chappelle said he was not anti-transgender but critical of political correctness. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

In July 2022 the First Avenue venue in Minneapolis cancelled a show on the day of the performance, citing backlash at his earlier material; he performed that night at another theatre in the same city. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} Jonathan Greenblatt, national director of the Anti-Defamation League, criticised his 2022 Saturday Night Live monologue on rising antisemitism as perpetuating stereotypes. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} In September 2025 he performed at the inaugural Riyadh Comedy Festival, which Human Rights Watch criticised as an event used to improve the Saudi government's image. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

Against this, critics and writers have since 2006 called him the "comic genius of America", and Rolling Stone ranked him No. 9 in its 2017 list of the "50 Best Stand Up Comics of All Time". {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en} In politics, he endorsed Andrew Yang in the 2020 presidential election; at a Boston show in October 2023 he described Israel's actions in the Gaza war as war crimes while also condemning Hamas's October 7 attacks and United States aid to Israel, to a mixed audience reaction. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}

On his faith, the sources record his appearance in a video explaining the religious history of the Well of Zamzam in Mecca, and his statement that his faith helped him use his career to benefit people. {cite:wikipedia-dave-chappelle-en}
