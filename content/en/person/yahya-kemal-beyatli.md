## Summary
Yahya Kemal Beyatlı (born Ahmet Âgâh; 2 December 1884 – 1 November 1958) was a Turkish poet, author, politician and diplomat, born at Skopje and died at Istanbul. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en} {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-tr} Wikidata carries two death values for him (1 and 2 November 1958); this entry follows the three articles, which agree on 1 November. {cite:wikidata-yahya-kemal-beyatli} He wrote as Yahya Kemal and under pen names including Agâh Kemal, Esrar, Mehmet Agâh and Süleyman Sadi, and took Beyatlı as his surname after the 1934 Surname Law. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en} His poetry is described as a modern continuation of the classical tradition, built on a close feel for the rhythm and melody of Turkish, and his poem "Aziz İstanbul" is among his most famous praises of the city. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en}

## Life and career
Yahya Kemal was born at Skopje in 1884, grew up in a household where hymns and chants were sung, and received his first lessons in religion from his mother. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en} He left for Paris as a young man in 1903 and moved in anti-regime exile circles, returned to Istanbul in 1912, and became a professor of the History of Western Literature at Istanbul University in 1915 on Ziya Gökalp's recommendation. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en} He co-founded the magazine Dergâh with Ahmet Haşim in 1921, sat in parliament as deputy for Urfa (1923–1926), Yozgat, Tekirdağ and Istanbul, and served as ambassador to Poland (1926–1929) and Portugal (1930–1932) and envoy to Madrid, then as Turkey's first ambassador to Pakistan in 1947; his health failing, he returned to Istanbul in 1949. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en} {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-tr} He died at Istanbul on 1 November 1958 and was buried at Aşiyan Asri Cemetery. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-tr}

## Works
His prose appeared in volume after his death: Aziz İstanbul (1964), Eğil Dağlar (1966), Siyasi Hikayeler (1968), Siyasi ve Edebi Portreler (1968), Edebiyata Dair (1971), Tarih Müsahabeleri (1975) and Bitmemiş Şiirler (1976), together with his memoirs Çocukluğum, Gençliğim, Siyasi ve Edebi Hatıralarım (1973). {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en} Much of his verse is in the classical aruz metre with a renewed imagery, and his celebrated poem "Sessiz Gemi" (The Silent Ship) joins melody to a longing for what is past. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-tr}

## Positions and criticism
This section is read as a presentation of positions reported from the sources, not as a verdict of the museum.

The sources present his devotion to the classical Turkish poetic heritage and his effort to renew rather than break it, so that his verse became a bridge between Ottoman and Republican literature. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-en} The English article also notes that his poetry reveals a sensibility towards Islam and that his mother was his first teacher of religion — but no source names a school for the man himself: the Arabic article's infobox religion field is empty, and Wikidata gives only the generic value Islam, so his school is left unnamed in this entry. {cite:wikipedia-yahya-kemal-beyatli-ar} {cite:wikidata-yahya-kemal-beyatli}
