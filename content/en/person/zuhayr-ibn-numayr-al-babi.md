## Summary
Abu Abd al-Rahman Zuhayr ibn Numayr al-Babi was a preacher whose life was given to patience and certainty, and who was aided with victory and firm standing. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 513, sequence 514, on pages 147 to 150. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/147} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/150}

## Patience and certainty
Ahmad ibn Asim relates that Zuhayr said this matter is not completed except by two things, patience and certainty: certainty without patience is not complete, and patience without certainty is not complete. Abu al-Darda gave them an example of two ploughmen digging the earth, so that when one sits the other sits. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/147}

## Zuhayr's farewell
Ahmad ibn Asim relates that he heard his maternal uncle Abd al-Aziz ibn Yusuf say: I wanted to leave Basra, and I began with Yahya ibn Said, then Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, then Zuhayr, and asked whether he needed anything. He said: yes, one thing that is important: fear God, for by God, a man who fears Him is dearer to me than these pillars turned to gold. When he turned to leave, Zuhayr called him back with another request: do not enter a judge's presence, nor the presence of anyone who enters a judge's; in this land for fifty years I have not looked at the face of a judge or a governor. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/147}

## The blind reader and this world
Ahmad ibn Asim walked holding Zuhayr's hand and they came to a blind man reciting. When Zuhayr heard him he stopped and said: do not be deceived by his recitation; by God, it is worse than singing and the lute. Days later the man rose toward the Banu Qushayr; Ahmad greeted him and reminded him of Zuhayr's words, and Zuhayr replied that seeking this world with drums, song and the lute is better than seeking it through religion. He also said: I do not know that I have trusted God for a single hour. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/148}

## The plague and the boy
Husayn ibn Jumayl relates that Zuhayr said: if you can be more base than a dog in God's sight, do so. When word came to them from Isfahan of many deaths and famine, Zuhayr said: do not feel safe from death because it is rare, and do not fear it because it is many. He told of a man who had lived through the plague: they walked through the tribes and buried the dead; when the dead were too many they sealed the houses where the people had died. Once they entered a house, searched it, found no one alive, and sealed its door. When the plague passed they came back, opened the door, and found a boy alive in the middle of the house, a dog having come through a crack in the wall and nursed him. Zuhayr said that later he saw this boy in the mosque of Basra, holding his beard. He often recited a line whose sense is: how long will you busy yourself with your world while the One who works for God is busy beyond it? {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/148} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/149}

## Advice on parting
Al-Bahili relates that he led Zuhayr, and when he wanted to part from him he asked for advice. Zuhayr said: when you see a man who does not deal justly with himself, if you can, do not see him at all. When Zuhayr lost his sight late in life, one of his brothers met him and greeted him; Zuhayr asked who the man was, and when the man was shaken, Zuhayr saw his distress and said: brother, I had a crust with a little fat, and when it fell, its loss grieved me more than the loss of my sight. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/149}

## Parents and prayer
Ahmad ibn Asim relates that he entered on Zuhayr and asked whether he had a father; he said no; a mother; he said no. Zuhayr said: God is greatest; how long does a branch remain after its root? Pray and beseech God for them, for it has reached me that God raises parents through the blood of their children, and he raised his hands. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/149}

## Accusation of heresy and lineage
Abd al-Rahman ibn Umar relates that a man of the Qadariyya said to Zuhayr: I hear that you are a heretic. Zuhayr said: heretic, heretic? No, I am not a heretic, but a bad man. Sahl ibn Asim relates that a man asked Zuhayr where he came from; he said: from one whom God blessed with Islam. The man said he meant lineage; Zuhayr answered with a verse on the absence of lineages on the Day of Resurrection, which is not typed here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/149}

## Speech, this world and the people
Zuhayr told Sahl ibn Asim: fear God, for that is dearer to me than that this wall should become gold. He said: a man repenting is dearer to me than God returning my sight, and a man repenting is dearer to me than the pillars of the mosque becoming gold for me. He said: I have kept company with people for fifty years and have seen none who did not follow his desire; I dislike hearing the sound of a beating in my neighbour's house and being told that so-and-so erred. A man who swore by God he heard Zuhayr say: we are more like those who do not believe in God than I am like those who do; some who heard it wept, some cried out, some shook, and some were stunned. Zuhayr said he wished his body were cut with shears so that people would obey God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/149} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/150}

## His state after the fall
Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Ghaffar al-Kirmani relates that he climbed to Zuhayr after he had fallen from his roof, his sight gone, his face shattered. He asked how he was, and Zuhayr said: as you see; I would not be pleased to be stronger than this creation; this is the world, let it do as it wishes. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/150}
