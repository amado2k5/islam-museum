M'hamed Chenik (1889–1976) was a Tunisian politician and businessman born at Tunis in May 1889 to a bourgeois family.{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-fr}{cite:wikidata-mhamed-chenik} He left Sadiki College on his father's sudden death, qualified as a chartered accountant and rose to co-direct the Union commerciale tunisienne, the first specifically Tunisian economic organisation; meeting Abdelaziz Thâalbi at Marseille in July 1919, he joined the new Destour party.{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-fr} In 1922 he founded the Tunisian Credit Cooperative, the first Tunisian bank, and presided over the Tunisian Chamber of Commerce from 1921 to 1942 and over the Tunisian section of the Grand Conseil.{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-fr}{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-en} In 1939 he founded the STUFIT spinning and weaving company, which he directed until his death, and he farmed more than a thousand hectares as one of the first Tunisians to mechanise agriculture.{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-en}

Moncef Bey chose Chenik to head his national-union government of January to May 1943, cut short by the bey's deposition.{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-fr}{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-ar} Under Lamine Bey he again headed the government from August 1950 to March 1952, negotiating with France for internal autonomy; when France broke off the talks, Chenik carried the Tunisian case to the United Nations in January 1952 and was arrested with his ministers that March and assigned to residence in the south.{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-fr} After independence in 1956 he opened the Constituent Assembly as its oldest member and withdrew from politics; he died at Radès on 20 November 1976, and President Bourguiba honoured him at a solemn ceremony.{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-fr}{cite:wikipedia-mhamed-chenik-ar}
