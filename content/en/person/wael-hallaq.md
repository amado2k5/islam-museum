## Summary
Wael Hallaq is a Palestinian-Canadian professor at Columbia, among the leading academic voices on the history of Islamic jurisprudence in the West. His project combines the institutional history of the sharia with a critique of European modernity. {cite:wikipedia-wael-hallaq}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Nazareth in 1955, he studied in Haifa then emigrated to the United States, taking a doctorate at the University of Washington, embracing Islam and entering academic Islamic studies. {cite:wikipedia-wael-hallaq}

## Career and works
He taught at McGill University in Canada then Columbia in New York. His books include Shari’a: Theory, Practice, Transformations, charting jurisprudence across the ages; The Impossible State, arguing that modern-state categories contradict the logic of sharia governance; and Restating Orientalism, a critique of Edward Said’s method. {cite:wikipedia-wael-hallaq}

## Method and contribution
Hallaq argues a modern “Islamic state” is logically impossible because the sharia was a decentralised moral order, not a state apparatus. His thesis is received seriously in the academy and contested by Islamists who see the state as a legitimate goal and by Orientalists who reject his generalisations; the museum attributes these positions without adjudicating. {cite:wikipedia-wael-hallaq}
