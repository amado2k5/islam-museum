## Overview
Abd Allah ibn Amir al-Yahsibi, kunya Abu Imran, one of the seven readers. It is said that he took the Qur'an from [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]] and recited to him. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.75}

He is in the first generation of the Successors from the people of Damascus, and died there in 118 AH. He transmitted from a group of the Companions, among them Wathila ibn al-Asqa, Fadala ibn Ubayd and Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan. Those who transmitted from him include Yahya ibn al-Harith al-Dhamari, Isma'il ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi al-Muhajir, Hisham ibn al-Ghaz and Thawr ibn Yazid. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.75}

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

He is Abu 'Imran 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amir ibn Yazid ibn Tamim ibn Rabi'a al-Yahsubi, imam of the people of al-Sham. He read on Abu Hashim al-Mughira ibn Abi Shihab al-Makhzumi, without dispute among the verifiers, and on [[person/abu-al-darda|Abu al-Darda']], as al-Dani asserted and Ibn al-Jazari finds sound; al-Mughira read on [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|'Uthman ibn 'Affan]], and 'Uthman and Abu al-Darda read on the Prophet. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 154} He died in Damascus on the day of 'Ashura' 118 AH. His birth is put in 21 AH or in 8 AH, with disagreement. Ibn al-Jazari calls him a great imam and an eminent successor who led the Muslims in prayer in the Umayyad Mosque for many years in the days of 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz and before and after him, so that the caliph prayed behind him; the imamate, the judgeship and the headship of teaching the reading were joined in him, and people agreed on his reading and accepted it. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 154} His two transmitters are [[person/hisham-ibn-ammar|Hisham ibn 'Ammar]] and [[person/ibn-dhakwan|Ibn Dhakwan]], and Ibn al-Jazari counts a hundred and thirty lines of transmission. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 154}
