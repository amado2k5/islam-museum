Jamal al-Husayni (1893 – 5 July 1982) was a Palestinian politician from Jerusalem, educated at St George's School and, briefly, in medicine at the Syrian Protestant College in Beirut before the First World War cut his studies short.{cite:wikipedia-jamal-al-husayni-en}{cite:wikipedia-jamal-al-husayni-ar} By 1921 he was secretary of the Executive Committee of the Palestine Arab Congress and served the Supreme Muslim Council in the same capacity; in April 1935 he founded the Palestine Arab Party, and in 1937 he joined the Arab Higher Committee, whose chairman he later became.{cite:wikipedia-jamal-al-husayni-en}

Exiled when the Mandate authorities banned the nationalist organisations in 1937, he led the Arab delegation to the London Conference of 1939, was interned in Southern Rhodesia from 1941 to 1945, and represented the Palestinian Arabs before the Anglo-American Committee and at the United Nations in 1947–48.{cite:wikipedia-jamal-al-husayni-en}{cite:wikidata-jamal-al-husayni} In the autumn of 1948 he was foreign minister of the All-Palestine Government; from 1950 he joined the entourage of King Abdul Aziz of Saudi Arabia and became a close adviser to King Saud, and he died at Riyadh on 5 July 1982.{cite:wikipedia-jamal-al-husayni-en}{cite:wikipedia-jamal-al-husayni-ar}
