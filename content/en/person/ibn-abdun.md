Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abdun al-Tujibi was a writer and jurist of Seville who lived in the second half of the 5th/11th century, long enough to witness the end of al-Mu'tamid's reign and the Almoravid period. Very little is known of his life: none of the sources read gives a firm birth or death date, and the catalogue tradition dates his death to 529 AH (1134/35 CE) {cite:wikipedia-ibn-abdun-ar}{cite:garcia-sanjuan-ibn-abdun-2008}.

## The treatise on the judiciary and hisba

His one securely attributed work is the Risala fi'l-qada' wa'l-hisba (Treatise on the Judiciary and Market Inspection), among the earliest surviving manuals of hisba - the regulation of markets and public morals - in al-Andalus. Levi-Provencal edited it in 1955 in his collection of three Andalusi hisba treatises, and a Dar Ibn Hazm edition appeared in 2009. The treatise portrays Almoravid Seville, including its rules for the protected Jewish and Christian communities, which Alejandro Garcia-Sanjuan analysed in a 2008 study {cite:wikipedia-ibn-abdun-ar}{cite:garcia-sanjuan-ibn-abdun-2008}.

## Was he a qadi?

Ibn Abdun is often styled a qadi, but Garcia-Sanjuan reports Pedro Chalmeta's argument that he may never have held the office - that he was merely a candidate for the qadiship. This record therefore describes him as the jurist who wrote on the judiciary and hisba, without asserting that he served as a judge {cite:garcia-sanjuan-ibn-abdun-2008}.

## Not the poet of the Abduniyya

He must not be confused with Abd al-Majid ibn Abdun, the Badajoz court poet whose Abduniyya qasida laments the fall of the Banu al-Aftas: a different man, with a different full name, city and profession, who shares only the Ibn Abdun name. A third namesake, Muhammad ibn Abdun al-Jabali, was a physician and mathematician. This record covers the Sevillian jurist and hisba author alone {cite:wikidata-ibn-abdun}{cite:garcia-sanjuan-ibn-abdun-2008}.
