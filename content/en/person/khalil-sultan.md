Khalil Sultan was born at Herat on 24 Jumada II 786 AH (13 August 1384), the son of [[person/miran-shah|Miran Shah]] by his wife Sevin Beg, called Khanzada, and so a grandson of [[person/timur-lang|Timur]].{cite:wikipedia-khalil-sultan-en}{cite:wikidata-khalil-sultan} He enjoyed his grandfather's particular favour, distinguished himself in the Indian campaign, and in 1402 was given rule of the Ferghana Valley.{cite:wikipedia-khalil-sultan-en}

On Timur's death in 1405 Khalil viewed himself as the successor: he gained control of Samarkand and of Timur's treasury, set up the nine-year-old prince Muhammad Jahangir with the puppet title of Khan, struck coinage in his own name, and had the khutba read for himself.{cite:wikipedia-khalil-sultan-en} His position then weakened. The Samarkand nobility despised his wife Shad Malik Agha and her influence — Woods cautions that this picture derives from a hostile narrative tradition that laid his misjudgements, and the loss of Samarkand, entirely at her door — and famine made him more hated still.{cite:wikipedia-khalil-sultan-en} On 13 May 1409 Shah Rukh entered Samarkand unopposed; Khalil surrendered to him, received back his captured wife, and was appointed governor of Rayy.{cite:wikipedia-khalil-sultan-en}

Khalil Sultan died at Rayy on 3 November 1411 (Wikidata gives 4 November).{cite:wikipedia-khalil-sultan-en}{cite:wikidata-khalil-sultan} Hafiz-i Abru and the Nusakh-i jahan-ara report that he died of an illness, and Woods lists his death as natural causes; Ibn Arabshah claimed he had been poisoned by Shah Rukh — a claim Woods considers unfounded.{cite:wikipedia-khalil-sultan-en}
