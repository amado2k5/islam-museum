## Brief
The Negus, whom the Arabic sources name Ashama ibn Abjar, was the Christian king of Abyssinia who took in the first Muslim emigrants and refused to hand them over. In tradition he is the model of a just foreign ruler.

## Life
When the harm in Mecca grew long, the Prophet sent a group of Muslims across the sea to Abyssinia, describing its king as one under whom "no one is wronged." Mecca sent envoys asking for the emigrants to be returned {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. In *The Sealed Nectar*, the Prophet is described as calling him "a just king under whom no one is wronged," and advising the Muslims to emigrate to his land to save their religion {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

At the Negus's throne, [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far]] described what his people had been and what the Prophet called them to. He then recited from the Qur'an's account of Mary and Jesus. The sources say that the king wept until his beard was wet, and said that the difference between their two faiths is no more than a line drawn in the sand {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. He sheltered the [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|first group]] in the best of neighbourliness, refused to hand the emigrants over to the two Qurashi envoys, and returned the gifts of [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-rabia|Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'a]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

The emigrants remained under his protection for years. A later tradition adds that the Negus himself embraced Islam, and that the Prophet performed the funeral prayer for him in absentia. Historians treat this as tradition rather than documented fact {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
In Muslim memory, Abyssinia is the first foreign land that sheltered Muslims, and the Negus stands at the head of this museum's gallery of protectors who were not themselves of the community.

## For young visitors
A Christian king was asked to return a group of emigrants. He heard their story, refused, and gave them safety, and Muslims have honoured him for it ever since.

## The Quraysh delegation to him (*The Sealed Nectar*, pp. 94–96)
Quraysh sent Amr ibn al-As and Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'a with gifts, asking for the return of the emigrants. The Negus heard from [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far]] the opening of the chapter of Mary, and wept until his beard was wet. He refused to hand the emigrants over and returned the gifts ([[event/quraysh-envoys-to-the-negus|the Quraysh delegation to the Negus]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.95} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.96}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] gives a whole lesson to Umm Salama's hadith of the migration to Abyssinia, which he calls established, narrated by Ibn Ishaq in the Sira and by Ahmad in the Musnad by way of him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:04:00} In his telling the Negus refused to hand over people who had sought his protection without first hearing them; [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far ibn Abi Talib]] spoke for the Muslims and recited the opening of Kaf Ha Ya Ayn Sad, and the Negus and his bishops wept until their beards and their scrolls were wet, and the Negus said that this and what Moses brought came from one lamp-niche. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:13:00} When Ja'far was asked about Jesus he said Jesus was God's servant and messenger, His spirit and His word cast into Mary, and the Negus said Jesus was not more than that by the length of a twig; he returned the gifts, saying God had taken no bribe from him when He restored his kingdom. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:16:00} He adds that a man contested the Negus's throne, and the Muslims feared a ruler who would not know their due, but the Negus prevailed. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:17:30}

Al-Heweny names the Negus Ashama, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:43:00} and takes the hadith of [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]]'s conversion at his hand, which is in the Musnad of Ahmad with a chain he calls good, as evidence of the Negus's Islam; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:44:30} he says Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri was the Prophet's envoy to him and was also charged with having him marry the Prophet to Umm Habiba. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:52:00} He returns to the Negus's stand in another lesson, saying he would not hand them over without hearing them, and compares this with how states today hand over wanted men. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:28:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:31:00}
