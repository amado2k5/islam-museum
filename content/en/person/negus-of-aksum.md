## Brief
The Negus — the Arabic sources call him Ashama ibn Abjar — was the Christian king of Aksum who took in the first Muslim refugees and refused to give them up. He is the tradition's model of a just foreign ruler.

## Life
When persecution in Mecca grew unbearable, the Prophet sent a band of Muslims across the Red Sea to Abyssinia, telling them its king "wronged no one at his side." The Meccans sent envoys to demand the refugees back {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

Before the Negus's throne, [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far]] described what his people had been and what the Prophet taught, then recited the Qur'an's telling of Mary and Jesus. The sources say the king wept until his beard was wet, and declared the difference between their faiths no more than a line in the sand {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. He sheltered the first group in the best of neighbourliness, refused to surrender the migrants to the two Qurayshi envoys, and returned the gifts of [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-rabia|Abdullah ibn Abi Rabia]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

The exiles stayed under his protection for years. Later tradition adds that the Negus himself embraced Islam, and that the Prophet prayed a funeral prayer for him in absentia — which historians treat as tradition rather than documented fact {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
In Islamic memory, Abyssinia is the first foreign land that sheltered Muslims — and the Negus stands at the head of the museum's gallery of protectors who were not themselves of the community.

## For young visitors
A Christian king was asked to hand back a group of refugees. He listened to their story, refused, and let them stay — and Muslims have honored him for it ever since.
