## Brief
Mut'im ibn Adi was a chief of Banu Nawfal who never became a Muslim — and yet protected the Prophet at his most exposed, giving him safe-conduct back into Mecca when no Muslim dared show himself there.

## Life
When the Prophet returned from [[event/journey-to-taif|Ta'if]] — rejected, stoned, without a tribe to shelter him — he sent asking for Mut'im's protection. Mut'im met him armed, with his sons, and declared the *jiwar* from the Kaaba: Muhammad was under his safeguard, and Mecca had to accept it {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

Earlier that same year he had been one of the Qurashi notables who moved to tear up the boycott document against Banu Hashim {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. He died around 619 CE, before the Hijra, still on the religion of his people {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
The tradition remembers him without trying to claim him: the Prophet is reported to have said that had Mut'im lived and asked him for the prisoners of Badr, he would have given them. A protector honored as a protector — no more, and no less.

## For young visitors
When the Prophet came back to Mecca with nowhere safe to stand, a man who didn't believe in his message stood up armed and said "he is under my protection." And Mecca let him in.
