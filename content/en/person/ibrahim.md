## Summary
Ibrahim, the great forefather of the Arabised Arabs in the account of al-Raheeq al-Makhtum. His origin is the town of [[place/ur|Ur]] in Iraq. He migrated to Harran and then to [[place/palestine|Palestine]], and he settled [[person/hajar|Hagar]] and [[person/ismail|Isma'il]] in Mecca. With his son he raised the foundations of the [[building/kaaba|House]], and he proclaimed the pilgrimage among the people {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20}.

## From Ur to Palestine
Ibrahim's origin is the land of [[place/iraq|Iraq]], from a town called Ur on the western bank of the Euphrates near Kufa. Excavations have brought wide details about this town, about Ibrahim's family, and about the religious and social conditions there {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 1/553-556}. He migrated from there to Haran, and then to Palestine, which he took as the base of his call. He made tours across these lands and others {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 1/108}.

## In Egypt
He once came to [[place/misr|Egypt]], where the Pharaoh plotted against his wife [[person/sara|Sara]], but God foiled the plot. The Pharaoh recognised the strong bond Sara had with God, and so he gave Hagar to her in service as a mark of respect for her. Sara then gave her in marriage to Ibrahim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/474} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/34}.

## Settling Hagar and Isma'il
Ibrahim returned to Palestine, and God granted him Isma'il by Hagar. Sara grew jealous until she forced him to take Hagar and her small son away. He brought them to the Hijaz and settled them in a barren valley beside the Sacred House, which at that time was only a raised mound of earth, like a hillock {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/474-475}. He placed them beneath a tree above Zamzam, when no one was in Mecca and there was no water. He left with them a bag of dates and a waterskin, and returned to Palestine {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18}. See [[event/settlement-of-hajar-and-ismail|The settlement of Hagar and Isma'il]].

## His journeys to Mecca
Ibrahim used to travel to Mecca from time to time to look in on what he had left there. The sources record four of his journeys: the journey of the vision and [[event/sacrifice-of-ismail|the ransom of Isma'il]]; two journeys in which he met Isma'il's wives; and a third journey in which he met Isma'il. On that journey they built the Ka'ba and raised its foundations, and Ibrahim proclaimed the pilgrimage to the people as God had commanded him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/475-476}. Details are in [[event/ibrahim-visits-ismail-in-mecca|Ibrahim's visits to Mecca]] and [[event/building-of-the-kaaba|The raising of the foundations of the Ka'ba]].

## On lineage
The lineage of the [[group/adnan|Adnanite Arabs]] ends with him through Isma'il, [[person/qaydar|Qaydar]] and [[person/adnan|Adnan]]. Some scholars held that there were forty fathers between Adnan and Ibrahim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/7-17}. The author quotes Muslim's hadith that God chose Isma'il from among the offspring of Ibrahim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} {cite:sahih-muslim 2/245}.

## What is not mentioned here
Ibrahim's call to his people, the story of the fire, and his accounts in the Qur'an are presented in their own entries. This entry presents what the introduction of al-Raheeq al-Makhtum mentions. On religion, the author notes that most of the Arabs were on the religion of Ibrahim until time lengthened over them (see [[concept/diyanat-al-jahiliyya|The religions of the Arabs]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35}.

## For young visitors
Ibrahim is a prophet who travelled from Iraq to Palestine and placed his wife and son in Mecca. Then he built God's House with his son and called people to visit it.

## Above Ibrahim in al-Raheeq al-Makhtum
In the third part of the Prophet's lineage, the author mentions Ibrahim son of Tarih, whose name was Azar, son of Nahur, son of Saruj or Sarugh, son of Ra'u, son of Falakh, son of Eber, son of Shalakh, son of Arpakhshad, son of Sam, son of Nuh, son of Lamak, son of Mutawshalakh, son of Akhnukh, said to be Idris, son of Yard, son of Mahla'il, son of Qaynan, son of Anush, son of Shayth, son of Adam. The author considers that this part certainly contains inaccurate material. He notes that the sources differ in how some names are pronounced, and that some names are missing {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.48} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.49} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/2-4} {cite:khulasat-al-siyar p.6}. See [[concept/nasab-al-nabi|The Prophet's lineage]].

## Sha'rawi on Abraham's trial and the imamate
Sha'rawi holds that God tested Abraham with commands, that a trial is an examination which, if passed, is a good for the one tested, and that Abraham carried out his duties with love and more than was asked. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.569}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.572} When Abraham asked that the imamate pass to his descendants, God replied that His covenant does not reach the wrongdoers. Sha'rawi reads that prophethood is not an inheritance and is not gained by lineage or blood, but passes on by values, as in the story of Noah's son. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.573}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.574}

## For Sprouts
Ibrahim was a prophet of God. He was born in Iraq, then travelled to Palestine. He brought his wife Hajar and his little son Ismail to Mecca. Mecca was a valley with no plants. When Ismail grew up, he built the Kaaba with his father, stone upon stone. Then Ibrahim called people to come on the Hajj.

## For Explorers
Ibrahim was a prophet who lived long ago. He came from a town called Ur in Iraq, and he moved from there to Harran and then to Palestine, which became the centre of his call. God gave him a son named Ismail by his wife Hajar. Ibrahim brought Hajar and the little Ismail to the Hijaz and settled them in a valley with no plants and no water, at the place of the Kaaba. He left them dates and water and went back to Palestine. From time to time he travelled to Mecca to visit them, and the books record four of his journeys. On one of them he met Ismail, now a grown man, and together they built the Kaaba and raised its foundations stone upon stone. Then Ibrahim called out to people to come on the Hajj to God's House, as God had commanded him. Many Arab tribes trace their descent from Ibrahim through Ismail.

## For Scholars
Ibrahim is a prophet whom the Qur'an describes as a hanif, one who submitted to God, and whom the sira books count as the forefather of the Adnani Arabs. The Sealed Nectar tells his story in its introduction: he came from the town of Ur on the western bank of the Euphrates in Iraq, a town whose life excavations have described in detail. He migrated from there to Harran and then to Palestine, which he made the base of his call, and he travelled through the country. In Egypt the Pharaoh plotted against his wife Sara and God turned the plot back; the Pharaoh gave Hajar to serve her, and Sara gave Hajar to Ibrahim in marriage. God gave him Ismail by Hajar, and he then took the two to the Hijaz and settled them in a valley without crops at the site of the House, which at that time was no more than a rise in the ground; he left them dates and water and went back. He travelled to Mecca from time to time, and the sources preserve four of his journeys: the journey of the vision and the ransom of Ismail, two journeys on which he met Ismail's wives, and the journey on which he built the Kaaba with Ismail and raised its foundations, after which he called people to the Hajj as God had commanded him. The Adnani Arabs trace their line to Ibrahim through Ismail, Qaydar and Adnan; many scholars held that forty generations lie between Adnan and Ibrahim, and the author notes that the sources differ on the line above Adnan. Ibrahim's call to his people, the story of the fire and his accounts in the Qur'an are told in their own records in the museum.

## Abraham in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episodes 4 and 5)
The Sheikh reflects on Abraham's return from Mecca and his settling some of his offspring in a valley without crops, and asks listeners to imagine a father's feelings (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:27:00} He holds that had Abraham's feelings prevailed, God's command would not have been carried out, and that the world follows people's moods (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:27:30}
He says that Noah's son was not of his family, and that worldly affections change in Paradise (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:10:30} He mentions Abraham's prayer for his father, who died in disbelief, and that the father remains outside Paradise, and gives the example of the Prophet and his uncle (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:11:30}
