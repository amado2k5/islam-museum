## Overview
Al-Walid, known as Abu Rakwa — an Umayyad of the line of Hisham b. 'Abd al-Malik, nicknamed for the rakwa water-skin he carried on his travels in the Sufi manner; he met the shaykhs, wrote hadith in Egypt, moved to Mecca then Yemen, then returned to Syria. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.234-235}

## His call and his end
He preached for the Qa'im of the line of Hisham b. 'Abd al-Malik and took the pledge from any who yielded; settling at al-Hilla as a schoolmaster, Arab boys gathered to him and he told them he was the Imam he preached for, styling himself "the Rebel by God's Command, Victorious over God's enemies." He entered Barqa, struck the coinage in his name, preached and cursed al-Hakim; al-Hakim sent al-Fadl b. 'Abdallah, who defeated him, and the land of Nubia surrendered him after delivering him there on a pretended covenant — he was carried to Egypt, where al-Hakim mounted him on a camel in public disgrace, then killed him. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.234-235}
