Abdalhaqq Bewley is a British translator and writer who has spent more than half a century rendering classical Islamic texts into English and teaching Islam in Muslim communities around the world. The year of his birth is not attested in the sources his record rests on, and it is left unrecorded rather than guessed. He became a Muslim during a visit to Morocco in 1968 and spent the following three years studying there; returning to England he met and married the translator Aisha Abdarrahman, and together they settled into a life of translation and community work{cite:botswana-zakah-talks-intro-2009}{cite:dar-al-taqwa-islamic-will-bios-1995}.

Bewley worked with Shaykh Abdalqadir as-Sufi establishing Muslim communities — teaching in the south-east of Nigeria, helping to found the community of Spanish Muslims at Córdoba that later built the mosque opposite the Alhambra at Granada, and spending time in Bermuda and the United States{cite:botswana-zakah-talks-intro-2009}. Through most of the 1980s he was imam of the mosque at Norwich and ran a Muslim school there; in the early 1990s he lived in Germany, and in Cape Town he taught Islamic studies at the newly opened Dallas College and served as imam-khatib of the Jumu'a Mosque in its first year{cite:botswana-zakah-talks-intro-2009}. His published translations with Aisha Bewley include The Noble Qur'an: A New Rendering of Its Meaning in English (1999), Al-Muwatta of Imam Malik and Muhammad: The Messenger of Allah, the English rendering of Qadi Iyad's ash-Shifa{cite:dar-al-taqwa-islamic-will-bios-1995}; his own books include Islam: Its Basic Practices and Beliefs, Zakat: Raising a Fallen Pillar, The Four Madhhabs of Islam and The Natural Form of Man{cite:botswana-zakah-talks-intro-2009}.
