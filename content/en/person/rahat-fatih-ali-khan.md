# Rahat Fateh Ali Khan

## Summary

Rahat Fateh Ali Khan (born 9 December 1974) is a Pakistani Punjabi singer, primarily of qawwali, and one of Pakistan's most popular and highest-paid singers; he also sings ghazals and light music and is well known as a playback singer in Hindi and Pakistani cinema. {cite:wikipedia-rahat-fatih-ali-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-rahat-fatih-ali-khan}

## Early life

He was born in Faisalabad into a Punjabi family of qawwals and classical singers: the son of Farrukh Fateh Ali Khan, grandson of Fateh Ali Khan and nephew of [[person/nusrat-fatih-ali-khan|Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan]]. He sang with his uncle and father at three, was trained in qawwali by his uncle from seven, accompanied him on tour from five, and was chosen by him to lead the party after his death. {cite:wikipedia-rahat-fatih-ali-khan-en}

## Career

He first sang in public in 1979, aged nine, on the anniversary of his grandfather's death; from fifteen he was a core member of his uncle's party, touring Britain with him in 1985. With his uncle and Eddie Vedder he contributed to 'Dead Man Walking' (1995), and he sang on the scores of 'The Four Feathers' (2002) with James Horner and 'Apocalypto' (2006). His Hindi playback debut was 'Mann Ki Lagan' in 'Paap' (2003), a composition of his uncle's. {cite:wikipedia-rahat-fatih-ali-khan-en}

In 2014 he became the first Pakistani to sing at a Nobel Peace Prize concert, performing 'Tumhe Dillagi', 'Mast Qalandar' and 'Aao Parhao'. He has appeared in five Coke Studio editions — 'Chhaap Tilak' with [[person/abida-parvin|Abida Parveen]], 'Afreen Afreen' with Momina Mustehsan, and 'Aaj Rang Hai' with Amjad Sabri, the latter's last performance before his assassination on 22 June 2016. His 'Zaroori Tha' (2014) passed a billion YouTube views, as The Times of India reported on 5 January 2021. {cite:wikipedia-rahat-fatih-ali-khan-en}

## Recorded episodes

The article reports that Indian Customs held him and his entourage in 2011 for undeclared goods; that in 2018 he was at the centre of a rift in the Fateh Ali Khan family over song rights, saying he was Nusrat's adopted successor and needed no one's permission to sing his songs; and that in 2024 he apologised publicly after a video appeared to show him assaulting a member of his household staff. The museum reports what the sources state, without judgement. {cite:wikipedia-rahat-fatih-ali-khan-en}
