## Overview
Abd Allah ibn Umar ibn Muhammad ibn Ali, Abu al-Khayr al-Baydawi, a jurist, Qur'an commentator, theologian and expert in the principles of jurisprudence. His birth date is unknown; he died in 685 AH (1286 CE). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

He is the author of the commentary "Anwar al-Tanzil", which is [[work/anwar-al-tanzil|Anwar al-Tanzil]] in the museum's records. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan}

The dates come from one source and need a second.

## On the Halabi title page and in his preface
The cover of part 4 of the Dar al-Kutub al-Arabiyya al-Kubra printing (Cairo, 1330 AH) gives his name as al-Qadi Nasir al-Din Abu Sa'id Abd Allah ibn Umar ibn Muhammad al-Shirazi al-Baydawi, after al-Bayda', a village in the district of Shiraz; it says he died in 791 AH, a date that differs from the biographical works (685 or 691 AH). {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-halabi-1330-v4 p.1} In the preface to [[work/anwar-al-tanzil|Anwar al-Tanzil]] he calls tafsir the chief of the religious sciences and says he meant to gather in it the best of what reached him from the Companions, the Successors and the early generations, with later scholars' fine points and the well-known readings. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.5}

## In Tafsir al-Manar
[[person/rashid-rida|Muhammad Rashid Rida]] quotes al-Baydawi at the opening of the qibla passage of al-Baqara ([[passage/quran-2-142|al-Baqara 142]]): the "foolish" are those whose judgement is light, who make light of imitation and of turning away from reflection. {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.3}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes al-Baydawi as a Shafii jurist and Ashari theologian, trained in Baghdad and living in Shiraz, and says he died probably in 1286, possibly as late as 1316. It is best known, it says, for a compact Qur'an commentary with particular attention to grammar and the variant readings, titled Anwar al-Tanzil wa-Asrar al-Ta'wil, which builds on the Kashshaf of [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] (died 1144). {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e319}

The Dictionary says the work became standard across the Muslim world, drew many supercommentaries, is studied in madrasa courses on interpretation, and was among the first Qur'an commentaries printed in Europe, in 1846-48. It also credits him with writing in history, law, grammar and theology. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e319} The year 1286 agrees with the death date in the record; 1316 is a later alternative the record does not carry.
