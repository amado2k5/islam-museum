## Overview
Ali Dawah is a British Muslim YouTuber and street preacher, known for his debates and conversations at Speakers' Corner in London's Hyde Park, the traditional open-air venue for public debate. {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah} {cite:youtube-ali-dawah}

Published biographies give his birth name as Erdi Kılıç and record that he grew up in London in a Kurdish family with roots in Kayseri, Turkey; by his own account his upbringing was largely non-religious and his teenage years involved trouble at school and involvement with local gangs. {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah}

By his own account he embraced Islam around 2012, describing it as the turning point at which he gave up his previous lifestyle; in 2013 he stepped onto the streets with a camera to share what he had learned. {cite:islamicfinder-ali-dawah} {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah}

The published biographies conflict on his birth date and birthplace, so the museum records no birth date for him.

## Career and work
He launched his YouTube channel in September 2013 with videos recorded at Speakers' Corner, building his content around street interviews, religious debates, and answers to common questions and misconceptions about Islam, in a style mixing humour with serious discussion and aimed particularly at younger viewers. {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah}

His channel had about 1.36 million subscribers as of October 2026, a platform figure that changes constantly. {cite:youtube-ali-dawah}

He co-founded the Salam Initiative, a project whose goal is teaching people across the globe how to perform Salah, Wudu, and read the Quran; the profile publishing this records that the project has taught tens of thousands of people how to pray. {cite:islamicfinder-ali-dawah}

He maintains that he has never taken a payment for his dawah work and does it completely for free, according to the profile. {cite:islamicfinder-ali-dawah}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of different positions reported by the sources, not as a verdict of the museum on them.

One profile describes Ali Dawah as a polarising figure who is frequently discussed and disputed, inside and outside Muslim communities, and reports that a widely shared, heated exchange with the activist Tommy Robinson drew split reactions: some viewers praised him for directly challenging Robinson, while others felt the confrontation deepened tensions rather than encouraging dialogue. {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah}

His statements on subjects including LGBTQ+ rights, feminism and polygamy have drawn significant backlash, including from within Muslim communities, with critics arguing that some of his positions are needlessly divisive, while supporters counter that he is articulating traditional Islamic positions without apology, according to the same source. {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah}

A community wiki for YouTubers (Wikitubia) records controversy over answers he gave at Speakers' Corner in 2018 and 2020 when asked about marriage for a girl who has reached puberty, answers that drew wide criticism; they are documented here as that source reports them. {cite:wikitubia-ali-dawah}

Critics have also accused him of selectively editing conversations and mischaracterising people he has debated; the profile describes these as contested claims rather than settled facts, and reports that he has defended his record publicly. {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah}

## Personal life
Biographies record that he married in 2017 and has children, without consistently confirmed family details, and the museum publishes nothing further. Ali Dawah has spoken publicly about living with retinitis pigmentosa, a condition that is gradually taking his eyesight, saying that he is adapting to the sight he still has and remains determined to continue his work, according to a profile reporting his podcast remarks. {cite:islamicinfocenter-ali-dawah} {cite:islamicfinder-ali-dawah}
