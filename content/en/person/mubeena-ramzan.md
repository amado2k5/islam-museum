## Summary
Dr Mubeena Ramzan is a Kashmiri educator and social worker from Sopore in the Baramulla district of Jammu and Kashmir. She founded and runs Jamia Islamia Mahdul Muslimat, a women's religious seminary educating girls in Sopore and Srinagar, and heads Ansar un Nisa, a socio-religious organisation for women. The Muslim 500 lists her under Social Issues for her work educating and empowering Kashmiri women. {cite:themuslim500-mubeena-ramzan}

## Education and turning to religious teaching
Ramzan earned a doctorate in Islamic studies, and according to press coverage she turned to founding a girls' seminary after personal experience of the conflict years in Kashmir — a decision she has described as answering both the educational deprivation of girls in the valley and the need she perceived for religious instruction in a setting women could attend. Sopore, the northern town where she established her institution, was one of the places most marked by the militancy-era violence of the 1990s and the counter-insurgency that accompanied it. {cite:themuslim500-mubeena-ramzan}

## Jamia Islamia Mahdul Muslimat
Her seminary, Jamia Islamia Mahdul Muslimat, operates branches in Sopore and Srinagar and takes in girls — including, by reporting, orphans and the daughters of families bereaved by the conflict — for a curriculum combining Islamic studies with general schooling. The institution reflects a wider phenomenon in the Kashmir Valley since the 1990s: the growth of women's madrasas answering demand for female religious education in a socially conservative environment where mainstream schooling was repeatedly disrupted by strikes, curfews and unrest. {cite:themuslim500-mubeena-ramzan}

## Ansar un Nisa
Through Ansar un Nisa, the women's socio-religious organisation she heads, Ramzan has organised welfare work, preaching directed at women, and community programmes in north Kashmir. Reporting on her work presents her as part of a small but visible cohort of Kashmiri women religious entrepreneurs — founders who built institutions in a field of public religious leadership otherwise dominated by men. {cite:themuslim500-mubeena-ramzan}

## Standing and notes on this record
The Muslim 500's profile presents her as a woman educating and empowering women in a conflict-marked region; coverage of her has at times been polemical — her seminary work has been both praised as female empowerment and viewed with suspicion in polarised reporting about religious schooling in Kashmir — and the museum reports these characterisations attributed, without verdict. This record's sourcing is thin: it rests on the Muslim 500 profile and press coverage rather than on a full independent biography, and that thinness is stated on the record itself. No source read states her birth year or names a school for her personally, and this record records neither. {cite:themuslim500-mubeena-ramzan}

## Context
Women's religious education in Kashmir sits at the meeting of two strong currents of the valley's modern history: a long tradition of home-centred female piety and Quran instruction, and the post-1990s institutional growth of Islamic schooling as public education struggled under prolonged unrest. Into that space stepped a handful of women founders, of whom Ramzan is among the most cited; their institutions gave girls a recognised route into religious learning and, often, into social-organisational roles that had previously been closed to them. Supporters read her school as an answer to both educational disruption and female exclusion; critics of madrasa expansion in Kashmir raise questions about curricula and oversight — a debate the museum reports attributed, without verdict. {cite:themuslim500-mubeena-ramzan}
