## Overview
Al-Walid ibn Zidan ibn Ahmad al-Sa'di was one of the kings of the Sa'di Sharifian state at Marrakesh {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 117}. With his brother Ahmad he rose against their third brother Abd al-Malik, when the latter was pledged allegiance at Marrakesh after their father's death in 1037 AH, and after wars the two were defeated {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 117}. Al-Walid went on moving from land to land until Abd al-Malik pardoned him; he returned to Marrakesh and won over the chiefs of the state, who killed Abd al-Malik and pledged allegiance to him in 1040 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 117}.

## His rule
He confined himself to Marrakesh and its districts while strife raged at Fez and the emirates of the Maghrib were divided among the sons of Zidan as rival factions {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 117}. Al-Zirikli describes him as outwardly religious, gentle, a lover of scholars, some of whom wrote books in his name; the people were content with him, and he was fond of al-sama', listening, by night and by day. He adds that he killed many of the Sharifs, his cousins {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 117}.

## Death
Some Turks of his soldiery killed him by treachery in his palace at Marrakesh; al-A'lam gives the year of his death as 1045 AH (1636 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 117}.
