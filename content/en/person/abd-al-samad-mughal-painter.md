## Overview
Abd al-Samad was a painter trained at the court of the Safavid Shah Tahmasp; with Mir Sayyid Ali he was one of the two painters [[person/humayun-mughal|Humayun]] brought from the Iranian court when he regained his dominions in 1554, introducing to India the latest developments of the Iranian book. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 120}

## Career
After Humayun met the shah's painters in exile he sent for them in 1546; they left Tabriz in 1548, waited at Kandahar until calm returned to Kabul, joined him there in 1549 and accompanied him back to India in 1554. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 144} Welch attributes to him [[object/princes-of-the-house-of-timur|Princes of the House of Timur]], the earliest known work by these artists, in which he worked under the influence of his colleague Mir Sayyid Ali; typical of him are bold arm gestures, flights of birds, the treatment of trees and vegetation and a convincing arrangement of forms in space. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 120} {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 144}
