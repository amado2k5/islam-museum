## Summary
[[group/hawazin|Hawazin]]'s knight and poet and chief of [[group/banu-jusham|Banu Jusham]]; a very old, blind man carried in his howdah on the day of Hunayn. He denounced [[person/malik-ibn-awf|Malik ibn Awf]]'s plan of driving out the dependants and the wealth, and was killed at [[place/awtas|Awtas]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa pp. 80-82, 93-94}.

## His stand at Hunayn
When the people alighted at Awtas, Durayd heard the children crying and the sheep bleating; he summoned Malik ibn Awf and said: "Malik, you have become your people's chief, and this is a day that will have days after it — why do I hear the camels' moaning, the donkeys' braying and the infants' crying?" Malik answered: "I drove the people's wealth and women with them so every man fights for his family and his property." Durayd rebuked him: "a sheep-herder, by God! Does anything turn back the routed? If the day is yours nothing but a man with his sword will help you; and if it is against you, you are disgraced in your family and your wealth" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/81}.

## His death
After Hunayn's rout [[person/rabia-ibn-rufy|Rabi'a ibn Rufay' al-Sulami]] caught up with him at Awtas in his litter and struck him with his sword — to no effect. Durayd told him: "how ill your mother armed you! Take this sword of mine, strike with it and raise it off the bones" — and he killed him with his own sword {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/93-94}.

## For young visitors
An aged, wise knight-poet of Hawazin who warned his leader against risking the women and wealth in battle; the leader did not hear his words, and the defeat came.
