## Summary

Timur Shah Durrani (December 1746 – 18 May 1793) was the second ruler of the Durrani Empire, reigning from November 1772 until his death, and the second son of the empire's founder, Ahmad Shah Durrani {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ur}. Where his father's reign was one of conquest, Timur Shah's was one of consolidation: he moved the capital from Kandahar to Kabul, kept the Punjab frontier against the Sikh misls, and put down the revolts that his centralising rule provoked {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}.

## Early life

Timur Shah was born at Mashhad {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ar}. The English article gives December 1746, citing Drahm and Hari Ram Gupta, while the Arabic article gives 1748, and the record preserves both {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ar}. He rose quickly through marriage to a daughter of the Mughal emperor Alamgir II, receiving the city of Sirhind as a wedding gift, and in 1757, aged about eleven, his father made him viceroy of the Punjab, Kashmir and the Sirhind district for a year, with Toryal Khan Afridi set over his training in horsemanship and swordsmanship and over his safety {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ar}.

## Accession and the move to Kabul

Timur Shah ascended the throne in November 1772 on his father's death {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}. At Peshawar the zamindar Fayz Allah Khan Khalil, exploiting alarm at Sikh pressure on the Punjab's Muslims, won leave to raise tribal levies and used them in a plot to seize the Bala Hissar and kill the shah; the rising was crushed in heavy fighting, and Fayz Allah, lured to Kabul by a letter of pardon written and sealed on a leaf of the Quran, was arrested and beheaded {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}. After the revolt Timur Shah returned to Kabul and formally established it as the imperial capital in place of Kandahar, favouring its central position and its Qizilbash administrators, with Peshawar kept as the winter capital {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ar}.

## The Punjab, Kashmir and Bukhara

The Sikhs had taken Multan in 1772, and when Timur Shah's envoy Haji Ali Khan was killed by the Bhangi chiefs his army fell on the Sikh camps near Rohtas in 1779 and besieged and recovered Multan in January 1780 {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}. In Kashmir the governor Azad Khan stopped remitting revenue and defeated two imperial expeditions before Timur Shah sent a larger army under Madad Khan and Payandah Khan; abandoned by his own commanders and betrayed by the raja who sheltered him, Azad Khan shot himself in December 1786, and Timur Shah, admiring his courage, ordered his burial with respect and provided for his mother {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}. In 1788 he prepared to march into India to rescue his brother-in-law, the blinded Mughal emperor Shah Alam II, and stood down only on learning the Marathas had already restored him; the same years brought a march north to the Amu Darya against Shah Murad of Bukhara, who had demanded the recall of the governors of Aqcha and Balkh {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}.

## Death and legacy

Late in 1792, setting out on another Indian campaign, Timur Shah settled the empire's government — Herat to Prince Mahmud, Kandahar to Prince Humayun, Kabul to Prince Zaman — and at Peshawar fell ill with severe intestinal inflammation {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}. He insisted on returning to Kabul, the capital he preferred, reached it in early May 1793 and died a few days later, on 18 May 1793 {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikidata-timur-shah-durrani}. His mausoleum at Kabul became one of the city's notable landmarks, and five of his sons in time ruled or contended for the throne, Zaman Shah succeeding him {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}.

## Faith

No source read names a legal school for the man himself. The English article's infobox files his religion as Sunni Islam and the Arabic article's infobox files Islam generically — infobox filings, which name no school in any text — and Wikidata carries no religion value for him {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ar} {cite:wikidata-timur-shah-durrani}. What the sources describe is the Sunni Afghan state he inherited and ruled, his campaigns in defence of the Punjab's Muslim communities, and the pardon sealed on a Quran leaf with which he drew Fayz Allah Khan to Kabul {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en}. Dynastic tradition and documented practice name no school for the man, so his faith is recorded as unknown.

## Notes for researchers

The sources disagree on his birth year — December 1746 in the English article and 1746 at year precision in Wikidata, against 1748 in the Arabic article and the harvest queue — and the English article is inconsistent on his death, its lead giving 20 May 1793 against 18 May in its body, its succession box, the Urdu article and Wikidata; both rival readings are preserved in the record {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ur} {cite:wikidata-timur-shah-durrani}. His accession is likewise dated November 1772 in the English lead, 4 June 1772 in its succession box and 16 October 1772 in the Urdu article {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-en} {cite:wikipedia-timur-shah-durrani-ur}.
