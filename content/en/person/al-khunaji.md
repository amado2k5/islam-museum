## Overview
Al-Khunaji, the Shafi'i chief qadi; born Jumada I 590, he studied in the non-Arab lands, then came to Egypt and held its judgeship — seeking and attaining — and plunged into the sciences of the ancients until he alone held their headship in his time, giving fatwa and debating. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 244-245}

## His absentmindedness
He composed al-Mujiz, al-Jumal, Kashf al-asrar and Kitab adwar al-hayat; his blind pupil al-'Izz al-Irbili mourned him in verse. Absentmindedness would overtake him in the rational problems he pondered: once at the sultan's he put his hand into the spout-ring of a ewer and forgot himself in thought — the company rose while he remained seated, his finger holding him down, until a blacksmith was brought to free it. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 244-245}
