Mustapha Cherif (born 1950) is an Algerian philosopher and writer, born in Miliana.{cite:wikipedia-mustapha-cherif-fr}{cite:wikidata-mustapha-cherif} He took doctorates in philosophy and in sociology at the École des hautes études en sciences sociales in Paris (1978), founded and presided over Algeria's University of Continuing Education, served as minister of universities in 1990–1991 and as Algeria's ambassador to Egypt from 1994 to 2001, and has lectured at Yale, Cambridge and the Collège de France.{cite:wikipedia-mustapha-cherif-fr}{cite:wikipedia-mustapha-cherif-ar}

He is a leading figure of interreligious dialogue: he took part in the first international colloquium on Islam and Christianity at Córdoba in 1975, co-founded the Groupe d'amitié islamo-chrétienne in 1993 alongside Father Michel Lelong, and was received by Popes John Paul II, Benedict XVI and Francis; in 2012 he received the UNESCO-Sharjah Prize for Arab Culture.{cite:wikipedia-mustapha-cherif-fr} His twenty books, translated into twelve languages, treat Islam, modernity and the dialogue of civilisations; no source attests a school of faith for him personally, and his record leaves it unknown.{cite:wikidata-mustapha-cherif}
