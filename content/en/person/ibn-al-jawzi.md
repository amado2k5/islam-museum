## Overview
Abd al-Rahman ibn Ali ibn al-Jawzi, a historian and preacher, author of a history of the Abbasid caliphs that runs down to the days of al-Nasir. *Shadharat al-Dhahab* says that he was born in 510 AH or earlier, and that he was attributed to a quarter of Basra called the quarter of al-Jawz. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 809-810}

His aunt brought him to the mosque of Abu al-Fadl ibn Nasir, who was his maternal uncle. The uncle cared for him, had him hear hadith, and he memorised the Qur'an and heard from about eighty-seven people. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 809-810}

He preached from a young age. He related more than once that the size of his gathering was estimated at a hundred thousand, and the caliph al-Mustadi' attended his sessions several times from behind a curtain. He wrote on exegesis, hadith, jurisprudence, asceticism, preaching, reports, history and medicine. He died in 597 AH. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 809-810}

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Ibn al-Jawzi — distinguished from Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya — as the model preacher-creator: orphaned of his father young, raised by his mother, and taken by his aunt who noticed his talents to the mosque of Abu al-Fadl ibn Nasir, who undertook his care {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:03:30}.
Of his early devotion: he memorised the Qur'an young under the masters of the readings, left home only for Friday prayer, never played with the boys, and in Sayd al-Khatir describes studying by the river while the boys went down to the Tigris {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:05:00}.
He quotes Sayd al-Khatir on himself: 'I am a man to whom learning was made lovable since childhood, and not one art but all its arts; nor does my ambition in an art stop at part of it — I seek its utmost' — with his complaint that life is too narrow for his longing {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:07:30}.
He studied under eighty-six shaykhs of whom he says 'I took each one's knowledge entire'; began writing hadith at eleven; and began preaching and teaching at Baghdad's great mosque before twenty {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:10:00}.
He offers a critical reading (the speaker's view): Ibn al-Jawzi's great distinction was preaching, not precision in scholarship — one who takes every art masters none — so he finds in him errors in creed, hadith and fiqh, while no preacher of his power has passed over the ummah since the Companions {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:17:30}.
Of his fame: he relays an estimate of a hundred thousand at his sitting (allowing exaggeration, he says); the caliph himself attended; Ibn Jubayr describes his session in 580 AH — 'they fell upon him like moths upon a lamp' — and Ibn Rajab 'when he preached, hearts were seized and souls, not pockets, were rent' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:18:30}.
His address to the caliph: 'O commander of the faithful, if I speak to you I fear you, and if I keep silent I fear for you — and I put my fear for you before my fear of you'; and the slander of enviers that he stirred people against the caliph, for which he was confined in a house at Wasit five years until his son Yusuf obtained intercession through the caliph's mother {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:13:00}.
Of his works: Zad al-Masir fi Ilm al-Tafsir, twenty-seven works on the Qur'an and its sciences, Talbis Iblis — setting out how Satan enters the human soul and beguiles it even through learning — and biographies of the Companions and jurists; Ibn Taymiyya, he says, saw over nine hundred of his works while some historians put them near a thousand {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:20:00}.
A Hanbali yet not a partisan, he departed from the school on some questions and some of Ahmad's followers resented him for it; and he built five madrasas in Baghdad, the first in Darb Dinar, teaching fourteen lessons there on opening day {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:21:30}.
His second critique: on examination he found in him a degree of self-admiration, for he speaks often of himself — distinguishing this from arrogance, which despises others (the speaker's view) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:16:00}.
He died on Friday night, the twelfth of Ramadan 597 AH, after five days' illness; the markets closed for his funeral and many never reached his grave for the crowd; he was buried near Imam Ahmad's grave {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:23:00}.
And he reckons that dividing his writings by his days gives him about forty pages composed every day of his life {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep12 00:24:00}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] introduces Ibn al-Jawzi at the opening of his commentary on [[work/sayd-al-khatir|Sayd al-Khatir]] as a preacher to the common people who explained things to them with affection because he mixed with them. He says that more than a hundred thousand people repented at his hands and about twenty thousand embraced Islam, that the caliph used to attend his preaching sessions, and that he spoke extempore words that the most eloquent could not polish. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:03:30} These figures are the ones al-Heweny gives in his lesson. He says that Ibn al-Jawzi, the student of [[person/ibn-aqil|Ibn Aqil]], took his care for time from his teacher, and that he wrote in Sayd al-Khatir that he set aside for visitors' hours tasks that needed no thought, such as cutting paper and sharpening pens. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:47:00}

He mentions that Ibn al-Jawzi repeated his reply to Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami in his book on the difficulties of the two Sahihs, under the Musnad of Umar rather than of Ali, because the words in the story were addressed to Umar. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:23:30} For all his respect he criticises him in several places: he holds that Ibn al-Jawzi often relies in his preaching on weak, fabricated and false hadith; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:48:00} he judges false a report Ibn al-Jawzi cites in the story of A'isha's garment, because its chain contains Ishaq ibn Bishr, author of al-Mubtada', whom he calls a liar by agreement; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:40:00} and he holds that Ibn al-Jawzi made light of the deeds of the three men of the cave, so that he makes his explanation of their hadith a reply to him, asking his audience: if you were the fourth, which deed would you plead? {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:56:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:57:00}

## In the editor's introduction to Zad al-Masir
Abd al-Razzaq al-Mahdi gives his name as Jamal al-Din Abu al-Faraj Abd al-Rahman ibn Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ali al-Qurashi al-Taymi al-Bakri, the Hanbali preacher, tracing his descent to Abu Bakr al-Siddiq; he was known as Ibn al-Jawzi after the Jawz landing in Baghdad, or, it is said, after a walnut tree (jawza) in his ancestor's house at Wasit. {cite:zad-al-masir-dar-al-kitab-al-arabi-2001-v1 p.7} He says he was born in Baghdad in 508 AH, or 510, or 514; memorised the Qur'an and read it in the variant readings; and took up preaching, until kings and viziers attended his sessions, one of which was reckoned at a hundred thousand people. {cite:zad-al-masir-dar-al-kitab-al-arabi-2001-v1 p.7} He quotes al-Muwaffaq al-Maqdisi that he was the imam of his age in preaching and master of many disciplines, and [[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]] that a number of Hanbali shaykhs objected to his leaning to figurative reinterpretation in some of his words, that his statements on this are inconsistent, and that he revered [[person/ibn-aqil|Abu al-Wafa' ibn Aqil]] and followed most of what he said. {cite:zad-al-masir-dar-al-kitab-al-arabi-2001-v1 p.8} His works are said to have reached two hundred and fifty, among them in tafsir [[work/zad-al-masir|Zad al-Masir]], Nuzhat al-A'yun al-Nawazir and Umdat al-Rasikh on abrogation; in hadith al-Mawdu'at and al-Ilal al-Mutanahiya; and on creed Daf' Shubah al-Tashbih. He died on the eve of Friday, 12 Ramadan 597 AH, aged eighty-seven, and was buried at Bab Harb near Imam Ahmad. {cite:zad-al-masir-dar-al-kitab-al-arabi-2001-v1 p.8}

## In Talbis Iblis
On the title page of the second Muniriyya printing of his [[work/talbis-iblis|Talbis Iblis]] he is styled "the hadith master and imam Jamal al-Din Abu al-Faraj 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Jawzi al-Baghdadi", with his death dated 597 AH. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 5} In the book's preface he says he composed it to warn of the devil's trials and uncover what is hidden, and divided it into thirteen chapters that begin with holding to the Sunna and the community and end with long hope. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 8} {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 9} In it he mentions that he compiled a separate book on the reports about [[person/al-hallaj|al-Hallaj]]. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 176} In his closing note the corrector Muhammad Munir al-Dimashqi calls the author "the scholar of the horizons and preacher of Iraq". {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 407}
