Nawab Nauroz (Nowroz) Khan — Dada Nowroz, "grandfather Nowroz", to the Baloch — was the head of the Zarakzai (Zehri) tribe of Jhalawan, in the Kalat khanate, and the leader of the Baloch insurgency of 1958–60. His birth year is not securely known: the English article's lead gives "1868?", its text says only that he was born some time in the 1860s or 1870s, and Wikidata gives 1874, the year carried in his record. What is certain is that by 1958 he was an old man, the senior tribal figure of his country, at the head of a largely nomadic people in the mountains of Jhalawan. {cite:wikipedia-nowroz-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-nowroz-khan}

His rising grew out of the grievances left by Kalat's accession to Pakistan in 1948 and the dissolution of the Baloch states into the One Unit of West Pakistan in 1955. When the Pakistani government arrested the Khan of Kalat, Ahmad Yar Khan, in October 1958, Nowroz Khan took to the hills. His band fought several sharp skirmishes with forces led by Lieutenant-Colonel Tikka Khan. {cite:wikipedia-nowroz-khan-en}

The end of the rising is the story Balochistan remembers. Tikka Khan sent Nowroz a copy of the Quran, asking him to negotiate a truce for the sake of the Quran, and Nowroz agreed to surrender on 15 May 1959 in exchange for amnesty. When he came down from the hills he was arrested, with about 150 of his followers, among them his sons and nephews. On 15 July 1960 seven of the leaders were hanged in Hyderabad jail. Nowroz himself was spared execution on account of his age; he died in Kohlu jail in 1964. {cite:wikipedia-nowroz-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-nowroz-khan}
