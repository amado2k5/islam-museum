## Summary
Muhammad Al-Jasser is a Saudi economist who has served since 2021 as President and Chairman of the Islamic Development Bank Group, the largest multilateral development institution of the Muslim world, and was re-elected in 2026 for a second term. Earlier he led the Saudi Central Bank as governor and served as Minister of Economy and Planning, combining monetary policy, national planning and multilateral Islamic finance across a single career. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-jasser}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1955 in Buraidah in the Qassim region, and studied economics at San Diego State University in California, where he earned his bachelor's and master's degrees, before returning to Saudi Arabia to begin a career spanning four decades between the central banking apparatus and economic institutions. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-jasser}

## Career
He joined the Saudi Arabian Monetary Authority in 1981 and rose through its ranks until appointed governor in 2009, steering Saudi monetary policy in the post-financial-crisis years, then became Minister of Economy and Planning in 2011, a post he held until 2015. After a period in economic advisory work he was chosen President of the Islamic Development Bank Group, succeeding Bandar Hajjar in August 2021, and the Board of Governors re-elected him in 2026 for a second term. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-jasser} {cite:muslim500-muhammad-al-jasser}

## At the Islamic Development Bank
He leads a bank of fifty-seven member countries that finances development projects worth billions of dollars annually from Africa to Asia. In his public statements he has called for deeper economic integration among member states and for expanding Islamic finance tools toward sustainable development, food security and climate resilience. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-jasser}

## Recognition
He has sat on the boards of Arab and international economic and academic institutions and appears in rankings of influential figures in Arab and Islamic economics; his career is noted for the rare combination of central banking, national planning and multilateral development finance. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-jasser}

## Legacy
His trajectory reads as the path of Saudi technocratic economics: from the central bank to the planning ministry to the presidency of the leading Islamic development institution. His tenure at the bank is seen as continuing Saudi Arabia's central role in financing Islamic development, with a stated turn toward climate and food-security issues across the Global South. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-jasser}
