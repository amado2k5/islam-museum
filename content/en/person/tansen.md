## Summary
Tansen — born Ramtanu Gaud Panday, about 1493, and known by the honorific Mian Tansen — was a Hindustani classical musician, composer and vocalist, and the most celebrated musician of the Mughal emperor Akbar's court. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Akbar counted him among the Navaratnas, the nine jewels of the court, and gave him the title Mian, an honorific meaning a learned man. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} He is remembered for his Dhrupad compositions, for creating several new ragas, and as a founding figure of the Hindustani tradition whom many gharanas claim as their lineage founder. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

His biography is unusually hard to separate from legend: the English article warns that his birth year is unclear and that historical facts about him are difficult to extract from the extensive and contradictory legends that surround him. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} That uncertainty reaches his faith. He was born into a Hindu family and is claimed by Hindu and Sufi traditions alike, and whether he converted to Islam is disputed by the very sources that tell his life; this record preserves the dispute and settles none of it. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} {cite:wikipedia-tansen-ur}

## Early life and schooling
Tansen's date of birth is unclear: most accounts place it about 1493, or between 1493 and 1506, and Encyclopedia.com heads its entry with the years 1506–1589. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} {cite:encyclopedia-com-tansen} He was born into a Hindu Gaur Brahmin family in Gwalior, in modern Madhya Pradesh, and his childhood name was Ramtanu. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} His father Mukund Ram — also given as Mukund Gaud or Mukund Chand — was a wealthy poet and accomplished musician of Gwalior who for some time was a Hindu temple priest in Varanasi. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} The Urdu article likewise gives his birth name as Ramtanu Pandey and his birth at Gwalior, in the house of a temple priest. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-ur}

He showed musical talent at the age of six, and the ruler of Gwalior conferred on him the honorific title "Tansen". {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} He was discipled for a time to Swami Haridas, the legendary composer from Vrindavan and court musician of Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior, from whom he acquired his love of Dhrupad and of composing in the local language rather than Sanskrit. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Encyclopedia.com likewise says he was sent to Vrindavan, near Mathura, to study under Haridas. {cite:encyclopedia-com-tansen}

His schooling is the first place the traditions divide. The legendary accounts of his formation differ depending on whether the story has Hindu or Muslim origins: Hindu accounts centre on the bhakti saint Swami Haridas, while Muslim biographies name the Sufi mystic Muhammad Ghaus of Gwalior as the influence on him. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Hagiographies mention that Tansen met Muhammad Ghaus, and that the meeting brought a strong Sufi influence on him. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Bonnie Wade, the scholar of South Asian music cited in the article, judges that Tansen clearly connected with Muhammad Ghaus as well as with Haridas, but that the evidence suggests he was less affiliated with either religion than with music. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

## The courts of Gwalior and Rewa
Tansen got his first break as a musician and composer in the court of Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} He spent most of his adult life in the court and under the patronage of the Hindu king of Rewa, Raja Ramchandra Singh Baghel, who reigned from 1555 to 1592, and there his musical abilities and studies gained widespread fame. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} He was a close confidant of Ramchandra Singh, and the two used to make music together. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

## Akbar's court
Tansen's reputation brought him to the attention of the Mughal emperor Akbar, who sent messengers to Raja Ramchandra Singh requesting that Tansen join the musicians at the Mughal court. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Tansen initially refused to go and sought to retire into solitude, but Ramchandra Singh persuaded him and sent him to Akbar's court with gifts. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} In 1562, at about the age of sixty, Tansen — still a Vaishnava musician, in the English article's words — arrived at Akbar's court for the first time, and his performances became the subject of many court historians. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Wikidata's structured record of his working life likewise begins in 1562. {cite:wikidata-tansen}

At court he became one of the treasured Navaratnas, and it was there that he received the honorific Mian, so that he is known as Mian Tansen. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} The Urdu article notes the same sequence: after he was included among Akbar's Navaratnas, people addressed him respectfully as Mian Tansen. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-ur} Abul Fazl, the chronicler of Akbar's reign, praised him in the Ain-i-Akbari, and the presence of musicians like Tansen at court formed part of Akbar's attempt to accept and integrate the Hindu and Muslim traditions within the empire. {cite:encyclopedia-com-tansen} {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

## Compositions and writings
Tansen was a composer, musician and vocalist to whom a large number of compositions in the northern Indian subcontinent are attributed, and an instrumentalist who popularised and improved musical instruments. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} His compositions employed Dhrupad and were mostly derived from the Hindu Puranas, composed in Braj Bhasha in praise of gods and goddesses such as Ganesha, Saraswati, Surya, Shiva and Vishnu, though he also composed eulogies for kings and for Akbar. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} He is credited with creating several new ragas, and Encyclopedia.com describes him as an exponent of the gaurhar bani style of dhrupad, who reshaped the form by introducing Persian nuances such as meend and gamaka and who codified the ragas, listing about four hundred. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} {cite:encyclopedia-com-tansen} Two books on music are attached to his name, Sri Ganesh Stotra and Sangita Sara; Encyclopedia.com names Sangita Sara and Ragamala as important documents on music. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} {cite:encyclopedia-com-tansen}

His influence was central to creating the Hindustani classical ethos, and he is regarded as the founder of its first gharana, the Gwalior Gharana; a number of descendants and disciples trace their lineage to him. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

## Family
The English article records that Tansen married a woman of Gwalior named Hussaini, and that their four sons and one daughter all became proficient musicians; the daughter, Saraswati, a veena player, married into another family of musicians. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Encyclopedia.com gives the same marriage and children. {cite:encyclopedia-com-tansen} The article also carries, expressly as legend, the story that he had been married to a daughter of Akbar named Mehrunissa, and that for that marriage he had to convert to Islam. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} The museum records the story where the article puts it — among the legends — and it is one of the readings in the dispute set out below, not a fact of his life. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

## Death and the dispute over his faith
The year of Tansen's death, like much of his biography, is unclear, and the sources divide along the same lines as the rest of his story. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} According to some Islamic historians he died in 1586 in Delhi, and Akbar and much of his court attended a funeral procession that was completed according to Hindu customs in Gwalior. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} The Akbarnama of Abul Fazl, followed by notable Muslim and Hindu historians, gives 26 April 1589 as the date of his death, with a funeral observed mostly by Muslim customs. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} Wikidata records the same day, 26 April 1589, with Agra as the place of death. {cite:wikidata-tansen} Encyclopedia.com gives his death at the age of eighty-three, around 1589. {cite:encyclopedia-com-tansen} His remains were buried in the mausoleum complex of his Sufi master Muhammad Ghaus in Gwalior. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

The dispute over his death is bound up with the dispute over his faith. One tradition, carried in the marriage legend above, holds that he converted to Islam; the Urdu Wikipedia states flatly that, though Hindu by birth, he accepted Islam in his youth. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} {cite:wikipedia-tansen-ur} Wikidata's structured religion field likewise gives Islam. {cite:wikidata-tansen} Against these, the English article records that scholars refute the view that Tansen converted to Islam, pointing to his belief in the Hindu tradition and his many compositions on Hindu gods, while allowing that some of his later compositions show traces of Sufi influence. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} He continued, late in life, to compose in Brajbhasha invoking Krishna and Shiva. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} The museum's ruling for this record is that the question stays open: the readings are preserved, and his faith is recorded as unknown. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} {cite:wikipedia-tansen-ur} {cite:wikidata-tansen}

## Legacy and legends
Every December an annual festival, the Tansen Samaroh, is held in Gwalior to celebrate his life and legacy, and the Tansen Samman award is given to exponents of Hindustani classical music. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} The fort at Fatehpur Sikri is strongly associated with his years at Akbar's court: at the Anup Talao tank near the public audience hall, it is said that Tansen performed ragas at different times of day while the emperor and a select audience honoured him with coins. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} A crater on the planet Mercury has been named in his honour. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}

The bulk of his biography as handed down in court histories and gharana literature consists of inconsistent and miraculous legends: that he brought down rain by singing Raga Megh Malhar, lit lamps by performing Raga Deepak, and calmed a wild white elephant with his singing. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en} The sources themselves mark these as legends, and the museum repeats them only as such. {cite:wikipedia-tansen-en}
