## Summary
Kutayfat ibn al-Afdal was the son of the vizier al-Afdal. He seized power in Egypt after the death of the caliph al-Amir in 1130, declared the Fatimid dynasty deposed, and was killed in 1131.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), signed S. M. Stern, says that after the death of the caliph al-Amir on 12 Dhu 'l-Qa'da 524 (17 October 1130), two favourites of the late caliph, Hazarmard and Barghash, made al-Amir's cousin 'Abd al-Majid temporary regent. Four days later the army raised Kutayfat, who took the title al-Afdal, to the vizierate. He declared the Fatimid dynasty deposed and placed the empire under the Expected Imam of the Twelver Shi'a. 'Abd al-Majid was removed and imprisoned, and Kutayfat ruled as a dictator. Coins of 525 bear the name of "al-Imam Muhammad Abu 'l-Qasim al-Muntazir", and coins of 526 give the vizier the title of representative and lieutenant. Kutayfat did not outlaw Isma'ilism, and the college of qadis he appointed included an Isma'ili, a Hanafi, a Shafi'i and an Imami. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 237}

Kutayfat was killed while riding outside the city, and 'Abd al-Majid was brought out of prison on 16 Muharram 526 (8 December 1131). He was proclaimed caliph under the title al-Hafiz li-Din Allah. The event was commemorated every year until the end of the Fatimid dynasty. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 237}
