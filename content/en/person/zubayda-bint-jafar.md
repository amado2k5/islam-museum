## Overview
Umm Ja'far Zubayda bint Ja'far ibn al-Mansur. al-Baladhuri reports that the first to dig the river of al-Maymun was a steward of hers named Sa'id ibn Zayd {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}.

## Her works, as al-Mas'udi reports them
al-Mas'udi describes her as the best of people in deeds in the days of [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]], because of the rest-houses (dur al-sabil) she established in [[place/mecca|Mecca]], the cisterns, ponds and wells, and the endowments she made for them. She dug the spring of al-Mashash in the Hijaz and smoothed the course of its water to Mecca, and what she spent on it reached one million seven hundred thousand dinars {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.671-672}.

He reports that she was the first to use vessels of gold and silver set with jewels {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.670-671}.

## With al-Rashid
al-Mas'udi reports that she came in to al-Rashid to reproach him for appointing her son Muhammad over Iraq without the army and the commanders, and that she bore a grudge against Ja'far ibn Yahya. She performed the pilgrimage with Salih ibn al-Rashid and the people in the year 208 AH {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.340-341} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.747}.

## In Kahhala's A'lam al-Nisa'
Kahhala says "Zubayda" was a nickname that stuck, her name being Amat al-Aziz: her grandfather al-Mansur, dandling her as a plump, healthy child, would call her "Zubayda" ("little butter"). {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.21} He describes her as a woman of great reach in civilisation, building and patronage of men of letters, poets and physicians; [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]] married her in Baghdad in 165 AH, in al-Mahdi's caliphate, and she bore him Muhammad al-Amin. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.21} He cites al-Khatib al-Baghdadi for her words to al-Ma'mun when he entered Baghdad after her son's death — that she had congratulated herself on his caliphate before seeing him, and that if she had lost a son who was caliph she had been given in exchange a son who was caliph whom she had not borne; al-Ma'mun thereafter sent her each year a hundred thousand dinars and a million dirhams. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.27} Of her generosity to poets: she gave part of this to Abu al-Atahiya, sent al-Abbas ibn al-Ahnaf money for a verse she admired, and interceded with al-Rashid for Abu al-Atahiya when her son al-Qasim had him beaten and confined. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.27} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.28} He reports that she was the first to use vessels of gold and silver set with jewels and domes of silver and ebony, and that she died in Baghdad in Jumada I 216 AH. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.33}
