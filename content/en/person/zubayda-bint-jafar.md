## Overview
Umm Ja'far Zubayda bint Ja'far ibn al-Mansur. al-Baladhuri reports that the first to dig the river of al-Maymun was a steward of hers named Sa'id ibn Zayd {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}.

## Her works, as al-Mas'udi reports them
al-Mas'udi describes her as the best of people in deeds in the days of [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]], because of the rest-houses (dur al-sabil) she established in [[place/mecca|Mecca]], the cisterns, ponds and wells, and the endowments she made for them. She dug the spring of al-Mashash in the Hijaz and smoothed the course of its water to Mecca, and what she spent on it reached one million seven hundred thousand dinars {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.671-672}.

He reports that she was the first to use vessels of gold and silver set with jewels {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.670-671}.

## With al-Rashid
al-Mas'udi reports that she came in to al-Rashid to reproach him for appointing her son Muhammad over Iraq without the army and the commanders, and that she bore a grudge against Ja'far ibn Yahya. She performed the pilgrimage with Salih ibn al-Rashid and the people in the year 208 AH {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.340-341} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.747}.

## In Kahhala's A'lam al-Nisa'
Kahhala says "Zubayda" was a nickname that stuck, her name being Amat al-Aziz: her grandfather al-Mansur, dandling her as a plump, healthy child, would call her "Zubayda" ("little butter"). {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.21} He describes her as a woman of great reach in civilisation, building and patronage of men of letters, poets and physicians; [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]] married her in Baghdad in 165 AH, in al-Mahdi's caliphate, and she bore him Muhammad al-Amin. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.21} He cites al-Khatib al-Baghdadi for her words to al-Ma'mun when he entered Baghdad after her son's death — that she had congratulated herself on his caliphate before seeing him, and that if she had lost a son who was caliph she had been given in exchange a son who was caliph whom she had not borne; al-Ma'mun thereafter sent her each year a hundred thousand dinars and a million dirhams. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.27} Of her generosity to poets: she gave part of this to Abu al-Atahiya, sent al-Abbas ibn al-Ahnaf money for a verse she admired, and interceded with al-Rashid for Abu al-Atahiya when her son al-Qasim had him beaten and confined. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.27} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.28} He reports that she was the first to use vessels of gold and silver set with jewels and domes of silver and ebony, and that she died in Baghdad in Jumada I 216 AH. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.33}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] speaks of Zubayda bint Ja'far, wife of Harun al-Rashid, in the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth episodes of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]]; the archive lacks the episode in which he first told her story. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:02:30} He tells how, after a quarrel, Harun swore she would not spend the night in his dominion, and Abu Yusuf, the student of Abu Hanifa, solved it by having her spend the night in a mosque, which belongs to God. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:03:30} He says she was related to nine caliphs, was raised in luxury but taught the Qur'an, the Sunna, literature and poetry, and was generous to excess. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:04:30}

On her pilgrimage of 186 AH, he says, she saw how pilgrims suffered for water, sold at ten dirhams, and called engineers, who found the nearest steady spring ten miles away across mountains. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:08:00} The channel was cut through rock from outside the sanctuary to inside it at a cost he puts at two million gold dinars, from her own money; when her agent reported early costs she told him to go on even if each stroke of the axe cost a dinar. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:10:30} At the final reckoning she washed the account books away, leaving the account for the Day of Reckoning, and she had cisterns and water works built at the stations of the road back to Baghdad. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:13:30} He cites Ibn Jubayr and al-Yafi'i on these works, still visible to travellers. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:17:30} He also says she kept a hundred slave women who recited the Qur'an through the day, wrote short replies on petitions admired for their concision, corresponded with al-Asma'i on language and listened to scholars' debates from behind a curtain. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep28 00:23:00}

The twenty-ninth episode turns to her grief. He tells that when her son al-Amin moved against his brother al-Ma'mun, she charged al-Amin's commander, if al-Ma'mun were taken, not to humiliate or chain him; al-Amin was defeated and killed in 198 AH. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep29 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep29 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep29 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep29 00:06:30} Al-Ma'mun came to her, asked her forgiveness and offered to be a son to her, and she answered that she could hardly grieve for a son who had left her a brother like him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep29 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep29 00:08:30} She spent the rest of her life in prayer and made the pilgrimage every year. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep29 00:12:00}
