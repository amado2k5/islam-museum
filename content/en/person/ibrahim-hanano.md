# Ibrahim Hanano

## Summary
Ibrahim Hananu (1869–1935) was a Syrian revolutionary and former Ottoman municipal official of Kurdish origin who led a revolt against the French colonial presence in northern Syria in the early 1920s. He went on to become a founding figure of the National Bloc and a member of the constituent assembly that drafted Syria's first republican constitution. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-ar}

## Early life and education
Hananu was born to a wealthy Kurdish landholding family at Kafr Takharim and raised at Aleppo. His birth year is disputed in the English article: Keith David Watenpaugh's Being Modern in the Middle East gives 1879, while Sami Moubayed's Steel & Silk gives 1869; the Arabic article pairs 1869 with 1286 AH and Wikidata gives 1869. He studied at the Imperial High School in Aleppo and then at the Ottoman Law Academy of the Mülkiye school in Istanbul, where as a student he joined the Committee of Union and Progress. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-ar} {cite:wikidata-ibrahim-hanano}

He briefly taught at the military academy, then joined the Ottoman bureaucracy before retiring to manage his estates. A Syrian source from the United Arab Republic era indicates that he embraced nationalism when the Arab Revolt broke out in 1916, joined Faisal's Arab army and entered Aleppo with the Allies in 1918; in Aleppo he was associated with the League of National Defense and the Arab Club. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-en}

## The Hananu Revolt
Breaking out in the autumn of 1919, as the French army landed on the Syrian coast and prepared to occupy the whole country, Hananu's revolt coordinated Aleppo, Idlib and Antakya in one campaign: French troops were disarmed, railroads and telegraph lines destroyed, tanks sabotaged and French attacks on Aleppo foiled. When the French took Aleppo on 23 July 1920 he retreated to Kafr Takharim and reorganised the revolt; the rebels formed a civilian government based at Armanaz and sent Hananu to Turkey to seek aid from Mustafa Kemal's nationalist movement, which was fighting the French in Cilicia. Turkish assistance ended with the Franklin-Bouillon Agreement of October 1921, and the revolt collapsed; its organisation of the northern areas has been read as a prototype of Syrian self-government. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-en}

## Trial and national politics
In 1922 Hananu was arrested and tried before a French military criminal court; his lawyer Fathallah Saqqal argued that he was a political opponent, not a criminal. The French attorney general demanded his execution, but the judge released him on 25 March 1922 under an agreement with the French government. He was placed under house arrest and watched by French intelligence, and released after the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-en}

Hananu was one of the founding fathers of the National Bloc, which steered Syria's independence struggle until 1946; he sat on its permanent council and headed its political bureau. In 1928 he sat in the constituent assembly that drafted the constitution of 1930, and in the 1930s he was known as a hard-liner who refused to negotiate with France until it pledged complete, unconditional independence. In September 1933 a man named Nazi al-Kousa shot him in the legs at Kafr Takharim in an assassination attempt; the shooter was sentenced to ten years and later pardoned by the French commissioner. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-ar}

## Death
Hananu died at Aleppo on 21 November 1935 — the Arabic article pairs the date with 24 Sha'ban 1354 AH — and the English article attributes his death to tuberculosis. A three-day mourning period followed, with newspapers and magazines published under black covers; he is considered one of the most celebrated figures of the resistance to the French Mandate. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-hanano-ar}
