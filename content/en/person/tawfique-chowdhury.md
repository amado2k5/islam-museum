## Summary
Sheikh Dr Tawfique Chowdhury is a Bangladeshi-born Australian physician and Islamic teacher, founder and Executive Chairman of Mercy Mission Worldwide — an umbrella of Muslim charitable, educational and media ventures that operates across dozens of countries. {cite:wikipedia-tawfique-chowdhury}

## Formation
Born in Bangladesh and raised in Australia, Chowdhury qualified as a medical doctor before travelling to study at the Islamic University of Madinah, where he graduated in Islamic theology and went on to teach. The combination of a medical degree and a seminary education became a signature of his public persona — the doctor-shaykh. {cite:wikipedia-tawfique-chowdhury}

## Mercy Mission
Founded in the 2000s, Mercy Mission Worldwide gathered under one roof a family of projects: the AlKauthar Institute, which delivers short intensive courses on Islamic texts taught in English across five continents; the Ramadan TV broadcast venture; charity programmes including feeding and orphan-support work; and the Being ME conferences for Muslim women. At its height the movement described itself as operating in scores of countries. {cite:muslim500-tawfique-chowdhury}

## Work and criticism
Supporters credit Chowdhury with professionalising Muslim community work — bringing management structures, governance and scale to mosque-linked charity — and with training a generation of English-speaking students in classical texts. Critics within the community have at times questioned the movement's finances and expansion model; the museum records the assessment attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-tawfique-chowdhury}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as one of the most institutionally productive Muslim social entrepreneurs of the Anglophone world — a figure whose movement joined schooling, broadcasting and charity under a single leadership. {cite:muslim500-tawfique-chowdhury}
