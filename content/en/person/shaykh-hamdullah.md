# Shaykh Hamdullah

## Amasya and the six scripts

Hamdullah was born at Amasya, in northern Anatolia, probably in 1436; his family had come from Bukhara, and his father, Mustafa Dede, was a sheikh of the Suhrawardiyya order. Some researchers place the birth as early as 1429, and the record preserves both readings. He learned his first writing from Sufi Yahya Çelebizade and then took the six scripts — the aklâm-ı sitte — from Hayreddin Mar'ashi, in the line of Yaqut al-Musta'simi, the great Baghdad master; Mar'ashi also granted him his ijaza in calligraphy. {cite:source/sakip-sabanci-shaykh-hamdullah} {cite:source/wikipedia-shaykh-hamdullah-ar}

At Amasya he taught calligraphy to Prince Bayezid, the governor of the province, and granted the prince his own ijaza; he also copied manuscripts for the personal library of Bayezid's father, Sultan Mehmed II. When Bayezid came to the throne in 1481, Hamdullah moved to Istanbul at his invitation and signed himself "scribe to Sultan Bayezid Han". {cite:source/sakip-sabanci-shaykh-hamdullah}

## Qibla of the Scribes

In Istanbul he worked from the palace atelier attached to the Old Palace, and his hand set the pattern that Ottoman calligraphy followed for centuries. He wrote copies of the Qur'an — tally of the Arabic biography, forty-seven of them — along with prayer books, albums and loose sheets, and his monumental inscriptions went up on Bayezid's mosques in Istanbul and Edirne and on the Firuz Ağa and Davud Pasha mosques. Later generations called him Kıbletü'l-Küttâb, "the qibla of the scribes", the direction to which calligraphers turned. {cite:source/wikipedia-shaykh-hamdullah-ar} {cite:source/dergipark-shaikh-hamdullah-school}

## Withdrawal and death

When Bayezid was deposed in 1512, Hamdullah withdrew from society; he spent his last years at home in prayer, receiving students. In 1520 the new sultan, Süleyman, received him and asked for a Qur'an; Hamdullah excused himself on grounds of age and recommended his student Muhyiddin Amâsî, and the sultan gave him a sable-lined robe. He died a few months later, in 926 AH/1520 CE, asking that his tombstone bear no name; he is buried in Karacaahmet Cemetery at Üsküdar. {cite:source/sakip-sabanci-shaykh-hamdullah}

## The hand that became a standard

What Hamdullah perfected in the cursive scripts became, in time, the model by which Qur'ans were copied across the Islamic world; the standard mushaf style traces back through his students' students to his Amasya training. The Sakıp Sabancı Museum's exhibition and catalogue made his surviving Qur'ans, albums and tools the reference point for the Ottoman calligraphy that followed. {cite:source/dergipark-shaikh-hamdullah-school} {cite:source/sakip-sabanci-shaykh-hamdullah}
