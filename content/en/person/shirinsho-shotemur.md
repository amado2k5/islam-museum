# Shirinsho Shotemur

Shirinsho Shotemur (Tajik: Shirinshoh Shohtemur; 1 December 1899 – 27 October 1937) was a Tajik Soviet politician who played a leading part in the founding of Soviet Tajikistan. He was born in Shughnon District in the Emirate of Bukhara to a poor farmer family, worked at a factory in Tashkent from 1914 to 1918, and began a political career in 1921, when he joined the Communist Party and was sent back to the Pamirs as a member of a political-military team; in 1923–1924 he worked as an instructor of the department of national minorities of the Tajik Communist Party Central Committee and at the same time headed the Tajik communist section.{cite:wikipedia-shirinsho-shotemur-en}

Shotemur was one of the main initiators of the Tajik Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic within the Uzbek SSR, established in 1924. From 1927 he was the Tajik ASSR's representative in the Uzbek SSR; in 1929 he successfully insisted on joining Sughd Province to the Tajik ASSR, and the same year he initiated Tajikistan's exit from the Uzbek SSR and the establishment of the new Tajik Soviet Socialist Republic. He held many leading posts in the Tajik government and party, and from 1933 he chaired the Central Executive Committee of the Tajik SSR.{cite:wikipedia-shirinsho-shotemur-en}

In 1937 Shotemur was charged with participation in an anti-Soviet nationalist organisation and arrested in Moscow; the Military Collegium of the Supreme Court of the USSR sentenced him to death, and he was executed on 27 October 1937. He was posthumously rehabilitated in 1956. After Tajikistan's independence he was named a Hero of Tajikistan on 27 June 2006, jointly with Nusratullo Maksum; his portrait appeared on the three-somoni banknote in 2010, and the Tajik Agrarian University was renamed in his honour in 2009.{cite:wikipedia-shirinsho-shotemur-en}
