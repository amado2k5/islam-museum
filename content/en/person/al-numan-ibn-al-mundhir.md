## Summary
Al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir was king of [[place/al-hira|al-Hira]]. Kisra grew angry with him over a slander contrived by [[person/zayd-ibn-adi-al-ibadi|Zayd ibn Adi al-Ibadi]]. He entrusted his family and wealth to [[person/hani-ibn-masud|Hani' ibn Mas'ud]], lord of the Shayban, and then went to Kisra, who imprisoned him until he died. Hani's refusal to hand over the deposit was the cause of the [[event/battle-of-dhu-qar|Day of Dhu Qar]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}.

## Slander and imprisonment
The kingship continued after [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-ma-al-sama|al-Mundhir ibn Ma al-Sama]] in his line, until the reign of al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir. Kisra grew angry with him because of Zayd ibn Adi's slander, and sent for him. Al-Nu'man set out and alighted in secret at Hani' ibn Mas'ud's, and deposited with him his family and wealth. Then he went to Kisra, who imprisoned him until he died {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}.

## What followed
Kisra appointed [[person/iyas-ibn-qabisa|Iyas ibn Qabisa al-Ta'i]] over al-Hira in his place, and ordered him to demand from Hani' the surrender of what he held. Hani' refused and announced war on the king, and the battle of Dhu Qar followed {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}.

## Notes for researchers
The author does not name this Kisra on this page. The one meant is generally known as Khosrow II (Parviz). No link has been made to his entry until the page is checked against a second source.

## For young visitors
Al-Nu'man was a king who entrusted a friend with his family and wealth before going to an angry king. He was imprisoned and died, and his friend defended the trust with the sword.

## In al-Dinawari
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports that al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir hosted Sayf ibn Dhi Yazan at al-Hira and promised to take him on his yearly embassy to the Persian king. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 63} He says Parviz killed him and gave his kingdom to Iyas ibn Qabisa al-Ta'i, Parviz arguing that al-Nu'man had sided with the Arabs, who believed that rule was passing to them. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 108} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 109}
