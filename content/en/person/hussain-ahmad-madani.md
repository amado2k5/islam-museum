## Summary
Indian Deobandi scholar and hadith master (1879–1957), Shaykh al-Hadith and long-serving rector of Darul Uloom Deoband. He led scholarly opposition to British rule and opposed the Partition of India, advocating 'composite nationalism' (Muttahida Qaumiyat); his works include Naqsh-e-Hayat and his letters. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-ahmad-madani}

## Life and career

Hussain Ahmad Madani was born in 1879 in Bangarmau, Uttar Pradesh, India, and raised in Medina where his family had emigrated — whence his nisba 'al-Madani' — then returned to India, entered Darul Uloom Deoband and graduated under Shaykh Mahmud Hasan Deobandi and Khalil Ahmad Saharanpuri. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-ahmad-madani}

He taught at Deoband until he became Shaykh al-Hadith and its rector, graduating thousands of scholars; with his teacher he joined the 'Silk Letter Movement' against Britain and was interned in Malta for three years during the First World War. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-ahmad-madani}

## Position on Partition and works

He led the Jamiat Ulama-i-Hind against colonialism and joined the Indian National Congress in the independence movement, defending 'composite nationalism' — that Indian Muslims and non-Muslims form one nation against Britain — and so opposed the demand for a separate Pakistan, a stance that made him one of the most debated scholars. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-ahmad-madani}

His works include Muttahida Qaumiyat aur Islam in Urdu, Naqsh-e-Hayat, a two-volume autobiography, his commentary on Tirmidhi, and letters collected in Maktubat-e-Shaykh al-Islam. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-ahmad-madani}

## Legacy

After independence he remained in India as head of Jamiat Ulama-i-Hind and Darul Uloom Deoband, working to secure the place of Indian Muslims in the new state until his death in Deoband in 1957. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-ahmad-madani}

His honorific in Deobandi literature is 'Shaykh al-Islam'; his political stance inspired the discourse of composite nationalism in India, while others see a position overtaken by the events of Partition — the museum presents both descriptions. {cite:wikipedia-hussain-ahmad-madani}
