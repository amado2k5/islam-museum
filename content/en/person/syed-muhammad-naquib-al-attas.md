## Summary

Syed Muhammad Naquib al-Attas (1931–2026) was a Malaysian philosopher and metaphysician, among the first to formulate a systematic critique of secularism from within Islamic philosophy, and the founder of ISTAC in Kuala Lumpur.{cite:wikipedia-syed-muhammad-naquib-al-attas}

## Life and education

He was born in Bogor, Java, into a Hadhrami Alawid family, studied in Malaysia, then in Britain (Sandhurst and the University of London) and at McGill in Canada, where he specialised in Malay literature and Sufism, before returning to university teaching; the state later granted him the title of Royal Professor.{cite:wikipedia-syed-muhammad-naquib-al-attas}

## Intellectual project

His central thesis is the "Islamisation of contemporary knowledge": freeing knowledge from secular Western world-concepts and rebuilding it on an Islamic conception of God, man and religion, as set out in Islam and Secularism (1978) and Prolegomena to the Metaphysics of Islam (1995). In 1987 he founded ISTAC as a graduate institution for Islamic thought, designing its buildings himself in an Islamic style.{cite:wikipedia-syed-muhammad-naquib-al-attas}

## Works and standing

He also wrote on the history of Sufism and Malay literature, including studies of Hamza Fansuri and Nuruddin al-Raniri. Admirers rank him among the greatest contemporary philosophers of the Malay world, while critics regard "Islamisation" as a hermeneutic project rather than a scientific method — the museum records both views.{cite:wikipedia-syed-muhammad-naquib-al-attas}
