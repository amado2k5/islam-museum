## Summary
Abu Abdallah Makhul ibn Abdallah al-Shami, the jurist of Syria in his time; al-Zuhri counted the scholars of his age as four: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab in Medina, al-Sha'bi in Kufa, al-Hasan al-Basri in Basra, and Makhul in Syria. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Origins
Ibn Makula mentioned him in al-Ikmal under the entry for Shadhil, giving his line as Makhul ibn Abi Muslim, his mother Shirah bint Shadhil ibn Sind. Al-Khatib reported that his grandfather Shadhil, a man of Herat, married a daughter of a king of Kabul and died leaving her pregnant; she bore Shahrab, who grew up among his uncles in Kabul and fathered Makhul, who was taken captive as a boy, came into the hands of Sa'id ibn al-As, and was gifted to a woman of Hudhayl who freed him. Other reports make him the mawla of a woman of Qays or of Abu Layla. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Standing and speech
He taught al-Awza'i and Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz; no one of his time gave more verdicts, and he never issued one without saying that opinion errs and hits the mark. He heard Anas ibn Malik, Wathila ibn al-Asqa' and Abu Hind al-Dari, and was based in Damascus. He had a marked lisp, substituting one letter for another; he once spoke for qadar and later recanted. It is said he met five hundred of the Prophet's Companions. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
