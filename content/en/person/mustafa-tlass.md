## Overview
Mustafa Abdul Qadir Tlass (11 May 1932 – 27 June 2017) was a Syrian military officer and politician who served as minister of defence from 1972 to 2004, one of Hafez al-Assad's most trusted loyalists and a member of the four-man Regional Command of his era, remaining in post into the first years of Bashar al-Assad's rule. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} He was also an author and the founder of a publishing house, and some of his books and public statements drew wide controversy inside Syria and abroad. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} He is the father of Manaf Tlass, the Republican Guard general who defected in 2012. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

## Early life and education
Tlass was born in Lower al-Rastan (al-Tahtani), near Homs, into a family the article describes as a prominent Sunni Muslim family from an area with a long tradition of producing military officers; his father, Abdul Qadir Tlass, was a local chief (mukhtar) under the French Mandate, his paternal grandmother was of Circassian origin and his mother of Turkish descent. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} He received his primary and secondary education in Homs according to the English article, while the Arabic article records his early schooling in al-Rastan and his secondary certificate in Homs in 1951. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-ar} He joined the Ba'ath Party at the age of fifteen and entered the Homs Military Academy in 1952, where his close friendship with Hafez al-Assad began; he later completed general staff training at the Voroshilov Academy in Moscow. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-ar}

## Career
Tlass and Assad served together in Cairo during the United Arab Republic union of Syria and Egypt (1958–1961) and both worked to break up a union they viewed as unfairly balanced in Egypt's favour. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} In the 1960s Assad rose through the 1963 Ba'athist coup and promoted Tlass to high military and party posts: Ba'athist army commander of Homs in 1965, then chief of staff in 1968. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} In 1969 Tlass led a military mission to Beijing and secured weapons deals with the Chinese government. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

After Hafez al-Assad seized power in the 1970 "Corrective Revolution", Tlass became minister of defence in 1972 and held the post for over three decades; by the early 2000s he was also deputy prime minister, and within the party he headed the military bureau and the military committee. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} The article notes that Tlass and Assad favoured a coordinated military command with Egypt, which contributed to the two-front assault of the October 1973 war; the Arabic article describes him as one of the war's chief planners on the Syrian side and a commander on the Quneitra front. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-ar}

## After Hafez al-Assad
After Hafez al-Assad's death in 2000, Tlass sat on the nine-member committee that oversaw the transition and, as defence minister, oversaw the constitutional amendment lowering the presidential age from 40 to 34 that enabled Bashar al-Assad's succession; he and his supporters were viewed by many as opponents of Bashar's discreet liberalisation and as defenders of established privileges. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} Hasan Turkmani replaced him as defence minister in 2004, and he quit the regional command in 2005. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} After the uprising against Assad began in 2011, Tlass left Syria for France for what he described as medical treatment; opposition accounts state that he left partly in response to the government's violent crackdown in his home town of al-Rastan. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

## Writings and publishing
Tlass cultivated a reputation as a man of culture and became a patron of Syrian literature. He wrote books on Syria's military and political history, general Arab history and poetry, among them a study of Marshal Zhukov's strategy, the subject of his 1986 Sorbonne dissertation defence, "Zenobia: Queen of Palmyra" (1985), and his memoirs "Mirat Hayati" (Reflections of My Life), first published in 2005 and eventually extended to five volumes. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-ar} The Arabic article lists a long bibliography of his poetry and history, including "Sayf Allah: Khalid ibn al-Walid", "The Algerian Revolution" and a dictionary of Arabic names. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-ar} He founded the publishing house Dar Tlass, which by the late 1980s was a major commercial publisher issuing more than thirty titles a year; the house was internationally criticised for publishing material described as anti-Semitic. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section presents positions reported in the sources; it is not the museum's verdict on any of them.

**Role in repression:** The article states that as defence minister Tlass was functional in suppressing all dissent, Islamist or democrat, and that, according to The New York Times, he was closely involved in the government's response to the 1982 uprising in Hama; in an interview he stated that he had approved many death sentences whose number he could no longer recall, and that during the 1980s "150 death sentences a week were carried out by hanging in Damascus alone", describing the approach to power as: "We used weapons to assume power, and we wanted to hold onto it." {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} The scholar As'ad AbuKhalil argues, in the article's account, that Tlass suited Assad as defence minister in that "he had no power base, he was mediocre, and he had no political skills, and his loyalty to his boss was complete". {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

**Documented public statements:** The article records that in 1998 he boasted to the newspaper Al Bayan that he had given the green light to "the resistance" in Lebanon to attack and kill 241 US Marines and 58 French paratroopers (the 1983 Beirut barracks bombings), while sparing the Italian contingent out of admiration for the actress Gina Lollobrigida, and stated the same year that there was no such country as Jordan, only "South Syria". {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} It also records his boast to the National Assembly that he had awarded the Medal of the Republic's Hero to a soldier from Aleppo who, he said, had killed 28 captured Israeli soldiers with an axe in the October War; his statement during the 1991 Gulf War of "overwhelming joy" at Iraqi Scud missiles falling on Israel; and his public denunciation of Yasser Arafat in 1998. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} In 2000 the widow and children of Ira Weinstein, killed in a 1996 Hamas suicide bombing, filed a lawsuit against Tlass and the head of Syrian military intelligence in Lebanon, Ghazi Kanaan, charging that they were responsible for providing the perpetrators with material resources and training. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

**The Matzah of Zion:** In 1983 Tlass published "The Matzah of Zion", which revived the blood-libel accusation attached to the Damascus affair of 1840 and argued that no Arab country should ever sign a peace treaty with Israel; the article describes the accusation as an ancient blood libel and as false, and records that Tlass reprinted the book several times, defended it to Der Spiegel as "an historical study" based on documents, that it was translated into English in 1991, and that it was later used in international forums as a purported basis for renewing the charge. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} Of his "Zenobia", scholars quoted in the article note that he presented Palmyra as part of "Greater Syria", linked its struggle against Rome to the modern conflict with Israel, and that the book is an example of the Syrian government's use of ancient history to support nationalism and shape public memory under Ba'athist rule. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

**The memoirs:** The article records that the memoirs "Mirat Hayati" were widely ridiculed around the Arab world and outraged Bashar al-Assad, making various claims about ordering summary executions and crediting Tlass himself with bringing Hafez and Bashar al-Assad to power. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en}

## Personal life and death
Tlass married Lamia al-Jabiri, a member of the Aleppine aristocracy, in 1958; they had four children: Nahid, Firas, Manaf and Sarya. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} He died on 27 June 2017 at the age of 85; the English article names the Avicenne Hospital in Bobigny, France, while the Arabic article names a Paris hospital and dates his death to 5 Shawwal 1438 AH. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-tlass-ar}
