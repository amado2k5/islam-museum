# Houari Boumédiène

Houari Boumédiène (1932–1978) was an Algerian officer and politician who ruled Algeria from 1965 until his death. Born Mohammed ben Brahim Boukharouba at Medjaz Amar in Guelma province in eastern Algeria, he was educated first at a Qur'anic school in Guelma, then at the Arabic secondary school and the Islamic Institute in Constantine, and studied at al-Azhar in Cairo. He took the name Houari Boumédiène as a nom de guerre in 1955, when he joined the National Liberation Front. {cite:source/wikipedia-houari-boumediene-ar} {cite:source/wikipedia-houari-boumediene-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-houari-boumediene-ur}

## From the liberation army to power

Boumédiène rose through the ranks of the National Liberation Army until he commanded it by 1960, and after independence President Ahmed Ben Bella appointed him minister of defence. On 19 June 1965 he led the bloodless coup that overthrew Ben Bella, took the chairmanship of the Revolutionary Council, and governed Algeria with Arab socialist and pan-Arab policies, nationalising hydrocarbons and building a national industrial base. {cite:source/wikipedia-houari-boumediene-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-houari-boumediene-ar}

## President, and death in office

Under the constitution of 1976 the presidency was restored, and Boumédiène was elected president with 99.46 per cent of the vote; he remained in office until his death in Algiers on 27 December 1978, when Rabah Bitat succeeded him on an interim basis and then Chadli Bendjedid. {cite:source/wikipedia-houari-boumediene-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-houari-boumediene-ar}
