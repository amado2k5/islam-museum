## Overview
Abd al-Aziz ibn Rufay al-Asadi, of Ta'if and then Kufa, Abu Abd Allah, is called by al-Dhahabi the reliable hadith transmitter {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}. The sign printed with his entry shows his hadith is in the six canonical books {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Anas ibn Malik, the judge Shurayh, Zayd ibn Wahb, Ubayd ibn Umayr and others, and al-Bukhari said he saw A'isha {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}. Shu'ba, Sufyan, Abu al-Ahwas, Sharik, Jarir ibn Abd al-Hamid, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and others narrated from him, and among his own teachers and peers Amr ibn Dinar {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}.

## What the scholars said
More than one scholar called him reliable, and his hadith comes to about sixty reports {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}. It is said that he rarely married a woman who did not ask for divorce, because of how much he enjoyed her {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}. Al-Dhahabi gives with his own chain the hadith he narrated from Suwayd ibn Ghafala from Abu Dharr, that whoever dies without associating anything with God enters Paradise, and calls it sound and with a high chain {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}.

## His death
He lived to a great age, about a hundred or ninety years, and died in 130 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2285}.
