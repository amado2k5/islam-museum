## Summary
Hassan II was king of Morocco from 1961 until his death in 1999. He was born on 9 July 1929 at Rabat, the eldest son of Mohammed V, received an education combining traditional Islamic instruction with modern studies at the Royal College in Rabat, and pursued legal studies at the University of Bordeaux. He accompanied his father during his exile and took part in the negotiations that culminated in Morocco's independence in 1956; as crown prince he was named commander-in-chief of the Royal Armed Forces. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-ii-of-morocco-en}

## Reign
On his accession in 1961 Hassan inherited a fragile political system. In 1962 he promulgated Morocco's first constitution, formally establishing a constitutional monarchy and a multi-party system while reserving extensive executive powers for the king. His early reign was marked by instability, including the Sand War with Algeria in 1963 and the Casablanca protests of 1965, after which he declared a state of exception and ruled by decree for several years. He oversaw the recovery of Sidi Ifni from Spain in 1969. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-ii-of-morocco-en}

He survived two major coup attempts — the Skhirat attack of 1971 and the air force attack of 1972 — which reinforced his determination to centralise power. In 1975 he organised the Green March, a mass mobilisation asserting Moroccan sovereignty over the former Spanish Sahara, leading to Morocco's control over much of the territory and the prolonged Western Sahara conflict. His rule combined state-building and economic development with strict political control, characterised by an authoritarian system particularly during the Years of Lead. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-ii-of-morocco-en}

Hassan died at Rabat on 23 July 1999 and was buried in the Royal Mausoleum there; he was succeeded by his son Mohammed VI. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-ii-of-morocco-en} {cite:wikipedia-hassan-ii-of-morocco-ar}
