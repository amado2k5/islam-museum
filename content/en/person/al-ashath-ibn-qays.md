## Summary
[[person/al-ashath-ibn-qays|Al-Ash'ath ibn Qays]] ibn Ma'dikarib al-Kindi, kunya Abu Muhammad. His name was Ma'dikarib — he was nicknamed al-Ash'ath ("the dishevelled"); Muhammad ibn Yazid said his head was always unkempt, so he was called al-Ash'ath {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}.

## The delegation
Ibn Sa'd said he came as a delegate to the Prophet in year 10 AH with seventy riders of Kinda; he was of Kinda's kings, lord of the Hadramawt quarter-tribute per Ibn al-Kalbi. Bukhari and Muslim recorded his hadith in the Sahih {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}.

## The Ridda
Al-Ash'ath had apostatised among the apostate Kindis; captured and brought to Abu Bakr, he became Muslim and was released, and Abu Bakr married him his sister Umm Farwa — a long story {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}. Al-Waqidi relates from Zayd ibn Aslam from his father that he heard al-Ash'ath say to Abu Bakr when brought in the Ridda: "keep me for your war and marry me your sister" — and he did {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}. In al-Tabarani's account, when Abu Bakr married him his sister, al-Ash'ath drew his sword, entered the camel market and began hamstringing every camel; the people cried "al-Ash'ath has disbelieved!" — when finished he cast down his sword and said: "by God I have not disbelieved, but this man married me his sister; had we been in our land the wedding feast would be other than this — people of Medina, eat; camel owners, come take their prices" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}. And in Qays ibn Abi Hazim's account from Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, al-Ash'ath put Jarir forward in a funeral prayer saying: "he did not apostatise — I had apostatised" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}.

## The conquests and the strife
He witnessed the Yarmuk in Syria, al-Qadisiyya and others in Iraq, settled in [[place/kufa|Kufa]], and witnessed Siffin with [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] — reports exist of him with Ali {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}.

## His death
Khalifa, Abu Nu'aym and more than one said he died forty nights after Ali's killing and al-Hasan ibn Ali prayed over him; it is also said he died in 42 AH — and an account in al-Tabarani suggests he outlived that {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 70}.
