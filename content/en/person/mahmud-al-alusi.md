## Summary
Abu al-Thana' Shihab al-Din Mahmud ibn Abd Allah ibn Mahmud ibn Darwish ibn Ashur al-Alusi of Baghdad, exegete and mufti of Baghdad and author of [[work/ruh-al-maani|Ruh al-Ma'ani]], traced his descent to al-Husayn ibn Ali. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.27} The origin of the name al-Alusi is disputed: some link it to Alus, a village on the Euphrates near Anat, which Muhammad Bahjat al-Athari favoured; other views exist. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.27}

## Birth and upbringing
He was born in al-Karkh, Baghdad, shortly before the Friday prayer on 14 Sha'ban 1217 AH, into a learned family. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.28} Before he was seven he had memorised the Qur'an together with the Ajurrumiyya, Ibn Malik's Alfiyya and the Rahbiyya on inheritance, and read Ghayat al-Ikhtisar in Shafi'i law; before ten he had mastered Arabic and read some Shafi'i and Hanafi law, logic and hadith. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.28} In the preface to his commentary he says he read by moonlight when candles ran short and was speaking on the finer points of tafsir before he was twenty. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.4}

## Teachers
The thesis Juhud al-Alusi al-Balaghiyya lists among his best-known teachers: Mulla Husayn al-Juburi, from whom he learned the Qur'an; his father Abd Allah, for grammar, inheritance and Shafi'i law; Ala' al-Din Ali al-Mawsili, with whom he stayed fourteen years until he graduated and received his licence; Abd Allah al-Umari, for the readings of Nafi', Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr; Ali ibn Muhammad Sa'id al-Suwaydi, with whom he read Ibn Hajar's Sharh al-Nukhba; and [[person/khalid-al-naqshbandi|Diya' al-Din Khalid al-Naqshbandi]], with whom he read the question of the divine attributes and from whom he took the Naqshbandi path; others licensed him, among them Abd al-Rahman al-Kuzbari and Ahmad Arif Hikmat, whom al-Alusi licensed in turn. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.29} {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.30} {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.31}

## Teaching and the office of mufti
Licensed at twenty-four, he was appointed that same day to teach at a college, then taught at many others until he became mufti, and was consulted from distant lands; his students included his brothers Abd al-Rahman and Abd al-Hamid. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.32} Because the office of mufti went only to a Hanafi and he was a Shafi'i, he studied the Hanafi school in a short time. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.41} His grandson Abu al-Ma'ali reports that while mufti he followed Abu Hanifa in transactions but kept his own school in worship, and after his dismissal would say: "I am a Shafi'i unless the evidence shows me otherwise." {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.57}

## Works
The thesis, citing al-Athari, says his works exceeded twenty, apart from fatwas, letters and poems, and lists among them [[work/ruh-al-maani|Ruh al-Ma'ani]]; al-Ajwiba al-Iraqiyya 'ala al-As'ila al-Iraniyya; al-Ajwiba al-Iraqiyya 'ala al-As'ila al-Lahuriyya, answering a question from the scholars of Lahore about those who revile the Companions; al-Nafahat al-Qudsiyya fi al-Radd 'ala al-Imamiyya; Kashf al-Turra 'an al-Ghurra on language; a gloss on Sharh al-Qatr written at thirteen and finished by his son [[person/noman-al-alusi|Nu'man]]; Ghara'ib al-Ightirab, on his journey to Istanbul and his own life; and Nashwat al-Shumul and Nashwat al-Mudam, on his journey to Istanbul and his return. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.34} {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.35} {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.36} {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.37} Whether Daqa'iq al-Tafsir is his is disputed, as the thesis notes. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.38} {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.39}

## Standing
Abbas al-Azzawi says the modern age in Iraq should be called "the age of al-Alusi", and al-Zirikli calls him "Salafi in belief, a mujtahid"; on his creed see [[question/was-al-alusi-salafi-in-creed|Was al-Alusi Salafi in creed?]]. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.44} The poet Abd al-Ghaffar al-Akhras praised him in thirty-seven poems. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.45}

## Death
His fortunes turned after his time as mufti and he travelled to Istanbul; after thirteen months he came back, met cold and heavy rain on the way, and remained ill until he died in Baghdad in Dhu al-Qa'da 1270 AH, aged fifty-three. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.58}
