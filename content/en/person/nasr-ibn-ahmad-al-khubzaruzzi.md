## Summary
Abu'l-Qasim Nasr ibn Ahmad al-Khubzaruzzi, the Basran poet, was illiterate — he could neither spell nor write — and baked rice-bread in a shop at a spot in Basra. He would recite his ghazal verses while crowds pressed upon him, taking blessing in hearing his poetry and marvelling at his state. The poet Abu'l-Husayn Muhammad ibn Ahmad, known as Ibn Lankak al-Basri, used to frequent his shop to hear his verse; he took care of him and collected a diwan for him. Al-Khatib mentioned him in his history; al-Mu'afa ibn Zakariya al-Nahrawani and Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Nawshari transmitted from him, and the two Khalidis mentioned him in their book. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
