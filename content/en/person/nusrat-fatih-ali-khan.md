# Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

## Summary

Ustad Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (13 October 1948 – 16 August 1997) was a Pakistani singer, Hindustani classical vocalist, songwriter and composer, above all associated with qawwali, the Sufi devotional song of Pakistan and India. He belonged to the Qawwal Bachon ka Gharana of Delhi and is known as 'Shahanshah-e-Qawwali'. {cite:wikipedia-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan}

## Early life

Born Pervez Fateh Ali Khan at Lyallpur (now Faisalabad), he came from a Punjabi Muslim family from Jalandhar in which qawwali had passed down for some six centuries. He was the fifth child and first son of the musicologist and qawwal Fateh Ali Khan; his younger brother was Farrukh Fateh Ali Khan, and Badar Miandad and Sher Miandad were his cousins. His father wanted him to be a doctor or an engineer, qawwals then having low standing, but relented when he saw his gift. {cite:wikipedia-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan-en}

## Career

He began as the tabla player of his father's party; after his father's death in 1964 he became its lead singer in 1965 alongside his uncle Mubarak Ali Khan, and led it from 1971. Early hits included 'Ni Main Jana Jogi De Naal', to Bulleh Shah's verse (1971), and 'Haq Ali Ali'. He sang mainly in Urdu and Punjabi, and occasionally in Persian, Braj Bhasha and Hindi. {cite:wikipedia-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan-en}

In the 1980s he signed with Oriental Star Agencies of Birmingham and sang at WOMAD in London in the summer of 1985, in Paris (1985, 1988), Japan (1987) and New York (1989). In 1988 he worked with Peter Gabriel on the score of 'The Last Temptation of Christ' and in 1989 signed with Real World, reaching a wider audience with albums such as 'Mustt Mustt' (1990). In 1995 he sang with Eddie Vedder for 'Dead Man Walking', and he scored Indian and Pakistani films and performed in over forty countries. {cite:wikipedia-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan-en}

## Death

He died of a sudden cardiac arrest at the Cromwell Hospital in London on 16 August 1997, aged 48, having travelled there for treatment of liver and kidney problems; his body was taken to Faisalabad. His legacy is carried by his nephews [[person/rahat-fatih-ali-khan|Rahat Fateh Ali Khan]] and Rizwan-Muazzam. {cite:wikipedia-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan}

## Honours

He received the [[award/pride-of-performance|Pride of Performance]] from the President of Pakistan in 1987, the UNESCO Music Prize in 1995, the Fukuoka Asian Culture Prize for arts and culture in 1996 and the Hafiz Ali Khan Award in 1997, and was nominated for two Grammy Awards in 1997. As of 2001 he held the Guinness record for 'most qawwali recordings', with over 125 albums. {cite:wikipedia-nusrat-fatih-ali-khan-en}
