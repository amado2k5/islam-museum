## Summary
Shams al-Din Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahim al-Mizzi, the timekeeper (690–750/1291–1349), studied Qur'an readings and Arabic, then excelled in astronomy and mathematics; he was famous for unusual kinds of astrolabe, and it is said that his astrolabes sold in his time for ten dinars or more and his quadrants for two. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 20}

## Life and work
- He studied in Cairo under Ibn al-Akfani and lived in Damascus as timekeeper at the Umayyad Mosque. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 20}
- His interests were the making of observational instruments, the motion of the sun, and the study of apogee and perigee. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 20}
- His works include treatises on the folded quadrant, the winged quadrant and the hidden quadrant, *Tuhfat al-albab fi al-amal bil-asturlab* and *Kashf al-rayb fi al-amal bi-rub' al-jayb*. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 20}
- The book mentions a fine sundial bearing his signature in the British Museum. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 21}

He should not be confused with the hadith scholar Jamal al-Din al-Mizzi.
