## Summary
Atifete Jahjaga (born 20 April 1975) is a Kosovo Albanian politician and former police officer who served as the third President of Kosovo from 7 April 2011 to 7 April 2016. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en} {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ar}

She was Kosovo's first woman president, its first non-partisan presidential candidate, and the youngest person elected to the office — chosen not from politics but from the top of the Kosovo Police, where she had risen to deputy director with a general's rank. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en} {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ur}

Her presidency worked the themes of European integration, anti-corruption, women's advancement and support for survivors of wartime sexual violence. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

## Early life and education
Jahjaga was born on 20 April 1975 at Gjakova, then part of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, into a Kosovo Albanian family whose paternal ancestry came from Berisha in northern Albania. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en} {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ur}

She attended elementary and secondary school in Gjakova and graduated from the Faculty of Law of the University of Pristina in 2000. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

In 2006/07 she completed a graduate and certificate programme in police management and criminal law at the University of Leicester, and she trained at the George C. Marshall European Center for Security Studies in Germany and at the FBI National Academy in the United States, receiving a postgraduate certification in crime science from the University of Virginia in 2007. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en} {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ur}

## Police career
After the Kosovo War she completed the Kosovo Police Academy and rose through the new force — major, then colonel, then major general. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en} {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ur}

She served as Deputy Director of the Kosovo Police and briefly as acting General Director in 2010, holding the rank of general lieutenant colonel, described in the sources as the highest rank achieved by a woman officer in Southeastern Europe. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en} {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ur}

The Urdu article records that American officers and diplomats marked her out as a representative of Kosovo's new generation of officials, presenting her to senior American visitors. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ur}

## Election
On 6 April 2011 Jahjaga was announced as the consensus presidential candidate of the governing PDK, the opposition LDK and the New Kosovo Alliance, with the support of the United States ambassador, Christopher Dell. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

The next day the Assembly elected her in the first round, by 80 votes to 10 of the 100 MPs present — the only Kosovar president elected at the first ballot. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

The Arabic article notes that her election met internal controversy at home and a welcome in Europe. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ar}

## Presidency, 2011–2016
In 2012 she returned the draft Criminal Code to the Assembly over provisions on defamation and the disclosure of journalistic sources, judging them against European standards, and she established a National Council for European Integration and a National Anti-Corruption Council, the latter on 14 February 2012. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

She used the presidential pardon sparingly — granting none in 2015 — a restraint that drew criticism from the Ministry of Justice and from prisoner advocates, as the article records. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

She mediated the political impasse that followed the 2014 elections, work for which President Barack Obama commended her. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

Against violent extremism she addressed the UN Security Council in September 2014 and in March 2015 decreed the law banning Kosovars from joining foreign armed conflicts. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

At Easter 2013 she visited the Serbian Orthodox monastery of Dečani as a gesture of inter-religious tolerance. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

In March 2014 she founded the National Council for Survivors of Sexual Violence, and in 2012 she hosted the International Women's Summit in Pristina, whose outcome document is known as the Pristina Principles; she joined the Council of Women World Leaders in May 2012. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

In 2015 she secured a €22 million donation from Sheikha Bint Mubarak of the United Arab Emirates for a paediatric surgical hospital in Kosovo, and in December 2015 Kosovo passed the Millennium Challenge Corporation scorecard and was selected as eligible for a compact. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

The article records that during her term her office ranked as the most trusted institution in Kosovo. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

## After the presidency
In March 2018 Jahjaga founded the Jahjaga Foundation, working on women's empowerment, youth and the survivors of wartime sexual violence. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

Her honours include an honorary doctorate from Durham University in 2013, the Clinton Global Initiative's Leadership in Public Service Award in 2014, and an honorary doctorate of laws from the University of Leicester in 2015. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en}

## Belief and affiliation
The English article names no religion for Jahjaga; the Arabic article says only that she descends from a Muslim family, and Wikidata gives Islam generically. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-en} {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ar} {cite:wikidata-atifete-jahjaga}

A family's described identity is not an attestation of her own school, so her faith is recorded as unknown in the data. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ar}

## Personal life
The Arabic article records that Jahjaga is married and has no children. {cite:wikipedia-atifete-jahjaga-ar}
