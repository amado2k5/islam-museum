## Overview
Badi al-Zaman Abu al-Izz Ismail ibn al-Razzaz al-Jazari was an engineer and author on mechanical devices. TDV calls him an Islamic scholar and says little is known of his life beyond his book's introduction {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. It records service to the Artuqids at [[place/amid|Amid]] in 1181–1206 and a book commissioned by Nasir al-Din Mahmud, completed in 1205 according to the entry {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. Service dates are not converted into birth and death dates.

## Engineer and court
TDV connects the book to Artuqid patronage; the Met's catalogue likewise identifies Nasir al-Din Mahmud, ruler of Amid, as its commissioner {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506} {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Catalogue Entry}. This documents work and patronage, not a childhood or training supplied from conjecture.

TDV's description “Islamic scholar” is the Muslim identity basis here. The passage gives no personal school: the museum records its Sunni default frame explicitly, not inferred from his name, origin or workplace {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}.

## The book of devices
TDV gives the titles *Kitab fi marifat al-hiyal al-handasiyya* and *al-Jami bayn al-ilm wa-l-amal al-nafi fi sinaat al-hiyal*. Its six divisions cover clocks, vessels for drinking and banquets, handwashing and bloodletting vessels, fountains and automatic musical instruments, water-raising machines and miscellaneous devices {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. The first four have ten chapters each and the last two five each: fifty devices in the account {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}.

The encyclopedia describes coloured drawings, lettered parts and instructions moving from the general form to making components, assembly and operation {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. It is therefore not simply a picture gallery of marvels: manufacturing detail belongs to its text.

## Improving machines
TDV places him in a tradition extending from Hellenistic engineering to the Banu Musa, describing an engineer improving earlier work, not the inventor of every underlying principle in his book {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. Attribution of a particular device is kept separate from claiming he invented clocks, pumping and automated motion as a whole.

An example is the [[invention/elephant-clock|elephant clock]]. The Met describes the rider and bird moving, a ball passing to a dragon and then a vessel and gong every half hour, and counting balls to determine elapsed time {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Catalogue Entry}. Features TDV assigns to another clock or a pump are not transferred to this machine.

## Surviving texts and images
TDV records Donald Hill's complete English translation in 1974 and Ahmad Yusuf al-Hassan's Arabic edition in 1979 using the Bodleian and Istanbul copies {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. These are editions reported by the reference, not primary pages this article claims to have independently read.

Met folio 57.51.23 preserves the elephant-clock image in a copy by Farrukh ibn Abd al-Latif, dated 715/1315, probably produced in Syria or Iraq {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Overview}. Copyist and original engineer-author are distinct. The page witnesses the book's transmission and illustration, not a surviving original machine.
