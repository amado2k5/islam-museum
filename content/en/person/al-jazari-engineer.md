## Overview
Badi al-Zaman Abu al-Izz Ismail ibn al-Razzaz al-Jazari was an engineer and author on mechanical devices. TDV calls him an Islamic scholar and says little is known of his life beyond his book's introduction {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. It records service to the Artuqids at [[place/amid|Amid]] in 1181–1206 and a book commissioned by Nasir al-Din Mahmud, completed in 1205 according to the entry {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. Service dates are not converted into birth and death dates.

## Engineer and court
TDV connects the book to Artuqid patronage; the Met's catalogue likewise identifies Nasir al-Din Mahmud, ruler of Amid, as its commissioner {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506} {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Catalogue Entry}. This documents work and patronage, not a childhood or training supplied from conjecture.

TDV's description “Islamic scholar” is the Muslim identity basis here. The passage gives no personal school: the museum records its Sunni default frame explicitly, not inferred from his name, origin or workplace {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}.

## The book of devices
TDV gives the titles *Kitab fi marifat al-hiyal al-handasiyya* and *al-Jami bayn al-ilm wa-l-amal al-nafi fi sinaat al-hiyal*. Its six divisions cover clocks, vessels for drinking and banquets, handwashing and bloodletting vessels, fountains and automatic musical instruments, water-raising machines and miscellaneous devices {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. The first four have ten chapters each and the last two five each: fifty devices in the account {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}.

The encyclopedia describes coloured drawings, lettered parts and instructions moving from the general form to making components, assembly and operation {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. It is therefore not simply a picture gallery of marvels: manufacturing detail belongs to its text.

## Improving machines
TDV places him in a tradition extending from Hellenistic engineering to the Banu Musa, describing an engineer improving earlier work, not the inventor of every underlying principle in his book {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. Attribution of a particular device is kept separate from claiming he invented clocks, pumping and automated motion as a whole.

An example is the [[invention/elephant-clock|elephant clock]]. The Met describes the rider and bird moving, a ball passing to a dragon and then a vessel and gong every half hour, and counting balls to determine elapsed time {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Catalogue Entry}. Features TDV assigns to another clock or a pump are not transferred to this machine.

## Surviving texts and images
Cleveland's folio preserves the [[invention/jazari-peacock-washer|peacock washer design]]: collected water raises a float, releasing a soap-bearing figure and then a towel-bearing one; used water drains through a spigot {cite:cleveland-jazari-peacock verso catalogue}. The museum attributes the book to al-Jazari but explicitly says construction of this device is uncertain. Its leaf is a Syrian copy of 1315, not a surviving machine {cite:cleveland-jazari-peacock recto catalogue}.

Another example is the [[invention/jazari-double-piston-pump|double-piston pump]]: flowing water drives a wheel and gears, with a pin and slotted rod converting rotation into reciprocating piston-rod motion {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. Al-Hassani explains a synchronized pair; the Science Museum holds a half-size model, not an original machine from al-Jazari's lifetime {cite:fstc-jazari-mechanical-genius pump discussion} {cite:smg-jazari-double-pump catalogue}. Al-Hassani gives book completion as 1206, against TDV's 1205. The pump record preserves the difference as a documentary date, not a dated physical installation {cite:fstc-jazari-mechanical-genius biography} {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}.

TDV records Donald Hill's complete English translation in 1974 and Ahmad Yusuf al-Hassan's Arabic edition in 1979 using the Bodleian and Istanbul copies {cite:tdv-cezeri pp. 505–506}. Earlier references were indirect; the new selected Hill reading is specified below, not a claim to reading the entire book or1979 Arabic edition.

Met folio 57.51.23 preserves the elephant-clock image in a copy by Farrukh ibn Abd al-Latif, dated 715/1315, probably produced in Syria or Iraq {cite:met-elephant-clock-575123 Overview}. Copyist and original engineer-author are distinct. The page witnesses the book's transmission and illustration, not a surviving original machine.

## Castle clock and calibration

The first clock chapter describes the [[invention/jazari-castle-clock|castle clock]], reporting failed inherited divisions before empirical calibration of the water outlet {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.17–18,24–25}. Seasonal day/night divisions require an operator to return water and set indicators; displaying sun and moon is not independently computing their positions {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.39–41,editor-notes-I.1}. Hill's introduction derives1204 or1206 from manuscript differences, alongside TDV's1205: a documentary-date disagreement, not a verified construction year {cite:jazari-hill-1974 introduction-life} {cite:tdv-cezeri pp.505–506}. Boston14.533 is an anonymous1354 manuscript painting, not an original machine or autograph {cite:mfa-castle-clock-14533 metadata}.

## The musical boat and its limits

CategoryII, chapter4 describes a [[invention/jazari-musical-boat|musical display boat]] using carried water in bursts for hand movements and whistling, collecting it in the hull for return the following day {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.107–109}. The harpist's fingers do not touch the strings, and the drawing legend's peg count conflicts with the earlier wording; Hill also identifies the missing sailor drive and lengthening gaps as water falls {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.109,256}. This is a finite assembly account, not proof of the first programmed robot or perpetual motion. Freer F1930.73 is a1315 copy credited to calligrapher Farruq ibn Abd al-Latif, not original hardware or the author's portrait {cite:freer-jazari-musical-boat catalogue}.

## Fountain alternation and its conditions

The [[invention/jazari-alternating-fountain|two-bucket alternating fountain]], IV.1, uses a small branch to fill a timing bucket that switches supply between two tanks, alternating one vertical jet and six arcs; external water, elevation and calibration are conditions, not perpetual motion or measured precision {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.157–158,263}. The author's criticism of Banu Musa accounts leaves original-versus-copy confusion unresolved, while Hill distinguishes omitted bucket projections and wrongly oriented bearings from his modern interpretation {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.157,263}. This is a separate assembly role, not each nozzle counted as a new invention.

## Candle clock and calibration

I.7 describes the [[invention/jazari-swordsman-candle-clock|swordsman candle clock]]: a calibrated candle rises under a weight, uncovering successive ball chambers; each ball moves a wick-trimming figure before discharge through the falcon {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.83–86}. The author reports an earlier lion clock whose mechanism he did not know and repeated movement trials, not invention of the first candle clock; Hill identifies Oxford drawing travel/ball-position problems and modern inferred details {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.83,86,252–253}. Freer F1930.71 is a1315 copy credited to calligrapher Farruq ibn Abd al-Latif, not original hardware or the author's portrait {cite:freer-jazari-candle-clock catalogue}.

## Chest lock and its conditions

VI.3 describes the [[invention/jazari-twelve-letter-lock|twelve-letter chest lock]], four groups of three letter settings and a shared closure, explicitly acknowledging earlier two-, four- and six-letter locks {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.199–201}. Hill separates omitted or conflicting drawing details from modern interpretation and notes a physical-protection limitation; setting count is not security certification {cite:jazari-hill-1974 pp.268–269}. Attribution concerns an integrated improvement, not the first letter lock or proven ancestry of every comparative chest and modern lock.
