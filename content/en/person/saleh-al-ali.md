# Saleh al-Ali

## Summary
Sheikh Saleh al-Ali (1883 – 13 April 1950) was a Syrian Alawite military commander who led the Alawite revolt of 1919–1921 against the French Mandate of Syria. After a general amnesty in 1922 he abstained from all political activity until his death at Tartus. {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-en} {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-ar}

## Background
Salih al-Ali was born at al-Shaykh Badr, in the Syrian Coastal Mountain Range. The year of his birth is given two ways: the English article's lead and infobox, the Arabic article and the Urdu article's body give 1883, while the Urdu article's lead gives 1884, the English article's death-age template computes from 1884 and files him under "1884 births", and Wikidata carries both values. He was born to a family of Alawite notables; the Arabic article names his father, Sheikh Ali Salman of al-Murayqib, as a sheikh, religious man and poet and one of the known sheikhs of the Alawite ta'ifa, and his mother as Hababa, daughter of Sheikh Ali Eid of Bsharaghi near Jableh. {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-en} {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-ar} {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-ur} {cite:wikidata-saleh-al-ali}

He reportedly clashed with the Ottomans in 1918, before their withdrawal from Syria, killing two Ottoman soldiers who were harassing a wife of his father — an act that gained him a local reputation as a rebel. The Ottoman state sentenced him to death and he declared his revolt, making the Sheikh Badr area his base. After his father's death he built a shrine for him and reportedly performed miracles at the site, according to local legend. {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-en} {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-ar}

## Rebellion against the French
Al-Ali led the revolt of the coastal mountains against the French from 1919. The balance shifted in France's favour after they conquered Damascus, defeating a makeshift army at the Battle of Maysalun on 24 July 1920. Around this time al-Ali began collaborating, through Ibrahim Hananu's mediation, with Turkish Kemalist forces fighting the French in southern Anatolia; a letter addressed directly to Mustafa Kemal in January 1921 asking for weapons for their common "jihad" against the French is preserved in the Turkish ATASE military archives in Ankara. {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-en}

When he refused the French offer of a separate Alawite state, the French placed him under house arrest and tried to groom his younger brother Mahmoud as a political substitute; the brother refused. Al-Ali remained in hiding until General Gouraud issued a general amnesty in 1922, returned home, and abstained from all political activity until his death on 13 April 1950 at Tartus. {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-en} {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-ar}

## Legacy
In the early 1930s al-Ali returned to political activity against the religious-political proselytising of French and Western missions among Muslims generally and Alawites especially, and his activity culminated in his refusal of a separate Alawite state and his declaration that the mountain was part of Syria. After independence he became a celebrated figure: in his first public appearance since 1922 he was guest of honour of President Shukri al-Quwatli at the Evacuation Day celebrations on 17 April 1946. {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-en} {cite:wikipedia-saleh-al-ali-ar}
