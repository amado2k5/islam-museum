# Nuh Ha Mim Keller

## Summary

Nuh Ha Mim Keller (born 1954) is an American Islamic scholar, teacher and author who lives in Amman, Jordan, and a translator of a number of Islamic books.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en} The Arabic article calls him a shaykh of the Shadhili order in the United States and a senior fellow of the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute for Islamic Thought in Jordan.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-ar}

## Early life and conversion

Keller was born in 1954 to a third-generation German American family in the state of Washington, and studied philosophy and Arabic at the University of Chicago and the University of California, Los Angeles.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en} He converted to Islam from Roman Catholicism in 1977, and has cited the writings of the Islamic philosopher Seyyed Hossein Nasr as one of the reasons for his conversion.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en} He worked as a commercial fisherman in the North Pacific before his prolonged study of the Islamic sciences.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en}

## Study and the Shadhili path

Keller studied the Islamic sciences with prominent scholars in Syria and Jordan and was authorized as a shaykh in 1996.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en} He joined the Shadhili Sufi order, becoming a disciple of the Sufi poet Abd al-Rahman al-Shaghouri of Damascus, from whom he received his authorization, from 1982 until al-Shaghouri's death in 2004; the Arabic article dates his taking of the order from al-Shaghouri to 1988.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en}{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-ar} He lives in Amman, where he established a zawiya in the early 2000s; at its height the resident community was believed to amount to around 60 families, with hundreds of visiting students and families.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en}

## Translations and writings

His English translation of Umdat al-Salik, Reliance of the Traveller, is a Shafi'i manual of sacred law and the first Islamic legal work in a European language to receive the certification of Al-Azhar University.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en} In 2022 he released a translation of the Quran titled The Quran Beheld.{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en} His own works include The Shadhili Tariqa: Notes from the Hashimi-Darqawi Order (1999), Becoming Muslim (2001), Sufism and Islam (2002) and Sea Without Shore: A Manual of the Sufi Path (2011).{cite:wikipedia-nuh-ha-mim-keller-en}
