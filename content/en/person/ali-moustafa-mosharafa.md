## Summary
Egyptian theoretical physicist and mathematician (1898–1950), among the first theoretical physicists of the modern Arab world. He contributed to quantum theory and relativity, wrote papers on radiation and matter praised by Einstein, and was the first Egyptian dean of the Faculty of Science at Cairo University. {cite:wikipedia-ali-moustafa-mosharafa}

## Life and career

Ali Moustafa Mosharafa was born in 1898 in Damietta, Egypt, excelled in his studies and was sent to Britain, taking his bachelor's at Nottingham then a doctorate at King's College London in 1923 — among the youngest ever awarded in his field. {cite:wikipedia-ali-moustafa-mosharafa}

He returned to teach in the Faculty of Science at Cairo University, was promoted professor in the 1930s, and in 1936 became its first Egyptian dean after the post had been held by foreigners. {cite:wikipedia-ali-moustafa-mosharafa}

## Research

He published dozens of papers on quantum theory, relativity, radiation and matter, engaging the ideas of Max Planck and Einstein and correcting or extending certain relativistic and quantum results; Einstein is reported to have praised his research as among the most important in its subject. {cite:wikipedia-ali-moustafa-mosharafa}

He held a vision of science education in Egypt in Arabic, calling for the translation of scientific terms and the founding of an indigenous research tradition, and kept combining teaching with international publication despite scarce means. {cite:wikipedia-ali-moustafa-mosharafa}

## Legacy

He died suddenly in 1950 at 51 and was mourned by Egypt as a great scientist; a theoretical-physics prize at Cairo University and various institutions and schools bear his name. {cite:wikipedia-ali-moustafa-mosharafa}

In Egypt he is remembered as the pioneer of theoretical physics there and as a folk figure — 'Doctor Musharrafa' became synonymous with genius in Egyptian popular culture. {cite:wikipedia-ali-moustafa-mosharafa}
