## Overview
Tariq ibn Ziyad, a commander in al-Andalus. The reports about him vary, and what follows is a summary of what the museum's sources contain.

al-Tabari reports that [[person/musa-ibn-nusayr|Musa ibn Nusayr]] became angry with him, dismissed him, and sent him to Toledo in the year 93 AH {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.6 p.481}.

al-Baladhuri reports that he was an agent of Musa and the first to raid al-Andalus, in the year 92 AH. He conquered it and then conquered Toledo. al-Maqqari reports that Musa sent him across with seven thousand men, most of them Berbers, in four ships, and that he landed at the mountain named after him, Jabal Tariq, in Sha'ban of the year 92 AH {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.267}.

al-Maqqari also reports that when Lodharic advanced on him, Musa sent him reinforcements of five thousand, so that they became twelve thousand {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.278}.

al-Salabi's book records his crossing in Rajab of the year 92 AH and his landing near a mountain known by his name. It treats the sermon attributed to him as a point of stylistic and historical doubt, and the report of the burning of the ships is confused {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.68} {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.70}.

He took part in [[event/conquest-of-al-andalus|the conquest of al-Andalus]] and [[event/conquest-of-tulaytula|the conquest of Toledo]].

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Ibn Abd al-Hakam calls him Tariq ibn Ziyad in one place and Tariq ibn Amr in two. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 275} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 276} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 283} Musa put him over Tangier in the year 92; he had with him a slave-girl called Umm Hakim, whom he left on an island thereafter called the Island of Umm Hakim. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 276} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 278} He took Julian's two daughters as hostages. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 277} Musa later wrote to him not to go beyond Cordoba, and when they met at Cordoba Tariq told him, "I am only your client, and this conquest is yours." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 280} Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports that Musa later imprisoned him and meant to kill him; Tariq sent word to al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, who wrote threatening Musa if he harmed him, and Musa released him. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 283} See [[event/conquest-of-al-andalus|the conquest of al-Andalus]] and [[object/table-of-solomon|the Table of Solomon]].
