## Overview
Abu al-Atahiyya, Isma'il ibn al-Qasim, who was a seller of jars and a poet. Al-Mas'udi relates that he was among the easiest of people in speech and the most able to put words into metre, and that he composed a great deal of love poetry about Utba, a slave-girl of [[person/al-khayzuran|al-Khayzuran]], so [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] flogged him and then gave him fifty thousand dirhams. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.305-307}

In the view of Ahmad Amin he was the leader of religious poetry and the first to it among the clients. {cite:duha-islam p.339} It is said that people disputed which of the two was the better poet, he or [[person/abu-nuwas|Abu Nuwas]], without a definite preference. {cite:duha-islam p.460-461}

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), signed by A. Guillaume, gives Abu 'Ishaq Isma'il b. al-Qasim b. Suwayd b. Kaysan, nicknamed Abu l-'Atahiya ("father of craziness"), as born in Kufa or 'Ayn al-Tamr in 130/748 and dying in 210/825 or 211/826. His family had been clients of the 'Anaza for generations, and his father was a cupper; as a youth the poet himself sold earthenware in the streets. The article ties his bitterness to a sense of social inferiority, and it links his lack of formal study to the freshness of his style. He first rose by a panegyric on al-Mahdi, but his love-poems for 'Utba, a slave-girl, led to a flogging and exile to Kufa, and his praise of al-Hadi annoyed Harun al-Rashid, who imprisoned him with Ibrahim al-Mawsili. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 126}
