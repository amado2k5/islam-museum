## Summary
Hussein Arnous, born in 1953 in the village of al-Tah in the Ma'arrat al-Nu'man district of Idlib, is a Syrian civil engineer and politician who served as prime minister of Syria from 11 June 2020 to 14 September 2024 under former president Bashar al-Assad, after state media announced his appointment shortly after it was reported that Assad had fired his predecessor Imad Khamis amid a worsening economic crisis. {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-ar}

## Early life and education
Arnous was born in the village of al-Tah in the Ma'arrat al-Nu'man district, Idlib; both articles and Wikidata record his birth at year precision only (1953). In 1978 he earned a degree in civil engineering from the University of Aleppo. {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-ar}

## Career
After graduating, Arnous worked with the Idlib Engineering Syndicate and headed its branch from 1989 to 1994. From 1992 to 2002 he managed the General Company for Roads and Bridges, then served as assistant minister of transport from 2002 to 2004 and as executive director of the General Establishment for Road Transport until 2009. He then served as governor of Deir ez-Zor (2009–2011) and of Quneitra (2011–2013). {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-ar}

He was appointed minister of public works on 9 February 2013, continued in the post after the ministry was renamed public works and housing, and held it until 26 November 2018. He then served as minister of water resources from 26 November 2018 to 30 August 2020. {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-ar}

### Premiership
State media announced his appointment as prime minister on 11 June 2020, shortly after it was reported that President Bashar al-Assad had fired Imad Khamis amid a worsening economic crisis. He was tasked with forming a government on 25 August 2020, confirmed by Assad on 30 August and sworn in three days later; he formed a new government in August 2021 after the May 2021 presidential election, and remained in office until 14 September 2024. {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-ar}

**The 2014 entry ban:** in 2014, as a serving minister, Arnous was included on a list of Syrian government ministers banned from entering the United States or the European Union. {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-ar}

**The circumstances of his appointment:** his appointment as prime minister followed, according to the English article's account of state media reporting, the dismissal of his predecessor amid a deteriorating economic crisis — the context that marked his four years heading the government until September 2024. {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-en}

## Impact and audience
Arnous represents the technocratic-administrative pattern of the late Syrian governments: an engineer who rose through the engineers' syndicate, the roads companies, two governorships and the service ministries to head the government for four years at the height of the economic crisis, with little media profile in the cited sources. {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-en} {cite:wikipedia-hussein-arnous-ar}
