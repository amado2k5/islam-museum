## Overview
Al-Kahina, queen of the Berbers. Al-Baladhuri says that Hassan ibn al-Nu'man al-Ghassani, governor of Ifriqiya, attacked her and she defeated him, then he attacked her a second time and killed her. Ibn Khaldun names her Dahya, Matiya daughter of Tifan, queen of the Aurès mountains. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1607-1608}

## In al-Baladhuri
Al-Kahina defeated Hassan, and he fled to palaces in the region of Barqa, called the Palaces of Hassan. Then he attacked her a second time and took captives from among the Berbers. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} See [[event/hassan-vs-kahina|Hassan and al-Kahina]] and [[group/berbers|the Berbers]].

## In Ibn Khaldun
He says that her people were of the Jarawa, and that Hassan advanced on her, and they fought at the Wadi Maskiyana, where the Muslims were routed and she captured Khalid ibn Yazid al-Qaysi. She remained in the rule of Ifriqiya for five years, and she ruined cities and estates. Then the Berbers gave their allegiance to Hassan, and she was defeated and killed in the Aurès mountains. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1607-1608}

## In al-Salabi
He says that Hassan met her at a river called Nini or Maskiyana, where she was victorious. Then he killed her after Khalid ibn Yazid al-Absi showed him where the gatherings were, and her killing was at a well named after her near Gabes. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.59-60}

## Notes for researchers
- The accounts differ on the place of her killing (the Aurès mountains or near Gabes) and on the lineage of Khalid (al-Qaysi or al-Absi).
- Ibn Khaldun's account in the text gives the years 79 AH and 74 AH for Hassan; the order needs to be checked.

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Ibn Abd al-Hakam calls her queen of the Berbers, who had taken most of Ifriqiya. She defeated Hassan ibn al-Nu'man and captured eighty of his men, treated them well and let them go, all except a man of Banu Abs called Khalid ibn Yazid, whom she adopted. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 270} Khalid wrote secretly to Hassan, once hiding the letter in a loaf and once in a hollowed saddle-bow, and each time al-Kahina came out crying, "My sons, your ruin is in something people eat," and then, "your ruin is in a dead plant of the earth." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 270} When Hassan marched against her she came out with her hair loose, saw a red cloud and said it was the dust of the Arab horse; she commended her two sons to Khalid and sent him to obtain safe-conduct for them. She was killed at the foot of a mountain, at the place named Bi'r al-Kahina; the manuscript leaves the date of her death blank. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 271}
