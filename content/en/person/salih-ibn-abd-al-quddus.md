## Overview
Salih ibn Abd al-Quddus of Basra was brought before [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] on a charge of heresy (zandaqa). He professed repentance, and al-Mahdi released him. Then al-Mahdi took him back and reminded him of his saying that an old man does not give up his habits. He replied that al-Mahdi too would not give up his habits until he died, and al-Mahdi ordered him killed. al-Suyuti records this in Tarikh al-Khulafa' {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.444}. See [[event/execution-of-salih-ibn-abd-al-quddus|the execution of Salih ibn Abd al-Quddus]].

Ibn al-Nadim lists him among the theologians who professed Islam while concealing heresy {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.473}.

The appendix to the Fihrist reports that when a son of his died he grieved deeply, and Abu al-Hudhayl al-Allaf, the head of the Mu'tazila, came to him as one who shared his grief. His book "al-Shukuk" is described as such that whoever reads it begins to doubt what was certain, and to doubt what never was, until he imagines it was {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.525-526}.

These descriptions are transmitted by the historians. The museum attributes them to their sources and does not judge them.
