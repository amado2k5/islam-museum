## Summary

Mustafa Mahmoud (1921–2009) was an Egyptian physician and writer who made television and the book together a pulpit for joining science to faith; his programme Science and Faith was among the longest-running on Arab television.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-mahmoud}

## Life

Born in Shibin al-Kom, Monufia, he graduated in medicine and practised for years before devoting himself to writing. He passed through a youthful philosophical doubt he described in My Journey from Doubt to Faith, then returned to writing in defence of faith.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-mahmoud}

## Books and programme

He wrote some 89 books, from early fiction such as The Impossible to philosophical essays like God and Man, Dialogue with an Atheist Friend and The Quran: An Attempt at a Contemporary Understanding. His programme Science and Faith (1972–2000) used scientific phenomena as an entry to speaking of creation.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-mahmoud}

## Standing and debate

A wide public loves him as a populariser of science and faith; scholars criticised some of his Quranic readings and personal theories (such as his grammatical theory of the letter ghayn) as unsourced — the museum records both. He died in 2009; a large mosque in Mohandessin bears his name.{cite:wikipedia-mustafa-mahmoud}
