# Gabdulla Tukay

Ğabdulla Möxəmmətğərif ulı Tuqay (26 April 1886 – 15 April 1913) was a Tatar poet, critic and publisher, regarded as the founder of modern Tatar literature and of the modern Tatar literary language. He was born at Qushlawich in Kazan Governorate, the son of the village's hereditary mullah, who died when the boy was five months old; his mother died in 1890, and the orphan was passed between his grandfather, a Kazan tanner's family and a peasant household at Kzyl Utar, where he first attended a madrasa.{cite:wikipedia-gabdulla-tukay-en}{cite:wikipedia-gabdulla-tukay-ru}

From 1895 he lived at Uralsk, studying at the Motıyğiya madrasa and a Russian school, and from 1904 he worked in Kamil Motıyği's publishing house and the Tatar periodicals Fiker, Əlğəsrəlcadid and Uqlar, becoming a feared satirist whose main target was the conservative clergy. His poem "Kitmibez!" ("We Don't Leave!") answered the Black Hundreds' call for the Tatars to emigrate to Turkey, and his fairy-tale poem Şürəle drew on Pushkin.{cite:wikipedia-gabdulla-tukay-en}

In Kazan from the autumn of 1907 he wrote for Əl-İslax, Yäşen and Yalt-Yolt and published book after book of verse, including poems for children. Journeys in 1911–1912 took him to Astrakhan, Ufa, Saint Petersburg and the Kazakh steppe, where he drank kumiss for his health; the diagnosis kept from him was the last stage of tuberculosis. Hospitalised on 26 February 1913, he went on writing in the Klyachkin hospital and died there on 15 April 1913, aged twenty-six; he was buried in Kazan's Tatar cemetery. His "Tuğan tel" ("Oh, My Mother Tongue!") is the unofficial hymn of the Tatar language, and his birthday, 26 April, is kept as the Day of the Tatar Language.{cite:wikipedia-gabdulla-tukay-en}

The English and Russian articles, both following his standard biography, agree on his life; Wikidata carries his dates in the Julian form. Wikidata gives his religion as Islam in general, but no text names a school for him: his father and grandfathers' mullah posts are family background, and the biography's report that the sixteen-year-old lost interest in Qur'an study is conduct, not a statement of unbelief, so his faith is recorded as unknown.
