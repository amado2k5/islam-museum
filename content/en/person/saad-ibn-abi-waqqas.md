## Brief
Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas was one of the first Muslims, the archer of Badr, and the commander who broke the Sasanian army at al-Qadisiyya. The city of Kufa was among his foundations.

## Life
A young Qurashi of [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]], the clan of the Prophet's own mother, Sa'd embraced Islam at the hands of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] while still under twenty, among the first ten believers {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He witnessed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], which tradition calls the first arrow shot in the cause of Islam, and the battles of Medina after it {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

In 636 CE, as commander on behalf of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], he met the Sasanian army at [[event/battle-of-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]] in Iraq and defeated it in a battle lasting several days, after which all the lands of Persia opened to the Muslims {cite:tabari-donner-1993}. On Umar's command he founded the garrison city of Kufa and governed Iraq. Then he withdrew to his estate at Aqiq, near Medina, and died around 674 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Kufa, the seat of Ali's rule, the cradle of Arabic grammar and early jurisprudence, and the base of the east, began as Sa'd's garrison camp. The saying attributed to the Prophet, "O God, make Sa'd's arrow find its mark," made him the tradition's model archer.

## For young visitors
Sa'd became a Muslim as a teenager, shot what tradition calls the "first arrow," and grew up to defeat one of the two great empires of his world.

## In the secret call (*The Sealed Nectar*, p. 76)
The book counts him among those who embraced Islam through the call of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] during the years of [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|the secret call]]. He was one of the eight who preceded others, forming the vanguard and foremost of Islam {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. The book mentions him with his Zuhri lineage {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy mentions Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas in several places in the series. He tells that Sa'd made the people swear by God to reveal who had opened the breach into al-Madain, and that the man disappeared after opening it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:20:00}.

He cites Umar ibn al-Khattab's letter to Sa'd at al-Qadisiyya: the Muslims are helped by their obedience to God and the enemy by his disobedience, and the sins of an army are more to be feared than its enemy {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:25:00}. Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds, as his own opinion, that this letter shows religion covers everything and is not separate from politics {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:26:00}.

He says Sa'd was known among the Arabs for his bravery, that he was the fifth to accept Islam, and that he came to Islam through Abu Bakr {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:40:30} (needs_source).

He names Sa'd's command at al-Qadisiyya among the conquests {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:30:30}, describes Sa'd praying before al-Qadisiyya {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:08:00}, and returns to Umar's words to Sa'd that victory comes through obedience to God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 01:13:00}.

He recounts that the Prophet said "my father and mother for you" to Sa'd, and that this phrase was said to no one but Sa'd and one other Companion {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:52:00}. He says Umar named six men, Sa'd among them, from the ten promised Paradise, to choose a successor from among themselves {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:49:30}, and that Sa'd withdrew from the caliphate together with Talha and al-Zubayr {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:51:00}.

He recounts that in Iraq Sa'd prayed that God show a sign against a man who was spreading slander about him, and a she-camel dragged that man until he died {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:40:30}. He also recounts that Sa'd prayed against a neighbour woman who spread false accusations; she fell into a well and died {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:41:30} (needs_source). Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds, as his own opinion, that the prayer of the wronged is not turned away {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:41:30}.

He imagines, as his own opinion, that Sa'd, if alive today, would set up factories producing arms for the Muslims {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:17:00}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says that the Companions divided in the strife between Ali and Mu'awiya and that Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas was among those who kept out of it: he left Medina, built himself a house at al-Aqiq, the blessed valley by Dhu al-Hulayfa, and withdrew from people. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:11:30} He relates a parable Sa'd gave for this: travellers on a road were overtaken by thick fog; some went right, some went left, and others stayed where they were until the fog lifted, and found themselves on the original road. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:12:00} Al-Heweny explains that those who went right and left exercised judgement and may have erred, while those who stayed were certainly right, since all had agreed up to that point. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:13:00}

In another episode he says Sa'd was proverbial for devotion to his mother, and that the verse on parents who strive to make one associate partners with God, yet are to be kept company kindly, was revealed because of him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:09:00} When he embraced Islam his mother swore not to eat or drink until she died, so that he would be shamed as his mother's killer; al-Heweny compares this to a hunger strike. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:09:30} He relates from Sa'd that when they wanted to feed her they prised her mouth open with a stick, and that Sa'd told her that if she had a hundred souls leaving her one by one he would not leave his religion. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:10:30} Al-Heweny reads the verse as teaching firmness in religion together with gentle speech, and sees in Sa'd's words to his mother a harshness that the verse came to refine; this is his reading. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:11:00}
