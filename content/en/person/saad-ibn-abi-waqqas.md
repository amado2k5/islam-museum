## Brief
Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas was one of the first Muslims, an archer at Badr, and the general who broke the Sasanian Empire's army at al-Qadisiyya. The garrison city of Kufa was his foundation.

## Life
A young Qurayshi of [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]] — the Prophet's mother's own clan — Sa'd embraced Islam through [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] when barely in his teens, among the first dozen converts {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] — tradition calls him the first to loose an arrow in the defense of Islam — and through the Medinan battles {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

In 636 CE, commanding for [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], he met the Sasanian army at [[event/battle-of-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]] in Iraq and, in a multi-day battle, destroyed it — opening the whole Sasanian heartland {cite:tabari-donner-1993}. On Umar's orders he laid out the garrison city of Kufa and governed Iraq before retiring to his estate near Medina, where he died about 674 {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Kufa — seat of Ali's caliphate, crucible of Arabic grammar and early law, launching point of the east — began as Sa'd's camp. And the saying recorded of the Prophet praying for "Sa'd's shooting" made him the tradition's emblematic archer.

## For young visitors
Sa'd joined when he was a teenager, shot the tradition's "first arrow," and grew up to defeat one of the two superpowers of his world.
