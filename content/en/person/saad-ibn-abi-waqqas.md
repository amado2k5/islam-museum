## Brief
Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas was one of the first Muslims, the archer of Badr, and the commander who broke the Sasanian army at al-Qadisiyya. The city of Kufa was among his foundations.

## Life
A young Qurashi of [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]], the clan of the Prophet's own mother, Sa'd embraced Islam at the hands of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] while still under twenty, among the first ten believers {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He witnessed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], which tradition calls the first arrow shot in the cause of Islam, and the battles of Medina after it {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

In 636 CE, as commander on behalf of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], he met the Sasanian army at [[event/battle-of-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]] in Iraq and defeated it in a battle lasting several days, after which all the lands of Persia opened to the Muslims {cite:tabari-donner-1993}. On Umar's command he founded the garrison city of Kufa and governed Iraq. Then he withdrew to his estate at Aqiq, near Medina, and died around 674 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Kufa, the seat of Ali's rule, the cradle of Arabic grammar and early jurisprudence, and the base of the east, began as Sa'd's garrison camp. The saying attributed to the Prophet, "O God, make Sa'd's arrow find its mark," made him the tradition's model archer.

## For young visitors
Sa'd became a Muslim as a teenager, shot what tradition calls the "first arrow," and grew up to defeat one of the two great empires of his world.

## In the secret call (*The Sealed Nectar*, p. 76)
The book counts him among those who embraced Islam through the call of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] during the years of [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|the secret call]]. He was one of the eight who preceded others, forming the vanguard and foremost of Islam {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. The book mentions him with his Zuhri lineage {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy mentions Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas in several places in the series. He tells that Sa'd made the people swear by God to reveal who had opened the breach into al-Madain, and that the man disappeared after opening it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:20:00}.

He cites Umar ibn al-Khattab's letter to Sa'd at al-Qadisiyya: the Muslims are helped by their obedience to God and the enemy by his disobedience, and the sins of an army are more to be feared than its enemy {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:25:00}. Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds, as his own opinion, that this letter shows religion covers everything and is not separate from politics {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:26:00}.

He says Sa'd was known among the Arabs for his bravery, that he was the fifth to accept Islam, and that he came to Islam through Abu Bakr {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:40:30} (needs_source).

He names Sa'd's command at al-Qadisiyya among the conquests {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:30:30}, describes Sa'd praying before al-Qadisiyya {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:08:00}, and returns to Umar's words to Sa'd that victory comes through obedience to God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 01:13:00}.

He recounts that the Prophet said "my father and mother for you" to Sa'd, and that this phrase was said to no one but Sa'd and one other Companion {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:52:00}. He says Umar named six men, Sa'd among them, from the ten promised Paradise, to choose a successor from among themselves {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:49:30}, and that Sa'd withdrew from the caliphate together with Talha and al-Zubayr {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:51:00}.

He recounts that in Iraq Sa'd prayed that God show a sign against a man who was spreading slander about him, and a she-camel dragged that man until he died {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:40:30}. He also recounts that Sa'd prayed against a neighbour woman who spread false accusations; she fell into a well and died {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:41:30} (needs_source). Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds, as his own opinion, that the prayer of the wronged is not turned away {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:41:30}.

He imagines, as his own opinion, that Sa'd, if alive today, would set up factories producing arms for the Muslims {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:17:00}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says that the Companions divided in the strife between Ali and Mu'awiya and that Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas was among those who kept out of it: he left Medina, built himself a house at al-Aqiq, the blessed valley by Dhu al-Hulayfa, and withdrew from people. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:11:30} He relates a parable Sa'd gave for this: travellers on a road were overtaken by thick fog; some went right, some went left, and others stayed where they were until the fog lifted, and found themselves on the original road. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:12:00} Al-Heweny explains that those who went right and left exercised judgement and may have erred, while those who stayed were certainly right, since all had agreed up to that point. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep02 00:13:00}

In another episode he says Sa'd was proverbial for devotion to his mother, and that the verse on parents who strive to make one associate partners with God, yet are to be kept company kindly, was revealed because of him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:09:00} When he embraced Islam his mother swore not to eat or drink until she died, so that he would be shamed as his mother's killer; al-Heweny compares this to a hunger strike. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:09:30} He relates from Sa'd that when they wanted to feed her they prised her mouth open with a stick, and that Sa'd told her that if she had a hundred souls leaving her one by one he would not leave his religion. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:10:30} Al-Heweny reads the verse as teaching firmness in religion together with gentle speech, and sees in Sa'd's words to his mother a harshness that the verse came to refine; this is his reading. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep09 00:11:00}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In episode 24 of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] says that Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas accepted Islam when the Muslims could be counted on the fingers of one hand, that he was the only Companion for whom the Prophet offered both his parents as ransom, that his prayers were answered, and that he shed the first blood and shot the first arrow in God's cause {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:01:00}; that he was the first to shoot an arrow in the cause of Islam is also stated in this record from Ibn Sa'd. {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley} He identifies him as Abu Ishaq Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas Malik ibn Uhayb and calls him the hero of al-Qadisiyya who put out the Magians' fire. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:01:30} He says Sa'd grew up in Mecca in a noble and wealthy house, kept away from idle pastimes, and gave himself to making arrows, mending bows and horsemanship until he was the best archer in Mecca. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:01:30}

He cites from [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] Sa'd's saying that he spent seven days as a third of Islam, which he takes to mean that he was the third Muslim. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:02:30} He narrates that when his mother learned of his conversion she swore neither to eat nor drink until she died, so that he would be shamed as her killer; she held out for a day and a night until she was exhausted, and he told her that if she had a hundred souls and they left her one by one he would not leave his religion; so she ate, and the verse was revealed about the two of them that forbids obeying parents in associating partners with God while commanding kind companionship with them. He attributes this to [[work/sahih-muslim|Sahih Muslim]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:03:00} He relates from [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir]], in a hadith he attributes to [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]] and calls sound by the criteria of the two shaykhs, that the Prophet pointed to Sa'd, said he was his maternal uncle, and challenged anyone to show him an uncle like him; he explains that the Prophet's mother was of [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]], and Sa'd was of them too {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:04:30}, as this record also states from Ibn Sa'd. {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}

He cites the hadith of [[concept/ashara-mubashshara|the Ten promised Paradise]] from [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Sa'id ibn Zayd]], attributing it to [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] with [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|al-Albani]]'s grading of sound in Sahih al-Jami', and lists them: [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]], [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]], [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda]], [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha]], [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], Sa'd and Sa'id ibn Zayd. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:05:00} He relates from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], in a hadith he attributes to al-Hakim's Ma'rifat al-Sahaba and to [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]]'s grading of sound, that the Prophet told his Companions a man of the people of Paradise was about to come in to them, and Sa'd came in. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:05:30} He relates from al-Bukhari and Muslim that the Prophet would hand him arrows, offer his parents as his ransom and tell him to shoot, and in one version prayed that his aim be true and his prayer answered, and no arrow of his ever missed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:06:00} He relates from Muslim that at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] a polytheist was cutting deep into the Muslims; the Prophet handed Sa'd an arrow with no head, Sa'd shot it and struck the man's forehead, the man fell and was exposed, and the Prophet laughed until his back teeth showed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:07:00} He relates from Muslim, in Sa'd's own report, that at Uhud he saw two men in white fighting for the Prophet whom he had never seen before and never saw again, and cites [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] that they were [[person/jibril|Gabriel]] and [[person/mikail|Michael]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:07:30} He cites the Prophet's prayer that God answer Sa'd whenever he called on Him, attributing it to [[person/al-tirmidhi-muhammad-ibn-isa|al-Tirmidhi]] and al-Hakim, and says al-Hakim graded it sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:08:30}

He narrates that Umar appointed Sa'd over [[place/kufa|Kufa]], whose people he describes as given to trouble, and that Umar would send [[person/muhammad-ibn-maslama|Muhammad ibn Maslama]] to take a governor round the mosques and ask about him; the people praised Sa'd in every mosque except the mosque of Banu Abs. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:09:30} There a man named Usama ibn Qatada, called Abu Sa'da, stood up and accused Sa'd of not judging justly, not dividing fairly and not going out with the raiding parties; the speaker answers that Sa'd was the third Muslim and the conqueror of Iraq, southern Turkey, most of Iran, [[place/armenia|Armenia]] and [[place/azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:10:30} In his view Umar removed Sa'd not for weakness or betrayal but because he preferred to keep him for the people of Medina; Sa'd had been wronged, and he recalls that there is no veil between the prayer of the wronged and God. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:11:30} He narrates that Sa'd prayed four things against the man for his four charges: that God blind him, lengthen his life, make his poverty lasting and expose him to temptations; the man went blind, grew poor and begged, and lived so long that his eyebrows fell over his eyes, and past eighty he would accost slave girls and say that Sa'd's prayer had struck him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:12:00} He says that Umar, as he was dying, placed Sa'd among the six of the Shura and said that if Sa'd did not become caliph, whoever took charge should seek his help; the speaker reads this as Umar pointing to him. Uthman was chosen, sought his help and appointed him over Kufa a second time. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:15:00}

He says Sa'd fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], Uhud, the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Trench]], the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest of Mecca]], [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]] and every campaign with the Prophet. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:16:00} He narrates that when Umar heard the Persians were preparing to attack Medina he swore to hurl the kings of the Arabs against the kings of the Persians, gathered the horsemen and meant to lead the army himself; advised to stay, he chose Sa'd, who was then over the alms of [[group/hawazin|Hawazin]], and described him as a lion, and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf added to the description; and Umar ordered him to write to him every day. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:16:30} He says that at [[event/battle-of-al-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]] the Muslims numbered, by his count, thirty-six thousand, and the Persians two hundred and forty thousand with sixteen elephants. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:17:30} He says the battle lasted four days and forty thousand Persians were killed, among them their commander [[person/rustam-farrukhzad|Rustam]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:18:30} He narrates that before the battle Sa'd was struck by sciatica and many boils and could not put on his armour, so he made another man commander in the field and directed the battle from the roof of a tall house, sending down his orders; when the army murmured about it, he came down and showed them his body, and they excused him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:18:30} He says the Companions killed all the elephants and those riding them, and he counts al-Qadisiyya and [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|al-Yarmuk]] the greatest battles of the age of the Rightly Guided Caliphs, the first led by Sa'd and the second by [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:19:30} Sa'd then took [[place/al-madain|al-Mada'in]], all of [[place/iraq|Iraq]], southern Turkey, most of Iran, Armenia and Azerbaijan. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:20:00}

He relates from Abu Hamid [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]], in the fourth volume of the [[work/ihya-ulum-al-din|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]], that Sa'd came to Mecca after he had gone blind, and people asked him to pray for them; [[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-saib|Abd Allah ibn al-Sa'ib]], the Qur'an reciter of Mecca, asked why he did not pray for his own sight to be restored, and Sa'd said God's decree was dearer to him than his sight. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:21:00} He says Sa'd was past seventy when he died, and relates from his son [[person/musab-ibn-sad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Mus'ab]] that he wept with his father's head in his lap, and Sa'd told him not to weep, for God would not punish him and he was of the people of Paradise. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:22:00} He asked for an old woollen cloak he had worn at Badr and had kept to be shrouded in. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:22:30} He died at al-Aqiq and was carried to [[place/medina|Medina]], where [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]], its governor, prayed over him, as did the Prophet's wives and thousands of Companions and Followers, and he was buried in [[place/baqi-al-gharqad|al-Baqi']]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep24 00:23:00} He also discusses Sa'd's position in the strife after Uthman's killing and the account of his prayer against a man who reviled Ali, which is not written here.
