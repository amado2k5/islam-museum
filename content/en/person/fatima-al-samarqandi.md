## Summary

Fatima bint Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Ahmad al-Samarqandi was a Hanafi jurist, daughter of [[person/ala-al-din-al-samarqandi|Ala' al-Din al-Samarqandi]], author of [[work/tuhfat-al-fuqaha|Tuhfat al-Fuqaha']], and wife of [[person/ala-al-din-al-kasani|Ala' al-Din al-Kasani]], author of [[work/badai-al-sanai|al-Bada'i']]. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75} The reports about her here come from the introduction by the editors of the Bada'i' edition, who mostly quote Ibn al-Adim. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75}

## Her learning

She studied law with her father and memorised his book al-Tuhfa. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75} Ibn al-Adim relates from his father that she transmitted the school's doctrine well; that when her husband al-Kasani erred in a fatwa she would correct him and show him the mistake; and that she gave fatwas, and her husband respected and honoured her; fatwas at first went out in her hand and her father's, and after her marriage to al-Kasani in the hand of all three. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75}

## Her marriage

The editors relate that she was very beautiful, that rulers of Rum sought her hand and her father refused, and that he then married her to his student al-Kasani when al-Kasani wrote al-Bada'i' as a commentary on al-Tuhfa, making the book her dower. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74}

## In Aleppo

Ibn al-Adim relates from Dawud ibn Ali, one of the jurists of the Halawiyya in Aleppo, that it was she who began the custom of breaking the Ramadan fast for the jurists at the [[building/al-halawiyah-madrasa|Halawiyya]] madrasa: she took off two bracelets, sold them and with the price provided the meal every night, and the custom continued to his own day. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76} When Nur al-Din Mahmud wanted to speak to her through a eunuch about her husband staying in Aleppo, she veiled herself from him and wrote to her husband that it was not lawful for the eunuch to look at her, until a woman was sent to her. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76} She lived in Aleppo until her death; her husband was later buried beside her inside the shrine of Abraham the Friend of God outside Aleppo, and he visited her grave every Friday night. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76}
