## Summary
Ali ibn Nasr, Abu al-Hasan, titled Muhadhdhib al-Dawla, lord of al-Bataʾih, who was faithful, generous and high-minded. The fearful sought his protection, and kings took refuge with him in times of calamity. He died in the year 409 AH. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.290-291}

## Sheltering al-Qadir Billah
The greatest of Muhadhdhib al-Dawla's achievements was the arrival of the caliph [[person/al-qadir-billah|al-Qadir Billah]] at al-Bataʾih, where he took refuge for nearly two years when he fled the wrath of al-Taʾiʿ. Muhadhdhib al-Dawla served him and cared for him in the best manner until al-Qadir was summoned to the caliphate. The vizier Abu Shuja said: "The days crowned his splendour with the sojourn of al-Qadir Billah in his protection, and fashioned for him a merit as a lineage, and made it a cause of his deserving praise." {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.290}

## Wealth and building
His vast iqtaʿ estates in al-Bataʾih yielded him thousands of kurr of wheat, barley and rice, and hundreds of thousands of dinars. He became related by marriage to King [[person/baha-al-dawla|Baha al-Dawla]] through his daughter, and lent him money in his hardships. He built the [[building/jami-al-qatia|Qatiʿa Mosque]] in Baghdad and water fountains in Wasit for travellers. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.149,290-291}
