## Summary
Indian Deobandi hadith scholar (1875–1933), Shaykh al-Hadith at Darul Uloom Deoband, among the greatest teachers of the two Sahihs in the subcontinent. Known for prodigious memory and authentic scholarship; his published lectures include Fayd al-Bari on Sahih al-Bukhari, Al-'Arf al-Shadhi and Mushkilat al-Qur'an. {cite:wikipedia-anwar-shah-kashmiri}

## Life and formation

Muhammad Anwar Shah Kashmiri was born in 1875 in Kashmir into a learned family; as a boy he moved to Deoband and studied under its great shaykhs, then travelled to the Hijaz to read under the scholars of Medina and the Haram. {cite:wikipedia-anwar-shah-kashmiri}

He was formed especially by Mahmud Hasan Deobandi (Shaykh al-Hind) and Khalil Ahmad Saharanpuri until he stood among the luminaries of the Deobandi school in hadith and usul, famed for a prodigious hadith memory. {cite:wikipedia-anwar-shah-kashmiri}

## Teaching and works

He taught at Mazahir Uloom in Saharanpur then at Darul Uloom Deoband, where he held the chair of hadith; his lectures on Bukhari, Tirmidhi and Hanafi fiqh drew the best students from India and beyond. {cite:wikipedia-anwar-shah-kashmiri}

His lectures were compiled into books after his death, among them Fayd al-Bari ala Sahih al-Bukhari in four volumes, Al-'Arf al-Shadhi on Sunan al-Tirmidhi, Mushkilat al-Qur'an, a treatise on al-hadith al-mu'dal, and treatises on usul and creed. {cite:wikipedia-anwar-shah-kashmiri}

## Legacy

Deobandi biographical literature styles him with epithets like 'encyclopedia of the age'; he trained students who became hadith masters of the subcontinent's schools, among them Muhammad Zakariyya Kandhlawi and others. {cite:wikipedia-anwar-shah-kashmiri}

He died in Deoband in 1933; the volumes of Fayd al-Bari and Al-'Arf al-Shadhi remain pillars of the modern hadith-commentary library in India, Pakistan and beyond. {cite:wikipedia-anwar-shah-kashmiri}
