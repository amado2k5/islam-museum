## Summary
Abu Jahl, "Father of Ignorance," is the Muslims' name for Amr ibn Hisham, whom his own people called "Abu al-Hakam" ("Father of Wisdom"). He was the driving force of Mecca's opposition to Muhammad, and the story of that opposition is, to a large degree, his story.

## Life
A chief of [[group/banu-makhzum|Banu Makhzum]], Abu Jahl appears in the sources wherever pressure on the Muslims was organized: the boycott document against [[group/banu-hashim|Banu Hashim]] hung in the Kaaba, the council at [[event/plot-of-dar-al-nadwa|Dar al-Nadwa]] that planned the Prophet's assassination, and the documented torment of the weak. The tradition attributes to him the killing of Sumayya bint Khayyat {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He led Mecca's army to relieve the caravan at Badr, and his voice prevailed over those calling for a return. He was killed in the defeat, in March 624 CE {cite:watt-1953-mecca}.

## Legacy
His death at Badr is a hinge of the sira: the opposition lost its driving force in a single day. The museum presents him as the sources do, as the most consequential of the Prophet's opponents and the first great "what if" of the story.

## For young visitors
Abu Jahl fought harder than anyone to stop the new religion, and he died in its first great battle. His sons later embraced Islam.

## Slapping Asma bint Abi Bakr (The Sealed Nectar, p.165)
When the Quraysh went to the house of Abu Bakr and asked Asma about her father, and she said she did not know, Abu Jahl struck her a slap that knocked off her earring. This was at the time of the [[event/hijra-night-escape|Prophet's departure from his house and his hiding in the cave]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.165} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/487}.

## Taunting Sa'd ibn Mu'adh (The Sealed Nectar, p.195)
He met Sa'd ibn Mu'adh circling the Kaaba in Mecca and said to him that he was circling in safety while they had sheltered those who had turned away from the old religion. Sa'd replied that his road passes by the people of Medina ([[event/quraysh-letter-to-ibn-ubayy|Quraysh's threat]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.195}.

## Leading Mecca's army to Badr (The Sealed Nectar, pp.206–208)
He was the commander of Mecca's army. He disobeyed al-Akhnas ibn Shariq when al-Akhnas advised turning back, and said that he would not return until he reached Badr, slaughtered camels, served wine, and let the Arabs hear of their gathering. He also did not let the Banu Hashim turn back ([[event/badr-expedition-setup|Preparations before Badr]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.206} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.207} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.208}.

## His death at Badr (The Sealed Nectar, pp.220–221)
Two young men of the Ansar killed him, and then Ibn Mas'ud finished him off. The Prophet called him the Pharaoh of this community ([[event/death-of-abu-jahl|The death of Abu Jahl]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.221}.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.) entry gives his full name as Abu l-Hakam Hisham b. al-Mughira of the Banu Makhzum of Quraysh, also called Ibn al-Hanzaliyya after his mother, Asma' bint Mukharriba. He was born about 570, and his mother became a Muslim and lived beyond 13/635. Shortly before the Hijra he succeeded al-Walid b. al-Mughira as leader of the Makhzum, and he was less willing than al-Walid to compromise with Muhammad. The article says he was largely responsible for the boycott of Hashim and al-Muttalib, and that he persuaded Abu Lahab to withdraw protection from the Prophet. Many persecutions of Muslims are attributed to him, though the article doubts that all of them happened. He led the relief force for the Syrian caravan in 2/624 and was killed at Badr. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 134}

## For Sprouts
Abu Jahl — his real name was Amr ibn Hisham — was a Meccan chief and the harshest opponent of the Prophet in Mecca. He harmed the first Muslims and led Mecca's war against them until he fell at Badr in 624 CE. 'Abu Jahl' is the name the Muslims gave him, in place of 'Abu al-Hakam', which his own people used.

## For Explorers
Amr ibn Hisham ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumi — known in Muslim sources as Abu Jahl though his people called him Abu al-Hakam — was a chief of Banu Makhzum and the strongest face of opposition to Islam in Mecca. The sira shows him leading the campaign of harm against the Muslims: mocking the Prophet, pressuring the weak clients of the clans, and the insult that led to Hamza's conversion. Historians credit him with pushing Quraysh to march out to protect Abu Sufyan's caravan and then to fight at Badr, where he fell in 2 AH (624 CE) at the hands of Mu'adh ibn Amr and Mu'awwidh ibn Afra — with a further account of Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud striking his head. Historians read his life as the fiercest side of Meccan opposition to the early call.

## For Scholars
Abu Jahl — Amr ibn Hisham ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumi — was a Qurashi chief and the most prominent face of opposition to the Prophet's call; the Muslims renamed him Abu Jahl ('father of ignorance') from his clan's title Abu al-Hakam ('father of wisdom'). In the sira he leads boycott and harm: Ibn Hisham records his mockery of the Prophet, his retort to Abu Talib, his pressure on the weak Muslim clients, and the famous account that he killed Sumayya, the first martyr of Islam in the received tradition. Ibn Hisham also records him deciding for war when the caravan was nearly safe: 'we will not return until we reach Badr' — and when the armies met in 2 AH (624 CE) he was killed; the reports name Mu'adh ibn Amr and Mu'awwidh ibn Afra as striking him down and Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud finishing and taking his head to the Prophet. Sunni and sira literature treat him as the model of obstinate opposition, while historians place him inside the rivalry of Banu Makhzum and Banu Hashim in Meccan politics.
