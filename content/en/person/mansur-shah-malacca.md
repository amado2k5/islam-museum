## Summary
Sultan Mansur Shah (d. 1477) was the sixth sultan of Malacca, succeeding his father Muzaffar Shah in 1459 and ruling until 1477, the reign in which the sultanate reached its greatest territorial extent. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikidata-mansur-shah-malacca} His birth name was Raja Abdullah, and his posthumous name Marhum Bukit. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-ms} The English article's infobox prints his reign as 1456–1477 against its own lead's 1459–1477, and both frames are preserved. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} No source consulted gives a birth year, and the museum's queue figure of 1430 is attested nowhere. {cite:wikidata-mansur-shah-malacca}

## Expansion and marriage alliances
Mansur Shah pursued expansion across the Malay Peninsula and eastern Sumatra: Perak, Bernam, Manjung, Rupat, Singapore and Bintan came under Malacca's control, Siak became a vassal, and Pahang, Kampar and Inderagiri were pressed, with his sons set over conquered states as they grew. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-ms} Marriage alliances bound the conquests together — his daughter Princess Mahadewi married the king of Siak, Siantan and Indragiri came as dowry with his Majapahit marriage, and he married princesses of conquered states including Puteri Wanang Sri of Pahang and Raden Galoh Cendera Kirana; such alliances were a factor in Islam's expansion in maritime Southeast Asia. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} His most famous wife, the Chinese princess Hang Li Po, is doubted by the article itself: no Ming source records a princess of that name, and the reigning Ming emperor of Mansur's day was Yingzong, not Yongle. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en}

## Palace and economy
The Malay Annals' description of Mansur Shah's palace is among the most detailed accounts of a building in Malay historical sources; the palace itself was razed after the Portuguese capture of Malacca in 1511, and a replica, funded under Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad, opened on 17 July 1984. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} His commercial policy cut the cost of trading at Malacca: a preferential tariff charged merchants from west of Malacca, such as Arabia and India, 6 per cent, merchants of maritime Southeast Asia 3 per cent, and merchants from China, Japan and Java nothing at all. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-ms}

## Islam and scholarship
Mansur Shah had a personal interest in Islam: he encouraged scholarship in Islamic theological studies and studied Sufism himself, as a pupil of Maulana Abu Bakar, who had brought the Darul Manzum to Malacca. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-ms} He ordered the work translated into Malay — the English article names the translator as Makhdum Patakan, the Malay article places the translation at Pasai — and referred religious questions to the scholars of Pasai for their expertise. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-ms} Tomé Pires, quoted in the Malay article, also credits him with building a beautiful mosque at the foot of Bukit Melaka near the bridge over the Malacca River. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-ms} Study of Sufism is attested practice; no source consulted names a Sufi order or a legal school for the sultan himself.

## Death and the tombstone
Mansur Shah died in 1477 — the Malay article specifies October of that year. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-ms} {cite:wikidata-mansur-shah-malacca} Two tombstones long preserved at Malacca bear his name: the plate inscription, as deciphered by Jean Pierre Moquette with Winstedt's help, styles him "the just Sultan, the magnanimous ruler, Sultan Mansur Shah son of the deceased Muzaffar Shah" and dates his passing to a Wednesday of Rajab in the year 882 of the Hijra — 1477. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en} A rival decipherment of the same stones, Hervey's, reads the year as 880 (1475), the figure Winstedt carried in the title of his 1918 paper on the tomb; the 882/1477 reading is the one the standard accounts keep, and both are recorded here. {cite:wikipedia-mansur-shah-malacca-en}
