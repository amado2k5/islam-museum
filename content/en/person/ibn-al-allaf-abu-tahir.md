## Summary
Abu Tahir Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Muqri', known as Ibn al-Allaf, was a Baghdadi hadith transmitter and preacher of the fifth century AH and the third link in the chain of Ahmad ibn Hanbal's [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]] {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.40}.

## His place in the chain of Fada'il al-Sahaba
Ibn al-Allaf read the book to [[person/al-qatiqi|Abu Bakr al-Qati'i]] on Saturday 16 Jumada al-Akhira 368 AH {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.55}. It was later read back to him, while he listened, in Dhu al-Qa'da 436, by [[person/abu-al-husayn-al-tayuri|Abu al-Husayn al-Mubarak ibn Abd al-Jabbar al-Sayrafi]] {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.55}. The title page states that Abu al-Husayn's transmission from the beginning of the book to the end of the merits of Ali is from Ibn al-Allaf, from Ibn Malik al-Qati'i {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.54}.

## His standing and his gatherings
[[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi]] said: I wrote from him; he was truthful, discreet, of evident dignity, of good bearing and fine conduct {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.40}. He lived on Darb al-Diwan near Abu al-Qasim ibn Bishran, held a preaching session in the Mahdi mosque, and later took a teaching circle in the Mansur mosque in Baghdad {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.40}.

## His death
He died on the eve of Friday 24 Rabi' al-Akhir 442 AH, and Ibn al-Imad says much the same {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.40}. The editor's sources for his biography are *Tarikh Baghdad* and *Shadharat al-Dhahab* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.40}.
