## Summary
Ibrahim al-Koni is a Libyan novelist of Tuareg origin who made the Sahara a complete literary space comparable to García Márquez’s seas and forests. He lives in Switzerland and writes in Arabic. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-al-koni}

## Life and upbringing
Born in 1948 in Libya’s Fezzan region, he grew up in a Tuareg environment without writing, learned Arabic later and studied at Moscow’s Gorky Literary Institute, carrying the Sahara’s oral memory with him. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-al-koni}

## Career and works
He has published over eighty works of novels, stories and texts, including Gold Dust on the gold obsession in the desert, Anubis, The Bleeding of the Stone on idol-worship and trade, and The Magi. His works are widely translated and he has been repeatedly nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-al-koni}

## Method and contribution
Al-Koni fuses Tuareg myth with Islamic Sufism and the structures of world myth, presenting the desert as a centre of knowledge rather than a margin. Critics say he widened the map of the Arabic novel southwards, though some note recurring themes; the museum attributes these judgements to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-al-koni}
