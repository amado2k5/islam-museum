## Summary
Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Shawkani, later al-San'ani, was a Yemeni scholar of [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]], described by al-Zirikli as a jurist practising independent reasoning (mujtahid). {cite:al-aalam} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.4} He was born on Monday 28 Dhu al-Qa'da 1173 AH (July 1760) at Hijrat Shawkan, as found in his own hand, and died on the night before Wednesday 27 Jumada II 1250 AH (October 1834) in Sana'a. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.4} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.1} Trained in the Zaydi school, he gave up imitation (taqlid) to call for following the evidence, served as judge of Sana'a, and wrote many books, among them [[work/nayl-al-awtar-shawkani|Nayl al-Awtar]] and his commentary [[work/fath-al-qadir|Fath al-Qadir]]. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.6} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.26}

## The name Shawkani
The biographer in the Dar al-Ma'rifa edition quotes al-Shawkani's own [[work/al-badr-al-tali|al-Badr al-Tali']]: his father was known in Sana'a as al-Shawkani after Shawkan, a village of al-Sahamiyya, one of the tribes of Khawlan, less than a day's journey from Sana'a. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.4} He adds that the attribution is not strictly exact, since the family's home was a place called Hijrat Shawkan, separated from Shawkan by a large, long mountain; that is why his people were named after it. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.4}

## Upbringing and studies
He grew up in Sana'a under his father's care. He learned the Qur'an from several teachers, completing it with the jurist Hasan ibn Abd Allah al-Habal, and then memorised many standard texts: al-Azhar of Imam al-Mahdi in fiqh, al-Usayfiri's abridgement on inheritance, al-Hariri's Mulha, Ibn al-Hajib's Kafiya and Shafiya, al-Taftazani's Tahdhib, al-Qazwini's Talkhis, al-Jazari's poem on the readings, and others. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.4}

He studied with his father and with a group of Sana'a scholars: in fiqh he stayed some thirteen years with [[person/ahmad-ibn-muhammad-al-harazi|Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Harazi]], under whom he qualified; grammar, morphology, logic, rhetoric and legal theory he read with [[person/al-qasim-ibn-yahya-al-khawlani|al-Qasim ibn Yahya al-Khawlani]], [[person/abd-allah-ibn-ismail-al-nihmi|Abd Allah ibn Isma'il al-Nihmi]] and others. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.4} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5} He read al-Kashshaf with al-Sa'd's gloss under [[person/al-hasan-ibn-ismail-al-maghribi|al-Hasan ibn Isma'il al-Maghribi]]; heard the whole of Sahih al-Bukhari from Ali ibn Ibrahim ibn Ahmad ibn Amir, and heard Sahih Muslim, the Sunan of al-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud and parts of the Muwatta', al-Nasa'i and Ibn Maja; and read with [[person/abd-al-qadir-ibn-ahmad-al-sanani|Abd al-Qadir ibn Ahmad]] the commentary on Jam' al-Jawami', parts of Fath al-Bari and of Ibn Taymiyya's Muntaqa, and parts of al-Jawhari's Sihah and the Qamus. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5}

## His students
Among those who studied with him were his son Ali ibn Muhammad al-Shawkani, Husayn ibn Muhsin al-Sab'i al-Ansari al-Yamani, Muhammad ibn Hasan al-Shijni al-Dhamari, Abd al-Haqq ibn Fadl al-Hindi and [[person/al-sharif-muhammad-bin-nasir-al-hazmi|al-Sharif Muhammad ibn Nasir al-Hazimi]]. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5} His commentary is transmitted by a chain reaching him through his son, the judge Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Shawkani (d. 1281 AH). {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.10}

## His school and the controversy around him
His biography says he trained in the school of Imam Zayd, excelled in it and gave fatwas, then studied hadith until he "threw off the halter of imitation" and took up independent reasoning. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5} He wrote al-Sayl al-Jarrar on Hada'iq al-Azhar, rejecting what had no evidence behind it, and many of his contemporaries rose against him; he wrote the treatise [[work/al-qawl-al-mufid-fi-adillat-al-ijtihad-wa-al-taqlid|al-Qawl al-Mufid fi Adillat al-Ijtihad wa'l-Taqlid]], over which strife broke out in Sana'a between the followers of imitation and the followers of evidence, the former believing he meant to demolish the school of the Prophet's family. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5} The biographer answers that al-Shawkani set out the virtues of the Prophet's family in his book Durr al-Sahaba, and that he treated all the schools "exactly alike". {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5} He describes his creed as that of the early generations, taking the divine attributes in their apparent sense without figurative interpretation, on which he wrote [[work/al-tuhaf-fi-madhahib-al-salaf|al-Tuhaf bi-Madhhab al-Salaf]]. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.5} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.6} The editor of the Dar al-Wafa' edition illustrates this with his commentary on God's rising over the Throne, where he lists fourteen opinions and prefers that of the early generations, "that He rose over it without asking how". {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.50}

## The judgeship
Abd al-Rahman Umayra quotes al-Badr al-Tali' that al-Shawkani was devoted to study and writing and kept away from all officials; a week after the death of the judge Yahya ibn Salih al-Shajari al-Sahuli the caliph's men came asking him to take the judgeship, and after long hesitation he accepted at the insistence of senior scholars and notables, who feared the office would go to someone whose religion and learning could not be trusted. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.26} The editor lists the reasons his students gave for his acceptance: he saw it as a chance to spread the Sunna, it would lessen the attacks on him, and refusing the ruler might have had consequences. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.26} His tenure spanned the reigns of three of Yemen's imams: al-Mansur Ali ibn al-Mahdi al-Abbas (d. 1224 AH), then his son al-Mutawakkil (d. 1231 AH), then al-Mahdi Abd Allah (d. 1251 AH). {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.26} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.27}

## His works
His biography lists many works, among them Nayl al-Awtar, a commentary on Muntaqa al-Akhbar; Adab al-Talab wa-Muntaha al-Arab; Tuhfat al-Dhakirin, a commentary on Iddat al-Hisn al-Hasin; Irshad al-Thiqat ila Ittifaq al-Shara'i' on the agreement of the revealed laws on God's oneness, the hereafter and prophethood; Sharh al-Sudur fi Tahrim Raf' al-Qubur on the prohibition of raised graves; al-Risala al-Mukammala fi Adillat al-Basmala; many treatises on divorce, nursing, listening to music, prayer and other questions; and his collected fatwas, al-Fath al-Rabbani. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.6} The Dar al-Wafa' edition states that the original of Fath al-Qadir is kept in the library of the Great Mosque of Sana'a in six large volumes, no. 79 tafsir, under the title Matla' al-Badrayn wa-Majma' al-Bahrayn, and that those who counted it a separate book were mistaken. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.43}

## Irshad al-Fuhul

Among his works on legal theory is [[work/irshad-al-fuhul|Irshad al-Fuhul ila Tahqiq al-Haqq min Ilm al-Usul]], written at the request of a group of scholars to show the stronger from the weaker positions in the field, and finished on Wednesday 4 Muharram 1231 AH (December 1815). {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.4} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.289} In it he rejects the claim that the age was empty of independent reasoners, holds that forbidding imitation is the majority view, and refers to his treatise al-Qawl al-Mufid fi Hukm al-Taqlid and his book Adab al-Talab wa-Muntaha al-Arab. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.256} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.269} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.270}
