# Murad III Bey

Murad III Bey (1680–June 1702), Abu al-Zafar Murad ibn Ali Bey, was the last Bey of Tunis of the Muradid dynasty, ruling from 1699 until his assassination during the Revolutions of Tunis, the crisis that preceded the rise of Husayn I {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-en}. His father was Ali Bey, a son of Murad II Bey, and his mother a Bedouin woman {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-en}. After his father was murdered — the English and French articles name his uncle Muhammad Bey al-Muradi as the killer, the Arabic article names Ramadan Bey — the boy was adopted by Muhammad, and then by his other uncle, Ramadan Bey {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-fr} {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-ar}. Ramadan, suspecting a plot, imprisoned him and ordered his eyes put out; Murad escaped before the sentence, took refuge in the mountains of Jebel Ousselat, raised rebels who took Kairouan, marched on Tunis, drove Ramadan from the Bardo palace and had him executed, and was elected bey by the Divan on 16 March 1699 {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-en}.

In power he set out to eliminate his enemies, and the executions he ordered earned him the Tunisians' nickname Murad Bu Bala, bala being a broad Turkish sabre {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-fr}. Ibn Abi Dhiaf and the chroniclers of the time relate his savageries: digging up the remains of his uncles Muhammad and Ramadan to fire his musket at their corpses, and killing opponents with their whole families {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-en}. Kairouan, which had received him poorly in his flight, was besieged and sacked in 1699; on the western front he pursued the Algerians onto their own soil and attacked Constantine in 1701, in retaliation for the Algiers militia's attack on Tunis in 1694 {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-fr}.

While his lieutenant Ibrahim Sharif was in Istanbul recruiting janissaries, the Ottoman government charged him with ending Murad's abuses, and a new war with the Dey of Algiers began {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-en}. The three articles' leads and infoboxes date the killing to 9 June 1702, but the English narrative dates Ibrahim's blunderbuss shot on the banks of Wadi Zarka to 2 June, and the French narrative dates the assassination in his carriage at Oued Zarga to 2 June as well {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-en} {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-fr}. Returning to Tunis, Ibrahim assassinated the remaining Muradid princes to seize power for himself, and the dynasty ended with Murad {cite:wikipedia-murad-iii-bey-en}.
