## Summary
[[person/zaynab-bint-muhammad|Zaynab]] was the eldest of the [[person/muhammad|Prophet's]] daughters and the first of them to marry. She was born some years before the mission; one account puts it about ten years before. She was married to her maternal cousin [[person/abu-al-as-ibn-al-rabi|Abu al-As ibn al-Rabi']], whose mother was Hala bint Khuwaylid {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}.

## Migration, and her husband's continued faith
Ibn Sa'd, with a sound chain from al-Sha'bi, relates that Zaynab emigrated with her father, while her husband refused to embrace Islam. The Prophet did not separate them {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}.

## Abu al-As at Badr
Al-Waqidi reports from Aisha that Abu al-As fought at Badr with the polytheists and was taken captive. His brother Amr came to ransom him, and Zaynab sent with him a necklace that [[person/khadija|Khadija]] had sent along with her to Abu al-As at her wedding. When the Prophet saw it he recognised it and was moved, and he remembered Khadija. He spoke to the people, and they released Abu al-As and returned the necklace. He required of Abu al-As that he leave Zaynab free to go {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}. Al-Waqidi adds: "This is more firmly established with us" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}.

## Protection (ijara)
Ibn Ishaq relates, on the authority of Yazid ibn Rumman, that the Prophet led the dawn prayer, and Zaynab called out: "I have granted protection to Abu al-As ibn al-Rabi'." When the prayer ended, the Prophet said: "Did you hear what I heard?" He said he had known nothing of it until he heard it, and that protection given by a Muslim, even the least of them, is binding on all {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}.

In al-Waqidi's version, Abu al-As left with a Quraysh caravan. The Prophet sent [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] with a hundred and seventy riders. They met the caravan at al-Is in Jumada al-Ula of 6 AH, and captured some of its men, including Abu al-As. He entered upon Zaynab, and she gave him protection. He then discharged the obligations he had to their owners in Mecca and returned. He embraced Islam in Muharram of 7 AH, and the Prophet returned Zaynab to him under the original marriage {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}.

## Points of disagreement
- Whether al-Qasim was born before Zaynab or after her is disputed {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}.
- Al-Sha'bi's report of her protection of Abu al-As and of his Islam: Ibn Hajar gives it in Abu al-As's entry. He judges its chain to al-Sha'bi sound, but the report is a mursal and anomalous one, contradicted by what is more firmly established in Ibn Ishaq's maghazi {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1715}.

## Her death and children
Ibn Hajar reports from Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr ibn Hazm that Zaynab died at the beginning of 8 AH {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}. Muslim reports from Umm Atiyya that when Zaynab died the Prophet said: "Wash her an odd number of times, three or five" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}. She bore Ali to Abu al-As, who died close to puberty during the Prophet's lifetime, and Umama, who lived until [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] married her after Fatima {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1887}.

## In the chapter on the marriage to Khadija (The Sealed Nectar, p.61)
The Sealed Nectar lists the Prophet's children by [[person/khadija|Khadija]]: al-Qasim, then Zaynab, then Ruqayya, Umm Kulthum, Fatima and Abdullah. It says that the daughters all reached Islam, embraced it and emigrated, and that death overtook them during his lifetime except Fatima. It notes slight variation among the sources, and gives the view it judges more probable {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.61} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/190-191} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p.60} {cite:fath-al-bari 7/507}.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the sixth episode of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]] to Zaynab, the eldest of the Prophet's four daughters by Khadija; the archive's eighth file carries the same recording. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep08 00:01:00} He says she was married young, before the mission, to Abu al-As ibn al-Rabi', whose mother Hala bint Khuwaylid was Khadija's sister, and that their children were Ali and Umama. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:02:30} She became Muslim while he was travelling; he heard her out but would not follow, fearing people would say he had changed his religion to please his wife, and she stayed with him in Mecca. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:04:30}

Abu al-As fought with Quraysh at Badr and was among the seventy captives. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:06:00} Zaynab sent his ransom with a necklace Khadija had given her on her wedding day; the Prophet knew it and was deeply moved, and he asked the Companions, "if you wish", to return her necklace and her captive, which they did. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:08:00} The condition was that Abu al-As send Zaynab to her father; she asked him once more to become Muslim, he refused, and she left. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:09:30} On the road, in her fourth month of pregnancy, Habbar ibn al-Aswad struck her litter with his spear; she lost the child and bled for the rest of her life. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep08 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:11:00}

Later a Quraysh caravan led by Abu al-As was taken by a raiding party under Zayd ibn Haritha, and he came to Zaynab's door by night as a fugitive. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:13:00} She took him in, and at the dawn prayer called out from the women's rows that she had given him protection; the Prophet confirmed it, and his goods were returned to him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:17:30} In Mecca he repaid everyone whose money he carried and then declared his Islam, saying he had held back in Medina so that no one would think he did it for the money. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:19:00} He came to Medina six years after they had parted, and the Prophet returned her to him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:19:30} She died at the start of 8 AH, of the bleeding that never stopped, and after the conquest of Mecca the Prophet pardoned Habbar when he came as a Muslim. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:21:30} He sees in her a model of loyalty, patience and firm faith. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep06 00:22:30}
