## Overview
Ruzbihan ibn Abi al-Nasr al-Baqli al-Fasawi al-Shirazi, a Sufi scholar from Shiraz, titled Sadr al-Din, according to "Mawsu'at a'lam al-ulama wa al-udaba" {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}.

## Standing
The encyclopedia reports that he had a share in several sciences, and that he is among the great authors of Persian Sufism {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}.

## Death
He died in 606 AH (1209 CE), according to the encyclopedia {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam presents Ruzbihan Baqli (died 1209) as a Persian Sufi who was born in Pasa and is buried in Shiraz. It says he is best known for a life of ecstasy and visions and for his writings on shath, the Sufi expression of ecstasy. He wrote in Arabic and Persian on subjects ranging from Qur'anic commentary and poetry to theology, jurisprudence and grammar, and it names as the most important of his Sufi writings Kashf al-asrar (The unveiling of secrets), the Abhar al-ashiqin (Jasmine of the lovers) and the Sarh al-shathiyyat (Commentary on ecstatic sayings). {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2034}
