## Brief
Ibn Sina, known in Europe as "Avicenna," was a Persian physician and philosopher who lived around 980–1037 CE. His two books, *The Canon of Medicine* and *The Healing*, were studied for centuries from Central Asia to Western Europe.

## Life
Ibn Sina was born in the village of Afshana near [[place/bukhara|Bukhara]] around 980 CE, and he grew up in a city ruled by the Samanid dynasty. In his autobiography, which his student al-Juzjani completed, he describes a boy who mastered the Qur'an and the first sciences at an early age, and then turned to logic, mathematics and medicine {cite:gohlman-1974}.

He left Bukhara after about 1002 CE and worked as a physician, and at times as a vizier, at several courts in Iran. In his last years he served [[person/ala-al-dawla-muhammad|Ala al-Dawla]] in [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]]. He died in [[place/hamadan|Hamadan]] in June 1037 CE (Ramadan 428 AH) {cite:gutas-2014}.

## Works
Among his best-known books are [[work/al-qanun-fi-al-tibb|The Canon of Medicine]], an encyclopaedia of medicine, and [[work/kitab-al-shifa|The Healing]], a broad philosophical compendium. He wrote mostly in Arabic and also wrote in Persian {cite:gutas-2014}.

## Legacy
Later Muslim thinkers engaged with him in two directions. [[person/al-ghazali|Al-Ghazali]] criticised some of his views, while many others built on his system. Latin translations carried his medicine and philosophy into medieval Europe.

## For young visitors
Imagine a doctor and thinker so respected that his book of medicine was taught in classrooms for hundreds of years.

## Notes for researchers
The birth year is usually given as 980 CE, although some accounts point to 973 CE. The dates are kept here as the sources give them, and any Hijri conversion is marked as derived.
