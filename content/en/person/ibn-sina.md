## Brief
Ibn Sina, known in Europe as Avicenna, was a Persian physician and philosopher who lived around 980–1037 CE. His *Canon of Medicine* and *Book of Healing* were studied for centuries from Central Asia to Western Europe.

## Life
Ibn Sina was born near [[place/bukhara]], at Afshana, around 980 CE, and grew up in a city then ruled by the Samanid dynasty. His own account, completed by his student al-Juzjani, describes a boy who mastered the Qur'an and the basic sciences early and then pursued logic, mathematics and medicine {cite:gohlman-1974}.

He left Bukhara after about 1002 CE and worked at several courts in Iran as a physician and, at times, a vizier. In his last years he served [[person/ala-al-dawla-muhammad|Ala al-Dawla]] at [[place/isfahan]]. He died at [[place/hamadan]] in June 1037 CE (Ramadan 428 AH) {cite:gutas-2014}.

## Work
Among his best-known books are [[work/al-qanun-fi-al-tibb|the Canon of Medicine]], an encyclopaedic medical text, and [[work/kitab-al-shifa|the Book of Healing]], a wide-ranging philosophical summa. He wrote mostly in Arabic and also in Persian {cite:gutas-2014}.

## Legacy
Later Muslim thinkers engaged with him in both directions: [[person/al-ghazali]] criticised some of his views, while many others built on his system. Latin translations carried his medicine and philosophy into medieval Europe.

:::perspectives
- **Academic**: Historians note that his father is reported to have been Ismaili, and that Ibn Sina's own religious affiliation is debated {cite:gutas-2014}.
:::
