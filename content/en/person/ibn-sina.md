## Brief
Ibn Sina, known in Europe as "Avicenna," was a Persian physician and philosopher who lived around 980–1037 CE. His two books, *The Canon of Medicine* and *The Healing*, were studied for centuries from Central Asia to Western Europe.

## Life
Ibn Sina was born in the village of Afshana near [[place/bukhara|Bukhara]] around 980 CE, and he grew up in a city ruled by the Samanid dynasty. In his autobiography, which his student al-Juzjani completed, he describes a boy who mastered the Qur'an and the first sciences at an early age, and then turned to logic, mathematics and medicine {cite:gohlman-1974}.

He left Bukhara after about 1002 CE and worked as a physician, and at times as a vizier, at several courts in Iran. In his last years he served [[person/ala-al-dawla-muhammad|Ala al-Dawla]] in [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]]. He died in [[place/hamadan|Hamadan]] in June 1037 CE (Ramadan 428 AH) {cite:gutas-2014}.

## Works
Among his best-known books are [[work/al-qanun-fi-al-tibb|The Canon of Medicine]], an encyclopaedia of medicine, and [[work/kitab-al-shifa|The Healing]], a broad philosophical compendium. He wrote mostly in Arabic and also wrote in Persian {cite:gutas-2014}.

## Legacy
Later Muslim thinkers engaged with him in two directions. [[person/al-ghazali|Al-Ghazali]] criticised some of his views, while many others built on his system. Latin translations carried his medicine and philosophy into medieval Europe.

## For Sprouts
A wise physician and scholar who wrote a medical encyclopedia that helped doctors care for patients and protect their health.

## For Explorers
Ibn Sina was a physician and polymath born near Bukhara. He authored The Canon of Medicine, which served as the primary medical textbook in universities across the world for centuries, describing illnesses and treatments, as well as The Book of Healing covering natural sciences, astronomy, and philosophy.

## For Scholars
Abu Ali al-Husayn ibn Abd Allah ibn Sina (c. 370–428 AH / 980–1037 CE), the polymath philosopher and physician known to the West as Avicenna. Author of an immense corpus across diverse disciplines, his medical masterwork 'The Canon of Medicine' synthesized classical medicine with systematic clinical observation, anatomy, and pharmacology, remaining a standard university textbook in both East and West for centuries. In philosophy, his encyclopedic 'Kitab al-Shifa' (The Book of Healing) articulated a comprehensive framework uniting logic, natural philosophy, and metaphysics.

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Ibn Sina — 'al-Shaykh al-Ra'is' — as the model of concentration on one field, philosophy his means and medicine his end {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:06:30}.
Of his upbringing: born Safar 370 AH to a Persian family, Qur'an and grammar by ten; raised among an Isma'ili father and brother, he rejected the doctrine — 'I hear them and know what they say and my soul does not accept it' (correcting the charge of Isma'ilism) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:03:00}.
He began medicine at sixteen saying 'medicine is not among the hard sciences, so no wonder I excelled in the shortest time'; he read philosophy under al-Natili then left his shallowness to read alone — Aristotle's Metaphysics defeated him through forty readings until al-Farabi's book on its aims unlocked it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:06:30}.
Of his discipline: 'I never slept a whole night nor spent a day but in learning'; when perplexed he would repair to the mosque praying 'until the closed opened to me and the hard eased'; and when sleep overcame him he turned to a drink and back to reading {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:08:30}.
Of the princes' patronage: he cured Mansur ibn Nuh al-Samani where every physician had failed, who opened his library of treasures to him, and Nuh ibn Mansur continued the care — 'inner and material stability turn into output' (the speaker's lesson) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:10:30}.
Imprisoned in the political turmoil, he emerged into hiding at Ibn Ghalib the perfumer's, where in that enforced retreat he composed most of his works in the natural and divine sciences {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:11:30}.
His contest with Abu Mansur al-Jubba'i: worsted in language, he bent three years over it until he composed odes in the rarest words, presented them to the caliph aged and weathered, and al-Jubba'i could not explain them {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:14:30}.
Of his medical firsts: first describer of meningitis, anthrax and stroke, discoverer of the roundworm eight hundred years before the Italian physician, cardiac stimulants and pre-surgical anaesthetics, psychotherapy and the diseases of passionate love {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:16:30}.
Of his physics: light outruns sound, hearing relates to air's undulation, and an instrument measuring minute lengths precisely {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:16:45}.
Of his educational theory in Kitab al-Siyasa: upbringing from birth to marriage, the first seven years building character, individual differences, moral reward and punishment without vengeance {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:21:30}.
Of his works: over a hundred books; the Qanun a base reference of European universities to the seventeenth century; al-Shifa' in eighteen volumes with a complete original at Oxford; al-Insaf in astronomical observations; and al-Mukhtasar al-Awsat in logic {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:23:00}.
Of his death: illness recurring weekly until he said 'the manager who has failed to manage my body profits nothing from treatment — I who taught you medicine'; he abandoned treatment and died in 428 AH {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep27 00:24:30}.
