## Summary
[[person/al-walid-ibn-uqba|Al-Walid ibn Uqba]] ibn Abi Mu'ayt al-Umawi, kunya Abu Wahb; "brother of [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]] by his mother", their mother Arwa bint Kurayz {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1570}. His sister was [[person/umm-kulthum-bint-uqba|Umm Kulthum bint Uqba]] {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}.

## His father and his Islam
Ibn Hajar mentions his father Uqba was killed in captivity after the battle of Badr, having been captured there, the Prophet ordering his killing; he had said "Muhammad, who will be for the little ones?" and was answered: "the Fire" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1570}. Al-Walid and his brother Umara became Muslim on the conquest day {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1570}.

## The verse ascribed to him
It is said [[passage/quran-49-6|al-Hujurat 6]] descended about him {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1570}.

## The governorship of Kufa
Al-Walid grew up in Uthman's care until, made caliph, Uthman appointed him over [[place/kufa|Kufa]] after dismissing Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas — "and the people found that grave". He was brave, a poet, generous; Mus'ab al-Zubayri said he was among Quraysh's men and chiefs {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}. Khalifa said his governorship of Kufa came in year twenty-five; he raided Azerbaijan in year twenty-eight as the people's commander and was dismissed in year twenty-nine {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}.

## His dismissal
Ibn Hajar says the story of his leading the dawn prayer four rak'as while drunk is "well-known and recorded", and the story of his dismissal once wine-drinking was established on him is likewise well-known and recorded in the two Sahihs; Uthman dismissed him after his flogging and appointed Sa'id ibn al-As over Kufa {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}. It is said some of Kufa's people bore false witness against him out of partisanship — related by al-Tabari and rejected by Ibn Abd al-Barr {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}.

## The strife and his end
When Uthman was killed al-Walid kept clear of the strife, witnessing neither Ali's side nor another's — though he stirred Mu'awiya to fight Ali by his letters and poetry — and dwelt at al-Raqqa until he died {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}. Abu Uruba al-Harrani said he died in Mu'awiya's caliphate {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}. He related from the Prophet, Uthman and others; from him relate Haritha ibn Mudarrib, al-Sha'bi, Abu Musa al-Hamdani and others {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1571}.
