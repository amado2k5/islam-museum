## Summary
Zeinaba Kane is a Senegalese constitutional lawyer and academic combining two tracks: a doctorate in law from Cheikh Anta Diop University in Dakar and research in constitutional law, and the presidency of the women's wing of the Rassemblement Islamique du Sénégal (RIS-Alwahda), the country's largest Islamic umbrella with more than forty member associations. {cite:wise-zeinaba-kane}

## Formation
She took her doctorate in law at Cheikh Anta Diop University in Dakar, the leading francophone university of West Africa, specialising in constitutional law, and has published research including a study of the adversarial principle before the constitutional judge in Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal. {cite:wise-zeinaba-kane}

## Organisational work
From 2002 to 2008 she led the women's organisation of the Association of Muslim Students in Senegal, then rose to the presidency of the women's wing of RIS-Alwahda, the largest umbrella of Senegalese Islamic associations — making her among the highest-ranking women in the country's organised Muslim movement. Alongside her organisational role she directs communications for the NGO REFAC, which works to improve the conditions of rural women. {cite:wise-zeinaba-kane} {cite:muslim500-zeinaba-kane}

## Writing and media presence
She has written articles on the identity of Muslim women in Senegal and on feminism in Islam, appears regularly on Senegalese radio and television to discuss the place of Muslim women in society, and gives public lectures on International Women's Day on legal equality between the sexes in the Senegalese constitution and the Islamic heritage. {cite:wise-zeinaba-kane}

## Legacy
Her career reads as the model of a francophone African woman combining rigorous secular university formation with organic leadership inside an Islamic institution — a rare bridge between the two worlds; Senegalese press attributes to her the image of the learned advocate defending women's standing in the language of constitution and Islam together. {cite:wise-zeinaba-kane}
