## Summary
Ibtihaj Muhammad is an American sabre fencer, author and entrepreneur, born on 4 December 1985 in New Jersey and raised in Maplewood. She won team gold at the 2014 World Fencing Championships and five World Championship medals in all. At the Rio 2016 Olympics she became the first American woman to compete at the Olympic Games wearing a hijab, the first Muslim American woman to win an Olympic medal, and the first Black woman to win an Olympic medal in the sabre event, when she took bronze in the women's team sabre. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-ur}

She was named one of Time 100's Most Influential People in the World in 2016, serves as a sports ambassador for the United States Department of State, and is a New York Times bestselling author. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en}

## Early life and education
She was born in New Jersey and raised in Maplewood; Wikidata gives her birthplace as Maplewood, and the Arabic article agrees. She has two older siblings, Brandilyn and Qareeb, and two younger siblings, Asiya and Faizah. The Arabic article adds that her family is African American and converted to Islam, that her father, Eugene Muhammad, is a retired police officer and her mother, Denise, was an elementary-school teacher. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-ar} {cite:wikidata-ibtihaj-muhammad}

She began fencing at 13 at Columbia High School in New Jersey, and in 2002 joined the Peter Westbrook Foundation in New York City. After graduating from Columbia High School in 2003 she attended Duke University in Durham, North Carolina, where she was a three-time All-American, graduating in 2007 with a double major in International Relations and African & African-American Studies. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-ar}

## Fencing career
She qualified for her first United States national team in 2010, becoming the first woman of colour on the women's sabre team, and was a member of the national team from 2010 to 2017. She is a two-time national champion (2009, 2017), a 19-time World Cup medallist, and was ranked as high as No. 7 in the world. She won five senior World Championship medals (2011, 2012, 2013, 2015), and at the 2014 World Championships, in Kazan as the Arabic article notes, she won gold with the United States women's sabre team. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en} {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-ar}

### Rio 2016
At the 2016 Summer Olympics she became the first American woman to compete in the Olympics in hijab, the first Muslim American woman to win an Olympic medal, and the first Black woman to win an Olympic medal in the sabre event, when the team defeated Italy 45-30 in the bronze-medal match. The Guardian wrote in 2016 that, visibly Muslim because of her hijab, she became "one of the best symbols against intolerance America can ever have". {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en}

## Ventures and advocacy
In 2014 she and her siblings launched a clothing company, Louella by Ibtihaj, to bring modest fashionable clothing to the United States market. She is a sports ambassador with the U.S. Department of State's Empowering Women and Girls Through Sport Initiative and has travelled to various countries for dialogue on the importance of sports and education. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en}

In 2017 Mattel honoured her as a Barbie "Shero", modelling the first hijabi Barbie — and the first fencer Barbie — in her likeness. In 2019 she was recognised as a Global Ambassador for Special Olympics and continues to support people with intellectual disabilities, including work with Special Olympics athletes during the 2025 Global Week of Inclusion. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en}

## Books
Her debut memoir, Proud: My Fight for an Unlikely American Dream, appeared in 2018, with a young-readers edition, Proud: Living My American Dream, the same year. Her picture-book series — The Proudest Blue: A Story of Hijab & Family (2019), The Kindest Red: A Story of Hijab and Friendship (2023) and The Boldest White: A Story of Hijab & Community (2024) — became instant New York Times bestsellers, and the first and third were twice finalists for the Forest of Reading's Blue Spruce Award, in 2021 and 2024. {cite:wikipedia-ibtihaj-muhammad-en}
