## Summary
Moroccan hadith master and jurist (1902–1960), the eldest of the three Ghumari brothers, famed for prodigious memory and breadth of transmission until styled 'the encyclopedia of his age'. His works include Al-Mudawi and Al-Mu'jam al-Asghar, and treatises scrutinising al-Suyuti's compilations. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ghumari}

## Life and formation

Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Siddiq al-Ghumari was born in Tangier in 1902 and raised in his father's scholarly circle; he memorised the Qur'an and the manuals young, then travelled with his father to Egypt, studied under its scholars, and moved between Fez, Cairo and the Hijaz. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ghumari}

From youth he was famed for astonishing memory — he had the books of hadith and literature with their chains by heart — so that contemporaries said no hadith or verse ever puzzled him. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ghumari}

## Works and polemics

His works include Al-Mudawi li-'Ilal al-Kanz al-Madfun on hadith defects, Al-Mu'jam al-Asghar, a commentary on the Tahawiyya creed, and treatises in hadith, fiqh and literature. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ghumari}

He fought well-known hadith polemics, among them his critique of al-Suyuti's compilations and his treatise on the fabrications in al-Durr al-Manthur, plus correspondence with contemporary muhaddiths — some documenting disagreements with al-Albani over grading. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ghumari}

## Legacy

Ahmad al-Ghumari is named at the head of the three Ghumari brothers: he combined elevated transmission, critical editing and scholarly contention, leaving treatises that became references in delicate hadith questions. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ghumari}

He died in Cairo in 1960; his brothers Abdullah and Abd al-Aziz continued his way, and the works of the three Ghumaris are still read and edited in the libraries of hadith heritage. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ghumari}
