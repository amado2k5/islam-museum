## Summary
Abu Abd al-Rahman Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal — "the imam, the hafiz, the proof", as the editor of *Fada'il al-Sahaba* describes him — was the son of [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Imam Ahmad]] and the transmitter of his books {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.41}. He was born in 213 AH and died in 290 AH {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.41-42}.

## His studies and teachers
He sought knowledge from a young age and narrated from his father, from Ibrahim ibn al-Hajjaj, Ahmad ibn Mani' al-Baghawi, [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]] and a great many others, more than four hundred {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.41}. Al-Nasa'i, Abu Bakr al-Shafi'i, Ibn Abi Hatim, [[person/al-qatiqi|Abu Bakr al-Qati'i]] and others narrated from him {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.41}.

## Praise from the imams
Ibn al-Munadi wrote in his history that no one in the world narrated more from Ahmad than he did, and that the great shaykhs bore witness to his knowledge of transmitters, hidden defects of hadith and names and his constant pursuit of study — some even going too far and ranking him above his father in knowledge and breadth of hearing {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.41-42}. [[person/al-daraqutni|Al-Daraqutni]], asked about him and Hanbal ibn Ishaq, said: "Two trustworthy, noble men" {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.42}. Abu Bakr al-Khallal said: Abd Allah was a righteous man, truthful in speech and very modest {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.42}.

## His bond with al-Qati'i
Al-Qati'i related that Abd Allah ibn Ahmad used to come to them and his father's uncle would read to him; Abd Allah would sit al-Qati'i, then a boy, in his lap, and when told "he is hurting you" he would say "I love him" {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.40}. Through al-Qati'i people heard from Abd Allah the *Musnad*, *al-Zuhd* and *al-Fada'il* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.40}.

## His additions to Fada'il al-Sahaba
Abd Allah transmits [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]] from his father and adds hadiths from his own teachers; these form the second kind of material in the book according to the editor, and weak reports are more frequent in them than in Ahmad's own transmission {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.46}. In the book his formula "Abd Allah told us: my father told me" marks what he had from Ahmad, and "Abd Allah told us: so-and-so told me" marks his additions {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.55-58}. Among his additions he records that he asked his father about a hadith on Abu Bakr's wealth narrated by Sufyan ibn Uyayna from al-Zuhri; Ahmad rejected it, saying that the narrator Wa'il had not heard from al-Zuhri and that it was a mistake {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.84-85}.
