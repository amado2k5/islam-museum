## Overview
Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Maqdisi al-Bashshari, known as Shams al-Din, a Hanafi geographer who died around 380 AH. He is the author of "Ahsan al-Taqasim fi Ma'rifat al-Aqalim". {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.206} See [[work/ahsan-al-taqasim|Ahsan al-Taqasim fi Ma'rifat al-Aqalim]].

## The journey and the book
The sources say that he travelled the regions and measured their extents in farsakhs, and spent more than ten thousand dirhams on his journeys. He lived in neighbourhood with others, fought, and garrisoned the frontiers. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.206}

Al-Muqaddasi himself describes his book as not written at random nor relying on hearsay, but that he left the caravans to see each town for himself. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp.40-41}

## In the preface of his book (Leiden edition)
The title page of the Leiden edition names him the shaykh and imam Shams al-Din Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Bakr al-Banna' al-Shami al-Maqdisi, known as al-Bashari. {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.9} In his preface he describes his path to the knowledge he sought: travelling the lands, meeting scholars, serving kings, sitting with judges, studying with jurists, frequenting men of letters, Qur'an readers and hadith writers, mixing with ascetics and Sufis, attending storytellers, trading in every town, along with "tasting the air, weighing the water, great toil, spending money and seeking what is lawful". {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.12} He says he sailed about two thousand parasangs on the southern sea and went round the whole Arabian peninsula from al-Qulzum to Abbadan, keeping company with men born and raised on it, captains, agents and merchants, and saw logbooks they studied, from which he took notes. {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.20}

He says he did not publish his book until he had reached forty, trodden every region and served the people of learning and religion, which fell in 375 AH in the capital of Fars. {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.18} {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.19} He criticizes predecessors such as al-Jayhani, al-Balkhi and Ibn al-Faqih, and says he included only what he saw or what trustworthy people agreed on, attributing what he doubted to its source. {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.13} {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.18} See [[work/ahsan-al-taqasim|Ahsan al-taqasim]] and [[concept/aqalim-al-muqaddasi|the fourteen regions]].
