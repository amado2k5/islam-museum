## Summary
Uthman Taha is a Syrian calligrapher whose hand is the most reproduced in the history of Qur'an printing: the mushaf he wrote in Ottoman-style naskh has been printed by the King Fahd Glorious Qur'an Printing Complex in Madinah since the 1980s in hundreds of millions of copies distributed worldwide. {cite:wikipedia-uthman-taha}

## Life and training
He was born in 1934 in Aleppo, a city with a deep tradition of Arabic calligraphy. He trained under masters of the Ottoman school — the line of naskh and thuluth inherited from the great Turkish calligraphers — and received his ijaza, the licence attesting mastery, before devoting himself almost entirely to writing copies of the Qur'an. {cite:wikipedia-uthman-taha}

## The Madinah mushaf
In the early 1980s the King Fahd Complex in Madinah commissioned a new standard text of the Qur'an for mass printing, and Taha wrote it by hand in naskh, completing the task around 1985. The resulting mushaf — with its crisp script, sura headings and verse numbering — became the most widely printed Qur'an in history, and his penmanship defined the visual look of the mushaf for generations of readers. He went on to write further complete copies, and reference accounts credit him with more than ten handwritten mushafs in all. The museum holds a record for the printed edition at object/mushaf-uthman-taha. {cite:wikipedia-uthman-taha}

## Standing
Calligraphers and scholars describe him as the greatest living mushaf calligrapher and the continuer of the Ottoman naskh tradition into the print era. The Muslim 500 has listed him among influential Muslims in the arts category — an unusual honour for a calligrapher whose name, unlike his script, is little known outside specialist circles. {cite:wikipedia-uthman-taha}
