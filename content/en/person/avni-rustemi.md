Avni Rustemi (22 September 1895 – 22 April 1924) was an Albanian teacher and patriot born at Libohovë, the son of the steward of a great landowner's estates, and educated in Janina, Istanbul and at a teaching college in Geneva.{cite:wikipedia-avni-rustemi-en}{cite:wikipedia-avni-rustemi-sq} He taught in southern Albania from 1917 and threw himself into the patriotic societies of the new state. On 13 June 1920, outside the Hotel Continental in Paris, he shot and killed Essad Pasha Toptani, the politician Albanians remember as a collaborator in the partition of their lands; a French court acquitted him that December, and he returned home a national hero.{cite:wikipedia-avni-rustemi-en}

In 1921 he co-founded the Atdheu federation of patriotic societies and the national teachers' association, and sat in the National Assembly with the democratic opposition; after government pressure dissolved Atdheu, he co-founded the Bashkimi society in Tirana, which rallied the anti-Zogist opposition around discipline, patriotism and self-improvement.{cite:wikipedia-avni-rustemi-en} On 20 April 1924 he was shot in Tirana and died of his wounds two days later, an assassination most scholars attribute to Ahmet Zogu's orders; his funeral at Vlorë, attended by over 10,000 people, helped provoke the June Revolution.{cite:wikipedia-avni-rustemi-en}{cite:wikidata-avni-rustemi}
