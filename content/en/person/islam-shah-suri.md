Jalal Khan - the future Islam Shah - was born, on the English article's account, in 1507 at Delhi (Wikidata gives 1510), the second son of Sher Shah Suri, founder of the Suri Empire. When Sher Shah died in 1545, an emergency meeting of the nobles set aside the elder brother, Adil Khan, and chose Jalal Khan for his greater military ability. He was crowned on 26 May 1545 and took the title Islam Shah; Arabic sources also know him as Salim Shah {cite:wikipedia-islam-shah-suri-en}{cite:wikidata-islam-shah-suri}.

## Reign

Adil Khan marched on his brother and was defeated near Agra; he fled for help and was later killed. Islam Shah then set about mastering an Afghan nobility used to treating the sultan as first among equals: he purged nobles he suspected despite their service under his father, subordinated the aristocracy to the crown, and continued Sher Shah's centralisation, ruling the empire as a single unit under a central administration. He repelled Humayun's one attempt to invade his kingdom, and campaigned as far as Kalinjar in his last years {cite:wikipedia-islam-shah-suri-en}.

## Death and succession

Islam Shah died on 22 November 1554. His unfinished tomb still stands about a kilometre north-west of Sher Shah's tomb at Sasaram. He was succeeded by his twelve-year-old son Firuz Shah, who was murdered within days by Muhammad Mubariz Khan - who seized the throne as Muhammad Adil Shah - after which the Suri state quickly unravelled {cite:wikipedia-islam-shah-suri-en}{cite:wikipedia-islam-shah-suri-ar}.
