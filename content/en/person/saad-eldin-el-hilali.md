## Summary
Saad El-Din Mas'ad Ahmed Hassan Hilali is a professor of comparative fiqh at Al-Azhar University's Faculty of Sharia and Law in Cairo, born in Mansoura, Dakahlia Governorate, in 1954. Educated entirely within Al-Azhar's system from preparatory school onward, he rose to a full professorship in the Faculty of Sharia and Law in 1996, served as dean of Al-Azhar's faculties of Islamic and Arabic studies in Aswan and then Damietta, and headed the Department of Fiqh and Usul at Kuwait University's Faculty of Sharia. He has written numerous books on comparative fiqh and its contemporary questions, the best known of them Islam and the Humanity of the State (2012). {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} {cite:maqall-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

The wider public knows him from television. He held the religious segment on Amr Adib's Kol Youm on ONTV, then became the standing religious-segment holder on Adib's Al-Hekaya on MBC Masr from the programme's launch on 15 September 2018. His stated method, in these segments and in his books, is to present all the jurists' opinions on an issue without concealment — "what is renewed is the understanding, not the text" — a method that has won him a large audience and repeated formal objections from Al-Azhar and Dar al-Ifta bodies, which this article reports as attributed claims, with no verdict from the museum. {cite:mbc-masr-alhekaya-segment} {cite:aljamila-alhekaya-launch-2018} {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

## Early life and education
He was born in Mansoura in 1954 and began primary school in 1960. In 1965 he entered the Al-Azhar institutes at preparatory level, and his whole schooling from then on was Azhari. In 1973 he moved to the Faculty of Sharia and Law at Al-Azhar University in Cairo, graduating in 1978. In his own account of his life he graduated with honours and was briefly appointed to the Public Prosecution, leaving after about three weeks to take up university teaching. {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

He took his master's degree in 1982 with an edition and study of the Book of Dowry (Kitab al-Sadaq) from al-Mawardi's al-Hawi al-Kabir, and his doctorate in 1985 with a thesis titled "Lesion and its effect on contracts in Islamic jurisprudence and positive law" (al-Ghabn wa-atharuhu fi al-'uqud). The details of the two theses rest on a single critical biography of him and are recorded here on that basis until a second source confirms them. {cite:alsiyars-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

## Academic career and posts
He was appointed a teaching assistant (mu'id) at the Faculty of Sharia and Law in Cairo in 1979 and climbed the faculty ladder there to a full professorship in comparative fiqh in 1996 — the chair he still holds in the same faculty. He has supervised and examined many master's and doctoral theses at Al-Azhar and at other Egyptian and Arab universities. {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar}

In the mid-1990s he served as dean of Al-Azhar's Faculty of Islamic and Arabic Studies at its Aswan branch (c. 1994–95) and then at its Damietta branch (c. 1995–97). On secondment to Kuwait University he headed the Department of Fiqh and Usul in the Faculty of Sharia between 2002 and 2004 (another biography gives 2001–2003), and after returning he headed the Department of Comparative Fiqh at the Faculty of Sharia and Law in Cairo. {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} {cite:maqall-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali} Before the Kuwait secondment, his published CVs record two secondments to Saudi Arabia between 1988 and 1993, first to the Girls' College of Arts in Dammam and then to the College of Education in Madinah, followed by the Aswan deanship in 1994 and Damietta in 1995. {cite:mawdoo3-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar}

His biographies also list membership of the permanent scientific committee for professorial promotions in comparative fiqh, of the Fiqh Encyclopaedia committee of the Supreme Council for Islamic Affairs, of the religious committee of the Egyptian Radio and Television Union (now the National Media Authority), and of the National Council for Women in 2016, as well as work as rapporteur of a culture committee. A profile published in 2018 also names him a member of the Committee of Fifty that drafted the post-2012 constitutional amendments — a single-source item, recorded as such. {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} {cite:maqall-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali} His committee memberships, as his published CVs list them, also include the National Specialized Councils (Azhari-education section), the Ministry of Justice's good-offices committee, and the research-ethics committee of the National Research Centre and the Academy of Scientific Research in Cairo. {cite:mawdoo3-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar}

## His method
El-Hilali describes his method in a phrase he repeats often: "What is renewed is the understanding, not the text" — the revealed text is fixed; what renews is human understanding of it. On that basis he holds that anyone who speaks publicly about religion must present all the jurists' opinions on an issue, and that concealing some of them from the public is tadlis — a deception practised on the audience. The individual then chooses, "asking his own heart", and sovereignty in such matters rests with people rather than with a clerical gatekeeper; he has described the professional-preacher role itself as an accretion on Islam. {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

On Al-Hekaya he has said that Al-Azhar is "guardian of the scholarly trust, not guardian over religion itself", noting that religious works predate Al-Azhar's founding. He publishes his fiqh opinions himself on his official Facebook page, which had 166,775 followers as read on 18 November 2026; it is the only self-published channel for him that the research could verify — no personal official website and no published email address were found. {cite:arabkhabar-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:facebook-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

## Media work
His first regular television home was, by a single-source journalistic account, the Azhari satellite channel. He then held the religious segment of Amr Adib's Kol Youm on ONTV and moved with Adib to Al-Hekaya on MBC Masr, which launched on 15 September 2018 with El-Hilali named among its segment holders from the start, alongside the actress Ragaa El-Gedawy and chef Gigi Zayed. {cite:aljamila-alhekaya-launch-2018} {cite:alsiyars-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

MBC's own programme description gives the segment's format in the channel's words: El-Hilali "presents all the fiqh opinions on the issue in his smooth, simplified style, while Amr Adib asks the questions a viewer might ask." His appearances alternate between in-studio discussions and phone-ins reacting to the week's topic. {cite:mbc-masr-alhekaya-segment}

Among the segment's widely covered moments: a June 2023 phone-in permitting the festival sacrifice (udhiya) with poultry for those who cannot afford livestock, citing a position transmitted from Ibn Hazm. In September 2026 he treated men wearing gold, arguing the question is not one-sided and citing reports of Companions — al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, Hudhayfa and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas — wearing gold rings; the Egyptian Dar al-Ifta publicly restated the majority prohibition in response. In November 2026 he noted on the programme that the classical majority of jurists had permitted child marriage and that Egyptians abolished it by statute, not by fatwa committees; when news sites ran headlines he considered distorted, he sent an open letter, reported on 10 November 2026, to Khaled El-Balshy, head of the Journalists' Syndicate, asking for the press code of ethics to be enforced and pointing to the original episode recording as the reference. {cite:alkhalij24-udhiya-poultry-2023} {cite:eyar24-gold-dar-alifta-2026} {cite:klyoum-journalists-letter-2026}

Beyond Al-Hekaya, he appeared on Al Arabiya's Direct Question (Su'al Mubashir) — including the 19 April 2025 episode in which he set out his inheritance reading — in a phone-in on Al-Hadath responding to Al-Azhar's disavowal, in segments on Egyptian Channel One, and on DMC's La'allahum Yafqahun in 2020. On radio he presented a short daily Ramadan programme on Nogoum FM, With the Clear Warner (Ma'a al-Nadhir al-Mubin), and Our Children (Awladuna) on Radio 9090 from December 2015. In June 2026 he was a guest on the Hakawi podcast with Amal El-Hennawy, where he expressed regret over his youthful absorption in what he called the "one-track religious discourse" of al-Jam'iyya al-Shar'iyya in Mansoura. His recorded lectures also circulate in a numbered series, Majlis al-Fiqh (The Fiqh Council), whose episodes passed 670 in the autumn of 2026; the research could not confirm the ownership of the channel hosting it. {cite:ecrf-freedom-of-belief-april-2025} {cite:alsiyars-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:youtube-majlis-al-fiqh-series} In October 2025, after the mass mawlid of al-Sayyid al-Badawi in Tanta, he used an Al-Hekaya segment to present shrine-building, tabarruk and intercession at saints' shrines as contested questions of fiqh, as Masrawy reported on 18 October 2025. {cite:masrawy-hilali-badawi-2025}

## Books
El-Hilali has written many books on comparative fiqh and its contemporary questions. One of his profiles puts the total above seventy, in addition to hundreds of papers; the research could not verify that figure against a catalogue, so this article names only the documented titles. {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar}

His best-known book is Islam and the Humanity of the State (al-Islam wa-insaniyyat al-dawla), subtitled "A foundational study treating doubts and recommendations, and an initiative towards the inclusive humane state", published by the Egyptian General Book Organisation in 2012 in about 328 pages. It is his most debated book: Ali Gomaa, the former Grand Mufti of Egypt, published a sharply critical review of it in Al-Azhar's magazine. His book lists also carry a title The Humane State in Islam (al-Dawla al-insaniyya fi al-Islam); whether it is an edition of the 2012 book or a separate work could not be verified. {cite:alsiyars-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar}

His other documented titles include: The Genetic Fingerprint and Its Sharia Implications (al-Basma al-wirathiyya wa-'ala'iquha al-shar'iyya), which won the Kuwait Foundation for the Advancement of Sciences prize for the best Arabic book of 2003; The Sharia Grounding of Alcohol and Drugs: A Comparative Fiqh Study, published by the Islamic Organization for Medical Sciences; the Encyclopaedia of Funeral Jurisprudence in seven volumes; the Encyclopaedia of the Fiqh of Drug Rulings in three volumes; Composite Financial Transactions; The Financial Crisis and Islamic Solutions; Human Rights in Islam, also in an expanded form comparing it with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights; The Fiqh and Legislative Aspect of Cloning; Usul Skill and Its Effect on Fiqh Maturity and Renewal; The Thirties (thirty-issue studies) in Contemporary Fiqh Questions; The Fiqh Guide to Women's Most Private Matters; The Contemporary Elderly Question and Their Specific Rulings in Islamic Fiqh; The Profession and Its Ethics; and Islam's Position on Apostasy. The sources give no publication years or publishers for most of these titles, and none are invented here. {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} {cite:books-library-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

## Awards
He received the Kuwait Foundation for the Advancement of Sciences prize in 2003, for The Genetic Fingerprint and Its Sharia Implications as the best Arabic book of that year; a prize of the Islamic Organization for Medical Sciences in 2006; and Egypt's State Encouragement Award in legal and social sciences in 2007. One of his biographies additionally claims a Nile Award in 2010 and an honorary doctorate in 2013; both are single-source and unverified, and are recorded here as claims, not facts. {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} {cite:books-library-saad-eldin-el-hilali} {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

## Positions and reception
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources and attributed to those who hold them. It is not a verdict by the museum on any of them.

**His method as he states it.** El-Hilali holds that the speaker on religion must present every juristic opinion, that renewal falls on understanding rather than on the text, and that the individual chooses by asking his own heart. He applies this to many questions that have drawn wide debate, among them the following. {cite:shbabbek-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

**Inheritance (April 2025).** He argued that no Qur'anic text prohibits equality between male and female heirs of the same degree of kinship, such as a brother and a sister, and offered two readings of the inheritance verse: a "preference" reading, and a "protection" reading that takes the verse as a guarantee of the female's share in an age that denied her any share at all. He made the case on Al Arabiya's Direct Question on 19 April 2025 and elsewhere. {cite:ecrf-freedom-of-belief-april-2025} {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} In support of the reading he cited precedents he presented as established practice: equal inheritance between the sexes in Turkey since 1937, and Egypt's pensions law 148 of 2019, under which a pension passes to male and female heirs alike; he also proposed a popular referendum on changing the inheritance laws, while stressing that the current law stands until any change. {cite:sadaelbalad-azhar-rejects-hilali-inheritance-2025}

**The institutional response.** Al-Azhar's Global Centre for Electronic Fatwa issued a formal statement rejecting the position: inheritance texts, it said, are definitive (qat'iyya) and open neither to alteration nor to ijtihad; calls for a "personal religiosity" were described as an encroachment on the Sharia; and the renewal of the Islamic sciences, it said, "is not done on TV screens or among non-specialists". Senior Al-Azhar figures, among them former undersecretary Abbas Shouman, also rebutted him publicly. El-Hilali's own response to Al-Azhar's disavowal, given by phone-in on Al-Hadath, was that the matter "is up to Al-Azhar… what matters to me is that God does not disavow me; I will meet God with a sound heart." {cite:klyoum-azhar-rejects-inheritance-2025} {cite:ecrf-freedom-of-belief-april-2025} {cite:arabkhabar-saad-eldin-el-hilali} Separately, on 20 April 2025, Al-Azhar University's official spokesman, Ahmed Zare', stated that the university disavowed the statements as a personal opinion contrary to the established tenets of the Sharia and the consensus of scholars; he described El-Hilali as the university's professor emeritus (al-ustadh al-mutafarrigh). This was a university-level disavowal, distinct from the Fatwa Centre's statement. {cite:alayam-azhar-university-hilali-2025}

**Other documented positions and responses.** From 2018 he has argued that the word "hijab", as a term for head-covering, does not appear in the Qur'an and that the ruling is a juristic inference — a position Al-Azhar and Dar al-Ifta figures have repeatedly contested. In 2022, citing Abu Hanifa, he argued that a non-intoxicating amount of barley beer or non-grape nabidh is permissible and only the intoxicating amount prohibited. He has also held that a wife may not open her late husband's phone. Dar al-Ifta's public response to his 2026 men's-gold segment, restating the majority prohibition, is recorded above. {cite:wikipedia-saad-eldin-el-hilali-ar} {cite:eyar24-gold-dar-alifta-2026}

**The 2014 speech.** In February 2014, speaking at a ceremony honouring the families of police killed in service, El-Hilali said, as the press reported his words, that God had sent two men to Egypt, "as He sent Moses and Aaron before", naming the Defence Minister Abdel Fattah al-Sisi and the Interior Minister Mohamed Ibrahim; Al Jazeera also reported that he likened the minister's escape from an assassination attempt to Abraham's deliverance from the fire. Al-Azhar issued a statement in response. The wording of the quotation varies slightly between reports. {cite:elwatan-hilali-moses-aaron-2014} {cite:aljazeera-hilali-moses-aaron-2014} {cite:arabi21-azhar-statement-hilali-2014}

**Book criticism.** Ali Gomaa's sharply critical review of Islam and the Humanity of the State in Al-Azhar's magazine is the most prominent written confrontation between El-Hilali and the Al-Azhar establishment over one of his books. {cite:alsiyars-saad-eldin-el-hilali}

**Legal proceedings.** Press reports state that the lawyer Ahmed Mahran filed an administrative-court lawsuit seeking El-Hilali's dismissal from his post and a ban on his fatwas and media appearances, on grounds including contempt of religion. The research could not verify the lawsuit's outcome; it is recorded as a press report, not a judicial result. {cite:arabkhabar-saad-eldin-el-hilali}
