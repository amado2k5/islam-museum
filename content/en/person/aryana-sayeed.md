## Summary
Aryana Sayeed is an Afghan singer, songwriter and television personality born in Kabul in 1985, often described as Afghanistan's best-known female pop star and an outspoken advocate for Afghan women; The Muslim 500 lists her among influential figures in arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-aryana-sayeed} {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## Life
She left Afghanistan as a child during the civil war, living in Pakistan and Switzerland before settling in London, where she began her music career; she later returned regularly to Kabul to perform and to sit on judging panels for the talent shows Afghan Star and The Voice of Afghanistan. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## Music
Singing mainly in Dari, she released hits including Banoo-ye Ahanin ⁦(Lady of Iron)⁩, Afghan Pesarak and a popular rendition of the national anthem, mixing pop production with Afghan melodic traditions and themes of love and women's strength. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## Advocacy
Her concerts in Kabul — including a 2017 show held despite security threats and a stadium performance on Afghan Independence Day — were treated in the press as statements for women's public presence. She left Afghanistan in August 2021 during the August 2021 takeover of Afghanistan by an armed group and continued performing abroad. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## Assessment
Supporters see her as a symbol of Afghan women's freedom of expression; religious conservatives criticised her stage presence. The museum reports these positions attributed to their sources, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed} {cite:muslim500-aryana-sayeed}


## Return to Kabul
Her decision to keep performing in Afghanistan while based in London — judging Afghan Star and The Voice of Afghanistan and staging concerts in Kabul — was treated by supporters as defiance of those who would silence women in public life. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## Banoo-ye Ahanin
The song Banoo-ye Ahanin ⁦('Iron Lady')⁩ became an anthem of Afghan women's resilience; her 2017 stadium concert in Kabul and later shows drew huge crowds despite security warnings, and her Independence Day performance was widely reported as a statement. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## After 2021
Evacuated during the August 2021 fall of Kabul, she continued performing abroad for the Afghan diaspora and speaking for Afghan women, and remains one of the most-followed Afghan artists online. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed} {cite:muslim500-aryana-sayeed}

## From exile to stage
Born in Kabul in 1985, she spent childhood years in Pakistan and Switzerland before settling in London; she began recording in Dari in the mid-2000s and rose through Afghan-diaspora television before breaking through inside Afghanistan itself. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## Television judging
Her years judging Afghan Star — Afghanistan's flagship singing competition — and The Voice of Afghanistan made her a weekly presence in Afghan homes and a visible argument for women in public cultural life. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## Fashion and controversy
Her performance wardrobe and public persona drew both large fandom and conservative criticism inside Afghanistan; press coverage describes her concerts as focal points for arguments about women's presence onstage. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed} {cite:muslim500-aryana-sayeed}

## Afghan women's public presence
Sayeed's career became inseparable from the question of Afghan women's presence in public life. Her judging roles on Afghan Star and The Voice of Afghanistan placed her in the centre of prime-time television inside Afghanistan; her stadium concerts in Kabul and her wardrobe and public persona drew both massive fandom and conservative opposition. Press coverage throughout the 2010s treated her performances as focal points in the argument over women's cultural presence — making her a political figure simply by continuing to perform. {cite:wikipedia-aryana-sayeed}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists her among the most influential Muslims in arts and entertainment, citing her profile as Afghanistan's leading female pop singer and her role in sustaining Afghan women's public cultural presence through the years of conflict and the return to power of forces hostile to it. {cite:muslim500-aryana-sayeed}
