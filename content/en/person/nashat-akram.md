# Nashat Akram

## Summary

Nashat Akram Abid Ali Al-Eissa is an Iraqi playmaker, born on 12 September 1984 in Hillah, nicknamed “the Maestro”. He was a leading figure in the Iraq team that won the 2007 AFC Asian Cup, saw a 2008 move to Manchester City collapse over a work permit, and played for FC Twente in their Dutch title-winning season and for clubs across Iraq, Saudi Arabia, the Emirates, Qatar and China. {cite:wikipedia-nashat-akram-en} {cite:wikipedia-nashat-akram-ar} {cite:wikidata-nashat-akram}

## Career

Akram came through the youth sides of Al-Quwa Al-Jawiya and Al-Zawraa and began his senior career with Salahaddin and Al-Shorta in Iraq, then played for Al-Nassr and Al-Shabab in Saudi Arabia and Al-Ain in the Emirates, who signed him for a million dollars in 2007. In January 2008 he agreed to join Sven-Göran Eriksson's Manchester City after a trial — Eriksson called him an elegant footballer — but the move collapsed when he was refused a work permit, because Iraq stood outside FIFA's top seventy. {cite:wikipedia-nashat-akram-en} {cite:wikipedia-nashat-akram-ar} {cite:wikidata-nashat-akram}

He moved instead to Al-Gharafa in Qatar, then in May 2009 signed for FC Twente; the club won the Dutch league in 2009–10, though injuries limited his part, and he was released in June 2010. Later clubs included Al-Wakrah and Lekhwiya in Qatar, Al-Nasr in Dubai, a return to Al-Shorta, Erbil, and Dalian Yifang in China. For Iraq he was the playmaker of the side that won the 2007 AFC Asian Cup. {cite:wikipedia-nashat-akram-en} {cite:wikipedia-nashat-akram-ar} {cite:wikidata-nashat-akram}
