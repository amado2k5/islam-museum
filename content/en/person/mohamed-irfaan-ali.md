## Summary
Mohamed Irfaan Ali is the president of Guyana, sworn in on 2 August 2020 after the most disputed election in his country's recent history. A People's Progressive Party/Civic (PPP/C) politician of Indo-Guyanese Muslim background, he is Guyana's first Muslim head of state and the youngest president in its history. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-irfaan-ali}

## Life and formation
He was born on 25 April 1980 in Leonora on the West Coast Demerara, into a Muslim family, and grew up playing cricket and football before studying business and municipal management in Guyana and at Anton de Kom University in Suriname, later completing a doctorate in urban and regional planning at the University of the West Indies. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-irfaan-ali}

## Political career
He entered parliament in 2006 and served as minister of housing and water and later of tourism, industry and commerce under presidents Bharrat Jagdeo and Donald Ramotar, credited in his party with expanding house-lot distribution. In opposition after 2015 he became the PPP/C's presidential candidate and was declared winner of the March 2020 election only after a five-month recount and court battle that ended with his August swearing-in. {cite:wikipedia-mohamed-irfaan-ali}

## Presidency
His presidency coincides with Guyana's transformation by offshore oil into one of the world's fastest-growing economies: he has championed a "One Guyana" platform, gas-to-energy projects, infrastructure expansion and greater Caribbean integration, while questions over oil revenue management and the 2020 vote's aftermath remain contested between government and opposition.
