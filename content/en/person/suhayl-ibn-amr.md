## Brief
Suhayl ibn Amr was Quraysh's orator — the voice sent to negotiate. At Hudaybiyya he sat across from the Prophet dictating terms; three years later he was a Muslim, and the tradition remembers him eulogizing the Prophet in Mecca.

## Life
Captured at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and ransomed, Suhayl remained one of Mecca's public faces: when the Prophet came to make pilgrimage in 628, it was Suhayl whom Quraysh sent to turn him back — and who ended by signing the [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|Treaty of Hudaybiyya]], arguing the points clause by clause {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He converted at the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest]]. The tradition records the Prophet saying of him that day: he is a man you will see stand in a station that will gladden you — read as fulfilled when Suhayl steadied Mecca with a speech at the news of the Prophet's death {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He died in the plague of Amwas, about 639 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
The diplomat of the opposition became the orator of the transition — the story of the treaty man who stayed to give the funeral speech.

## For young visitors
Suhayl was sent to argue with the Prophet — and later gave the speech that calmed Mecca when the Prophet died.
