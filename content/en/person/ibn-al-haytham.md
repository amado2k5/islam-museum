## Summary
Al-Hasan ibn al-Haytham, an Arab scholar of mathematics and physics, known for his book [[work/kitab-al-manazir|Kitab al-Manazir]] on [[concept/optics|optics]]. The editor of the book's edition describes him as having lived in the fifth century AH, and holds that his book is the most valuable thing Arab scholars produced in physics {cite:manazir-sabra-1983 p.9}.

## Fourth powers and volume

The [[invention/ibn-al-haytham-fourth-power-volumes|fourth-power method]] differs from the [[invention/camera-obscura|dark-room contribution]]: it connects numerical sums and exhaustion to a solid rotated about an axis perpendicular to the parabola's axis, giving8/15 of its cylinder, not the earlier axial half-cylinder result {cite:katz-calculus-islam-india-1995 pp.165–168} {cite:bressoud-calculus-before-newton-i pp.2–4}. Katz separates the author's n=4 illustration from the modern general derivation. Neither modern integral notation nor all calculus is attributed to him {cite:katz-calculus-islam-india-1995 pp.166–167}. Bressoud finds no evidence of work beyond fourth powers, although the scheme can be extended {cite:bressoud-calculus-before-newton-iii pp.2–3}.

Individual identity evidence remains in the *Optics* opening in his name and TDV's account of writings defending prophethood/discussing Mu'tazilite positions. The Sunni frame is declared, not a documented personal legal school or an inference from Fatimid patronage {cite:manazir-sabra-1983 scan-p.60} {cite:tdv-ibnul-heysem pp.82–87}. Cairo is biographical context, not a verified composition location; European transmission of summation ideas remains a research question in Katz's account {cite:katz-calculus-islam-india-1995 pp.166,174}. ## For Sprouts
A clever scientist who studied light through experiments and discovered how our eyes see the world around us.

## For Explorers
Al-Hasan ibn al-Haytham was a pioneering physicist, mathematician, and optics scholar who lived in Basra and Cairo. In his famous Book of Optics, he relied on rigorous scientific experimentation and geometric proofs, demonstrating that vision occurs when reflected light enters the eye, laying the foundation for modern cameras and lenses.

## For Scholars
Abu Ali al-Hasan ibn al-Haytham (c. 354–430 AH / 965–1040 CE), a preeminent physicist, mathematician, and astronomer of Basra and Fatimid Cairo. Celebrated as the father of modern optics for his seminal 'Kitab al-Manazir' (Book of Optics), he refuted ancient emission theories and established that vision results from light rays entering the eye from illuminated objects. His rigorous commitment to controlled empirical experimentation, mathematical optics, refraction, and camera obscura principles laid the bedrock of the modern scientific method.
