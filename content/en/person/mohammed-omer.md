## Summary
Mohammed Omer is a Palestinian journalist who began reporting from Rafah in the southern Gaza Strip as a teenager and became one of the youngest recipients of the Martha Gellhorn Prize for Journalism, in 2008. {cite:muslim500-mohammed-omer} {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-omer}

## Formation
Born in 1984 in Rafah, he worked as a translator and fixer for foreign reporters before reporting under his own name for outlets including the Washington Report on Middle East Affairs, Al Jazeera and the Electronic Intifada. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-omer}

## Career
His dispatches documented daily life under blockade and military operations; returning from receiving the Gellhorn Prize in London in 2008 he was detained at the Allenby crossing, an incident that drew international attention to journalists' treatment. He later published Shell Shocked ⁦(2015)⁩, an on-the-ground account of the 2014 Gaza war. {cite:muslim500-mohammed-omer} {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-omer}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him under Media as Gaza's emblematic freelance war reporter. His work's perspective — reporting from inside Gaza under bombardment — is praised by press-freedom organisations and contested by critics of his outlets' editorial lines; the museum records positions attributed. {cite:muslim500-mohammed-omer}
