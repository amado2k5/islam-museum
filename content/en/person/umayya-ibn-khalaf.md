## Brief
Umayya ibn Khalaf was a headman of Banu Jumah — the master who tortured the enslaved Bilal on the burning sands of Mecca, and one of the chiefs killed at Badr.

## Life
The sources preserve Umayya above all in one scene: his slave [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal]] staked out on the roasting ground, a rock on his chest, until [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] arrived, paid over the odds, and freed him {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. It is one of the earliest documented confrontations of the story — owner against convert, wealth against faith {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

At Badr he marched with the Meccan elite and was killed — the tradition records the Ansari fighters settling with him for that morning on the sands {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

## Legacy
In the museum's telling, Umayya stands opposite Bilal: the man who owned a human being, against the voice that called millions to prayer. His name survives mainly as a measure of how high the cost of belief ran in the first years.

## For young visitors
Umayya tortured his own slave for believing. The slave's name is remembered with honor today — and the master's mostly is not.
