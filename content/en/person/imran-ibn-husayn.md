## In brief
Imran b. al-Husayn b. Ubayd b. Khalaf, Abu Nujayd al-Khuza'i, Companion of the Messenger of God; his conversion came at the time of Abu Hurayra's, and he was among those Umar b. al-Khattab sent to the people of Basra to teach them fiqh, and he held the judgeship of Basra. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.32}

## His standing
He was among those whom the angels greeted; he suffered hemorrhoids and was cauterised for them. Zurara b. Awfa, al-Hasan, Muhammad b. Sirin, Zuhdam al-Jarmi, Amir al-Sha'bi, Ibn Burayda, Mutarrif b. Abdallah b. al-Shikhkhir, Abu Raja' al-Utaridi, and others narrated from him; he was among the long-lived Companions who lived sixty-seven years. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.32}

## His death
He died in 52 AH. {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.1 p.32}

## In Khalid Muhammad Khalid's Rijal Hawl al-Rasul
[[person/khalid-muhammad-khalid|Khalid Muhammad Khalid]] titles his chapter on him in [[work/rijal-hawl-al-rasul|Rijal Hawl al-Rasul]] "Like the angels", and says he came to the Prophet to pledge allegiance in the year of Khaybar and vowed, from the moment he put his right hand in the Prophet's, to use it only for good and noble deeds; yet he would weep and say, "Would that I were ashes scattered by the wind." {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.549} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.550} The author links his life to the hadith of "an hour and an hour", in which the Prophet told his Companions that if they remained always as they were in his presence the angels would shake their hands. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.550} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.551}
He records that Umar ibn al-Khattab sent him to [[place/basra|Basra]] to teach its people the religion, and that [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]] and [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Ibn Sirin]] said no Companion of the Messenger of God who came to Basra was more excellent than Imran ibn Husayn. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.551} When the great conflict broke out between the parties of Ali and Mu'awiya he called on people to stay out of the fighting, saying that to herd a few goats on a mountaintop until death overtook him was dearer to him than to shoot one arrow at either side, whether it hit or missed; he advised: "Keep to your mosque; if they come in upon you, keep to your house." {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.551} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.552} He suffered a painful illness for thirty years without complaint, saying, "The dearest thing to me is what is dearest to God," and told his family, "When you come back from burying me, slaughter and feed people." {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.552}
