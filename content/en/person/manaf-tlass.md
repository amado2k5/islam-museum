## Overview
Manaf Mustafa Tlass (born 1964) is a former brigadier general of the Syrian Republican Guard who was a member of Bashar al-Assad's inner circle before defecting in July 2012 and declaring support for the opposition — the first Republican Guard commander to defect from the Syrian military. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar} He is the son of Mustafa Tlass, Syria's former defence minister and a pillar of the Hafez al-Assad era. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar}

## Early life and education
Tlass was born in al-Rastan in 1964, the son of Mustafa Tlass and Lamia al-Jabiri, a member of an aristocratic Aleppo family. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar} The articles describe the Tlass family as the most famous Sunni family in Syria, known for supporting the government, with members who had also worked for the Ottoman authorities and then for the French after the First World War. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} His elder brother is the businessman Firas Tlass. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar}

He was a close friend of Bassel al-Assad, Hafez al-Assad's eldest son and heir apparent until his death in a 1994 car accident, and later became close to Bashar al-Assad, having attended military college with him; Bashar regarded the Tlass brothers as peers and friends. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} In 1980 he took part in a military training camp organised by the Revolutionary Youth Union, and afterwards attended Damascus University for civil engineering. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en}

## Career
After Hafez al-Assad's death in 2000, Tlass became one of Bashar al-Assad's closest aides and a member of the Ba'ath Party's central committee the same year; he was re-elected to it in June 2005 and was regarded as a potential future leadership candidate. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar} He tried to widen Assad's base of support by introducing him to members of the Sunni merchant class, and advocated reform as early as 2005 while stressing that Assad was the best hope for it. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en}

Tlass was promoted to one-star general in the Republican Guard, one of the core units used against the uprising that began in 2011, and commanded the 104th Brigade, stationed in Douma and Harasta, alongside Brigadier General Issam Zahreddine — the brigade previously led by Bashar al-Assad before his presidency and by Bassel al-Assad until his death. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar}

## Defection
The articles report that Tlass grew increasingly frustrated with the security forces' violent crackdown on protesters, that he was the first government official to meet the opposition, in March 2011, seeking dialogue and a political solution, and that he took part in reconciliation efforts in rural Damascus — Douma, Daraa, al-Tall, Homs and his home town al-Rastan — efforts said to have led to his house arrest from May 2011 until his defection. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar}

In early July 2012 Tlass was reported to have defected to Turkey with 23 other officers after Syrian intelligence discovered his opposition links; Syrian National Council member Bashar al-Heraki stated that Tlass was one of the government's main figures and that his defection signalled Bashar al-Assad's waning power. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} On 6 July 2012 French foreign minister Laurent Fabius stated that Tlass was on his way to Paris to join his family; his wife and son, who were in Beirut when he left Damascus, followed him to Paris. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en}

## After the defection: public appearances
President François Hollande confirmed on 17 July 2012 that Tlass was in Paris, and the same day Tlass published a statement through Agence France-Presse calling for "a constructive transition" in Syria and saying the Syrian army had fought against the Syrian people. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} On 24 July 2012 he appeared in a video address broadcast from Saudi Arabia — his first public appearance since defecting — describing himself as "a defected member of the Syrian army, who refuses criminal violence" and calling on Syrians to unite for a "free and democratic Syria" not built on revenge, exclusion or monopoly. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar}

Tlass visited Saudi Arabia in the last week of July 2012, in a visit organised by Prince Bandar bin Sultan; he performed the Umrah rituals in Mecca, confirmed his defection in an exclusive video for the Al Arabiya channel and gave his first interview to the newspaper Asharq Al-Awsat. He then went to Turkey on 26 July 2012, meeting foreign minister Ahmet Davutoğlu and intelligence undersecretary Hakan Fidan. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section presents positions reported in the sources; it is not the museum's verdict on any of them.

Activists' accounts of Tlass's role in the uprising differ: some argued he had been under house arrest and exempted from army duty since 2011, while others stated the family was under strict supervision for a time over suspected sympathy with the uprising. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} Sources close to Tlass quoted him accusing Assad's government of "taking the country to hell" and saying that in Assad's place he would have "done an Atatürk" or resigned in the uprising's second month. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} His cousin Abdul Razzak Tlass announced that Manaf had supplied him and several Free Syrian Army units with arms against the military campaign on al-Rastan, and his brother Firas declared his support for the opposition and admitted offering humanitarian and relief aid to the Farouq Brigades, commanded by their cousin. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar}

## Personal life
Both the English and the Arabic articles state that Tlass is a Sunni Muslim. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en} {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-ar} He is married to Tala Kheir, from the Damascus upper middle class, and they have two sons and a daughter. {cite:wikipedia-manaf-tlass-en}
