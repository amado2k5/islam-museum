## Overview
Abu Ya'qub Yusuf ibn Abd al-Mu'min, the second Almohad caliph, son of [[person/abd-al-mumin-ibn-ali|Abd al-Mu'min ibn Ali]]. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.130}

## Taking power
He deposed his brother Abu Abd Allah Muhammad, who had been heir apparent and whose name was struck on the dinars, in Sha'ban 558 AH, and he held power alone. He appointed his brother Abu Hafs Umar over the island of al-Andalus. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.130}

## His character
Ibn Khallikan relates that he grew up among knights and scholars. He was a faqih and a memorizer, with an inclination toward wisdom and philosophy more than toward literature. The Yusufi dinars are attributed to him in the Maghrib. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.130-132}

## Al-Andalus and Ifriqiya
He entered al-Andalus in 566 AH with a hundred thousand horsemen and camped at Seville. He began to recover Muslim lands from the Franks, besieged Toledo and then withdrew. He took Gafsa in 575 AH. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.131} {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.383}

## His death
He crossed to al-Andalus in 580 AH and besieged Santarem for about a month. He fell ill and died in Rabi al-Awwal of the same year, and was carried to Seville. It is said he was struck by an arrow. Ibn al-Athir relates that he died without a will, so the commanders and the sons of Abd al-Mu'min made his son Ya'qub king. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.132}
