## Summary

Ghiyas ud din Balban (1216 - 13 January 1287), styled Ulugh Khan before his kingship, was the ninth sultan of the Mamluk Sultanate of Delhi and its most powerful ruler. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} An Ilbari Turk captured by the Mongols as a boy, he was bought by Iltutmish in 1232 and enrolled among the Forty, the leading Mamluk officers. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ur} He then rose to be deputy and vizier under Sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud, took the throne in 1266, and reigned for twenty-one years, restoring the sultanate's authority. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ar}

## From slavery to the vizierate

Balban was born in 1216 according to the English article, which is consistent with its statement that he took the throne in 1266 at the age of fifty, while the Urdu article gives 1200 and the Wikidata item gives 1201. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ur} {cite:wikidata-ghiyas-ud-din-balban} He was the son of an Ilbari chief, was captured by the Mongols as a child, and was raised by Khawaja Jamal-ud-din of Basra, whom the texts describe as a righteous man, before Iltutmish bought him at Delhi in 1232. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ur} After Iltutmish's death Balban climbed the ladder of office until he became deputy to Sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud and then his all-powerful vizier, bearing the title Ulugh Khan. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ar}

## His kingship and the breaking of the Forty

Balban took the throne in 1266 after Mahmud's death, and his first act was to break the power of the Forty, who had previously controlled the throne. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} He crushed the revolt of the Mewatis and the highwaymen who plundered the roads of the Doab, restoring security around the capital, and rebuilt the frontier fortresses to defend the sultanate against the Mongols. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} He subdued Bengal in 1280 after its governor's rebellion, restoring Delhi's control of the province. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en}

## Kingship doctrine and court

Balban's doctrine of kingship held that the sultan is the shadow of God on earth; he imposed at his court the ceremonies of prostration and foot-kissing, and celebrated Nowruz as a royal festival. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} Khawaja Jamal-ud-din of Basra, who bought and raised him, is described as a Sufi, which is a description of the master rather than of the slave who became sultan, and the texts read name no Sufi order or legal school for Balban himself. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en}

## Death

The greatest tragedy of his reign was the killing of his eldest son Muhammad, whom he had designated his heir, by the Mongols in a frontier attack. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ar} Balban then bequeathed the throne to his grandson Kai Khusrau, Muhammad's son, over his surviving son Bughra Khan, and died on 13 January 1287 according to the English article and the Wikidata item, a date the Arabic article supports by placing his death at the end of 685 AH, corresponding to 1287. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-en} {cite:wikidata-ghiyas-ud-din-balban} {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ar} The 13th of January 1287 corresponds to 21 Dhu al-Hijja 685 AH. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ar} The Urdu article, by contrast, ends his reign in 1286, a year-level variant that is noted but not followed. {cite:wikipedia-ghiyas-ud-din-balban-ur}
