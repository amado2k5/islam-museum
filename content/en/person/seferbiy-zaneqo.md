# Seferbiy Zaneqo

Seferbiy Zaneqo (1798 – 1 January 1860) was a Circassian prince, diplomat and resistance leader of the Zan family of Anapa, the son of the wealthy merchant Mehmed Giray Bey. The English and Russian accounts agree on his birth in 1798 near Anapa; his own tribal origin is reported differently, as Natukhay in some accounts and as Khegayk in others.{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-en}{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-ru}

Sent to Russia as a hostage in 1807, he was educated at the Richelieu Lyceum in Odessa and entered Russian service as a cadet, then fled to the mountains with his brother. The English account, following James Bell, places him for a time among the Circassian Mamluks in Egypt; he then joined the Ottoman service, became a colonel and deputy to Hajji Hassan Pasha, governor of Anapa, and was taken prisoner when Anapa fell in 1828 and held at Odessa.{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-en}{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-ru}

In the spring of 1831 he led a Circassian delegation of some two hundred men to Constantinople to seek Ottoman and British support, settled at Samsun, and worked with the British agent David Urquhart; the seizure of the British schooner Vixen in November 1836, in which he was involved, became an international incident. Exiled to Edirne after Russian protests, he returned to prominence during the Crimean War, when the Ottomans appointed him governor (vali) of Circassia with the honorary rank of pasha in 1854.{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-en}{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-tr}

From 1854 he led the Circassian resistance in the western Caucasus, clashing repeatedly with Muhammad Amin, the naib of Imam Shamil, whose authority he rejected in favour of an Ottoman-aligned national leadership. In 1859 he headed the Circassian Confederation, and he is credited with the design of the Circassian national flag. He died on 1 January 1860 in Shapsughia and was buried in the Vordobgach valley; his son Karabatir continued the resistance after him.{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-en}{cite:wikipedia-seferbiy-zaneqo-ru}

The English, Russian, Turkish and Arabic accounts — the Arabic a translation of the English — and the Wikidata item agree on the outline of his life, including the birth year 1798.{cite:wikidata-seferbiy-zaneqo} None of them names a school or order for Zaneqo personally: the Sufi movement and the imamate appear in them as the context of the wider resistance, not as his affiliation, so his faith is recorded as unknown.
