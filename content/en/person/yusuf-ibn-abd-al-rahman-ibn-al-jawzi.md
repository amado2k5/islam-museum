## Overview
Yusuf ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Ali Ibn al-Jawzi al-Qurashi al-Taymi al-Bakri al-Baghdadi, Muhyi al-Din Abu al-Mahasin, was ustadh al-dar (steward of the household) and envoy of the caliphate of al-Musta'sim, a man of Baghdad; the entry gives 580 to 656 AH, 1185 to 1258 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 235}. He was the son of the great scholar Abu al-Faraj Ibn al-Jawzi, who died when Yusuf was seventeen; the mother of the caliph al-Nasir then took him under her care, and he studied jurisprudence under his father and others {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 235}.

## Offices and embassies
He held the office of hisba on both sides of Baghdad and the oversight of public endowments, and the chancery letters sent to Egypt, Anatolia, Syria, the East, Mosul and the Jazira were several times written by him; he related hadith in Baghdad, Egypt and elsewhere {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 235}. The caliph al-Mustansir sent him on a mission to Aleppo in 634 AH, and its king died; to Anatolia, and its sultan died; to al-Malik al-Ashraf in 635, and he died; and to Ashraf's brother al-Adil, who also died. People came to take his arrival as an ill omen, and Abu al-Qasim al-Sinjari wrote two verses asking the caliph to be gentle, saying he had sent among them a messenger whose envoy was Azra'il, the angel of death {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 235}.

## Schools and books
He founded the Jawziyya school in Damascus and was appointed to teach at the Mustansiriyya in Baghdad, and later became ustadh al-dar of the caliphal household in the days of al-Musta'sim {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 235}. His books include "Ma'adin al-Ibriz fi Tafsir al-Kitab al-Aziz," "al-Madhhab al-Ahmad fi Madhhab Ahmad" (printed) and "al-Idah" on dialectic; he also wrote good verse {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 235}.

## Death
The Mongols killed him, in al-Zirikli's words as a martyr, bound and helpless, with his three sons, on the day Hulagu entered Baghdad, outside the wall of Kalwadha {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 235}.
