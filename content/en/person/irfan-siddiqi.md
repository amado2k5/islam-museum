## Summary
Irfan Siddiqi is a Pakistani-American experimental physicist whose work on measuring quantum systems has helped build the measurement science on which today's superconducting quantum computers stand. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology, citing foundational contributions to quantum measurement and the invention of the Josephson bifurcation amplifier; Wikipedia records his professorship at the University of California, Berkeley and his position at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. {cite:muslim500-irfan-siddiqi} {cite:wikipedia-irfan-siddiqi}

## Formation
Siddiqi was born in Karachi, Pakistan, educated at the University of Cambridge and at Harvard, and took postdoctoral work at Yale — a trajectory through the laboratories where superconducting circuits were being turned into artificial atoms. He joined the Berkeley physics faculty in 2006 and built the Quantum Nanoelectronics Laboratory there. {cite:wikipedia-irfan-siddiqi}

## Science
His laboratory specialises in the hardest part of quantum information: reading a quantum state without destroying it. The Josephson bifurcation amplifier he developed turned a Josephson junction's nonlinear oscillation into an ultra-sensitive detector able to read out superconducting qubits at nearly the quantum limit — work that became part of the measurement toolkit of the field and fed directly into the hardware of quantum computing. {cite:wikipedia-irfan-siddiqi} {cite:muslim500-irfan-siddiqi}

## Honours
His recognitions include the American Physical Society's George E. Valley Jr. Prize and fellowship of the APS, and he directs the Advanced Quantum Testbed at Berkeley Lab — a national facility where new quantum processors are characterised and improved. {cite:wikipedia-irfan-siddiqi}
