## Summary
Haidar Bagir built Mizan, the Bandung publishing house that from the 1980s made serious Islamic books — theology, philosophy, Sufism — bestselling reading for Indonesia's new Muslim middle class, and he has remained one of the country's most public scholar-entrepreneurs. {cite:muslim500-haidar-bagir} {cite:wikipedia-haidar-bagir}

## Formation
Born in 1957 in Solo, Central Java, into a family of hadith scholars, he studied at IAIN Syarif Hidayatullah in Jakarta and later earned a doctorate from the University of Indonesia's philosophy programme; he also undertook research at Harvard's Center for Middle Eastern Studies. {cite:wikipedia-haidar-bagir}

## Career
He founded Mizan in 1983, growing it into a group spanning general and Islamic publishing, film and education. Alongside publishing he wrote books on Islamic philosophy and Sufism, taught, and directed philanthropic ventures in education and community development through family foundations and the Mizan network. {cite:muslim500-haidar-bagir} {cite:wikipedia-haidar-bagir}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him under Philanthropy, Charity and Development as the publisher who most shaped how urban Indonesia reads Islam — scholar, businessman and patron in one career. {cite:muslim500-haidar-bagir}
