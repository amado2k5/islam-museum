## Summary
Hatim ibn Arif al-Awni is a contemporary Meccan hadith scholar and professor of hadith sciences at Umm al-Qura University. He represents a critical current within Saudi hadith scholarship, calling for rereading the rules of narrator evaluation in light of the early critics’ practice. {cite:wikipedia-hatim-al-awni}

## Life and upbringing
Born in 1965, he studied in Saudi religious institutes and universities to a doctorate in hadith sciences, reading under the shaykhs of Mecca and Madinah. {cite:wikipedia-hatim-al-awni}

## Career and works
He teaches hadith sciences at Umm al-Qura University and served a term on the Saudi Shura Council. His books include al-Mursal al-Khafi on a subtle class of interrupted hadiths, Khulasat al-Ta’sil on hadith sciences, and al-Manhaj al-Muqtarah on understanding prophetic traditions. {cite:wikipedia-hatim-al-awni}

## Method and contribution
His thesis holds that many later rules of hadith criticism grew stricter than early practice, so he argues for broader acceptance of hasan hadith and easing where later scholars hardened. Some researchers welcome this while other hadith scholars object that it is lax; the museum reports the disagreement attributed, without adjudicating. {cite:wikipedia-hatim-al-awni}
