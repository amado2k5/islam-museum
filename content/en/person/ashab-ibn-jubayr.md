## Overview
Ash'ab ibn Jubayr of Medina, known as Ash'ab the Covetous and as Ibn Umm Hayda, is the proverb for greed according to al-Dhahabi {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1138}. The literary sources add the kunya Abu al-Ala and place him among the jesters of Medina; the card's earlier summary draws on al-Nuwayri's Nihayat al-Arab.

## Teachers and students
He narrated little from Ikrima, Salim and Aban ibn Uthman {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1138}. Ma'di ibn Sulayman and Abu Asim al-Nabil narrated from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1138}. Al-Dhahabi says he was a man of jesting and uninvited visits, and yet lies were told about him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1138}. It is said he came as a delegate to al-Walid ibn Yazid, and it is said Ash'ab was the maternal uncle of al-Asma'i {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1138}.

## Anecdotes as al-Dhahabi gives them
Abu Asim relates that Ash'ab narrated from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas that God has two favours on His servant, then fell silent; asked to name them, he said that one Ikrima had forgotten and the other was himself {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1138}. In al-Zubayr's account, when asked whether they should marry him, he said to find him a woman in whose face he could belch and she be sated, and who ate a locust's thigh and was overfull {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1139}. Abu Asim says Ibn Jurayj asked him how far his greed went, and he answered that no bride was ever led to her husband but he swept his house hoping a gift would reach him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1139}. Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Muqri reports him saying that he never went out in a funeral and saw two people whispering without supposing the dead man had left him something in his will {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1139}. It is also said he sang well {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1139}.

## His death
It is said he died in 154 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1139}. Al-Nuwayri instead says he lived on until the days of al-Mahdi; the two reports differ and the museum shows both without choosing.
