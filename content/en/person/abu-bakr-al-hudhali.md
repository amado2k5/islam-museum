## Overview
Salama ibn Abdallah, Abu Bakr al-Hudhali, was a Basran, a scholar of the days of the Arabs and their accounts, and one of the hadith transmitters. Al-Safadi gives his death as 159 AH, which is 775 or 776 CE on the usual tables (derived) {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 pp. 201–202}.

## Life
He met al-Zuhri, al-Hasan al-Basri and Muhammad ibn Sirin, was in the company of the caliph al-Mansur, and was a learned transmitter of historical reports (akhbari) {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 pp. 201–202}. Al-Safadi adds that Yaqut gave the name as Salama, while the Shaykh Shams al-Din gave it as Salma ibn Abdallah ibn Salma {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 pp. 201–202}.

## Judgements on him
Al-Safadi reports that Yahya al-Qattan was not satisfied with him, that Ibn Ma'in said he was worth nothing, that Ahmad called him weak, that al-Bukhari said he was not a reliable memoriser, and that Ibn Majah transmitted from him {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 15 pp. 201–202}.
