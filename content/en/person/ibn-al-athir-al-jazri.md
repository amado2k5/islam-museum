## Overview
[[person/ibn-al-athir-al-jazri|Ibn al-Athir al-Jazari]], Abu al-Sa'adat al-Mubarak ibn Muhammad, a scholar of hadith and of language, author of *al-Nihaya fi Gharib al-Hadith wa al-Athar* (The Final Word on Obscure Hadith and Report), which is his most important work, and of *Jami' al-Usul fi Ahadith al-Rasul* (Compendium of the Roots in the Hadiths of the Messenger). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.268} {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.269}

## His brother
His brother was Izz al-Din Ibn al-Athir the historian, author of *al-Kamil fi al-Tarikh* (The Complete History). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.268} The book states that Ibn al-Athir al-Jazari himself was a Shafi'i. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.268}

## His work and illness
He held the diwan of al-Jazira, and then became one of the companions of Izz al-Din Mas'ud ibn Mawdud in Mosul, who appointed him head of the chancery (diwan al-insha'). After Mas'ud's death the diwan passed to his son Nur al-Din Arslan Shah. Gout kept him from writing, so he stayed at home, visited by scholars and students. It is said that he completed most of his works while ill, dictating them to his pupils. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.268}

## From the editor's biography in Jami' al-Usul
In his introduction to [[work/jami-al-usul|Jami' al-Usul]], 'Abd al-Qadir al-Arna'ut gives his full name: Majd al-Din Abu al-Sa'adat al-Mubarak b. Muhammad b. Muhammad b. 'Abd al-Karim b. 'Abd al-Wahid al-Shaybani al-Jazari, later of Mosul, known as Ibn al-Athir. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 17}

**Birth and upbringing:** he was born in one of the two months of Rabi' in 544 AH at [[place/jazirat-ibn-umar|Jazirat Ibn 'Umar]], a town above Mosul three days' journey away, as Yaqut says; he grew up there and studied Qur'an commentary, hadith, grammar, lexicography and fiqh with its scholars, then moved in 565 AH to [[place/mosul|Mosul]], where he stayed until his death. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 17}

**Teachers:** he read literature with Nasih al-Din Sa'id b. al-Mubarak Ibn al-Dahhan of Baghdad, [[person/al-azdi-yahya-ibn-sadun|Abu Bakr Yahya b. Sa'dun of Cordoba]] and the blind grammarian Abu al-Hazm Makki b. al-Rayyan; he heard hadith in Mosul from its preacher Abu al-Fadl 'Abd Allah b. Ahmad al-Tusi and others, and in Baghdad, which he visited on pilgrimage, from 'Abd al-Wahhab b. Sukayna and others, then returned to Mosul and taught there. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 17}

**Service in the chanceries:** Yaqut reports from his brother Abu al-Hasan that he held the treasury for Sayf al-Din Ghazi b. Mawdud, then the chancery of Jazirat Ibn 'Umar, then deputised in the chancery at Mosul for the vizier Jalal al-Din al-Isfahani, then served [[person/mujahid-al-din-qaymaz|Mujahid al-Din Qaymaz]], the regent; when Qaymaz was arrested in 589 AH he entered the service of the atabeg [[person/izz-al-din-masud|'Izz al-Din Mas'ud b. Mawdud]] and then of his son [[person/nur-al-din-arslan-shah|Nur al-Din Arslan Shah]], becoming the leading man of the state — the sultan would come to his house once he could no longer walk. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 18}

**Declining the vizierate:** he was offered the vizierate more than once and refused, explaining that he was old and had served learning all his life; that even with his utmost effort he could not do justice its due; that if a ploughman were wronged in the farthest corner of the sultan's lands the wrong would be laid at his door; and that kingship cannot be run without some harshness that did not befit him — so the sultan excused him. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 18-19}

**Illness and retirement:** when he was crippled late in life, a Maghribi treated him with an ointment of his own making that began to work; but he asked his brother [[person/ibn-al-athir-izz-al-din|'Izz al-Din 'Ali]] to pay the man and send him away, saying he was now free of the company of emirs and sultans, who came to him rather than he to them, and wished to live out his days free and spared humiliation; he kept to his house, visited by scholars and rulers, until he died in Mosul in 606 AH. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 19}

**Works:** among them *al-Nihaya* on the rare words of hadith in alphabetical order; *al-Shafi fi Sharh Musnad al-Shafi'i*; *al-Insaf bayn al-Kashf wa-al-Kashshaf*, combining the Qur'an commentaries of al-Tha'labi and al-Zamakhshari; *al-Badi'* and *al-Bahir fi al-Furuq* on grammar; *Tahdhib Fusul Ibn al-Dahhan*; treatises on arithmetic; and *Jami' al-Usul fi Ahadith al-Rasul*. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 17-18}
