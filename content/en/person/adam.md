## Overview
Abu al-Bashar, the father of humankind. Al-Bukhari relates that God created him, that he was sixty cubits tall, and that God ordered him to greet the angels. What he heard of their greeting was his own greeting and the greeting of his descendants. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari-bugha 3148}

In the hadith of the Night Journey, the Prophet saw him in the lowest heaven, and said to him: "Welcome, son and prophet." {cite:sahih-al-bukhari-bugha 3035}

The Encyclopaedia of Islam article on Adam sets out the Qur'anic account, the later Muslim narrations and the scholarly comparison in the sections below.

## Adam in the Qur'an, as the Encyclopaedia of Islam summarises it
The Encyclopaedia of Islam article on Adam, by J. Pedersen, opens with the Qur'anic account as Muslims read it. God tells the [[concept/malaika|angels]] that He is about to place a substitute, a khalifa, on earth. The angels ask whether He will put there one who will do harm and shed blood, while they themselves praise and sanctify Him. God then teaches Adam the names of all things. When the angels do not know the names, Adam teaches them. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

God orders the angels to prostrate themselves before Adam, and they do so, except [[person/iblis|Iblis]], who answers out of pride that he is superior because he was made of fire while Adam was made of clay. Iblis is expelled from the garden. Adam and his wife are placed there to live pleasantly, with one order: not to come near a certain tree. Satan makes them slip and removes them from their state. He whispers to them, according to the article's summary, that the tree was forbidden lest they become angels and live for ever. They eat, become aware of their nakedness and cover themselves with the leaves of the garden. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

God then sends them down to earth. When Adam asks for forgiveness, God promises him guidance. The article adds that the Qur'an speaks of a covenant God made with Adam, which Adam forgot, and of God's reminder to the children of Adam not to serve Satan. It notes that God chose Adam, as later He chose [[person/nuh|Nuh]] and the families of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]] and Imran, and that only [[person/isa-ibn-maryam|Isa]] was, like Adam, created in a special way. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

## Later Muslim tradition: the stories of the prophets
The article explains that after the Qur'an the stories about Adam grew. They are found in hadith collections, in collections of stories of the prophets, in works of general history and in commentaries on the Qur'an. The author says these stories also reflect, to a great extent, Jewish and Christian influence; that is his academic assessment, and the article's account of the narrations follows. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

**The clay and the body.** According to the narrations, God sent [[person/jibril|Gabriel]] and then Michael to take a handful of clay, but the earth refused. The [[concept/malak-al-mawt|angel of death]] was sent and took red, white and black clay by force, which is given as the reason people differ in colour. Adam is said to be named from the surface of the earth, adim. The clay was kneaded until it became sticky, then slimy and stinking, and at last a body of dry clay. Some authors add that Iblis entered Adam's mouth and came out again, and the reverse. God then blew the spirit into him; it passed from the brain to the eyes, the nose and the whole body, which became flesh, blood, bone, veins and sinews. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

A tradition ascribed to the Prophet is reported in which the dust for the head came from the Kaaba, for the breast and back from [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]], and for the thighs from [[place/yemen|Yemen]], among other places. The article cites [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]], [[person/al-masudi|al-Mas'udi]], al-Kisa'i and [[person/abu-ishaq-al-thalabi|al-Tha'labi]] for these accounts. The beauty and the length of Adam's body are mentioned in Muslim tradition, with al-Tha'labi as the cited source. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

**The tempter, the serpent and the tree.** The article points out that the Qur'an speaks only of Satan as the one who led Adam and his wife astray, while Muslim tradition also brings in the serpent. In al-Tabari's commentary the serpent speaks by order of Iblis, or Iblis is carried into the garden in its mouth or belly. In the story collections of al-Kisa'i and al-Tha'labi, a peacock appears: Iblis, kept out by God, tells the peacock that all creatures will die and that he can show the tree of eternity; the peacock tells the serpent, Iblis rushes into the serpent's mouth, enters the garden and speaks through it to Adam and Eve, and Eve eats of the tree. The fruit is variously named, and the article says the same opinions are found in Jewish, Christian and Muslim sources. For Eve it refers readers to a separate entry; the museum record is [[person/hawwa|Hawwa']]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

**The descent.** Because Adam was told to go down to earth, paradise was thought to be in heaven. The article reports al-Tabari saying that the tradition placing Adam in India was refuted by no Muslim, Christian or Jewish scholar. The most common tradition is that Adam came down in Ceylon, [[place/sarandib|Sarandib]]; Eve in [[place/jeddah|Jidda]]; Iblis in [[place/baysan|Baysan]], or Maysan or [[place/ubulla|Ubulla]]; and the serpent in [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]] or in the desert. Adam and Eve later met at [[place/muzdalifa|Muzdalifa]] and [[place/arafat|Arafa]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

**Pilgrimage, crafts and languages.** In connection with this, the article says, it is told that Adam carried out the ceremonies of the pilgrimage, that the Black Stone was sent to him from heaven, and that he then built the [[building/kaaba|Kaaba]]. With Eve he is also said to have learned the use of fire, agriculture and handicraft, and the article remarks that this tradition is of Jewish origin. Al-Tha'labi is quoted as saying that Adam even coined dirhams and dinars. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

Following on the naming, it is said that Adam learned all nouns, greetings and religious formulas. The article reports differing statements about his language: al-Halabi says he spoke Arabic in Paradise but Syriac on earth, and wrote the twelve known kinds of writing; al-Kisa'i says he spoke seven hundred languages, the best of which was Arabic. A further sentence about books attributed to him is cut off in the OCR text and is not given here. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

**Children.** When Adam and Eve were united they had children, first [[person/qabil|Kabil]] and [[person/habil|Habil]], each born with a twin sister. Adam married each to the other brother's twin, which made Kabil jealous, and he killed Habil. Shith, born without a sister, was Adam's favourite and his spiritual heir, his wasi. Adam had many other children, one of them named Abd al-Harith. Al-Tha'labi says Eve bore a boy and a girl twenty times, and that Adam's offspring numbered forty thousand before he died. The article adds that al-Halabi names five gods of the Arabs as sons of Adam, whose images Iblis made and later generations worshipped. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

**David's years.** God rubbed Adam's back and all his offspring appeared to him, among them [[person/dawud|David]]. When Adam learned that David would live only a short time, he gave him forty years of his own lifetime, or fifty or seventy in other versions, and so fell short of the thousand destined for him. The article cites al-Tabari, Ibn Sa'd and al-Tha'labi. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

**Date, death and burial.** According to the tradition cited from al-Tabari, al-Mas'udi and [[person/al-yaqubi|al-Ya'qubi]], Adam was created on a Friday, the sixth of Nisan, year one; he was expelled the same day, and died on a Friday on the same date of the month. He was buried with Eve in a cave called Maghara al-Kunuz, at the foot of [[place/jabal-abi-qubays|Abu Qubays]] near [[place/mecca|Mecca]]. Al-Tha'labi relates that after the flood he was brought to Jerusalem. The article notes that a Christian tradition takes him from the ark to Golgotha, the centre of the earth, and that a chapel of Adam is situated in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

## Academic comparison with Jewish and Christian traditions
This section gives the observations of the article's author, not the Muslim account. He holds that the non-Biblical elements of the Qur'anic story have parallels in Jewish and, in some cases, Christian tradition. The conversation between God and the angels before Adam's creation, and Adam's superiority through his knowledge of the names, are known, the author says, from Jewish works such as Bereshit Rabba. The prostration of the angels is not commanded by God in Jewish writings; in them the angels wish to honour Adam as if he were God and are prevented when God makes Adam sleep. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

The author also cites Christian parallels: Athanasius mentions, and rejects, the idea that Satan fell because he refused to prostrate before Adam; the Vita Adami, of uncertain origin, has the angel Michael prostrate himself and call on the others; and the Syriac Cave of Treasures has God give Adam power over all beings, with the angels worshipping him except the jealous devil, who is turned out of the heavens. A covenant with Adam and his remorse are mentioned in Jewish writings and in Augustine. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196}

For the later narrations, the author compares the clay taken from the place of the temple or from the whole world in Jewish tradition, with Adam first shaped as a lifeless body, and notes a similar Christian tradition in Cyprian and Augustine. He compares the serpent, through whose mouth Satan speaks in Christian tradition, with the Muslim serpent; the fruit named in Jewish sources as grape, fig or wheat; the gift of life years to David in Jewish tradition; and the burial at Golgotha. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 196} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

## Adam as first prophet, and in Sab'iyya and Sufi thought
The article says Adam was not only the first man but also the first prophet, and that his position was shaped by Muslim ways of thinking. Just as Jesus was the second Adam in Christianity, a connection was made in Islam between Adam and [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]], with Adam as the first and Muhammad as the last messenger, rasul. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

The article then describes ideas that it assigns to the Sab'iyya system and to Sufi writing. They are presented as the ideas of particular systems, not as the general Muslim account, and are labelled as such here. In the Sab'iyya system Adam is the first of the seven natiqs, and some say that men and natiqs existed before him; Seth was his wasi. The system distinguishes a universal Adam, identical with the intellect, from whom emanation began, and a particular Adam, the first in the period of veiling. It is the ideal Adam before whom the angels prostrated, because God's spirit was in him. The article says this is sometimes described as an incarnation, hulul, continued by transmigration, tanasukh, and that the deified ideal man was identified with the perfect man of Hellenistic thought. It adds that [[person/al-hallaj|al-Hallaj]] gave the same notion the name nasut. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

In [[concept/tasawwuf|Sufism]], the article says, Muhammad became the centre of mankind, so that his essence, haqiqa, or his light, nur, is said to have manifested itself in Adam. On this view all creatures were created for Muhammad's sake, and Adam and his offspring were created of his light. The article cites al-Mas'udi, al-Sira al-Halabiyya and al-Tha'labi. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 197}

## For Sprouts
Adam was the first human created by God, who taught him all the names and placed him in the Garden, and he is the father of all humanity.

## For Explorers
The Qur'an relates that Adam was the first human created by God from clay, honored by divine breath and endowed with the knowledge of all names. Placed with his spouse in the Garden before dwelling on Earth to cultivate it with goodness, he was the first prophet and the father of humankind, honored with moral agency and stewardship.

## For Scholars
The father of humanity and the first prophet in Islamic theology. The Qur'an chronicles his creation from clay and dust, the breathing of life into him, the prostration of angels in honor of divine command, and his instruction in the names of all things, signifying the primacy of knowledge in the human calling of stewardship (khilafa). Residing with his spouse in the Garden, they descended to Earth following their repentance, which God accepted. Reflected throughout the Qur'an and canonical Hadith corpora, Adam represents the ontological unity of the human race and the genesis of moral agency grounded in divine guidance.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)
The Sheikh says that Adam repented in Paradise and came down to earth seeking forgiveness, cleansed of his sin; he did not descend with his sin, but after God had forgiven him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:43:30}. He holds that the Paradise Adam lived in, even if it was a garden in this world on his account, is one a person will return to if he has planted good in it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:43:30}.

## In Yusuf al-Qaradawi's lessons Ta'ammulat Qur'aniyya
[[person/yusuf-al-qaradawi|Yusuf al-Qaradawi]] says Adam is the first father of humankind, created by God's hand, given of His spirit and honoured by the angels' prostration, and that Noah is humanity's second father ([[passage/quran-3-33|Al Imran 33]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:11:00} He notes that the Torah dates Adam, while the Qur'an fixes no date for Adam or Noah. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:08:30} He sets out the disagreement over Adam's prophethood: the majority of Muslim scholars hold that he was a prophet, while some date prophethood from Noah; he prefers the first, because humans were created under obligation and obligation is known only through prophethood. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-1/ep01 00:10:30}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme In the Courts of the Hadith Qudsi
**Created upon the form.** In episode 6 [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|al-Sha'rawi]] says the pronoun to which the hadith's mention of the form refers may point to God or to Adam. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:19:30} If it refers to God, it means for him that Adam's limbs answer the will of his self without intermediary, as the universe submits to God when He wills, not the reality of the divine essence. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:20:30} If it refers to Adam, it means he was not created an infant who then grew but a complete man of sixty cubits, because God made him with His hands and not by the word "Be". {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:21:00} He holds that stature began to decrease once secondary causes, marriage and procreation, came in. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:21:00}

**The greeting to the angels.** He says Adam was told to greet a particular group of angels, not all of them, because among the angels are "the exalted" ('alin), enraptured ones who know only God and were not included in the command to bow. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:22:30} He holds that the angels Adam greeted are those connected with the human being, the guardians and those who manage affairs, and that the greeting sets between server and served a bond in which neither looks down on the other. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:24:30} He says the mission of the religions is to establish peace within the self, with people, with the universe and with one's Lord. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:26:00} He holds that people enter the Garden in Adam's form so that they enjoy it with the original gift with which they were created. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep06 00:29:30}

**The garden of trial.** In episode 27 he says that the garden Adam lived in was not the Garden of eternity, which has no exit and which Satan does not enter, but a period of training in which slips were not punished, with only one prohibition, a tree. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep27 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep27 00:23:30} He holds that Adam was not yet a prophet in that period, and that his being chosen came after it. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep27 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep27 00:28:30} This is his view, close to what is described under [[concept/jannat-al-tajriba|the garden of trial]].

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On [[passage/quran-2-30|verse 30]] and after, al-Tabari gives through Ibn Abbas the account of Adam's making by God's own hand from sticky clay, the breathing in of the spirit, his sneeze and praise, and God's teaching him all the names. A second report from Ibn Abbas has the Angel of Death take red, white and black earth for the clay, which is why Adam's children differ. Al-Tabari gives these reports to clarify meaning, not to rest an argument on their soundness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 455} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 459}

On [[passage/quran-2-31|verse 31]], al-Tabari reports that Adam was so named because he was made from the "adim" of the earth, and from the Prophet, by a chain the editor calls sound, that he was made from a handful taken from all the earth, so his children came according to the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 480} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 482}
