# Turar Ryskulov

Turar Ryskulov (26 December 1894 – 10 February 1938) was a Kazakh Soviet politician, born at the Besagash tract in East-Talgar volost of Semirechye Oblast, in the family of a nomadic herder. He took part in the Central Asian revolt of 1916 and in the revolution in Turkestan and Kyrgyzstan, and in 1919 he chaired the Muslim Bureau of the Turkestan Communist Party.{cite:wikipedia-turar-ryskulov-en}{cite:wikipedia-turar-ryskulov-ru}

After the Red Army took Tashkent, he became chairman of the Central Executive Committee of the Turkestan Soviet Republic in January 1920, and proposed that Turkestan be an independent republic ruled by a separate Turkic Communist Party — a proposal Lenin overruled when he summoned Ryskulov to Moscow in May 1920. He served as deputy people's commissar for Nationalities under Stalin in 1921–1922, returned to Tashkent as chairman of the Council of People's Commissars of the Turkestan Republic until the national delimitation of 1924, worked for the Comintern in Ulaanbaatar, where he assisted in the creation of the Mongolian People's Republic, and from 1926 to 1937 was deputy chairman of the Council of People's Commissars of the Russian republic.{cite:wikipedia-turar-ryskulov-en}{cite:wikipedia-turar-ryskulov-ru}

Ryskulov was arrested on 21 May 1937 in the Great Purge, sentenced to death on 8 February 1938 and shot in Moscow two days later; the Russian article records that he was buried at the Kommunarka special object. He was rehabilitated on 8 December 1956, and is honoured in Kazakhstan today with a university, a district and streets bearing his name.{cite:wikipedia-turar-ryskulov-en}{cite:wikipedia-turar-ryskulov-ru}{cite:wikidata-turar-ryskulov}
