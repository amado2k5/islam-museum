## Summary
Algerian civilizational philosopher (1905–1973), among the foremost theorists of modern Islamic renaissance. Exiled in France and Egypt where most of his books were written, his works include The Conditions of Renaissance, The Problem of Ideas in the Muslim World, The Qur'anic Phenomenon and Vocation of Islam; he coined the concept of 'colonizability'. {cite:wikipedia-malek-bennabi}

## Life and career

Malek Bennabi was born in 1905 in Constantine, Algeria, under French colonial rule; educated in its schools, he went to France in 1930 for work and study and stayed nearly a quarter-century, between the electrical engineering he studied and the civilizational reflection that became his life's project. {cite:wikipedia-malek-bennabi}

After Algeria's independence he returned in 1963 to work in higher education and cultural administration, then settled to write; his books had already been published in Cairo and Beirut and read by many young Arabs. {cite:wikipedia-malek-bennabi}

## Intellectual project

His project was built on the question of renaissance — how does the Muslim world emerge from decline? In The Conditions of Renaissance, The Problem of Civilization and The Problem of Ideas in the Muslim World he dissected the factors of civilizational rise and decline, coining concepts such as 'colonizability', the 'network of social relations' and the 'Islamic personality'. {cite:wikipedia-malek-bennabi}

Other books include The Qur'anic Phenomenon — reflections on the Qur'an's relation to the Muslim person — Vocation of the Muslim World, Ideological Struggle in the Colonized Countries and The Birth of a Society, besides the memoir Witness of the Century. {cite:wikipedia-malek-bennabi}

## Legacy

Bennabi died in Algeria in 1973; his books remain among the most widely read works of renaissance thought in the Arab world, and conferences and research centres bearing his name convene around his project. {cite:wikipedia-malek-bennabi}

He is remembered as a thinker who forged a distinctive language for analysing Muslim civilization; scholars differ in judging some of his theses, but his question of the conditions of renaissance remains present in contemporary Islamic thought. {cite:wikipedia-malek-bennabi}
