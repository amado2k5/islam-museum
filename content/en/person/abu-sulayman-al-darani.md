## Summary
Abu Sulayman al-Darani, Abd al-Rahman ibn Ahmad ibn Atiyya al-Ansi, was one of the great ascetics of Syria, named after Daraya, a village of [[place/damascus|Damascus]] (or an estate beside it); Ibn al-Jawzi places him in the fifth generation of the people of Syria in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]] (no. 757). {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816} He transmitted from [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]], Abu al-Ashhab al-Utaridi, [[person/abd-al-wahid-ibn-zayd|Abd al-Wahid ibn Zayd]], Alqama ibn Suwayd and others, and his disciple [[person/ahmad-ibn-abi-al-hawari|Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari]], Hisham ibn Khalid and Humayd ibn Hisham al-Ansi transmitted from him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17456}

## Hunger and fear
Ibn Abi al-Hawari reports him saying: "The key to this world is satiety and the key to the next is hunger; the root of all good in both is fear of God. God gives the world to those He loves and those He does not, but hunger is kept in His treasuries and given only to those He loves; to leave one morsel of my supper is dearer to me than to eat it and pray the whole night." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816} From Musa ibn Imran: "When the soul is hungry and thirsty the heart becomes clear and tender; when it is full and quenched the heart goes blind." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.817}

## The night and devotion
He said: "Were it not for the night I would not love to remain in the world — and I do not love to remain in it to dig rivers or plant trees." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816} He related that on a cold night, praying in the mihrab, he hid one hand from the cold, and a voice called to him; after that he vowed never to pray without both hands out. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.817} He said, "In Iraq I acted, and in Syria I came to know"; his son Sulayman explained that his father's knowledge of God in Syria came from his obedience in Iraq. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816}

## From his wisdom
Among the sayings Ibn al-Jawzi records: whatever distracts you from God — family, wealth or children — is ill-omened for you; those who disobey God do so because they are of little worth to Him, and had they been precious to Him He would have kept them from sin; the higher the station of the heart, the swifter the punishment comes to it; when the hereafter is in the heart the world comes to crowd it, but when the world is in the heart the hereafter does not crowd it, for the hereafter is noble and the world is base; and whoever thinks well of God but does not fear Him is deceived. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.817} In his intimate prayer he would say: "If You call me to account for my evil, I will call on Your generosity; if You seize me for my sins, I will come to You with Your oneness." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.817}

## The caliph's sermon
Ibn Abi al-Hawari relates that he heard Abu Ja'far weeping in his Friday sermon and meant to rise and admonish him with what he knew of his deeds; then he reflected that if he stood up before the caliph with people watching, vanity might enter him, and he might be ordered away and killed without a sound intention — so he sat and kept silent. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.816}

## His transmission
Ibn al-Jawzi says he heard much hadith and met Sufyan al-Thawri and others, but gave himself to devotion rather than transmission; he found only three hadiths with chains from him, and quotes al-Khatib that he knew no other connected report from him than the first. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.821} The third is the report of Suwayd ibn al-Harith al-Azdi's delegation, which he heard from an old man on the coast of Damascus, Alqama ibn Yazid ibn Suwayd. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.821} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.822}

## His family
He had two sisters, [[person/abda-sister-of-abu-sulayman-al-darani|Abda]] and Amina, who al-Sulami says stood very high in intelligence and religion. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.858} Among the women of Syria he praised was [[person/umm-harun-al-dimashqiyya|Umm Harun]]. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.860}

## His death
He died in 205 AH; Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami said 215, but the first is more correct for Ibn al-Jawzi. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.822} The Kuwaiti Encyclopedia of Fiqh gives both: 215 from Sa'id ibn Hamdun, al-Sulami and Abu Ya'qub al-Qurrab, and 205 from his disciple Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17456}

## For young visitors
Abu Sulayman was an ascetic from the village of Daraya near Damascus who loved to pray at night, and said that a little hunger makes the heart gentle and clear.
