## Summary
Dr Omar Hatamleh is a Jordanian-American aerospace engineer and technologist who spent nearly three decades inside NASA and emerged as one of the agency's early public authorities on artificial intelligence. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology, citing a 29-year NASA career including service as the first Chief AI Officer at the Goddard Space Flight Center. {cite:muslim500-omar-hatamleh}

## NASA career
Hatamleh joined NASA after engineering training and rose through programmes in mission design, systems engineering and space operations — a career that touched the International Space Station era, Earth-science missions and the agency's internal study of how emerging technologies would change spaceflight. At the Goddard Space Flight Center in Maryland he became the centre's first Chief AI Officer, charged with weaving machine learning into mission planning, spacecraft operations and scientific analysis. {cite:muslim500-omar-hatamleh}

## The AI-and-space voice
Alongside the engineering, Hatamleh built a second career as a public explainer: books and lectures on how artificial intelligence will transform space exploration and, more broadly, the future of work and decision-making — including published work on AI and its consequences and regular speaking on innovation strategy for government and industry. The museum records the scope of this public output as attributed to the Muslim 500 profile, which calls him a global authority on AI and space technology. {cite:muslim500-omar-hatamleh}

## After NASA
Following his NASA years he has worked as an independent adviser and educator on AI strategy, executive education and space innovation, advising organisations navigating the same transformation he oversaw inside the agency. {cite:muslim500-omar-hatamleh}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Hatamleh stands for the engineer-educator of the AI turn: a Jordanian-American who spent a career moving spacecraft and finished it teaching a space agency — and a public — to think about the machines that will fly next. {cite:muslim500-omar-hatamleh}

## The administrator as futurist
Hatamleh's public profile is unusual for a space-agency engineer: he writes books on artificial intelligence and the future of governance, speaks at international conferences on technology policy, and has built a second career as a futurist-commentator on the tools his agency uses. The Muslim 500's entry records this combination — the ISU director of research who is also a technology author — as the reason a Jordanian engineer appears in its science pages alongside the spacefarers and the Nobel-adjacent researchers. {cite:muslim500-omar-hatamleh}

## The engineer as author
Hatamleh's record is the museum's example of the engineer who treats writing as a second career: the space-agency administrator who argues that the technologies his agency uses — artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, big data — will transform governance as surely as they transform rockets. The argument is his; the platform is the international conference circuit; and the result — a Muslim engineer who can speak to both the space industry and the policy world — is the profile his Muslim 500 entry records. {cite:muslim500-omar-hatamleh}
