## Overview

Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Nafi, Ziryab, was a musician associated with Baghdad, Kairouan and [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]]. Accounts attribute singing instruction and oud refinements to him; his wider portrait needs older reports distinguished from later idealization {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465} {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 pp.155–158,166}. TDV leaves his birthplace/date uncertain, gives arrival822 and death238 AH/852. This page does not repeat the former uncited children's text's exact birth year {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465}.

## Training and travel

TDV describes training under Ibrahim and Ishaq al-Mawsili, life in Kairouan under the Aghlabid Ziyadat Allah and settlement with Abd al-Rahman II {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465}. Maqqari recounts his own preferred oud, performance before Harun and departure amid professional jealousy: an attributed narrative, not a contemporary proceeding we inspected {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.122–124}. Reynolds notes differing sources on the journey and chronology, rather than one certain composite life {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 p.156}.

## Instrument and teaching

The [[invention/ziryab-fifth-string|fifth-string account]] places a red string between mathlath and mathna, symbolizing soul in a humoral analogy, and separately attributes an eagle-quill plectrum {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.126–127}. This is not invention of the oud or a surviving reconstruction with complete dimensions/tuning {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}. TDV describes teaching rhythm, words, melody and performance subtleties; the artist should not be reduced to a string count {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465}.

## Family and court

Maqqari describes allowances for him and his children and sons/daughters practicing singing: a craft of instruction, competition and patronage, not isolated genius {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.125,129}. Reynolds restores Ibn Hayyan's accounts of Ubayd Allah's innovations and family interaction omitted by Maqqari. This relies on the scholar's comparison, not independent reading of that original {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 p.161}.

## The satire of al-Ghazzal

Maqqari reports a harsh lampoon by al-Ghazzal, followed by Abd al-Rahman's order of exile and the poet's departure to Iraq. This earlier museum account is retained; its separate passage was not rechecked for this extension {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.2 p.262, prior museum reading}.

## Cultural image and identity

TDV brings clothing, food and hygiene stories alongside music and reports the ten-thousand-song memory tradition. These are attributed accounts, not independent certificates for every invention claim {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465}. Reynolds finds omitted rivals and less flattering anecdotes in a portrait evoking an ideal Andalusi past; criticism of the portrait is not proof every detail is false {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 pp.158–166}.

Al Jazeera explicitly places him in its Muslim Scholars series: the named biographical classification used here, not self-reported belief. Sunni is the separately declared museum frame, not a personal school attested by that biography {cite:aljazeera-ziryab-2022 introduction}. Reynolds's probable convert-parent hypothesis remains his interpretation, not a religion inference from ancestry {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 p.166}. This is artistic history, not a religious ruling on singing.

## For Sprouts

Books say Ziryab added a string to an older instrument. Ask which part existed before him and who tells the story instead of calling him inventor of the whole instrument {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.126}.

## For Explorers

Ziryab learned within a musical tradition and taught others; children practiced the craft too. Critical comparison reveals how some of their roles disappeared from the famous biography {cite:tdv-ziryab-arslan-erkocoglu pp.464–465} {cite:reynolds-ziryab-2008 p.161}.
