## Overview
Ahmad ibn al-Mudabbir, appointed by the caliph [[person/al-muhtadi|al-Muhtadi]] over the land-tax of Palestine. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 561-563}

Al-Mas'udi says he had seven boon companions whom he found no pleasure without, each of whom was expert in a kind of learning. He relates his well-known episode with Ibn Duraj al-Tufayli, who entered his session dressed as one of his boon companions and out-did them at chess, backgammon, the lute and the tanbur, until he struck his chamberlain with fifty lead pellets fired from a pellet-bow. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 561-563}

His birth and death are not set out here and need an additional source.

## In al-Ya'qubi
[[person/al-yaqubi|Al-Ya'qubi]] relates that al-Mu'tamid wrote to Ahmad ibn Tulun, governor of Egypt, ordering him to return the land-tax offices to Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Mudabbir, then his prisoner. He was released on Saturday, with seven nights of Dhu al-Qa'da remaining, and took charge of the land tax, having been held for nine months and twenty-five days. The year in the copy read here does not fit the order of the narrative, so it is not given. {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 620}
Al-Mu'tamid then gave him the land tax of Syria in 257 AH, removing him from that of Egypt, which went to Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Shuja', known as the vizier's nephew; Ibn al-Mudabbir left al-Fustat for Syria in Muharram 258 AH, settled there and took charge of its land tax. {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 622} {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 623}
