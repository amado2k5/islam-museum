# Adonis

Ali Ahmad Said Esber (born 1 January 1930), known by the pen name Adonis, is a Syrian poet, essayist and translator. He led a modernist revolution in Arabic poetry in the second half of the twentieth century, exerting an influence on Arabic verse comparable, in the words of The Guardian's Maya Jaggi, to T. S. Eliot's in the anglophone world. His publications include twenty volumes of poetry and thirteen of criticism, and he has long been a perennial contender for the Nobel Prize in Literature. {cite:wikipedia-adonis-en} {cite:wikipedia-adonis-ar}

## Early life and education
He was born at the village of al-Qassabin near Latakia in northwestern Syria, to a modest Alawite farming family. Unable to afford formal schooling for most of his childhood, he learned the Qur'an at the village kuttab and memorised classical Arabic poetry, to which his father had introduced him. In 1944 he recited one of his poems before Shukri al-Quwatli, president of the newly established Syrian republic, who was visiting al-Qassabin, and asked to be sent to school; a scholarship to the French lycée at Tartus followed. He graduated in 1949, took a BA in philosophy at the Syrian University (now Damascus University) in 1954, and later earned a doctorate in Arabic literature from Saint Joseph University in Beirut in 1973. While serving in the military in 1955–56 he was imprisoned for his membership in the Syrian Social Nationalist Party. {cite:wikipedia-adonis-en}

## The pen name
He adopted the name Adonis at seventeen, after a number of magazines had rejected his work under his real name, choosing it, in the Guardian's account, to alert napping editors to his precocious talent and to his pre-Islamic, pan-Mediterranean muses. {cite:wikipedia-adonis-en}

## Beirut and Paris
In 1956 he left Syria for Beirut, where he joined a community of artists, writers and exiles. In 1957 he co-founded with Yusuf al-Khal the magazine Shi'r ("Poetry"), arguably the most influential Arab literary journal, which became the main platform of the modernism movement in Arabic literature. He did not rejoin its editors when it resumed in 1967, and in 1968 he founded another periodical, Mawaqif ("Positions"), which continued until 1994; from 2011 to 2013 he edited Al-Akhar ("The Other"). From 1970 to 1985 he was professor of Arabic literature at the Lebanese University, and he also taught at Damascus, the Sorbonne and at Georgetown and Princeton. He emigrated to Paris in 1980 to escape the Lebanese civil war and settled there with his family in 1985; Paris has remained his primary residence. {cite:wikipedia-adonis-en} {cite:wikipedia-adonis-ar}

## Poetry and criticism
His third collection, The Songs of Mihyar the Damascene (1961), marked a definitive break with existing poetics and has been said to have reshaped the possibilities of Arabic lyric poetry. His multi-volume anthology of Arabic poetry, Diwan ash-Shi'r al-Arabi, covering almost two millennia of verse, has been in print since 1964. His critical study The Static and the Dynamic (al-Thabit wa-l-Mutahawwil) examines the tension between tradition and innovation in Arab culture. His dozen books of translation into Arabic include the poetry of Saint-John Perse and Yves Bonnefoy and the first complete Arabic translation of Ovid's Metamorphoses (2002). With his use of Sufi vocabulary, he became a leading exponent of the neo-Sufi trend in modern Arabic poetry. Among his honours are the Bjørnson Prize (2007) and the Goethe Prize (2011). {cite:wikipedia-adonis-en} {cite:wikipedia-adonis-ar}

## Views and controversies
In his book Identité inachevée he expresses opposition to "religion as an institution imposed on the whole of society" but support for individual religious freedom, and describes himself as a "pagan mystic", setting out a mysticism in which truth is not ready-made but must be sought, dug up and discovered. In a 2021 interview he said that religion and poetry were contradictory, because religion is an ideology, an answer, whereas poetry remains always a question. The Arabic article sets out his criticism of Salafi-Wahhabism, and records the controversies around him: his expulsion from the Arab Writers Union, death threats against him, and calls to burn his books. {cite:wikipedia-adonis-en} {cite:wikipedia-adonis-ar}
