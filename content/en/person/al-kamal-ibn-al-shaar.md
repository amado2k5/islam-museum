## Summary
al-Kamal ibn al-Sha'r, the Mosul hafiz, author of 'Uqud al-Juman fi Shu'ara' al-Zaman — died at Aleppo in 654 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 266}.

## Details
Abu al-Barakat al-Mubarak ibn Abi Bakr ibn Hamdan al-Mawsili, known as al-Kamal ibn al-Sha'r, composed 'Uqud al-Juman fi Shu'ara' al-Zaman ("Strings of Pearls: Poets of the Age"), a biographical anthology of the poets of his time and near-contemporaries that became a standard work of literary biography for later authors {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 266}. Ibn al-'Imad records his death at Aleppo under the year 654, naming him chiefly by his famous book {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 266}. The work joined poetry to history, preserving a generation of seventh-century AH literati who would otherwise be lost; its author was a hafiz of Mosul living through the Mongol upheavals that successively struck Mosul and Aleppo, and his compilation saved the literary face of that turbulent age {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 266}.
