## Summary
Islam Ibrahim Behery Helal is an Egyptian writer and television presenter, born in 1974 in Siflaq, in Sohag Governorate in Upper Egypt. He became one of the most contested public voices in Egypt through his programme "With Islam Behery" (Ma'a Islam Behery) on the Al Kahera Wal Nas channel, in which he subjected the classical books of the Islamic heritage — the hadith collections, the four schools of law and the exegesis literature — to a sustained critical re-reading, and urged his viewers to go directly to the Qur'an rather than take their religion from fatwas. Al-Azhar condemned the programme as an insult to Islam; the channel suspended it in April 2015, and within weeks a court had sentenced him to five years in prison for contempt of religion. The sentence was reduced to one year on appeal, and in November 2016 President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi pardoned him. He returned to television with a succession of programmes — "Al-Kharita" (The Map), "Islam Hurr" (Free Islam), "Al-Bousla" (The Compass) and "Hiwar ma'a Islam" (Dialogue with Islam) — and in May 2024 he was a founding trustee of the Takween Foundation for Arab Thought, from whose board he was removed that September after a court upheld a three-year sentence against him in a bounced-cheque case. {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-ar} {cite:telegraph-egypt-behery-takween-2024}

Behery describes his own project, in his words, as a defence of the religion itself against what he calls the impurities of the heritage books: the problem, as he puts it, is not the Qur'an but the man-made heritage built around it. His critics, including Al-Azhar and Salafi writers, accuse him of insulting Islam, fabricating and truncating texts, and attacking the classical imams. This article records both his framing and theirs, as attributed claims, and gives the documented course of his career and cases. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile} {cite:memri-behery-skynews-2023}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1974 in Siflaq, a village in Sohag Governorate. The sources disagree about his exact birth date: most Egyptian press reports, and the text of the Arabic Wikipedia article, give 5 April 1974, while the same article's infobox gives 5 May 1975. The English Wikipedia article gives only the year. The museum therefore records the year only. {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-ar}

He graduated from a faculty of law. By his own account, given in an interview with Sabah Al-Kheir magazine and quoted by the Ida2at site, he was blocked from postgraduate study after graduating, travelled to Kuwait, and was appointed a researcher at the Kuwaiti Ministry of Awqaf. He later took a master's degree in "Methods of Dealing with the Heritage" from the University of Wales in the United Kingdom, as the Arabic Wikipedia article and several Egyptian press profiles report. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile} {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-ar}

## Beginnings in print, and the Youm7 years
Behery says he began publishing his ideas in independent newspapers in 2003, writing, in his words, "in defence of the religion against the impurities of the heritage books". In 2007 the editor Khaled Salah invited him to write for the Youm7 newspaper about religious heritage. He worked at the Youm7 Foundation and headed its Islamic Studies Centre. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile}

He has published no standalone book that the museum could verify; his writings are the newspaper and magazine articles of these years and after. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile}

## Television: the debates and "With Islam Behery"
His first television prominence came in 2013, in televised debates on Tony Khalifa's programme "Ajra' al-Kalam" (The Boldest Talk) on Al Kahera Wal Nas: one against the Salafi preacher known as Abu Yahya, and one against Mahmoud Shaaban, who famously raised a shoe at him during the exchange. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile}

His own programme, "With Islam Behery" (Ma'a Islam Behery), followed on the same channel, and was in its third season by September 2014, which places its beginning around 2012–2013; its exact launch date is not documented in the sources consulted. On it he worked through the heritage books — hadith, jurisprudence and exegesis — questioning their rulings and their authority, and told viewers not to take fatwas from scholars but to go directly to the Qur'an. The Oasis International Foundation, describing his method, calls it Qur'an-centred, notes that he rejects many hadiths, and names the modernists Muhammad Abduh and Mahmoud Shaltout among his inspirations. {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:oasis-islam-behery-quran-study}

In April 2015 he took part in two widely watched debates: one with Osama al-Azhari and Habib Ali al-Jifri, arranged on Khairy Ramadan's programme, and one with Abdullah Rushdy, Al-Azhar's official representative. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile}

## The confrontation with Al-Azhar and the 2015 case
Al-Azhar condemned the content of his programme as an insult to Islam, lodged complaints with the public prosecutor and sued him. Behery himself said that he faced 48 lawsuits and complaints. {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:alkhaleej-online-behery-case}

In April 2015 Al Kahera Wal Nas suspended his programme indefinitely. In late May 2015 the Misr El-Qadima misdemeanour court convicted him of contempt of religion and sentenced him to five years with labour. In October 2015 a court rejected his appeal against the five-year sentence. In December 2015 the sentence was reduced to one year, and he served the term from that month. {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-ar} {cite:alkhaleej-online-behery-case}

On 17 November 2016 President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi pardoned him, as part of a pardon of 82 young detainees; he was released about a month before his term would have ended. Some secondary sources date the pardon loosely to December 2016; the dated Egyptian reporting gives 17 November 2016, and that is the date recorded here. {cite:aswat-masriya-behery-pardon-2016}

The confrontation did not end with the pardon. On 29 October 2017 the Administrative Court ruled to suspend Behery from appearing on television, and banned the airing or re-airing of his programme, in a suit brought by Al-Azhar's Grand Imam, Ahmed el-Tayyib. In the same year Ahmed Hosny, then president of Al-Azhar University, declared Behery an apostate on television; the remarks caused a public outcry and ended with Hosny's removal. {cite:egypt-today-behery-tv-ban-2017} {cite:alarab-hosny-behery-2017}

## Return to television
After his release he returned with new programmes on new channels. In Ramadan 2017 he presented "Al-Kharita" (The Map). On 5 November 2018 he began "Islam Hurr" (Free Islam) on Alhurra. He later hosted "Al-Bousla" (The Compass) on TEN TV, which the channel's own descriptions say is directed at people's minds and aims to revive what it calls individual responsibility in religion; the programme's year is not stated in those descriptions. Most recently he presents "Hiwar ma'a Islam" (Dialogue with Islam), a weekly discussion programme on Al-Ghad's digital platforms, on Saturdays at 4 p.m. Cairo time. He is also a frequent talk-show guest, appearing, for example, on Amr Adib's "Kull Yawm" on ON TV on 25 April 2017, and in a Sky News Arabia interview on 29 January 2023. {cite:oasis-islam-behery-quran-study} {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:ten-tv-albousla-behery} {cite:alghad-hiwar-maa-islam} {cite:memri-behery-skynews-2023}

## His project, in his own framing
Behery presents his work as a critical re-reading of the Islamic heritage: the hadith collections, the four schools of law and the exegesis literature. He argues that this heritage is man-made, that it contains rulings of violence and of takfir (declaring other Muslims unbelievers), and that it gave rise to extremist movements. The heritage, not the Qur'an, is "the problem", in his phrase. In the Sky News Arabia interview of 29 January 2023 he said: "Islam is suitable for all times and places — and I agree wholeheartedly", locating the problem in the heritage rather than in the religion. His Facebook page's biography, as quoted by his critics, states his project as "purifying the heritage and engaging directly and freely with the sacred texts". He is a leading public face of the call, taken up in the el-Sisi era, for a "renewal of religious discourse", and a prominent critic both of Al-Azhar's claims to a monopoly over religious interpretation and of the Muslim Brotherhood and Salafism. {cite:memri-behery-skynews-2023} {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile}

## Takween, and the cheque cases of 2024
In May 2024 the Takween Foundation for Arab Thought was launched at the Grand Egyptian Museum, and Behery was a founding member of its board of trustees, alongside Ibrahim Issa, Youssef Ziedan, Firas al-Sawwah, Olfat Youssef and others. On 11 May 2024 he defended the foundation on Amr Adib's programme as promoting tolerance and dialogue. {cite:ahmed-youssef-takween-2024}

That summer he was arrested twice in bounced-cheque cases. On 4 August 2024 Cairo security arrested him at dawn to execute six in-absentia judgments; he filed oppositions and was released, and several of the cases were subsequently closed by reconciliation, among them cases before the Nasr City and October misdemeanour courts in October 2024. On 22–24 September 2024 the Misr El-Qadima appeals court upheld a three-year sentence, with a fine of EGP 20,000, in one cheque case (case no. 13656 of 2021). He was arrested on 24 September and then released on bail of EGP 20,000 after filing an opposition-appeal, with a hearing set for 23 December 2024. Behery commented on his personal Facebook page that "the matter is over; all the cases are fabricated and malicious, by people I do not know". The final outcome of the December 2024 opposition-appeal could not be verified in the sources consulted for this article; he has continued to appear in the media since, and is not reported to be in prison. {cite:her-news-behery-arrest-2024} {cite:arabkhabar-behery-bail-2024}

After the September ruling, the Takween Foundation announced his removal from its board of trustees. {cite:telegraph-egypt-behery-takween-2024}

## Perspectives and criticism
This section sets out the positions as the sources report them. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**His own position.** Behery says he writes and broadcasts in defence of Islam itself: his articles, from 2003, were written "in defence of the religion against the impurities of the heritage books", and his programme biography speaks of purifying the heritage and engaging directly and freely with the sacred texts. Testimony before the Heritage Committee of Canada's House of Commons in 2017 described him as "a practising Muslim" who used a weekly television show to examine the roots of Islamic fundamentalism. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile} {cite:canada-hoc-behery-testimony-2017}

**Al-Azhar and the religious establishment.** Al-Azhar condemned his programme's content as an insult to Islam, complained to the public prosecutor, sued him, and its Grand Imam brought the suit that ended his television appearances in 2017. A president of Al-Azhar University declared him an apostate on television in 2017, and was removed after the outcry. {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:egypt-today-behery-tv-ban-2017} {cite:alarab-hosny-behery-2017}

**Salafi and Islamist critics.** Writers in that current accuse him of fabricating and truncating texts and of attacking the four imams and the compilers al-Bukhari and Muslim. The rebuttal literature began at once: the Salafi site Ana al-Salafi, for example, published its own account of the April 2015 debates within days, asking whether Behery had been "debating or manoeuvring". These accusations are recorded here as his critics' claims; the museum has not adjudicated them. {cite:ida2at-islam-behery-profile} {cite:anasalafy-behery-debates-2015}

**His faith, as this museum records it.** English Wikipedia describes him as an Egyptian Muslim writer, and the Canadian parliamentary testimony calls him a practising Muslim; his own framing is that of a Muslim reformer defending his religion. His opponents have publicly called him an apostate and an atheist. Because the question is itself one of the things in dispute, the museum records his faith as unknown on his data record, with the reasoning set out there in full. {cite:wikipedia-islam-behery-en} {cite:canada-hoc-behery-testimony-2017}
