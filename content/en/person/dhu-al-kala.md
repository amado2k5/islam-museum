## Overview
Dhu al-Kala, a king of Himyar, whose story appears in al-Mas'udi's "Meadows of Gold" when he came to [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]]. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.596}

## The delegation
He came with a thousand slaves, besides those of his own clan who were with him, wearing the crown, robes and striped cloaks. When he saw that Abu Bakr wore a plain wrap and a cloak, he took off his own garments and dressed as he did. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.596}

One day he was seen in a market in Medina with the skin of a sheep on his shoulders. His people were alarmed and said that he had shamed them before the Emigrants and the Helpers. He answered that he did not wish to be a tyrant in the age of ignorance and a tyrant in Islam, and that obedience is only given through humility and renunciation of the world. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.596}

## His death
It is said that he was killed at Siffin with Hashim ibn Utba al-Mirqal, and his standard-bearer was a man from Udhra. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.674}

The data does not establish the date of his visit, and there is no other source to confirm it; an additional source is needed.
