## Summary
Muhammad ibn Shuja al-Thalji, Abu Abd Allah, a Baghdadi Hanafi, a companion of al-Hasan ibn Ziyad, the leading jurist of Iraq in his time and eminent in law, hadith and Qur'anic recitation. The book gives 266 AH as his death year. It notes a leaning towards Mu'tazilite views. His works include al-Manasik, Tashih al-athar, al-Nawadir and a book on partnership in trade (mudaraba). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15418}

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to Muhammad ibn Shuja' Ibn al-Thalji, the Hanafi jurist of Baghdad, Abu Abd Allah, author of many works. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 577} He recited the Qur'an to al-Yazidi, narrated from [[person/ibn-ulayyah|Ibn Ulayya]] and Waki', and studied law with al-Hasan ibn Ziyad al-Lu'lu'i and others. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 577} Ahmad ibn Kamil said: he was the jurist of Iraq in his time; Ibn al-Munadi said: he studied law and taught people the Qur'an. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578} He reports al-Hakim saying he saw Ibn al-Thalji's *Kitab al-Manasik* in more than sixty large parts. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578}

**The quarrel with Ahmad's followers.** Al-Dhahabi says it is reported in more than one way that Ibn al-Thalji spoke against [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] and his followers, saying: "what did Ahmad ever stand up for!" {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 577} Al-Marrudhi reported going to him and reproaching him, and Ibn al-Thalji said he spoke of "God's speech" as one speaks of "God's heaven and God's earth" — a genitive of ownership. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 577} Al-Dhahabi records harsh sayings of his about Ahmad's followers and Ahmad himself, and that his will directed that nothing from his third be given except to one who said the Qur'an was created. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578} Ibn Adi reported from Musa ibn al-Qasim that Ibn al-Thalji used to disparage [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]], but at his death asked God's mercy on him, mentioned his learning and said: I have taken back what I used to say about him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578}

**The judgeship he did not receive.** Al-Dhahabi reports from [[person/ali-ibn-al-jahm|Ali ibn al-Jahm]] that Ishaq ibn Ibrahim, governor of Baghdad, pressed [[person/al-mutawakkil|al-Mutawakkil]] to make Ibn al-Thalji a judge; al-Mutawakkil asked Ali who he was, Ali said he was one of the followers of [[person/bishr-ibn-ghiyath-al-marisi|Bishr al-Marisi]], and the caliph tore up the letter — which, Ali said, was why he himself was later sent away to Isbijab. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 577} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578}

**The charge of forgery.** Ibn Adi said that he forged hadiths of anthropomorphism and attributed them to the hadith people in order to disgrace them. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 577} Ibn Adi cites from him a report about the creation of "the horse", which al-Dhahabi calls among the plainest of lies, and which he holds was forged by the Jahmiyya to argue that attributing "self" and "speech" to God is a genitive of ownership and honour. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 579} [[person/zakariyya-al-saji|Zakariyya al-Saji]] said: a liar who schemed to invalidate hadith in support of personal opinion. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578}

**His devotion and death.** Al-Dhahabi says that despite his failings he was a man of recitation and worship, who died prostrating in the afternoon prayer, "and may he find mercy, God willing". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578} He died in 266 at the age of eighty-six, suddenly, in Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578}

- **Sunni: Ahmad ibn Kamil and Ibn al-Munadi, and al-Dhahabi on his devotion**: the jurist of Iraq in his time, a devout teacher of the Qur'an who died in prostration. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 578}
