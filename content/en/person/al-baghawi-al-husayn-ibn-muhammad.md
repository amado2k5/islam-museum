## Overview
Abu Muhammad al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn al-Farra al-Baghawi, a hadith scholar and jurist from the town of Bagh in Khurasan, between Merv and Herat. Among his works is "Sharh al-Sunna". The encyclopaedia gives his birth as 436 AH and his death as 510 AH. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.654}

## What Shadharat al-Dhahab says
Shadharat al-Dhahab says that he died in 516 AH in Merv al-Rudh in Shawwal, and was buried beside his teacher, the judge Husayn. It describes him as a noble, ascetic and content man. It relays from Ibn al-Ahdal that his works include "Ma'alim al-Tanzil", "al-Jam' bayn al-Sahihayn" and "al-Masabih", and in jurisprudence "al-Tahdhib" and "Sharh al-Sunna". It says he would not give a lesson except in a state of ritual purity. It relays from al-Subki that he never saw him choose anything without finding on examination that it was stronger than the alternatives. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.529}

The year of death differs between the encyclopaedia (510 AH) and the Shadharat (516 AH), and the question needs an additional source.

## In the biography by the editors of Tahdhib Tafsir al-Baghawi
The editors of Tahdhib Tafsir al-Baghawi call him the imam, hafiz, jurist and mujtahid Muhyi al-Sunna Abu Muhammad al-Husayn ibn Mas'ud ibn Muhammad al-Farra' al-Baghawi the Shafi'i, also titled Rukn al-Din; al-Farra' refers to making and selling furs, and al-Baghawi to a town in Khurasan between Marw and Herat called Bagh or Baghshur, as al-Sam'ani says in al-Ansab. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.5} They say most sources do not give his year of birth; Yaqut in Mu'jam al-Buldan gives 433 AH and al-Zirikli in al-A'lam 436 AH. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.5}

Among his teachers they list the Shafi'i jurist [[person/qadi-husayn-al-marwarrudhi|al-Qadi Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Marwazi]], Abd al-Wahid ibn Ahmad al-Mulayhi of Herat, Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Abd al-Samad al-Turabi of Marw, the leading transmitter of Khurasan, and Abu Bakr Ya'qub ibn Ahmad al-Sayrafi of Nishapur. Among his students: Abu Mansur Muhammad ibn As'ad al-Attari, known as Hafada; Abu al-Futuh Muhammad ibn Abi Ja'far al-Ta'i of Hamadan; and his brother al-Hasan ibn Mas'ud al-Baghawi, who studied law with him. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.5}

They count him among the imams of the early-generation way who kept to the Qur'an and Sunna in creed, especially on the divine names and attributes, quoting Ibn Qadi Shuhba that he was "on the way of the salaf" and Tashkopruzade that he was "sound in creed". {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.6} They describe him as an authority in the Shafi'i school who was not partisan for it but followed the evidence, and quote the praise of al-Dhahabi, al-Suyuti and Ibn Kathir. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.6} His works include al-Tahdhib in Shafi'i law, [[work/maalim-al-tanzil|Ma'alim al-Tanzil]], [[work/sharh-al-sunnah-baghawi|Sharh al-Sunna]] and [[work/masabih-al-sunna|Masabih al-Sunna]]. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.6} He died at Marw al-Rudh in Shawwal 516 AH, was buried beside his teacher al-Qadi Husayn, and lived some seventy-odd years; this agrees with the year 516 in Shadharat al-Dhahab. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.6}
