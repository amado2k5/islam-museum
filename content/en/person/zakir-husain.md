## Summary
Zakir Husain (8 February 1897 – 3 May 1969) was an Indian educationist and politician who served as vice president of India from 1962 to 1967 and as president from 13 May 1967 until his death in office. A founding member of Jamia Millia Islamia, he was its vice-chancellor for over two decades, then vice-chancellor of Aligarh Muslim University and governor of Bihar, before reaching the presidency as the first Muslim to hold it. {cite:wikipedia-zakir-husain-en} {cite:wikidata-zakir-husain}

## Jamia and Aligarh
Zakir Husain Khan was born in Hyderabad into an Afridi Pashtun family, schooled at Etawah, and educated at the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College, Aligarh, and the University of Berlin, where he took a doctorate in economics. A close associate of Mahatma Gandhi, he was a founding member of Jamia Millia Islamia, established as an independent national university during the Non-cooperation movement, and served as its vice-chancellor from 1926 to 1948. In 1937 he chaired the Basic National Education Committee, whose Nai Talim scheme proposed free and compulsory education in the mother tongue. After independence he stayed in India and became vice-chancellor of Aligarh Muslim University (1948–1956), which he helped retain as a national institution. {cite:wikipedia-zakir-husain-en} {cite:wikipedia-zakir-husain-ar}

## Governor, vice president and president
Husain sat in parliament as a nominated member from 1952 to 1957, received the Padma Vibhushan in 1954, and governed Bihar from 1957 to 1962. He was vice president of India from 1962 to 1967 — receiving the Bharat Ratna in 1963 — and succeeded Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan as president on 13 May 1967. He died in office in New Delhi on 3 May 1969, the first Indian president to do so.
