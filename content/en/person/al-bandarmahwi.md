## Overview
Hamid ibn Yusuf ibn Hamid, Diya' al-Din al-Iskandari al-Bandarmahwi: a Hanafi jurist, Naqshbandi, from the lands of Rum, who lived from 1111 to 1172 AH (1700–1758 CE). Born and educated in Istanbul, he travelled through Syria and Egypt taking knowledge from their scholars, stayed a while in Medina, then settled in Bandirma where he died. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.162}

His works in legal theory, hadith and creed include Jami' al-Faharis, a large volume which al-Baghdadi says he held in the author's own hand; Takhrij Ahadith Shari'at al-Islam; Ta'rifat al-Fuhul on legal theory; Shuhud al-Fara'id; Mukhallafat Hukama' al-Yunan on logic; Muhimmat al-Kafi on prosody and rhyme; and Shuhud Kitab on the terminology of letters, of which a good copy survives at Riyadh University. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.162}
