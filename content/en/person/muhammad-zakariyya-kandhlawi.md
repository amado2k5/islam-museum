## Summary
Indian Deobandi hadith scholar (1898–1982), Shaykh al-Hadith at Mazahir Uloom, Saharanpur. A leading hadith authority of twentieth-century South Asia; his works include the encyclopedic Awjaz al-Masalik commentary on Malik's Muwatta, Lami' al-Darari on the sira, and Faza'il-e-A'mal, widely read in the Tablighi Jamaat. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zakariyya-kandhlawi}

## Life and formation

Muhammad Zakariyya was born in 1898 in Kandhla, Uttar Pradesh, India, into a learned family; he memorised the Qur'an young and studied under his father and Khalil Ahmad Saharanpuri among the shaykhs of Deoband and Mazahir Uloom, attaching himself to Shaykh Khalil until he became one of his closest students. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zakariyya-kandhlawi}

He travelled repeatedly to the Hijaz for hajj and to sit with the scholars of the two sanctuaries, and accompanied Mawlana Muhammad Ilyas Kandhlawi, founder of the Tablighi Jamaat, becoming one of its foremost elders and guides. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zakariyya-kandhlawi}

## Teaching and works

He taught hadith at Mazahir Uloom in Saharanpur for more than half a century, graduating thousands of hadith scholars and jurists, and his lecture cycle on Sahih al-Bukhari became famous across the Muslim world. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zakariyya-kandhlawi}

His works include Awjaz al-Masalik ila Muwatta Malik in about twenty volumes, Lami' al-Darari on the sira, Al-Kawkab al-Durri on the Muwatta of Muhammad al-Shaybani, and the Faza'il series — Faza'il-e-A'mal, Faza'il-e-Qur'an, Faza'il-e-Dhikr — circulated in dozens of languages through the Tablighi Jamaat. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zakariyya-kandhlawi}

## Legacy

In his last years he moved to Medina, where he taught in the Prophet's Mosque; he died there in 1982 and was buried in al-Baqi' cemetery, mourned by scholars of East and West. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zakariyya-kandhlawi}

He is remembered today as one of the last links of the great South Asian hadith school, and his commentaries and devotional compilations are read on a wider scale than those of any other contemporary Indian hadith master. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-zakariyya-kandhlawi}
