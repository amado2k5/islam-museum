## Overview
Musa ibn Ulayy ibn Rabah al-Lakhmi, a client of the tribe, of Egypt, Abu Abd al-Rahman, is called by al-Dhahabi the imam, memoriser, reliable, the great and just governor and deputy of Egypt for Abu Ja'far al-Mansur for some years {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3987}. The sign printed with his entry shows that Muslim and the four Sunan compilers recorded his hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3987}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated much from his father, and from Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir, Ibn Shihab, Yazid ibn Abi Habib and others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3987}. Usama ibn Zayd al-Laythi, who died some time before him, Yahya ibn Ayyub, al-Layth, Ibn Lahi'a, Sa'id ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Jumahi, Waki', Ibn Wahb, Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn Mahdi, Abu Nu'aym, Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Muqri, Abd Allah ibn Salih the scribe and others narrated from him, the last of them to die being the blind al-Qasim ibn Hani ibn Nafi al-Adawi {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3987}.

## What the scholars said
Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Yahya ibn Ma'in, al-Ijli and al-Nasa'i called him reliable, and Abu Hatim al-Razi said he was a righteous man who kept his hadith exactly, adding nothing and omitting nothing, and one of the reliable Egyptians {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3987}.

## Birth and death
Ibn Yunus says he was born in Ifriqiya in 90 AH and died at Alexandria in 163 AH, and Yahya ibn Bukayr, Khalifa, Abu Ubayd and others agree on the death {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3987}. Ibn Hibban says he was born in 89 AH, and it is said his governorship over the province of Egypt lasted six years and two months {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص3987}.
