## Overview
Nabit, also called Nabayut, is the first of the twelve sons of [[person/ismail|Ismail]] that al-Rahiq al-Makhtum counts, born to [[person/bint-mudad-al-jurhumi|the daughter of Mudad]] al-Jurhumi {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.230}. The accounts of his other brothers have been lost to time, except the lines of Nabit and [[person/qaydar|Qaydar]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20}.

## The Nabataeans
The author notes that the Nabataean civilization, descended from Nabit, flourished in northern Hijaz. They built a strong government whose reach extended to its frontiers, and they made [[place/petra|Petra]] their capital. No one could oppose them until the Romans came and overcame them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20}. See [[polity/nabataean-kingdom|the Nabataean kingdom]].

## al-Nadwi's view of the Ghassanids and the Ansar
The author reports that [[source/tarikh-ard-al-quran|Sulayman al-Nadwi]] concluded, after study and inquiry, that the kings of the Ghassan and also the Ansar of the Aws and Khazraj were not of the lineage of Qahtan, but of the family of Nabit ibn Ismail, and that they were their remnants in those lands {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran 2/78-86}. This differs from what the author himself says when dividing the emigrants of Kahlan, where he places the Aws, the Khazraj and Jafna among [[group/al-azd|the Azd]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}. The question is disputed among genealogists.

## Governance of Mecca
Ismail held the leadership of Mecca and the custody of the House for his whole life. Then two of his sons, Nabit and then Qaydar, held it, or the reverse is said. Leadership then passed to their grandfather [[person/mudad-ibn-amr|Mudad ibn Amr al-Jurhumi]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.230-237}. The author points out that [[source/ibn-hisham-sira|Ibn Hisham]] mentions among Ismail's sons only Nabit's governance {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/111-113}.

## For young visitors
Nabit was a son of the Prophet Ismail. From his descendants came the Nabataeans, who built the city of Petra, carved into the rock.
