## Summary
Dr Abeer Al-Namankany is a Saudi paediatric dentist and clinical researcher whose name is carried by a measurement tool used around the world: the Abeer Children Dental Anxiety Scale, known in the literature as MCDASf — a self-report scale in which young children point to faces, not words, to say how afraid they are of the dentist. The Muslim 500 places her in Science & Technology as a clinician and researcher behind drug-free approaches to children's dental fear. {cite:muslim500-abeer-al-namankany}

## The problem she addressed
Dental fear and anxiety in childhood is among the commonest reasons children miss care: a frightened child cannot sit still, the treatment suffers, and the fear can persist into adulthood. Measuring that fear accurately — especially in children too young to fill in a written questionnaire — was the gap her research addressed. {cite:muslim500-abeer-al-namankany}

## The Abeer scale
Her scale answers with pictures: children choose among illustrated faces to report how they feel before and during dental treatment, giving clinicians and researchers a quick, validated, child-centred measure that has been translated and tested in many countries. It sits alongside her broader work on non-pharmacological behaviour management — techniques for calming and treating anxious children without sedation or drugs. {cite:muslim500-abeer-al-namankany}

## Research and standing
Al-Namankany has published in international journals of paediatric dentistry on anxiety assessment and behaviour guidance, trained abroad and worked as a clinician and researcher in Saudi Arabia. The Muslim 500 records her as a Saudi clinician and researcher whose inventions — the scale and the drug-free psychotherapy techniques around it — are used in paediatric dental practice. {cite:muslim500-abeer-al-namankany}

## Significance
In the museum's record she represents a kind of Muslim 500 figure whose influence is quiet and clinical: a Saudi woman researcher whose tool helps frightened children get through the dentist's chair in clinics far from where she designed it — contribution measured in calmer appointments rather than headlines. {cite:muslim500-abeer-al-namankany}

## Why a face matters
The elegance of Al-Namankany's instrument is its humility: it asks a frightened child to do the one thing every child can do — point at a face. In paediatric dentistry, where the patient's cooperation is half the treatment, a valid measure of fear changes what a clinician can know before the chair tilts back. That the scale is used and validated internationally — cited in the paediatric-dental literature under her name — is the quiet form influence takes in clinical science: not fame, but a better question asked in thousands of clinics. {cite:muslim500-abeer-al-namankany}
