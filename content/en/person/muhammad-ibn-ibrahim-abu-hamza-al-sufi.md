## Summary
Abu Hamza al-Sufi al-Baghdadi, one of the senior Sufi shaykhs of the third century. He spoke in the Rusafa mosque and then in the city mosque, and he was a scholar of the readings, especially the reading of Abu Amr. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 152}.

He sat with Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and when a question was put to Ahmad he would say: "What do you say about it, O Sufi?" He also sat with Bishr ibn al-Harith, Abu Nasr al-Tammar and Sarri al-Saqati, and travelled with Abu Turab al-Nakhshabi. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 152}.

It is related of him that he fell into a well, and when people came to fill it in, he regarded it as an act of trust in God not to speak. Ibn al-Jawzi notes that his silence in such a case contravenes the Sharia, and it was said that the one who fell into the well was Abu Hamza al-Khurasani, not the Baghdadi. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 152}.

## The charge of indwelling, as told in Talbis Iblis
[[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] reports from Abu 'Abd Allah al-Ramli that Abu Hamza spoke in the congregational mosque of Tarsus and was well received, until one day, while he was speaking, a crow cried on the mosque roof and Abu Hamza shrieked "Labbayk, labbayk" ("Here I am"); they accused him of heresy, calling him "an incarnationist heretic", and his horse was sold by public cry at the mosque door: "This is the heretic's horse." {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 173} He reports from Abu 'Ali al-Rudhbari that he was accused of believing in indwelling because whenever he heard a sound such as the wind blowing, water running or birds crying he would cry out "Labbayk, labbayk", and from Abu 'Ali too that he took such sounds only as a summons from God waking him to remembrance. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 173} He relates from al-Sarraj that Abu Hamza entered the house of [[person/al-harith-al-muhasibi|al-Harith al-Muhasibi]], a sheep bleated, and he gasped and said "Here I am, my Lord"; al-Muhasibi grew angry, took a knife and said: "If you do not repent of this state of yours, I will slaughter you." {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 173} Talbis Iblis names him only as Abu Hamza; linking these reports to Abu Hamza of Baghdad, the subject of this page, is an inference from the context of the section, not a statement in the text. Ibn al-Jawzi places these reports in his section on "what is reported of the group's bad belief"; Abu 'Ali al-Rudhbari's explanation is the Sufi explanation he quotes.
