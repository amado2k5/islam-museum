## Summary
Ala al-Din Tekish, Khwarazmshah, was the father of Ala al-Din Muhammad. He took Rayy in 588 AH at the invitation of Qutlugh Inanj son of al-Bahlawan. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.232-233}

His brother Sultanshah had marched on Khwarazm and its people kept him out; he died in 589 AH. Tekish then took over Merv, his brother's realm and treasuries, and made his son Qutb al-Din Muhammad governor of Nishapur. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.232-233}

In 590 AH his army killed Sultan Tughril, the last of the Seljuks of Iraq, near Rayy. He took Hamadan and the Jibal, handed them to Qutlugh Inanj, and returned to Khwarazm. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.232-233}

## His coins
Shohruxmirzo Ismoilov dates his reign 567–596 AH (1172–1200) and reports finding 59 of his coins, struck at Khwarazm and Nishapur, whose dates are hard to fix; he counts 22 types. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 103} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 118}
- Early in his reign he struck three kinds in the name of the caliph al-Mustadi, styling himself "the greatest king, Ala al-Dunya wa al-Din Abu al-Muzaffar Tekish", that is with the title "king". {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 103}
- He then struck in the name of the caliph al-Nasir li-Din Allah with the title "the greatest sultan". {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 104} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 105}
- Among his fulus are a type on the Chinese model with a square at its centre, a hexagonal one bearing an eight-pointed star that the author calls a "Star of David", and five fals types in al-Nasir's name that the author places in Khwarazm, since the parts of Khurasan he held usually struck gold dinars. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 103} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 104} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 105}
- One of his types names Tughanshah, lord of Nishapur, as "the exalted sultan" and heir of Sanjar. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 106}

The author sees the expansion of the state's borders beginning under Tekish and peaking under his son Muhammad. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 163}
