## Summary
Moza bint Nasser is a Qatari sheikha, the second wife of the former emir [[person/hamad-bin-khalifa-al-thani|Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani]] and mother of the current emir [[person/tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani]], and one of the most visible leaders of education and social development in the Arab world. She has chaired the Qatar Foundation for Education, Science and Community Development since its founding in 1995, built Education City with its branch campuses of global universities, and launched international initiatives for out-of-school children and youth employment. {cite:wikipedia-sheikha-moza-bint-nasser}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1959 in Al Khor, Qatar, studied sociology at Qatar University, graduating in 1986, and later earned a master's degree in public policy in Islam from Hamad Bin Khalifa University — the national university she herself helped create. She grew up in a family connected to Qatari public life; some relatives spent years in Kuwait before she returned to Qatar as a young woman. {cite:wikipedia-sheikha-moza-bint-nasser}

## Qatar Foundation and Education City
She co-founded the Qatar Foundation for Education, Science and Community Development in 1995 and chairs its board, making it an umbrella for dozens of educational, research and community institutions. Its flagship project is Education City, a campus on the edge of Doha opened progressively from 2003, hosting branch campuses of Carnegie Mellon, Georgetown, Northwestern, Texas A&M, Virginia Commonwealth and Weill Cornell Medicine, alongside Hamad Bin Khalifa University and research bodies such as the Qatar Science and Technology Park. {cite:wikipedia-sheikha-moza-bint-nasser} {cite:muslim500-sheikha-moza-bint-nasser}

## International initiatives
She leads several global initiatives: Educate A Child, launched in 2012 to bring primary schooling to out-of-school children, which has worked with partners to reach millions of children across dozens of countries; Silatech, founded in 2008 to connect Arab youth to employment and enterprise; and the Qatar Fund for Development for external aid. She has chaired the board of Qatar University and served as UNESCO Special Envoy for Basic and Higher Education, in which capacity she pressed for the protection of education in conflict zones. {cite:wikipedia-sheikha-moza-bint-nasser}

## Honours and public standing
She holds honorary memberships and doctorates from several universities and appears regularly on the Forbes list of the world's most powerful women. Her public image combines personal visibility with the actual chairmanship of large educational institutions, and she is presented in Arab and Western media as the architect of Qatar's wager on education as a path toward a knowledge economy. {cite:wikipedia-sheikha-moza-bint-nasser}

## Legacy and reported debate
Supporters of her education project argue that she redrew the map of higher education in the Gulf by importing prestigious universities and opening them to Qatari and Arab students; some Western writers criticise her through the lens of Qatar's wider regional role, and some question the economic return on Education City — the museum reports these views attributed, without verdict. Her most durable legacy is a generation of Arab graduates educated in world-class branch campuses in Doha rather than abroad. {cite:wikipedia-sheikha-moza-bint-nasser}
