## Summary
Abu Ishaq Ibrahim ibn Umar ibn Ahmad ibn Ibrahim ibn Isma'il al-Barmaki was a Hanbali jurist of Baghdad, born in 361 AH and died in 445 AH {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.36}.

## His character
Ibn Abi Ya'la says in *Tabaqat al-Hanabila* that he was devout, ascetic, a jurist, expert in the law of inheritance and other fields, and that he had a teaching circle in the Mansur mosque {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.36}.

## His transmission of Fada'il al-Sahaba
The editor names him, with [[person/ibn-al-mudhhib|Ibn al-Mudhhib]], as one of the two pupils of [[person/al-qatiqi|Abu Bakr al-Qati'i]], who transmitted [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]] from Abd Allah ibn Ahmad {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.36}. A margin note in the book's unique manuscript, "this hadith is not in al-Barmaki's copy", shows that the manuscript was collated with his copy {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.36}. The title page states that Ibn Nasir transmitted the section from the beginning of the merits of Uthman to the end of the merits of Ali from Abu al-Husayn al-Tuyuri, from Abu Ishaq al-Barmaki, from Ibn Malik al-Qati'i, and that the merits of al-Hasan, al-Husayn, Fatima and Khadija also came through Abu Ghalib al-Qazzaz al-Muqri' from him {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.54}.
