## Overview
Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Amr ibn Abd al-Khaliq al-Basri al-Bazzar, a memoriser and well-known hadith scholar. He was known as al-Bazzar because he worked in flaxseed oil. In his old age he spread his hadith in Isfahan, Baghdad, Egypt, Mecca and Ramla. He was born in 210 AH (825 CE) and died in 292 AH (905 CE). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## Lineage and upbringing

He was Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Amr ibn Abd al-Khaliq ibn Khallad ibn Ubayd Allah al-Ataki al-Azdi of Basra, known as al-Bazzar. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.15} The sources give no exact date of birth; al-Dhahabi says he was born in the 210s AH, in Basra, where he grew up in a family devoted to hadith: his father and his maternal uncle Muhammad ibn al-Farz were narrators, and he relates from both in his Musnad. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.15} He heard hadith before the age of ten from Musa ibn Isma'il al-Minqari (d. 223 AH), related from [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] and Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, and turned to writing in his youth. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.16}

## Travels

The sources do not say he travelled to seek hadith; he seems to have relied on the scholars of [[place/basra|Basra]] and those who came there. In old age he travelled to spread his hadith: in Baghdad, says [[person/abu-al-shaykh-al-isfahani|Abu al-Shaykh]], its memorisers gathered and knelt before him to write; in Egypt he related his Large Musnad from memory; and he went twice to [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]], the second time in 286 AH, relating his Small Musnad there. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.17}

## Standing

The thesis quotes praise from his contemporaries and later scholars: Ibn Abi Khaythama called him a pillar of Islam, likened to Ibn Hanbal in his asceticism and scrupulousness; Abu al-Shaykh called him one of the world's memorisers; and [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib al-Baghdadi]] called him trustworthy and a memoriser who wrote the Musnad, discussed its hadiths and showed their defects. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.28} There is also criticism: [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] reported that he erred in chains and texts, having related the Musnad in Egypt from memory and erred in many hadiths, and that [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]] criticised him; see [[question/was-al-bazzar-reliable|Was al-Bazzar reliable?]]. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.29}

## Works and death

Of his works only [[work/musnad-al-bazzar|the Large Musnad]] and a book on invoking blessings on the Prophet, in manuscript in the Hüseyin Çelebi library in Bursa, have come down; biographers also name a Small Musnad, a Sunan, dictations (Amali), a book on drinks and the prohibition of intoxicants, and a part on whose hadith is to be left or accepted. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.31} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.32} He died in [[place/ramla|al-Ramla]] in Palestine in Rabi' al-Awwal 292 AH, the majority view; Ibn Qani' reported from his son that he died in 290 AH. His son Abu al-Abbas Muhammad, a trustworthy narrator from whom al-Daraqutni heard, died in 339 AH. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.33}
