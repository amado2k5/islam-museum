## Summary

Muhammad Nasiruddin al-Albani (1914–1999) was a hadith scholar of Albanian origin who lived between Damascus and Amman, among the most famous examiners of the isnads of the great hadith collections in the twentieth century and founder of the Salafi method of 'authentication and weakening'.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-nasiruddin-al-albani}

## Life and formation

Born in Shkodër, Albania, he migrated as a child to Damascus, learned under the scholars of Syria and specialised in hadith and its sciences, worked in Damascus and Amman libraries, and taught many students.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-nasiruddin-al-albani}

## Works and method

His books include Silsilat al-Ahadith al-Sahihah, Silsilat al-Ahadith al-Da'ifah wal-Mawdu'ah, Sifat Salat al-Nabi and Al-Irwa' fi Takhrij Ahadith Manar al-Sabil. His method judges narrations by chain strength and hidden defects, grading reports of the Sunna by soundness.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-nasiruddin-al-albani}

## Controversy and standing

Supporters call him the renewer of contemporary hadith science; traditional scholars objected to his grading verdicts and to a fiqh that binds itself to no school. His hadith school shaped Salafism worldwide. He died in Amman in 1999 — the museum records both descriptions.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-nasiruddin-al-albani}
