# Asma Jahangir

Asma Jilani Jahangir (27 January 1952 – 11 February 2018) was a Pakistani human-rights lawyer and activist who co-founded and chaired the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan and spent almost four decades defending women, children, religious minorities and prisoners in Pakistan. She served as United Nations Special Rapporteur on extrajudicial executions (1998–2004), on freedom of religion or belief (2004–2010) and on the situation of human rights in Iran (2016 until her death), and in 2010 became the first woman president of the Supreme Court Bar Association of Pakistan. {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-en} {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-ar} {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-ur} {cite:wikidata-asma-jahangir}

## Early life and education

Jahangir was born and raised in Lahore into a prosperous, politically active Urdu-speaking Muhajir family of Kakazai Pashtun origins. Her father, Malik Ghulam Jilani, was a civil servant who entered politics on retirement and was repeatedly jailed or held under house arrest for opposing military dictatorships, among them for denouncing the government's conduct in what is now Bangladesh; her mother, Begum Sabiha Jilani, was educated at Forman Christian College and ran her own clothing business. Jahangir protested against her father's detention as a schoolgirl, took her BA at Kinnaird College and her LLB at Punjab University Law College in 1978, and was called to the Lahore High Court in 1980 and the Supreme Court in 1982. {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-en}

## Activism

In the 1980s Jahangir became a pro-democracy activist and was jailed in 1983 for taking part in the Movement for the Restoration of Democracy against the Zia-ul-Haq regime. With her sister Hina Jilani she set up the AGHS Legal Aid Cell in Lahore in 1986, Pakistan's first free legal aid centre, which also runs the Dastak shelter for women; in 1987 she co-founded the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan, serving as its secretary-general and then, from 1993, its chairperson. She spent her career defending the rights of women, children and religious minorities, was a staunch critic of the Hudood Ordinances and the blasphemy laws enacted under Zia-ul-Haq's Islamization programme, campaigned against bonded labour, child labour and capital punishment, and took cases for women who had married by choice against rulings that required a guardian's consent. She was detained again under the 2007 emergency declared by Pervez Musharraf, played a prominent part in the Lawyers' Movement, and in 2017 became the first Pakistani to deliver the Amartya Sen Lecture at the London School of Economics, calling for a counter-narrative of liberal politics against religious intolerance. {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-en} {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-ar}

## Death and legacy

Jahangir died in hospital at Lahore on 11 February 2018, aged 66, of a brain haemorrhage following a stroke. She had received, among many honours, the Martin Ennals Award (1995), the Ramon Magsaysay Award (1995), the Four Freedoms Award for freedom of worship (2010), the North–South Prize (2012), Pakistan's Hilal-i-Imtiaz and — posthumously in 2018 — the Nishan-e-Imtiaz and the United Nations Prize in the Field of Human Rights. The Guardian described her as having "towered over Pakistan's human rights war" for almost four decades; The New Yorker called her Pakistan's "most visible and celebrated — as well as most vilified — human-rights lawyer". {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-en} {cite:wikipedia-asma-jahangir-ar}
