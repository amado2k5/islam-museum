## Summary
Pakistani chemist of Indian origin (1897–1994), a pioneer of natural-products chemistry. He isolated bioactive alkaloids from Rauwolfia and neem, founded Pakistan's Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (PCSIR) and the HEJ Research Institute of Chemistry at the University of Karachi. {cite:wikipedia-salimuzzaman-siddiqui}

## Life and education

Salimuzzaman Siddiqui was born in 1897 in Lucknow, British India, where he received his early education, studying philosophy and Persian before turning to chemistry. He travelled to Germany and earned a doctorate in chemistry from the University of Frankfurt in 1927. {cite:wikipedia-salimuzzaman-siddiqui}

After returning to India he worked in several research institutions, including the Indian Council of Medical Research, where he began his celebrated investigations of Indian medicinal plants. {cite:wikipedia-salimuzzaman-siddiqui}

## Research and institutions

Siddiqui produced pioneering work isolating bioactive alkaloids from Rauwolfia serpentina, a plant of traditional Indian medicine, and trained a Karachi research team that elucidated the structures of numerous compounds; his research later extended to the neem tree, which became one of his signature subjects. {cite:wikipedia-salimuzzaman-siddiqui}

After settling in Pakistan following Partition, he founded the Pakistan Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (PCSIR) in 1953 and chaired it for years, then established the Postgraduate Institute of Chemistry at the University of Karachi — later named the HEJ Research Institute after his colleague Husain Ebrahim Jamal — which became a leading centre of natural-products chemistry in the region. {cite:wikipedia-salimuzzaman-siddiqui}

## Legacy

Siddiqui received several international and national honours, including Pakistan's civil awards and the Nishan-e-Imtiaz, and was a Fellow of the Royal Society. He is remembered in Pakistan as one of the founders of its organised scientific research. {cite:wikipedia-salimuzzaman-siddiqui}

He died in Karachi in 1994; the institute he established still bears the HEJ name and draws researchers in organic and natural-products chemistry from around the world. {cite:wikipedia-salimuzzaman-siddiqui}
