## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Abbad al-Tamimi, called later Muhammad ibn Abbad and Ibn Abbad, was over the chancery of [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] with Umayya ibn Sudus ibn Shayban, and inclined to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] and his school. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.17} After the entry into Sana'a he and Abu al-Atahiya were the men closest to al-Hadi. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.18} The identity of "Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Abbad" with "Muhammad ibn Abbad" is inferred from the context and is not stated.

## His death at Shibam
Al-Hadi left him as deputy at Shibam when he climbed Jabal [[place/bayt-dhukhar|Bayt Dhukhar]]; the opponents entered Shibam and he was killed, in Jumada II 288 AH. Al-Hadi came down from the mountain, drove them off, entered Shibam and buried Ibn Abbad. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.19} See [[event/revolt-at-shibam-ibn-abbad-killed-288]].

## The vizier of Abu al-Atahiya on p. 110
On p. 110, in the account of Abu al-Atahiya, the book names his vizier as "a man called Muhammad ibn Abi Abbad of Tamim", one of those with affection and desire for the truth who wrote to al-Hadi. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.110} He is most likely the man killed at Shibam in 288 AH, but the text here gives no name for his father and does not link him to that event, so the identity is not asserted. See [[event/abu-al-atahiya-promises-horse-and-sends-his-brother-jarrah-285]].
