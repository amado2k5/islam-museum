## Summary
Keinan Abdi Warsame (born 1 February 1978 by the English article; Wikidata carries two further dates in the same year), known professionally as K'naan, is a Somali-Canadian rapper, singer-songwriter and filmmaker, born in Mogadishu, Somalia. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} {cite:wikidata-knaan} He fled the Somali civil war with his family to New York and then Toronto at the age of thirteen, and rose to international fame with "Wavin' Flag", chosen as Coca-Cola's promotional anthem for the 2010 FIFA World Cup. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

He won a Juno Award for his debut album The Dusty Foot Philosopher (2005), two Junos for Troubadour (2009), a Grammy Award in 2024 for the song "Refugee" in the category Best Song for Social Change, and the FIPRESCI Prize at the Toronto International Film Festival for his directorial debut, Mother Mother (2024). {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

## Career
K'naan was born in Mogadishu into an artistic family: his grandfather was a famous poet and his aunt Magool a renowned singer, and his name means "traveller" in Somali. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} He spent his childhood in Mogadishu; after the civil war began, three of his friends were shot when he was twelve, and he narrowly escaped death at his school when he mistook a grenade for a potato and threw it away before it detonated, which prompted his mother to seek a visa so the family could join his father, who worked as a taxi driver in New York. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} At thirteen he moved with his mother and two siblings to the United States, staying in New York for half a year before relocating to Toronto, Canada, where his family still resides. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

In Toronto he learned English partly by listening to hip-hop albums by artists such as Nas and Rakim, teaching himself rap diction phonetically before he could speak the language, and then began rapping himself. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} In 1999 he performed a spoken-word piece before the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees criticising the UN's failed peacekeeping missions to Somalia; the Senegalese singer Youssou N'Dour, in the audience, invited him to contribute to his 2001 album Building Bridges, through which K'naan toured the world. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

His debut album The Dusty Foot Philosopher was released in 2005, won the Juno Award for Rap Recording of the Year in 2006 and the BBC Radio 3 Award for World Music in the newcomer category for 2007. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} Troubadour followed in February 2009 and widened his fame; across his albums he has collaborated with artists including Mos Def (Yasin Bey), on the track "America", Nas, Nelly Furtado and Adam Levine. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

## Works
His studio albums are The Dusty Foot Philosopher (2005), Troubadour (2009) and Country, God or the Girl (2012), the last met with little of Troubadour's acclaim; after its release he published an article in the New York Times explaining and apologising for the change in his sound under pressure from label executives and A-list producers. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} In 2012 he published a children's book, When I Get Older: The Story Behind Wavin' Flag, and contributed with Howard Shore to the Cosmopolis (2012) soundtrack. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

He recorded versions of "Wavin' Flag" with local artists in many countries — including an Arabic version with Nancy Ajram — and the song reached the Top 10 in eleven countries and number one in six; he performed it during the FIFA World Cup Trophy Tour, which took him to 86 countries. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} His film Mother Mother (2024), his debut as a director, premiered in the Discovery programme at the Toronto International Film Festival and won the FIPRESCI Prize. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out positions and responses as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

K'naan has remained committed to his Somali roots and is often regarded as a spokesperson for the Toronto Somali community: invited by the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation in 2007 to reflect on the removal of the Islamic Courts Union in Somalia, he declared his support for the Union, pushed back against Western critiques of Muslim governing systems, and has spoken against the clan system in Somalia and in immigrant communities in Canada. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en} His early song "Soobax" — "come out" in Somali — criticises the warlords who held power in Mogadishu, and his songs mix English and Somali. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

In 2011 he became a co-spokesman with Bono to raise awareness of that year's drought in East Africa, and he promoted the Canadian Bill C-393 to increase medical assistance to African countries, which was not passed by the Senate. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}

In September 2024 he was charged in Quebec City with sexual assault over an alleged incident in 2010; he pleaded not guilty, his trial began in March 2026, and in September 2026 the judge found him not guilty, both accounts being plausible and so leaving reasonable doubt. {cite:wikipedia-knaan-en}
