## Summary
Akbar Shah Husaini, son of the saint [[person/shah-raju|Shah Raju]], is described by Mark Zebrowski as a mystical poet, a passionate student of Hindu philosophy and literature and an intimate friend of Sultan [[person/abul-hasan-qutb-shah|Abul Hasan]]. He translated Hindu books into Persian and wrote treatises in Sanskrit and other Indian languages. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 186}

## Under Abul Hasan
The author suggests that the rise of Hindu influence under Abul Hasan — the ministry of the Telugu Brahmin Madanna and the issuing of farmans in Persian with Telugu translation — may reflect Akbar Shah Husaini's ecumenical influence; this is the author's assessment. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 186} Two portraits of him survive, among them Akbar Shah Husaini Reading a Book of Prayers, signed by Rahim Khan. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 174} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 186}
