## Summary
He met Abu Khaythama on the road seeking the Prophet; they travelled together until they drew near Tabuk, and Abu Khaythama asked him to fall behind so he could come to the Prophet first. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/164}

## In the Tabuk expedition
Ibn Ishaq names him in Abu Khaythama's report: he caught up with Abu Khaythama on the road while seeking the Prophet, and they travelled together until they drew near Tabuk. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/164} Abu Khaythama asked him to fall behind so he could reach the Prophet first, and he did. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/164}

## Tribe
Ibn Ishaq's lineage for him: of Banu Jumah. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/164}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- The book does not state here that this "Umayr ibn Wahb" is the well-known Umayr ibn Wahb who became Muslim after Badr; the museum has not merged the two Companions.

## In Khalid Muhammad Khalid's Rijal Hawl al-Rasul
[[person/khalid-muhammad-khalid|Khalid Muhammad Khalid]] titles his chapter "Devil of the Jahiliyya, Disciple of Islam". He says that at Badr Umayr was one of the Qurayshi leaders, sent to estimate the Muslims' numbers; he rode round their camp and reported "three hundred men, a little more or less", warning that they were men "with no defence or refuge but their swords" — and that the Meccans called him "the devil of Quraysh". {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.324} He then relays from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr how Umayr sat with Safwan ibn Umayya in the Hijr after Badr, Safwan taking on his debt and family if he would kill the Prophet; how he came to Medina girt with his sword on the pretext of ransoming his captive son; and how the Prophet told him what had passed between him and Safwan, at which he embraced Islam, saying no one but he and Safwan had been present. The Prophet said: teach your brother the religion, teach him the Qur'an and free his captive. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.325} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.326} He relates that Umayr asked leave to return to Mecca to call its people to Islam, and many accepted Islam through him; and that on the day of the conquest of Mecca he sought safe-conduct for his kinsman Safwan ibn Umayya, who had fled towards Jidda, and the Prophet gave him his turban as a sign of it — Safwan came back with him and later embraced Islam. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.328} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.329} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.330} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.331}
