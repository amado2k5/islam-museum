## Summary

Samar bint Muhammad Badawi ⁦(born 28 June 1981)⁩ is a Saudi human rights activist whose decade-long record of lawsuits, campaigns, arrests and awards made her one of the most documented figures of the Saudi women's rights movement: she sued her own father under the guardianship system, campaigned for women's driving and the vote, received the US International Women of Courage Award in 2012, and was imprisoned in 2018–2021. {cite:wikipedia-samar-badawi} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 184}

## Early life

Born in Jeddah to a Saudi father and a Lebanese mother, she is the sister of the writer and activist Raif Badawi. According to reporting gathered in the sources, she suffered physical abuse from her father for years, and in March 2008 fled to a women's shelter in Jeddah, the Protection Home. {cite:wikipedia-samar-badawi}

## The adhl case

Her father, as her male guardian under Saudi Arabia's guardianship system, filed a charge of disobedience ⁦(ʿuquq)⁩ against her; she answered with a suit of her own — adhl, the juristic term for a guardian who unlawfully prevents a woman's marriage — asking the court to remove him as guardian. After she missed trial dates an arrest warrant issued, and she was imprisoned on 4 April 2010. In July 2010 the Jeddah General Court ruled in her favour; she was released on 25 October 2010 and her guardianship was transferred to an uncle, after a local and international support campaign. {cite:wikipedia-samar-badawi}

## Driving and the vote

In 2011 she joined the women driving campaign, driving regularly from June and helping women drivers deal with police and court procedures; that November she and Manal al-Sharif filed charges before the Grievances Board against the traffic directorate for refusing their licence applications. She also sued the municipal affairs ministry for rejecting her voter registration for the 2011 municipal elections. On 8 March 2012 the US State Department gave her the International Women of Courage Award. {cite:wikipedia-samar-badawi}

## Arrests and release

A travel ban followed in 2014, and a brief arrest in January 2016. In July 2018 she was arrested again alongside other women's rights activists; Canada's public call for her immediate release that August triggered a major diplomatic rupture between Ottawa and Riyadh. She was released in 2021 under restrictions reported by rights monitors. {cite:wikipedia-samar-badawi}

## Assessment

Badawi's record is unusual in that nearly every episode is documented through courts and official acts rather than journalism alone: a guardianship case won, a suffrage suit, a driving prosecution, an international award, two imprisonments. The museum reports the course of events and the competing characterisations of them — activist heroism to her supporters, lawbreaking to her prosecutors — without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-samar-badawi} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 184}
