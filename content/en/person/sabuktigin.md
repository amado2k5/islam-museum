## Overview
Amir Sabuktigin Abu Mansur Nasir al-Dawla, father of Sultan Mahmud; he reached Bukhara in the days of the Samanid Nuh ibn Mansur in the retinue of Abu Ishaq ibn Alptigin as his chamberlain, and the state's leaders recognised his valour and firmness and foresaw his rise. When Abu Ishaq went out as governor of Ghazna and took possession, Sabuktigin shared the departure, commanding his men and guarding his frontier; when Abu Ishaq died leaving no kin fit to succeed, the people agreed to give Sabuktigin the command and pledged to him. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Rule and death
Once secure he launched raids on the marches of India, taking many fortresses in wars beyond description as his realm widened; he reached Balkh from Tus, fell ill, longed for Ghazna and set out in that state, dying on the road in Sha'ban 387 — his coffin was carried to Ghazna — and the poets of his age mourned him, among them his secretary Abu'l-Fath al-Busti. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
