## Summary
Fatou Bensouda is a Gambian jurist who broke successive ceilings: the first woman to serve as The Gambia's Minister of Justice and Attorney General, then the first African and first Muslim woman to serve as Chief Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court in The Hague (2012–2021), and her country's High Commissioner to the United Kingdom since 2022. {cite:wikipedia-fatou-bensouda}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1961 in Banjul, studied law at the University of Ife in Nigeria and at the International Maritime Law Institute in Malta where she was the first woman to graduate, then returned to The Gambia to begin a rising judicial career. {cite:wikipedia-fatou-bensouda}

## National and international career
She rose through the Gambian judiciary to become director of public prosecutions and then Minister of Justice and Attorney General (1998–2000). She moved to the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR) in Tanzania as prosecutor and then deputy prosecutor, working on the major genocide cases. She joined the ICC in 2004 as deputy prosecutor under Luis Moreno Ocampo, and in 2012 the Assembly of States Parties elected her unanimously Chief Prosecutor for a nine-year term. {cite:wikipedia-fatou-bensouda} {cite:muslim500-fatou-bensouda}

## As Chief Prosecutor
She opened investigations into sensitive files including Georgia, Ukraine, Palestine and Afghanistan, and faced intense political pressure: in 2020 the United States imposed sanctions on her by executive order in response to the Afghanistan and Palestine investigations — unprecedented measures against an international official, lifted in 2021 — while she also drew accusations of bias and slowness from various quarters. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-fatou-bensouda}

## After the court
In 2022 she was appointed High Commissioner of The Gambia to the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth, returning from international criminal justice to national diplomacy with a dual file: international law and bilateral relations. {cite:wikipedia-fatou-bensouda}

## Recognition and legacy
She holds international awards for human rights and legal leadership and is presented in the literature of international justice as the first African to sit in the prosecutor's seat in The Hague at a time of the court's hardest political tests: investigations involving great powers and retaliatory sanctions. {cite:wikipedia-fatou-bensouda}
