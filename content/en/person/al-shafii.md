## Overview
Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i al-Muttalibi, imam of the Shafi'i school, who died in the days of al-Ma'mun. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.518}

## His death
Al-Mas'udi says he died on the night of Friday in Rajab 204 AH, and was buried the next morning at the age of fifty-four. [[person/al-sari-ibn-al-hakam|Al-Sari ibn al-Hakam]], the governor of Egypt, prayed over him. He was buried in the cemetery of the Banu Abd al-Hakam. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.417-418}

## In al-Suyuti
Al-Suyuti relates that al-Mutawakkil regretted not having reached his time, and mentioned a dream he related from the Prophet about Ibn Idris. Al-Suyuti considers that al-Mutawakkil was a follower of the Shafi'i school, and that he was the first of the caliphs to adopt it. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.545-546}

## Related works
The museum's data attribute to him the book [[work/ikhtilaf-al-hadith-shafii|Ikhtilaf al-Hadith]] {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan}, and make him the founder of [[group/shafii-madhhab|the Shafi'i school]].

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 10)

The Sheikh reports from Imam al-Shafi'i that he thought poetry diminishes the dignity of scholars, and mentions that the Arabs said the most eloquent poetry is the most untrue {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:30:30}.

## For Sprouts
Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i was a great scholar who lived more than twelve hundred years ago; he gathered learning in Mecca, Medina, Iraq and Egypt and wrote the foundations of fiqh. Many Muslims follow his school today, and among his books are al-Risala and al-Umm.

## For Explorers
Abu Abdullah Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i (150–204 AH), the imam of the Shafi'i school, was born in Gaza and raised in Mecca, memorizing the Qur'an and the Muwatta young. He travelled for hadith to Mecca, Medina, Iraq and Yemen, was among Malik's foremost students, then settled in Baghdad and then Egypt where he completed his school and wrote most of his works. He authored al-Risala in legal theory — the first known book on the discipline — and al-Umm in fiqh, and many imams followed his school. He died in Egypt where his tomb is.

## For Scholars
Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i al-Qurashi al-Muttalibi (150–204 AH) is the imam of the Shafi'i school and the first to compose a separate book on legal theory. Born in Gaza, he was brought to Mecca as a child, memorized the Qur'an and the Muwatta, and travelled for hadith between the Hijaz, Iraq and Yemen; he kept close to Malik in Medina until his death, and argued with the Hanbalis, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan and the Iraqis, taking and replying. He settled in Baghdad then Egypt, where his school took its final form and he dictated the 'new' versions of his books. His works include al-Risala — the first known written treatise on usul al-fiqh — al-Umm, the Mukhtasar of al-Muzani and the Sunan, and fine verses on asceticism are told of him. He laid a method joining the people of hadith and opinion, building on Book, Sunna, consensus and analogy; among his students are al-Rabi' al-Muzani and al-Buwayti. He died in Fustat in 204 AH and is buried there, and his school spread through Egypt, Syria, Yemen and Southeast Asia.
