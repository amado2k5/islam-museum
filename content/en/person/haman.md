## Summary
The Sheikh describes Haman as a dangerous figure mentioned six times in the Qur'an, Pharaoh's right hand, and says: how many Hamans there are in ancient and modern history (needs_source: the count as the Sheikh gives it). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:02:00}

## The Sheikh's account of the hieroglyphs
He says that when he studied the scriptures before the Qur'an he found no mention of Haman in the Torah or the Gospel. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:02:30} He relates that Champollion came to Egypt with the French expedition, and that the Rosetta Stone, kept in his account in the British Museum and the Egyptian Museum, is partly in Greek and partly in hieroglyphs; once the hieroglyphic letters were deciphered, it was found among Pharaoh's ministers in Musa's time that Haman was minister of fortresses and building works, a 'minister of construction', which he links to Pharaoh's order to Haman to build him a tower. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:04:00} He holds that this proves decisively that the Qur'an is God's word, and says European archaeologists embraced Islam because of it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:04:30}
