## Summary
Palestinian-American philosopher of comparative religion and Islamic thought (1921–1986). He founded the Islamic studies program at Temple University, co-founded the International Institute of Islamic Thought (IIIT) and championed the 'Islamization of Knowledge' project; his books include Al-Tawhid: Its Implications for Thought and Life and the Cultural Atlas of Islam. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-raji-al-faruqi}

## Life and career

Ismail al-Faruqi was born in Jaffa, Palestine, in 1921, educated in its schools and at the American University of Beirut, then emigrated to the United States where he took a doctorate in philosophy at Indiana University in 1952, pursuing further studies at al-Azhar and other American universities. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-raji-al-faruqi}

He taught at American and Canadian universities, then founded and led the Islamic studies program at Temple University in Philadelphia from 1968, attracting students from across the Muslim world who later became professors in their home countries. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-raji-al-faruqi}

## Work and project

His books include Al-Tawhid: Its Implications for Thought and Life, the Cultural Atlas of Islam, Christian Ethics and Islam in the major-religions series; his writing aimed to present the Islamic vision to Western university students in academic language. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-raji-al-faruqi}

In 1981 he co-founded the International Institute of Islamic Thought (IIIT) in Virginia and promoted the 'Islamization of Knowledge' project, which calls for rebuilding the human sciences on the foundations of tawhid — a project still pursued in several academic institutions. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-raji-al-faruqi}

## Death and legacy

In May 1986 al-Faruqi and his wife Lamya were stabbed to death in their Philadelphia home, a crime that puzzled investigators and shocked the American Muslim community and the academic world. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-raji-al-faruqi}

He is counted among the founders of university Islamic studies in North America, leaving behind an academic program, an institute and textbooks still taught; the 'Islamization of Knowledge' project still draws support and debate in Muslim academic circles. {cite:wikipedia-ismail-raji-al-faruqi}
