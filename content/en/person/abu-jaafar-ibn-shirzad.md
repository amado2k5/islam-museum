## Overview
Abu Jafar ibn Shirzad, secretary to [[person/tuzun|Tuzun]], who ruled Baghdad at the end of Tuzun's time. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 610-613}

## In Baghdad
Tuzun sent him from Wasit to Baghdad, where he ruled and gave orders and prohibitions. He guaranteed the thief Hamdi against robbery in Baghdad for twenty-five thousand dinars a month. Hamdi would raid people's houses with torches and candles. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 610-613}

## After Tuzun's death
Tuzun died in the days of al-Mustakfi, and Ibn Shirzad hoped for the kingdom and swore the troops to himself. The caliph gave him a robe of honour, then Ibn Buyah entered Baghdad and he went into hiding. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 610-613}

Ibn al-Athir says that he hid with al-Mustakfi when Baghdad was in turmoil with the approach of [[person/muizz-al-dawla|Muizz al-Dawla]], and that his rule lasted three months and twenty days. He then reappeared, and Muizz al-Dawla gave him the kharaj and the collection of revenues. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 336}

## In Miskawayh's Tajarib al-umam
In [[work/tajarib-al-umam|Tajarib al-umam]], [[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] follows Ibn Shirzad's career through 332-334 AH in detail.

### Tuzun's secretary
Among the events of 332 Miskawayh relates that [[person/tuzun|Tuzun]] had an epileptic fit while holding audience; Ibn Shirzad and Musa ibn Sulayman leapt up, held a cloak in front of his face and screened him from the people so that they would not see him, and the people were told that the amir "had a rush of bile from a hangover". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.93} His service as Tuzun's secretary lasted "two years and seventeen days". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.111}

### His command in Baghdad
When Tuzun died in Muharram 334, Ibn Shirzad was at Hit; the army agreed to give him the command. He camped at Bab Harb at the beginning of Safar, and on Friday 2 Safar the whole army agreed on him and swore to him. He made [[person/al-mustakfi-billah|al-Mustakfi]] swear to him twice, before the judges and notaries and then before the chief Turks and Daylamites. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.111} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.112} He had meant to hand the command to Nasir al-Dawla, but when Nasir al-Dawla sent him flour and bills of exchange worth five hundred thousand dirhams, which made little difference to the shortage, he dropped the plan and kept the command himself. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.112}

Ibn Shirzad raised the pay of the Turks and Daylamites; the shortage grew worse, so he turned to confiscations and levied sums on secretaries, tax agents, merchants and all classes to pay the troops. Two informers known as Harut and Marut came to him before dawn to denounce people, until the merchants fled and ruin followed. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.112} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.113} When Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Buwayh reached Bajisra, he and al-Mustakfi went into hiding; his command had lasted "three months and twenty days". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.114}

### Secretary to Mu'izz al-Dawla
Mu'izz al-Dawla asked the caliph's leave to employ him and the caliph granted him safety; Ibn Shirzad reappeared and managed the land tax and revenue collection. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.115} After al-Mustakfi's deposition he served al-Muti' "in the place of the viziers, without the title of vizier". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.119} Later Mu'izz al-Dawla demanded a hostage from him because he found him "evasive in money matters", and he handed over his brother Abu al-Hasan Zakariyya. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.119}

Miskawayh relates, from Thabit, from Abu al-Faraj ibn Abi Hisham, that Abu al-Faraj told Mu'izz al-Dawla that Ibn Shirzad had never been counted among the competent secretaries. Mu'izz al-Dawla replied that he had not employed him for his competence, but because he had found him holding the command in Baghdad, master of the caliphate and his equal, and wanted "to bring him down from that position to making him a secretary". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.120}

### With Nasir al-Dawla
Ibn Shirzad went into hiding again on Monday 9 Sha'ban, then reappeared to receive Jubayr ibn Abd Allah ibn Hamdan, brother of [[person/nasir-al-dawla|Nasir al-Dawla]], when he reached Baghdad, and managed affairs on Nasir al-Dawla's behalf. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121} In [[event/battle-of-baghdad-946|the war]] he enlisted the common people and the ayyarun, and went out on the river every day in armed boats full of Turks shooting arrows at the Daylamites. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.123} When the Daylamites crossed he urged Nasir al-Dawla to ride out; riding out himself, he saw the men in flight and fled with them, going with Nasir al-Dawla and the Turks to Ukbara. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.126} Afterwards Mu'izz al-Dawla granted Ibn Shirzad's estates among the lands he gave away, as told in [[event/muizz-al-dawla-iqta-334|the grants of the Sawad]]. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.128} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.129}
