## Overview
Tahir ibn al-Husayn, an Abbasid commander, who took Baghdad in the year 198 AH and became governor of Khurasan in the year 205 AH {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.8 p.472} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.8 p.577}.

## His title
al-Mas'udi reports that he was given the title "Dhu al-Yaminayn" (Lord of the Two Hands), because on the day of al-Rayy he struck Abbas ibn al-Layth with both his hands on his sword {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.375-376}.

## The siege of Baghdad
al-Mas'udi reports that he camped in the orchard known as the Tahiri Gate of the Rams, then moved from al-Yasiriyya to Bab al-Anbar. When al-Amin asked for safe conduct, Tahir said, in substance, that he would not accept until al-Amin placed his hand in his and came down under his judgement {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.383-385}.
