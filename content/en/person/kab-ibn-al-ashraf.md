## Brief
Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf was a poet of Banu Nadir who turned his craft to war after Badr — elegies to make Mecca weep and rise, satires of Muslim women to wound. He was assassinated at Medina with the Prophet's sanction.

## Life
A Jew through his father of Banu Nadir and Arab through his mother, Ka'b rode to Mecca after [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and composed elegies for its dead that the sources credit with stoking Quraysh's revenge; back in Medina he satirized Muslim women in verse {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

In the autumn of 624 a band of the Ansar led by Muhammad ibn Maslama killed him at his stronghold — the sources record that the Prophet authorized it, asking "who will deal with Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, for he has harmed God and His Messenger?" {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

:::perspectives
Sunni tradition narrates the killing as an authorized act of war against a combatant-poet whose incitement after Badr made him a belligerent.

Historians treat it as a political assassination in Medina's factional struggle, and note the sources differ on the details and on how directly the Prophet directed it.
:::

## For young visitors
Ka'b fought with poems instead of swords — and in that war it was enough to get him killed.
