## Brief
Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, a poet of Banu Nadir, who turned his art to war after Badr: elegies that mourned Mecca and stirred it up, and satire of the women of the Muslims that wounds. He was assassinated in Medina with the Prophet's permission.

## Life
Jewish through his father, of Banu Nadir, and Arab through his mother, Ka'b rode to Mecca after [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and composed elegies for its dead. The sources attribute to these elegies the incitement of Quraysh to take revenge. He then returned to Medina and satirised the women of the Muslims in verse {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

In the autumn of 624 CE a band of Ansar led by Muhammad ibn Maslama killed him in his fortress. The sources record that the Prophet authorised it, saying: "Who will deal with Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf? He has harmed God and His Messenger" {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## For young visitors
Ka'b fought with poems instead of swords, and in that war it was enough to get him killed.
