Salih Safvet Bašić (1886–1948) was a Bosnian pedagogue from Duvno (now Tomislavgrad), born into a merchant family and educated at the mekteb and primary school of his home town before secondary school and the Faculty of Philosophy in Istanbul, where he stood out among the Bosnian students for his advanced views and his uncompromising opposition to the Austro-Hungarian occupation of Bosnia and Herzegovina.{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-hr} Returning home in 1910, he declined to seek office under a government he opposed and lived by trade until the First World War forced him to take a teaching post at the Sharia school in Sarajevo rather than be mobilised into the Austrian army; he was known as a connoisseur of Oriental languages, literature and history, and as a lecturer and pedagogue.{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-bs}

Under the Islamic Community's new constitution of 1936 he was appointed the first naib (deputy) of the religious leadership, seated in Belgrade, serving until Fehim Spaho's election as reis-ul-ulema in 1938; on the reis's death in 1942 he was appointed acting reis-ul-ulema, and he held the community's helm until Ibrahim Fejić's installation in 1947 — never formally holding the reis title himself.{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-hr} His wartime record is marked by refusal: the Ustaša government's imposed constitution for the Islamic Community never entered into force on his watch, and in 1945 he rejected Pavelić's demand that the Supreme Islamic Leadership sign an appeal against the national liberation movement.{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-hr} He retired to Duvno and died there in 1948.{cite:wikipedia-salih-safvet-basic-bs}{cite:wikidata-salih-safvet-basic}
