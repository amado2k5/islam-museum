## Summary

Wajid Ali Shah (30 July 1822 – 1887) was the eleventh and last King of Awadh, reigning from 13 February 1847 until the British annexed his kingdom on 11 February 1856 {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-en}.

## Exile and the arts

Deposed on a charge of misrule, he was exiled to Garden Reach at Metiabruz, a suburb of Calcutta, where he lived on a pension and recreated the court culture of Lucknow around him {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-en}. He was a poet, playwright and prolific writer — his Sawat-ul-Qalub runs to more than a thousand pages — and a dancer and patron under whom Kathak reached new heights and the Lucknow gharana took shape {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-en}. His first wife was Alam Ara, called Khas Mahal; his second, Muhammadi Khanum, better known as Begum Hazrat Mahal, led the resistance at Lucknow in 1857 as regent for their son Birjis Qadr {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-en}. At Metiabruz he built the Sibtainabad Imambara, and there he was buried {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-ar}.

## Faith

His faith is recorded as Shia Twelver, documented. The Arabic article gives his religion as Muslim Shia {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-ar}, and the English Oudh State article states, citing the Encyclopaedia of Islam, that the Nawabs of Oudh were Shia Muslims who promoted Shia as the state religion {cite:wikipedia-oudh-state-en}; his own documented practice matches the Twelver context — he founded the Sibtainabad Imambara, a Husainiya for the Muharram rites, and was buried in it {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-ar}. Under the founder's mapping of 1 November 2026 these attestations settle shia-twelver; Wikidata's religion values are structured data and were not counted.

## Notes for researchers

The English and Arabic articles and Wikidata agree on his birth (30 July 1822, paired in Arabic with 12 Dhu al-Qa'da 1237 AH) at Lucknow {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-ar} {cite:wikidata-wajid-ali-shah}. His death date is debated — the English and Arabic articles give 1 September 1887, the Arabic pairing it with 13 Dhu al-Hijja 1304 AH, while Wikidata gives 21 September 1887 {cite:wikipedia-wajid-ali-shah-ar} {cite:wikidata-wajid-ali-shah}. Lucknow and Calcutta have no museum place records yet, so no place edges are recorded.
