## Overview
Najm al-Din Abu'l-Jannab al-Khuyuqi Ahmad ibn 'Umar ibn Muhammad, the ascetic and exemplar, shaykh of Khwarizm known as "al-Kubra"; he travelled the lands on foot and mounted and met more shaykhs than can be counted, donning the Naharjuri Sufi cloak from Shaykh Isma'il al-Qasri, and outstripped his peers in solving difficult questions while still young. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## His learning and martyrdom
He heard al-Hafiz Abu'l-'Ala in Hamadan and al-Silafi in Alexandria, devoted himself to the Shafi'i school and tafsir — he wrote a commentary in twelve volumes — and Fakhr al-Din al-Razi acknowledged his merit; he was a refuge for strangers, of great standing, fearing no blame. According to Ibn al-Ahdal he was martyred at Khwarizm during the Mongol cataclysm: he sent his companions away to escape, refused to leave, called the prayer with those who remained, then fought until an arrow struck him. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}
