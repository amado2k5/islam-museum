## Summary

Fatima Mernissi (1940–2015) was a Moroccan sociologist and university professor, among the best-known feminist writers in the Arab world, combining sociological research with critical rereading of Islamic sources.{cite:wikipedia-fatima-mernissi}

## Life and formation

Born in Fez, she studied in Morocco and then at Brandeis University in the United States, where she took a doctorate in sociology, returning as professor at Mohammed V University in Rabat and writing in French and Arabic.{cite:wikipedia-fatima-mernissi}

## Books and method

In Le Harem politique (The Veil and the Male Elite, 1987) she examined hadiths used to exclude women from politics and questioned the authenticity of some; other books studied fear of modernity, Islam and democracy, and the women Companions. Her method blends source rereading with comparative sociology.{cite:wikipedia-fatima-mernissi}

## Controversy and standing

The West celebrated her as a pioneer of "Islamic feminism", while many Muslim scholars saw her hadith discussion as breaching the discipline's methods — the museum records both positions without a verdict.{cite:wikipedia-fatima-mernissi}
