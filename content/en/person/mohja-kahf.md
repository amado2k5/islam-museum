## Summary
Mohja Kahf is a Syrian-American writer who moves between poetry, the novel and the academy. Her writing centres on the veil, identity and migration, and is read within post-9/11 literature retelling American Muslim experience. {cite:wikipedia-mohja-kahf}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Syria in 1967, she migrated with her family to the United States as a child, studied comparative literature to the doctorate and became a university professor. {cite:wikipedia-mohja-kahf}

## Career and works
Her best-known works include the novel The Girl in the Tangerine Scarf, about a Muslim girl growing up in America; E-mails from Scheherazad, reshaping the female voice of the Arabic heritage; and Hagar Poems, recovering the figure of Hagar in the Abrahamic imagination. {cite:wikipedia-mohja-kahf}

## Method and contribution
She uses narrative and verse to dismantle stereotypes of Muslim women without abandoning the insider position of a Muslim speaker. Critics say her voice widened the presence of Muslim women in American letters, and her writing is sometimes described as bold in questioning tradition; the museum attributes these descriptions to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-mohja-kahf}
