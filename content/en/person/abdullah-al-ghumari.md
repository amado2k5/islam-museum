## Summary
Moroccan hadith scholar and jurist (1910–1992) of the scholarly Ghumari family of Tangier. One of the leading Moroccan muhaddiths of the twentieth century, he authored dozens of works on hadith, jurisprudence and literature, including Al-Ibtihaj and Tawjih al-Inaya. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-al-ghumari}

## Life and formation

Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn al-Siddiq al-Ghumari was born in 1910 in Tangier, Morocco, into a long-established house of learning and hadith, and was raised with his brothers Ahmad and Abd al-Aziz in the study circles of their father, the scholar Muhammad ibn al-Siddiq al-Ghumari, who taught in Tangier before emigrating to Egypt. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-al-ghumari}

He memorised the Qur'an young and studied under his father and the scholars of his time in Morocco, Egypt and the Hijaz, receiving hadith authorisations from many teachers until he was counted among the most reliably transmitted authorities of Morocco. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-al-ghumari}

## Works

He authored dozens of books and treatises on hadith and its sciences, jurisprudence, literature and refutations, among them Al-Ibtihaj bi-Adab al-Isnad, Tawjih al-Inaya li-Tahrir al-Tanbih and Al-Qawl al-Muqni', a hadith rejoinder to the scholar al-Albani. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-al-ghumari}

His style was marked by wide command of the great hadith compilations and boldness in grading reports; he conducted well-known scholarly polemics with contemporaries, documented in published exchanged letters. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-al-ghumari}

## Legacy

Together with his brothers Ahmad and Abd al-Aziz he is counted a pillar of a scholarly family that revived hadith authorship in modern Morocco; hadith scholars and researchers across East and West studied under him, and his treatises are still taught and critically edited. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-al-ghumari}

He died in 1992 and was buried in Tangier; the Ghumari name remains synonymous with hadith scholarship in contemporary Morocco. {cite:wikipedia-abdullah-al-ghumari}
