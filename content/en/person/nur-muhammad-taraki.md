## Summary
Nur Muhammad Taraki (14 July 1917 – 9 October 1979) was an Afghan communist politician, journalist and writer. He founded the People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan (PDPA) and was its general secretary from 1965 to 1979, and he headed the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan as chairman of the Revolutionary Council from April 1978 until Hafizullah Amin overthrew him in September 1979; he was murdered the following month. {cite:wikipedia-nur-muhammad-taraki-en}

A note on sources: the Arabic Wikipedia article named for him narrates a different life — born in 1913 at Paghman, with economics degrees from Columbia and Harvard — which contradicts the English article and Wikidata and appears to conflate him with Hafizullah Amin. The museum registers that article with the caveat recorded, and takes no fact from it. {cite:wikipedia-nur-muhammad-taraki-ar}

## Early life and career
He was born at Nawa in Ghazni Province, the oldest of three children of a Ghilji Pashtun peasant family of the Tarakai clan. Wikidata gives his birth as 15 July 1917, one day later than the English article, so the date is carried as debated. He attended a village school in Nawa, left at fifteen to work as a clerk in Bombay, where night courses brought him into contact with Indian communists, and later graduated from Kabul University. {cite:wikipedia-nur-muhammad-taraki-en} {cite:wikidata-nur-muhammad-taraki}

He became deputy head of the Bakhtar News Agency and a known author and poet; his best-known book, De Bang Mosaferi, treats the hardships of Afghan workers and peasants, and his socialist-realist writings were translated and praised in the Soviet Union. After work at the Afghan embassy in Washington — where he publicly denounced the royal government and was recalled — and at the United States mission in Kabul, he founded a translation bureau and then, in 1965, the PDPA. {cite:wikipedia-nur-muhammad-taraki-en}

## Leadership
The PDPA split in 1967 into his Khalq faction and Babrak Karmal's Parcham. After the assassination of the leftist Mir Akbar Khyber in April 1978 and the arrest of PDPA leaders, the Saur Revolution of 27 April 1978 overthrew and killed President Daoud Khan, and on 1 May Taraki became chairman of the Revolutionary Council, combining the roles of head of state and head of government. {cite:wikipedia-nur-muhammad-taraki-en}

His short rule was marked by a purge of Parchamites, mass repression centred on Pul-e-Charkhi prison, and radical Marxist policies — land reform, the end of forced marriage, women brought into political life — that challenged Afghanistan's traditional structures and its deep Islamic religious culture and provoked a growing rebellion. Amin, his protégé, cultivated a cult of personality around him as "Great Leader" and "Great Teacher", then turned against him: Taraki was overthrown on 14 September 1979 and murdered on Amin's orders, on 9 October by the dating the museum follows (the English article's body and the Urdu article give 8 October), while Kabul press reported that he had died of illness. {cite:wikipedia-nur-muhammad-taraki-en} {cite:wikipedia-nur-muhammad-taraki-ur}
