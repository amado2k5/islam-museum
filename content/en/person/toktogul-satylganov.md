# Toktogul Satylganov

Toktogul Satylganov (25 October 1864 – 17 February 1933) was a Kyrgyz akyn — an improvising poet-singer — and composer, the best-known folk musician of Kyrgyzstan; the town of Toktogul in the Jalal-Abad region is named after him, and his portrait appears on the Kyrgyz 100 som note {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en}.

## Life

He was born in the village of Kushchusu in the Ketmen-Töbö valley, then in the Khanate of Kokand; the village now lies under the waters of the Toktogul Reservoir {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en}. The Kyrgyz article's infobox names Sasyk-Zhiide, where he died, as his birthplace, against the English and Russian articles and the Great Soviet Encyclopedia tradition {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-ky} {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-ru}. He was already a well-known poet and composer with democratic views in the Tsarist colonial era in southern Kyrgyzstan {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en}.

On the eve of the 1898 Andijan revolt led by the Sufi ishan Madali, Toktogul was harshly criticising the local Kyrgyz lords of the Ketmen-Töbö valley. His political foes accused him of taking part in the revolt — a false accusation, the English article states — and he was jailed and exiled to Siberia, to the village of Kuitun near Irkutsk, returning in 1905 {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en}.

## Soviet fame

His fame reached its height in the Soviet era, when the state promoted his works as those of a musician of the people and he was known across Kyrgyzstan simply as "Toktogul" {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en}. The distinction rested largely on his pre-revolutionary works, which Soviet reading interpreted as reflecting class struggle; modern interpretations suggest they had more to do with clan rivalries {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en}. He welcomed the revolution in song. He died on 17 February 1933 at Sasyk-Dzhiide {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en} {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-ru}. His songs remain popular among Kyrgyz performers, and streets, parks and schools across the country bear his name {cite:wikipedia-toktogul-satylganov-en}.
