## Overview
Abu Talib Muhammad b. 'Ali b. 'Atiyya al-Harithi, the preacher of Mecca, author of Qut al-qulub; he was not of Mecca's people but of al-Jabal — he settled at Mecca and was named for it. A righteous man strenuous in worship who used riyada much — it is said he abandoned food so long his skin greened. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His preaching
He entered Basra after Abu'l-Hasan Ibn Salim's death and attached himself to his doctrine; he came to Baghdad and preached, but muddled his speech — he was left and shunned, and kept from speaking thereafter. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), signed by L. Massignon, describes Abu Talib al-Makki as a mu'tazili-minded theologian and mystic who died in Baghdad in 386/996-8, and as head of the dogmatic school of the Salimiyya in Basra. His chief work is the Qut al-Qulub, whole pages of which al-Ghazali copied into his Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din. The article lists as sources Murtada's Ithaf, al-Sha'rani's Latai'f and Ibn 'Abbad al-Rundi's al-Rasa'il al-Kubra. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 172}

## His episode in Baghdad, as told in Talbis Iblis
[[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] relates through al-Khatib al-Baghdadi from Abu Tahir Muhammad ibn 'Ali al-'Allaf that Abu Talib al-Makki entered Basra after the death of Abu al-Hasan ibn Salim and attached himself to his teaching, then came to Baghdad, where people gathered to him in his preaching circle; he became confused in his speech, and it was recorded that he said "Nothing is more harmful to the creature than the Creator", so people declared him an innovator and shunned him, and after that he stopped addressing the public. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 168} Ibn al-Jawzi adds his critique of Abu Talib's [[work/qut-al-qulub|Qut al-Qulub]] for its false hadith and fabricated prayers. {cite:talbis-iblis-muniriyya-1928 p. 168}
