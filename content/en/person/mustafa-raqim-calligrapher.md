## Summary
Mustafa Rakim (1757–1826) was one of the first masters in designing calligraphic panels that could be transferred to other surfaces such as pottery or stuccoed walls. Renowned for his proficiency in large scripts, which he also used for monumental inscriptions, he applied to thuluth jali (Turkish celi) the principles that [[person/hafiz-osman|Hafiz Osman]] had developed for thuluth. He made stencils to reproduce his compositions, some of which survive; he also designed the inscriptions on his brother's gravestone in the Edirnekapı cemetery. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 541} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 565}

## The Sabancı panel
A fabric panel in the Sabancı Collection, made after 1809, shows the quality of his work: a text in thuluth jali in two sizes, its words ingeniously fitted together into large letters, that can be read on three levels of expanding complexity — the names God and Muhammad on the bottom line; a rhyming phrase meaning "God, He is my Lord; Muhammad is my prophet"; and smaller complementary phrases in the interstices that expand it into a full profession of faith. [[person/sheila-blair|Blair]] notes its balance and symmetry and the regular sprinkling of vocalisation and diacritics. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 541} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 542}

## His signature
He fitted his signature, katabahu rakim ("Rakim wrote it"), into a triangular composition at bottom left between the second mim and the final dal of Muhammad, in the style developed for the sultan's tughra, combining the curvilinear elements of thuluth with the unauthorised connections of tawqi': initial ra' is a horizontal S-curve crossing the cross-stroke of kaf, and alif rises from below the baseline to cross qaf-mim. He omitted all dots from it, though others who adopted his style kept some. Blair counts such large compositions among the most notable Ottoman contributions to calligraphy. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 542} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 543}
