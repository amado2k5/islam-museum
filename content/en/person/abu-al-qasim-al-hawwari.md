## Overview
Abu al-Qasim ibn Yusuf ibn Abi al-Qasim ibn Abd al-Salam al-Umawi al-Hawwari al-Awfi was a well-known Hanbali ascetic, the head of the zawiya at Hawwari. Ibn al-Imad lists him among the deaths of 663 AH, which corresponds to 1265 CE by the museum's conversion {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 7 p. 544}. The editor of the Shadharat notes that the spelling of the place name varies between sources and that he found no mention of it in the geographical works available to him {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 7 p. 544}.

## Life
Ibn al-Imad calls him good and upright, with followers, companions and disciples in many villages of Hawran, in the mountain and in al-Bathaniyya, who did not attend listening sessions accompanied by the tambourine {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 7 p. 545}. He died in his town of Hawwari at the end of the year; funeral prayer in absentia was offered for him at Jerusalem on the day of the Feast of Sacrifice, and at Damascus on the nineteenth of Dhu al-Hijja {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 7 p. 545}.

## His successor
His son Abd Allah took his place after him. He is described as having some training in jurisprudence and as given to asceticism, with companions of his own, and as a man sought out and visited in his town {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 7 p. 545}. Ibn al-Imad says he lived to ninety, and that when he set out to bid farewell to some relatives in the direction of al-Karak, on the Hijaz side, death overtook him there at the start of Dhu al-Qa'da in 730 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 7 p. 545}.
