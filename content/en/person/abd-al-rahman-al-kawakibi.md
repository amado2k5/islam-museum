## Overview
Abd al-Rahman ibn Ahmad al-Kawakibi (1849–1902 CE), called al-Sayyid al-Furati, was a traveller, sociologist and one of the men of Islamic reform. {cite:al-aalam tarjama}

## Life
He was born and educated in Aleppo, where he founded the newspaper "al-Shahba'", which the government closed. He was given many posts; then his opponents of reform slandered him, and he was imprisoned and lost his property. He went to Egypt, made two journeys to the lands of the Arabs and to East Africa and some lands of India, and settled in Egypt until he died. {cite:al-aalam tarjama}

## Works
His works include "Umm al-Qura" and "Taba'i' al-Istibdad", which had a wide echo when they appeared. Al-Zirikli says that he was great in his intellect, his resolve and his learning, one of the great men of the modern renaissance. {cite:al-aalam tarjama}

The year of his death is converted from around 1320 AH, and the conversion is not certain. {cite:al-aalam tarjama}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes al-Kawakibi, who died in 1902, as a Syrian Islamic revivalist and advocate of an Arab caliphate, and gives two further names for him, al-Sayyid al-Furati and Traveler K. It says he was influenced by the reform ideas of [[person/jamal-al-din-al-afghani|Jamal al-Din al-Afghani]], who died in 1897, and of [[person/muhammad-abduh|Muhammad Abduh]], who died in 1905. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1246}

It summarises his programme as a call to bring Islam back to its first purity. In its account he held that foreign notions and distortions, including mysticism, fatalism, sectarianism and uncritical imitation, had bred ignorance and docile obedience to rigid scholars and tyrannical rulers, who curbed liberties, spread a false religion and damaged the community's moral, social, educational and financial order, and it gives the [[place/ottoman-empire|Ottoman]] state as the example. He called for Arab independence and for an elected Arab caliph whose powers would be limited, as the footing for Islam's revival. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1246} This is the Dictionary's summary of al-Kawakibi's view; its description of mysticism as a corrupting influence is the entry's wording, not this record's judgement.
