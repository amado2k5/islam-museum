# Bibi Khanoom Astarabadi

Bibi Khanoom Astarabadi (c. 1858/59–1921) was an Iranian writer and pioneer of women's education, best known for the satire Ma'ayib al-Rijal and for founding one of Iran's first schools for girls in Tehran. {cite:wikipedia-bibi-khanoom-astarabadi-en}

## Life

She was born in Astarabad (present-day Gorgan) into a family in royal service: her father, Mohammad Baqer Khan, held the court office of ishik aqasi bashi, and her mother Khadijeh Khanom, a daughter of the Mazandarani mojtahed Molla Kazem, ran the school of the royal andarun. Taught by tutors at home, she married Musa Khan Vaziri, a military officer from the Caucasus, at about thirteen, and had seven children. {cite:wikipedia-bibi-khanoom-astarabadi-en}{cite:wikipedia-bibi-khanoom-astarabadi-fa}

## The Failings of Men

In 1894/95 she wrote Ma'ayib al-Rijal (The Failings of Men), a point-by-point satirical reply to the anonymous Ta'dib al-Nisvan (The Chastisement of Women), which had instructed women in unquestioning obedience. Her answer attacked polygamy, domestic violence and the denial of education to women, and the work is counted among the earliest feminist writings in Iran. {cite:wikipedia-bibi-khanoom-astarabadi-en}

## The School for Girls

In 1907 she opened the School for Girls (Madreseh-ye Dokhtaran) in her own Tehran home, teaching girls a curriculum that included Persian literature, Arabic, arithmetic, history and geography. Religious scholars attacked the school — one fatwa declared her an apostate — but she defended it in person before the minister of education, and it continued. She died in Tehran in 1921. {cite:wikipedia-bibi-khanoom-astarabadi-en}{cite:wikipedia-bibi-khanoom-astarabadi-fa}
