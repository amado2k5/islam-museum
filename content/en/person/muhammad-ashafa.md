## Summary
Imam Muhammad Ashafa is a Nigerian preacher and peacemaker from Kaduna who, together with Pastor James Wuye, lived a story now taught in peace schools worldwide: the two were young leaders of rival Muslim and Christian militias in northern Nigeria in the early 1990s, and became partners who co-founded the Interfaith Mediation Centre (IMC), documenting their reconciliation in the film The Imam and the Pastor. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ashafa}

## Upbringing and formation
He was born into a Muslim religious family in northern Nigeria, trained in Islamic studies and preaching and qualified as an imam in Kaduna State mosques, then specialised in peacebuilding and conflict resolution in local and international training institutions. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ashafa}

## From enmity to partnership
In the sectarian violence of Kaduna in the early 1990s Wuye lost an arm in clashes and Ashafa lost companions and teachers; the two stood on opposite sides of the conflict. They met in 1995 through a local initiative, beginning a slow process that turned the enemies into friends and co-founders of the Interfaith Mediation Centre (IMC) in Kaduna, which today is run by co-executive directors: an imam and a pastor. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ashafa} {cite:muslim500-muhammad-ashafa}

## The mediation centre
The centre led programmes to defuse sectarian violence in several Nigerian states: mediation between Muslim and Christian communities, local peace committees, training of former militia youth, and seasonal interventions protecting Muslim and Christian festivals from attack. Under its auspices the historic Kaduna peace agreement between Muslim and Christian religious leaders was signed, and its model was carried — in training and consultancy — to African, Asian and multilateral bodies, with Ashafa advising governmental and non-governmental organisations. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ashafa}

## Documentation and honours
The reconciliation was filmed in The Imam and the Pastor (2006), shown at international festivals and used as a peace-education tool; the two men received international peacebuilding awards including the Bremen Peace Prize and religious and humanitarian distinctions, and were granted honorary recognition by academic and international institutions. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ashafa}

## Legacy
The Ashafa–Wuye experiment is read as the strongest living African example of personal reconciliation made reproducible: they did not stop at ending fighting but built an institution turning former enemies into permanent local mediators — a model taught in conflict-resolution programmes from Africa to Asia. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ashafa}
