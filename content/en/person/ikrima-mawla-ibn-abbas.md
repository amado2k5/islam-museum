## Overview
Ikrima, known by the kunya Abu Abdallah, a client of Abdallah ibn Abbas. He died in 105 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.376-377}

## The disputation
Al-Mas'udi says that he disputed with his master over the one who was commanded to be sacrificed: was it Isaac or Ishmael? He argued for Isaac from the Qur'an. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.376-377}

## Death
He died in 105 AH, on the day that Kuthayyir Azza died. People said: "The greatest of the jurists and the chief of the poets have died." In the same year al-Sha'bi also died. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.376-377}

## In al-Taqrib
Ibn Hajar lists him under no. 4673: "Ikrima, Abu Abdallah, the client of Ibn Abbas, Berber by origin: firmly trustworthy, a scholar of Qur'anic exegesis; his being belied on the authority of Ibn Umar is not established, nor is an innovation established from him; of the third (generation); he died in 104 AH, and it was said later." {cite:taqrib-al-tahdhib p.437}

## The critics' judgements
Ibn Hajar cites Kashif al-Dhahabi: he narrated from his master Ibn Abbas, from Aisha and from Abu Hurayra; Ayyub, al-Hadhdha', Abd al-Rahman Abu al-Ghasil and many narrated from him; he was reliable though an Ibadi who held the doctrine of the sword; Muslim narrated for him in a joined report while Malik avoided him; he died in 106 AH, and it was said 107. {cite:taqrib-al-tahdhib p.437} In Marasil Abi Zur'a he narrates from Abu Bakr and Ali as mursal reports, and Abu Hatim says he did not hear from Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas nor from Aisha; Ibn Hajar remarks that his narration from Aisha in Sahih al-Bukhari weighs in favour of his having heard from her, and that Abu Hatim's report from his father contains a contradiction. {cite:taqrib-al-tahdhib p.437}

## Note on his death year
The death date is disputed: Muruj al-Dhahab gives 105 AH, al-Taqrib "104, and it was said later", and Kashif al-Dhahabi "106, and it was said 107". All three reports stand on record with their disagreement. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.376-377}{cite:taqrib-al-tahdhib p.437}

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
[[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] opens his entry in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] by saying that Ikrima, client of Ibn Abbas, was "one of the vessels of knowledge; he was criticised for his opinion, not for his memory, and was accused of the Kharijite view". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93} He notes that a group declared him trustworthy, that [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] relied on him, that [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] avoided him and cited him only a little, paired with others, and that [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] turned away from him except in one or two hadiths. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93}

**Praise of his learning.** Al-Dhahabi reports that [[person/jabir-ibn-zayd|Jabir ibn Zayd]] would send questions to Ikrima, saying: this is the client of Ibn Abbas, this is the sea, ask him; in another version: this is the most learned of men. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93} Shahr ibn Hawshab said: Ikrima is the scholar of this community; [[person/said-ibn-jubayr|Sa'id ibn Jubayr]] was asked whether he knew anyone more learned than himself and answered: yes, Ikrima. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93} [[person/amir-al-shabi|Al-Sha'bi]] said no one was left who knew God's Book better than Ikrima, and [[person/qatada-ibn-diama|Qatada]] said Ikrima was the most knowledgeable of men in exegesis. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 95} [[person/ayyub-al-sakhtiyani|Ayyub]] was asked whether Ikrima was suspect; he was silent for a while, then said: as for me, I did not suspect him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93} Ikrima is reported as saying that [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] used to put a fetter on his foot to make him learn the Qur'an and law, and that he sought knowledge for forty years and gave legal answers at the door while Ibn Abbas was inside the house. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 95} Ahmad ibn Hanbal said: Ikrima was among the most learned of men. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96}

**The attacks on him.** Wuhayb witnessed Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Ansari and Ayyub mention Ikrima: Yahya said "a liar", Ayyub said "he was no liar". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 94} Abd Allah ibn al-Harith reported that he went in to Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas and found Ikrima tied up at the door of the latrine, and Ali said: this wretch lies about my father. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 94} Ibn al-Musayyab is reported to have told his client Burd: do not lie about me as Ikrima lied about Ibn Abbas; the same is reported of Ibn Umar to Nafi', but it is not sound. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 97} [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Ibn Sirin]] said: it would not grieve me if he were among the people of Paradise, but he is a liar; Ibn Abi Dhi'b said: he was not trustworthy. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 94} [[person/tawus-ibn-kaysan|Tawus]] said: had Ibn Abbas's slave feared God and held back from his talk, riding camels would have been saddled to reach him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 94} Muhammad ibn Sa'd said: he had much knowledge and hadith, a sea among seas, but his hadith is not relied on and people speak against him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 94} Al-Dhahabi records that [[person/hammad-ibn-zayd|Hammad ibn Zayd]], on the last day of his life, related a saying that Ayyub attributed to Ikrima about the ambiguous verses of the Qur'an, and al-Dhahabi comments: "what an evil phrase". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 94}

**The Kharijite view.** Ibn al-Madini reported, from Ya'qub al-Hadrami from his grandfather, that Ikrima stood at the mosque door and said: there is none inside but an unbeliever; and that he held the Ibadi view. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 95} Yahya ibn Bukayr said that Ikrima came to Egypt on his way to the Maghrib, and that the Kharijites of the Maghrib took from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96} Ibn al-Madini said he held the view of Najda al-Haruri; Mus'ab al-Zubayri said he held the Kharijite view and claimed that Ibn Abbas held it too; and [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata']] is reported as saying that he was an Ibadi. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96} Ahmad said that he held the view of the Sufriyya and left no region unvisited — Khurasan, Syria, Yemen, Egypt and Ifriqiya — coming to governors in search of their gifts. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96} The general question is set out in [[question/riwayat-ahl-al-bida|narration from people of innovation]].

**His sale and freeing.** Ibn Sa'd reported, from al-Waqidi, that Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas sold Ikrima to Khalid ibn Yazid ibn Mu'awiya for four thousand dinars; Ikrima told him: you have sold your father's knowledge; so Ali asked to cancel the sale, cancelled it and freed him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 95}

**His death.** Mus'ab al-Zubayri said that the governor of Medina sought him, and he hid with Dawud ibn al-Husayn until he died in his house. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96} Al-Dhahabi reports that he and [[person/kathir-azza|Kuthayyir Azza]] died on the same day; in one account people attended Kuthayyir's funeral and left Ikrima's, in another only the black slaves of Medina attended either. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96} Al-Dhahabi gives three dates for his death: the year 105 according to a group, 106 according to al-Haytham and others, and 107 according to another group. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Jabir ibn Zayd, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Sha'bi, Qatada and Ayyub, and al-Bukhari**: a sea of learning and the most knowledgeable in exegesis; al-Bukhari relied on him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 95}
- **Sunni: Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Ansari, Ibn Sirin, Ibn Abi Dhi'b, and those who attributed the Kharijite view to him**: a liar or untrustworthy, or a holder of the Kharijite view; Malik and Muslim avoided him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 94} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 96}
- **Sunni: al-Dhahabi**: criticised for his opinion, not his memory. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93}
