## Summary

Abu al-Muzaffar Muhammad Anusha Khan (died 1686) was khan of Khwarazm (Khiva) from 1663 to 1686, the only son of the khan Abu al-Ghazi Bahadur and his successor, and he continued his father's policy of strengthening the khan's power.{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-ru}{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-en}

## The campaigns against Bukhara

Anusha took part in his father's military campaigns from his adolescence; in about 1657, during Abu al-Ghazi's invasion of Karmina, he was fourteen years old, when his father's surrounded army broke through the troops of Abdulaziz Khan and scattered the Bukhara army.{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-en} He then launched repeated raids on the Bukhara Khanate, briefly took Samarkand in 1685 but was soon forced to leave it, and once captured the city of Bukhara itself and had the khutba preached in his own name.{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-ru}{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-en}

## Russia and building at Khiva

In 1669 and 1675 Anusha Khan tried to restore trade and diplomatic relations with Russia, having sent a letter to Tsar Alexei Mikhailovich in 1668.{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-ru} In his reign a number of buildings were raised at Khiva, among them a madrasa and a bath known by his name, and two great canals were dug, the Shahabad and the Yarmish.{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-ru}

## Deposition and death

His rule ended in a conspiracy of courtiers who deposed and blinded him, and he died afterwards; the Russian article gives his death in 1686 and reports 1687 from the Kazakhstan National Encyclopedia.{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-ru} He had two sons, Khudaydad Khan and Arang Khan.{cite:wikipedia-anusha-khan-en}
