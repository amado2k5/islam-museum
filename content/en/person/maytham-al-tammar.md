## Overview
Maytham ibn Yahya al-Asadi by clientage, known as Maytham al-Tammar, was a slave of a woman of the Banu Asad; Ali ibn Abi Talib bought him from her and freed him, and he remained devoted to him, settling at Kufa afterwards. {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.336}

Its governor Ubayd Allah ibn Ziyad imprisoned him for his attachment to Ali, then had him crucified on a trunk, whereupon he kept recounting the merits of the Banu Hashim; told that the slave was disgracing them, Ibn Ziyad ordered him bridled — making him, as al-Zirikli records, the first man bridled in Islam — and then pierced him with a spear, ten days before al-Husayn's coming to Iraq, in 60 AH (680 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.7 p.336}
