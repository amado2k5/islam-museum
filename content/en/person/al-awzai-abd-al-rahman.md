## Overview
Al-Awza'i, Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr ibn Yuhmid, Abu Amr, a jurist and hadith scholar. The encyclopaedia describes him as the imam of Syria and its first jurist in his time, and gives his birth as 88 AH and his death as 157 AH according to the common report. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

Historians differ on the origin of his nisba to al-Awza'. Al-Mas'udi says his home was in al-Awza' in Damascus and that he was not of them, but was from the captives of the people of Yemen, and that he died in 157 AH at the age of ninety. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 293} Al-Shirazi quotes Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi as saying that no one in Syria knew the Sunna better than him. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p. 41}

## In al-Dhahabi's Siyar
Al-Dhahabi calls him shaykh al-Islam and the scholar of the people of Syria, recording that he lived in the Awza' quarter — the small 'Uqayba outside Bab al-Faradis in Damascus — then moved to Beirut as a frontier devotee until he died; some say he was born in Baalbek. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2202}

He narrated from 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, Abu Ja'far al-Baqir, 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb, Makhul, Qatada, al-Zuhri, Yahya ibn Abi Kathir, Hassan ibn 'Atiyya, Isma'il ibn 'Ubaydallah ibn Abi al-Muhajir, Ibn al-Munkadir, Maymun ibn Mihran and Nafi' the client of Ibn 'Umar; a great many Syrians and travelling students narrated from him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2202}
