## Overview
Theodore Trithurios appears in the Encyclopaedia of Islam as one of the two Byzantine leaders of the army that fought the Muslims at the Yarmuk in 636, where he is called the Sakellarios, the treasurer. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 302} The same encyclopaedia, in its article on Ajnadayn, names the commander of the Greek forces there in 634 as Theodorus, brother of the emperor [[person/heraclius|Heraclius]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 227} The Yarmuk article speaks in one passage of Theodore Trithurios and in the next sentences of "Theodore, the brother of Heraclius", and the two readings fit one man. The articles never put the two descriptions side by side in a single sentence, however, so the identification is the museum's reading and not a statement of the source. The Latinised spelling Theodorus Trithurius is added as an alternative name. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 302} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 227}

## At Ajnadayn, 634
The article on Ajnadayn dates the battle to Jumada I or II of 13 AH, July-August 634, between the Arab invaders and the Greek defenders of Palestine. It says the Greek forces were commanded by Theodorus, brother of Heraclius, and that some early Arabic sources also mention a certain Artabun, possibly Aretion. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 227} The Arab forces, in the article's view, were most probably under [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]], and the numbers on both sides were probably nearer ten thousand than the large figures in the Arabic sources. The Greeks were severely defeated and withdrew to Damascus, leaving Palestine open. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 227} See [[event/battle-of-ajnadayn|the battle of Ajnadayn]] and [[place/ajnadayn|Ajnadayn]].

## At the Yarmuk, 636
The article on the Yarmuk says that Byzantine forces came from Hims, then called Emesa, under the general [[person/vahan-armenian-commander|Vahan]], probably the master of soldiers for the East, and Theodore Trithurios, the Sakellarios. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 302} The author adds that there are contradictory reports on whether Theodore, the brother of Heraclius, was present. He took part in planning some of the campaign, but was probably recalled to Constantinople in disgrace before the final stages of the battle. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 302} The emperor himself was not present at the battle and was staying first at Hims and then at Antioch. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 301} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 302} See [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|the battle of the Yarmuk]] and [[place/yarmuk|the Yarmuk]].

The article also says the Heraclian dynasty seems to have tried to shift responsibility for the disaster away from itself, for example to an alleged abortive rebellion of the Armenian general Vahan, or to a failure to follow Heraclius's instructions about Arab ambushes, or to the climate. It does not tie any of these to Theodore. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 302}

## What the sources do not say
The articles read give neither the dates of Theodore's life nor the reason for his recall, and they give no Arabic form of his name. The Arabic form is left open until it is checked in an Arabic chronicle, and nothing is added here from memory.
