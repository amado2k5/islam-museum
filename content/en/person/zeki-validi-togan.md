# Zeki Velidi Togan

Zeki Velidi Togan (Akhmet-Zaki Validov; 10 December 1890 – 26 July 1970) was a Bashkir historian, Turkologist and leader of the Bashkir revolutionary and liberation movement. He was born at Kuzyanovo in Ufa Governorate, taught at the Qasimiyä madrasa in Kazan in 1912–1915, and in 1915–1917 served in the bureau supporting the Muslim deputies at the State Duma.{cite:wikipedia-zeki-validi-togan-en}

In 1917 he was elected to the Millät Mäclese and, with Şərif Manatov, organised the Bashkir Shuro; at the Bashkir Congress in Orenburg in December 1917 he declared autonomous Bashkiria. Arrested by the Soviet forces in February 1918, he escaped and fought first under Dutov and Kolchak, then, after the RSFSR promised the Bashkirs autonomy, with the Bolsheviks, chairing the Bashkir Revolutionary Committee from February 1919 to June 1920. Disillusioned, he moved to Central Asia and became a leader of the Basmachi movement and chairman of the National Union of Turkistan from 1920 to 1923, before emigrating in 1923.{cite:wikipedia-zeki-validi-togan-en}{cite:wikipedia-zeki-validi-togan-ru}

From 1925 he lived in Turkey and held the chair of Turkish history at Istanbul University from 1927; driven out by controversy in 1932, he took a doctorate at Vienna in 1935 and taught at Bonn and Göttingen. Detained in the Pan-Turkist trials of 1944 and sentenced in 1945, he was released on retrial in 1947. In 1953 he organised the Institute for Islamic Studies at Istanbul University, and in 1967 he received an honorary doctorate from the University of Manchester. He died at Istanbul on 26 July 1970 and was buried in the Karacaahmet cemetery there.{cite:wikipedia-zeki-validi-togan-en}{cite:wikipedia-zeki-validi-togan-ru}{cite:wikipedia-zeki-validi-togan-tr}
