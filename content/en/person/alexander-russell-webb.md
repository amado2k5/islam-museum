## Summary
Alexander Russell Webb (9 November 1846 – 1 October 1916), who took the name Muhammad, was an American journalist, publisher and diplomat. Raised a Presbyterian, he was United States consul at Manila when he declared himself a Muslim in 1888, and became one of the earliest prominent American converts, writing, publishing and lecturing for the faith in the United States and abroad. {cite:harvard-shelf-alexander-russell-webb-2011 post} {cite:aramcoworld-muslim-in-victorian-america-review note} {cite:wikipedia-alexander-russell-webb-en Biography}

## Background
He was educated at Claverack College near Hudson, New York, and worked as a journalist in Missouri, including on the *Missouri Republican* of St Louis. In September 1887 President Cleveland appointed him consul at Manila in the Philippines. The Harvard Library post says he was raised a Presbyterian and left that upbringing to look into other faiths. {cite:wikipedia-alexander-russell-webb-en Biography} {cite:harvard-shelf-alexander-russell-webb-2011 post}

## The path to Islam
The English Wikipedia says he was first drawn to Islam in 1886 through the writings of Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, was visited at Manila by a businessman, Hajee Abdulla Arab, and formally declared himself a Muslim in 1888. The Harvard Library post also dates the conversion to 1888 and adds that he declared himself a Muslim before he had met any Muslims; the AramcoWorld note gives 1887. The museum states both years. The article records that he later called Ghulam Ahmad a man of God, and adds that Ahmadiyya sources do not say whether Webb was an Ahmadi. {cite:wikipedia-alexander-russell-webb-en Biography} {cite:harvard-shelf-alexander-russell-webb-2011 post} {cite:aramcoworld-muslim-in-victorian-america-review note}

## After embracing Islam
After his conversion he toured India, speaking at Poona, Bombay, Calcutta, Hyderabad and Madras and criticising Muslims who imitated Western ways; in 1892 he went to Egypt and Turkey and in 1893 resigned his post and returned to the United States. In [[place/new-york-city|New York]] he founded the Oriental Publishing Company and the journal *The Moslem World*, whose first issue appeared on 12 May 1893 and which ended after seven monthly issues in November 1893, largely for lack of money; he also founded a short-lived mosque on Broadway and study circles. At the Parliament of the World's Religions at Chicago he gave two addresses on 20 and 21 September 1893, and he published *Islam in America* the same year. Later the Ottoman sultan Abdulhamid II appointed him honorary Turkish consul in New York; from 1898 he lived at Rutherford, New Jersey, where he edited the *Rutherford Times*. His life is told in Umar F. Abd-Allah's *A Muslim in Victorian America* (Oxford University Press, 2006). {cite:wikipedia-alexander-russell-webb-en Biography} {cite:harvard-shelf-alexander-russell-webb-2011 post} {cite:aramcoworld-muslim-in-victorian-america-review note}

## Difficulties and reactions
The sources read do not report how his family reacted. They show a venture short of money: his journal closed after seven issues largely for lack of funds, his mosque and study circles were short-lived, and the AramcoWorld note says his experience raised problems that American Muslims still face, among them funding, organisation and community unity. {cite:wikipedia-alexander-russell-webb-en Biography} {cite:aramcoworld-muslim-in-victorian-america-review note}

## Criticism and debate
No criticism of Webb is reported in the sources read. They differ on a point of record: the English Wikipedia and the Harvard Library post date his conversion to 1888, AramcoWorld to 1887, and the Harvard post calls him the first American to have converted where other texts say the first prominent one; the museum uses the narrower wording. The English Wikipedia article is itself tagged for insufficient citations, so only its dates and posts are used. {cite:harvard-shelf-alexander-russell-webb-2011 post} {cite:aramcoworld-muslim-in-victorian-america-review note} {cite:wikipedia-alexander-russell-webb-en Biography}
