# Inayatullah Khan Mashriqi

Inayatullah Khan Mashriqi (25 August 1888 – 27 August 1963), known as Allama Mashriqi, was a British Indian, later Pakistani, mathematician, logician, political theorist and Islamic scholar, and the founder of the Khaksar movement {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}. The Arabic article calls him an Indian Muslim mathematician and scholar {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-ar}, and the Urdu article gives the same birth and death dates {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-ur}.

## Early life and Cambridge

He was born on 25 August 1888 to a Punjabi Muslim Rajput family from Amritsar {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}. Educated first at home and in Amritsar schools, he took a master's in mathematics at the University of the Punjab with first position at eighteen, then moved to England in 1907 and matriculated at Christ's College, Cambridge {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}{cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-ur}. He was awarded a foundation scholarship in May 1908 and first-class honours in Mathematics Part I in June 1909, placed among the wranglers, and went on to take several triposes {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}.

## Tazkirah and public service

In 1924, at thirty-six, he completed the first volume of his book Tazkirah, a commentary on the Qur'an in the light of science; it was nominated for the Nobel Prize in 1925, on condition of translation into a European language, and he declined the translation {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}. He served in the education administration of British India, and eventually settled at Ichhra, Lahore, on his pension {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}.

## The Khaksar movement

Around 1930 he founded the Khaksar movement, a paramilitary organisation aiming both to revive Islam among Muslims and to advance the condition of the masses irrespective of faith, sect or religion {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}{cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-ar}. Its symbol was the belcha, the spade. He was imprisoned repeatedly — under the British and, after 1947, in Pakistan, where he faced charges of attempting to overthrow President Ayub Khan's government; none of the charges was proven and he was acquitted in each case {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}. After an assassination attempt on Muhammad Ali Jinnah on 20 July 1943 by a man assumed to be a Khaksar worker, Mashriqi deplored the attack and denied any involvement {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}.

## His affiliation, as the sources state it

The articles attest the man as a Muslim and an Islamic scholar, born into a Muslim family {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}{cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-ar}; no article text names a legal school for him. Wikidata gives his religion as Sunni Islam {cite:wikidata-mashriqi}, a single structured value, and the Urdu article's categories file him under Pakistani Sunnis {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-ur}; a filing and a structured value together are one source family, so the museum's data record states exactly this and leaves his school unrecorded under the two-source rule.

He died at the Mayo Hospital in Lahore on 27 August 1963, after a short battle with cancer; his funeral prayers were held at the Badshahi Mosque and he was buried at Ichhra {cite:wikipedia-mashriqi-en}.
