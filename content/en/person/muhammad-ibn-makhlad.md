## Overview
Muhammad ibn Makhlad al-Attar was a hadith transmitter who died in 331/942-3. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 856} The Encyclopaedia of Islam has no article of its own on him; it mentions him in its entry on the philologist [[person/ibn-khalawayh|Ibn Khalawayh]], where it names him among Ibn Khalawayh's teachers of hadith. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 856} This record is therefore short, and it says only what that source supports.

## What the source says
The article on Ibn Khalawayh lists several of his teachers: Qur'an readers, grammarians and the hadith teacher Muhammad ibn Makhlad al-Attar. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 856} It points readers to the notice of this teacher in the Tarikh Baghdad (volume iii, pp. 310-311) and to al-Dhahabi's Tadhkirat al-huffaz, eleventh generation, number 40, adding that in al-Dhahabi's text the name Ahmad is given by mistake instead of Makhlad. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 856} It gives his death as 331/942-3. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 856}

Ibn Khalawayh went to Baghdad in 314/926 to study while still young. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 856} The article does not say when he studied hadith under Muhammad ibn Makhlad, but the pairing places the teacher in the Baghdad of those years. The museum does not give him a residence on that basis alone.

## A name to keep apart
The same name appears in the article on [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud al-Sijistani]], which says that Muhammad ibn Makhlad said that the traditionists accepted Abu Dawud's Sunan without question, just as they accepted the Qur'an. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 133} That article does not give a fuller name, a nisba or dates, so it is not shown that this is the same man as al-Attar, and the museum does not merge the two. Other people called Ibn Makhlad in the Encyclopaedia are secretaries and viziers of the Abbasid period, a different group. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 891}

## What is not known
The sources read here give no birth date, no place of birth, no list of his own teachers or students beyond Ibn Khalawayh, and no work by him. A full biography would have to be written from the Tarikh Baghdad entry itself, which has not yet been read for this record.
