## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Ahmad ibn Isma'il, Abu Ishaq al-Khawwas, was a Sufi, the unmatched shaykh of his time and a contemporary of al-Junayd. Born at Sirr man Ra'a (Samarra), he died at the congregational mosque of Rayy in 291 AH (904 CE). Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi says he had composed books. The name al-Khawwas means a seller of palm-leaf wares. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.28}

## In al-Risala al-Qushayriyya
In [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]] al-Qushayri gives a notice of Abu Ishaq Ibrahim ibn Ahmad al-Khawwas: a peer of al-Junayd and al-Nuri, with a large share in trust in God and spiritual disciplines, who died at Rayy in 291 AH; he suffered from a stomach ailment, and each time he rose he made ablution and returned to the mosque to pray two rak'as, until once he entered the water and died in it. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 183} He quotes his sayings: knowledge is not in the amount of narration; the learned man is the one who follows knowledge, acts on it and imitates the Sunna, even if his knowledge is little; and the cure of the heart is five things — reciting the Qur'an with reflection, an empty stomach, standing in prayer at night, humble supplication before dawn, and sitting with the righteous. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 183}
