## Summary
Muhammad Tahir Kamel al-Musawi is a British orthopaedic and peripheral nerve surgeon of Iraqi origin who moved his name from London operating theatres to global attention when he volunteered in Gaza hospitals during the healthcare catastrophe that began in late 2023. Across his missions he performed hundreds of complex operations under wartime conditions and became a face of international medical solidarity with the Strip. {cite:muslim500-muhammad-tahir-surgeon} {cite:aljazeera-muhammad-tahir-surgeon}

## Early life and training
He was born in the 1980s in Wales to an Iraqi family from Najaf, and grew up and studied medicine in Britain, specialising in trauma, upper-limb and peripheral nerve surgery, working as a consultant within the National Health Service in London before joining humanitarian work. {cite:aljazeera-muhammad-tahir-surgeon}

## The Gaza missions
He entered the Strip in April 2024 with a medical delegation of the FAJR Scientific foundation, crossing at Rafah shortly before the crossings closed. Across three missions totalling about seven months he worked between the European Hospital in the south and Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital in the centre, performing hundreds of complex operations — the sources report more than 300 operations and over 1,200 injured treated — including reimplanting the arm of a nine-year-old girl severed in a strike on Deir al-Balah. He declined to leave after his first mission's term, announcing he would stay on his own responsibility, at times operating for more than a full day without pause. {cite:aljazeera-muhammad-tahir-surgeon} {cite:muslim500-muhammad-tahir-surgeon}

## Documentation and testimony
He documented his own experience on social media, filming cases inside the hospitals and gathering millions of followers, and became the subject of The Mission, a documentary by Oscar-nominated filmmaker Mike Lerner following his third mission into the Strip. He appeared in Arab and international media as a witness recounting what he saw inside the collapsed health system. {cite:muslim500-muhammad-tahir-surgeon} {cite:aljazeera-muhammad-tahir-surgeon}

## Public standing
Media present him as the model of the volunteer war surgeon, attributing to him the motto "If not me, then who?" His public image in Arab and international opinion is that of a medical hero, and no substantive political controversies around him are recorded in the sources read. {cite:muslim500-muhammad-tahir-surgeon}

## Legacy
He represents a new generation of Muslim doctors in the West who converted professional skill into direct humanitarian action in disaster zones and made self-documentation a form of testimony; his story is read in relief-medicine literature as one of the most visible volunteer-surgeon accounts of the Gaza crisis. {cite:aljazeera-muhammad-tahir-surgeon}
