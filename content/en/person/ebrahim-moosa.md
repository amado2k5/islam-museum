Ebrahim Moosa (born in South Africa — the English biography's infobox gives 1957, Wikidata 1950, and the year is recorded as debated) is a South African scholar of Islamic thought. He completed his theological training in the early 1980s at Darul Ulum Nadwatul Ulama in Lucknow, India, graduating with a specialisation in the traditional Islamic sciences, and took his doctorate at the University of Cape Town, where he then taught.{cite:wikipedia-moosa-en}{cite:wikidata-moosa}

He was a visiting professor at Stanford (1998–2001), taught religion and Islamic studies at Duke University (2001–2014), and in 2014 moved to the University of Notre Dame as Mirza Family Professor of Islamic Thought and Muslim Societies, co-directing the Contending Modernities programme. His Ghazali and the Poetics of Imagination (2005) won the American Academy of Religion's Best First Book award, and What Is a Madrasa? (2015) introduced the South Asian seminary tradition to a wide readership.{cite:wikipedia-moosa-en} His Indian training was at Nadwa, not Deoband — Deoband is a subject of his research — and no source names a legal school for him, so his record leaves his faith unknown.{cite:wikipedia-moosa-en}{cite:wikidata-moosa}
