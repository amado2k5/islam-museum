## Overview
Harun al-Rashid, son of Muhammad [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]], the fifth caliph of the Abbasids. The sources preserved in the museum show that he was known for his activity on the frontiers with the Byzantines, for pilgrimage and raiding, and for some accounts of pastimes and horsemanship. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.456-461}

## As commander and caliph of the frontiers
Al-Baladhuri reports that he raided the Byzantines as commander of the summer campaign on behalf of his father in 163 AH, besieged Dhamalu, and reached a settlement with its people on terms of safe conduct; they were settled in Baghdad at the place called Samalu. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} In 171 AH he learned that the Byzantines had planned to fortify Tarsus, so he got ahead of them by rebuilding it through Harthama ibn A'yan and Faraj ibn Sulaym. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} In 180 AH he ordered the building and fortifying of the spring of Zarba, and named the frontier cities "the Strongholds". {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} See [[event/captives-of-samalu|the captives of Samalu]] and [[event/founding-of-tarsus|the founding of Tarsus]].

## Caliphate and death
Al-Mas'udi reports that he was given the oath of allegiance on the morning of the night al-Hadi died, in 170 AH, when he was twenty-one years and two months old, and that he died at Tus, in the village of Sanabad, in 193 AH, after a reign of twenty-three years and six months. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.322-325} His last pilgrimage was in 188 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.330-331}

## In the books of biography
Al-Suyuti reports that he prayed one hundred cycles of prayer every day and gave away a thousand dirhams from his wealth every day. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.456-461} Al-Mas'udi mentions that people called his days "the days of the bride" for their brightness and abundant good. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.670} Al-Suyuti, citing Muhammad ibn Ali al-Khurasani, says that he was the first Abbasid caliph to play polo and chess. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.470-471}

The statements on this page are attributed to their authors, and they have not been compared with one another in the museum's data; they need an additional source.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 8)

The Sheikh says that Harun al-Rashid, when dying, called on the One whose kingdom never ends to have mercy on one whose kingdom had ended, and that he pronounced the testimony of faith {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:47:30}.

He then tells an exchange in this context in which a man is asked what he would pay for a cup of water if it were withheld from him and says he would buy it with half his kingdom; asked again about water that went in and did not come out, he says he would buy its release with the other half of his kingdom {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:48:00}.

## For Sprouts
Harun al-Rashid was the fifth Abbasid caliph, acclaimed in 170 AH at twenty-one. He is remembered for the frontier wars with Byzantium, for pilgrimage and campaigning, and people called his days 'the days of the bride' for their splendor; he died at Tus in 193 AH after twenty-three years and six months.

## For Explorers
Harun ibn Muhammad al-Mahdi al-Rashid, fifth Abbasid caliph. Al-Baladhuri records him raiding the Romans as commander of the summer expedition on his father's behalf in 163 AH, besieging Samalu until its people settled for safe conduct; in 171 AH he forestalled the Romans by building up Tarsus under Harthama ibn A'yan and Faraj ibn Sulaym, and in 180 AH ordered 'Ayn Zarba built, naming the border cities 'the Guards'. He was acclaimed caliph in 170 AH at twenty-one years and two months on al-Mas'udi's account; he died at Tus in the village of Sanabadh in 193 AH, his rule lasting twenty-three years and six months, his last Hajj in 188 AH. Al-Suyuti says he prayed a hundred rak'as and gave a thousand dirhams daily; people named his days 'the days of the bride' in al-Mas'udi; and al-Khurasani makes him the first Abbasid caliph to play polo and chess.

## For Scholars
Harun ibn Muhammad al-Mahdi al-Rashid, fifth Abbasid caliph. Al-Baladhuri records him raiding the Romans as commander of the sa'ifa on his father's behalf in 163 AH — besieging Samalu until its people settled for safe conduct and were settled at Baghdad in the place called Samalu — in 171 AH forestalling the Romans' fortifying of Tarsus by building it under Harthama ibn A'yan and Faraj ibn Sulaym, and in 180 AH ordering 'Ayn Zarba built and naming the border cities 'al-Awasim'. He was acclaimed on the very night al-Hadi died in 170 AH, twenty-one years and two months old per al-Mas'udi; he died at Tus in the village of Sanabadh in 193 AH, his rule twenty-three years and six months, his last Hajj in 188 AH. Al-Suyuti says he prayed a hundred rak'as daily and gave a thousand dirhams in alms each day; the people named his days 'the days of the bride' for their splendor per al-Mas'udi; and al-Khurasani calls him the first Abbasid caliph to play polo and chess. These sayings are attributed to their speakers and need additional sourcing.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh relates that a righteous man came in to Harun al-Rashid as he called for a cup of water and asked him: for how much would you buy it if it were withheld while you were dying of thirst? Half my kingdom. And if it stayed in you and would not come out? The other half. Then, said the man, woe to a kingdom not worth a drink of water going in and coming out (needs_source: the story is the Sheikh's narration). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:07:00}
