## Overview
Taqi al-Din Ahmad ibn Abd al-Halim ibn Taymiyya al-Harrani al-Hanbali, known by the title Shaykh al-Islam. A Hanbali jurist and theologian from Harran and Damascus. {cite:aqida-wasitiyya}

## Life
The title page of the Salafiyya Press edition of *al-Aqida al-Wasitiyya* gives 661 AH as his year of birth and 728 AH as his year of death. These dates come from one source and need an additional source. {cite:aqida-wasitiyya}

## His books
He wrote [[work/aqida-wasitiyya|al-Aqida al-Wasitiyya]] in 698 AH, setting out the creed in the manner of the People of the Sunna and the Community and the early generations. {cite:aqida-wasitiyya} He is also credited with [[work/iqtida-al-sirat-al-mustaqim|Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim]] (The Demand of the Straight Path). {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan}

## The dhimmis in 709 AH
Al-Suyuti says that in 709 AH the vizier spoke of returning the dhimmis to wearing white turbans, and that they committed to the treasury seven hundred thousand each year. Ibn Taymiyya spoke out strongly to overturn this, and the matter was dropped. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.734-735}

## Ghazan in 700 AH
Ibn al-Imad says that [[person/ghazan-khan|Ghazan]] crossed the Euphrates with his army and headed for Aleppo. Ibn Taymiyya sent a dispatch by post to Cairo to rouse people to holy war, and met the chief emirs. The people of Damascus were afraid and gathered in the citadel, and the fear subsided when Ghazan turned back. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.429}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 8)
The Sheikh relates that Ibn Taymiyya was asked whether he was a believer, a question hard to answer, and that he first replied that he was if faith meant belief in God, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day and the divine decree (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:26:00}. He says that he answered a second time that he was a believer also if faith meant that the heart trembles when God is mentioned [[passage/quran-8-2|Al-Anfal 2]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:26:30}. The Sheikh recalls the saying of Abu Bakr that, with one foot in Paradise and one outside, he would not feel secure from God's plan (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:27:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy recounts that Ibn al-Qayyim described his teacher Ibn Taymiyya as one who subdued his desire until desire no longer ruled him (needs_source: the description is paraphrased) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 01:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:39:00}.

He cites a saying of Ibn Taymiyya that God grants victory to a just state even when it is not Muslim, and the sheikh repeats the saying in the episode (needs_source: the wording is as the sheikh gives it, without a source in this episode) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:43:30}.

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme (part 1 of 2)
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Ibn Taymiyya as 'Shaykh al-Islam, imam of his age', explaining his name: he was named for his grandmother Taymiyya the preacher — 'see how great women bring forth the great' — and distinguishes him from his grandfather Majd al-Din, author of al-Muntaqa in the hadiths of rulings {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:02:00}.
His general observation: over ninety percent of leading and learned families' children grow into creators in their fields — contrary to common belief (the speaker's view) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:04:00}.
Of his life: born in Harran on Monday 10 Rabi' I 661 AH into a scholarly family, he studied under more than two hundred shaykhs and sat for fatwa and teaching in his father's place at twenty {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:07:30}.
Of his description: fair with black hair and little grey, squarely built, broad-shouldered, deep-voiced, a glint and sharpness in his eyes; quick to anger at times yet quelling it by patience {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:05:00}.
Of his asceticism: al-Bazzar's 'we never saw a scholar content with as little of this world as he' — he never married, took no house, wealth or office, not even the permitted {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:10:00}.
The outing story: when his family returned from a picnic they chided him for staying behind — 'I gained nothing from you; in your absence I memorised this volume', and it was Junnat al-Nazir {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:12:30}.
Of the scholars' testimony: Ibn Daqiq al-Id's 'I saw a man with all the sciences between his eyes, taking of them what he willed', al-Suyuti's 'my eye never glanced one broader in knowledge or quicker in wit' with asceticism and standing for truth, and al-Dhahabi's 'a marvel of intelligence and quickness of grasp, a head in knowledge of Book and Sunna and scholars' differences… counted among jurists he is the absolute mujtahid' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:15:30}.
Of his worship: after dawn prayer, remembrance and supplication until sunrise, speaking to none without need — 'this is my provision' — his limbs trembling in prayer {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:18:00}.
His childhood: his Qur'an teacher offered forty dirhams monthly at his father's behest to spur him, but the boy refused — 'I have covenanted with God not to take payment for the Qur'an' — at seven {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:20:30}.
Of his generosity: passing a beggar while owning nothing, he stripped off his garment and gave it with apology; in knowledge he answered a questioner with the schools' views, the weighing, and related questions until his critics faulted him — the speaker replies 'the fault is ignorance and pride; he loved to benefit' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:23:30}.
Of his humility: he met every questioner with warmth and gentleness until the questioner was the one to leave — sharp in debate, yet with common folk of utmost kindness {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep21 00:24:30}.

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme (part 2)
Al-Suwaidan continues, noting that those who revere Ibn Taymiyya and those who oppose him alike have not truly known him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:02:00}.
Of his courage: he fought the Tatars before and after their occupation of Syria, riding through the army steadying the men and charging himself, sharing in the conquest of Acre by deed and counsel; his saying 'a man fears other than God only for a sickness in his heart'; and the chamberlain's story of placing him at 'the station of death', where he raised his eyes to heaven long, then charged {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:06:30}.
Of his clemency: he was imprisoned for his views some twenty-one times by the count, driven by Egypt's opposing scholars; yet when al-Malik al-Nasir returned and asked him about executing the judges who had harmed him, Ibn Taymiyya magnified them and said 'whoever harmed me is pardoned — I do not seek vengeance for myself', until the sultan forgave them; and Ibn Makhluf's words 'we roused against him and could not prevail; when he prevailed over us he pardoned us and pleaded our case' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:10:30}.
Of his independent rulings: the oath by divorce counts as an oath, not divorce, with expiation; and the triple divorce in one sitting counts as one — both caused his imprisonment, and the speaker notes the latter is the settled view today {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:14:30}.
Of his stands: he bore the banner against innovation and the stray sects; had no quarrel with Sunna-grounded Sufism — counting al-Jilani and al-Junayd among the people of truth — but with the innovators in intercession, grave-invocation and invented devotions; and confronted the Tatars who professed Islam while permitting wine {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:17:30}.
In prison: he taught the inmates fiqh and led them in prayer making a school of them; and his words 'my imprisonment is a retreat, my exile a journey, my killing martyrdom — what can my enemies do to me?' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:18:30}.
Of his works: the collected Fatawa in thirty-seven volumes, al-Fatawa al-Kubra in five, Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa-l-Naql in seven, Minhaj al-Sunna al-Nabawiyya, and al-Sarim al-Maslul ala Shatim al-Rasul {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:21:00}.
Of his death: he died in Damascus' citadel on the night of Monday 20 Dhu al-Qa'da 728 AH after books and ink were denied him — having completed the Qur'an in prison eighty-two and three-quarter times — and hundreds of thousands gathered for his funeral, senior scholars completing his unfinished recitation in his honour {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep22 00:23:30}.

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives Ibn Taymiyya one of the longest entries in the book: born on 10 Rabi' al-Awwal 661, he was brought from Harran by his father. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/144} Ibn Hajar narrates the councils and trials he went through in Damascus and Cairo, and his return to Damascus with al-Nasir's army in Shawwal 712 after more than seven years away. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/145} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/146} He quotes Ibn Fadl Allah that in 700 Ibn Taymiyya came to Cairo by the post to urge jihad. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/152} He died in the prison of the Citadel of Damascus on the night of Monday 20 Dhu al-Qa'da 728. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/149} Ibn Hajar gives al-Dhahabi's praise of his memory, exegesis and independent reasoning, and al-Dhahabi's remark that he did not hold him infallible and differed with him on some questions, and he lists the views attributed to him that people disputed and that lengthened his imprisonment, as reports rather than judgements. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/155} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/156} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/158}
