## Summary
Senegalese novelist and educator (1929–1981) writing in French, a pioneer of African women's literature. Her epistolary novel So Long a Letter (Une si longue lettre) won the first Noma Award for Publishing in Africa in 1980, followed by Scarlet Song; her writing joined a critique of women's condition to social sensitivity. {cite:wikipedia-mariama-ba}

## Life and education

Mariama Ba was born in Dakar, Senegal, in 1929 into a bourgeois Muslim family; she lost her mother young and was raised by her grandmother in the traditional way, studied in Qur'anic school, and was then sent by her father — a minister in the colonial administration — to French schools despite family objections. {cite:wikipedia-mariama-ba}

She excelled in her studies until she earned a teaching diploma from the Rufisque teacher-training school, and worked as a schoolteacher for years before turning to writing and to trade-union and women's activism. {cite:wikipedia-mariama-ba}

## Works

In 1979 she published her first novel So Long a Letter (Une si longue lettre), a series of letters in which a widow writes to her friend reviewing her life in a polygamous marriage that collapsed; it met worldwide acclaim, won the first Noma Award for Publishing in Africa, and was translated into many languages including Arabic. {cite:wikipedia-mariama-ba}

Her second novel, Scarlet Song (Un chant écarlate), was published after her death: it treats a mixed marriage between a Senegalese man and a French woman, and likewise questions of identity and culture; she also wrote articles and speeches on girls' education, inheritance and polygamy. {cite:wikipedia-mariama-ba}

## Legacy

Mariama Ba died in 1981 at 52 after a long illness, before seeing the full extent of her fame; today she is counted a founder of Francophone African women's literature, and her first novel a set text in many universities. {cite:wikipedia-mariama-ba}

Critics describe her as having carried the experience of the African Muslim woman into world literature in a calm, persuasive voice; her novels sparked debates in Senegal on polygamy and women's rights that remain alive. {cite:wikipedia-mariama-ba}
