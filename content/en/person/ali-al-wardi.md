## Summary
Iraqi sociologist, historian and intellectual (1913–1995), a pioneer of sociology in Iraq and the Arab world. He taught at the American University of Beirut and then at the University of Baghdad, applying Ibn Khaldun's method to Iraqi social history in works such as Social Glimpses of Modern Iraqi History, Preachers of the Sultans and The Nature of Iraqi Society. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-wardi}

## Life and formation

Ali al-Wardi was born in Baghdad in 1913 in the Kadhimiya district and received his early schooling there before travelling to the United States for university study. He earned a doctorate in sociology from the University of Texas in 1950 and returned to Iraq to teach and research. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-wardi}

He taught for a time at the American University of Beirut, then settled as professor of sociology in the College of Arts at the University of Baghdad, where he spent most of his academic career and supervised generations of Iraqi students. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-wardi}

## Works and method

His best-known works include the six-volume Social Glimpses of Modern Iraqi History, Preachers of the Sultans, The Nature of Iraqi Society, The Farce of the Human Mind and The Myth of Refined Literature. They combined historical analysis with an engaging style that reached far beyond academia. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-wardi}

Al-Wardi built his analysis on Ibn Khaldun's sociology, reading Iraqi history through the tension between nomadism and urban civilization and through the 'dual personality' of the Iraqi individual between tribal and civic values — theses that remain widely debated by admirers and academic critics alike. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-wardi}

## Legacy

Al-Wardi is among the most widely read Iraqi intellectuals of the twentieth century; his books were translated into several languages and continue to stir debate in Arab social thought. While some scholars criticize his generalizations, his work remains a foundational reference for the study of Iraqi society. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-wardi}

He died in 1995, enjoying in Iraq a broad popular esteem well beyond the university; for many readers his name became synonymous with the project of understanding Iraqi society in accessible scientific language. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-wardi}
