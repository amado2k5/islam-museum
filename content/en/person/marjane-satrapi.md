## Summary
Marjane Satrapi (1969–2026) was an Iranian-French comic-book author, artist and film director. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} Her autobiographical graphic novel Persepolis, about her childhood in Iran and adolescence in Europe after the Iranian Revolution, made her one of the most widely read Iranian authors in the world. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en}

## Life
Satrapi was born in Rasht, in northwestern Iran, and grew up in Tehran in an upper-middle-class family; both her parents were politically active on the left. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} Her family's official registry dates give her birth as 21 September 1969, correcting the long-published 22 November 1969 that the Arabic article still uses. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-ar} At fourteen, in 1983, her parents sent her to Vienna to escape the regime's repression; after a period that included homelessness and a near-fatal illness, she returned to Iran and took a master's degree in visual communication from Islamic Azad University. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} She later settled in France, first in Strasbourg and then in Paris. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en}

She was married to the Swedish actor and producer Mattias Ripa from 1996 until his death in April 2025. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} Satrapi died in Paris in June 2026; her death certificate gives 3 June 2026, while some reporting gave 4 June. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en}

Satrapi kept her own beliefs a private matter. Interviewers described her as not a religious person, and she said she was against fundamentalism rather than against any religion, and that religion is a personal affair she did not make public; the museum therefore records her faith as unknown rather than from the atheist category filings attached to her encyclopedia entries. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en}

## Work
Persepolis was published in French in four parts from 2000 to 2003 and in English in two parts in 2003 and 2004; its film adaptation, which Satrapi co-directed, shared the Jury Prize at Cannes in 2007, and she became the first woman nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} Her other comics include Chicken with Plums and Embroideries, and her films include The Voices and Radioactive, a biopic of Marie Curie. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} In 2023 she edited and co-created Woman, Life, Freedom, an anthology documenting the Iranian protest movement. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en} She received the Princess of Asturias Award in 2024. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-ar} In January 2025 she refused France's Legion of Honour in protest at French visa policy toward Iranians. {cite:wikipedia-marjane-satrapi-en}
