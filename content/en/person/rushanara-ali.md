## Summary
Rushanara Ali is a British Labour Party politician, born at Bishwanath, Sylhet, Bangladesh, on 14 March 1975. She has been a Member of Parliament since 2010, for Bethnal Green and Bow until 2024 and since then for Bethnal Green and Stepney. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en} {cite:wikidata-rushanara-ali}

She served as Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Homelessness and Democracy from July 2024 to August 2025. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

## Early life and education
Rushanara Ali was born at Bishwanath, Sylhet District, Bangladesh. She emigrated with her family to the East End of London at the age of seven, attended Mulberry School for Girls and Tower Hamlets College, grew up in Tower Hamlets, and was the first in her family to go to university, reading philosophy, politics and economics at St John's College, Oxford. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

## Career before Parliament
She began her career as a research assistant to Michael Young, working on a project that paved the way for Tower Hamlets Summer University, and helped to develop Language Line, a national telephone interpreting service in over 100 languages. Between 1997 and 1999 she was parliamentary assistant to Oona King, MP for Bethnal Green and Bow. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

She worked on human rights issues at the Foreign Office from 2000 to 2001, was a research fellow at the Institute for Public Policy Research focusing on anti-discrimination, and from 2002 to 2005 worked in the community cohesion unit at the Home Office, leading a work programme in the aftermath of the 2001 riots in Burnley, Bradford and Oldham. She later worked as associate director of the Young Foundation. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

In March 2009 The Guardian listed her as one of the most powerful Muslim women in Britain. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

## Parliamentary career
She was chosen in April 2007 as Labour's candidate for Bethnal Green and Bow and was elected in May 2010 with a majority of 11,574. She is the first person of Bangladeshi origin to have been elected to the House of Commons, and along with Shabana Mahmood and Yasmin Qureshi became one of the United Kingdom's first female Muslim MPs. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

She served as Shadow Minister for International Development (2010–2013) and then Shadow Minister for Education. In September 2014 she resigned from the shadow education team to abstain on the motion permitting military action against Islamic State in Iraq. In April 2016 the prime minister appointed her Trade Envoy to Bangladesh. In September 2023 she was appointed Shadow Minister for Investment and Small Business. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

She retained her seat at successive elections, and in 2024 won the new Bethnal Green and Stepney seat with a reduced majority of about 1,700. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

## Ministerial office and resignation
After Labour's 2024 victory she was appointed Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Building Safety and Homelessness. In October 2024 she relinquished the building safety portfolio after Grenfell Tower fire survivors called for her resignation over her attendance at a conference sponsored by a firm criticised in the Grenfell Inquiry, and retained homelessness while also taking on the democracy role. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

On 7 August 2025 she resigned as a minister following reports that she had evicted tenants from a property she owned while serving as homelessness minister and later raised the rent. In her resignation letter she said she had at all times followed all relevant legal requirements and was resigning to avoid becoming a distraction for the government. The museum reports her account as given, without a verdict of its own. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum. In 2013 Ali voted in favour of the Same Sex Marriage Bill, which drew support from pro-LGBT activists and condemnation from some mosque imams, according to the article, and she later defended a constituent in an alleged homophobic hate-crime case. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

During the Gaza war she faced criticism from some constituents for following her party's whip on a ceasefire motion; the article also records that she had herself called for a humanitarian ceasefire and continued to do so into 2024. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}

## Reach
Her public significance lies in being the first person of Bangladeshi origin in the House of Commons, one of Britain's first female Muslim MPs, and a community-cohesion researcher and practitioner before she entered Parliament. {cite:wikipedia-rushanara-ali-en}
