## Summary
Chief of Tayy, who came with his people's delegation in 9 AH. He embraced Islam and his Islam was sound. The Prophet named him Zayd al-Khayr {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.453}.

## His account
The delegation of Tayy came, and Zayd al-Khayl was among them. They spoke with the Prophet, who offered them Islam; they embraced it and their Islam was sound {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.453}. The Prophet said of Zayd: "No man of the Arabs has been described to me for a virtue and then come to me without falling short of what was said of him, except Zayd al-Khayl, for he did not reach all that is in him." He named him Zayd al-Khayr {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.453}.

## For young visitors
Zayd was a famous chief among his people, known for generosity and courage. When he met the Prophet, the Prophet was so taken with him that he named him "Zayd the Good".

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
He came in the [[event/delegation-of-tayy|delegation of Tayy]], of which he was the chief. The Prophet offered them Islam, and they embraced it and their Islam was sound {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/224}. Ibn Ishaq, relating from men of Tayy whom he considered trustworthy, gives the Prophet's words about him. The Prophet named him Zayd al-Khayr, granted him [[place/fayd|Fayd]] with its lands, and wrote this down for him {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/224}. When he set out homeward, the Prophet said: "If Zayd survives the fever of Medina..." and called it by a name that the narrator did not preserve. When Zayd reached a water in Najd called [[place/farda|Farda]], the fever struck him and he died. As he sensed death he recited two lines about his people's departure and his being left behind in a tent at Farda {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/224}. After his death his wife burned the documents the Prophet had granted him {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/224}.

## In Abd al-Rahman Ra'fat al-Basha's Suwar min Hayat al-Sahaba
[[person/abd-al-rahman-rafat-al-basha|Al-Basha]] in [[work/suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba|Suwar min Hayat al-Sahaba]] draws two portraits of Zayd: one of the Jahiliyya from the adab books, and one of Islam from the sira books. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.124} In the first he relates, from al-Shaybani from an old man of Banu Amir, the story of a man whose people were struck by drought and who went out to win wealth for his family: he spent the night behind an aged man in a tent and drank his milk, then drove off the camels of a huge horseman; the horseman overtook him and put arrows through all three knots in the stallion's halter, and when the man learned that this was Zayd al-Khayl and the old man his father Muhalhil he surrendered. Zayd treated him generously — not giving him those camels, which belonged to a sister of his, but raiding Banu Numayr and giving him about a hundred she-camels, with men to escort him back to al-Hira. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.124} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.125} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.126} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.127} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.128}
In the second, he came with a large delegation of Tayy, among them Zirr ibn Sadus, Malik ibn Jubayr and Amir ibn Juwayn, and found the Prophet preaching; Zirr grew envious, said he saw a man who would rule the necks of the Arabs, and went to Syria and became a Christian, while Zayd declared the two testimonies, and the Prophet said, "Rather, you are Zayd al-Khayr." {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.128} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.129} He relates that the Prophet told him, "You have two qualities God and His Messenger love: deliberation and forbearance"; that Zayd asked for three hundred horsemen to raid the Byzantines; and that the Medina fever struck him on his way home, when he told his companions to keep him away from the lands of Qays, for he would fight no Muslim until he met God — and he died on the road to Najd. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.130} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.131}

## In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lesson
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy relates in episode 9 of his [[work/tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem|Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Karim]] that Zayd al-Khayr, asked by the Prophet what he felt, said he loved good and the people of good, and yearned for good when it passed him by (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:24:30} The Sheikh sees in this a sign that God wills good for the servant in the hereafter (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:25:00}
