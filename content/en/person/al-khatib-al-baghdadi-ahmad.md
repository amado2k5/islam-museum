## Overview
The hafiz Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn 'Ali ibn Thabit, known as al-Khatib al-Baghdadi — Baghdad's traditionist and its historian; born in 392, son of Abu al-Hasan, the preacher of Darzijan below Baghdad {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 86}, he died on Monday 7 Dhu al-Hijja 463 and was buried at Bab Harb beside Bishr al-Hafi {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 107}. He authored the Tarikh Baghdad, which the Baghdadis heard from him at the Nizamiyya madrasa {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 107}.

## From his life
He began hearing hadith at eleven, travelled to Basra at twenty and to Nishapur at twenty-three, then to Isfahan — al-Barqani having written a letter of recommendation for him to Abu Nu'aym {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 87-91}. He journeyed to al-Sham as a pilgrim, hearing at Damascus, Tyre and Mecca, where he met al-Quda'i and read Sahih al-Bukhari in five days upon Karima al-Marwaziyya; at Tripoli he held a preaching disputation with the qadi al-Husayn ibn Bishr of Dar al-'Ilm {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 92-93}.

Ibn al-Jawzi relates that the vizier Ibn al-Muslima showed him a document some Jews had produced, claiming it dropped the poll tax from the people of Khaybar and carrying the Companions' witness; al-Khatib declared it forged, since it bore Mu'awiya's witness though he embraced Islam only at the Conquest while Khaybar fell in year seven, and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh's though he died at Banu Qurayza — which was much admired {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 pp. 265-267}.

In the Basasiri fitna he hid and left Baghdad for al-Sham in 451 — Damascus, Tyre, Tripoli and Aleppo — residing at Tyre and visiting Jerusalem, then returned by way of al-Rahba and entered Baghdad in Dhu al-Hijja 462 {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi pp. 92-93} {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 pp. 265-267}. Long a Hanbali, he was harassed by its partisans and moved to the Shafi'i school {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 pp. 265-267}; about sixty works are counted for him — his books burned after his death and most of them survived {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 99}.

## In the Hyderabad editors' biography in al-Kifaya
The editors of the Da'irat al-Ma'arif al-Uthmaniyya edition of [[work/al-kifaya-fi-ilm-al-riwaya|al-Kifaya]] appended a biography of al-Khatib compiled from the biographical works. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 452} They quote the *History of Baghdad*'s entry on his father Ali ibn Thabit, a preacher, who said his origin was Arab and that he had a clan living in the region of the Euphrates. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 452}

**His birth.** The editors set out the disagreement: Thursday, six days before the end of Jumada al-Akhira 392 according to Ibn al-Subki and Ibn Khallikan; 391 in the handwriting of Ibn Khayrun as cited in *al-Muntazam*; 392 according to al-Dhahabi, Ibn al-Sam'ani and Ibn al-Salah; al-Nawawi gives only 391; and al-Sakhawi notes that 392 is what al-Khatib himself is reported to have said. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 452}

**The imams' praise.** They quote Ibn al-Sam'ani: the imam of his age without dispute and the hafiz of his time without contest, who wrote close to a hundred works that became the mainstay of the hadith people; Ibn Makula: the Baghdadis had no one like him after [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]]; and Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi: he was likened to al-Daraqutni and his peers. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 453} {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 454} They record that he read Sahih al-Bukhari with Karima al-Marwaziyya in five days, and walked the streets reading a fascicle in his hand. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 453}

**His school and creed.** The editors quote Ibn al-Jawzi that al-Khatib had first followed Ahmad's school, that the Hanbalis turned on him when they saw him inclining towards innovators, and that he then moved to al-Shafi'i's school and wrote against them in his works; the editors hold that a fair reader finds no partisanship in Ibn al-Jawzi's examples, and that by "innovators" he perhaps meant the Ash'aris. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 454} They quote Ibn al-Subki, citing al-Kattani, that in theology he followed Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari; al-Dhahabi, that his position on the divine attributes was to pass them on as they came; and Ibn al-Subki's reply that this too is a view of al-Ash'ari. The editors conclude that al-Dhahabi quoted in *al-Tadhkira* texts of al-Khatib that state plainly that his creed was that of the early generations. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 454} {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 455}

**His wealth and endowment.** They record that he was wealthy, spent on the hadith people and students, and declined people's gifts; when he fell ill he endowed all his books and distributed his wealth to good causes, after asking leave of the caliph [[person/al-qaim-billah|al-Qa'im bi-Amr Allah]], since his only heir was the public treasury. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 455} {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 456} They quote Abu Zakariyya al-Tabrizi, who said al-Khatib visited him in his room in the minaret of the Damascus mosque and left him dinars to buy pens. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 456}

**Death and burial.** They say that the sources agree on the date of his death, and quote *al-Muntazam*: he used to say he had drunk Zamzam water intending to enter Baghdad, teach his History there and be buried beside [[person/bishr-al-hafi|Bishr ibn al-Harith]]; he died in the forenoon of Monday 7 Dhu al-Hijja 463 in a room in Darb al-Silsila next to the Nizamiyya college; Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi carried his bier, and he was buried beside Bishr. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 456} Ahmad ibn Ali al-Turaythithi had dug a grave there for himself and objected; Abu Sa'd the Sufi asked him: if Bishr were alive and you and al-Khatib went in to him, which of you would sit beside him? He said: al-Khatib — and was content. {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 456}
