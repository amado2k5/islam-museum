## Overview
Abd al-Jabbar ibn Ahmad ibn Abd al-Jabbar al-Hamadhani al-Asadabadi, known as the Qadi al-Qudat (chief judge), was a theologian and legal theorist of the [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazili]] school. The Mu'tazila were a rationalist school of kalam that differs from the Sunni creed, which is this museum's default frame; both sources below present him as the leading figure of that school in his day. Al-Zirikli gives his kunya as Abu al-Husayn and al-Safadi gives it as Abu al-Hasan. He held the judgeship of Rayy and died there; al-Zirikli dates his death to 415 AH, 1025 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 273}, while al-Safadi says 414 AH, adding that some say 415 {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 21}.

## His life
Al-Zirikli writes that the Mu'tazila gave him the title Qadi al-Qudat and gave it to no one else, and that he took up the judgeship of Rayy and died in that city {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 273}. Al-Safadi adds that he followed the Shafi'i school in fiqh while being a Mu'tazili in theology, that he had much money and land, and that he lived to more than ninety, a year beyond ninety in al-Safadi's words {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 21}. If that age is accepted with a death in 415 AH, his birth falls near 324 AH, about 935 CE; this figure is derived from al-Safadi's remark and is not a date printed in his text.

### How he became judge of Rayy
Al-Safadi relates that [[person/sahib-ibn-abbad|al-Sahib ibn Abbad]] asked Abu Abd Allah al-Basri, the master of the Mu'tazili school, to send a man who would call people to the doctrine by his knowledge and his conduct. Al-Basri sent Abu Ishaq al-Nusaybi, who had a fine style and memory but did not suit the Sahib because of his harsh temper; the Sahib sent him five hundred dinars, clothes and a mount and dismissed him. He then wrote again, asking for a man who would win people more by his intellect than by his learning, and al-Basri sent Abd al-Jabbar, in whom the Sahib saw a mountain of knowledge and a refined character, and he found favour. He became chief judge of Rayy and its districts only after refusing, resisting and being pressed by the Sahib {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 21}.

### Stories from his circle
Al-Safadi reports that he once said in a lesson that a certain question would cost him one hundred and thirty thousand dirhams. Asked why by his students, he explained that a young man from Qazwin had attended him not out of love of learning but for his standing; a claim concerning the mint at Qazwin was then made against the young man and fixed at that sum, and the young man came to him to complain. At the Sahib's session the Sahib asked him about [[passage/quran-5-116|Qur'an 5:116]], whether any Christians say that Mary is divine; he answered that this is an argument by implication, that what Christians say of Jesus would oblige them to say the same of Mary. The Sahib also asked about [[passage/quran-76-3|Qur'an 76:3]] and why a fa'il form stands beside a fa'ul form; he answered that God's favours to his servants are many, so any thanks in return is small and any ingratitude great. The Sahib's face brightened, Abd al-Jabbar spoke for the man from Qazwin, and the Sahib struck off one hundred thousand dirhams and said that the surcharge should go too {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 21}.

Al-Safadi also says that before his connection with the Sahib he lived on what his fiqh gave him, with a wife and a child, and that one night he bought oil to treat a skin complaint, then, once it was dark, weighed whether to rub it on or to burn it in the lamp so as to read; he decided to burn it. Soon after, the Sahib sent for him and made him judge, and he acquired wealth. Al-Safadi says he was described as careless of other people's rights {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 21}.

### After the Sahib's death
Al-Safadi writes that he headed his letters to the Sahib with the words his servant, his creation, his plant, Abd al-Jabbar, and changed the form as his standing grew; the Sahib joked to his companions that if the judge stayed longer with them he would head his letters to them simply al-Jabbar. When the Sahib died, Abd al-Jabbar said he would not ask God's mercy for him because he had not shown repentance, and people criticised and loathed him for it. After the Sahib's death [[person/fakhr-al-dawla|Fakhr al-Dawla]] arrested him, fined him three million dirhams and removed him from the judgeship of Rayy, appointing in his place Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Abd al-Aziz [[person/al-jurjani-ali-ibn-abd-al-aziz|al-Jurjani]], author of the Wasata; it is said that he sold a thousand Egyptian shawls to meet the fine {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 22}.

### Al-Safadi's judgement
Al-Safadi closes with a harsh verdict that belongs to him alone and is not the museum's voice. He calls Abd al-Jabbar the shaykh of the Mu'tazila and head of their community and says that, in al-Safadi's account of the Mu'tazili position, he held that a Muslim stays in the Fire for ever over a quarter dinar. He adds that Abd al-Jabbar gathered his money from the judgeship, from unjust rulings and from bribes, and took office from people who were, by his own school's standard, wrongdoers and even unbelievers {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 22}. This is the language of an opponent in doctrine and is not how the Mu'tazila describe their own position.

## His works
Al-Zirikli lists many books, among them Tanzih al-Qur'an an al-Mata'in (printed), al-Amali, al-Majmu' fi al-Muhit bi-l-Taklif (its first part printed), Sharh al-Usul al-Khamsa (printed), [[work/kitab-al-mughni-abd-al-jabbar|al-Mughni fi Abwab al-Tawhid wa-l-Adl]] (eleven parts printed), [[work/tathbit-dalail-nubuwwat-sayyidina-muhammad|Tathbit Dala'il al-Nubuwwa]] (printed) and Mutashabih al-Qur'an (printed). He also names a printed study by Dr Abd al-Karim Uthman, Qadi al-Qudat Abd al-Jabbar ibn Ahmad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 273}. Al-Safadi says he wrote well-known works on i'tizal and on Qur'anic exegesis {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 21}.
