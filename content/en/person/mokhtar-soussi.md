## Overview
Muhammad al-Mukhtar ibn Ali ibn Ahmad al-Ilghi al-Susi was a historian, jurist and man of letters who wrote poetry and was known as the Minister of the Crown (wazir al-taj). He was born in the town of Ilgh in the mountains of Sous in the south of Morocco, into a learned Berber family. His father was the greatest shaykh of the Darqawi order, and he grew up in a Sufi setting. Al-Zirikli gives his years as 1318 to 1383 AH, 1900 to 1963 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.

## His education
He learned Arabic and excelled in it, and studied the religious sciences and literature in Sous, in Marrakesh and then in Fes. Al-Zirikli says he became Salafi in creed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.

## Stands and ministry
When the French issued the Berber Decree under the Protectorate he opposed them and openly joined the national movement in his region; they arrested him and held him in a detention camp with his colleagues among the leading Moroccan nationalists, then released him and forced him to live in his town for five years. When the scholars were asked to pledge allegiance to Ibn Arafa after the exile of Muhammad V, he refused and stayed loyal to Muhammad V. After Morocco's independence he was appointed Minister of Endowments in the first government. Muhammad V set up a permanent ministry of his own, the Ministry of the Council of the Crown, which ranks ahead of the other official ministers except the head of the council of ministers and does not fall with governments, being tied to the king personally. It had three ministers, one of them al-Mukhtar al-Susi, who remained to the end of his life {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.

## His works
His chief work is al-Ma'sul, twenty printed volumes, on the history of the region of Sous, its tribes, families, writers and notables. Among his other printed works are Khilal Jazula, three parts; al-Tiryaq al-Mudawi; al-Ilghiyyat, three parts; and Ilgh Qadiman wa-Hadithan, published after his death. Al-Zirikli lists these manuscripts kept in his private library: Taqat Rayhan, an abridgement of Rawdat al-Afnan by al-Ikrari; al-Fath al-Quddusi, a miscellany of about fifteen parts; Munyat al-Mutatalli'in ila man fi al-Zawiya al-Ilghiyya min al-Munqati'in, two small parts; al-Tanbih, on the merits of a jurist called Sidi Ahmad; al-Ru'asa' al-Sussiyyun; a lecture on the rebels of Sous, about twenty of them; Mudun Sus al-Mawjuda wa-l-Mundathira, a treatise; Mutra'at al-Ku'us, on some works of the writers of Sous; Madaris Sus wa-l-Ulama' alladhina Darrasu fiha, in narrative form; Jawf al-Fara', a literary collection in three volumes; Ala Qimmat al-Arba'in, memoirs of his life to that age; Akhlaq wa-Adat Sussiyya, unfinished; Qata'if al-Lata'if, a collection of tales; Min Marrakesh ila Ilgh, a journey written down in 1354 AH with reports on Haha and Agadir; Asanid wa-Ijazat Sussiyya; and Min Afwah al-Rijal, in manuscript, ten parts {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.

## His death
In his last years he suffered from diabetes and was hurt in a car accident, and he died at Rabat {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 29}.
