# Muhammad al-Mahdi al-Fasi

Muhammad al-Mahdi al-Fasi (1624–1698), in full Abu Isa Abu Abdallah Muhammad al-Mahdi ibn Ahmad ibn Ali ibn Yusuf al-Fihri al-Fasi, was a well-known Sufi, biographer and historian from Fes, of the prominent al-Fasi family, whose Andalusian ancestors had come from Malaga in the fifteenth century and settled in Fes, rising in scholarship, Sufi circles and trade {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi-en} {cite:igi-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi}.

He was born at Ksar al-Kebir on 17 May 1624 — 29 Rajab 1033 AH — and died on 20 February 1698; he was buried after the afternoon prayer in the mausoleum of his great-grandfather Abu al-Mahasin Yusuf al-Fasi, founder of the Fasi Sufi network {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi-ar} {cite:igi-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi}. He began his studies with his father Ahmad ibn Yusuf al-Fasi and his paternal uncle Abd al-Qadir ibn Ali al-Fasi, and Qasim al-Khasasi became his shaykh in the spiritual path {cite:igi-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi}. He mastered Arabic, jurisprudence, Qur'an commentary, hadith and history; Muhammad ibn al-Tayyib al-Qadiri called him one of the senior scholars and a renovator of teaching and authorship in his time, and Muhammad ibn Ja'far al-Kattani described him as the hafiz, the proof, the scholar of deep Sufi knowledge {cite:igi-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi}.

His particular interest was Sufism, above all the Shadhili branches of the Maghreb and the paths of Ahmad Zarruq and Muhammad al-Jazuli {cite:igi-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi}. He wrote three commentaries on al-Jazuli's Dala'il al-Khayrat, the Matali' al-Masarrat the best known, the Mumti' al-Asma' on al-Jazuli and his successor al-Tabba and their followers, and its supplement al-Ilma' {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi-en}. For his great-grandfather he wrote al-Jawahir al-Safiyya and Rawdat al-Mahasin al-Zahiyya, and on the customs of the people of Fes, al-Arf al-Asi fi al-Urf al-Fasi {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi-ar}. His commentary on the Dala'il remained his most famous work, helped by Ottoman interest in verifying its manuscripts in the eastern Islamic world {cite:igi-muhammad-al-mahdi-al-fasi}.
