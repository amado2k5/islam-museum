Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Warraq was an Andalusi historian, geographer and genealogist, born in 904 at Guadalajara — Wadi al-Hijara, 'the valley of stones' — who moved to Kairouan and grew up there, then returned to Cordoba under the caliph al-Hakam II al-Mustansir, at whose court he stood in high favour.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-ar}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-en} For al-Mustansir he wrote a large book on the geography of North Africa, 'Ifriqiya and its kingdoms', and many works on the history of its kings and their wars, with accounts of Tahert, Oran, Ténès, Sijilmasa and other places.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-ar}

None of his books survives whole. Abu Ubayd al-Bakri drew on him extensively in the Book of Roads and Kingdoms, preserving long extracts of his production a century later, and Ibn Idhari of Marrakesh kept fragments of his short treatises on African towns.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-ar}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-en} From those extracts it appears that al-Warraq was the first to mix geography and history in his writing: every geographical subject comes accompanied by its historical context and a detailed description.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-en} Ibn Hazm mentioned that his roots lay in the Zenata, the Berber tribal confederation.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-en}

Al-Warraq died at Cordoba in 973 according to the Arabic biography and Wikidata; the English biography gives 973 or 974, and the record preserves both.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-ar}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq-en}{cite:wikidata-muhammad-ibn-yusuf-al-warraq}
