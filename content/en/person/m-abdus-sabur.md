## Summary

Muhammad Abdus Sabur is a Bangladeshi-born peacebuilder based in Bangkok who has spent four decades building Muslim civil-society networks across Asia: co-founder and secretary-general of the Asian Muslim Action Network ⁦(AMAN)⁩, initiator and secretary-general of the Asian Resource Foundation ⁦(ARF)⁩, and co-director of Bangkok's School of Peace Studies and Conflict Transformation. {cite:muslim500-m-abdus-sabur} {cite:qantara-abdus-sabur}

## From Bangladesh to Bangkok

Sabur comes from a village in present-day Bangladesh and witnessed the violence of partition-era upheaval and its aftermath, during which he helped Hindu families caught in the strife — an experience that brought him into contact with the Bangkok-based Asian Cultural Forum on Development ⁦(ACFOD)⁩. Elected to its council in 1979 as its youngest member, he moved to Bangkok and has been based there since. {cite:qantara-abdus-sabur}

## AMAN

At ACFOD he came to feel that Muslim charitable energy in Asia flowed almost wholly into mosques and madrasas while little addressed the deeper structures of poverty and injustice — a contrast he drew from the Buddhist, Christian and Hindu social organisations around him. In 1990 he brought together progressive Muslim scholar-activists from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Thailand at Chiang Mai, and the Asian Muslim Action Network was born: AMAN — 'peace' in Arabic and the languages it seeded — with the Mumbai scholar Asghar Ali Engineer as convenor and Sabur as general-secretary. Its stated aim was to carry a socially engaged reading of Islam to Muslim youth, and to work with other faith communities for human dignity and social justice. {cite:qantara-abdus-sabur} {cite:muslim500-m-abdus-sabur}

AMAN's practical programmes include an annual three-week peacebuilding course in Bangkok, scholarships for Southeast Asian Muslim students to study the lives of the region's Muslim communities, and the quarterly magazine AMANA, published in five languages on women's rights, justice and interfaith learning. {cite:qantara-abdus-sabur}

## ARF and the School of Peace Studies

In 1996 he was among the initiators of the Asian Resource Foundation, an interfaith platform for cooperation on peace and development whose priorities run from children's education and emergency relief to women's empowerment and youth leadership. He also co-directs the School of Peace Studies and Conflict Transformation in Bangkok. {cite:muslim500-m-abdus-sabur}

His edited volumes include Understanding Human Rights: Perspectives from South Asia, Interfaith Conference on the Culture of Peace, Globalization and Asian Perspectives for an Alternative Development and Towards Peace in Multi-Ethnic Asia. {cite:muslim500-m-abdus-sabur}

## Assessment

Sabur's own account of his vocation is unassuming: 'people need to meet, so we are not working in silos.' His record is that of a connector — between Muslim activists and the wider Asian NGO world, between charity and structural critique, between the mosque and the human-rights conversation. {cite:qantara-abdus-sabur} {cite:muslim500-m-abdus-sabur}
