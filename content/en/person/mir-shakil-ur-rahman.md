## Summary
Mir Shakil-ur-Rahman is a Pakistani media owner born in 1952, editor-in-chief and chief executive of the Jang Media Group — Pakistan's largest newspaper and broadcast house; The Muslim 500 lists him among influential media figures. {cite:muslim500-mir-shakil-ur-rahman} {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## The group
He inherited and expanded the family's publishing business — the Jang daily, Pakistan's largest-circulation Urdu newspaper — into a media conglomerate adding The News International in English, Geo News television ⁦(2002)⁩, Geo Entertainment and sports channels. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## Geo era
Under his ownership Geo became Pakistan's dominant private news network — and a repeated target of regulatory and political pressure, including broadcast bans and cable blocks. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## Detention
He was arrested in March 2020 in a decades-old property case and held for months before release on bail; press-freedom organisations described the case as political pressure on an independent media house, an assessment the museum reports attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## Assessment
He is credited with building Pakistan's largest independent media institution; debates over the group's power and politics are reported attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman} {cite:muslim500-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}


## Jang Group
As chief executive he led the group's growth into the country's dominant media conglomerate — Jang newspaper, The News, Geo's channels and Awaz — shaping Urdu and English media for decades. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## 2010 crisis and 2020 detention
His 2020 NAB detention — held for months on charges later seen as politically contested — and the group's earlier confrontations with governments made him a central figure in Pakistan's press-freedom debates. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## Geo's impact
Geo's launch in 2002 under the group's stewardship transformed Pakistani television news, ending state monopoly and creating the model of combative 24-hour broadcast that later channels adopted. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman} {cite:muslim500-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## Origins
Born in Karachi in 1954 into the family behind the Jang newspaper — founded by his father Mir Khalil-ur-Rahman in 1939 — he rose through the group to become its chief executive and the country's most powerful media owner. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## Building the group
His expansion of the family's newspaper business into Geo's channels and the Awaz platform — launched under his chairmanship — created the model of the vertically integrated Pakistani media conglomerate. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## The 2020 detention
His 2020 NAB detention — held for months on charges later contested as politically motivated — made him a central figure in Pakistan's press-freedom debates, with international press bodies calling for his release. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman} {cite:muslim500-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}

## The Jang conglomerate
Mir Shakil-ur-Rahman's leadership of the family business behind Jang — the newspaper founded by his father in 1939 — built Pakistan's dominant media conglomerate: Jang newspaper, The News, Geo's channels and Awaz shaping Urdu and English media for decades. Geo's 2002 launch transformed Pakistani television news, ending state monopoly and creating the combative 24-hour broadcast model. His 2020 NAB detention — held for months on charges later contested as politically motivated — made him a central figure in press-freedom debates. {cite:wikipedia-mir-shakil-ur-rahman}
