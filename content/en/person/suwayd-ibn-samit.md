Suwayd ibn al-Samit al-Ansari al-Awsi was one of the poets and notable men of Yathrib, known for bravery and nobility. His people called him “al-Kamil” (the Perfect). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He came to Mecca for the pilgrimage or the lesser pilgrimage, and the Prophet invited him to Islam. Suwayd said, “Perhaps what you have is like what I have?” The Prophet asked, “What do you have?” He said, “The Majalla of Luqman,” meaning his wisdom. The Prophet said, “This is good speech, but what I have is better than this: a Qur'an that God has sent down as guidance and light.” He then recited to him and invited him to Islam, and Suwayd found it good. Not long after, Suwayd was killed in Yathrib in the Battle of Buath between the Aws and the Khazraj. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Islam at the pilgrimage season
He came to Mecca for the pilgrimage or the lesser pilgrimage. The Prophet offered him Islam, and he said, “Perhaps what you have is like what I have.” The Prophet offered him the wisdom of Luqman. The Prophet said that he had something better than that, and he recited the Qur'an to him. Suwayd became Muslim and said, “This is indeed a good saying.” ([[event/offering-islam-to-the-tribes|the offer of Islam to the tribes]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.131} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.132} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/424-425}.
