## Overview
Al-Haytham ibn Adi al-Ta'i, one of the akhbaris whom al-Mas'udi relies on for the numbers of the dead and the battles. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.659}

## In al-Mas'udi
He went with al-Sharqi ibn al-Qutami and Abu Mikhnaf in holding that the dead at Siffin from both sides were seventy thousand: forty-five thousand from the people of Syria and twenty-five thousand from the people of Iraq, among them twenty-five men of Badr. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.659} He relates that Muhammad ibn Talha was called Abu al-Qasim, contrary to al-Waqidi. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.659} Al-Bahlul ibn al-Abbas related from him, from Yazid al-Raqashi, the evening tales of al-Saffah, and the report of the Tanukhi and the slave-girl who satirised the tribes. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.257-261}

He died in 206 AH, and he was disparaged about his lineage, so verses were said on that subject. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.426} He related from his father that Uthman sent him as a collector among the Banu Udhra, and he found Urwa ibn Hizam a young man in a tent, chanting, who died in his presence while his mother was an old woman. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.539-540}

## In Ibn al-Nadim
Ibn al-Nadim relates from Ishaq al-Mawsili his words about a tale concerning al-Haytham ibn Adi and Hisham al-Kalbi. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.301}
