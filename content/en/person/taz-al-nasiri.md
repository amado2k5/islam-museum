## Overview
Sayf al-Din Taz al-Nasiri was a mamluk of [[person/al-nasir-muhammad-ibn-qalawun|al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun]] who rose to be one of the managers of Egypt in the 750s, was governor of Aleppo, and ended his life blinded and in exile. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/15} Ibn Hajar calls him a famous amir, brave and bold, who loved and honoured scholars and was ready to return to the truth. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/214}

## Rise
Ibn Hajar says Taz was only a boy in al-Nasir Muhammad's day, grew up with al-Nasir Ahmad at Karak, and under al-Muzaffar Hajji was one of the six who managed the realm; his standing rose further under [[person/al-nasir-hasan|al-Nasir Hasan]]. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/214} Ibn Taghribirdi lists him among the nine counsellors of 748. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/188} A daughter of al-Nasir Muhammad, his wife, died around this time. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/247}

## The pilgrimage of 751
Taz and the amir Buzlar went out with the pilgrim caravan that year. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/218} At Mina on the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja a clash broke out between the Egyptian escort and the armed retinue of the Rasulid sultan of Yemen, al-Mujahid, who had come to Mecca with a large following and was suspected of designs on the city. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/226-227} Ibn Taghribirdi says the fighting lasted until after the afternoon, and the Yemeni camp broke up. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/227} Ibn Hajar summarises that Taz arrested Baybugha Urus on the Hijaz road, together with al-Mujahid, the ruler of Mecca and the ruler of Medina, and brought them all to Cairo. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/214} In Cairo he stood in the citadel and pleaded for al-Mujahid. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/229}

## House and power
In Rabi I 753 Taz began building a palace in Cairo, finished it in 754, and gave a feast to the sultan and the amirs; Ibn Taghribirdi says no sultan had visited an amir's house since al-Nasir Muhammad. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/265} {cite:nujum-zahira 10/285} The Encyclopaedia of Islam lists him with [[person/shaykhu-al-umari|Shaykhu]] and [[person/sarghitmish|Sarghitmish]] as the triumvirate that won out after the fall of Hasan in 752, and says he accused Sarghitmish in Rabi I 753 of trying to restore Hasan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1058} Ibn Taghribirdi describes how Taz's brothers and followers rose against Sarghitmish while Taz was away hunting, and were defeated when Shaykhu armed about seven hundred of his mamluks. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/286} The other Encyclopaedia article says Taz and the amir Minkali had pressed Hasan to abdicate and arranged the succession of al-Salih. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 259}

## Aleppo, blinding and release
When Hasan was restored on 2 Shawwal 755, he at once ordered Taz's arrest; Shaykhu interceded and Taz left Cairo on 6 Shawwal for the governorship of Aleppo, arriving there on 5 Dhu al-Qada. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/302} Early in 758, on Sarghitmish's urging, Hasan had him seized, chained and taken to Alexandria. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/307} Ibn Hajar tells a fuller story: Taz was said to be preparing to rebel, an Aleppo amir rose against him, and he was summoned to Cairo; he agreed only on his own terms, but a day beyond Damascus the brother of the governor, Ali al-Mardini, seized him; Hasan ordered his eyes blinded, and he was held at Karak, then Alexandria. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/214}

After Hasan's death, Yalbugha freed him. Taz came before the new sultan with a veil over his eyes, was given a robe, and asked to live in Jerusalem; his brothers Jantamur and Qultay, whom Hasan had already freed, came with him. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/4} Ibn Taghribirdi says he died in Jerusalem in 763, and calls him one of the brave men. {cite:nujum-zahira 11/15} Ibn Hajar says instead that he was moved to Damascus at the end of 762, was given a tarkhan emirate, and died on 20 Dhu al-Hijja 763. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/214}
