Mir Azam Jan Khan (about 1870 – 10 September 1933) was Khan of Kalat from December 1931 until his death. He was born to Khudadad Khan — his mother was a Gichki — and served in his father's reign as commandant of Kalat's artillery regiment. When the British deposed his father in 1893 and offered him the vacant throne, he refused it in words the sources have kept: "A father in prison and in exile, and his son on the vacant throne?" He shared his father's exile instead, was held for a time at Loralai, and after his release served as an assistant to the political agent at Zhob. In 1922 his brother Mahmud Khan II sent him to govern Makran as its nazim, and when Mahmud fell severely ill in 1920 he had already been brought in, with British approval, to administer the state, which he did for some seventeen months. {cite:wikipedia-azam-jan-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-azam-jan-khan-ur} {cite:wikidata-azam-jan-khan}

Mahmud Khan died on 3 November 1931 without leaving issue by a wife of position, and Azam Jan succeeded in December 1931 with the unanimous approval of the sardars of Balochistan. He replaced the prime minister Shams Shah with Gul Muhammad Khan, abolished forced labour throughout his dominions, removed restrictions on the movement of agricultural and other goods, and promised more schools and hospitals and relief for the people of Kachhi. His formal installation came on 26 April 1932 at Quetta, where the Viceroy, Lord Willingdon, arrived by air — the first time a Viceroy had installed a Khan of Kalat — and a durbar was held at the racecourse with two hundred Kalat nobles attending. He was appointed Knight Grand Commander of the Order of the Indian Empire on 3 June 1932 and held a personal salute of twenty-one guns. {cite:wikipedia-azam-jan-khan-en}

He died on 10 September 1933 and was succeeded by his son Ahmad Yar Khan, the last Khan of Kalat. {cite:wikipedia-azam-jan-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-azam-jan-khan}
