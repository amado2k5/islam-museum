## Summary

Lotf Ali Khan Zand (c. 1769 – 1794) was the last ruler of Iran from the Zand dynasty, ruling from 1789 to 1794 after a decade of infighting among the successors of the dynasty's founder, Karim Khan {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}. Contemporary and later sources dwell on his striking appearance — tall, athletic, renowned for horsemanship and swordsmanship — and John Malcolm wrote that his beauty and manly grace were renowned throughout Persia {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}.

## Accession

The son of Jafar Khan, Lotf Ali claimed the throne in 1789 on his father's death: Jafar Khan had been poisoned by a slave bribed by a rival kinsman, Sayed Morad Khan, and on hearing of the murder Lotf Ali marched on Shiraz, where Sayed Morad was forced to surrender and was executed {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}.

## The loss of Shiraz

His principal rival was Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}. In 1791, marching to re-establish his control over Isfahan, Lotf Ali left Shiraz behind him; its kalantar, Haji Ibrahim, whose loyalty he already suspected, arrested the remaining Zand officers, declared the city his own and offered it to Agha Mohammad Khan, and most of Lotf Ali's men, fearing for their families inside the barred gates, deserted him {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}. With a handful of followers he fought on from Bushehr and Bandar Rig, defeated two Qajar forces, and in a night raid near Persepolis scattered Agha Mohammad Khan's much larger army — but the Qajar ruler had remained hidden in his pavilion, regrouped at dawn, and Lotf Ali had to retreat {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}.

## Kerman, Bam and death

Driven east, Lotf Ali took Kerman in 1794 and held it four months against Agha Mohammad Khan; gold coins were struck there in his name {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}. When disaffected troops opened the citadel gates he fled by night to Bam, whose ruler betrayed him; tradition has him fight fourteen men single-handedly for two hours before he was overcome {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}. Delivered to Agha Mohammad Khan, he was blinded, imprisoned and tortured at Tehran and choked to death in late 1794; his tomb is in the Imamzadeh Zeyd in Tehran's old bazaar {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}. Agha Mohammad Khan's revenge on Kerman — its men killed or blinded, its women and children enslaved, the city destroyed over ninety days — closed the Zand era {cite:wikipedia-lotf-ali-khan-zand-en}.
