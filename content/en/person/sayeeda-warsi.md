## Summary
Sayeeda Warsi, Baroness Warsi, is a British politician and lawyer, born Sayeeda Hussain Warsi in Dewsbury, West Yorkshire, on 28 March 1971, the daughter of Pakistani immigrants. A solicitor before entering politics, she was raised to the peerage in 2007, co-chaired the Conservative Party from 2010 to 2012, and became the first Muslim to serve in the British Cabinet. She resigned from the government in August 2014 over its policy on Gaza, and left the Conservative Party in 2024. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en}

## Life
Warsi grew up in West Yorkshire in a family of Pakistani Muslim immigrants from Bewal; her father built a bed-manufacturing business after starting work as a mill worker and bus driver. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} She qualified as a solicitor and worked for the Crown Prosecution Service before standing for Parliament. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} She married her cousin Naeem in 1990, a marriage that ended in divorce in 2007, and in 2009 married Iftikhar Azam; the couple live in Wakefield with five children. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} She speaks Punjabi, Urdu and Gujarati besides English, and set up the Baroness Warsi Foundation for social mobility, gender equality and religious understanding. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} She is identified generically as a Muslim in the sources — the first Muslim Cabinet minister, and "a Muslim herself" in a Telegraph piece the article quotes — but no source names a school or order for her, so her faith is recorded as unknown. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} {cite:wikidata-sayeeda-warsi}

## Work
After standing unsuccessfully for Dewsbury in 2005 — the first Muslim woman selected as a Conservative candidate — Warsi was made a life peer in 2007 and served as Shadow Minister for Community Cohesion. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} In May 2010 she became Minister without Portfolio in the Cabinet and co-chairwoman of the Conservative Party, the first Muslim woman to sit in the Cabinet. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} In the 2012 reshuffle she became Senior Minister of State at the Foreign and Commonwealth Office and Minister for Faith and Communities, a role created for her, in which she worked on religious tolerance and chaired the government's Islamic Finance Task Force. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} On 5 August 2014 she resigned, calling the government's Gaza policy "morally indefensible" and not in Britain's national interest. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en} Afterwards she became a leading voice on Islamophobia within the Conservative Party, pressing for an independent inquiry; in September 2024 she resigned the party whip and left the party. {cite:wikipedia-sayeeda-warsi-en}
