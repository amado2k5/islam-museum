# Ali-Akbar Dehkhoda

Ali-Akbar Dehkhoda (1879–1956) was an Iranian lexicographer, linguist and satirical writer, author of the most extensive dictionary of Persian ever published. {cite:wikipedia-ali-akbar-dehkhoda-en}

## Formation and the constitutional years

He was born in Tehran in 1879 to a family from Qazvin — the English infobox's "Qazvin" is the family's origin, not his birthplace — and lost his father at nine. He graduated from the School of Political Science, served at the Iranian embassy in the Balkans, and on returning joined the Constitutional Revolution: with Mirza Jahangir Khan he published Sur-e Esrafil, writing its famous satirical column Charand-o-Parand under the pen name Dakho. Exiled after the 1908 coup, he published the paper from Europe, returned in 1911 to the Majlis, and later taught at Tehran University. {cite:wikipedia-ali-akbar-dehkhoda-en}{cite:iranica-ali-akbar-dehkhoda}

## The dictionary

His life's work, the Loghat-nameh or Dehkhoda Dictionary, grew over forty-five years into fifteen volumes; after his death Mohammad Moin completed the unfinished volumes at his request. He also published Amsal o Hekam (Proverbs and Sayings), a French-Persian dictionary, and a translation of Montesquieu's Spirit of the Laws. He died at Tehran on 26 February 1956 by Iranica and the Persian article — the English article gives 9 March — and was buried at the Ibn Babawayh cemetery in Ray. {cite:wikipedia-ali-akbar-dehkhoda-en}{cite:iranica-ali-akbar-dehkhoda}
