## Overview
Al-Mu'tamid billah, an Abbasid caliph who was given the allegiance in Rajab 256 AH after the death of al-Muhtadi, and died in 279 AH, so his caliphate lasted twenty-three years. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.9 p.474} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 571}

## His accession
Al-Suyuti relates that he was Ahmad ibn al-Mutawakkil, and that his mother was Fityan. He was held in the Jawsaq when al-Muhtadi was killed, and they brought him out and gave him the allegiance. He then appointed his brother al-Muwaffaq over the East, and made his son Ja'far his heir, and titled him "al-Mufawwid ila Allah". {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 564}

Al-Mas'udi gives his kunya as Abu al-Abbas and says his mother was a Kufan concubine. He was given the allegiance on Tuesday, fourteen nights before the end of Rajab 256 AH, at the age of twenty-five. He died in Rajab 279 AH at the age of forty-eight. He appointed as vizier Ubayd Allah ibn Yahya ibn Khaqan, then al-Hasan ibn Makhlad, then Sulayman ibn Wahb, then Sa'id. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 571}

## His portrayal by historians
Al-Suyuti says that he was immersed in amusement and pleasures and occupied himself away from his subjects, so people disliked him and loved his brother Talha. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 564}

Al-Mas'udi says that he was devoted to music and entertainments. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 588}

## Note on the data
The data page ends with a sentence about Yusuf ibn Tashfin's capture of him and his march to Marrakesh, attributed to Ibn Khaldun. This does not fit an Abbasid caliph who died in 279 AH, and it may be from the account of [[person/al-mutamid-ibn-abbad|al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad]]. It needs an additional source.

## In al-Ya'qubi
[[person/al-yaqubi|Al-Ya'qubi]] says al-Mu'tamid ala Allah, Ahmad ibn Ja'far al-Mutawakkil, received the oath on the day al-Muhtadi was killed, Tuesday with fourteen nights of Rajab 256 AH remaining, and, as he does for each caliph's accession, records the positions of the planets that day. He made Ubayd Allah ibn Yahya ibn Khaqan his vizier and entrusted his affairs to him, and wrote to the provinces for the oath: in Khurasan Muhammad ibn Tahir ibn Abd Allah ibn Tahir gave it, on the Euphrates districts Malik ibn Tawq al-Taghlibi, in Diyar Mudar, Diyar Rabi'a and the military district of Qinnasrin Abu al-Saj Dewdad al-Ushrusani, and in Egypt the Turk Ahmad ibn Tulun. {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 619} {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 620}
Isa ibn al-Shaykh ibn al-Salil al-Rab'i refused the oath in Palestine, al-Ya'qubi relates, so al-Mu'tamid sent the Turk Amajur with seven hundred men. Isa besieged him in Damascus; Amajur broke out, killed Isa's son Mansur and captured his deputy Abu al-Sahba', whom he beheaded and crucified, and Isa withdrew to al-Ramla. Al-Mu'tamid then sent the eunuch al-Husayn, known as Irq al-Mawt, with a safe-conduct for Isa's life, property and children and the governorship of Armenia; Isa accepted, left the country in Jumada II 257 AH and handed what he held to Amajur. {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 620} {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 621}
