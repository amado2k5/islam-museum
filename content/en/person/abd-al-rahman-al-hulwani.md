## Overview
Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Muhammad al-Halwani (al-Hulwani), Abu Muhammad ibn Abi al-Fath, was a man of [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]] whom al-Zirikli describes as an exegete and Hanbali jurist versed in letters {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 327}. Al-Zirikli gives his birth in 490 AH, 1097 CE, and his death in 546 AH, 1151 CE; al-Safadi likewise gives 490 AH for his birth and 546 AH for his death {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 327} {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 149}.

## Life
Al-Zirikli says he traded in vinegar and accepted nothing from anyone, and that the name al-Halwani comes from the sale of sweets {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 327}. Al-Safadi says he studied law with his father, explained the Qur'an in forty-one parts and transmitted that work {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 149}. He was a jurist who gave fatwas and from whom the people of his quarter, al-Ma'muniyya in Baghdad, benefited; he narrated from his father, from Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Ayyub al-Bazzaz and from al-Mubarak ibn Abd al-Jabbar ibn Ahmad al-Sayrafi {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 18 p. 149}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists "al-Tabsira" on law, "al-Hidaya" on the principles of jurisprudence, and a "Tafsir al-Qur'an" in 41 parts {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 327}.
