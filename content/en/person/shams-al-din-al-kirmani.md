## Overview
Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Ali ibn Sa'id, Shams al-Din al-Kirmani, was a scholar of hadith whose family came from Kirman. Al-Zirikli gives his dates as 717 to 786 AH, that is 1317 to 1384 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 153}.

## Life
He became well known in Baghdad. Ibn Hajji says he devoted himself to spreading knowledge in Baghdad for thirty years. He stayed for a time in Mecca and finished there his al-Kawakib al-Darari fi Sharh Sahih al-Bukhari, printed in twenty-five small parts; Ibn Qadi Shuhba says it contains slips and much repetition, especially in fixing the names of narrators {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 153}. He died on his way back from the pilgrimage to Baghdad and was buried there {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 153}.

## His works
His books include Dama'ir al-Qur'an, in manuscript; al-Nuqud wa-l-Rudud fi l-Usul, in manuscript, together with its abridgement; a commentary on Ibn al-Hajib's Mukhtasar, which he called al-Sab'a al-Sayyara, the seven wandering stars, because he gathered seven commentaries in it; and Unmudhaj al-Kashshaf, a gloss on al-Kashshaf, in manuscript within a collection in the municipal library, no. 1956 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 153}.
