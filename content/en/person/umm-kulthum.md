## Summary
Umm Kulthum was an Egyptian singer and film actress, known as Kawkab al-Sharq ("Star of the East"), "the Voice of Egypt" and "Egypt's Fourth Pyramid". {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} She was the most celebrated Arab singer of the twentieth century, and her monthly radio concerts drew listeners across the Arab world for some forty years. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} In 2023 Rolling Stone ranked her 61st among the 200 greatest singers of all time. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}

## Names and the disputed birth year
The English article gives her full name as Fatima Ibrahim es-Sayyid el-Beltagi. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} The Arabic article gives the name as it appears on her birth certificate: Umm Kulthum Ibrahim es-Sayyid el-Beltagi. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-ar} Her birth year is genuinely disputed, and this museum records the dispute rather than resolving it: the Arabic article's lead and the traditional Egyptian date give 31 December 1898, the English article's lead gives 4 May 1904, and Wikidata carries both dates as separate claims. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-ar} {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} {cite:wikidata-umm-kulthum} The Arabic article's text adds a third date, saying that "official records" give 4 May 1908. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-ar} The date followed in her data record is 31 December 1898, and she died on 3 February 1975 — aged 76 on the 1898 date, or 70 on the 1904 date the English article uses. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} {cite:wikidata-umm-kulthum}

## Early life
She was born in Tamay al-Zahayra, a village in the Dakahlia Governorate of the Nile Delta. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} {cite:wikidata-umm-kulthum} Her father Ibrahim was the imam and muezzin of the village, and her mother was Fatmah el-Maleegi. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} As a child she memorised the Qur'an at the village kuttab. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} She began to sing religious hymns with her father's ensemble, dressed in boy's clothing, because a girl's public singing was not accepted in her village world. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}

## Cairo and the making of a star
She moved to Cairo in 1923, the move that turned a village hymn-singer into a professional artist. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} The poet Ahmed Rami wrote 137 songs for her. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} Her composers included Mohamed el-Qasabgi, Zakaria Ahmed and Riad el-Sunbati, the three men who shaped her classical style. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} In the mid-1960s she began working with Mohamed Abdel Wahab, the other giant of the age, on "Inta Omri" ("You Are My Life"). {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} She later worked with the younger composer Baligh Hamdi, whose songs renewed her repertoire for a new generation. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}

## The radio concerts
From 1934 she gave a concert on the first Thursday of every month, broadcast live on Egyptian radio. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} The monthly concerts continued for some forty years, and streets across the Arab world emptied as people gathered around radios to hear them. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}

## Films
She acted in six films, from Weddad in 1936 to Fatimah in 1947. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}

## The nation's voice
Her song "Wallahi Zaman Ya Silahi" ("By God, It Has Been a While, My Weapon") served as Egypt's national anthem from 1960 to 1979. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} After Egypt's defeat in the 1967 war she toured giving "Art for the War Effort" concerts, raising money for the Egyptian armed forces at home and abroad. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}

## Health, marriage and later years
She suffered from Graves' disease, an illness of the thyroid that affected her eyes, and she underwent surgery in the United States in 1953. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} Her trademark dark sunglasses were connected with the condition and the stage lights that hurt her eyes. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} In 1954 she married Dr Hasan al-Hifnawi, her physician; the Arabic article gives his name as Hasan es-Sayyid el-Hifnawi. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-ar} The marriage had no children. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}

## Death and legacy
She died in Cairo on 3 February 1975, of kidney failure. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} Her funeral procession drew some four million mourners, one of the largest gatherings of its kind. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} She was buried in a mausoleum in the City of the Dead in Cairo, near the tomb of Imam al-Shafi'i; Wikidata names the Al Basatin cemetery as her burial place. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en} {cite:wikidata-umm-kulthum} The Umm Kulthum Museum, holding her possessions and recordings, opened in 2001 at the Manesterly Palace in Cairo. {cite:wikipedia-umm-kulthum-en}
