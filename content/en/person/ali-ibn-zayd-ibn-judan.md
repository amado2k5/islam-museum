## Summary
Ali ibn Zayd ibn Abd Allah ibn Zuhayr ibn Abi Mulayka ibn Jud'an, Abu al-Hasan al-Qurashi al-Taymi of Basra, is described by [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] as one of the scholars of the Successors. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 127} He narrated from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], [[person/abu-uthman-al-nahdi|Abu Uthman al-Nahdi]] and [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]], and [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]], Abd al-Warith and many others narrated from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 127} He died in 131 AH. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 129}

## His standing in Basra
Al-Jurayri said: three jurists of [[place/basra|Basra]] went blind — Qatada, Ali ibn Zayd and Ash'ath al-Huddani. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 127} Mansur ibn Zadhan said: when [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]] died, we told Ali ibn Zayd to take his seat. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 127} When [[person/hammad-ibn-salama|Hammad ibn Salama]] was told that Wuhayb claimed Ali had no memory, he said: how could Wuhayb have sat with Ali? Only the leading men sat with him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 127}

## The critics divided
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: softening and weakening**: Shu'ba said: Ali ibn Zayd told us, and he used to raise reports to the Prophet; once he said: he told us before he became confused. [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Ibn Uyayna]] weakened him; [[person/hammad-ibn-zayd|Hammad ibn Zayd]] said he used to invert hadiths; [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|al-Qattan]] avoided his hadith; [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said weak; [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] and Abu Hatim said he is not a proof; [[person/ibn-khuzayma|Ibn Khuzayma]] said he did not rely on him for his poor memory; al-Fasawi said he became confused in old age. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 127} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 128}
- **Sunni: a middle verdict**: Ibn Ma'in is reported as saying "not that strong", once "nothing", and elsewhere "dearer to me than Ibn Aqil and Asim ibn Ubayd Allah"; Abu Hatim said his hadith may be written and he preferred him to Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad; al-Tirmidhi said truthful; al-Daraqutni said: to me there is still some softness in him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 128} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 129}
:::

## Shi'ism and objectionable reports
Yazid ibn Zuray' is reported as calling him a Rafidi, and al-Ijli said he inclined to Shi'ism and was not strong. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 127} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 128} Al-Dhahabi cites hadiths he finds objectionable, among them one on black banners coming from Khurasan, which he thinks munkar, noting how al-Thawri and others give its chain differently, and one on the death of the Prophet's daughter Ruqayya, which he calls munkar because it has Fatima present at the burial; a copyist's note adds that neither the Prophet nor Umar was in Medina then, both being at Badr. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 128} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 129} Sufyan said: I wrote a big book from him and abandoned it out of disregard for it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 129}
