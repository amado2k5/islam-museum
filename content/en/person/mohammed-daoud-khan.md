## Summary
Mohammad Daoud Khan (18 July 1909 – 28 April 1978) was an Afghan military officer and politician of the Musahiban royal family. He was prime minister of Afghanistan from 1953 to 1963 under his cousin King Zahir Shah, overthrew the monarchy in the coup of 1973, and served as the first president of the Republic of Afghanistan until he was assassinated in the Saur Revolution of 1978. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-daoud-khan-en}

## Early life and military career
He was born in Kabul, the eldest son of the diplomat Prince Mohammad Aziz Khan, an older half-brother of King Mohammad Nadir Shah. His father was assassinated in Berlin in 1933 while serving as ambassador to Germany, and Daoud and his brother Naim came under the tutelage of their uncle Mohammad Hashim Khan. Educated in France, he served as a provincial governor, then as commander of the Central Forces from 1939, leading Afghan forces in the tribal revolts of 1944–1947, and later as defence minister, interior minister and ambassador to France. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-daoud-khan-en}

## Premiership
Appointed prime minister in September 1953, he held the office for a decade. His tenure was noted for a foreign-policy turn toward the Soviet Union, completion of the Helmand Valley project, and tentative steps in the emancipation of women. He pressed the Pashtunistan claim against Pakistan, rejecting the Durand Line, and sent troops into Bajaur in 1960 and 1961. Having failed to persuade the king to implement a one-party system, he was forced to resign in 1963. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-daoud-khan-en}

## Presidency
On 17 July 1973, with the backing of army officers, he overthrew the monarchy in a nearly bloodless coup while the king was abroad, proclaimed the Republic of Afghanistan and became its first president, ruling autocratically through his National Revolutionary Party. His regime combined socialist-leaning reforms with nationalism; the 1977 constitution is described by the article as having a nationalist bend in addition to previous socialism and Islam. He purged communists from his government and sought to distance Afghanistan from Soviet dependence, which drew criticism from Moscow. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-daoud-khan-en}

## Death
On 28 April 1978 he was deposed and assassinated, with most of his family, in the Saur Revolution led by the military and the People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan. His body was discovered thirty years later and identified by a small golden Quran, a gift from King Khalid of Saudi Arabia, that he always carried; he received a state funeral in 2009, and his resting place is near Taj Beg hill in Kabul. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-daoud-khan-en}

## Personal life
He was married to Zamina Begum, a sister of King Zahir Shah — so that the king was at once his cousin and his brother-in-law — and had eight children. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-daoud-khan-en}
