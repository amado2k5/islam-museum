## Summary
Amr ibn Uthman al-Makki, one of the Sufi shaykhs of the third century. He heard from Yunus ibn Abd al-Ala and al-Rabiʿ ibn Sulayman, and Jaʿfar al-Khuldi related from him. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 205}.

When he was appointed judge of Jeddah, al-Junayd cut off relations with him and said: "I will not speak to one who used to show asceticism and then was seen to expand his pursuit of worldly wealth." {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 205}.

He died in Baghdad in the year 297 AH; it is also said 291 AH, but the first date is more sound. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 205}.

## In al-Risala al-Qushayriyya
In [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]] al-Qushayri gives a notice of Abu 'Abd Allah 'Amr ibn 'Uthman al-Makki: he met Abu 'Abd Allah al-Nibaji and kept company with [[person/abu-said-al-kharraz|Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz]], was "the shaykh of the Sufis and leader of the community in principles and the path", and died in Baghdad in 291 AH. {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 168} He quotes his teaching that whatever the heart imagines or occurs to thought — beauty, splendour, light, a figure or an image — God is far from it, citing the verses that deny God any likeness or equal; his saying, "Knowledge leads and fear drives, and the self is a balky mount between them: tend it with the governance of knowledge and drive it with the threat of fear"; and: "No expression falls upon ecstasy, for it is God's secret with the believers." {cite:risala-qushayriyya-minhaj-2017 p. 168}
