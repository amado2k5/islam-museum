## Overview
Muhammad ibn Isa ibn Sawra al-Sulami al-Bughi, a hadith scholar from Tirmidh on the river Jayhun. He wrote the "al-Jami' al-Sahih", known as the Sunan of al-Tirmidhi. He was born in 209 AH (824 CE) and died in 279 AH (892 CE). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.450}


## Life and travels
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, presents Abu Isa Muhammad ibn Sawra al-Tirmidhi as a great collector of hadith and the author of one of the Six Books, the [[concept/kutub-al-sitta|Kutub al-Sitta]]. It reports that he was born in 210 AH, 825 CE, near the town of [[place/tirmidh|Tirmidh]]; the museum's older notice above gives 209 AH, so the year of his birth is recorded with a difference between sources. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

According to the article, he travelled widely in search of traditions through [[place/khurasan|Khurasan]], [[place/iraq|Iraq]] and the [[place/hijaz|Hijaz]], and was among other things a pupil of [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]]. Late in life he went blind, allegedly from excessive weeping, and he died in Tirmidh in 279 AH, 892 CE. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## The Jami' al-Sahih
He is famous first of all for his [[work/jami-al-tirmidhi|al-Jami' al-Sahih]]. The article says this collection is generally considered to hold the fourth rank, after those of al-Bukhari, [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] and [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]]. In the Cairo edition of [[person/ahmad-muhammad-shakir|Ahmad Muhammad Shakir]] and colleagues, printed between 1937 and 1965, it contains 3,956 traditions. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

Like his predecessors, the article continues, al-Tirmidhi included many dozens of traditions that he supplied with single-strand [[concept/isnad|isnads]], for which he alone can be held responsible. These can be identified in the [[work/tuhfat-al-ashraf|Tuhfat al-Ashraf]] of [[person/jamal-al-din-al-mizzi|al-Mizzi]], who marks them with a single Arabic letter ta. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## Method and the Kitab al-Ilal
At the end of the Jami' an early and, in the article's words, extremely important theoretical work on hidden defects of traditions is appended, his [[work/kitab-al-ilal|Kitab al-Ilal]]. In it al-Tirmidhi explains how he classifies hadith, using three technical terms, [[concept/al-hadith-al-sahih|sahih]], [[concept/al-hadith-al-hasan|hasan]] and [[concept/al-hadith-al-gharib|gharib]], which he often combined in ways that seem contradictory. His methods drew some criticism and also bewilderment among fellow traditionists. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

For a detailed survey of these reactions, the article refers to the commentary of [[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali]], the [[work/sharh-ilal-al-tirmidhi|Sharh Ilal al-Tirmidhi]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## The Shama'il
Al-Tirmidhi also compiled a collection of traditions that deal only with the characteristics of the Prophet, the [[work/al-shamail-al-muhammadiyya|K. al-Shama'il]]. The article says it was probably modelled on the latter part of the biography of Muhammad by [[person/muhammad-ibn-sad|Ibn Sa'd]] in his [[work/al-tabaqat-al-kubra-ibn-sad|Tabaqat al-Kabir]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## His entry in Taqrib al-Tahdhib
Ibn Hajar writes: Muhammad ibn Isa ibn Sawra ibn Musa ibn al-Dahhak al-Sulami al-Tirmidhi, Abu 'Isa, author of "al-Jami'" — one of the imams, of the twelfth layer; he died in 279 AH {cite:taqrib-al-tahdhib p.558}. Al-Hububi, al-Haytham ibn Kulayb and many others narrated from him {cite:taqrib-al-tahdhib p.558}.

## For younger visitors
Abu 'Isa al-Tirmidhi wrote the book "al-Jami'", known as the Sunan of al-Tirmidhi, one of the most famous hadith collections among Muslims; he lived in the third century of the Hijra and died in 279 AH {cite:taqrib-al-tahdhib p.558}.

## Notes for researchers
- Taqrib al-Tahdhib, entry 6206 (Majmu'at al-kutub scan, p. 558).
- "Of the twelfth layer" is al-Taqrib's grading for the late third-century imams.
- The Taqrib vocalizes the father's name "Sawra"; the museum's summary and other works give "Sura".
