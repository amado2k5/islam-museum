# Pardis Sabeti

Pardis Christine Sabeti (born 25 December 1975) is an Iranian-American computational biologist, medical geneticist and evolutionary geneticist, a professor at Harvard University and the Broad Institute, known for statistical tests that detect natural selection in the genome and for genomic work on Ebola and Lassa fever. The English, Arabic and Urdu articles and Wikidata give her birth on 25 December 1975 in Tehran; the Persian article gives the same day in the Persian calendar, 4 Dey 1354. {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-en} {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-fa} {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-ar} {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-ur} {cite:wikidata-sabeti}

## Early life and education

Sabeti was born in Tehran to Nasrin and Parviz Sabeti; her family fled Iran in October 1978, shortly before the Iranian Revolution, when she was two, and settled in Florida. She attended Trinity Preparatory School, where she was a National Merit Scholar and valedictorian, then MIT, graduating in 1997 with a perfect grade average. As a Rhodes Scholar at Oxford she took a master's in human biology and a doctorate in evolutionary genetics (2002), and in 2006 she received her M.D. from Harvard Medical School summa cum laude. {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-en} {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-fa}

## Career and research

As a graduate student at Oxford and a postdoctoral fellow at the Broad Institute, Sabeti developed a family of statistical tests — extended haplotype homozygosity and its successors — that identify regions of the genome under positive natural selection. With Christian Happi and their teams she worked for a decade on Lassa fever in West Africa and in 2014 launched the African Center of Excellence for Genomics of Infectious Disease (ACEGID); during the West African Ebola outbreak her team's sequencing helped identify the first cases in Sierra Leone and Nigeria and traced the epidemic's spread. Her group later worked on Zika, the 2018 Lassa outbreak in Nigeria and COVID-19, and co-leads the Sentinel pandemic-preemption system. {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-en}

## Recognition

Sabeti received Smithsonian magazine's American Ingenuity Award in 2012 and the Vilcek Prize for Creative Promise in Biomedical Science in 2014. She was one of Time's Persons of the Year in 2014 (the Ebola Fighters), appeared in the Time 100 in 2015, received a Time 100 Impact Award in 2022, was inducted into the National Academy of Medicine in 2020, and was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 2026. She hosts the statistics series Against All Odds and Crash Course's Outbreak Science, and is the lead singer of the rock band Thousand Days. {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-en} {cite:wikipedia-sabeti-ar}
