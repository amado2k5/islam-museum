## Summary
Abu Ruh al-Faraj ibn Sa'id al-Sufi was reproached for the path of the imams and the pillars, and transmitted from them what the servants use to be healed. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 534, sequence 535, on pages 162 and 163. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/162} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/163}

## Istidraj, vanity and thanks
Uthman ibn Ammar relates that he heard Hammad ibn Zayd say: Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani, Yunus ibn Ubayd, Ibn Awn and Thabit al-Bunani gathered in one house. Thabit asked: how is a servant when he calls on God and God answers his prayer? Ibn Awn said: the trial is in himself. Thabit said: vanity overtakes him at what God has done for him. Yunus ibn Ubayd said: a servant is not vain at God's favour unless he is being led on. Ayyub asked: what is the sign of being led on? Yunus answered: when a servant holds a rank with God, keeps it, and gives thanks, God raises him to a more honourable rank; when he wastes thanks, God leads him on, so that his wasting of thanks is itself a leading on; vanity overcomes the recognition of being led on. When such a servant feels a movement of thanks in his heart, he examines where it came from; when he knows this truly, he submits, and when he submits God pardons his stumble. Hammad said: Ibn Umar was asked about istidraj and said it is a snare against the servants who waste. They all wept; Ayyub raised his hand and said: O knower of the unseen and the seen, we have no success unless You grant it and no strength unless You strengthen us. Ayyub was known to his companions as having a prayer that is answered. One phrase at the end is unclear in the scan and is not given here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/163}
