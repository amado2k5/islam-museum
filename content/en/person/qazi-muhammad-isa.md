Qazi Muhammad Isa (17 July 1914 – 19 June 1976) was a barrister and politician from Pishin who founded the Muslim League in Balochistan and carried the Pakistan movement into India's most distant province — the youngest member of the All-India Muslim League's Working Committee, a member of Pakistan's first Constituent Assembly, and later his country's ambassador to Brazil. The English and Arabic articles give his birth as 17 July 1914 at Pishin; Wikidata gives 13 April 1913 instead. He was a son of Qazi Jalaluddin, a qadi of Kandahar, and — in the English article's words, following Dawn's account of Pakistan's Hazaras — "from a Sunni Hazara of Sheikh Ali tribe"; the Arabic article likewise calls him a son of the Sunni Hazara tribe. He was schooled at Quetta, at the Sandeman and Government high schools. {cite:wikipedia-qazi-muhammad-isa-en} {cite:wikipedia-qazi-muhammad-isa-ar} {cite:wikidata-qazi-muhammad-isa}

In 1933 he went to England, took his law degree at the Middle Temple and was called to the Bar in January 1939; he had begun practice in Bombay in 1938, and it was there that he met Muhammad Ali Jinnah and resolved to build the League in Balochistan. He founded the Balochistan Muslim League and became president of its provincial organisation, sat as the youngest member of the League's Working Committee, and represented Balochistan at the Lahore Resolution session of March 1940. Between 1940 and 1947, by the standard account, he travelled some 300,000 miles campaigning for Pakistan across India, and he played a notable part in the North-West Frontier Province referendum that preceded Partition. {cite:wikipedia-qazi-muhammad-isa-en} {cite:wikipedia-qazi-muhammad-isa-ar}

In the new state he sat on the Committee on Minorities of the first Constituent Assembly, and diplomacy followed politics: ambassador to Brazil from 1951 to 1953, and a member of Pakistan's delegations to the United Nations in 1950, 1954 and 1974. He had served the Muslim League for thirty-seven years when he died at Quetta on 19 June 1976 — the year 1973, sometimes given for his death, is supported by none of the sources consulted. In 1990 Pakistan Post issued a 'Pioneers of Freedom' stamp in his honour. His sons carried the family's public life forward: Qazi Faez Isa became the twenty-ninth Chief Justice of Pakistan (2023–24), alongside his brothers Qazi Azmat Isa and Qazi Anwar Kamal Isa, and his nephew Ashraf Jehangir Qazi became a diplomat. {cite:wikipedia-qazi-muhammad-isa-en} {cite:wikipedia-qazi-muhammad-isa-ar} {cite:wikidata-qazi-muhammad-isa}
