## Summary
Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ishaq al-Kufi girded himself for the meeting with God and guarded against parting. He was miserly with the hours and grieved over their loss. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 514, sequence 515, on pages 150 and 151. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/150} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/151}

## Days are arrows
Abd Allah ibn Ali al-Umawi relates from Muhammad ibn Ishaq that a wise man said: days are arrows and people are targets, and time shoots at you every day with its arrows and uses you by its nights and days until it takes up all your parts. How long will you stay whole while the days fall on you and the nights hasten through your body? If the harm the days have done were uncovered, you would be lonely at every day that comes to you. The notice then describes the world: this world is the time in which your glance returns to you, for what has passed has escaped your grasp and what has not come you cannot know; hope is long and life is short, and all matters return to God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/150} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/151}

## A man of Abd al-Qays
A man of Abd al-Qays said: where are you going, or rather, where are you being driven? The driver of death follows your breaths swiftly, and the spirits gather for their need to leave the house of ending for the house of lasting. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/151}

## Verses of admonition
Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn al-Husayn al-Ajurri relates that Ibrahim ibn al-Junayd found verses written on the back of a book belonging to Muhammad ibn al-Husayn al-Barjalani. They speak of the admonitions of monks and of true reports about souls that disbelieve, and say that admonition makes the eye weep and leaves the soul lost among graves; they call on the one who has understanding to hasten, for death is the first visitor. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/151}

## The inheritance of hunger
Ibrahim relates from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn that he was told that Abd Allah ibn al-Darij, a worshipper, was asked by a man: O Abu Muhammad, these monks speak wisdom, yet they are people of unbelief and error; what is this? He said: the inheritance of hunger; you were given enjoyment by it, the inheritance of hunger; you were given enjoyment by it. The name in this report is unclear in the scan and is given as the context reads it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/151}
