# Kifayatullah Dehlvi

Kifayatullah Dehlvi (1875 – 31 December 1952), known as Mufti Kifayatullah, was an Indian Islamic scholar and Hanafi jurist who was regarded as the Grand Mufti of India {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}. He served as second rector of Madrasa Aminia in Delhi, was appointed interim president of Jamiat Ulama-e-Hind on its foundation in 1919, and became its second president after the death of Mahmud Hasan Deobandi, holding the presidency until 1940 {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}.

## Early life and education

He was born at Shahjahanpur in present-day Uttar Pradesh in 1875, into a family that had moved there from Bhopal; his father Inayatullah was poor, and the young Kifayatullah covered his own study expenses by weaving and selling caps {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}. The Urdu biography pairs his birth year itself: 1292 AH with 1875 {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-ur}. He began at a local maktab, continued at Madrasa Azeeziya and at Madrasa Shahi in Moradabad, and enrolled at Darul Uloom Deoband in 1895, completing his studies in 1898 at the age of twenty-two with the highest marks in his examinations {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}. The Arabic biography names his teachers as Abd al-Ali al-Miruthi, Khalil Ahmad Saharanpuri and Mahmud Hasan Deobandi {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-ar}, and the Urdu biography's teacher list likewise includes Mahmud Hasan {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-ur}.

## Teaching and fatwas

After graduating he taught at Madrasa Ainul Ilm in Shahjahanpur, where he also began answering fatwas and founded the monthly Al Burhan against Qadiani beliefs {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}. He then moved to Delhi as a teacher at Madrasa Aminia, implemented reforms in its curriculum, founded a debating assembly for the students, and in time became the madrasa's rector {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}. Over some fifty years he answered hundreds of thousands of fatwas, prized for their concision and their carefully written evidence; the courts of law preferred his rulings in religious cases, and his collected fatwas were published as Kifayat al-Mufti {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}{cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-ar}.

## Jamiat Ulama-e-Hind and public life

He was appointed the first, interim president of Jamiat Ulama-e-Hind at its foundation in November 1919, and after Mahmud Hasan Deobandi's death became its second president on 6 September 1921, serving until 7 June 1940 {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}. He was a founding member of Jamia Millia Islamia, New Delhi, sitting on its foundation committee under Mahmud Hasan {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}. He drafted a fatwa recommending a boycott of British goods that was signed by around five hundred Muslim scholars {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}.

## Faith, as the sources attest it

The English article's lead describes him in text as a Hanafi jurist {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}, the Urdu article's lead does the same {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-ur}, and the Arabic biography carries al-Hanafi in his lineage {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-ar}. The English article's infobox gives his denomination as Sunni, his jurisprudence as Hanafi and his creed as Maturidi {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}; Wikidata records his religion as Islam only {cite:wikidata-kifayatullah-dehlvi}. The museum's data record sets out exactly how these attestations combine, and what rests on the infobox alone.

## Works

His best-known publication is Ta'limul Islam, four volumes of questions and answers in simple language for children and adults; his fatwas form Kifayat al-Mufti, and his early Arabic poem Raudur Rayyaahin appeared in 1909 {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}.

In his final years he withdrew from politics; he died of a liver illness on 31 December 1952 — a date the Urdu biography pairs as 13 Rabi' al-thani 1372 AH {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-ur} — and was buried at Mehrauli in Delhi near Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki, his funeral attended by a hundred thousand people {cite:wikipedia-kifayatullah-dehlvi-en}.
