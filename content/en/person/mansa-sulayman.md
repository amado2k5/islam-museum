## Overview
Mansa Sulayman ibn Abi Bakr, king of [[polity/mali-empire|Mali]]. He is the brother of [[person/mansa-musa|Mansa Musa]], and succeeded him after his son Mansa Maghan. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## His rule
He ruled for twenty-four years, and the material Ibn Khaldun quotes describes his rule as firm, and under him the bounds of the kingdom widened. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

The year of his death is approximately 761 AH, reckoned from the sequence Ibn Khaldun gives, and needs an additional source.

## In Ibn Battuta's Rihla
[[person/ibn-battuta-muhammad|Ibn Battuta]] vocalizes his title "Mansa", says it means sultan and that Sulayman is his name, and calls him "a miserly king from whom no great gift is to be hoped", which is Ibn Battuta's judgement. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.551} He says he stayed some time in Mali without seeing him because he was ill; then the sultan gave a meal in memory of the Marinid sultan Abu al-Hasan, inviting the amirs, jurists, judge and preacher; Qur'an sections were brought and a full recitation completed, prayers were said for Abu al-Hasan and for Mansa Sulayman, and there Ibn Battuta greeted him. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.551}

He relates what happened during his stay when the sultan grew angry with his senior wife, his cousin "Qasa", meaning queen, who shared the kingship by the custom of the Sudan and whose name was mentioned with his from the pulpit: he imprisoned her and put his other wife "Banju" in her place; later Qasa was accused of corresponding with his fugitive cousin "Jatil" to depose him, and she sought refuge in the preacher's house. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.557} Ibn Battuta says the people of the Sudan disliked Mansa Sulayman for his miserliness; before him came Mansa Magha, and before him Mansa Musa, who was "generous and virtuous, loving the white men and treating them well", and gave Abu Ishaq al-Sahili four thousand mithqals in a single day. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.557}
