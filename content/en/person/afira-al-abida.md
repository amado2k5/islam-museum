## Summary
Ufayra the Devout was a woman of [[place/basra|Basra]] recorded by Ibn al-Jawzi in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]], who gave herself to worship and wept until she went blind. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.711}

## Her sayings
He relates that Rawh ibn Salama al-Warraq told her, "I hear you do not sleep at night", and she wept and said, "Sometimes I long to sleep and cannot; how can one sleep whose two guardian angels never sleep from him, night or day?" {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.711} When a visitor remarked how hard blindness is for one who once could see, she said: "The heart's blindness to God is worse than the eye's blindness to the world." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.711} Asked to pray for others, she said, "If sinners were struck dumb, your old woman would not speak — but the doer of good has bidden the wrongdoer to pray", and prayed for them. She used to say: "I disobeyed You with every limb of mine, each on its own; if You help me, I will obey You with every limb I disobeyed You with." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.711}

## For young visitors
A devout woman of Basra who wept from fear of God until she lost her sight, and said that blindness of the heart is worse than blindness of the eyes.
