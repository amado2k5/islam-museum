## Summary
Shaykh Abu Umar Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Qudama ibn Miqdam ibn Nasr al-Maqdisi al-Jamma'ili, later of Damascus and al-Salihiyya, the ascetic and devotee, brother of [[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|al-Muwaffaq Ibn Qudama]] and head of his family circle. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52} [[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]] gives him a long entry in [[work/dhayl-tabaqat-al-hanabila|al-Dhayl ala Tabaqat al-Hanabila]], drawn mostly from his nephew [[person/diya-al-din-al-maqdisi|the hafiz al-Diya']] and from [[person/sibt-ibn-al-jawzi|Sibt ibn al-Jawzi]]'s Mir'at al-Zaman, and notes that al-Diya' wrote at length about him. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 56} Shadharat al-Dhahab likewise remembers his asceticism, his worship and his service to widows and orphans. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 35-38}

## Birth and emigration
Al-Diya' says he was born in 528 AH at [[place/jammail|Jamma'il]], and saw this in his father's handwriting. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52} His father [[person/ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-qudama|Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Qudama]] took him, his brother al-Muwaffaq and their household to [[place/damascus|Damascus]] in 551 because the Franks had taken the Holy Land; they lodged for about two years in the mosque of Abu Salih outside the Eastern Gate, then moved to the mountain. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52} Abu Umar used to say: people said "the Salihiyya, the Salihiyya", naming us after the mosque of Abu Salih, not because we are righteous. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52} Ibn Rajab quotes the handwriting of Abu al-Faraj Ibn al-Hanbali that the mosque proved unhealthy and nearly forty of them died in one month, until his father advised them to move to the mountain, "and it was a blessed decision". {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52} Thus [[place/al-salihiyya|al-Salihiyya]] on the slope of Qasyun came into being.

## Learning and copying
Abu Umar memorised the Qur'an and recited it in the reading of Abu Amr; he heard hadith from his father, from Abu al-Makarim ibn Hilal, Abu Tamim Salman al-Rahbi and others, and in Egypt from the sharif Abu al-Mafakhir al-Ma'muni and from Ibn Barri the grammarian. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 52} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 53} [[person/abd-al-ghani-al-maqdisi|The hafiz Abd al-Ghani]] selected forty hadiths from his narrations for him, which he taught; al-Diya', [[person/abd-al-azim-al-mundhiri|al-Mundhiri]] and others heard from him, and his son Abu al-Faraj Abd al-Rahman memorised the Mukhtasar of al-Khiraqi from him. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 53} He studied the law of the school and read grammar with Ibn Barri in Egypt. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 53}

He wrote fast and much, sometimes two large quires a day: he copied Abu Nu'aym's al-Hilya, al-Baghawi's commentary, his brother's [[work/al-mughni|al-Mughni]] and Ibn Batta's al-Ibana, many copies of the Qur'an for his family and the Khiraqi for others, all without fee. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 53} Al-Diya' says God had combined in him knowledge of law, inheritance shares and grammar with asceticism, practice and attending to people's needs. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 53}

## Worship and renunciation
Al-Diya' gathers many pictures of his devotion: he never heard a supplication without memorising and using it, nor a hadith without acting on it; he never left the night prayer from his youth; and as an old man he led a hundred rak'as for the people on the middle night of Sha'ban. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 53} He recited a seventh of the Qur'an in the night prayer and another seventh by day, prayed seventy-two supererogatory rak'as in a day and night, never slept without ablution, and visited the graves every Friday after the afternoon prayer. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 54} His son Abd Allah said that late in life he fasted continuously, and when his family reproached him he said: I am making the most of my days; if I weaken I will be unable to fast, and if I die my deeds will cease. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 53}

He gave away his clothes until he was left in winter with a gown and no shirt; he wore coarse cloth and slept on a mat; his turban was a strip of lining from which he cut a piece for anyone who needed a rag or a shroud for a small child. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 54} He used to say: there is no knowledge except what goes with its owner into the grave; and: I am an ascetic — but in what is forbidden. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 55} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 56} Sibt ibn al-Jawzi reports that whenever he came down the mountain he gathered brushwood and carried it to the houses of widows and orphans, and at night brought them coins and flour without their knowing who he was. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 56}

## Awe, intercession and campaigning
He inspired such awe that people fell silent when he entered the mosque. He often wrote letters of intercession to officials; when one of them complained that he interceded for people in whose favour they did not wish to act, he replied: I have done what was asked of me; accept my note if you wish, or not — and the official said: we will never refuse it. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 55} He went on campaigns with [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Saladin]]; when Saladin besieged Jerusalem, [[person/al-malik-al-adil-ayyub|al-Adil]] came to visit him while he was praying, and he neither cut short his prayer nor left his litany. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 56} His brother al-Muwaffaq said: he is our shaykh; he raised us, was good to us and taught us; he was like a father to the family; it was he who emigrated with us, sent us to Baghdad and built the convent, then married us off, built houses for us, and built the school and the cistern. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 57} Al-Nasih Ibn al-Hanbali adds that he was honoured by [[person/nur-al-din-mahmud-ibn-zangi|Nur al-Din Mahmud ibn Zangi]], who visited him and built them a mosque and a fountain on the mountain, and that his school on the mountain was endowed for the Qur'an and law, where countless people memorised the Qur'an. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 58}

Sibt ibn al-Jawzi tells how Shaykh Abd Allah al-Yunini left the Friday prayer behind him because Abu Umar prayed in the sermon for al-Malik al-Adil, whom al-Yunini considered unjust; when Abu Umar later brought him food and mentioned a report about "the just king Khosrow", al-Yunini smiled, ate and said: this is nothing but a righteous man. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 57} Sibt ibn al-Jawzi describes him as following the way of the early generations, holding to the Book, the Sunna and the transmitted reports without disparaging the imams, and quotes verses of his affirming that the Qur'an is God's uncreated speech and that the divine attributes are passed over as they came, without likening. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 58} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 59}

## Wonders reported of him
Ibn Rajab relays from al-Diya', Sibt ibn al-Jawzi and others reports told of him as marvels (*karamat*): his prayer for rain at the Cave of Blood and the rain that is said to have followed, a little food that fed three hungry men, and accounts of the sick being cured. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 55} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 58} These are reports attributed in the book to their narrators, and the museum gives them as they were told.

## Death and funeral
Sibt ibn al-Jawzi relates that Abu Umar attended his session at the Qasyun mosque and was then carried to the convent, never to come again; he lay ill for some days without leaving any of his litanies. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 59} On the eve of Monday 18 Rabi' I 607 he gathered his family, faced the qibla, urged them to fear God, told them to recite Ya Sin, and his last words were a verse of Surat al-Baqara ([[passage/quran-2-132|al-Baqara 132]]). {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 59} No judge, scholar, emir or commoner stayed away from his funeral; the crowd was estimated at twenty thousand, and had soldiers not ringed the bier with swords nothing of his shroud would have reached the grave. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 60} He died at eighty, leaving neither dinar nor dirham, and was buried on the slope of Qasyun beside his father. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 60} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 61} The book then records dreams told about him and an elegy by Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Maqdisi. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 60} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 61}
