## Overview
Shaykh Abu 'Umar al-Maqdisi, the ascetic Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Qudama the Hanbali, brother of al-Muwaffaq; born at Jamma'il in 528, his father and brother emigrated with him to Damascus when the Franks seized the Holy Land, settling at the Abu Salih mosque then the Mountain — he joked that people said "Salihiyya" after that mosque, not because they were righteous. He memorised Qur'an, fiqh and hadith and copied many codices for his family: the Mughni for his brother, the Ibana for Ibn Qawta and al-Khiraqi for the people, all without pay. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.35-38}

## Worship and death
An ascetic imam who preached at the Mountain mosque until his death, he prayed seventy-two supererogatory rak'as a day and carried brushwood, dirhams and flour by night to the houses of widows and orphans, unknown; when he led the rain prayer the rain came at once and the wadis flowed. He died on Monday evening, 18 Rabi' al-Awwal 607, aged eighty, and a cloud shaded the people to his grave with a sound like bees; he was buried at the foot of Qasyun beside his father. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.35-38}
