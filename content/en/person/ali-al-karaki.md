## Overview
Al-Zirikli opens the entry by calling him an Imami usuli mujtahid, that is, a scholar of the Imami (Twelver) Shi'a: Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Abd al-Ali al-Karaki al-Amili, Abu al-Hasan, called al-Muhaqqiq al-Thani, and known as al-Ala'i {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 250}. He was born in Jabal Amil in Lebanon in 868 AH (1463 CE) and died in 940 AH (1534 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 250}.

## Travels and standing
He travelled to Egypt and studied under its scholars, went on to Iraq, and then settled in Persia {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 250}. The entry says that the Safavid shah Tahmasp honoured him and gave him the final word in the running of his realm, writing to all his lands to obey what the shaykh ordered and stating that sovereignty was in origin the shaykh's because he was the deputy of the Imam; the shaykh wrote to the provinces with the rule to follow on land tax and on what should be arranged in the affairs of the subjects {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 250}.

## His death and works
He died at Najaf of Kufa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 250}. Among his books are "Sharh al-qawa'id" in six volumes, and many commentaries, treatises and glosses {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 250}.
