## Summary

Hasan Çelebi ⁦(1937 – 24 February 2025)⁩ was the last great master of classical Ottoman mosque calligraphy, hailed in Turkey as 'the Master of Calligraphers' ⁦(Hattatların Reisi)⁩. His thuluth and naskh inscriptions crown some of the most visited mosques on earth — Istanbul's Blue Mosque and Grand Çamlıca Mosque among them — and he trained more than fifty calligraphers, carrying the icazetnamah chain of the art into the twenty-first century. {cite:aa-hasan-celebi} {cite:dailysabah-hasan-celebi} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 232}

## Early life

Born in 1937 in the village of İnciköy in the Oltu district of Erzurum, son of Tahsin Efendi and Sakine Hanım, he taught himself to read and learned the Qur'an from his uncle Yusuf Altaş, memorising it in his primary-school years. His interest in calligraphy began, by his own account, with imitating the inscriptions of his village mosque. In 1954 he moved to Istanbul for religious education at the Üçbaş and Çinili madrasas, and in 1956 was appointed muezzin of the Mihrimah Sultan Mosque in Üsküdar. {cite:dailysabah-hasan-celebi}

## Imam and calligrapher

After military service he served as imam at the Nasuhi Mehmed Efendi Mosque, spent the post-1960-coup years as muezzin in Yusufeli, Artvin, returned to Istanbul in 1963, and held a decade-long imamate at the Şeyh Mosque before the Selami Ali Efendi Mosque, retiring voluntarily in 1987. Alongside the imamate he pursued calligraphy from the age of twenty-four, taking thuluth and naskh from Hamid Aytaç — the last of the Ottoman-era masters — and ta'liq and riq'a from Kemal Batanay, earning his ijazas in each script. From 1976 he taught calligraphy; over fifty calligraphers received their ijazas from him, among them the American master Mohammed Zakariya. {cite:dailysabah-hasan-celebi}

## The works

His inscriptions and panels stand in the Blue Mosque and the Grand Çamlıca Mosque in Istanbul, the Sri Sendayan Mosque in Malaysia and mosques across the Muslim world; according to his Muslim 500 obituary he also restored calligraphic panels at Al-Masjid al-Nabawi in Madinah. Since 1992 he sat on the juries of IRCICA's international calligraphy competitions. His honours include the Necip Fazıl Honor Award, the Turkish Presidential Grand Award for Culture and Arts, and the Ministry of Culture's designation as a 'Living Human Treasure' in 2010 — the country's highest recognition for a bearer of intangible heritage. {cite:aa-hasan-celebi} {cite:dailysabah-hasan-celebi} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 232}

## Death

He died in Istanbul on 24 February 2025, aged 88. UNESCO Türkiye's notice remembered him as the foremost living representative of the hüsn-i hat tradition inscribed on UNESCO's heritage list in 2021. {cite:aa-hasan-celebi} {cite:wikipedia-hasan-celebi}
