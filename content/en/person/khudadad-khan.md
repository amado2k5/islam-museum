Mir Khudadad Khan (about 1841 – 20 May 1909) was the tenth Khan of Kalat. He was born to Mehrab Khan II and his wife Khadijah, and in 1857, aged sixteen, he succeeded his stepbrother Mir Nasir Khan II. His long rule was broken once, in 1863–64, when Sher Dil Khan usurped the throne, and it ended in 1893; Encyclopaedia Iranica sums it up as a period of serious conflicts with the sardars, in which the Khan never grasped the significance of the colonial power that continually frustrated his efforts to rule. {cite:wikipedia-khudadad-khan-en} {cite:iranica-baluchistan-i} {cite:wikidata-khudadad-khan}

His reign is the reign in which Balochistan was bound into the British system. In the 1870s, as Russia advanced in Central Asia, Captain Robert Sandeman was sent to Kalat and built the system of indirect rule through tribal chiefs under British supervision that took his name: the Khan's foreign relations were restricted, a permanent British garrison was placed in Kalat, the British administration became arbiter between the Khan and his sardars, and in return the Khan's annual subsidy was raised. The Mastung treaty of 1876, under which disputes among the Brahuis were referred to the Political Agent at Kalat, belongs to the same settlement. Khudadad had already tested the limits: in 1860 he tried to extend his control over parts of western Balochistan and was stopped by the British, and in 1863 he concluded the telegraph agreement for the line from Jask to Karachi directly with them. {cite:wikipedia-khudadad-khan-en} {cite:iranica-baluchistan-i} {cite:memoranda-indian-states-1921}

In November 1893 his position became untenable and he was forced to abdicate; the British offered the throne to his son Azam Jan, who refused it, and another son, Mahmud Khan II, succeeded and ruled until 1931. Khudadad lived on at Kalat for sixteen more years. The Wikipedia articles leave his death date unknown; the Government of India's official Memoranda on the Indian States 1921 records it exactly: "Mir Khudadad Khan died on 20th May 1909". {cite:memoranda-indian-states-1921} {cite:wikipedia-khudadad-khan-en}
