## Summary

Danishmand Khan (d. 1670), born Shafi'a Yazdi, was a Persian scholar and soldier from Yazd who worked as a merchant at Ahmednagar before entering Shah Jahan's military service, where in 1654/55 the emperor gave him the title Danishmand Khan — "the learned lord" — in recognition of his learning; he later held the office of Mir Bakhshi in the central administration under Aurangzeb. Knowledge of him is owed chiefly to the letters of the French traveller François Bernier, who stressed his scholarship and his great interest in Western science, a portrait the Italian traveller Niccolò Manucci confirmed.{cite:wikipedia-danishmand-khan-de}{cite:wikidata-danishmand-khan}

## From Yazd to the Mughal court

He came from Yazd and was educated there. His working life began in trade, as a merchant at Ahmednagar in the Deccan, before he changed course and entered military service under the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan. His learning, unusual in a soldier, brought him to the emperor's attention, and in 1654/55 Shah Jahan conferred on him the title by which history knows him, Danishmand Khan — the learned lord. Under Aurangzeb he rose to one of the great offices of the central administration, Mir Bakhshi.{cite:wikipedia-danishmand-khan-de}

## Bernier's learned lord

What posterity knows of Danishmand Khan comes largely through the French physician and traveller François Bernier, whose letters from India describe him with emphasis on his scholarship. Bernier drew attention in particular to the Mughal noble's keen interest in the sciences of the West — a curiosity that made him one of the most intellectually alive figures at the court Bernier observed. The Italian traveller Niccolò Manucci, who also spent years in Mughal India, independently confirmed the picture Bernier had drawn of him.{cite:wikipedia-danishmand-khan-de}

He died in 1670.{cite:wikipedia-danishmand-khan-de}{cite:wikidata-danishmand-khan} No text read names his place of death, and none dates his birth: the year 1625 sometimes attached to him is not attested in the sources for this record, and his faith is likewise recorded as unknown — a Persian of Twelver Yazd who served a Sunni court, with no text naming a school for the man himself.
