## Overview
Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr ibn Suwayd ibn Haritha al-Qurashi, also called al-Lakhmi, Abu Amr or Abu Umar, the Kufan memoriser, was known as al-Qibti {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2577}. He saw Ali and Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, lived a long life and became the chain-bearer of the people of Kufa {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2577}. According to al-Ijli he held the judgeship of Kufa {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}. The sign printed with his entry shows his hadith is in the six canonical books {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2577}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Jundab al-Bajali, Jabir ibn Samura, Jabr ibn Atik, Amr ibn Huraith, al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, Umm Atiyya, Rib'i ibn Hirash, Amr ibn Maymun al-Awdi, Musa ibn Talha, Abu Burda ibn Abi Musa and many Companions and senior Followers {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2577}. Shu'ba, al-Thawri, Mis'ar, Hushaym, Abu Awana, Isra'il, Za'ida, Hammad ibn Salama, Jarir ibn Abd al-Hamid, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and many others narrated from him, and among the early ones Shahr ibn Hawshab, which is in Sahih Muslim paired with another narrator {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2577}. Ali ibn al-Madini said he has about two hundred hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2577}.

## What the critics said
The critics differed. Al-Nasa'i and others said there is no harm in him, and al-Ijli called him sound in hadith and reliable, and said he held the judgeship of Kufa {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}. Abu Hatim said he was sound in hadith but not a memoriser and that his memory changed before his death, and Ishaq al-Kawsaj reports from Ibn Ma'in that he mixed things {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}. Ahmad ibn Hanbal, as reported by Ali ibn al-Hasan al-Hinjani, said his hadith was very unsettled for all his small output, that he did not think he had five hundred hadith, that he erred in many, and that Simak ibn Harb was sounder because the memorisers differed over Abd al-Malik's reports {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}. Against this, Muhammad ibn Sufyan al-Kufi reports from Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash that Abu Ishaq said to take knowledge from Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr, and al-Bukhari said he was among the most eloquent of people {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}.

## Stories told of him
Ibn Uyayna reports that when a man called him al-Qibti, he answered that al-Qibti was a swift horse of theirs {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}. He is also reported to have said that he narrated a hadith without leaving out a single letter {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}.

## His death
Abu Bakr ibn Abi al-Aswad reports from Abu Abd Allah al-Bajali that he died in 136 AH or about then, and another adds that it was in Dhu al-Hijja of that year {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}. The entry also gives his remark that in the year he spoke he had reached a hundred and three years {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2578}.
