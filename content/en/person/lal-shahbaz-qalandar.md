## Summary

Sayyid Uthman al-Marwandi, known as Lal Shahbaz Qalandar (1177 - 19 February 1274), was a Sufi saint and poet of Sehwan in Sindh {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en}. The Arabic article's prose states that he was a follower of the Suhrawardi order {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-ar}. The English and Urdu articles present him as a wandering Qalandar, and the competing readings are preserved in this record {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en} {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-ur}.

## Life and travels

He was born in 1177 at Marwand {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en}. He travelled widely through the Muslim world before settling at Sehwan, where he lived until his death {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en}. Tradition counts him among the Chahar Yar, "the four friends", with Bahauddin Zakariya, Baba Farid and Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en}. The name Qalandar marks a wandering spiritual man who has renounced the world {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en}.

## Sehwan and his shrine

He died at Sehwan on 19 February 1274, the day resting on the English article's cited Pakistan Today source {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en}. The Arabic article gives his death in 673 AH, and the Urdu article converts 673 AH to 1275, a year later than the English article; the disagreement is preserved in the record {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-ar} {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-ur}. His shrine at Sehwan draws large crowds of devotees, above all at his annual urs {cite:wikipedia-lal-shahbaz-qalandar-en}.
