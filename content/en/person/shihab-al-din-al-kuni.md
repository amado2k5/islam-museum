## Summary
Abu'l-Mahasin Yusuf ibn Isma'il ibn 'Ali ibn Ahmad ibn al-Husayn ibn Ibrahim, styled Shihab al-Din, of Kuna by origin and of Aleppo by birth, upbringing and death. He was an accomplished man of letters, master of prosody and rhyme, a poet who hit upon beautiful conceits within two or three verses; he has a large diwan in four volumes which Ibn al-Sha'ir says contains twenty thousand verses. He dressed in the old Aleppine manner with the slit turban, was constant at the circle of Shaykh Taj al-Din Ibn al-Khayr the grammarian, and sought out al-Malik al-Nasir Salah al-Din as a panegyrist — not one who earned his living by verse but one who said it out of passion. Between him and Ibn Khallikan there was companionship and familiarity from late 633 until his death. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
