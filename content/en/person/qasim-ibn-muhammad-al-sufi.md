## Summary
al-Qasim ibn Muhammad ibn Salama al-Sufi was a guardian of himself and spoke by the rules of monastic discipline. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 515, sequence 516, on pages 151 and 152. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/151} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/152}

## The aim of lovers and the fearful
al-Qasim relates that a monk in a church in Syria told him: the aim of lovers is to reach their wills, and the aim of the fearful is to reach safety from fear. Each is on good, though these are more worn in body and higher in rank of good. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/152}

## Abu Safwan the worshipper
al-Qasim relates from Abu Safwan the Syrian worshipper, who was at Mecca, that people passed a monk bent by striving, and called to him; he came to the edge of the path as if his spirit had been drawn out. They asked: on what do you work and wear yourself out? He said: on greed and hope. They asked: do lapses overtake you? He said: they have, at times of despair and dejection; fear is the trusted guard of the deed. They asked: when is a servant most constant in worship, and most active? He said: when love takes hold of the heart, it leaves no rest or pleasure except union. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/152}
