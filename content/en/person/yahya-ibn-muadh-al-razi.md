## Overview
Yahya ibn Mu'adh al-Razi, among the vigilant ascetics, a devout worshipper with disciples; he died in 206 AH and wrote the Book of the Seekers. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 260}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
[[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 463 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]]; the notice opens on page 51 with verses on sin and the seeking of pardon. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/51}

**Love and sin.** He said he believes in his Lord's love obediently, while sin comes to him unwillingly; so the hatred of sin goes away through the obedience of love, because God is the most merciful of the merciful. He prayed: if You do not show me the mercy of honour with You, show me the mercy of drawing me near to You. He said that if God laid His justice on people no good deed would remain to them, and if He laid His grace on them no sin would remain. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/52}

**Three kinds of people.** He said that bodies are in the prison of intentions, and that people are of three kinds: a man occupied with this world apart from God, who is blamed; a man occupied with the next world, who is praised; and a man occupied with God apart from everything else, who is brought near. He said the whole matter of religion turns on two things: the heart's contentment with the provision of one side, and striving for the provision of the other. He said that whoever has the scent of leadership on him does not prosper. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/53}

**The world as a bride.** He said: the world is a bride and its suitor is her hairdresser; with asceticism her hair is plucked, her face blackened and her clothes torn. Whoever divorces the world has the next world for his wife. The world is a woman divorced by the wise, whose waiting period never ends; so remember the next world and do not remember the world, take from the world what carries you to the next, and take nothing from it that keeps you from the next. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/54}

**Forgiveness and the sign of meeting God.** He said that perfect forgiveness rests on three things: good acceptance, following knowledge, and giving of one's bounty. He explained good acceptance: that you hear the clear proof and consider the intention, and do not shake your head as if you knew nothing of what you heard; for this brings its doer into pride and spoils his work. He said that the sign of one who meets God has three marks: he prefers His pleasure, keeps his piety close in hardship and ease, and opposes his desire. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/54}

**Seek your self.** He said that he sometimes saw a man say: for twenty years I have been seeking my Lord. He would say to him: woe to you, your Lord does not need you to lose yourself; seek your self until you find it, and when you find it you have found your Lord. He wondered at the one who came to him with a ball of thread whose end was lost and who sought it in an hour, while he himself had sought a lost end of thread for twenty years and could not find it. He said that before God the world does not weigh a gnat's wing, and that Paradise is the beloved of the believer, whom he sells for the hated thing, which is the world. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/55}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Yahya's sayings on pages 56–61 continue the notice. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/56-61}

**Three levels.** He said that people are of three kinds: a man whose hereafter occupies him from his livelihood, which is the station of the righteous; and a man whose livelihood occupies him for his hereafter, which is the station of the successful. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/56}

**Do not make asceticism a trade.** He said: do not make asceticism your craft, to gain the world by it; make it your worship, to attain the next world by it. If the people of this world thank you and praise you, leave their affair to the sellers of trinkets. He said that creatures cling to means, while the knower clings to the Lord of means, and his talk of God's greatness, power, generosity and mercy is his trade for his whole life, and goes with him into the grave. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/57}

**The springtime of goodness.** He said that the believers have in the next world a springtime of goodness; they travelled on the mounts of gratitude until they reached the mounts of His treasure. He said that the gnostic may be busy with his Lord and so turn away from boasting of appearances and from the gatherings of gifts, and from contention with opposites in the gatherings of trials. The most trustworthy hope is the servant's hope in his Lord, and the truest of thoughts is good thought of God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/58}

**In proportion to stillness.** He said that in proportion to the heart's stillness toward God's promise, life becomes pleasant for it; in proportion to its constancy in obedience, He adorns obedience in its breast; in proportion to its tongue's habit of remembrance, He keeps His kindnesses flowing; and in proportion to its shrinking from creatures, He makes it at home in His obedience. He said that if the reward for deeds were only what is hastened in this world, it would still be much. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/59}

**The mouths of men.** He said that men's mouths are their shops, their lips are their locks, and their teeth are their claws; when a man opens the door of his shop you can tell the perfumer from the farrier. He said that God has called you to the house of peace, so look at where you answer Him from: from your worldly life or from your grave? If you answer from your world you enter it; if you answer from your grave you are saved from it. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/60}

**Money and the world.** He said that money is near in its poison: if you do not know how to charm it, do not take it into your hand, for when it bites you it kills you. He said that the world is God's deadly poison for His servants; take from it what is taken from poison in medicine, so that you may be safe. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/60}

**Marks of the gnostic and the seeker.** Two qualities belong to the gnostic: he does not spread his state to anyone, and he does not probe anyone's state. A sign of the seeker is contentment with the decree, trust in the promise, working with sincerity, thanks for trial, repentance from every sin, and testing of one's intentions. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/61}

**Spirit and breath.** He said that spirits are spiritual and luminous, while breaths wander and are airy; spirits yearn for their origin in the highest places, and breaths yearn for their prison in the lowest. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/61}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Yahya's sayings on pages 62–67 continue the notice. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/62-67}

**The withered basil.** Yahya saw some sprigs of basil that boys had put in his room, now wilting; he came with water and gave it drink. Asked why, he said he saw the wet basil dried by being left without water, and his heart was wrung by it: it had stirred a tear in him, and it seemed to him to ask him for drink by its drooping. His father and brother urged him toward seeking this world; his brother recited verses asking for mercy for withered branches, and Yahya answered that whoever is tender toward a withered branch has more right to show mercy to his brother. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/62}

**Verses on sin and hope.** Yahya recited verses praising the Lord of majesty, and verses confessing sins that he does not deny, while hoping in God's forgiveness, and asking that his request be heard on the Day of Reckoning, when recompense is given for the terrors. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/63}

**The stations of the seekers.** He said that the stations toward which the substitutes of the next world strive are seven: repentance, then asceticism, then contentment, then fear, then longing, then love, then knowledge. By repentance they are cleansed of sins, by asceticism they leave the world, by contentment they put on the cap of servanthood, and by fear they cross the bridges of the Fire, by longing they deserve Paradise, by love they reach its delight, and by knowledge they reach God and remain in that for ever. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/64}

**The nature of obedience.** He said that the affair of the human being is strange: he has been charged with obedience differently from every other creature on earth and in heaven; and each matter of obedience must be drawn out by hard effort from between two opposing things. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/65}

**Wealth.** He said that wealth in the hands of the unjust is a cause of their ruin, and in the hands of the trustworthy is a cause of their nobility and deliverance. So wealth is not to be blamed; blame falls on what a person does with it. Sins are earned by the limbs, and neither an estate nor a shop has limbs; the limbs are yours, and sins are earned by them. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/66}

**The believer and the hypocrite.** He said that the believer is eager for Paradise and poor toward his Lord; the hypocrite is eager for this world and poor toward creatures. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/67}

**Three rules for the road of resolve.** He said that a wise man stated that whoever lacks these three qualities has not reached the road of resolve: first, as God gives your provision today through no other, do not rely on anyone else; as God shares nothing of what He gives with anyone, do not share in the work you do for Him, that is, showing off; and as God does not burden you today with tomorrow's work, do not seek tomorrow's provision by injustice, so that when He does not give it to you, you complain. He also said that when you look at things from Him they have another taste; that the truthful one is not the one who claims love and does not keep its limits; and that a man falls from a station he claims. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/67}
