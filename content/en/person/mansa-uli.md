## Overview
Mansa Uli, also given as Wali, was a king of the kingdom of Mali in West Africa. The Encyclopaedia of Islam calls him the son of the founder of Mali. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 278} Elsewhere in the same volume the founder is identified as Mari Jata, or Sunjata, the hero credited with establishing Mali. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 443} The museum therefore links him to [[person/mari-jata|Mari Jata]], while noting that the article on Mali does not name the father in that sentence.

## Reign
The article says he extended his father's conquests and secured Mali's northern frontiers in the Sahara. This, it says, allowed him to perform the pilgrimage to Mecca. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 278} On the way he passed through [[place/cairo|Cairo]] during the reign of Baybars, 658-76/1260-77. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 278}

## Place in a tradition
The article observes that there was a long tradition of royal pilgrims in West Africa, especially among the more powerful rulers. It notes the later pilgrimage of Sakura in 698-708/1299-1309 and says that the most famous royal pilgrim was [[person/mansa-musa|Mansa Musa]], who visited Cairo in 1324. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 278} Visits of Mali's kings to North Africa, Egypt and Mecca made Mali known as a Muslim kingdom rich in gold. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 278} The kingdom itself is described on the page for the [[polity/mali-empire|Mali empire]].

## Limits of the source
The Encyclopaedia gives no birth or death dates, no length of reign and no account of the pilgrimage itself beyond its passing through Cairo under Baybars. Other chronicles may add to this, and the museum has not yet recorded them.
