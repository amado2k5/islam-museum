## Summary
Moez Masoud is an Egyptian preacher, television presenter and producer born in 1978, part of the generation of new preachers who addressed youth in a contemporary idiom; The Economist described him in 2011 as one of the world's most influential presenters of the Islamic tradition. {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud} {cite:muslim500-moez-masoud}

## Life and upbringing
Raised in American schools in Kuwait and Cairo and speaking English from childhood, he graduated from the American University in Cairo in 2000 with a degree in economics, then took an MPhil in Psychology and Religion at Cambridge and pursued a PhD in philosophy of science, studying Islamic sciences under many sheikhs over the years. {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud}

## Turn and beginnings
His own accounts describe hard experiences — the deaths of close friends and surgery for a spleen tumour — that turned him to prayer and Qur'an memorisation; he first addressed audiences in New York state in 2000, then presented the English-language Parables in the Qur'an, followed by the Arabic Al-Tareeq Al-Sahh in 2007, which tackled drugs, relations and the roots of extremism, and an institute of the same name was founded. {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud} {cite:muslim500-moez-masoud}

## Media and production
He has worked as presenter, director and producer: television series including Thawra 'ala al-Nafs ⁦(2011)⁩, documentaries and feature production including the acclaimed film Clash, and songwriting and composing. He took part in the 2011 revolution and the post-Tahrir National Dialogue, and counts more than ten million followers across his platforms. {cite:muslim500-moez-masoud} {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud}

## Institute and film work
He founded the Al-Tareeq Al-Sahh Institute, which runs workshops on modernism and steers young Muslims toward constructive civic engagement, and expanded from presenting into production: songs he wrote and composed, documentaries and features including the critically acclaimed Clash — joining da'wah and cinema in one career. {cite:muslim500-moez-masoud}

## Fellowship and research
He is a Fellow of the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute for Islamic Thought in Amman and a research affiliate at Cambridge, with studies centred on existential questions, challenges to global co-existence and identity in the modern world — a line where academic training and da'wah work meet. {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud}

## Legacy and debate
His discourse is read as the model of the "new" da'wah: spirituality, interfaith dialogue and de-radicalisation in a language close to urban youth, while conservatives criticise his simplification of the tradition and critics note the overlap of his religious and political messaging; the museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud}
## The early programmes
His television presence began with the English-language Parables in the Qur'an, which drew a global audience in North America, Europe, Malaysia and Australia; he then launched the Arabic Al-Tareeq Al-Sahh in 2007 — the first Arab religious programme filmed entirely on location in the streets — addressing drugs, alcohol, gender relations and the roots of extremism with unusual candour. {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud}

## The new preachers
Religious-media observers place him in the generation of "new preachers" who appeared in the Arab world after 2000: leaders from outside the official religious institutions, speaking to youth in a contemporary idiom and direct social questions, joining spirituality to civic action — the generation that redrew the face of religious discourse on Arab satellite channels. {cite:wikipedia-moez-masoud}
