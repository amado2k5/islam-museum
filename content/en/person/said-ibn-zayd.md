## Summary
Said ibn Zayd al-Adawi al-Qurashi was one of the earliest Muslims during the [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|secret call]] in Mecca, according to the count in *The Sealed Nectar*. He became Muslim together with his wife Fatima bint al-Khattab {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}.

## In The Sealed Nectar
The book mentions him among those who followed the first vanguard: Said ibn Zayd al-Adawi and his wife [[person/fatima-bint-al-khattab|Fatima bint al-Khattab]] al-Adawiyya, sister of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. He was from the [[group/banu-adi|Banu Adi]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## Not covered here
This chapter of the book mentions only his early Islam. The rest of his story is found in its proper places in the book and in other sources.

## For young visitors
Said ibn Zayd became Muslim early, together with his wife Fatima. She was the sister of Umar ibn al-Khattab, who had not yet become Muslim.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In episode 9 of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']] [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] tells the life of Sa'id ibn Zayd, describing him as an early convert and an emigrant who took part in all the Prophet's campaigns except Badr, and in the conquests under [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:01:00}. He says Sa'id's father, [[person/zayd-ibn-amr|Zayd]], died before the mission as a monotheist, never bowing to an idol, never eating what was slaughtered for idols and never indulging in the forbidden, and that the Prophet told his children to ask forgiveness for him and said he would be raised as a community by himself {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:02:30}. In his account Sa'id was Umar ibn al-Khattab's cousin and his brother-in-law {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:03:30}; his sister [[person/atika-bint-zayd|Atika bint Zayd]] was married to [[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-bakr|Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr]] and then to Umar, both of whom died as martyrs, and then to [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]], so that the people of Medina said whoever wanted martyrdom should marry Atika {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:03:30}. He cites the hadith naming the ten given glad tidings of Paradise, which he attributes to al-Tirmidhi from [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], the tenth of them being Sa'id ibn Zayd {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:04:00}.

He says one of his merits was that his prayers were answered {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:05:00}, and relates from Sahih Muslim, through Hisham ibn Urwa from his father, that Arwa bint Uways claimed he had taken part of her land and brought him before the governor of Medina, [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]]; Sa'id said he would never take anything of hers after hearing the Prophet warn that whoever wrongfully takes a span of land will have it hung around him from seven earths, and Marwan said he would ask him for no proof after that {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:05:30}. Here he warns against seizing sisters' inheritance, with the example of a man who inherits ten feddans together with his sister and takes them all {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:06:30}. He then relates that Sa'id prayed that, if she was lying, God would take her sight and let her die on her land; she soon went blind, fell into a pit on her land and died, and he links this to the command to fear the prayer of the wronged {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:07:30}.

He says Sa'id lived in Mecca with the Prophet and suffered the persecution the emigrants suffered {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:08:30}, and that among his highest merits is that he and his wife were the cause of [[event/islam-of-umar|Umar's conversion]]: Umar set out with his sword to kill the Prophet and met a man of Banu Zuhra, who told him his brother-in-law and his sister had become Muslims {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:09:00}. [[person/khabbab-ibn-al-aratt|Khabbab ibn al-Aratt]] was teaching them the Qur'an and hid when Umar knocked; Umar threw Sa'id down, beat him and sat on him, then struck his sister [[person/fatima-bint-al-khattab|Fatima]] when she defended her husband, so that blood ran from her nose and mouth, and she declared the testimony of faith {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:10:00}. Umar softened and felt ashamed and asked for the written sheet; she told him only the pure may touch it, so he washed and read from the opening of Surat Ta-Ha up to the verse commanding worship and prayer, then asked to be taken to the Prophet {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:11:30}. Khabbab came out hoping that Umar had been reached by the Prophet's prayer that God strengthen Islam through whichever of the two Umars was dearer to Him, Amr ibn Hisham or Umar ibn al-Khattab, and took him to Dar al-Arqam on the edge of Mecca, where he became Muslim and the Companions cried out "God is greatest" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:12:30}. He concludes that all of Umar's deeds count in Sa'id's favour, since the one who guides to good is like the one who does it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:13:30}.

He explains Sa'id's absence from Badr by saying the Prophet set out for [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]]'s caravan and did not oblige the Medinans to come, and that after it Sa'id missed no campaign {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:15:00}. At [[event/battle-of-ajnadayn|Ajnadayn]], in an army led by [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]], Byzantine arrows fell so thickly that the horses bolted; Sa'id asked Khalid's leave to charge, the Muslims charged and won within an hour {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:15:30}. He places [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmouk]] at the end of Abu Bakr's time and the start of Umar's rule, in Jordan {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:17:00}, and says [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] addressed the Muslims, urging patience, levelled spears, cover behind shields and silence except for the remembrance of God {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:20:30}. He relates through Sa'id that a Muslim resolved on martyrdom and asked Abu Ubayda whether he had a message for the Prophet; he gave him greetings and asked him to say that they had found their Lord's promise true, and Sa'id watched him advance alone against the Byzantines {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:21:30}. Sa'id then dismounted, levelled his spear and was the first to kill the first Byzantine horseman who charged the Muslims {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:22:00}.

He describes him as an ascetic: Abu Ubayda made him governor of [[place/damascus|Damascus]] after its conquest, and he wrote back that he would not prefer office to fighting alongside them, and asked him to send another governor so he could rejoin the army {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:22:30}. He says Sa'id sought martyrdom on every field yet died in his bed, and perhaps attained it by his intention, citing the hadith that whoever sincerely asks God for martyrdom is brought to the rank of the martyrs even if he dies in bed {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:02:00}. In his account Sa'id died at al-Aqiq on the edge of Medina at the age of seventy-five, was carried to the Prophet's Mosque, where [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Abd Allah ibn Umar]] led the prayer over him, [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]] and Ibn Umar went down into his grave, and he was buried in [[place/baqi-al-gharqad|al-Baqi']] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep09 00:24:00}.
