## Overview
Nasr ibn Ibrahim ibn Nasr ibn Ibrahim ibn Dawud al-Nabulsi al-Maqdisi, Abu al-Fath, is called by al-Zirikli the shaykh of the Shafi'is in Syria in his time {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 20}. He was of Nablus origin and was known as Ibn Abi Hafiz {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 20}. The entry gives his years as 377 to 490 AH (987 to 1096 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 20}.

## Travels and residence
He set out on a journey when he was about twenty and studied jurisprudence in Tyre, Sidon, Gaza, Diyar Bakr, Damascus, Jerusalem, Mecca and Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 20}. He stayed ten years in Tyre and nine in Damascus, where he met the imam al-Ghazali, and he died there {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 20}. He lived on the yield of a piece of land he owned in Nablus and accepted nothing from anyone {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 20}.

## His works
Among his books are "al-Hujja ala tarik al-mahajja" on hadith, "al-Amali" (a manuscript fragment of it survives), "al-Tahdhib" on jurisprudence in ten volumes, "al-Kafi" on jurisprudence in one volume, "al-Taqrib" and "al-Fusul" {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 20}.
