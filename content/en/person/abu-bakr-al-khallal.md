## Summary
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Harun, Abu Bakr al-Khallal, who collected the dicta related from Imam Ahmad with their chains and travelled the lands to meet the Imam's companions {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 124}. He authored the book al-Jami'; Ibn al-Jawzi reckoned it at about two hundred parts, Ibn Qayyim at about twenty volumes or more {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 124}. The book became a root of Ahmad's school — the companions looked into it and composed the books of fiqh from it {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 124}. He died in 311 AH {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 124}.

## His notice in the introduction to Kitab al-Sunna
'Adil Al Hamdan gives his life in the introduction to his edition of [[work/kitab-al-sunnah-khallal|Kitab al-Sunna]]: Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Harun ibn Yazid of Baghdad, Abu Bakr al-Khallal, the name referring to making or selling vinegar (*khall*); born 234 AH, died 311 AH. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 7}
- **Gatherer of Ahmad's learning**: the editor quotes Ibn Abi Ya'la that he travelled to distant lands to collect the answers of [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] from those who heard them from him and from those who heard them from them; al-Khatib al-Baghdadi that no follower of Ahmad's school collected more of it; and [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] that he travelled to Fars, Syria and the Jazira, wrote from some hundred of Ahmad's companions, and that "before him the imam had no independent school, until he traced Ahmad's texts and recorded them". {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 7-8}
- He relates that [[person/abu-bakr-al-marrudhi|al-Marrudhi]] told him no one had taken such care over Abu 'Abd Allah's answers as he had, except a man in Hamadan called Mattawayh, who gathered seventy large parts. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 8}
- He quotes [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] that he collected some forty volumes of Ahmad's legal texts and, on the foundations of religion, Kitab al-Sunna in about three volumes, though he did not exhaust all that was transmitted from him. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 9}

**Teachers**: al-Hasan ibn 'Arafa, al-Marrudhi, Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Harb al-Kirmani, Ya'qub ibn Sufyan al-Fasawi, al-'Abbas al-Duri, [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud al-Sijistani]], Ahmad's sons 'Abd Allah and Salih, al-Maymuni, [[person/ibrahim-al-harbi|Ibrahim al-Harbi]], Abu Zur'a al-Dimashqi and others. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 9}
**Students**: [[person/abu-bakr-ghulam-al-khallal|Abu Bakr 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Ja'far]], who stayed so close to him that he was called "Ghulam al-Khallal", Muhammad ibn al-Muzaffar and al-Hasan ibn Yusuf al-Saymari. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 9}
**Works**: al-Jami' on Ahmad's law in some twenty volumes; al-'Ilal in three; on enjoining good and forbidding wrong; on encouraging trade, crafts and work; the generations of Ahmad's companions; on knowledge; on funerals; Ahmad's character; al-Siyar; the rulings concerning other religious communities; rare words; manners; and Kitab al-Sunna. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 10}

He himself relates that he read Kitab al-Sunna in [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]] for years, and that in 292 AH he shunned some who rejected the chapter on [[question/al-maqam-al-mahmud|the Praiseworthy Station]] and wrote about it to his teachers in Baghdad. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 171}
