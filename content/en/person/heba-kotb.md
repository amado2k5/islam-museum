## Summary
Heba Gamal Kotb is an Egyptian physician, a professor of forensic medicine at Cairo University, and the best-known presenter of sexual-health advice in the Arab world. Born in Cairo on 19 September 1967, she became in 2003 the first licensed sexologist in Egypt, and from 2006 her weekly satellite programme *Kalam Kibeer* — "The Big Talk" — carried her answers to viewers' questions about marital sexuality across the Arab world. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-ar} Her own CV goes further, presenting her as the first Egyptian and Arab woman physician specialised in sexual medicine, and her official site as the first sex therapist in the Arab world, who launched the profession of sex counsellor there; these are her own claims. {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015-ar} {cite:hebakotb-net-about}

What made the programme a phenomenon was its frame. Kotb is a hijab-wearing physician who grounds every answer in the Qur'an and in Islamic teaching, and who agreed from the outset not to discuss sex outside marriage. Western coverage reached for a comparison and called her "Egypt's Dr. Ruth" and "the Dr. Ruth of the Muslim World". {cite:abc-news-heba-kotb-2007} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

## Early life and education
Kotb was born and raised in Cairo. She was schooled entirely in French in Cairo; her published curriculum vitae records her French schooling, and her own profile lists the Lycée Al Horreya, class of 1985. As a girl she wanted to become a surgeon. {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

She studied medicine at Cairo University and took her MB BCh in medicine and surgery in December 1991, with distinction. She then specialised in forensic medicine at the same university: a master's degree in May 1997, with a thesis on the medicolegal implications of sexually transmitted diseases in sexually abused children, and a doctorate in May 2000, with a thesis entitled "Sexual Assaults and Their Health Consequences". {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015}

It was the master's research, she has said, that turned her towards sexology. Writing on sexually abused children, she realised how little she herself knew about normal sexuality. She began to research the subject and came across a passage of the Qur'an addressing sexual relations between husbands and wives, which she read as speaking to the woman's sexual rights. Of that moment she said: "I was so proud of my religion when I saw that. My religion was advanced enough to talk about women's rights in sexuality how many years before modern science did?" {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

She completed the turn in Florida. She took a diploma in sex therapy and human sexuality at the American Academy of Clinical Sexologists, and then a doctorate in clinical sexology at Maimonides University in Florida, completed in 2004 with a dissertation entitled "Sexuality in Islam"; the dissertation won the academy's best-research prize in 2004. In 2003, a year before the doctorate was completed, she became the first licensed sexologist in Egypt. {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en} {cite:arab-media-society-swank-2007}

## Academic career
Kotb teaches forensic medicine at Cairo University's Faculty of Medicine, Kasr Al-Ainy. Her curriculum vitae gives her post, from October 2005, as assistant professor of forensic medicine and clinical toxicology at the faculty, and the Associated Press profile of 2006 likewise places her at Cairo University. The Arabic Wikipedia article describes her in the same terms, as a professor of forensic medicine at Cairo University. {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015} {cite:ap-heba-kotb-2006} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-ar} Later sources give her the full rank: the Women and Memory Forum's directory of Egyptian women experts, read on 18 November 2026, styles her Professor of Forensic Medicine at Cairo University, and her official site states that she rose through the department to professorial rank. The sources read do not date the promotion. {cite:wmf-whoisshe-heba-kotb} {cite:hebakotb-net-about}

Her curriculum vitae also lists roles beyond the faculty: membership of the faculty and board of sages of the American Academy of Clinical Sexologists, consultancy to the Cairo office of the United Nations Population Fund on sexual-health education for young people and for couples about to marry, and work as a scientific consultant on sex-related fatwas for an Islamic counselling service. These roles are recorded here as her CV states them. {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015} Her Arabic CV, her official site and the Women and Memory Forum name the Islamic online service: she was the volunteer consultant for IslamOnline's section on marital and sexual problems. Her CV and site add that she wrote the sexuality curriculum of the Kuwaiti programme and foundation «كرسي النور» ("The Light Chair"), co-founded several NGOs in the Arab world working in family counselling and guidance, and was the first physician to give instructive courses to different groups, among them couples about to marry and married couples; these courses became the ones she now offers. They are recorded as she states them. {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015-ar} {cite:hebakotb-net-about} {cite:wmf-whoisshe-heba-kotb}

## The clinic and the column
In 2002 Kotb opened her Cairo clinic for sexology and marital counselling, an Islam-oriented practice offering sex therapy to male and female patients. In the same year she began writing a sex-advice column in a Cairo newspaper; several profiles identify the paper as Al-Ahram, and the Arabic Wikipedia article links her later articles in Al-Masry Al-Youm. The clinic and the column made her name in Egypt before television did. {cite:ap-heba-kotb-2006} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-ar} The sources date the two strands differently. The Associated Press dates her first newspaper column to 2002; the Arab Media Society study of May 2007 dates her write-in column in Al-Ahram, «الغرف المغلقة» ("Behind Closed Doors"), to 2004. Her CV lists her press sections as «الأبواب المغلقة» in the weekly Al-Ahram Al-Arabi, «مدرسة السعادة الزوجية» in Kol El Nas, «اسألوا هبة» in the weekly edition of Al-Masry Al-Youm, and the lead article of Al-Ahram's monthly medical supplement, among others. {cite:ap-heba-kotb-2006} {cite:arab-media-society-swank-2007} {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015-ar}

## The Big Talk
Her flagship programme, *Kalam Kibeer* ("The Big Talk"; in Arabic, «كلام كبير»), launched in 2006 on Al-Mehwar, an Egyptian satellite channel, and was broadcast across the Arab world. It was a weekly call-in show: viewers telephoned with questions about marital sexual life, and Kotb answered them on air, in detail, explicitly within an Islamic frame. {cite:ap-heba-kotb-2006} {cite:arab-media-society-swank-2007}

The range of questions the sources record is wide. As the English Wikipedia article summarises the programme from its cited reports, she discussed masturbation (which she discourages for women), oral sex (permitted, if both spouses consent), sexual positions, foreplay, orgasm, erectile dysfunction and premature ejaculation; she held that sex during a woman's period is prohibited in Islam, that anal sex is condemned and prohibited, that homosexuality is a disease, and she practised conversion therapy. Marital rape she discussed in terms of possessiveness and of a husband's feeling that his wife is not giving him time and attention. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

One boundary was set in advance: as part of her proposal for the programme she agreed not to discuss sex outside marriage. The show's frankness therefore operated entirely inside the marital frame, which is also the frame of her clinical and written work. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

She later stated that she registered the programme's name as her intellectual property in 2006, after the Al-Mehwar run. The programme continued in a second incarnation, *Kalam Kibeer Gedan* ("Very Big Talk"), on the Al-Hayat channel; the Arabic Wikipedia article likewise records that the programme moved from Al-Mehwar to a second programme on Al-Hayat. Clips and full segments of both programmes remain the core of her YouTube channel. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-ar} {cite:hebakotb-net}

## Other programmes
Beyond her own shows, Kotb appeared for years as a regular guest in segments on Al-Hayat programmes, among them *Biwodouh* («بوضوح»), presented by Amr El-Leithy — many of those episodes, uploaded to her channel around 2016–2017, work through viewers' marital problems in the same question-and-answer form — and *Bayt Al-Ezz* («بيت العز»), in segments uploaded around 2015. Her booking agency describes her as the host of six specialised television shows; only the two *Kalam Kibeer* programmes are independently documented in the sources read for this record, and the agency's larger number is therefore not used here. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-ar} {cite:mn2s-heba-kotb} Her own CV of 2015 gives a different account of *Bayt Al-Ezz*: it says she was then presenting the programme three times a week on the Egyptian channel Al-Tahrir. Both accounts are recorded here. The same CV names her specialised programmes as *Kalam Kibeer* (2006), *Bedoun Rotoush* («بدون رتوش», 2007) and *Kalam Kibeer Gedan* (2008). On Al-Nahar she presented *Eyadat Hobb* («عيادة حب»), a relationship-advice programme that Cairo 360 reviewed in April 2015. Her official site lists six programmes of her own, adding *Esh-ha Shabab* («عيشها شباب»); that title is recorded only on her site. {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015-ar} {cite:cairo360-eyadat-hobb-2015} {cite:hebakotb-net-about}

## Books and writings
*Lil Kubaar Faqat* («للكبار فقط», "Adults Only") is a series of question-and-answer books on sexual relations, treated from the perspectives of medicine and of religion and published in Cairo by Hala li-l-Nashr wa-l-Tawzi'. The Arab Media Society article of May 2007 describes it as a two-volume set compiled from her Al-Ahram column; her official site now counts four question-and-answer books. {cite:arab-media-society-swank-2007} {cite:hebakotb-net-about}

The New York Times reported on her work in 2009, and by 2017 she had published four books on sexual relationships. Her official site further credits her with two books on marital compatibility and disagreements and a forensic-medicine textbook for medical students; its Arabic page counts seven books in sexual medicine besides the textbook, and a three-volume encyclopedia of human sexuality in preparation. These titles could not be verified from catalogue sources when this record was compiled, so they are not listed here. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en} {cite:hebakotb-net-about}

Her newspaper columns, begun in 2002, continued alongside the books and the programmes, and her official site carries her articles together with the "Big Talk" magazine. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en} {cite:hebakotb-net}

## Online presence and courses
Kotb's television archive became her online presence. Her YouTube channel, "Dr. Heba Kotb | د. هبة قطب" (@HebaKotbSexologist), carries clips and full segments from *Kalam Kibeer*, *Kalam Kibeer Gedan*, *Bayt Al-Ezz* and her *Biwodouh* appearances, a lecture-style series, «محبكينها شويتين», on sexual culture in Islamic law, and recent short medical explainers in Arabic and English on subjects such as premature ejaculation, low desire and pain during intercourse, alongside occasional personal items. Some of the older television clips have been watched millions of times — the most-viewed items stand at roughly 6.5 and 5.7 million views — while recent uploads draw far smaller audiences, in the hundreds to the low tens of thousands. Her booking agency, MN2S, put the channel's subscribers at about 486,000 when its roster page was read on 17 November 2026; that figure is the agency's, not a platform count read directly, and it is recorded with that caution. {cite:mn2s-heba-kotb}

Her verified Facebook page, "Dr. Heba Kotb - الدكتورة هبة قطب", had more than two million followers in 2021, as Reuters reported; the MN2S roster page, read on 17 November 2026, gives 2.9 million Facebook followers, 601,200 on Instagram and 261,000 on TikTok, where her official account is @hebakotbofficial. These are agency figures, useful as orders of magnitude rather than audited counts, and they are dated here as the sources date them. {cite:reuters-conversion-therapy-2021} {cite:mn2s-heba-kotb}

Through her official site she runs the "Simply Happy" platform, which offers courses for those about to marry, for the married, on pregnancy, and on mother and child, together with her articles, videos and magazine. {cite:hebakotb-net}

## International coverage
The international press found Kotb within a year of the programme's launch, and its coverage is a large part of how she is documented. The Associated Press profiled her in late 2006, describing a conservative Muslim physician answering intimate questions on satellite television. The Wall Street Journal followed on 12 October 2006 with Mariam Fam's report, "An Arab Therapist Seeks to Lift the Veil on a Touchy Subject". {cite:ap-heba-kotb-2006} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

In April 2007 ABC News carried her on *Nightline* as "The Dr. Ruth of the Muslim World", and CNN's report of 26 April 2007, "Egypt's 'Dr. Ruth'", recorded her childhood ambition to be a surgeon and the thesis research that redirected her. In May 2007 she was interviewed on Al Jazeera English's *Riz Khan* programme. Salon's June 2007 account, "Sex and the married Muslim", remains one of the fullest English-language descriptions of her positions. Time magazine's February 2008 piece was titled "An Islamic Answer to Dr. Ruth", and on 5 June 2009 The New York Times published Robert F. Worth's "Challenging Sex Taboos, with Help from the Koran". {cite:abc-news-heba-kotb-2007} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

The comparison that runs through this coverage — to Ruth Westheimer, the German-American sex therapist known as Dr. Ruth — is the Western press's shorthand for a frank, elderly-by-format television adviser on sex; in Kotb's case the shorthand marks the contrast as much as the similarity, since her authority is presented as medical and religious at once. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en} {cite:abc-news-heba-kotb-2007}

In 2010 Kotb was one of four Egyptian women interviewed for the Egypt segment of Oprah Winfrey's episode "Marriage Around the Globe", narrated by the Danish reporter Nanna Norup. Muslimah Media Watch's review of February 2010 records that she later remarked that the guests had not been properly introduced and that their statements were broadcast as edited excerpts without context, and that on the day after the episode aired in Egypt she appeared widely on television and in newspaper interviews. The Women and Memory Forum lists an honour to her from Harpo Productions, the programme's producer, in 2010. {cite:mmw-oprah-egypt-2010} {cite:wmf-whoisshe-heba-kotb}

## Honours and recognition
Her CV and official site list the honours below, recorded here as she lists them; the Women and Memory Forum's directory independently lists four of them, marked as such. They are: the American Academy of Clinical Sexologists' best-research prize for her dissertation (2004, also listed by the Forum); an honour from the Faculty of Arts of Ain Shams University (2004); a certificate of appreciation from Sheikha Fatima bint Mubarak of the United Arab Emirates (2005); the excellence and pioneering award of the organisation Physicians for Peace (2005); two certificates from the Qatari family-guidance centre under the patronage of Sheikha Moza (2005 and 2007); an honour from Al-Azhar University (2006, also listed by the Forum); guest of honour at OPEC's annual conference in Vienna (2006); honours from the Lebanese Economy and Business foundation and from a conference in Bahrain on women's role in the media (both 2007); an honour for scientific distinction from Long Beach University in California, decided in February 2008 (also listed by the Forum); an honour from Harpo Productions (2010, listed by the Forum); an honour from the Kuwaiti Ministry of Health and from Sheikha Awrad al-Jaber al-Sabah (October 2011); an honour from the Algerian family-guidance foundation (2013); and guest of honour at the Muslim Family Conference in Istanbul (2016) and at the Arab Conference of Forensic Medicine in Kuwait (2017). {cite:heba-kotb-cv-2015-ar} {cite:hebakotb-net-about} {cite:wmf-whoisshe-heba-kotb}

## Her method and beliefs
Kotb's stated method is to ground sexual-health advice in the Qur'an and in Islamic teaching. She cites Surah al-Baqarah, the longest chapter of the Qur'an, as her inspiration for understanding human sexuality, noting that it speaks of the daily life of a man and a woman and commands the husband to give his wife pleasure. Her general argument, as Salon and ABC News reported it, is that Islam is permissive about sex within marriage, that a wife's sexual satisfaction is a religiously recognised right, and that the silence surrounding the subject is a product of culture, not of religion. To the Associated Press she said: "I'm very proud of my religion… My studies revealed to me more and more how Islam was ahead in all sexual matters." {cite:abc-news-heba-kotb-2007} {cite:ap-heba-kotb-2006} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

The same sources describe the other side of the position. Her stance is liberal by Egyptian standards and conservative by Western ones: she opposes sex outside marriage, condemns anal sex and homosexuality — which she has described as a disease — discourages female masturbation, holding that "a woman has to remain blank until she gets married", and has practised conversion therapy. These positions are recorded here as the sources report them. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

## Perspectives and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**Supporters and the programme's defenders** point to the space the programme opened: a woman physician, visibly observant, discussing marital sexuality on satellite television in medical language, under a religious frame that made the discussion possible for audiences who would not have accepted it in any other. The international coverage above largely took this view of her significance. {cite:abc-news-heba-kotb-2007} {cite:arab-media-society-swank-2007}

**Conservative clerical critics** attacked the programme from its first period. Sheikh Youssef al-Badri, a conservative cleric, accused it of "increasing the number of sex perverts", as Salon reported in 2007. The criticism was directed at the public discussion of the subject itself, not only at particular answers. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}

**Reporting on conversion therapy.** In June 2021 the Thomson Reuters Foundation reported that Arabic-language content promoting conversion therapy was thriving on Facebook despite the platform's ban on such content, and cited Kotb's page — then followed by more than two million people — among the material it examined. Facebook told the reporters that content offering to change a person's sexual orientation or gender identity is against its standards; the report recorded that Arabic posts continued to appear. The museum records the report as Reuters published it. {cite:reuters-conversion-therapy-2021}

**The 2025 programme-name dispute.** In July 2025 Kotb posted publicly that the ON channel's planned programme for the presenter Maha El-Sagheer, also to be called «كلام كبير», used the name of her 2006 Al-Mehwar programme, which she said she had registered as intellectual property in 2006. ON deleted the programme's promotional material. Kotb stated that she was not accusing El-Sagheer personally and did not know who had chosen the name. The episode is recorded from the Egyptian press coverage of July 2025. {cite:newsroom-info-heba-kotb-2025}

## Personal life
Kotb is married to Dr. Hesham Ali Hassan, a professor of ophthalmology at Cairo University's Faculty of Medicine, and has three daughters. This museum publishes no further family detail. {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-ar} {cite:wikipedia-heba-kotb-en}
