## Overview
Former shaykh of al-Azhar (d. 1383/1963); born at Minyat Mansur in the Buhayra, he took the Azhar's headship in 1378 AH and founded the Islamic Research Academy project; among his books is al-Islam 'Aqida wa-Shari'a, on which this entry rests. The dates come from Muhammad 'Imara's introduction to the book and have not yet been checked against a second source.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam presents Mahmud Shaltut (died 1963) as a religious scholar of Egypt who strove to reform al-Azhar, halting its decline and restoring the active part it once played in shaping Egypt's education, culture and political fate. It says he is chiefly remembered for his knowledge of Islamic law and Qur'anic interpretation, and that he envisaged a reformed Islam and a shariah that could become a source of modern legislation. According to the Dictionary he urged a more active role abroad for al-Azhar, in the struggle against religious fanaticism and for the unity of the ummah and the various schools of thought. It adds that reorganization and budget allowances let him practise ijtihad (independent legal reasoning), aiming to reform education by bringing in modern subjects and to put an end to religious fanaticism by reducing the differences among the legal schools (madhhabs). {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2159}
