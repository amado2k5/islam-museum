## Summary

Anthony Tan (born 1982) is a Singaporean businessman, the co-founder and chief executive officer of Grab, a publicly traded technology company and the first unicorn in Southeast Asia; in 2021 Forbes listed him as one of Singapore's richest people with an estimated net worth of $790 million {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en} {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-ms}.

## Life

Tan was born in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, in 1982 {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en} {cite:wikidata-anthony-tan}. His father, Tan Heng Chew, is an automobile manufacturing executive who served as president of Tan Chong Motor, the Malaysian company that assembles and distributes Nissan vehicles in Southeast Asia; his great-grandfather was a taxi driver and his grandfather introduced the Japanese automotive industry in Malaysia {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en} {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-zh}. He worked on the assembly line at his father's company and attended meetings with union bosses at a young age, started his first business venture at 11 retail trading X-Men comics, and at 14 volunteered to raise money for the AIDS Foundation {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en} {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-ms}. He attended the University of Chicago, earning a bachelor's degree in economics and public policy, and later an MBA from Harvard Business School {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en}. He is married to Chloe Tong, daughter of Tong Kooi Ong, and they have five children; concurrent with the company's move to Singapore, he took up Singapore citizenship in 2016 {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en}.

## Work

Tan was head of supply chain and marketing at the Tan Chong Group but chose not to work for the family business, developing his company idea after a Harvard friend visited Malaysia and complained about its taxi system {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en}. While attending Harvard Business School he partnered with his classmate Tan Hooi Ling — no relation, despite the shared surname — on making taxis safer in their home country; their business plan for a taxi booking app won second prize at the HBS New Venture Competition in 2011, and with the $25,000 prize money, their own funds and an investment from Tan's mother they launched MyTeksi in June 2012, headquartered in Kuala Lumpur {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en} {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-id}. By 2016 the company, backed by Singapore's state investment firm Temasek Holdings, had relocated to Singapore and rebranded as Grab {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en}. Tan was a speaker at the World Economic Forum in 2019 and a founding member of the AI for Good Global Commission {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en}. In November 2023 Grab faced boycotts in Indonesia and Malaysia after an Instagram story by his wife Chloe Tong, posted during a family heritage trip to Israel, resurfaced in which she expressed being "completely in love" with the country — the article presents the post as hers, not as a statement of his {cite:wikipedia-anthony-tan-en}.
