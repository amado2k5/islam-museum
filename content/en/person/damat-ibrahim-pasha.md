## Overview
Damat Ibrahim Pasha was grand vizier under Sultan Ahmad III; al-Sallabi counts him the first Ottoman official to acknowledge the importance of understanding Europe. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.311-312}

## The opening to Europe
He maintained regular contact with the European ambassadors resident in Istanbul and sent Ottoman ambassadors to the European capitals — above all Vienna and Paris — for the first time, charging them not only with treaty business but with supplying the state with information about European diplomacy and military power. In his period European influence began in palace-building and the adoption of European customs, the first stirrings of the modern Ottoman literary movement were recorded as translation into Turkish picked up, envoys were sent to France to inspect its manufactures, and a printing office was established in Istanbul. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.312}
