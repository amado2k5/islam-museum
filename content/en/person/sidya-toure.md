# Sidya Touré

Sidya Touré (15 August 1945 – 25 September 2026) was a Guinean politician who served as Prime Minister of Guinea from 1996 to 1999 and afterwards led the opposition Union of Republican Forces (UFR). {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en} {cite:wikidata-sidya-toure}

## Prime minister

Touré was born at Dimbokro in French West Africa, worked as a treasury inspector, and spent a period in exile in Côte d'Ivoire. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-fr} {cite:wikidata-sidya-toure} Regarded as a reform-minded technocrat, he was appointed prime minister by President Lansana Conté on 9 July 1996, becoming Guinea's first prime minister since 1984, after a coup attempt had shaken the regime. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en} When Conté changed course, Touré was dismissed on 8 March 1999 and succeeded by Lamine Sidimé. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en}

## Opposition leader

Touré became an opposition leader and president of the UFR. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en} He joined the boycott of the 2003 presidential election, was detained and charged with plotting against the government in 2004, and was cleared on appeal. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en} On 28 September 2009 he took part in the great opposition protest in Conakry; soldiers opened fire on the protesters, Touré suffered a serious head injury, and from a hospital bathroom he covertly telephoned the BBC to give his account. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en} Standing for the UFR in the 2010 presidential election, he placed third with 13.62% of the vote, accepted the confirmed result, and was courted as a potential kingmaker for the runoff; in September 2021 he returned to Guinea after ten months of exile in Europe. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en}

## Death

Sidya Touré died at a hospital in Paris on 25 September 2026, at the age of 81; the French article records that he was buried at the Williamsville cemetery in Côte d'Ivoire, and Jeune Afrique's obituary described him as an eternal opponent who died in exile. {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-en} {cite:wikipedia-sidya-toure-fr} {cite:wikidata-sidya-toure}
