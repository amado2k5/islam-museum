## Summary
Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ali al-Wahidi of Nishapur, Qur'an commentator and philologist, died in 468 AH. Hajji Khalifa calls his [[work/asbab-al-nuzul-wahidi|Asbab al-nuzul]] the most famous book in its field, abridged by Burhan al-Din Ibrahim ibn Umar al-Ja'bari. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.381} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.5}

## His nisba
The editor of Asbab al-nuzul, Kamal Basyuni Zaghlul, quotes Ibn Khallikan that he did not know the origin of this nisba and al-Sam'ani did not mention it, then found it refers to "al-Wahid ibn al-Din" of Mahra, as Abu Ahmad al-Askari states; al-Mukhtasar refers it to al-Wahid ibn Maysara. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.5} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.6}

## Birth and death
He was born in Nishapur, the year not fixed by his biographers, and died in Nishapur; the sources agree on 468 AH. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.6} The month is given as Jumada. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 329}

## Teachers and students
He heard tafsir from [[person/abu-ishaq-al-thalabi|Abu Ishaq Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Tha'labi]], grammar from Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Darir, and lexicography from Abu al-Fadl Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Arudi, and heard Abu al-Qasim al-Busti, Abu Uthman Sa'id ibn Muhammad al-Hiri and Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Farisi. Among his students were Ahmad ibn Umar al-Arghiyani and Abd al-Jabbar ibn Muhammad al-Khawari. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.6} The editor Mahir Yasin Fahl holds that what al-Wahidi ascribes to "the commentators" in general, or leaves unattributed, comes mostly from his teacher al-Tha'labi. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.54}

## His school, as the editor infers it
Zaghlul infers from his tafsir that he was Ash'ari in creed, citing his remark on the verse about the sealing of hearts that it "refutes the Qadariyya", and Shafi'i in law, citing his statement that the verse on listening silently to the Qur'an does not imply giving up recitation behind the imam. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.6} This is the editor's inference from his texts.

## Works
In tafsir he wrote three books, al-Wajiz, al-Wasit and al-Basit, and Asbab al-nuzul counts among the tafsir works; his other books include al-Da'awat, al-Mahsul, al-Maghazi, a commentary on the Diwan of al-Mutanabbi (printed in Berlin, 1858), al-I'rab fi ilm al-i'rab, Nafy al-tahrif an al-Qur'an al-sharif, al-Tahbir fi sharh al-asma' al-husna, and al-Wasit fi al-amthal (printed in Kuwait, 1975). {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.6} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.7}

## On his chains of transmission
Fahl says that al-Wahidi "was not a specialist in the science of prophetic hadith", so that weak and very weak chains entered Asbab al-nuzul. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.7} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.8}

The Gregorian year of death is derived (about 1075 CE).
