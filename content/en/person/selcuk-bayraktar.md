## Summary
Selçuk Bayraktar is the Turkish engineer and businessman who built Türkiye's indigenous armed-drone industry. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology as chairman and chief technology officer of Baykar, leading Türkiye's unmanned aerial vehicle development; Wikipedia credits him as the designer of the Bayraktar TB2, the combat drone that made the company a global name. {cite:muslim500-selcuk-bayraktar} {cite:wikipedia-selcuk-bayraktar}

## Formation
Born in Istanbul in 1979, the son of the engineer and entrepreneur Özdemir Bayraktar who founded the family firm, Selçuk trained as a pilot — he is himself a licensed pilot — and studied engineering at Istanbul Technical University before taking a master's at the University of Pennsylvania and doctoral work at MIT, which he left to return to the family company as Türkiye pushed for defence self-sufficiency. {cite:wikipedia-selcuk-bayraktar}

## Building the drone
At Baykar he led the engineering of successive unmanned aircraft — the mini-class Bayraktar systems, then the TB2, a medium-altitude long-endurance armed drone that entered service in 2014 and was exported widely. Conflicts from Libya and Nagorno-Karabakh to Ukraine made the TB2 a byword for cheap, effective airpower and made Baykar one of the world's largest UAV exporters; the follow-on Akıncı heavy drone and the Kızılelma jet-powered unmanned fighter extended the line. {cite:wikipedia-selcuk-bayraktar} {cite:muslim500-selcuk-bayraktar}

## Public figure
Bayraktar is a public figure well beyond engineering: he married Sümeyye Erdoğan, daughter of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, and speaks openly of the project's national and civilisational framing — an indigenous technology built free of foreign suppliers. Turkish press coverage also presents him as a practising Muslim; the museum reports his public standing attributed, without verdict on the politics in which the drones are used. {cite:wikipedia-selcuk-bayraktar} {cite:muslim500-selcuk-bayraktar}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Bayraktar is the engineer-statesman of Muslim technological self-reliance: the designer whose aircraft changed both Turkish industry and the wars in which it flew — a figure of influence exercised through hardware on a geopolitical scale. {cite:muslim500-selcuk-bayraktar} {cite:wikipedia-selcuk-bayraktar}

## Engineer, son-in-law, symbol
Bayraktar's record must hold two facts at once. The first is engineering: the Bayraktar TB2's architecture — low-cost airframes, indigenous mission computers, smart munitions — was a genuine technical achievement, and Selçuk led it after MIT. The second is political economy: the company's rise accelerated after 2016, and Bayraktar's marriage to President Erdoğan's daughter made him a figure of both admiration and criticism inside Turkish politics. The museum's record describes the technical achievement and attributes the political readings to those who make them; the listing itself records his global profile in technology. {cite:wikipedia-selcuk-bayraktar} {cite:muslim500-selcuk-bayraktar}

## The engineer's inheritance
Bayraktar's record is the museum's example of the engineer who inherits a company and transforms it: the son of a Turkish defence entrepreneur who took his father's automotive-parts firm and made it into the world's most talked-about drone manufacturer. The transformation is the story; the inheritance is the context; and the result — a Turkish Muslim engineer whose name is now synonymous with a class of weapon — is the profile his Muslim 500 entry records. The museum notes the technical achievement and leaves the political readings to those who make them. {cite:muslim500-selcuk-bayraktar} {cite:wikipedia-selcuk-bayraktar}
