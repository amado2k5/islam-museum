## Summary
[[person/mukhayriq|Mukhayriq]] al-Nadri, the Israelite, of Banu al-Nadir {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1382}. Al-Waqidi mentions he became Muslim and was martyred at Uhud; al-Waqidi and al-Baladhuri say it is also said he was of Banu Qaynuqa', or of Banu al-Qatyun — he was a scholar {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1382}.

## His bequest of his wealth
He bequeathed his wealth to the Prophet — seven orchards: al-Maythab, al-Sa'ifa, al-Dalal, Husna, Barqa, al-A'waf and the mashraba of Umm Ibrahim — and the Prophet made them alms {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1382}. In al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar's account in Akhbar al-Madina some names differ: he gives al-Maythar instead of al-Maythab and al-Ma'wan instead of al-A'waf {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1382}.

## Uhud
Umar ibn Shabba relates in Akhbar al-Madina from Ibn Shihab that the Prophet's endowed alms had been Mukhayriq's wealth bequeathed to him, and that he witnessed [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] and was killed there — and the Prophet said: "Mukhayriq is the forerunner of the Jews, Salman the forerunner of Persia, and Bilal the forerunner of Abyssinia" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1382}. Abd al-Aziz said: it has reached me that he was of the remnants of Banu Qaynuqa' {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1382}.

## Al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar's account
Al-Zubayr relates from Uthman ibn Ka'b ibn Muhammad that when the Prophet went out to Uhud, Mukhayriq said to the Jews: "will you not aid Muhammad? By God, you know that aiding him is binding upon you." They said: "today is the Sabbath." He said: "no Sabbath" — took his sword and went to the Prophet and fought until wounds finished him; when death drew near he said: "my wealth goes to Muhammad, to place where he wishes" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1382}.

## In the Sira and al-Rawd al-Unuf
Ibn Ishaq relates that Mukhayriq was of Banu Tha'laba ibn al-Fityawn, and that on the day of Uhud he said: "Jews, by God, you know that helping Muhammad is a duty upon you." They said, "Today is the Sabbath." He said, "There is no Sabbath for you," took his sword and gear and said, "If I am killed, my wealth goes to Muhammad, to do with as he wishes"; then he went to the Prophet and fought beside him until he was killed, and the Prophet said, as Ibn Ishaq heard, "Mukhayriq is the best of the Jews." {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 12}

In his commentary [[person/al-suhayli|al-Suhayli]] says that Mukhayriq was of Banu al-Nadir, and that when the Prophet returned from Uhud he made his property into endowments — the first endowment (*hubus*) made in Islam — reporting this from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi; al-Zuhri said they were seven orchards, and he names them, among them al-Safiya, al-Dalal, Burqa and the Mashraba of Umm Ibrahim, so called because she lived there. Al-Suhayli presents this as a completion of Ibn Ishaq's account and an additional benefit. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 47} On endowments see [[concept/waqf|waqf]].
