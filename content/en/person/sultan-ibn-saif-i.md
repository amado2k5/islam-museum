## Overview
Sultan ibn Saif ibn Malik al-Ya'ribi was, in al-Zirikli's words, the second of the Ya'ariba imams of the Ibadi school in Oman (the Ibadis are a school distinct from Sunni Islam) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 108}. He was pledged allegiance on the day Imam Nasir ibn Murshid died; the entry gives the year as 1050 AH, at Nizwa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 108}. The Omani chronicle in Badger's translation puts Nasir's death on Friday 22 April 1649, which is 1059 AH, and says the notables chose Sultan as his successor straight after {cite:badger-history-imams-seyyids-oman-1871 pp. 74, 77-78}; the museum follows 1059 AH for the oath of allegiance.

## His rule
He expelled the Portuguese from Muscat, which had been in their hands, built many ships with which he protected the shores of his country, and attacked Portuguese centres in India and on the coasts of Africa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 108}. Al-Zirikli cites Guillain's «Documents historiques»: the Portuguese traveller, the priest Manuel Godinho, wrote in his account of a journey from India to Portugal by way of the Persian Gulf in 1663 that Sultan ibn Saif was not content to drive the Portuguese from his land but pursued them into the territories they held, besieging Mombasa and harassing them at Pompée, and that his ships captured many Portuguese vessels {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 108}. Al-Zirikli adds that the kingdom of Oman flourished in his days {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 108}.

## Character and death
Al-Zirikli describes him as brave, resolute and humble towards his subjects, not shut away from them, walking the road alone, greeting people and talking with them {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 108}. He ruled until he died at Nizwa in 1091 AH, 1680 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 108}.
