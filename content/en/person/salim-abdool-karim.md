## Summary
Salim Safurdeen Abdool Karim is a South African physician, epidemiologist and virologist whose research has shaped the global response to HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis and COVID-19. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology; Wikipedia records his direction of CAPRISA, the Centre for the AIDS Programme of Research in South Africa, and his professorship at Columbia University. {cite:muslim500-salim-abdool-karim} {cite:wikipedia-salim-abdool-karim}

## Formation under apartheid
Abdool Karim was born in 1960 in Durban into a South African Indian Muslim family and came of age under apartheid, a system whose racial ordering shaped his early experience and, he has said in interviews, his commitment to science in the service of justice. He trained in medicine at the University of Natal, worked in public health, and took advanced training at Columbia University in New York before returning to South Africa as the AIDS epidemic grew. {cite:wikipedia-salim-abdool-karim}

## CAPRISA and HIV prevention
In 2002 he founded CAPRISA in Durban, which grew into one of the world's leading AIDS research centres. Its landmark CAPRISA 004 trial demonstrated that a vaginal gel containing the antiretroviral tenofovir reduced women's risk of HIV infection — a breakthrough in prevention science that proved a microbicide approach could work. His group also made defining contributions to understanding HIV-TB co-infection in the era when tuberculosis was the leading killer of South Africans with HIV. {cite:wikipedia-salim-abdool-karim} {cite:muslim500-salim-abdool-karim}

## COVID-19 and public leadership
When COVID-19 arrived, Abdool Karim became the public scientific face of South Africa's response: he chaired the Ministerial Advisory Committee advising the government, explained the evidence on lockdowns and variants to the nation, and joined international scientific bodies on pandemic response — work that made him a household name far beyond epidemiology. {cite:wikipedia-salim-abdool-karim}

## Honours
His recognitions include the Order of Mapungubwe, South Africa's highest honour for scientific achievement; election to leading academies; and international awards for AIDS research. He holds the position of CAPRISA professor at the University of KwaZulu-Natal and a chair at Columbia's Mailman School of Public Health. {cite:wikipedia-salim-abdool-karim}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Abdool Karim represents the scientist of the Global South whose work answers the diseases of his own society: an epidemiologist formed under apartheid whose trials changed how the world prevents HIV, and whose public voice carried a country through a pandemic. The Muslim 500 cites the impact of that work on thousands of lives. {cite:muslim500-salim-abdool-karim} {cite:wikipedia-salim-abdool-karim}

## A scientist in the political fray
Abdool Karim's career also belongs to the record of scientists who confront governments: he fought publicly against South Africa's AIDS-denialist era under President Mbeki, defending antiretroviral treatment when the state questioned it — an episode in which his epidemiology was also civic courage. Two decades later, during COVID-19, he stood on the opposite side of the same argument, advising a government through lockdowns and variants. The two stances look contradictory only until one sees the constant: the scientist answers the evidence, and the country gets the benefit of the argument. {cite:wikipedia-salim-abdool-karim} {cite:muslim500-salim-abdool-karim}

## The scientist in the room
Abdool Karim's record is the museum's example of the scientist who accepts the cost of public engagement: the epidemiologist who advised governments through two pandemics and paid the price of visibility — criticism, scrutiny, the distortion of advice into politics. That willingness to stay in the room, to keep answering the questions a government asks rather than the ones a scientist would prefer, is the profile his Muslim 500 entry records; the museum keeps it as the record of a scientist who chose the difficult path of relevance. {cite:muslim500-salim-abdool-karim}
