## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Abi Abla al-Uqayli al-Shami al-Maqdisi, Abu Ishaq, is called by al-Dhahabi the imam to be followed, shaykh of Palestine, one of the last of the Followers, a man of merit and standing {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. His kunya is also given as Abu al-Abbas, Abu Sa'id or Abu Isma'il, and he is said to be Ibrahim ibn Shamir ibn Yaqzan ibn Murtahil al-Ramli {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. He was born after the year 60 {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. The sign printed with his entry shows that al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i recorded his hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Wathila ibn al-Asqa', Anas ibn Malik, Abu Umama al-Bahili, Bilal ibn Abi al-Darda, Khalid ibn Ma'dan and many others; some say he met Ibn Umar, and if not, his narration from him is mursal {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. Ibn Ishaq narrated from him and died before him, as did Umar ibn al-Harith, and so did Ibn Shawdhab, Malik, al-Layth, Ibn al-Mubarak, Baqiyya ibn al-Walid, Muhammad ibn Humayr, Ayyub ibn Suwayd, Muhammad ibn Ziyad al-Maqdisi and many others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}.

## What the critics said
Yahya ibn Ma'in and al-Nasa'i called him reliable {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. Al-Hakim says he asked al-Daraqutni about him, who answered that the routes to him are not clean but that he himself is reliable {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. Some say he narrated about a hundred hadith, and al-Tabarani compiled a book of the hadith of the Syrian shaykhs in which Ibn Abi Abla's musnad came to seven leaves, about half of them objectionable in their chain back to him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}.

## His life and sayings
Al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik used to send him with the stipends of the people of Jerusalem, which he distributed among them {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. It is reported that Hisham wished to appoint him over the land tax of Egypt and he declined, saying he had no sight for the tax and no strength for it; Hisham grew angry, then laughed when he argued from the verse of the trust offered to the heavens, the earth and the mountains {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. Damra reports him saying that whoever carries odd knowledge carries much evil, and that he told men returning from the raid that they had come back from the lesser struggle to the greater, the struggle of the heart {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}. Damra also said he had seen no one more eloquent {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}.

## His death
Damra said he died in 152 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص678}.
