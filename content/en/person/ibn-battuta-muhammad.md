## Overview
Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Muhammad al-Lawati al-Tanji, known as Ibn Battuta, a traveller from Tangier in the Maghrib. He visited many lands of the Islamic world and wrote his well-known travel account. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

He was born in 703 AH (1304 CE) and died in 776 AH (1374 CE), according to report. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## For Sprouts
A brave traveler who set out from Tangier, journeyed for many years, and visited far lands to meet people and record his travels.

## For Explorers
Muhammad ibn Battuta was a Moroccan traveler who began his journey from Tangier to perform the Hajj, traveling across the Islamic world and beyond for nearly thirty years. He visited Mecca, Egypt, Africa, Persia, India, the Maldives, and China, dictating his famous travelogue describing peoples, cultures, and traditions.

## For Scholars
Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Battuta (703–779 AH / 1304–1377 CE), the renowned Moroccan scholar and explorer acclaimed as the foremost traveler of the medieval world. Departing Tangier in 725 AH for the pilgrimage, his journeys spanned roughly thirty years and 73,000 miles across North and West Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. Dictated to the scribe Ibn Juzayy in his travel narrative 'The Rihla' (Tuhfat al-Nuzzar), his account remains an invaluable historical and anthropological record of 14th-century societies across Afro-Eurasia.

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Lawati of Tangier, Abu Abd Allah Ibn Battuta, quoting Ibn al-Khatib that he set out east in Rajab 725, travelled through Persian Iraq, India, Sind and China, and was made a judge by the king of India. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/480} He returned to the Maghrib, went to the lands of the Sudan, and the ruler of Fez ordered his journey written down, which Ibn Juzayy edited. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/480} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/481} Ibn Hajar records that some accused him of lying, that Ibn Marzuq cleared him, and Ibn Marzuq's statement that he lived until the year 770 and died while serving as a judge. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 3/481}

## In the Khayriyya edition of the Rihla
[[person/ibn-juzayy-muhammad|Ibn Juzayy]] names him in the preface of the Rihla as the shaykh, jurist and traveller Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Lawati al-Tanji, known as Ibn Battuta, and known in the eastern lands as Shams al-Din. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.14} Ibn Juzayy reports that Ibn Battuta told him in Granada that he was born in Tangier on Monday 17 Rajab 703 AH. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.15} He left Tangier on Thursday 2 Rajab 725 AH for the pilgrimage, alone, while his father was still alive, at the age of twenty-two. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.15}

In Delhi Sultan [[person/muhammad-ibn-tughluq|Muhammad Shah]] made him judge of the capital with a salary of twelve thousand dinars a year; he objected that he followed the school of Malik while the people there were Hanafis and that he did not know their language, so the sultan appointed two deputies for him to consult. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.412} Ibn Khaldun, in a passage of the *Muqaddima* quoted by the edition's corrector, says the sultan employed him as a judge of the Maliki school. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.313}

At the end of the Rihla the order of Sultan [[person/abu-inan-faris|Abu Inan]] reaches him at Takedda; he leaves in Sha'ban 754 AH, reaches Fez on the eve of Eid al-Adha, kisses the sultan's hand and settles under his protection. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.565} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.566} Ibn Juzayy calls him "the traveller of the age", who chose Fez as his home after twenty-five years of travel. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.566} See also [[question/credibility-of-ibn-battuta|the question of believing his reports]].
