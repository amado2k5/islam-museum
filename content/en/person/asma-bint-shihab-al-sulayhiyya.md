## Summary
Asma' bint Shihab al-Sulayhiyya was one of the famous women of Yemen, wife of [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-al-sulayhi|Ali ibn Muhammad al-Sulayhi]], king of Yemen, and mother of his son King Ahmad al-Mukarram; the Friday sermon on Yemen's pulpits named her along with her husband, as [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] reports after al-Zirikli's *al-A'lam*. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.59}

## Captivity in Zabid
She went on pilgrimage with her husband in 459 or 458 AH; he was killed at Umm al-Duhaym, and his killer, Sa'id ibn Najah the Abyssinian, known as al-Ahwal, took her captive, set her in her litter and placed before it the heads of her husband and of a brother of his killed with him. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.59} She was held for eight months or a full year in [[place/zabid|Zabid]], the two heads hanging before the window of her house, while her son al-Mukarram in [[place/sanaa|San'a]] did not know where she was; when he learned of it he came with an army, defeated the Abyssinians, freed her, took down the heads and built a shrine over them. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.59}

## Her household
She returned with her son to San'a and died there. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.59} [[person/arwa-al-sulayhi|Arwa bint Ahmad al-Sulayhiyya]], who married al-Mukarram and later became queen of Yemen, grew up in her care. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31}

## For young visitors
Asma' was a queen in Yemen beside her husband, and her name was mentioned in the Friday sermon. After her husband was killed she was taken prisoner, until her son came with an army and rescued her.
