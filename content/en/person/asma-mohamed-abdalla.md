## Summary

Asma Mohamed Abdalla is a Sudanese diplomat who became her country's first female Minister of Foreign Affairs in September 2019, in the transitional cabinet of Prime Minister Abdalla Hamdok formed after the fall of Omar al-Bashir. Her appointment closed an arc of nearly thirty years: one of the first women to serve in Sudan's diplomatic corps, she had been dismissed by the very regime whose overthrow brought her back to head the ministry. {cite:wikipedia-asma-mohamed-abdalla} {cite:muslim500-asma-mohamed-abdalla}

## Early life and education

Born in Khartoum in 1946, she studied economics and political science at the University of Khartoum, graduating at the turn of the 1970s — the English-language record gives 1969, the Arabic reporting on her career gives 1971. Part of her education was undertaken in the United States. {cite:wikipedia-asma-mohamed-abdalla} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 159}

## The diplomatic career and the dismissal

On graduating she joined the Ministry of Foreign Affairs as one of the first three women ever employed by it, and rose to the rank of minister plenipotentiary and deputy head of the Americas department. She represented Sudan in African and European postings, including missions in Norway and Morocco and at the United Nations. {cite:wikipedia-asma-mohamed-abdalla}

After Omar al-Bashir's 1989 coup she was dismissed from the diplomatic service under the 'dismissal in the public interest' law with which the new government removed unaligned officials from the sovereign ministries; the sources differ on the exact year — BBC Arabic dates it to 1990, other reporting to 1991. Persecuted by the new authorities, she left Sudan and settled in Morocco with her husband, the painter Hassan Mohamed Osman. {cite:wikipedia-asma-mohamed-abdalla} {cite:muslim500-asma-mohamed-abdalla}

## The exile years

In exile she worked as a consultant to African and international organisations, among them the Islamic Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization ⁦(ISESCO/ICESCO)⁩ and UNICEF, and did work for the Arab League. UNICEF's scanned 1990 records list her as fourth vice-chairman of the UNICEF Executive Board for 1990/1991, though a later UNICEF compilation disputes the listing — a discrepancy the sources themselves record. In the later Bashir years she returned to Sudan and, with other dismissed diplomats, founded a translation bureau in 2009. {cite:wikipedia-asma-mohamed-abdalla}

## The revolution and the ministry

When the uprising against al-Bashir began in December 2018 she joined the Forces of Freedom and Change, the civilian coalition of the revolution, and served on its foreign-affairs working team, carrying the movement's demands to international forums and supporting bodies. She was not among the publicly discussed candidates for the foreign ministry, but Prime Minister Abdalla Hamdok put her name forward, citing her opposition record and her diplomatic experience. {cite:wikipedia-asma-mohamed-abdalla}

Sworn in on 1 September 2019 before the joint civilian-military Sovereign Council, she became the first woman to hold Sudan's foreign-affairs portfolio — one of four women in an eighteen-member transitional cabinet, in a country whose revolution had been notable for women's participation. She served until 9 July 2020, when she was succeeded by an acting minister in the cabinet reshuffle. {cite:wikipedia-asma-mohamed-abdalla} {cite:muslim500-asma-mohamed-abdalla}

## Assessment

Abdalla's story is frequently told as an emblem of the Sudanese transition: a professional diplomat purged for independence, three decades of exile and quiet work, then a return to head the institution that had removed her. The Muslim 500 lists her in its Political entries for that trajectory and for the precedent she set in her country's public life. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 159}
