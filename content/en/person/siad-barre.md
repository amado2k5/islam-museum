# Siad Barre

Mohamed Siad Barre (c. 1919 – 2 January 1995) was a Somali military officer and politician who seized power in the coup of 21 October 1969 and ruled Somalia until his overthrow on 26 January 1991.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-fr}

## Rise to power

Barre was born to pastoral parents of the Darod clan — his father Marehan, his mother Ogaden — at a time when Somalia kept no birth records; his year is speculated between 1909 and 1921, with c. 1919 conventional, and his unofficial birthplace Shilabo in the Ogaden against an official one at Garbaharey.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en} Orphaned at about ten, he joined the colonial police, studied at a military academy in Italy, and by independence in 1960 was vice-commander of the Somali army, becoming its commander in 1965.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en} Six days after President Shermarke's assassination, on 21 October 1969, Barre and the army overthrew the parliamentary government in a bloodless coup and installed a Supreme Revolutionary Council.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en}

## Presidency

Barre's regime adopted scientific socialism and, through the Somali Revolutionary Socialist Party founded in 1976, attempted to reconcile the official ideology with Islam, the official state religion, presenting Muslim principles of social progress, equality and justice as the ideology's own.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en} In January 1975 he ordered the execution of ten sheikhs arrested for opposing the new family law on women's inheritance.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en} His government ran mass literacy campaigns, joined the Arab League in 1974, fought the Ogaden War against Ethiopia (1977–1978), and from the late 1970s met shrinking popularity with systematic repression of the Majeerteen, Hawiye and Isaaq clans, including the killing of tens of thousands of Isaaq.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en} Weakened after the Cold War, the regime fell in January 1991; Barre fled Mogadishu on 26 January, failed to retake the capital, and went into exile, dying of a heart attack in Lagos, Nigeria, on 2 January 1995.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en}

## A note on the record

His birth data are unsettled — no records existed, the year is speculated across 1909–1921, and Wikidata additionally carries a second death value (2 February 1995) that the articles and the January 1995 obituaries contradict; the record follows c. 1919 and 2 January 1995 and keeps the variants in its notes.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-en}{cite:source/wikidata-siad-barre}

On faith, no text names a school for Barre personally: the French infobox gives his religion as Islam, Wikidata's single value is Islam, and the Urdu stub files him among Somali Muslims and Islamic socialists.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-fr}{cite:source/wikidata-siad-barre}{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-ur} What the texts describe is a state's programme — an official ideology reconciled with Islam as state religion, and a ruler executing clerics who resisted his family law — which is the record of a government, not the man's attestation. His faith is therefore entered as unknown.

No successor relation is written for him: an acting president, Sheikh Mukhtar Mohamed Hussein, held office for the six days between Shermarke's assassination and the coup, as the French infobox itself lists, and Barre in any case took power by coup rather than succession.{cite:source/wikipedia-siad-barre-fr}
