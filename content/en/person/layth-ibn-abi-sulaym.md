## Overview
Layth ibn Abi Sulaym, a client of Anbasa ibn Abi Sufyan, from Kufa. Al-Mas'udi says that he died in 158 AH in the days of al-Mansur. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.293}

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to al-Layth ibn Abi Sulaym of Kufa, al-Laythi, calling him "one of the scholars"; his symbol "[عو، م مقروناً]" means the authors of the four Sunan cite him and Muslim cites him paired with another. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 420} [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]], [[person/ibn-ulayyah|Ibn Ulayya]], Abu Mu'awiya and many others narrated from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421}

**The critics' sayings.** [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said: confused in hadith, but people narrated from him; Yahya and al-Nasa'i said: weak; [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]] also said: no harm in him, and: Layth is weaker than [[person/ata-ibn-saib|Ata ibn al-Sa'ib]]. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 420} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421} [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]] said: he became confused at the end of his life; [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] said: he was a man of the Sunna; what was held against him was only that he combined Ata, Tawus and Mujahid in one chain. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 420} Abd al-Warith said: he was one of the vessels of knowledge; Ibn Idris said: I never sat with Layth without hearing from him something I had not heard. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 420} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421} Abd Allah ibn Ahmad reported his father saying he had never seen [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya ibn Sa'id]] think worse of anyone than of Layth, [[person/ibn-ishaq|Muhammad ibn Ishaq]] and Hammam, and that no one could argue with him about them. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421} Isa ibn Yunus said he saw him after he had become confused, and sometimes passed him in mid-morning while he was on the minaret giving the call to prayer. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421}

**His worship and the first Shi'a of Kufa.** Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash said: Layth was among those who prayed and fasted most, and when he came upon something he did not turn it away. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 420} Ibn Shawdhab reported Layth saying: I knew the first Shi'a in Kufa, and they placed no one above Abu Bakr and Umar. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421}

**Some of his objectionable reports.** Al-Dhahabi cites from his hadith the mention of a sacrificial camel in the expiation for intercourse during Ramadan, which he calls objectionable, and the hadith "whoever says 'I am learned' is ignorant", which al-Tabarani said is narrated only with this chain. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 422}

**His death.** Al-Dhahabi writes: "It is said that Layth died in the year 143." {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 423} This differs from al-Mas'udi, who gives 158; both dates are kept. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.293}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Ahmad, Yahya ibn Ma'in, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Hibban and Yahya al-Qattan**: confused in hadith and weak; he became confused at the end of his life. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 420} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 421}
- **Sunni: al-Daraqutni, Abd al-Warith, and Ibn Ma'in in another saying**: a man of the Sunna and a vessel of knowledge, with no harm in him; only his combining of teachers was held against him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 420}
