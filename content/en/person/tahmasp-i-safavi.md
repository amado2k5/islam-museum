## Summary
Tahmasp I was the second shah of the Safavid state ([[polity/safavid-iran|Safavid Iran]]). The museum's record gives his years as 1514 to 1576, his birth at [[place/shahabad-isfahan|Shahabad]] near Isfahan, his death at [[place/qazvin|Qazvin]] and his burial at the [[place/imam-reza-shrine|shrine of Imam Reza]], all from open reference data {cite:wikidata}.
## In the Ottoman history
Salabi's history of the Ottoman state names him Shah Tahmasp, king of the 'Ajam, and calls the Safavids the Twelver Shi'a Safavids. After the naval battle of Lepanto, he writes, the pope tried to use the rivalries and differences of creed between the Safavids and the Ottomans, and wrote to Shah Tahmasp that he would never find a better chance to attack the Ottomans {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 282}. Later, the same author places an Ottoman campaign in 985 AH / 1577 CE after disorder in Persia following Tahmasp's death; it crossed the Caucasus and took Tiflis and Georgia, and the Ottomans entered Tabriz in 993 AH / 1585 CE {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 293}. These are the wording and view of one Ottoman-history author; the museum reports them as his.
