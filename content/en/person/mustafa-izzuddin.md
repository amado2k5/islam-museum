## Summary

Dr Mustafa Izzuddin is a Singaporean academic and voluntary community leader whose public work sits at the meeting of scholarship and interfaith service: an ASEAN Fulbright Fellow and a civilizational-dialogue fellow of ICESCO ⁦(the Islamic World Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization)⁩, he is listed in The Muslim 500's Social Issues entries for his leadership in interfaith dialogue and educational philanthropy. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 190}

This record rests on the single listed source and says so; what follows sets the documented facts in their Singaporean context without adding unattested detail.

## Context: Muslim civil society in Singapore

Singapore's Muslims — roughly a sixth of the city-state's population, mostly of Malay descent — live inside one of the world's most administratively structured multiracial societies. Muslim institutions there run from the statutory Islamic Religious Council ⁦(MUIS)⁩ and the registry-administered mosque network to a dense layer of voluntary welfare and educational associations. In such a setting, the boundary between academic work and community service is porous: scholars routinely sit on mosque boards, interfaith committees and educational endowments, and it is in exactly that space that Izzuddin's profile sits. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 190}

## Academic and interfaith work

His Muslim 500 entry describes him as an academic and a voluntary leader in interfaith dialogue and educational philanthropy. Two fellowships mark that profile: the ASEAN Fulbright fellowship, which supports scholars working on Southeast Asian regional questions, and a fellowship of ICESCO's civilizational-dialogue programme, the Rabat-based Islamic cultural organisation's track for dialogue between the Islamic world and other civilizations. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 190}

## Assessment

The Singaporean model of Muslim public life — professional, interfaith by default, and wired into both the state's structures and wider Asian networks — is the frame through which the entry reads. The museum records what is attested: a scholar whose influence runs through fellowships, voluntary leadership and the quiet circuit of dialogue work rather than through mass following. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 190}
