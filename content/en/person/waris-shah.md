## Summary

Waris Shah (1722–1798) was a Punjabi Sufi poet, author of the best-known rendition of the folk romance Heer Ranjha, which he completed in 1766 {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-en}.

## Life

Syed Mohammad Waris Shah was born at Jandiala Sher Khan into a Sayyid family; his father was Gulsher Shah and his mother Kamal Banu, and he probably lost both parents young {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-ur}. He was educated at Kasur under Ghulam Murtaza, alongside Bulleh Shah, and in his youth witnessed Ahmad Shah Abdali's 1748 battle with the governor of Lahore {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-ur}. After completing his education he went to Pakpattan and settled at nearby Malka Hans, in a cell adjoining the mosque whose call to prayer he led and whose children he taught; he never married {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-en}. The Urdu article dates his move to Malka Hans to 1758 and gives him forty years there until his death {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-ur}.

## Heer Ranjha

Waris Shah completed the Heer in 1766, joining the folk tale of doomed love to the stations of Sufi experience; it became Punjab's most famous romance, recited, sung and taught, its couplets current as proverbs {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-pa}. He left a second collection as well, but it is the Heer that made his name {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-ar}.

## Faith

His place in a Sufi order is attested for the man himself in two independent publications — the English article's lead names him a Punjabi Muslim Sufi poet of the Chishti order {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-en}, and the Urdu article records the tradition that, where Bulleh Shah took bay'a from Shah Inayat Qadiri, Waris Shah took bay'a in the line of Khwaja Fariduddin Ganjshakar, the Chishti line of Pakpattan, beside which he afterwards lived at Malka Hans {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-ur}. He is therefore recorded as sufi, documented, with his edge to the Chishtiyya. His Sayyid descent is family background and was not counted.

## Notes for researchers

His years are contested — the English article and Wikidata give 1722 to 1798 or 1799, while the Punjabi article lists scholarly birth estimates from 1704 to 1738, notes that many scholars hold to 1722, and quotes a shrine stone inscription giving 1722–1798 {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-pa} {cite:wikidata-waris-shah}. The Urdu article gives his birth on 5 Rabi al-thani 1130 AH, paired with 1718 CE, and his death on 24 June 1798 aged 76 — an age that fits 1722 — while warning that neither death year is certain and preserving Afzal Haq's tradition of 10 Muharram 1220 AH {cite:wikipedia-waris-shah-ur}. Wikidata's day precision for the death agrees with the Urdu article; its day precision for the birth, 23 January, has no article-text support {cite:wikidata-waris-shah}. Neither Jandiala Sher Khan nor Malka Hans has a museum place record yet, so he carries no place edges.
