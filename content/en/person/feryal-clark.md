## Summary
Feryal Demirci Clark is a British Labour Party politician, born in Malatya, Turkey, on 6 January 1979. She has been Member of Parliament for Enfield North since 2019. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en} {cite:wikidata-feryal-clark}

She served as Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for AI and Digital Government from July 2024 to September 2025. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

## Early life and education
Feryal Demirci was born in Malatya to Kurdish Alevi parents with roots in Kepez, Malatya Province, and moved to London at the age of eight. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

She studied biomedical science and then took a master's degree in bioinformatics at the University of Exeter, and worked in NHS pathology for six years. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

## Local government
She was elected in 2006 as a councillor for Brownswood ward in the London Borough of Hackney, represented Hoxton East and Shoreditch ward from 2014, and rose to become deputy mayor of Hackney and cabinet member for health, social care, leisure and parks. As a councillor she promoted cycling in the borough. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

She was seventh on Labour's London-wide list for the 2016 London Assembly election but was not elected. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

## Parliamentary career
At the 2019 general election she was elected MP for Enfield North with 51.8 per cent of the vote and a majority of 6,492. She became Labour's first ever Kurdish MP, and made her maiden speech in a Commons debate on health and social care. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

She was appointed Shadow Minister for Primary Care and Patient Safety in November 2021, then Shadow Minister for Health in September 2023. She was re-elected in 2024 with an increased majority of 12,736, and after the election was appointed Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for AI and Digital Government. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

## Positions and criticism

In January 2022 Clark called on the UK government to recognise the Armenian genocide, saying she was born in south-east Turkey and grew up hearing stories about the horrors faced by the Armenian people in that region. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

Clark endorsed Yvette Cooper in the 2015 Labour leadership election and Lisa Nandy in 2020, and is a supporter of Labour Friends of Israel, according to the article. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}

## Reach
Her public significance, as the article presents it, lies in being Labour's first Kurdish MP, and in her path from NHS laboratory work and Hackney local government to a ministerial office for digital government and artificial intelligence. {cite:wikipedia-feryal-clark-en}
