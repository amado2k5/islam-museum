## Summary
Ubayd ibn Umayr al-Laythi was one of the Successors; [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] gives him a chapter titled "The renunciation of Ubayd ibn Umayr" in [[work/kitab-al-zuhd-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Kitab al-Zuhd]], gathering his admonitions, transmitted from him by Mujahid, Abu Sufyan, Habib ibn Abi Thabit and others. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 458}

## His admonitions
- Prefer shame before God to shame before people. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 458}
- When God wills good for someone, He gives him understanding of religion and inspires him with right guidance. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 458}
- As Mujahid reports him: the most diligent today are like the idle of the past. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 458}
- God continues to have care for the servant as long as the servant has need of Him. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 458}
- When winter came he would say: People of the Qur'an, the night has grown long for your prayer and the day short for your fasting; if the night is too much for you to endure, if you fear to fight the enemy and are too miserly to spend, then remember God much; in one version, glorifying God is "weightier with God than two mountains of gold and silver". {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 458}
- It is reported from him that God detests the Qur'an reciter who is forever dressing up, riding about, coming in and going out. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 459}

## For young visitors
Ubayd ibn Umayr was a preacher who reminded people in winter that the long night is a chance to pray and the short day a chance to fast.

## Notes for researchers
- The scan once gives his name as "Ubayd Allah ibn Umayr"; the chapter title and the other reports read "Ubayd ibn Umayr".
