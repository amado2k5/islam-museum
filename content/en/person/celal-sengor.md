Celâl Şengör is a Turkish geologist, born on 24 March 1955 at Istanbul to a muhacir family from Rumelia; his full name is Ali Mehmet Celâl Şengör{cite:wikipedia-sengor-en}{cite:wikipedia-sengor-ar}. He graduated from Robert College and took his BS (1978), MS (1979) and PhD (1982) at the State University of New York at Albany, then became a professor in the Department of Geological Engineering at Istanbul Technical University, from which he is retired{cite:wikipedia-sengor-en}. He is a foreign member of the American Philosophical Society, the United States National Academy of Sciences, Academia Europaea, the Russian Academy of Sciences and many other academies, and a recipient of the Gustav Steinmann Medal{cite:wikipedia-sengor-en}.

Şengör writes a weekly popular-science column in the daily Cumhuriyet and is known for his outspoken Kemalist politics and public controversies{cite:wikipedia-sengor-en}{cite:wikipedia-sengor-tr}. On religion, his Turkish biography records that he has many times declared on the programmes he took part in that he is an atheist{cite:wikipedia-sengor-tr}. On 23 May 2022, on Habertürk's Teke Tek Bilim, he said that the prophets and events narrated in the Abrahamic scriptures have no historical documents behind them; the Presidency of Religious Affairs complained, the Istanbul prosecutor summoned him to testify, and the Science Academy of Turkey issued a statement on 5 September 2022 defending academic freedom in the matter{cite:bilimakademisi-sengor-statement-2022}.
