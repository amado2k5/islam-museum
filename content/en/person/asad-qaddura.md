As'ad Qaddura (1880–1959) was a Palestinian religious scholar from Safed who studied first at Damascus and then at al-Azhar under Muhammad Abduh, returning home with the title of an Azhar-trained scholar.{cite:wikipedia-asad-qaddura-ar}{cite:wikidata-asad-qaddura} He founded a national school in Safed's Red Mosque devoted to teaching Arabic, and held a succession of posts in the Ottoman sharia judiciary, receiving several decorations.{cite:wikipedia-asad-qaddura-ar}

Qaddura was mufti of Safed during the First World War, and under the British Mandate the Supreme Muslim Council appointed him sharia judge for Nazareth, Acre and Safed.{cite:wikipedia-asad-qaddura-ar} He chaired Safed's Arab committee in 1921, sat on the executive committees of the fourth, fifth and sixth Palestine Arab Congresses, and was among the signatories of the 1935 fatwa forbidding the sale of land to Jews.{cite:wikipedia-asad-qaddura-ar} After the Nakba of 1948 he fled to Damascus, where he died in 1959.{cite:wikipedia-asad-qaddura-ar}
