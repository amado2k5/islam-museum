# Alymbek Datka

Alymbek Datka (1799–1862), born Alymbek Asan biy uulu, was a Kyrgyz statesman of the Khanate of Kokand and the husband of Kurmanjan Datka, who succeeded him in the Alai {cite:wikipedia-alymbek-datka-ru}.

## Rise in Kokand

Born into the Bargy tribe in the Alai Valley, the son of the wealthy and influential Asan-biy, Alymbek was invited to Kokand by Madali Khan at twenty-seven and in 1831 received the title Datka, "the just ruler", from the same khan {cite:wikipedia-alymbek-datka-ru}. He was made ruler of the Andijan vilayet, moved his seat to Osh, and rose to become vizier of the khanate {cite:wikipedia-alymbek-datka-uz}. In 1832 he married Kurmanjan, then a young woman of the Osh region {cite:wikipedia-alymbek-datka-ru}.

## Revolts and last years

Alymbek led the Osh uprising of 1844 against Musulmankul, supported the Kyrgyz-backed claimant Khoja-Tora in Kashgar against the khan's own policy, and in 1858 helped raise Malla Khan to the Kokand throne at the battle of Samanchi, after which he again governed Andijan {cite:wikipedia-alymbek-datka-ru}. Accused before the khan of refusing battle with the Russians after Uzun-Agach, he plotted in turn; on 24 February 1862 he took part in the conspiracy that killed Malla Khan, briefly served as atalyk to the boy-khan Shah Murad, and was himself murdered by palace conspirators at Kokand that year {cite:wikipedia-alymbek-datka-ky} {cite:wikipedia-alymbek-datka-ru}. His widow Kurmanjan was recognised by the khans of Bukhara and Kokand as ruler of the Alai in his place.
