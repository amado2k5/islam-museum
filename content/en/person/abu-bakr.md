## Overview
[[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]], Abd Allah ibn Uthman ibn Amir, called "Atiq" and "al-Siddiq", the first of the Prophet's caliphs. He accompanied him in the emigration and succeeded him in leading the people in prayer. His full biography in "Usd al-Ghaba" runs from page 701 to page 713. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.701-713}

## His two titles
It is disputed why he was called "Atiq". It is said for the beauty of his face; it is said that there was nothing in his lineage that could be faulted; it is said that the Prophet told him: "You are God's freed man from the Fire." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.701} He was called "al-Siddiq" because he affirmed the Prophet when he told him of the Night Journey, and he said: "I affirm him in what is even more distant than that." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.701}

## His acceptance of Islam
Ibn al-Athir relates from Abu Bakr himself that he was in Yemen before the mission, and heard someone say that a prophet was awaited; then he met Waraqa ibn Nawfal and told him, and Waraqa said that this prophet would be of the middle of the Arabs in lineage; when the Prophet was sent, he believed and affirmed him. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.702} Ibn Abbas relates that Abu Bakr was the first man to accept Islam, as he cites from the verse of Hassan. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.703} Abu Bakr himself said: "I saw myself, and I was the fourth of Islam." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.703}

## The emigration
Anas relates that when the Prophet went out emigrating to Medina, Abu Bakr was with him, and he knew the road better; if anyone asked him "who is this with you?" he said: "This guides me to the way." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.704} Al-Bara' relates the story of Abu Bakr buying a saddle, and his asking al-Bara' to carry it, and al-Bara' insisted that he tell him about the departure. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.704}

## His standing with the Prophet
The Prophet said to Abu Bakr, according to Ibn Umar: "You are my brother and my companion in the cave." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.705} Ali relates that the Prophet said to the two of them on the day of Badr: "With one of you is Gabriel, and with the other Michael and Israfil." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.705} This is among the reports circulated in the books; its chain has not been checked here.

## His defence of the Prophet in Mecca
Ibn al-Athir relates that one of the polytheists choked the Prophet until he nearly killed him, and Abu Bakr came, seized his shoulder and pushed him away, and said: "Will you kill a man because he says: my Lord is God?" {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.706}

## The ten promised Paradise
Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf relates that the Prophet said: "Abu Bakr is in Paradise, and Umar is in Paradise..." and he counted ten. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.706}

## His merit after the Prophet
Ibn Umar relates that the people used to say that the best of the community was the Prophet, then Abu Bakr, then Umar. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.707} Al-Sha'bi relates from Ibn Abbas that he was the first to accept Islam. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.703} Abu Salih relates that Umar beat the man who preferred anyone to Abu Bakr, then preached: "Lo, the best of this community after its prophet is Abu Bakr." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.708}

## His caliphate
The latest report Ibn al-Athir gives from al-Hasan al-Basri is that the Prophet appointed Abu Bakr as successor, and that there is no doubt of that. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.711} Ibn Mas'ud relates that the Prophet said: "Follow those after me: Abu Bakr and Umar." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.711} It is related that Umar used to look after a blind old woman on the outskirts of Medina, and found that Abu Bakr had got to her before him, while he was then caliph, and said: "By my life, you are the one." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.710}

## The death of the Prophet
When the Prophet died Umar said that no one would say the Prophet had died without him striking him with his sword. They went to Abu Bakr and he came, uncovered the Prophet and confirmed his death, and recited: "Indeed you will die, and they will die." {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.712} People asked him about the prayer over him and his burial, and he answered them. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.712}

## His death
Ibn Ishaq said he died on Friday, seven nights before the end of Jumada al-Akhira 13 AH, and Umar ibn al-Khattab prayed over him. Others said he died on the evening of Monday, and it is said on the night of Tuesday, and it is said on the evening of Tuesday, eight nights before the end of Jumada al-Akhira. {cite:usd-al-ghaba p.713}

## For young visitors
Abu Bakr al-Siddiq was the first caliph of the Prophet. He accompanied him in the emigration and in the cave. He affirmed the Prophet when he told him of the Night Journey, so he was called al-Siddiq. He used to visit a blind old woman at night on the outskirts of Medina to see to her needs. When the Prophet died he uncovered his face, confirmed his death, and recited a verse reminding people that every soul will taste death.

## Notes for researchers
- All of the above is from Ibn al-Athir's "Usd al-Ghaba", which gathers the reports with their chains and notes the differences among them. The reports have not all been checked against the Sahihs and the Sunan.
- The reports that the Prophet designated Abu Bakr for the caliphate are the subject of well-known disagreement between the Sunnis and others; here we give the view of al-Hasan al-Basri as Ibn al-Athir reports it, and we do not weigh between the views.
- The duration of the caliphate and the day of death are not fixed here by a particular day; the reports differ in the same text, and a second source is needed before naming a day.
- The Quranic link: Abu Bakr recited the verse at death, namely [[passage/quran-39-30|Surat al-Zumar, 30]].

## His charge to Rafi' al-Ta'i
On [[event/expedition-of-dhat-al-salasil|the expedition of Dhat al-Salasil]] [[person/rafi-ibn-umayra|Rafi' ibn Abi Rafi' al-Ta'i]] accompanied him to learn from him, and he charged him with the oneness of God, the prayer, the alms, fasting in Ramadan, the pilgrimage and the washing from major impurity, and never to be in command over two men of the Muslims; he gave as the reason that the Muslims are God's protected ones and His neighbours and under His covenant, so God's protection should not be betrayed among them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/273} Rafi' mentions his Fadakiyya cloak which he used to fasten with a skewer, and that the people of Najd said when they apostatised: "We give our allegiance to the one with the cloak." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/273} When he became caliph Rafi' reminded him of his prohibition, and he said: "I find no alternative; I feared division for the community of Muhammad." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/274}

## In the secret call (Sealed Nectar, pp. 75–76)
The book counts him among the four who accepted Islam on the first day of the call, the intimate friend of the Prophet. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 1/50} He then became active in calling to Islam; he was a sociable, beloved and easy man of character and kindness, whose people came to him and befriended him for his knowledge, his trade and his good company. He began to call those he trusted among those who came to him and sat with him, and by his call [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]], [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]], [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd]] and [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha]] accepted Islam ([[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|the secret call]]). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}

## In the emigration to Abyssinia and the harm at Mecca (Sealed Nectar, pp. 117, 120)
When the harm grew after the death of Abu Talib, he sought to emigrate from Mecca, until he reached Barak al-Ghimad, and [[person/ibn-al-dughunna|Ibn al-Dughunna]] brought him back under his protection. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.117} One day while he was circling the House, [[person/utba-ibn-rabia|Utba ibn Rabi'a]] struck him very hard with two sandals until the Banu Taym carried him away in a garment, thinking he was dead. When he came to himself he asked about the Prophet, and his mother and Umm Jamil carried him to the house of al-Arqam. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.120}

## His title al-Siddiq (Sealed Nectar, p. 140)
The book says that he was called al-Siddiq for affirming the Night Journey when people denied it ([[event/isra-and-miraj|the Night Journey and the Ascension]]). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.140}

## Preparation for the emigration (Sealed Nectar, p. 157)
He prepared for the emigration; the Prophet said to him: "Wait a little," and he held himself back to accompany the Prophet, and fed two riding camels with the leaves of the sumac for four months ([[event/emigration-of-the-companions|the emigration of the Muslims]]). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.157} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/553}

## In the emigration (Sealed Nectar, pp. 161–166)
He went out with the Prophet from a small door in his house to the cave of Thawr. He entered it before him to clean it, and blocked a hole in it with his garment; he was stung in the foot, and did not move, lest he wake the Prophet ([[event/hijra-night-escape|the Prophet's departure from his house and hiding in the cave]]). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.164}

## For Sprouts
Abu Bakr al-Siddiq was the loyal companion of the Prophet, the first caliph, famous for truthfulness, kindness, and generosity.

## For Explorers
Abu Bakr al-Siddiq was the devoted companion of the Prophet and his partner on the migration to Medina, being the first adult free male to accept Islam. Known as al-Siddiq for his unswerving truthfulness, he spent his wealth freeing enslaved believers, and was chosen as the first caliph to preserve the unity of the community.

## For Scholars
Abdullah ibn Abi Quhafa, famed as Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (c. 51 BH – 13 AH / 573–634 CE), the Prophet's closest companion, his partner in the cave during the Hijra, and the first Rightly Guided Caliph (11–13 AH). The earliest adult male convert, he used his wealth in Mecca to ransom persecuted slaves like Bilal ibn Rabah. Elected at the Saqifa following the Prophet's passing, his leadership safeguarded the young commonwealth, initiated the first centralized compilation of the Qur'an, and dispatched expeditions to Syria and Iraq, embodying unshakeable faith and serene resolve.
