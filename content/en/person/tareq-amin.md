## Summary
Tareq Amin is a Jordanian-American technologist and telecommunications executive whose career links three regions: the American internet industry, Japan's mobile revolution and Saudi Arabia's artificial-intelligence build-out. The Muslim 500 places him in Science & Technology, crediting him with helping lead India's 4G rollout and the deployment of Open RAN, a new open architecture for mobile networks. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}

## Career in telecommunications
Amin built his reputation inside the carrier industry, working across hardware, software and network operations. He rose to prominence as a senior executive at Reliance Jio in India, where the group built a 4G network that brought hundreds of millions online in a few years, before joining Rakuten in Japan to build its mobile business from scratch. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}

## Rakuten Mobile and Open RAN
At Rakuten Mobile, where he served as chief technology officer and later chief executive of the mobile unit, Amin led the launch of what was widely described as the world's first fully virtualised, cloud-native mobile network built on Open RAN principles — an architecture that breaks the mobile network into interoperable components from many vendors rather than a single integrated supplier. The approach drew global attention as a possible template for lowering the cost of networks and loosening vendor lock-in. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}

## HUMAIN and the AI turn
In 2025 Amin moved to Riyadh to lead HUMAIN, the artificial-intelligence company established under Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund to develop Arabic-language AI models, data centres and AI services for the kingdom's diversification programme — a turn from networks to the intelligence that runs on them. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}

## Recognition
The Muslim 500 counts him among the influential Muslims in science and technology, citing his role in two of the largest network build-outs of the past decade and his current position at the front of a national AI programme. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}

## Significance
For the museum's record, Amin belongs to the generation of diaspora engineers whose work is measured in infrastructure: networks built, not words written. His trajectory — Jordanian-American formation, Indian mass-market scale, Japanese engineering ambition, Saudi state-scale AI — maps the geography of modern Muslim technologists. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}

## The Open RAN argument
Amin's name became attached to a technical argument with geopolitical stakes: whether mobile networks must be bought as sealed systems from a handful of vendors or can be assembled, like the internet itself, from interoperable parts. Rakuten's bet — the first nationwide greenfield network built this way — was watched by every operator and government considering whether Open RAN could break vendor lock-in. Whatever the verdicts on individual vendors, the demonstration that a cloud-native carrier could run at national scale is the engineering fact his tenure put on the record — the reason a Jordanian telecom executive appears in the Muslim 500's technology pages. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}

## The executive as technologist
Amin's public profile is unusual for a telecom executive: he speaks the language of cloud architecture, open interfaces and network virtualisation rather than the language of tariffs and market share. That technical fluency is what the Rakuten appointment traded on — and what made him, for a moment, the most watched operator in the industry. The museum's record notes the profile without adjudicating the debates around Open RAN's promises; the listing records the technologist who brought a new vocabulary to an old industry. {cite:muslim500-tareq-amin}
