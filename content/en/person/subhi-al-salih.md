## Summary
Lebanese scholar of hadith and Qur'anic sciences (1926–1986), a leading figure of modern Islamic studies. He taught at the Lebanese University and supervised generations of researchers; his books include Ulum al-Hadith — now the most widely taught university manual in its field — Mabahith fi Ulum al-Qur'an and Al-Nuzum al-Islamiyya. {cite:wikipedia-subhi-al-salih}

## Life and career

Subhi al-Salih was born in Beirut in 1926, studied the Islamic sciences and took a doctorate in arts at Cairo University, then built his university teaching career in Lebanon until he stood among the pillars of the Faculty of Arts at the Lebanese University. {cite:wikipedia-subhi-al-salih}

Alongside teaching he took part in Lebanese and Arab intellectual life and held cultural posts; he was assassinated in Beirut in 1986 during the Lebanese civil war. {cite:wikipedia-subhi-al-salih}

## Works

His most famous book is Ulum al-Hadith wa-Mustalahuhu (Hadith Sciences and Terminology: Exposition and Study), which arranged the subjects of hadith terminology in a clear teaching order with applied examples and became the adopted textbook in dozens of university Islamic-studies departments. {cite:wikipedia-subhi-al-salih}

Other works include Mabahith fi Ulum al-Qur'an, Al-Nuzum al-Islamiyya (Islamic Systems: Their Origin and Development), lessons in Islamic jurisprudence, and editions and studies in hadith and sira. {cite:wikipedia-subhi-al-salih}

## Legacy

Al-Salih is credited with making the hadith sciences accessible to university students without loss of scholarly precision, joining the authenticity of classical terminology to the method of modern academic exposition; some of his books were translated into other Islamic languages. {cite:wikipedia-subhi-al-salih}

His books are still printed and taught decades after his death, and in Lebanon he is remembered as the model scholar who served the tradition with the tools of the modern university. {cite:wikipedia-subhi-al-salih}
