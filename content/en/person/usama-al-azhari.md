## Summary
Usama al-Sayyid al-Azhari is an Egyptian Islamic scholar and, since July 2024, Egypt's minister of endowments (awqaf), the office responsible for mosques, preachers and religious property across the country. He is identified with the Al-Azhar establishment's response to extremist discourse and with Sunni Sufism. {cite:wikipedia-usama-al-azhari}

## Life and formation
He was born on 16 July 1976 in Alexandria, where he memorised the Qur'an in childhood and studied in the Al-Azhar system, taking his doctorate in hadith. Among the scholars he studied under was Ali Gomaa, later grand mufti of Egypt, whose circle shaped al-Azhari's orientation towards classical Ash'ari theology and Sufi practice. {cite:wikipedia-usama-al-azhari}

## Career
Al-Azhari became known publicly through lectures and television appearances arguing against extremist readings of scripture and defending Sufi traditions within Sunni Islam; his writings include works refuting the textual claims of violent movements and a study on the meaning of Sufism (Haqiqat al-Tasawwuf). He served as a religious adviser to the presidency and as an imam and preacher at major mosques before President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi named him minister of endowments in the cabinet reshuffle of July 2024. As minister he has overseen the unification of mosque sermons and the regulation of preaching permits — measures supporters describe as discipline and critics as state control; both readings are recorded attributed. {cite:wikipedia-usama-al-azhari}

## Standing
He appears in The Muslim 500 among the scholars and administrators shaping religious affairs in the Arab world, reflecting the reach of the Egyptian awqaf ministry and his own media presence in Arabic-speaking audiences. {cite:wikipedia-usama-al-azhari}
