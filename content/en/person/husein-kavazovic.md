## Summary
Husein ef. Kavazović is the reis-ul-ulema — grand mufti — of the Islamic Community of Bosnia and Herzegovina, elected in November 2012, the religious head of the Bosniak Muslims and one of the senior muftis of the Balkans. {cite:wikipedia-husein-kavazovic} {cite:muslim500-husein-kavazovic}

## Life and formation
He was born on 3 July 1964 in Jajce, in central Bosnia. He studied at the Gazi Husrev-bey madrasa in Sarajevo and then at al-Azhar University in Cairo, graduating in Islamic studies before Yugoslavia's collapse plunged Bosnia into war — a war in which his family and his generation of imams shared the fate of the besieged population. {cite:wikipedia-husein-kavazovic}

## Career
He served as imam in the Tuzla region and was elected mufti of Tuzla in the 1990s before the Islamic Community's assembly elected him reis-ul-ulema in 2012, succeeding Mustafa Cerić. His tenure has navigated the community's postwar reconstruction — mosques, madrasas, the restored Gazi Husrev-bey library — and its place between Sarajevo's politics, Belgrade's rivalries and the global Islamic organisations. {cite:wikipedia-husein-kavazovic} {cite:muslim500-husein-kavazovic}

## Positions and debate
He has preached a European-rooted Islam compatible with Bosnian statehood, warned against imported hardline currents and lobbied internationally on Srebrenica commemoration and Bosniak rights. Supporters describe a steady wartime-tested mufti; rival voices — including Cerić loyalists and the community's critics abroad — contest his quiet style and institutional alignment with the state. The museum records both readings attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-husein-kavazovic}
