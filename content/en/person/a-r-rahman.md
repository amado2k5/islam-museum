## Summary
A. R. Rahman is an Indian composer, singer and songwriter, nicknamed Isai Puyal ("Musical Storm") and "the Mozart of Madras". {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} Born a Hindu, he converted to Islam with his family in 1989, and his music — for Roja, Lagaan and Slumdog Millionaire among hundreds of films — made him India's most honoured film composer, with two Academy Awards and two Grammy Awards. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} His birth year is given as 1967 by the English article and Britannica, the date followed here, while Wikidata's preferred claim is 1966, with 1967 deprecated; the conflict is recorded in his data record. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} {cite:wikidata-a-r-rahman}

## Early life
He was born A. S. Dileep Kumar on 6 January 1967 in Madras, now Chennai. {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} His father R. K. Shekhar was a composer for Malayalam films — Britannica spells the name Sekhar — and died when Rahman was nine. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} His mother Kareema, born Kashturi, raised the family in straitened circumstances after his father's death. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} He began the piano at four, later played professionally to help support the family, and worked as a keyboardist and arranger for composers including Ilaiyaraaja. {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} He trained under Master Dhanraj and took a diploma in Western classical music from Trinity College of Music. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en}

## Conversion and the Sufi path
The English article tells the family's turn to Islam in these terms: Rahman was introduced to the Qadiri tariqa when his younger sister was seriously ill in 1984, Sufism influenced his mother, who was a practising Hindu, and eventually his family, and at the age of twenty-three, in 1989, he converted to Islam with them. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} His new name was formed from suggestions made through a Hindu astrologer — "Abdul Rahman" and "Abdul Rahim" — to which his mother added "Allah Rakha" after a dream. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} Britannica corroborates the conversion itself, saying that in his early twenties Rahman, along with his family, converted to Islam and adopted the name by which he is now known. {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} The Urdu article tells the turn differently, through the boy's own recurring illness and his mother's vow at a pir's shrine, after which the household entered Islam; it is an independent telling, not a translation of the English one. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-ur} The Urdu article also has a dedicated section on Sufism stating that Rahman believes in Sufism, visits the shrines of pirs and saints, and is a devotee (murid) of the Pakistani qawwali singer Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, and that he once acknowledged the spirituality in his music to be a gift of the saints. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-ur} On these two independent attestations — the English article naming the Qadiri tariqa, the Urdu article naming Sufism itself — this museum records his orientation as Sufi; no source names a legal school for him. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-ur}

## Rise
He began in advertising jingles, among them campaigns for Allwyn and Titan, and in 1992 he founded his own studio, Panchathan Record Inn. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} The director Mani Ratnam gave him his first film, Roja (1992), and its score won him a National Film Award and changed the sound of Indian film music. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} There followed Bombay, Rangeela, Dil Se.. — whose "Chaiyya Chaiyya" the English article links to Sufi mysticism — Taal and Lagaan. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} In 2002 Andrew Lloyd Webber commissioned his stage musical Bombay Dreams. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman}

## Global honours
For Danny Boyle's Slumdog Millionaire (2008) he won two Academy Awards — for the score, and for the song "Jai Ho", shared with the lyricist Gulzar — together with a Golden Globe, a BAFTA and, in 2010, two Grammy Awards. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} Couples Retreat (2009) was his first Hollywood film, and he scored 127 Hours in 2010. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} India honoured him with the Padma Shri in 2000 and the Padma Bhushan in 2010, and the English article counts seven National Film Awards in his name. {cite:britannica-a-r-rahman} {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} TIME placed him in its 100 most influential people in 2009, and the English article estimates his record sales at some 150 million, an estimate recorded as the article's own. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en}

## Institutions and later work
In 2008 he founded the KM Music Conservatory in Chennai, and in 2009 the Sunshine Orchestra, which trains underprivileged children in music. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} He became an ambassador for the Stop TB Partnership in 2004. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} His honorary doctorates include Berklee College of Music (2014) and Aligarh Muslim University, and a street in Markham, Ontario, was named for him in 2013. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} His directorial debut, the virtual-reality film Le Musk, came in 2017. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en}

## Personal life
He married Saira Banu in 1995, and they have three children, Khatija, Raheema and Ameen. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en} In November 2024 the couple announced their separation. {cite:wikipedia-a-r-rahman-en}
