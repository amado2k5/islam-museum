## Summary
Prince Bola Ajibola was the only Nigerian to sit on the bench of the International Court of Justice — a barrister who led his country's legal profession, served as its chief law officer, and built lasting Islamic educational institutions at home. {cite:muslim500-bola-ajibola} {cite:wikipedia-bola-ajibola}

## Formation
Born in 1934 in Ogun State into the royal family of Owu, he was called to the English bar at Lincoln's Inn and built a leading commercial practice in Nigeria, becoming president of the Nigerian Bar Association. {cite:wikipedia-bola-ajibola}

## Career
He served as Nigeria's attorney-general and minister of justice (1985–1991), then as judge of the International Court of Justice at The Hague (1991–1994), as a judge of the Yugoslavia and Rwanda tribunals' chambers, and later as Nigeria's high commissioner to the United Kingdom (1999–2002). In Nigeria he founded the Islamic Mission for Africa and Crescent University in Abeokuta, channelling his philanthropy into education. He died on 9 April 2023. {cite:muslim500-bola-ajibola} {cite:wikipedia-bola-ajibola}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 records him in its obituaries as the jurist who carried African Muslim legal talent to the highest court in the world. {cite:muslim500-bola-ajibola}
