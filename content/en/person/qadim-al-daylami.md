## Summary
Qadim al-Daylami kept company with Fudayl ibn Iyad and his peers and followed his way of humility and submission. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 474 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], on page 131. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/131}

## Fudayl on the one content with God
Qadim said: I asked Fudayl ibn Iyad who is content with God. He said: the one who does not wish to be other than the station in which He has placed him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/131}

## The monk's words
A worshipper of Bukhara, known as Abu al-Hasan, who came to them, related: a monk said to me one day: truly the limbs of those who labour and seek God are cut off in proportion to their knowledge of His majesty, and truly the toil and weariness are light for them in what they hope for, entry into His protection and the hope of reaching His pleasure. I said: admonish me. He said: the admonitions are in us and in you together, and our admonition is the weakness of bodies after strength and the faltering of frames after vigour. Then he said: the changing of states as hours pass; and then comes the ending of appointed times and the cessation of works. Then he wept. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/131}
