# Akhmed Tsalikov

Akhmed Tembulatovich Tsalikov (Akhmed Tsalykkaty; 1882 – 2 September 1928) was an Ossetian writer and public figure, a Menshevik leader and one of the most prominent Muslim politicians of Russia's revolutionary year. He was born at the aul of Nogkau in the Terek region, into the family of an Ossetian Muslim, was educated at the Stavropol gymnasium and from 1899 at the law faculty of Moscow University, and became a leader of the Menshevik faction of the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party.{cite:wikipedia-akhmed-tsalikov-ru}

He wrote for the newspapers Severny Kavkaz, Otkliki Kavkaza and the Tatar newspaper Süz, and in May 1917 sat on the organising committee of the All-Russian Muslim Congress in Moscow, where he spoke for the cultural autonomy of Russia's Muslims. The All-Russian Muslim Council proposed him, that summer, for a seat in the Provisional Government as minister without portfolio for Muslim affairs, and he was elected to the Constituent Assembly on the Muslim shuro list. In 1918 he headed the Mejlis of the Caucasus highlanders at Tiflis, and in 1919 he served on the Defence Council of the North Caucasus and Dagestan against Denikin.{cite:wikipedia-akhmed-tsalikov-ru}

After the Soviet takeover he emigrated, first to Czechoslovakia, where he edited the journal Kavkazsky Gorets and published his novel "Brother against Brother" at Prague in 1926, and then to Poland. He died at Praga, in Warsaw, on 2 September 1928; a memorial plaque was set up for him at Vladikavkaz in 2003.{cite:wikipedia-akhmed-tsalikov-ru}{cite:wikidata-akhmed-tsalikov}
