Awni Abd al-Hadi (1889 – 15 March 1970) was a Palestinian politician from Nablus, educated in Beirut, Istanbul and at the Sorbonne in Paris.{cite:wikipedia-awni-abd-al-hadi-en}{cite:wikipedia-awni-abd-al-hadi-ar} In 1911 he co-founded the Paris-based al-Fatat society, helped organize the Arab Congress of 1913, and in 1921 became the first chief of the Amiri Diwan in Transjordan.{cite:wikipedia-awni-abd-al-hadi-en}

Returning to Palestine, he co-founded the Istiqlal Party in 1932 and was elected its first president, served as general secretary of the Arab Higher Committee, and was barred from Palestine during the 1936–1939 revolt.{cite:wikipedia-awni-abd-al-hadi-en}{cite:wikipedia-awni-abd-al-hadi-ar} After 1948 he was a minister of the All-Palestine Government, then Jordan's ambassador to Cairo, minister of justice and senator; he died at Cairo on 15 March 1970.{cite:wikipedia-awni-abd-al-hadi-en}{cite:wikidata-awni-abd-al-hadi} His wife was the feminist activist Tarab Abdul Hadi.{cite:wikipedia-tarab-abdul-hadi-en}{cite:wikidata-awni-abd-al-hadi}
