## Summary
Abu al-Qasim Hibat Allah ibn al-Hasan ibn Mansur al-Tabari al-Razi al-Lalaka'i was a Shafi'i hadith master and jurist of Baghdad and the author of *[[work/sharh-usul-itiqad-lalakai|Sharh Usul I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna wa-al-Jama'a]]*. Late in life he went to [[place/dinawar|Dinawar]] and died there in Ramadan 418 AH. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 211} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 17} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 26}

## His name
The editor of his book cites al-Zabidi in *Taj al-'Arus* and Ibn al-Athir for the view that "al-Lalaka'i" refers, irregularly, to selling *lawalik*, a kind of footwear. The editor takes this to show that he lived by the work of his hands. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 17} Al-Khatib and Ibn al-Jawzi say he was of Tabari origin, from [[place/tabaristan|Tabaristan]]. He was also called al-Razi after [[place/rayy|Rayy]], where he heard hadith, before he came to [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]] and settled there. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 17}

## Studies and teachers
At Rayy he heard Ja'far ibn Abd Allah al-Fanaki, Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Imran al-Qassar and others. In Baghdad he studied Shafi'i law with [[person/abu-hamid-al-isfarayini|Abu Hamid al-Isfarayini]], and, according to al-Khatib, heard Isa ibn Ali the vizier's son, Abu Tahir al-Mukhallis, Abu al-Hasan al-Jundi and their generation. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 17} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 18} From the chains in his book the editor counts about 180 teachers from whom he narrated. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 18}

## Students
His students included [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib al-Baghdadi]] and Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn al-Husayn al-Ukbari, known as Ibn Judda. They also included his son Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Hibat Allah (409–472 AH), whom Ibn al-Salah called truthful and trustworthy, and Ahmad ibn Ali ibn Zakariyya al-Turaythithi, the leading Sufi shaykh of Khurasan. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 17} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 18} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 19} Al-Turaythithi is the one through whom this book is transmitted in the chain at its head. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 31}

## Works
The editor lists seven works:
- *Karamat Awliya' Allah*, appended to the creed book;
- *Asma' Rijal al-Sahihayn*;
- *Fawa'id*, held in the Zahiriyya and Dar al-Kutub;
- *Majalis*, held in the Zahiriyya and Dar al-Kutub;
- *al-Sunan*, arranged by legal chapters, as al-Kattani reports;
- *Sharh Usul I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna wa-al-Jama'a*, which has an abridgement by an unknown author called *Kashif al-Ghumma*;
- a commentary on the letter of Umar ibn al-Khattab to the Christians of Syria, which Ibn al-Qayyim quoted. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 19} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 20} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 21}

## Standing
Al-Khatib said of him, "We wrote from him; he understood and memorised." Al-Dhahabi called him "the teacher of hadith in Baghdad in his time" and said he studied law with Abu Hamid and excelled in the school. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 21} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 22} The editor gives examples of his knowledge of chains. Once he corrected for al-Barqani an error by Abu Mas'ud al-Dimashqi about the chain of the hadith "The signs of the hypocrite are three" in Muslim. Al-Khatib, too, used to show him narrations from his own teachers that he doubted. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 22} {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 23} The editor also records minor slips noted by al-Dhahabi, Ibn Hajar and al-Khatib. Among them are his calling Usama ibn Hafs unknown and his saying that Muslim narrated from Hariz ibn Uthman. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 25}

## Creed and school
The editor considers him a follower of the hadith scholars in creed, citing his praise of them in the introduction to his book, and a Shafi'i in law. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 26}

## Death
He spent his last years in Baghdad and then went to Dinawar on some business. He died there on Tuesday, 6 Ramadan 418 AH. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 26} The editor quotes Ibn al-Jawzi as saying that death overtook him before his books became widely known. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 26}
