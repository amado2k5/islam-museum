# Badr Hari

## Summary

Badr Hari is a Dutch-Moroccan kickboxer born in Amsterdam on 8 December 1984 to Moroccan parents of the Houara tribe. Known as "The Golden Boy" and "Bad Boy", he won the K-1 Heavyweight Championship in 2007 and the It's Showtime heavyweight title in 2009, reached the K-1 World Grand Prix final in 2008 and 2009, and compiled a record of 106 wins, 92 of them by knockout. He is widely considered one of the greatest kickboxers of all time. {cite:wikipedia-badr-hari-en} {cite:wikidata-badr-hari}

## Career

Hari began kickboxing at the age of seven and made his professional debut in 2000. He fought under the Dutch flag until 2005, when, after a loss to Stefan Leko and a hostile reception from the Amsterdam crowd, he chose to represent Morocco and changed trainers, joining Mike Passenier. In 2007 he knocked out Yusuke Fujimoto in 56 seconds to become K-1 Heavyweight Champion, and in 2009 he won the It's Showtime heavyweight title, which he lost to Hesdy Gerges by disqualification in 2010. He won the Global Fighting Championship tournament in Dubai in 2014, and made his Glory debut in 2016 against Rico Verhoeven, losing by technical knockout through an arm injury. His most recent bout recorded in the article was a loss to Uku Jürjendal at Glory 89 in October 2023. {cite:wikipedia-badr-hari-en} {cite:wikipedia-badr-hari-ar}

## Outside the ring

Hari's career ran alongside repeated criminal cases. He was convicted of aggravated assault in 2014 and sentenced to a year and a half in prison; on appeal in 2015 the sentence became two years with ten months suspended, a judgement upheld by the Supreme Court of the Netherlands in 2017, and he paid his victims €45,000 in compensation. The Arabic article also records his charitable work through a national foundation for social works in Morocco. {cite:wikipedia-badr-hari-en} {cite:wikipedia-badr-hari-ar}
