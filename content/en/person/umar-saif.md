## Summary
Dr Umar Saif is a Pakistani computer scientist, entrepreneur and policymaker who did as much as anyone to create Pakistan's modern technology ecosystem. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology as the creator of Pakistan's first startup incubators and the first Pakistani named to MIT Technology Review's TR35 list of young innovators; Wikipedia records his roles at the Punjab Information Technology Board and as interim federal minister. {cite:muslim500-umar-saif} {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif}

## Formation
Saif studied at LUMS in Lahore and took his doctorate at the University of Cambridge, returning to Pakistan to teach and to build. In 2011 MIT Technology Review named him among the world's top young innovators — the first Pakistani on its TR35 list — for work applying technology to problems of developing countries. {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif}

## Building the ecosystem
As chairman of the Punjab Information Technology Board ⁦(PITB)⁩ from 2011, Saif digitised public services at scale — from police records to disease-surveillance systems used to fight dengue — and founded Plan9, Pakistan's first technology startup incubator, alongside networks of freelancing centres and e-learning programmes. The incubator model he seeded became the template for the country's startup scene. {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif} {cite:muslim500-umar-saif}

## Academia and ventures
He served as founding vice-chancellor of the Information Technology University in Lahore and later returned to venture work as chief executive of aiSight.ai (formerly SMSall/AiSight), chief digital officer of the Jang media group and head of Khudi Ventures, investing in Pakistani startups — combining research credentials with hands-on ecosystem building. {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif}

## Minister and public figure
In 2023–2024 Saif served as interim federal minister for science and technology and information technology in Pakistan's caretaker government, steering digital-economy and AI policy at national level. His public profile in Pakistan — academic, incubator founder, broadcaster of tech optimism — makes him one of the country's most recognisable technology figures. {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Saif embodies state-scale technology building in the Muslim world: a Cambridge-trained scientist who came home to wire a province, incubate a generation of founders and carry the digital agenda to a federal ministry. The Muslim 500 counts the results — institutions, startups and digitised services — among the year's influential contributions. {cite:muslim500-umar-saif} {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif}

## The returnee's double role
Saif's story is a familiar South Asian arc — MIT PhD, return to build institutions at home — executed with unusual speed and an unusual second act. The first act was academic: founding centres at LUMS that made data science visible in Pakistani higher education. The second was governmental: the Punjab IT Board, Plan9, a ministerial chair. Each stage left physical traces — incubated companies, a provincial e-government apparatus — and also the controversy that follows academics who accept office in Pakistan's politicised appointments culture. The record holds both the institution-building and the debates around his interim appointment. {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif} {cite:muslim500-umar-saif}

## The academic in office
Saif's record is the museum's example of the academic who accepts the risk of government: the professor who leaves the university to build the institutions the university cannot, and who pays the price of that move in the politicised culture of Pakistani appointments. The pattern — institution-builder who becomes administrator, administrator who becomes controversial — is the one his Muslim 500 entry records; the museum keeps it as the profile of a technologist who chose the difficult path of public service. {cite:muslim500-umar-saif} {cite:wikipedia-umar-saif}
