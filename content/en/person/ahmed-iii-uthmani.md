## Overview
Ahmed III, son of [[person/mehmed-iv-uthmani|Mehmed IV]], was an Ottoman sultan who ruled from 1115 to 1143 AH (1703–1730 CE), succeeding [[person/mustafa-ii-uthmani|Mustafa II]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.310-311}. Al-Salabi says that the banner of jihad stayed raised in his time, and that the state regained the Morea and Azov {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.310-311}. The Ottomans surrounded the Russian tsar Peter and his army, but the grand vizier [[person/baltaci-mehmed-pasha|Baltaci Pasha]] lifted the siege and signed the [[event/treaty-pruth-1123|Treaty of Falksen]] (Pruth) in Jumada al-Akhira 1123 AH, and the sultan dismissed him {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.310-311}. The Treaty of Edirne followed in 1125 AH through the mediation of the Netherlands and England, and in it Russia gave up the Black Sea coasts {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.310-311}.

The Ottomans defeated the Venetians; Austria then prevailed, Belgrade fell in 1129 AH (1717 CE), and the [[event/treaty-passarowitz-1130|Treaty of Passarowitz]] was concluded in 1130 AH (1718 CE), by which Austria took Belgrade, most of Serbia and part of Wallachia, while the Morea returned to the Ottomans {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.310-311}. The Safavids fought the Ottomans, were defeated and lost Tabriz and Hamadan, and peace was made in 1140 AH (1728 CE) {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.311}.

The grand vizier in his reign was Damad Ibrahim Pasha, whom al-Salabi describes as the first Ottoman official to recognise the importance of knowing Europe, and who sent ambassadors to Vienna and Paris for the first time {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.311-312}. Al-Salabi adds that translation into Turkish grew active in those years and that a printing office was set up in Istanbul {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.312}. The [[group/janissaries|Janissaries]] then rose, deposed him and put his nephew in his place {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.311}.
