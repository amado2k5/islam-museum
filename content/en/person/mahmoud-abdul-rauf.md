# Mahmoud Abdul-Rauf

## Summary

Mahmoud Abdul-Rauf was born in Gulfport, Mississippi on 9 March 1969 as Christopher Wayne Jackson.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}{cite:wikidata-mahmoud-abdul-rauf}
He starred at Louisiana State University and was drafted third overall by the Denver Nuggets in 1990, playing nine seasons in the NBA.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}
Abdul-Rauf converted to Islam early in his professional career and legally changed his name in 1993.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}
He drew worldwide attention in 1996 when he refused to stand for the United States national anthem before games in protest; the league suspended him, and the dispute ended in a compromise under which he stood with his head bowed, reciting the Qur'an, during the anthem.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}

## Career

Abdul-Rauf starred at Louisiana State University from 1988 to 1990 and was regarded as one of the fastest shooters in college basketball.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}
In the NBA he played for the Denver Nuggets, Sacramento Kings and Vancouver Grizzlies, and recorded one of the highest free-throw percentages in league history.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-ur}
The 1996 protest cost him standing in the league, and after 2001 he continued his career in Russia, Italy, Saudi Arabia, Lebanon and elsewhere, retiring finally after a spell in Japan.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}
Louisiana State University honoured Abdul-Rauf in 2020 by retiring his number 35 jersey.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-ur}

## Works

Abdul-Rauf is remembered as one of the most skilful shooters of his generation; coach Phil Jackson later compared his quick shooting style to that of Stephen Curry.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-ur}
After retirement he became a public speaker on social justice and on the rights of Muslims in America, and his story became the subject of books and interviews documenting the 1996 protest.{cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-abdul-rauf-en}
