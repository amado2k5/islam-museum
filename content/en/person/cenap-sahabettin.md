## Summary
Cenap Şahabettin (born at Bitola on 21 March 1870 – died at Istanbul on 12 February 1934) was an Ottoman-Turkish poet, writer and physician, one of the leading representatives of the Servet-i Fünun literature after Abdülhak Hâmid. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-tr} He is known for the poem Elhan-ı Şita and for his travel books Hac Yolunda (1909), Afak-ı Irak (1917), Suriye Mektupları (1917) and Avrupa Mektupları (1919). {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en}

## Career
Cenap was born at Manastır (Bitola), where his father Osman Şehabeddin Bey was a major in the Ottoman army; his father was killed at the Siege of Plevna in the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, and the family moved to Istanbul when the boy was about six. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-tr} He was educated in Istanbul and at the military medical schools, graduated as a doctor captain in 1889, and was sent to Paris at the beginning of 1890 to specialise in skin diseases, staying about four years. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} On his return he practised and wrote, joined the Servet-i Fünun circle of Tevfik Fikret, and later taught at Istanbul University until student protests in 1922 over statements attributed to him — praise of the Greeks and disparagement of the Turkish War of Independence, which he denied — forced his resignation along with some colleagues; he held no official post afterwards. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-ar} He died at Istanbul of a heart attack — on 12 February 1934 by the Turkish article, the English infobox and Wikidata, though the English article's own death paragraph says 13 February. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-tr}

## Works
His poetry includes Tâmât (1886/1887), eighteen poems published while he was a medical student, and the celebrated Elhan-ı Şita and Yakazat-ı Leyliye. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} His plays include Yalan, Körebe (1917), Küçükbeyler and Merdud Aile; his prose includes the four travel books, Evrak-ı Eyyam (1915), Nesr-i Harp (1918), Nesr-i Sulh (1918) and studies of Shakespeare (1932) and Kadı Burhanettin. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-ar}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum.

The Turkish article presents him as the greatest innovator in Turkish poetry after Abdülhak Hâmid, working under symbolist and Parnassian influence, reshaping the aruz metre around sound and harmony, pioneering the free müstezat, and — with Halit Ziya Uşaklıgil — shaping modern Turkish prose between the classical and the plain styles. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-tr} The 1922 university protests, and his denial of the statements alleged against him, are recorded by the English article as the end of his official life. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en}

On his faith: none of the four articles carries a religion field or discusses his affiliation, and Wikidata has no religion value at all. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en} {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-tr} {cite:wikidata-cenap-sahabettin} His Hac Yolunda is pilgrimage travel literature; a work's subject is not an attestation of its author's school, so under the doubt rule his faith is recorded as unknown. {cite:wikipedia-cenap-sahabettin-en}
