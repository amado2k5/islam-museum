Mohammed el-Kurd (born 15 May 1998) is a Palestinian writer, poet and journalist from the Sheikh Jarrah neighbourhood of East Jerusalem.{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-el-kurd-en}{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-el-kurd-ar} Part of his family's home was seized by Israeli settlers in 2009, when he was eleven — the episode filmed in the documentary My Neighbourhood — and in 2021 he and his twin sister Muna became the best-known faces of the campaign against the Sheikh Jarrah evictions; the twins were named together to the Time 100 that year.{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-el-kurd-en}{cite:wikipedia-muna-el-kurd-en}

He studied creative writing in the United States (BFA at the Savannah College of Art and Design; MFA at Brooklyn College), became The Nation's first Palestine correspondent and an editor at Mondoweiss, and published the poetry collection Rifqa (2021), named for his grandmother, and the nonfiction book Perfect Victims: And the Politics of Appeal (2025).{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-el-kurd-en} The biographies record that he was born into a Muslim family; no source attests a personal school of faith for him, and his record leaves it unknown.{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-el-kurd-en}{cite:wikidata-mohammed-el-kurd}
