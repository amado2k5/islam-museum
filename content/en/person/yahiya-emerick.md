## Summary
Yahiya Emerick (1971 – 11 July 2026), born John Joseph Emerick, was an American teacher, writer and publisher. Raised in a Baptist family in the Midwest, he embraced Islam in 1988–1989 in his first year at university, then spent his working life teaching in Islamic schools and writing and publishing books that introduced Islam to English-speaking readers of different ages. {cite:muslimmatters-legacy-of-yahiya-emerick-2026 tribute} {cite:icna-yahiya-emerick-tribute-2026 tribute} {cite:wikipedia-yahiya-emerick-en Biography}

## The path to Islam
MuslimMatters says he was born in 1971 into an Irish-American Baptist family in the Midwest and, as a teenager, found the Christian doctrine of the Trinity hard to accept. The English Wikipedia places the first questioning at about fifteen, after a sermon on the Trinity. In his first year at Michigan State University, in the winter of 1988–1989, he studied the Qur'an for about six months and then embraced Islam, at about nineteen according to the English article. The sources read do not describe a ceremony or a place. {cite:muslimmatters-legacy-of-yahiya-emerick-2026 tribute} {cite:wikipedia-yahiya-emerick-en Biography}

## After embracing Islam
He worked as a history teacher, was vice-principal of a full-time Islamic school at Westbury, Long Island, and taught in Sunday schools and summer programmes across North America. He founded Amirah Publishing in 1992 to produce American-oriented literature on Islam, led the Islamic Foundation of North America and its bookstore, and sat on the Central Shura of the Islamic Circle of North America for the 1994–1995 term. The ICNA tribute counts more than 25 books written, edited or translated by him, where the English Wikipedia says more than 50; they include the *Complete Idiot's Guide to Understanding Islam*, Qur'an explainers for teenagers and the children's series about Ahmad and Layla Deen. He was also active in interfaith work. {cite:muslimmatters-legacy-of-yahiya-emerick-2026 tribute} {cite:icna-yahiya-emerick-tribute-2026 tribute} {cite:wikipedia-yahiya-emerick-en Biography}

## Difficulties and reactions
The sources read do not report how his family or community reacted to his conversion, and they describe no hardship from it. They record the end of his life: MuslimMatters says he died of cancer in the week it published its tribute, and the Islamic Circle of North America reports that he died on 11 July 2026 aged 55, with the funeral prayer on 12 July. A former student, Fawzia Syed, remembers him as her first Islamic Studies teacher, who inscribed books with the words "Thinking long term". {cite:muslimmatters-legacy-of-yahiya-emerick-2026 tribute} {cite:icna-yahiya-emerick-tribute-2026 tribute}
