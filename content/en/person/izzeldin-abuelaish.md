## Summary
Izzeldin Abuelaish is a Palestinian-Canadian obstetrician and gynaecologist known to Palestinians and Israelis as a humanitarian bridge: he was the first Palestinian doctor to work inside Israeli hospitals, then lost his three daughters and a niece to a tank shell on his house in Jabalia during the 2009 Gaza war, and turned the tragedy into a project of peace and education through the Daughters for Life Foundation. {cite:wikipedia-izzeldin-abuelaish}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1955 in the Jabalia refugee camp in the northern Gaza Strip, trained in medicine in Egypt, specialised in obstetrics and gynaecology in Jerusalem, London and Belgium, and took a master's in public health at Harvard. He practised at Shifa Hospital in Gaza then at Israeli hospitals including Sheba Medical Center (Tel Hashomer), becoming the first Palestinian to hold a medical position inside an Israeli health institution. {cite:wikipedia-izzeldin-abuelaish}

## The tragedy and the book
On 16 January 2009 an Israeli tank shell struck his house in Jabalia, killing his daughters Bessan, Mayar and Aya (21, 15 and 13) and his niece Noor, and wounding other daughters. Days later he appeared on Israeli television answering in a register of reconciliation rather than revenge, saying he wanted only that his loss be the last such tragedy. He documented the experience in I Shall Not Hate: A Gaza Doctor's Journey on the Road to Peace and Human Dignity, translated into many languages and widely received internationally. {cite:wikipedia-izzeldin-abuelaish} {cite:muslim500-izzeldin-abuelaish}

## Emigration and the foundation
He emigrated with his children to Canada and became professor of global health at the University of Toronto, and in 2009 founded the Daughters for Life Foundation, which grants scholarships to young women from the Middle East and North Africa to study sciences, health and business at universities in Canada, Britain and the United States — realising his daughters' dream of education. {cite:wikipedia-izzeldin-abuelaish}

## Nominations and honours
He has been nominated several times for the Nobel Peace Prize by parliamentarians and academics, holds honorary doctorates and international human-rights and peace awards, and lectures at peace and global-health conferences. {cite:wikipedia-izzeldin-abuelaish}

## Legacy
His biography is read in conflict literature as a rare model of the witness who refused hatred: a doctor who treated Israeli mothers daily, buried his daughters before the world's eyes, and answered with an educational foundation. Reconciliation advocates hold him as the strongest recent Palestinian testimony for coexistence, and he is quoted saying that education is the strongest weapon against the cycle of violence; the museum reports these readings attributed. {cite:wikipedia-izzeldin-abuelaish}
