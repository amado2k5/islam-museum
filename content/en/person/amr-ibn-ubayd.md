## Overview
Amr ibn Ubayd ibn Bab, a client of the Banu Tamim, known as Abu Uthman. He was among the shaykhs of the Mu'tazila and of the companions of [[person/wasil-ibn-ata|Wasil ibn Ata]]. He died in the days of al-Mansur in 144 AH, and it is said 145 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.214-216}

## What al-Mas'udi relates
Al-Mas'udi relates a saying of his about Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz: that he took the caliphate without its right, and then deserved it by justice when he took it. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.184} Al-Mas'udi also has reports of him with al-Mansur. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.214-216}

## What Ibn al-Nadim relates
A report in Ibn al-Nadim says that he withdrew from the session of Qatada after the death of al-Hasan, and Qatada called his group the Mu'tazila. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.557} In another place there is a report of his finding fault with Wasil for his long neck, Wasil's answer, and their debate. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim supplement p.525}

The data need an additional source: each report above rests on a single source.

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to Amr ibn Ubayd ibn Bab, Abu Uthman of Basra, describing him as "the Mu'tazili, the Qadari, for all his asceticism and devotion". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 273} He narrated from [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan]] and Abu Qilaba; from him narrated the two Hammads, Abd al-Warith, [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya al-Qattan]], Abd al-Wahhab al-Thaqafi and Ali ibn Asim. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 273} He was a client of Banu Tamim, and his father was one of al-Hajjaj's police. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 273} Ya'qub al-Fasawi records that he was a weaver. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 279}

## The rise of the Mu'tazila
Al-Dhahabi reports Ibn Ulayya saying that the first to speak of *i'tizal* was [[person/wasil-ibn-ata|Wasil al-Ghazzal]], and that Amr ibn Ubayd joined him; Wasil admired him and married him to his sister. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 275} Abu Awana witnessed Wasil, the orator of the [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazila]], speak eloquently before Amr, after which Amr said: do you think an angel or a prophet could add anything to this? {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 277} He reports Ibn Hibban saying that Amr was a man of piety and worship until he introduced what he introduced, and withdrew from al-Hasan's circle with a group, who were therefore called the Mu'tazila. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 274}

## The criticism of him
Ibn Ma'in said: his hadith is not written; al-Nasa'i: abandoned in hadith; [[person/ayyub-al-sakhtiyani|Ayyub]] and [[person/yunus-ibn-ubayd|Yunus]]: he lies; Humayd: he lied about al-Hasan; al-Daraqutni and others: weak. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 274} Ibn Hibban said he reviled the Companions and erred in hadith by mistake, not deliberately. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 274} Al-Fallas said: abandoned, a man of innovation, noting that Shu'ba narrated two hadiths from him and al-Thawri several. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 275} Asked whether he lied, Ibn Ma'in said: he was a missionary for his creed, distinguishing him from Qatada, Ibn Abi Aruba and Sallam ibn Miskin, who were truthful and did not call others to innovation. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 277} [[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Ibn al-Mubarak]] gave a similar answer when asked why he narrated from Sa'id and Hisham al-Dastuwa'i but left Amr though their view was the same: Amr called to his view and preached it openly, while they kept silent. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 275} Al-Dhahabi says it is established that Yahya ibn Sa'id left him in the end. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 276}

Among the reports al-Dhahabi gives of his positions: that he said if Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr and Uthman testified before him over a sandal-strap, he would not accept their testimony. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 275} Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh reported a saying of his about [[passage/quran-111-1|Surat al-Masad]] and predestination, which Mu'adh held against Yahya al-Qattan for narrating from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 276} Quraysh ibn Anas reported that Amr argued for the eternal punishment of a murderer from [[passage/quran-4-93|al-Nisa' 93]], and that when Quraysh answered with [[passage/quran-4-48|al-Nisa' 48]] on forgiveness for whom God wills, Amr gave no reply. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 277} [[person/abu-amr-ibn-al-ala|Abu Amr ibn al-Ala']] is reported debating him on promise and threat, distinguishing the promise (*wa'd*) from the threat (*i'ad*) and citing Arab verse. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 278} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 279} Al-Dhahabi records from [[person/hammad-ibn-zayd|Hammad ibn Zayd]] that Ayyub, Yunus and Ibn Awn did not return his greeting, and from [[person/hammad-ibn-salama|Hammad ibn Salama]] and others dreams in his dispraise. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 274} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 279}

## His asceticism and al-Mansur
Al-Dhahabi nonetheless reports [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan]] saying: what a fine young man Amr ibn Ubayd is, if only he does not innovate. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 279} He says that [[person/al-mansur|al-Mansur]] bowed before Amr's asceticism and devotion, and attributes to him the well-known lines "all of you hunt for prey, all of you walk softly — except Amr ibn Ubayd". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 279} He reports from [[person/ibn-qutayba|Ibn Qutayba]]'s *al-Ma'arif* that al-Mansur lamented him in verses on his grave at Marran. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 279}

## His death and al-Dhahabi's rebuttal of the "Dahri" charge
Al-Khatib said he died on the road to Mecca in 143, or 144. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 279} Ahmad ibn Zuhayr reported Ibn Ma'in calling him "an evil man of the Dahriyya" — those who say people are like crops — and saying he approved of the sword. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 280} Al-Dhahabi answers: the Dahriyya are unbelievers, "and Amr was not like that". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 280}
