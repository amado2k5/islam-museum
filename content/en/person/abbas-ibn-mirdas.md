## Summary
A horseman, poet and chief among the nobles of [[group/banu-sulaym|Banu Sulaym]]; he became Muslim shortly before the conquest of Mecca after a marvellous incident in which his idol Dimar called to him from its core, summoning to monotheism. He witnessed the conquest of Mecca and the [[event/battle-of-hunayn|battle of Hunayn]] commanding Sulaym's cavalry, and is famed for his long odes at Hunayn and in boastful verse {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/69-70, 102-112}.

## His conversion and campaigns
Ibn Hisham relates in the account of his conversion that he worshipped an idol called Dimar, and heard a voice from within it say: "tell the tribes of all Sulaym — Dimar has perished and the people of the mosque live"; so he burned his idol, joined the Prophet and became Muslim {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/69-70}. He led Banu Sulaym at the conquest of Mecca and at Hunayn, where his horsemen held firm when the armies broke {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/102}.

## His poetry
At Hunayn he recited long odes glorying in the Muslims' steadfastness and his people's valour, answering Hawazin's poets; among them his famous ode: "matters are decreed and matters come / people turn away and ages remain" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/102-112}.

## For young visitors
A brave Arab horseman and poet who left idol-worship and became Muslim with passion, led his people's cavalry in great battles, and composed immortal martial odes.
