## Summary
Mustafa Abu Sway is a Palestinian scholar of Islamic studies, professor at Al-Quds University in Jerusalem, and a frequent public commentator on the religious and legal questions surrounding the Al-Aqsa Mosque and the city's Islamic endowments (awqaf). {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-abu-sway}

## Life and education
He was born in 1957. He studied in the United States and earned his PhD at Boston College with a dissertation on the philosophy of al-Ghazali, the eleventh-century theologian whose work has remained the centre of his scholarship. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-abu-sway}

## Career
Abu Sway has spent his career at Al-Quds University, where he teaches Islamic philosophy and Sufism and was the university's first dean of student affairs. He sits on the Islamic Higher Council and on waqf bodies in Jerusalem, and in 2014 was appointed to the Integral Chair for the Study of Imam Al-Ghazali's Work, an endowed chair attached to the Al-Aqsa Mosque and Al-Quds University. He lectures internationally on the religious significance of Al-Aqsa and on the Islamic law of endowment, and writes on Jerusalem's waqf system — a body of work that places him among the scholars most often cited on the city's Islamic institutions. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-abu-sway}

## Standing and readings
He appears in The Muslim 500 within the scholarship category. Supporters read his work as scholarly service to a contested holy place; his advocacy on Al-Aqsa and the endowments sits inside the broader Palestinian-Jordanian custodianship arrangement over Jerusalem's holy sites, a politically sensitive subject on which the museum reports positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-mustafa-abu-sway}
