## Overview
Qays ibn Hubayra ibn al-Makshuh al-Muradi, lord of [[group/murad|Murad]] in the time of the Prophet, and a commander in the conquest of Iraq {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/230} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.357}.

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
When the Prophet's command reached them that the two should set out together to the Qurashi man who says he is a prophet, in order to learn his teaching, [[person/amr-ibn-madikarib|Amr ibn Ma'd Yakrib]] invited Qays to go. Qays refused and mocked his opinion, so Amr rode alone and became Muslim {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/230}. When this reached Qays, he threatened Amr and was harsh with him, saying: "You opposed me and abandoned my opinion." Amr then satirised him in [[work/amr-ibn-madikarib-poem-to-qays-ibn-makshuh|a poem]]; see [[event/delegation-of-zubayd|the arrival of Amr ibn Ma'd Yakrib]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/230} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/231}.

## In al-Baladhuri
Umar wrote to Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, who sent Qays to Sa'd with seven hundred men. It is disputed whether he took part in al-Qadisiyya or came after the Muslims had finished the battle. Umar ruled that if he arrived before the dead were buried, his share would be given to him {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.357}.
