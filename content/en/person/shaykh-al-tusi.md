## Summary
Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Tusi, 'Shaykh al-Ta'ifa' and leading scholar of Imami Shi'ism (385–460/995–1067) {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 pp. 744-745}.

## Life
Born at Tus in Ramadan 385/995, he moved to Buyid-dominated Baghdad in 408/1017 and studied under the leading Imami masters, above all al-Shaykh al-Mufid {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 744}. After al-Mufid's death in 413/1022 he became al-Sharif al-Murtada's principal disciple and succeeded him in 436/1044; the caliph al-Qa'im gave him the chief chair of theology, he inherited the great Imami libraries, and his Karkh house was the Imami intellectual centre for over a decade {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 pp. 744-745}.

## Karkh and Najaf
In the sectarian riots after Tughril Beg's takeover, the Shi'i quarters were attacked; in Safar 449 his house was stormed and his books and teaching chair burned in the Karkh market. He took refuge in Najaf, taught a limited circle there and died at the blessed Mashhad in Muharram 460/1067 {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.521} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 490}.
