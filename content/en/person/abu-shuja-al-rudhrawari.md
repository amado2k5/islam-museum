## Overview
Muhammad ibn al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Abdallah, Abu Shuja al-Rudhrawari, titled Zahir al-Din, was a vizier and a man of learning {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}. Al-Zirikli prints his birth as 437 AH, 1045 CE, and his death as 488 AH, 1095 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}.

## Life
Al-Zirikli says he was born at al-Ahwaz or at the fortress of Kankur, in the district of Hamadhan {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}. He held the vizierate under the Abbasid caliph al-Muqtadi from 476 AH; al-Zirikli quotes al-Dhahabi that Iraq prospered under him, and he was dismissed in 484 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}. He made the pilgrimage in 487 AH and stayed on in Medina until he died, and was buried at al-Baqi' {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}. Al-Zirikli says his conduct as vizier was good, that he was of ample intellect and learned in letters, that his verse was delicate, and that he wrote in the manner of Ibn Muqla {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}. His name refers to al-Rudhrawar in the Hamadhan region, the home of his family {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}.

## Works
He compiled books, among them the continuation of Miskawayh's Tajarib al-Umam, printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 100-101}.
