## Summary
Ahmad ibn al-Khidr, known as Ibn Khidruya al-Balkhi, was one of the shaykhs of Khurasan. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 459 of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], directly after [[person/bayazid-al-bistami|Abu Yazid al-Bistami]], and calls him the shaykh of Khurasan, known for his futuwwa (spiritual chivalry) and his detachment. He is the first of the eastern shaykhs whom Abu Nu'aym says he lists in brief, relying on [[person/al-sulami|al-Sulami]]'s [[work/tabaqat-al-sufiyya-sulami|Tabaqat al-Sufiyya]]. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/41-42}

## His wife Umm Ali and the visit to Abu Yazid
His wife, known as Umm Ali, came from a leading family. The notice tells that she released him from her dower on condition that he take her to Abu Yazid al-Bistami, and he took her. When she came in she sat before Abu Yazid with her face uncovered. Ahmad was surprised, and she answered that when she looked at Abu Yazid she lost all thought of her own self, and whenever she looked at her husband it returned. When Ahmad was leaving he asked Abu Yazid for counsel, and Abu Yazid said: learn futuwwa from your wife. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/42}

## A saying
[[person/al-sulami|Al-Sulami]] related to Abu Nu'aym Ahmad's saying that whoever wants God to be with him in every state should keep to truthfulness. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/42}

## His death
Muhammad ibn Hamid relates that he sat with him in his last moments, when he had reached ninety-five years. He was asked a question and his eyes filled with tears. He said that a door he had knocked at for ninety-five years was opening to him now, and he did not know whether it would open on happiness or on misery, so how could he have time to answer. He owed seven hundred dinars and his creditors were present. He prayed that God would settle the debt for him. A man knocked at the door, asked for the creditors and paid them, and then Ahmad died. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/42}

## Transmission
Abu Nu'aym gives a hadith on the pre-dawn meal from Sulayman ibn Ahmad, from "Ahmad ibn al-Khidr al-Marwazi" in Baghdad. He then notes that Sulayman called the man al-Marwazi, and that someone told him this was the Balkhi, a resident of Marw. Abu Nu'aym therefore passes on the attribution of this hadith to the subject of the notice with that reservation. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/42-43}
