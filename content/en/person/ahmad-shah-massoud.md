## Summary
Ahmad Shah Massoud (c. 1953 – 9 September 2001) was an Afghan military commander and politician, the foremost mujahideen commander of the Panjshir Valley against the Soviet occupation of Afghanistan, known as the Lion of Panjshir. He was minister of defence of the Islamic State of Afghanistan from 1992, led the United Islamic Front — the Northern Alliance — against the Taliban from 1996, and was assassinated by al-Qaeda suicide bombers two days before the September 11 attacks. He was posthumously named a national hero of Afghanistan. The sources do not agree on his birth date: the English article gives only c. 1953, the Urdu article 2 September 1953, and the Arabic article 2 January 1953 in its lead and infobox but 1953 or 1954 in its body; the variant is recorded in the data file. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-ar} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-ur}

## Early life and education
Massoud was born at Jangalak, Bazarak, in the Panjshir Valley, to a Tajik family; his father, Dost Mohammad Khan, was a colonel in the Royal Afghan Army — not the nineteenth-century emir of the same name. Following his father's postings he attended primary school in Herat, then the Franco-Afghan Lycée Esteqlal in Kabul, where he became proficient in French. He enrolled to study engineering at Kabul's Polytechnic Institute, and there became involved with the Muslim Youth, the student branch of Burhanuddin Rabbani's Jamiat-e Islami. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-en}

## Resistance
In July 1975 Massoud led the first Panjshir uprising against the government of President Daoud Khan; it failed, and he withdrew to Pakistan. After the communist coup of 1978 and the Soviet invasion of 1979 he built the Panjshir resistance into one of the most effective mujahideen fronts of the Soviet–Afghan War, beating back repeated Soviet offensives into the valley through the 1980s. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-ur}

## Defence minister and the civil war
Under the Peshawar Accord of 1992 Massoud became minister of defence of the Islamic State of Afghanistan and its main military commander, while rival warlords attacked Kabul and a second civil war began. The major criticism of his human-rights record, as the English article presents it, is the Afshar operation of February 1993: an Afghanistan Justice Project report describes him as failing to prevent atrocities carried out by his forces and those of their factional ally against civilians, and notes that he convened a meeting the next day that ordered a halt; Roy Gutman is cited arguing that the witness reports implicated only the allied Ittihad forces, which had not been under Massoud's direct command. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-en}

## Northern Alliance and assassination
When the Taliban took Kabul in 1996, Massoud — who rejected their interpretation of Islam — returned to armed opposition and became military leader of the Northern Alliance, which by 2000 held only a small part of the country. On 9 September 2001 two al-Qaeda assassins posing as journalists detonated a bomb hidden in a camera during an interview at Khwaja Bahauddin, Takhar Province, and killed him. He was buried at Bazarak. After the Taliban fell, President Hamid Karzai named him a national hero of Afghanistan. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-ar}

## Faith record
The Urdu article's text states that Massoud was Tajik by descent and Sunni by maslak (school). The English article's text says only that he came from a Tajik Sunni Muslim background — a background framing — and the Arabic article's infobox gives his religion as Sunni Islam, a structured value, while the Urdu infobox gives generic Islam. Under the museum's rule that a school is recorded only when two independent texts name it, one qualifying text is not enough, and his faith is recorded as unknown, with the attestations preserved in the data file for a founder's word. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-ar} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-shah-massoud-ur}
