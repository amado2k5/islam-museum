## Summary

Munira al-Qubaysi (1933–2022) was a Syrian teacher and founder of the Qubaysiat movement, which grew from home Quran circles in Damascus into a widely spread women's educational network across Syria, the Gulf, Jordan and Lebanon.{cite:wikipedia-munira-al-qubaysi}

## Life and the movement's beginnings

Born in Damascus, she graduated in the natural sciences and worked as a schoolteacher, then began in the 1960s to hold religious study circles for women in her home, which gradually expanded into private schools and institutes run entirely by women.{cite:wikipedia-munira-al-qubaysi}

## Method and spread

The Qubaysiat method rests on Quran memorisation and its sciences and moral upbringing within a tightly organised women-only environment, alongside private schooling and charitable work. The Syrian state formally recognised the movement in 2003, and it spread to several countries.{cite:wikipedia-munira-al-qubaysi}

## Assessment

Studies describe it as the world's largest Islamic educational network led by and teaching women. Press and academic reports have criticised its organisational secrecy and social class profile, while supporters highlight women's religious-educational empowerment — the museum records both characterisations.{cite:wikipedia-munira-al-qubaysi}
