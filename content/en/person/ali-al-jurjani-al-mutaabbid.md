## Summary
Ali al-Jurjani was one of the early worshippers, "one who has emptied himself of desires and adorned himself with solitude". [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 466 in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], directly after Yahya ibn Mu'adh; it begins on page 110 and runs to page 112. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/110-112}

## His meeting with al-Sari al-Saqati
Al-Sari al-Saqati tells that he set out from Baghdad for the frontier at Abbadan, to fast there in Rajab, Sha'ban and Ramadan, and that Ali al-Jurjani travelled with him in the boat. When the time came for his breaking of the fast, al-Sari took out two barley loaves and some ground salt; Ali looked at them for a long time and then said: your salt is ground, and you have many kinds of food with you; you will not succeed, and you will not enter the company of the lovers. Then he looked at a bag in which there was barley gruel, and took some of it. Asked what was wrong, he said: I calculated that between chewing and swallowing there are seventy glorifications, and I have not chewed a bird for forty years. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/110-111}

## The first five counsels
Then He supports you with these: life of hearts, clarity of reflection, understanding, the magnifying of God (the first word on page 112 is smudged in the scan; the likelier reading is "magnifying"), awakening from heedlessness, and aiding the homelands in obedience to God. Then God clothes you with five garments: kindness, forbearance, tenderness, mercy to the world, and awe of the Fire when you look upon it, which makes you remember God as Lord. Five things bind your heart: racing, haste, patience against the unlawful, true severance, and sound intention. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/111-112}
