## Summary
Abd al-Rahim ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Khwarazmi was a calligrapher of the Aqqoyunlu court. His father, Abd al-Rahman al-Khwarazmi, worked in Shiraz and then in Baghdad under the Qaraqoyunlu ruler Pir Budaq and developed a distinctive [[concept/nastaliq-script|nasta'liq]]; his brother Abd al-Karim was also a calligrapher whose specimens share many of the same features. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 324} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 354}

## At Ya'qub's court
Abd al-Rahim became close to [[person/yaqub-beg-aq-qoyunlu|Sultan Ya'qub]] and adopted the pen name Anisi ("friend"), used as an epithet with sultani ("royal") and ya'qubi ("Ya'qub's"). His swooping script, with its contrast of thick and thin, returning ya' and long tail on mim, resembles his father's. His work includes a manuscript copied for Prince Ya'qub at [[place/tabriz|Tabriz]] in 886/1481. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 15} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 325}

## The peripatetic Khamsa
Blair relates that a copy of Nizami's Khamsa begun for Prince Khalil called on Anisi to finish the text and on the painters Shaykhi and Darvish Muhammad to illustrate it. Unfinished at Khalil's death, it passed to his brother Ya'qub, who also died before it was done, and finally to [[person/ismail-i-safavi|Isma'il I]], founder of the Safavid dynasty, under whose patronage it was completed; in the early sixteenth century a colophon full of verbal and visual puns was added. Blair contrasts Anisi, who worked for the Aqqoyunlu, with [[person/sultan-ali-mashhadi|Sultan Ali Mashhadi]], the master at Herat. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 324} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 325} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 326}
