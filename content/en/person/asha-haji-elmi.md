## Summary
Asha Haji Elmi is a Somali peace and women's-rights activist known for inventing the "Sixth Clan": the idea of making Somali women — excluded from the five warring clan families — a united sixth "clan" that crosses tribal divisions to enter peace negotiations. For this path she received the 2008 Right Livelihood Award, and she is among the best-known faces of Somali civil society. {cite:wikipedia-asha-haji-elmi}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1962 in Mogadishu, studied economics at Somalia National University, then continued her education in the United States where she took qualifications in finance and administration, working as an accountant before dedicating herself to civil work after the collapse of the Somali state in 1991. {cite:wikipedia-asha-haji-elmi}

## The Sixth Clan and the peace process
At the Somali reconciliation conference in Arta (Djibouti) in 2000, she and fellow activists led the initiative declaring women a "sixth clan" demanding seats of their own in the transitional government; women obtained, for the first time, separate representation in formal Somali negotiations, and she herself entered the Transitional Federal Parliament (2004–2009) and the subsequent process. She also founded Save Somali Women and Children (SSWC), providing services to displaced women and victims. {cite:wikipedia-asha-haji-elmi} {cite:muslim500-asha-haji-elmi}

## Rights work
Through her organisations she worked against female genital cutting, documented sexual violence in conflict, and carried the cases of Somali women to the UN Security Council and international bodies; in women's-rights literature she is credited with articulating the Somali civil-peace position separating women's interests from militia calculations. {cite:wikipedia-asha-haji-elmi}

## Recognition
With her organisation she received the 2008 Right Livelihood Award for "continuing to lead at great personal risk the path to reconciliation, peace and democracy in Somalia", per the citation, among other rights prizes, and appears in rankings of influential African peacebuilders. {cite:wikipedia-asha-haji-elmi}

## Legacy
The Sixth Clan experiment is read in conflict studies as one of the smartest tools of African civil activism: the women did not ask for exceptional standing but declared themselves a "clan" that played by the system's own rules in order to break it from within — a language now taught in women's peace movements worldwide. {cite:wikipedia-asha-haji-elmi}
