## Overview
Hasan Pasha, son of Khayr al-Din Barbarossa, was beylerbey of Algiers and later commander-in-chief of the Ottoman fleet; al-Sallabi devotes a whole section to him titled "The mujahid Hasan ibn Khayr al-Din Barbarossa." {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.230}

## Governor of Algiers
On arrival he set about fortifying the city of Algiers at the points where Charles V's attack had exposed its weakness, consolidated order in the country and within the army, then turned to the problem of Tlemcen, where the Zayyanid house and the Spanish presence in Oran stood in the way. He defeated the Sa'dis at Tlemcen and consolidated Ottoman rule there in 959 AH (c. 1552 CE), pursued an independent policy that included declared hostility to France despite its official ties with the Ottoman state, and refused the French ambassador's offer of military aid. The Janissaries arrested him in the summer of 969 AH (1561 CE) and sent him in chains to Istanbul. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.230-231}

## Command of the fleet
When Selim II succeeded Suleiman the Lawgiver, he appointed Hasan ibn Khayr al-Din commander-in-chief of the Ottoman fleet; Hasan left Algiers for Istanbul in 975 AH (1567 CE), and Muhammad ibn Salih Ra'is succeeded him as beylerbey of Algiers. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.249}
