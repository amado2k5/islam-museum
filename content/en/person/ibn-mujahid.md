## Overview
Ibn Mujahid, born in Rabi' II 145 AH, shaykh of the Qur'an readers of his time and their foremost; Tha'lab used to say no one in our age knew God's Book better than Abu Bakr ibn Mujahid. He died and was buried in the Bab al-Bustan cemetery, as Ibn al-Jawzi records. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 283}

## With al-Tabari
Ahmad Khalid Babakr counts Ibn Mujahid — Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Musa ibn al-Abbas al-Tamimi of Baghdad, master of the reciters in his day and the first to fix the readings at seven in his book al-Sab'a — among the students of [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]]. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.33} He heard al-Tabari recite, admired it and said: I never heard anyone in the prayer-niche recite better than Abu Ja'far; the thesis cites Shawqi Dayf's introduction to al-Sab'a that he would stand long at the door of al-Tabari's mosque listening to him. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.33} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.76} He was keen to hear from al-Tabari, alone, Warsh's transmission of Nafi's reading by way of [[person/yunus-ibn-abd-al-ala|Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la]], but al-Tabari would only let him hear it together with others. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.33} Yaqut says he often prayed for mercy on al-Tabari, praised him and counted him among the great masters; yet Ibn al-Jazari says he disguised Ibn Jarir's name as "Muhammad ibn Abd Allah" when transmitting Nafi's reading from him, and the thesis notes that Ibn Mujahid never mentions al-Tabari in al-Sab'a or cites his view on any reading. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.33} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.34} The thesis also quotes his statement that the reading followed in the great cities is what people received from their predecessors, and that "reading is a sunna". {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.92}
