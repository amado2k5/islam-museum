# Bahadur Shah Zafar

Bahadur Shah Zafar (1775–1862), born Abu Zafar Siraj-ud-din Muhammad, was the twentieth and last Mughal emperor of India, and an Urdu poet who wrote under the pen name Zafar {cite:wikipedia-bahadur-shah-zafar-en}. He was born on 24 October 1775 at Delhi, the second son of the emperor Akbar II, and succeeded his father in 1837 as Bahadur Shah II; his authority was largely confined to the walled city of Delhi, while real power lay with the British East India Company {cite:wikipedia-bahadur-shah-zafar-en}{cite:wikidata-bahadur-shah-zafar}.

His court gathered the great Urdu poets of the age — Ghalib, Daagh Dehlvi, Momin Khan Momin, and his own mentor Sheikh Ibrahim Zauq — and in 1854 he appointed Ghalib his poet tutor {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-en}. In May 1857 mutinous sepoys reached Delhi and proclaimed him Emperor of India; when the British retook the city that September, he was arrested, tried by a military court, and exiled to Rangoon in Burma, where he died on 7 November 1862 and was buried the same day {cite:wikipedia-bahadur-shah-zafar-en}{cite:wikipedia-bahadur-shah-zafar-ar}.

His Urdu ghazals were collected after his death as the Kulliyyat-i-Zafar {cite:wikipedia-bahadur-shah-zafar-ur}. The English article records that he was a devout Sufi, regarded as a Sufi pir who accepted disciples, and quoted the contemporary Delhi Urdu Akhbaar calling him "one of the leading saints of the age"; the Urdu article likewise describes his deep knowledge of the Sufi path {cite:wikipedia-bahadur-shah-zafar-en}{cite:wikipedia-bahadur-shah-zafar-ur}. His favourite wife was the empress Zeenat Mahal {cite:wikipedia-zeenat-mahal-en}.
