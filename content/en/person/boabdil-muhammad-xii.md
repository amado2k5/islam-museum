## Overview
Abu Abd Allah Muhammad, the last sultan of the Banu Nasr in Granada. Al-Maqqari says that the Muslim kingdom of al-Andalus came to an end in his reign. He was the son of Sultan Abu al-Hasan, of the Khazraj Ansar. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.528-529}

## Handover and departure
He surrendered the Alhambra to the Christians on the second of Rabi al-Awwal 897 AH, on sixty-seven conditions, which they broke afterwards, according to al-Maqqari. He then moved to the Alpujarras, and then went to the Maghrib, where he stayed at Melilla and settled in Fez. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.528-529}

## In Fez
He built palaces there in the manner of the buildings of al-Andalus, which al-Maqqari saw and entered. He wrote to the Wattasid sultan of Fez a letter called "al-Rawd al-Atir al-Anfas", composed by Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Arabi al-Uqayli. He died in Fez in 940 AH and was buried near the musalla outside the Gate of the Law, and left two sons, Yusuf and Ahmad. Al-Maqqari says that in 1027 AH he saw his descendants attached to the endowments of the poor and the needy, and counted among the beggars. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.528-529}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] tells the story of Abu Abdallah Muhammad al-Saghir in episodes 6 and 7 of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]], and makes the end of his reign the image of the end of al-Andalus as a whole {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:15:00}.

### Background and succession
Al-Suwaidan relates that his father, whom he names Muhammad al-Ghalib billah, was married to [[person/aisha-al-hurra|Aisha al-Hurra]] and also had a Christian slave-girl named Thurayya (Zoraya), with whom he was infatuated and who bore him a son, Yahya; she pressed him to make her son heir {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:04:00}. The rightful heir, he says, was Aisha al-Hurra's son Abu Abdallah Muhammad, called al-Saghir ("the younger") to tell him apart from his uncle [[person/al-zaghal|Muhammad al-Zaghal]]; al-Suwaidan comments that he was small in years, in judgement and in results {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:05:00}. He relates that the father confined him and his mother, not in a prison but in a palace where their movements were restricted, to secure the succession for Yahya {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:05:30}.

### Revolt against his father
Al-Suwaidan says many people rejected the succession of Thurayya's son because his mother was a Christian slave, and held that the succession belonged by order to Abu Abdallah; his supporters went to where he was held with his mother and freed them both {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:06:00}. Once free, he relates, Abu Abdallah rose against his father, drove him out and took power in Granada; the father took refuge with his brother al-Zaghal in Málaga, and the country was split again, al-Saghir in Granada, al-Zaghal in Málaga and Almería with his brother the former ruler under his protection {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:07:00}.

### Captivity and the bargain with Ferdinand
Al-Suwaidan relates that Ferdinand used the quarrel to attack Granada's fortresses; al-Saghir at first fought him out of pride, but had no strength against the king of Spain, and Ferdinand captured him in one of the battles {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:08:00}. After his capture, he says, al-Zaghal attacked Granada and joined it to his realm, and al-Ghalib billah died a year later of grief and sorrow {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:08:30}. Al-Zaghal offered the king of Spain much money and his allegiance for his nephew's release, but was refused, al-Suwaidan says, because the king had agreed with the captive, then only 25, to help him return as king of Granada and expel al-Zaghal {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:10:00}. The plan, he relates, was for al-Saghir to enter from the north of Granada and raise revolt there with the king's weapons and money, while the king besieged Málaga from the south; in al-Suwaidan's view Ferdinand's aim was to tear al-Andalus apart and so master it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:11:00}.

### Granada alone
Al-Suwaidan relates that al-Saghir entered Granada from the north and seized it while his uncle was fighting the king of Spain at Málaga; Málaga was lost to the Christians, leaving al-Saghir only Granada and its surroundings {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:13:00}. Al-Zaghal, he says, did not pledge allegiance to his nephew but declared an independent state at [[place/wadi-ash|Wadi Ash (Guadix)]], east of Granada {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:13:30}. Al-Saghir asked the king of Spain to return Málaga as agreed; the king answered that he would keep his army there to protect it from al-Zaghal, and within a few months declared all its Muslim people slaves {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:15:00}. In 895 AH, 1490 CE, he says, the king of Spain took Almería and then Wadi Ash while al-Saghir gave his uncle no help; al-Zaghal fled to Tunis, Granada was besieged on every side, and al-Saghir was told to hand over the keys of the city {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:17:00}.

### The call to jihad, then surrender
Al-Suwaidan relates that al-Saghir saw they would never treat him fairly whatever he conceded, resolved to fight and proclaimed jihad, but that the actual defence was carried by a popular movement led by the emir Musa ibn Abi Ghassan, told in [[event/fall-of-granada-897|the surrender of Granada]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:05:30}. After seven months of this popular jihad, he says, a letter came from Ferdinand and Isabella asking for surrender, guaranteeing safety and offering to make him a vizier over Granada assisting the king of Spain; he accepted, and al-Suwaidan holds that his concern was to keep power {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:08:00}. He set two conditions, al-Suwaidan says: that the two monarchs swear to the terms, and that the Pope in Rome sign the agreement as its guarantor; both were granted {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:09:30}.

### Leaving the Alhambra
Al-Suwaidan relates that with his consent Abu Abdallah sent the monarchs lavish gifts; they entered the Alhambra preceded by monks, and he knelt and kissed Ferdinand's hand, an image in which, for al-Suwaidan, history summed up the end of al-Andalus {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:15:00}. He left the palace towards the Maghreb, he says, until he reached a hill overlooking the Alhambra, looked back and wept until his tears wet his beard, and his mother Aisha al-Hurra said to him: "Weep like a woman for a kingdom you could not defend like a man" {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:17:00}. The hill is still visited in Spain, he says, and known as "the Moor's last sigh"; this was on 2 Rabi al-Awwal 897 AH, 2 January 1492 CE {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:17:30}.

### In the Maghreb
Al-Suwaidan relates that Abu Abdallah then emigrated to the Maghreb, where its king arrested him on a charge of betraying the Muslims' land and put him in prison, and that historians report his children were seen many years later begging in the streets of the Maghreb {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:18:00}. (Nafh al-Tib, as given above on this page, has him settle in Fez and build palaces there, and al-Maqqari saw his descendants living on the endowments for the poor {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 528-529}.)

### His verdict
Al-Suwaidan calls Abu Abdallah al-Saghir weak and a traitor, holds that he allied with the Christians to stay in power, and makes his humiliating departure a lesson on abandoning jihad {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:18:00}. This is al-Suwaidan's judgement in his programme and is given here as his {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:04:00}.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "A Story and an Idea"
Besides "History Taught Me", [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives Abu Abdallah al-Saghir the eighth episode of [[work/kissa-wa-fikra-program|"A Story and an Idea"]], whose idea is surrender to the enemy. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:21:30} What follows is his account.

He opens with the scene of 1492: a man of about thirty weeping on a hill above the Alhambra, and his mother, [[person/aisha-al-hurra|Aisha al-Hurra]], telling him to weep like a woman for a kingdom he could not defend like a man. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:01:00} He calls him the last Muslim king in al-Andalus, who believed the enemy's promises and so brought tragedy on the whole Andalusian people. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:01:30} By the second half of the fifteenth century Granada was the only Muslim kingdom left, and no help came from North Africa as in the days of the Almoravids and Almohads. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:02:30} He tells of the split between Sultan Abu al-Hasan and his brother [[person/al-zaghal|al-Zaghal]], and of Abu al-Hasan's marriage to Thurayya, a Christian captive, which set her against Aisha over the succession and divided the people into two factions. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:06:00} Aisha's side won, and Abu Abdallah took the throne in 1482 at twenty-five. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:07:00}

While he was a captive, he says, Castile and Aragon made a secret agreement with him: he would be restored in Granada as their vassal, pay twelve thousand gold doblas a year and free Christian captives. Historians, al-Suwaidan says, describe him as weak of resolve, inexperienced and greedy for power. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:10:00} Back in Granada he fought his uncle and preached peace with Castile while Ronda (1485), Malaga and Baza fell; al-Zaghal in the end submitted, crossed to North Africa and died in Tlemcen. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:13:30}

The Castilians besieged Granada with eighty thousand men and built a walled camp; the only supply road ran through the Alpujarras. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:17:30} The cavalry commander Musa ibn Abi Ghassan led the defence; al-Suwaidan wishes young Muslims knew him as they know Tariq ibn Ziyad and Saladin. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:18:00} When the vizier Abu al-Qasim declared the provisions exhausted, the council chose surrender, under a treaty of sixty articles protecting mosques, worship, copies of the Qur'an and Muslim courts; in his words it remained "ink on paper", and the Muslims later faced death or forced conversion under the Inquisition. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:20:30} Musa warned the council not to trust the oaths, then rode out alone against a patrol of fifteen knights and, wounded, sank in the river under the weight of his armour. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:22:00} A secret annex, he says, gave Abu Abdallah and his family estates and money in return for persuading the people. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:23:00}

On 2 January 1492 the Christians entered and raised a silver cross on the highest tower, and Abu Abdallah handed over the keys. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:24:00} He spent a year at Andarax under watch, sold his property cheaply and moved to Fez, where he died; al-Suwaidan cites al-Maqqari as finding his descendants in Fez living on endowments for the poor. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:25:00} He lists the causes of the fall as sin and luxury, the loss of the spirit of jihad, division and rivalry for office, alliance with the enemy, power given to the unfit, rule without consultation, and scholars who failed to reform. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep08 00:26:30}
