# Saken Seifullin

Saken Seifullin (born Saduaqas; 15 October 1894 – 25 April 1938) was a Kazakh poet and writer, a pioneer and founder of modern Kazakh literature, and a statesman of the early Soviet Kazakh republics. He was born in a nomadic settlement in Akmolinsk Uezd, in what is today Karaganda Region, studied in a Russian-Kazakh school at the Spassk brass works and at Aqmola, taught Russian at a Muslim madrasa, and in 1913 entered the Omsk teaching seminary, where his first article appeared in the journal Ay Qap.{cite:wikipedia-saken-seifullin-en}{cite:wikipedia-saken-seifullin-ru}

In 1914 he helped head the Kazakh youth society Bırlık (Unity) at Omsk and published his first book of poetry, "Past Days"; in 1917 he founded the society Jas Qazaq (Young Kazakh). After the October Revolution he was elected to the Aqmola soviet and appointed people's commissar of education, was admitted to the Communist Party in February 1918, survived arrest by the Whites and Kolchak's "death carriage", and rose to chair the Council of People's Commissars of the Kirghiz ASSR. He founded and first headed the Union of Writers of Kazakhstan.{cite:wikipedia-saken-seifullin-en}{cite:wikipedia-saken-seifullin-ru}

Seifullin was arrested by the NKVD and executed at Almaty on 25 April 1938, deemed a threat to society and a nationalist; he was posthumously rehabilitated in 1957.
