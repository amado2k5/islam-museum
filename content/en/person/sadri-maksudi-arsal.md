# Sadri Maksudi Arsal

## Summary
Sadri Maksudi Arsal (23 July 1878 – 20 February 1957) was one of the leading figures in the national awakening of the Tatars in Russia in the early 1900s: a writer, lawyer, politician, professor and researcher of Turkic languages, and a delegate of the League of Nations. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} He was president of the short-lived Idel-Ural State. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} His birth name was Sadreddīn Nizāmeddin al-Maqsūdī; in the Russian State Duma he used the Russified Sadrutdin Nisamutdinovich Maksudov, and in Turkey he added "Arsal" to his name, going by it from 1935. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} The day-precision birth date comes from the Turkish article and Wikidata; the English article gives only the year 1878, and no source supports the year 1879 that appears in some lists. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-tr} {cite:wikidata-sadri-maksudi-arsal}

## Career
He was born in 1878 in the village of Taşsu near Kazan in the Russian Empire; the Turkish article names his father as the village imam Nizamettin Molla. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-tr} After graduating from the Teachers' Institute in Kazan in 1900, he travelled to Istanbul, where he stayed for a while; two years later he went to study at the Sorbonne in Paris, graduating with a master's degree in law. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} On his return to Russia he joined the Islamic Constitutional Party and sat as a representative of the Kazan district in the second Duma in 1907 and the third in 1908. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} In 1917 he chaired a national assembly held in Ufa, and he was then elected president of the short-lived Idel-Ural State. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en}

In 1918, after the rise of Bolshevism and the breaking up of his party, he escaped through Saint Petersburg to Finland, staying at Tampere in the home of the Finnish Tatar Imad Samaletdin. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} The next year he continued to Paris, where at the Peace Conference he spoke for the political rights of the people of the Idel-Ural region, without producing a positive result. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} In 1922 he returned briefly to Finland, where his family had fled, and together they travelled to Germany and settled in Berlin, where he focused on historical studies and refrained from political activity. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} He then moved to Paris, lecturing on Turkish history at the Sorbonne, and in 1925 moved to Turkey, where he worked as a lecturer and later as professor of legal history and philosophy at the universities of Ankara and Istanbul, and was eventually awarded an honorary title. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en}

## Works
He published literary and scholarly works in his fields, among them Türk dili için ("For the Turkish Language"), which treats the development of Turkish and proposes ways of improving it, such as removing foreign influences. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} He was elected to the Turkish parliament for three legislative periods. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} He died at Istanbul in 1957. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out positions as reported in the sources; it is not a verdict by the museum.

The sources present Arsal as a Tatar national activist working through law and institutions: a Duma deputy of the Islamic Constitutional Party, chairman of the 1917 Ufa assembly, president of the Idel-Ural State, and an advocate — unsuccessful at Paris — for the political rights of the Idel-Ural peoples. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} After his emigration he withdrew from politics in Berlin and turned to scholarship, and in Turkey he combined academic work on legal history and the Turkish language with three terms as a deputy. {cite:wikipedia-sadri-maksudi-arsal-en} No source cited here names a school of Islam for him personally. {cite:wikidata-sadri-maksudi-arsal}
