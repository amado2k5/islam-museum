## Summary
Kholoud Al-Faqih is a Palestinian judge who broke a century of male monopoly over the Sharia judiciary when she was appointed in 2009 to the Ramallah Sharia Court, becoming the first woman to serve as a Sharia judge in the Middle East — a turning point in the history of adjudication and family law in the Arab world. {cite:wikipedia-kholoud-al-faqih}

## Education
She studied law at Al-Quds University, graduating with honours in 1999, received her licence to practise in 2001, and completed a master's in private law at the same university in 2005. She worked as a lawyer at the Women's Center for Legal Aid and Counselling, then for five years (2003–2008) as defence counsel for battered women, building litigation experience in family and domestic-violence cases. {cite:wikipedia-kholoud-al-faqih}

## The road to the bench
In her work she noticed the total absence of women from the Sharia judiciary although many of its cases — divorce, custody, inheritance, maintenance — touch women directly; she prepared a legal-religious study of the obstacles, sat two competitive judicial exams in Ramallah in 2008 and scored among the highest in the Islamic-law sections; of 45 candidates nine passed, two of them women. In February 2009 President Mahmoud Abbas issued the decree appointing her (together with her colleague Asma al-Wahidi) to the Sharia judiciary. {cite:wikipedia-kholoud-al-faqih} {cite:muslim500-kholoud-al-faqih}

## On the bench and social resistance
Al-Faqih describes her first years as a second struggle after the appointment itself: she faced objections from male colleagues and litigants who refused to bring cases before a woman judge, and hardest of all, as she recounts, the refusal of some women themselves to deal with her, on the argument that the Sharia bench is a male preserve; her interviews record her insistence on continuing, holding that competence, not gender, is the measure of a judge. {cite:wikipedia-kholoud-al-faqih}

## The film and recognition
She became the subject of The Judge, Erika Cohn's documentary following the Palestinian Sharia court through its first woman judge, was ranked by CEO Middle East magazine in 2012 among the ten most powerful Arab women, and was named in some local listings as the most influential figure in the Muslim world in 2009. {cite:wikipedia-kholoud-al-faqih}

## Legacy
Her appointment opened the door to later female appointments in the Palestinian Sharia judiciary and set off a broad Arab and juristic debate on women's judicial authority; in Islamic feminist scholarship her path is read as an argument for an ijtihad that widens women's role within the institutions of fiqh rather than outside them. {cite:wikipedia-kholoud-al-faqih}
