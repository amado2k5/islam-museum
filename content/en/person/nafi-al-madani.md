## In brief
Abu Ruwaym Nafi' b. Abd al-Rahman b. Abi Nu'aym, mawla of Ja'una, the Medinan qur'an-reader, one of the Seven Readers; al-Asma'i says his origin was Isfahan, as the hadith master Abu Nu'aym relates in his Tarikh Isbahan. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His reading
He was the imam of the Medinans in qur'an-reading, the reader to whose recitation people came and to whose selection they returned, belonging to the second layer after the Companions; he was dark-skinned and given to playfulness. He read under Umm Salama's client, and had the zawiya at Quba' mentioned above; Ibn Abi Uways reports that Malik told him he had read under Nafi'. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

Ibn al-Jazari gives his kunya as Abu Ruwaym, or Abu al-Hasan: Nafi' ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Nu'aym al-Laythi al-Madani. He read on seventy of the successors, among them [[person/abu-jafar-al-qari|Abu Ja'far]], 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Hurmuz al-A'raj, Muslim ibn Jundub, Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, Salih ibn Khawwat, Shayba ibn Nisah and Yazid ibn Ruman; his chain runs through Ibn 'Abbas, Abu Hurayra and Ibn 'Ayyash to [[person/ubayy-ibn-kab|Ubayy ibn Ka'b]], and through Ibn 'Abbas to [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]], and so to the Prophet. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 121} He died in 169 AH on the sound view, was born about 70, and was of Isfahani origin and very dark in complexion; he led the people in the reading in Medina, where the headship of teaching came to him and people agreed on him after the successors, and he taught there for more than seventy years. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 122} Ibn al-Jazari relates that Malik ibn Anas said the reading of the people of Medina is a sunna, and when asked whether he meant Nafi's, said yes; and that Ahmad ibn Hanbal told his son that the reading he loved most was that of Medina, and failing that, Asim's. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 122} His two transmitters are [[person/qalun|Qalun]] and [[person/warsh|Warsh]], and Ibn al-Jazari counts a hundred and forty-four lines of transmission for his reading. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 121}
