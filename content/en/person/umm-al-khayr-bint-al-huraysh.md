## Summary
Umm al-Khayr bint al-Huraysh al-Bariqiyya was, in the words of [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']], one of the "mistresses of eloquence"; she lived at [[place/kufa|Kufa]], supported [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] at [[event/battle-of-siffin|Siffin]], and later came before [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]]. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.393}

## The summons
He relates that Mu'awiya wrote to his governor at Kufa to send her to him, promising to reward the governor according to what she said of him, good for good and evil for evil. When the governor read her the letter she said she was neither turning from obedience nor making false excuses, and that she had long wished to meet him over matters stirring in her breast. When he reminded her of Mu'awiya's promise, she said: "Your kindness to me will not make me dress up falsehood, nor will your acquaintance with me make me say anything of you but the truth." {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.393}

## In Mu'awiya's audience
Mu'awiya lodged her with the women of his household for three days, then received her on the fourth before an assembly, and a quick-witted exchange followed. He asked about her speech on the day [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar ibn Yasir]] was killed; she said she had neither prepared it beforehand nor polished it afterward — they were words her tongue threw out in the shock of battle. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.393} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.394} Mu'awiya had one of his men who had memorised it recite it: he described her that day on a dark camel, whip in hand, addressing Ali's army — urging them to stand firm, citing [[passage/quran-47-31|Muhammad 31]] on trial, praying that God unite them in piety, praising Ali for his kinship to the Prophet, his precedence and his feats at Badr, Uhud and Hunayn, and attacking Mu'awiya and the Syrians in harsh terms. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.394} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.395} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.396}

## Uthman, Talha and al-Zubayr
Mu'awiya said she had meant nothing by that speech but his death; she answered that it would not grieve her if it were so. He asked her about [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]]; she said people had made him caliph unwillingly and killed him willingly, then added that she meant no disparagement — he had been quick to good deeds and high in rank. Of [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha]] she said he was struck down where he felt safe, though the Prophet had promised him Paradise; of [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]] she first begged to be excused, then called him the son of the Prophet's aunt and his disciple, promised Paradise, and asked Mu'awiya by God's right to excuse her from such questions. He excused her and sent her home with honour. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.396}

## The sources of the story
The stories of these 'women who came before Mu'awiya' are told in the adab literature; [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] takes them from Ibn Tayfur's Balaghat al-Nisa', al-Iqd al-Farid, Subh al-A'sha, Ibn Asakir's history and Nihayat al-Arab. They voice the positions their speakers took in the civil war, and the museum gives them as attributed reports, without a verdict. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.396}

## For young visitors
Umm al-Khayr was an eloquent woman of Kufa who spoke to the army in wartime; later the caliph Mu'awiya called her and asked about her words, she answered frankly, and he honoured her and sent her home.
