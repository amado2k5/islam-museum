## Overview
Ali ibn Hamza al-Kisa'i, the shaykh of the Qur'an readers and grammarians of Kufa, and the teacher of al-Hadi, al-Amin and al-Rashid. He was buried in al-Rayy. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 331}

Al-Mas'udi says he died in 189 AH after travelling with al-Rashid to al-Rayy. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 331}

Ibn al-Nadim says he was one of the seven readers of Kufa, of foreign origin, and that he moved between the lands. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 76}

Ibn al-Nadim relates the story of his debate with Sibawayh, in which they referred to eloquent Bedouins and the correct answer was with al-Kisa'i. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 143}

## His place in al-Tabari's Jami' al-Bayan
The thesis counts Ali ibn Hamza al-Kisa'i (d. 182 AH in the thesis) among the scholars of language and grammar who influenced [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]], and cites Yaqut that al-Tabari set down in his commentary the sum of what al-Kisa'i said and of the books of al-Farra, al-Akhfash and Qutrub, sometimes without naming them. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.50} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.51}

## Chain and reading in Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr

Ibn al-Jazari names him Abu al-Hasan 'Ali ibn Hamza ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Bahman ibn Firuz al-Kisa'i al-Kufi. He read on [[person/hamza-al-zayyat|Hamza]], on whom he relied, and also on Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla and on [[person/isa-ibn-umar-al-hamdani|'Isa ibn 'Umar al-Hamdani]], and narrated letters of the reading from Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash, Isma'il ibn Ja'far and Za'ida ibn Qudama. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 182} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 183} He died in 189 AH on the best-known account, aged seventy, and was the leader of the people in the reading in his time and the most learned in it. Ibn al-Jazari quotes Ibn al-Anbari that he was the most learned of men in grammar, unmatched in rare vocabulary and unmatched in the Qur'an, so many came to him that he could not check each pupil's reading, and he would gather them in one session, sit on a chair and recite the Qur'an from beginning to end while they listened and noted down even its stops and beginnings; and Ibn Ma'in that he had seen no man more truthful in speech. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 183} His two transmitters are [[person/abu-al-harith-al-layth|Abu al-Harith]] and [[person/abu-umar-al-duri|al-Duri]], and Ibn al-Jazari counts sixty-four lines of transmission. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 182}
