## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Isfahani, Abu Ahmad, known as al-Assal, was a judge and scholar of hadith with wide learning, from the people of Isfahan. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 269 AH, 883 CE, and his death as 349 AH, 960 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 310}. Ibn al-Jawzi gives a longer lineage: Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Ibrahim ibn Sulayman ibn Muhammad, Abu Ahmad al-Assal al-Isfahani {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 14 p. 129}.

## Judgeship, teachers and travels
He held the judgeship of Isfahan and studied under the shaykhs of Isfahan, Hamadhan, Baghdad, Kufa, Basra, the two sanctuaries, Wasit, Rayy and Khuzistan {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 310}. Ibn al-Imad says he heard from Muhammad ibn Asad al-Madini, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Asim and scholars of their generations, travelled, collected and wrote, and was one of the leading authorities in the field {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 258}. Ibn al-Jawzi names among his teachers Muhammad ibn Ayyub al-Razi, Ibrahim ibn Zuhayr al-Halwani and Bakr ibn Sahl al-Dumyati, and others like them {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 14 p. 129}. He relays Abu Nu'aym al-Hafiz as saying that Abu Ahmad al-Assal held the judgeship and was among the foremost in memory, precision and knowledge {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 14 p. 130}.

## What scholars said of him
Ibn al-Imad quotes Abu Nu'aym al-Hafiz that he was among the great hadith memorisers; Ibn Manda that he had written from a thousand shaykhs and found none more exact than al-Assal; and Ibn Nasir al-Din that he was a great and precise hafiz {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 258}. From Ibn Bardis he adds that his sons Abu Amir, Abu Ja'far Ahmad, Ibrahim, al-Abbas and Abu Bakr Abd Allah narrated from him, as did Ibn Manda and Abu Nu'aym, and that he was one of the imams of hadith in understanding, precision and trustworthiness {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 258}. He then gives Abu Bakr ibn Ali's verdict that he was reliable and trusted, and Abu Ya'la's statement in al-Irshad that he was a precise hafiz and a scholar of this field {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 259}. Ibn al-Jawzi reports Ibn Manda's remark on his precision through a chain of narrators {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 14 p. 129}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists among his books al-Shuyukh, al-Tafsir, al-Tarikh, al-Amthal, a Musnad arranged by chapters, Gharib al-Hadith, Ahadith Malik, al-Ma'rifa and al-Raqa'iq {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 310}.

## Death
Ibn al-Imad places his death in Ramadan, at about eighty years of age or more, citing al-'Ibar {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 258}. Ibn al-Jawzi, relaying Abu Nu'aym, says he died in Ramadan of 349 AH {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi vol. 14 p. 130}.
