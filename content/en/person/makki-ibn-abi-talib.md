## Summary
Abu Muhammad Makki ibn Abi Talib ibn Hammush al-Qaysi the muqri'; originally of Qayrawan, he moved to al-Andalus and settled at Cordoba — among the deeply versed in the sciences of the Qur'an and Arabic, of fine understanding and character, prolific in Qur'anic works which he mastered and perfected, versed in the seven readings and their meanings; he emigrated to Egypt at thirteen and frequented the tutors, completed the Qur'an at Qayrawan in 374, then returned to Egypt a second time in 377. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His place in the entry of the readings into al-Andalus

Ibn al-Jazari says that al-Andalus and the lands of the Maghrib had none of the ten readings until the end of the fourth century, when some of their people travelled to Egypt and took the readings there; [[person/abu-umar-al-talamanki|Abu 'Umar al-Talamanki]], author of al-Rawda, was the first to bring the readings into al-Andalus and died in 429 AH, and he was followed by Abu Muhammad Makki ibn Abi Talib al-Qaysi, author of al-Tabsira and al-Kashf among other works, who died in 437 AH, and then by [[person/abu-amr-al-dani|Abu 'Amr al-Dani]], author of al-Taysir and Jami' al-Bayan. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 44}
