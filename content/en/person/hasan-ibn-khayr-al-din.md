## Summary
Son of [[person/khayr-al-din-barbarossa|Khayr al-Din Barbarossa]], of a noted Algerian family on his mother's side; governor of Algiers from 951/1544 until his deposition in 960/1552 {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 229-235}.

## Fortifications and Tlemcen
On arrival he refortified Algiers at the points [[person/charles-v|Charles V]]'s attack had shown weak, ordered the army, then turned to Tlemcen: leading the Muslim force he barred Oran's column from restoring [[person/abu-zayyan-ahmad|Abu Zayyan Ahmad II]], backed his ally [[person/al-hasan-zayyani-tlemcen|King Hasan]] who acknowledged Ottoman authority, and left a garrison at al-Mashwar castle; the Sa'dis he drove out, consolidating Ottoman rule by 959/1551 (printed '1151') {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 230-234}.

## Hostility to France
He pursued a policy hostile to every foreign power, France included despite its formal Ottoman ties; when her envoy offered military aid for the projected attack on Spain and relief for Andalusia's Muslims, he refused — declaring jihad the Muslims' own affair and that he would not win by an infidel against an infidel {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 234}.

## Deposition
The envoy returned and poisoned the Porte's ear: Hasan's absolute sway and expansion of his little kingdom — his mother being Algerian — would shatter the state's unity. Süleyman deposed him in Safar 960/January 1552, on the ground of harming neighbourliness with Morocco, giving the beylerbeylik to [[person/salih-rais|Salih Reis]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 234-235}.
