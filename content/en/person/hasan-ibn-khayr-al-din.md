## Summary
Son of [[person/khayr-al-din-barbarossa|Khayr al-Din Barbarossa]], of a noted Algerian family on his mother's side; governor of Algiers from 951/1544 until his deposition in 960/1552 {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 229-235}.

## Fortifications and Tlemcen
On arrival he refortified Algiers at the points [[person/charles-v|Charles V]]'s attack had shown weak, ordered the army, then turned to Tlemcen: leading the Muslim force he barred Oran's column from restoring [[person/abu-zayyan-ahmad|Abu Zayyan Ahmad II]], backed his ally [[person/al-hasan-zayyani-tlemcen|King Hasan]] who acknowledged Ottoman authority, and left a garrison at al-Mashwar castle; the Sa'dis he drove out, consolidating Ottoman rule by 959/1551 (printed '1151') {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 230-234}.

## Hostility to France
He pursued a policy hostile to every foreign power, France included despite its formal Ottoman ties; when her envoy offered military aid for the projected attack on Spain and relief for Andalusia's Muslims, he refused — declaring jihad the Muslims' own affair and that he would not win by an infidel against an infidel {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 234}.

## Deposition
The envoy returned and poisoned the Porte's ear: Hasan's absolute sway and expansion of his little kingdom — his mother being Algerian — would shatter the state's unity. Süleyman deposed him in Safar 960/January 1552, on the ground of harming neighbourliness with Morocco, giving the beylerbeylik to [[person/salih-rais|Salih Reis]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 234-235}.

## The second return
After [[person/hasan-corso|Hasan Corso]]'s death in 964/1557 the sultan restored him; he raised ten thousand Zawawa, married Sultan Kuku's daughter, set [[person/bu-yahya-al-rais|Bu Yahya]] over Badis, fought [[person/abd-al-aziz-ibn-abbas|Abd al-Aziz b. Abbas]] to his fall, and recognised [[person/ahmad-muqran-al-zawawi|Ahmad Muqran]] {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 245-249}.

## Arrest and the third return
The Janissaries arrested him in summer 969/1561, charging he meant independence, and shipped him bound to Istanbul; the sultan sent him back a third time in late 970/1562 with ten warships. He besieged Oran on 13 April 1563, lifted it as relief converged, joined the [[event/siege-of-malta-1565|Malta siege]] with twenty-five ships, and in 975/1567 Selim II made him commander-in-chief of the fleet {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 250-252}.

## For Sprouts
Hasan ibn Khayr al-Din Barbarossa ruled Algiers from 951 AH to his removal in 960 AH, then was restored by the sultan after Hasan Corso's death in 964 AH.

## For Scholars
Hasan ibn Khayr al-Din Barbarossa, governor of Algiers from 951 AH/1544 CE to his removal in 960 AH/1552 CE, his mother from a known Algerian family. On arrival he worked to fortify the city of Algiers where Charles V's attack had exposed weaknesses, and settled the order and the army; then he turned to the Tlemcen problem: he led the Muslim army, stopping the Spanish force of Oran from restoring Abu Zayyan Ahmad II, supported his ally King Hasan who acknowledged Ottoman authority, and left a garrison in the Mishwar citadel; he drove the Sa'dids from it and secured Ottoman rule there in 959 AH/1551 CE (the printed text '1151 CE'). He pursued a policy hostile to all foreign powers including France despite its official ties to the Ottoman state; when its ambassador offered military aid for the projected attack on Spain he refused, declaring the jihad's cause belongs to Muslims and he wins no victory with an unbeliever against an unbeliever. The French ambassador returned and poisoned the Porte's ear — that Hasan's absolute power and expanding kingdom would break the state's unity and threaten its body with split, his Algerian mother's conduct also raised in the warning — so Sultan Suleiman removed him in Safar 960 AH/January 1552 on a charge of harming good-neighborliness with Morocco, giving the beylerbeylik to Salih Ra'is. After Hasan Corso's death in 964 AH/1557 CE the sultan restored him to Algiers: he raised ten thousand from Zawawa, married the daughter of Kuko's sultan ibn al-Qadi, appointed Bu Yahya al-Ra'is over Bedis, and fought Abd al-Aziz ibn Abbas until he died, acknowledging his successor Ahmad Muqran.
