## Early years, 1027-68 AH (1618-58)
Abu al-Muzaffar Muhammad Muhyi al-Din Aurangzib 'Alamgir Badshah-i Ghazi was the third son of Shahjahan and Mumtaz Mahal, a daughter of Asaf Khan. He was born at Dhod in Malwa on 15 Dhu al-Qa'da 1027 AH (3 November 1618). He received a good education, and throughout his life could hold his own in disputation with the 'ulama and with men of letters; his Persian compositions were respected. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 817}

In 1044 AH (1635) he was made a commander of ten thousand. In 1045 AH (1636) he was appointed Viceroy of the Deccan, but he resigned in 1053 AH (1644), either out of religious fervour or from bitterness against his elder brother Dara, whom Shahjahan seems to have chosen as successor. He then governed Gujarat, and in 1055 AH (1646) was moved to the command at Balkh, but the Uzbeks were too strong and he had to give Balkh up. As governor of Multan from 1057 AH (1648) he was ordered to recapture Kandahar from the Persians; he besieged it twice, in 1058 AH (1649) and 1061 AH (1651), without success. He was Viceroy of the Deccan a second time from 1062 AH (1652), when his revenue official Murshid Quli Khan did much to restore the devastated territory. In 1065 AH (1655) he besieged Golkonda and could have ended the kingdom, but the Emperor ordered him to accept tribute and make peace; in 1066 AH (1657) he attacked Bijapur and took Bidar and Kalyani before being recalled to make peace. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 817-818}

## The war of succession, 1067-68 AH (1658-59)
Shahjahan fell ill on 27 Dhu al-Qa'da 1067 AH (about 9 September 1657), and his four sons prepared to fight for the throne. Aurangzib's conduct in that war shows him at his best as a general and an administrator, a standard he never reached again. Dara Shukoh, the heir designate at Agra, had the prestige of imperial authority, but lacked both organising ability and strategy. Shuja', the second son and governor of Bengal, proclaimed himself king and advanced on the capital, but was decisively beaten at Bahadurpur on 14 February 1658 by the imperial army under Raja Jai Singh and Sulayman Shukoh. Dara's southern army under Jaswant Singh was crushed by Aurangzib and Murad at Dharmat on 15 April 1658, and at Samugarh, eight miles from Agra, on 29 May 1658 Dara was defeated decisively. Aurangzib interned his father in the Agra fort, arrested Murad near Mathura, and sent him to Gwalior, where he was executed in 1072 AH (1661-2). {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 818}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "A Story and an Idea"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the twenty-fourth episode of his programme [[work/kissa-wa-fikra-program|"A Story and an Idea"]] to Aurangzeb, under the idea of "the sanctity of public money". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:01:30} What follows is his account; the length of reign and age at death he gives at the end do not agree with the birth and accession he gives at the start, so they are left out here.

He calls him the last great Mughal emperor and says Ali al-Tantawi called him "the last of the rightly guided caliphs". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:01:30} His father was [[person/shah-jahan-mughal|Shah Jahan]], builder of the Taj Mahal, whose grief for his wife weakened the sultanate. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:02:30} From youth Aurangzeb was serious, religious and a horseman, trained in leadership; scholars raised him in the Hanafi school, and he mastered recitation, Hanafi law and calligraphy and Arabic, Persian and Turkish. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:04:30} He led armies against revolts in his father's reign, then confined his father in comfort, with a balcony looking on his mother's tomb, and declared himself sultan at about forty. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:06:00}

He took no salary and lived by copying the Qur'an in his fine hand and selling the copies. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:06:30} Al-Suwaidan sets out the Islamic principles of public money as he sees them: God is its true owner, all have an equal right to benefit from it, it is spent as the Shari'a directs, officials are trustees, and the ruler takes only a wage as the community's employee. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:11:00} He sets Umar ibn al-Khattab and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz beside him as models, and against them an Umayyad caliph giving the poet Jarir thirty thousand dirhams for a poem and late Ottoman palace spending that reached a third of the state's revenue. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:17:30}

Aurangzeb, he says, abolished eighty kinds of tax; built mosques, schools, hospitals, roads and gardens; set up the hisba to keep order in markets and on roads; paid scholars only for teaching or writing; spent what he inherited on the poor; and sent hundreds of pilgrims each year at his own expense, since revolts kept him from going himself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:21:00} He brought the subcontinent under one rule, from the Himalayas to the ocean, and al-Suwaidan counts thirty battles in his reign, eleven led by Aurangzeb himself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:22:00} He fasted Mondays and Thursdays, led the tarawih prayers, and sat for petitioners three times a day with no chamberlain. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:22:30} He asked to be buried in the nearest Muslim cemetery in a shroud costing no more than five rupees, and died in 1707. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:23:00} Al-Suwaidan gives his full name as Abu al-Muzaffar Muhyi al-Din Muhammad Aurangzeb; weak heirs followed, and Muslim rule ended when the English deposed Bahadur Shah II in 1857. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:23:30} He closes with Umar ibn al-Khattab likening himself to the guardian of an orphan's property, abstaining if well off and taking fairly if in need. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep24 00:27:00}
