## Overview
Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Muhammad ibn Salm ibn al-Bara' al-Tamimi, Abu Bakr ibn al-Ja'abi, was a judge and one of the leading memorisers of hadith, from Baghdad. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 284 AH and his death as 355 AH, with 897 and 966 CE printed beside them {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 311}.

## Life
He held the judgeship of Mosul, where, according to al-Zirikli, his conduct was not well regarded. Al-Zirikli adds that he is said to have left instructions that his books be burned after his death, and they were burned {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 311}.

## What scholars said of him
Al-Zirikli says he was accused of laxity in religion. He quotes Ibn Nasir al-Din (in al-Tibyan) that at the end of his life no one but he remained who could verify the hidden defects of hadith and the biographies of narrators, and that he was charged with drinking and carelessness in prayer {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 311}. Al-Zirikli also says he followed a particular school of his own within Shi'ism; this is a view attributed to him in the entry and is not the Sunni position, which is the museum's first frame {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 311}.

## Works
He wrote many books on hadith, on teachers and on histories; al-Zirikli does not name them {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 311}.
