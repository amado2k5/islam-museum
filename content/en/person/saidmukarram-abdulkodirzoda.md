## Summary
Saidmukarram Abdulkodirzoda is the state grand mufti of Tajikistan, chairman since 2010 of the Shuroi Ulamo — the council of scholars that is the country's sole fatwa-issuing body — and head of the Islamic Center of Tajikistan. He is also Tajikistan's designated member of the International Islamic Fiqh Academy. {cite:muslim500-saidmukarram-abdulkodirzoda}

## Life and formation
He was born on 10 August 1963 in the Temurmalik district of Khatlon, son of the scholar Domullo Abdulqodir, and memorised the Qur'an in childhood. From 1995 to 2003 he studied at the International Islamic University in Islamabad — a bachelor's in usul al-din, then a master's in hadith and tafsir — before returning to Tajikistan for a second master's and a doctorate in Tajik language and literature at Tajik National University. {cite:muslim500-saidmukarram-abdulkodirzoda}

## Career
He taught at Imam al-Tirmidhi Islamic University, ran its academic department, served in the presidential executive office's religious-affairs section and headed religious-examination work at the Committee on Religious Affairs before the Islamic Center's board elected him chairman of the Shuroi Ulamo in October 2010 — succeeding Sheikh Amonulloh Nematzoda — making him the republic's chief religious authority. {cite:muslim500-saidmukarram-abdulkodirzoda}

## Role and setting
As mufti he operates inside one of the post-Soviet world's most regulated religious fields: the Shuroi Ulamo issues the fatwas the state accepts, sermons are guided, and independent religious education is curtailed. In September 2024 he survived a knife attack near Dushanbe's central mosque; prosecutors attributed the assault to an extremist-recruited assailant. Supporters describe a scholar stabilising religion under law; critics of Tajik policy see the muftiate as an instrument of control. The museum records both readings attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-saidmukarram-abdulkodirzoda}
