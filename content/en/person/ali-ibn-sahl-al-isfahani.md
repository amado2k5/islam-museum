## Summary
Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Sahl al-Isfahani al-Sufi, a scholar joined to God and preserved in virtue, who disciplined his own self until he refined it, went for days without food, and spent nights standing. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (printed number 687, pages 404 and 405). {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/404}

## His discipline
Abu Hamid Ahmad ibn Rustam, who is cited as the source, says that Ali was among those helped to oppose the self. He had been raised in luxury and comfort, and his discipline refined him; it sometimes kept him from food for twenty days, during which he stood through the night. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/404}

## Judgement and witnesses
He is reported, through Abu Abd Allah Ahmad ibn Ishaq al-Shammar, to have said that he never submitted a matter to judgement except with a guardian and two witnesses. The sentence has words unclear in the scan, so its full wording is not given. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/404}

## The dream
He tells that longing overcame him early in his path, distracting him from food and work, and in a doze he saw himself enter Paradise. He saw a great lofty palace, said to belong to Muhammad ibn Yusuf (the figure of printed 686), then another palace in which he was told: this is yours, Abu al-Hasan. A woman whose light overpowered everything appeared, then turned away, saying that he did not desire them. He then heard a voice reciting a line of verse, which he took as addressed to him; the line is not quoted here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/404}

## His letter to al-Junayd
Ali ibn Harun, a companion of Abu al-Qasim al-Junayd, relates that he read a letter that Ali ibn Sahl wrote to al-Junayd, which opens with a long prayer for success, union with God and contentment. The letter is not given in full. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## His death
His son relates that he heard companions of Ali ibn Sahl say that he used to say: my death will not be like yours, through ailments and sicknesses; it will come by a call that is answered. The first word of this phrase is unclear in the scan, and is not read here. One day, while seated among a company, he said: at your service, and fell dead. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}

## A report from Anas
He relates, through Sulayman ibn Ahmad, from Ali ibn Sahl al-Sufi al-Isfahani, from Ibn Mahdi, from Ali ibn Salih, from al-Qasim, from Humayd al-Tawil, from Anas, a report about helping one's brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed; helping an oppressor means restraining him from oppression. The wording is not given here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/405}
