## Overview
Abu al-Fath al-Karajki, whose name was Muhammad ibn Ali, was a scholar of the Shia. What follows is taken from the Hanbali chronicler Ibn al-Imad in Shadharat al-Dhahab, who calls him the head of the Shia and the author of many works {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 214}. Ibn al-Imad glosses his name by saying that al-Karajki means al-Khaymi {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 214}.

## Learning and companionship
According to Ibn al-Imad he was a grammarian, philologist, astrologer, physician and theologian, versatile in many fields, and one of the chief companions of al-Sharif al-Murtada {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 214}. He is the author of Talqin Awlad al-Mu'minin {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 214}.

## Death
He died at Tyre in Rabi' al-Akhir 449 AH, which corresponds to 1057 CE by the museum's conversion, as Ibn al-Imad lists him among the deaths of that year {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 5 p. 214}.
