Khalid Sheldrake (1888–1947), born Bertram William Sheldrake in London, was the son of a condiment manufacturer and was raised a Roman Catholic. As a schoolboy — in 1903 on the English article's account — he converted to Islam and took the name Khalid.{cite:wikipedia-khalid-sheldrake-en}

He founded journals and the Western Islamic Association, opened mosques at Peckham Rye and East Dulwich, helped finance London's first purpose-built mosque at Southfields, paid for indigent Muslims' funerals, and in 1932 conducted the much-reported mid-air conversion of Gladys Palmer Brooke over the English Channel.{cite:wikipedia-khalid-sheldrake-en} Scholarship on him stresses both his years inside the Woking mission's circle and his insistence on closeness to mainstream Islam, including a demand that the Lahore Ahmadiyya disavow its founder; no school is settled for him here.{cite:ryad-european-converts-interwar} In 1934 the First East Turkestan Republic invited him to become king of 'Islamestan'; he accepted the title in Beijing and set out for Kashgar, but the republic collapsed under Soviet-backed attack and he escaped to India and home. He died in London in 1947.{cite:wikipedia-khalid-sheldrake-en}
