## Overview
Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Tumart al-Nafzi, known as the Mahdi Ibn Tumart, founder of the Almohad call in the Maghrib: in 516 AH he began to oppose the Almoravid sultan, which weakened their power in al-Andalus. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 p.124}

A battle was fought between the two sides at al-Buhayra, in which the Almohad army under Muhammad al-Bashir al-Wansharisi was defeated and its commander killed; Ibn Tumart lost most of his ten companions and thousands of the flower of his army, his illness worsened, and he died in 524 AH (1130 CE), succeeded by Abd al-Mu'min ibn Ali who restored the Almohads' power. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 p.124}

## In Shadharat al-Dhahab — Ibn al-Imad al-Hanbali
Muhammad ibn Tumart, imam of the Masmuda and founder of the Almohad state, known as al-Mahdi. He was of Harga origin. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1668-1669}

## In Shadharat al-Dhahab — Ibn al-Imad al-Hanbali: In Ibn Khaldun
Ibn Khaldun says that he settled in Tlemcen, performed the pilgrimage, entered Iraq and met great scholars. They claimed he met Abu Hamid al-Ghazali, who gave him good news of the state. He then returned and disputed with the scholars of the Maghrib, and wrote *al-Murshida* on the Oneness of God. His view was the infallibility of the imam. They called him al-Mahdi in 515 AH, and he built a ribat at Igilliz where he taught the Oneness of God in the Berber tongue. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1668-1669}

## In Shadharat al-Dhahab — Ibn al-Imad al-Hanbali: In al-Shadharat
*Shadharat al-Dhahab* says that he was ascetic, brave, eloquent in enjoining good. It considers his claim to be an Alid Hasanid and infallible an empty claim; this is the judgement of the author of *Shadharat*, not the museum's. He met [[person/abd-al-mumin-ibn-ali|Abd al-Mu'min ibn Ali]] at the village of Malala, then entered [[place/marrakesh|Marrakesh]] and advised [[person/ali-ibn-yusuf-al-murabiti|Ali ibn Yusuf]]. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.550-551}
