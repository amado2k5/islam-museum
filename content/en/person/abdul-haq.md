# Maulvi Abdul Haq

**Maulvi Abdul Haq** (20 April 1870 – 16 August 1961), titled **Baba-e-Urdu**, was an Indian and later Pakistani Urdu scholar and linguist who spent his life advancing the Urdu language through the Anjuman Taraqqi-i-Urdu, which he served as secretary for nearly half a century.{cite:wikipedia-abdul-haq-en}{cite:wikipedia-abdul-haq-ar}

## Early life and formation

Abdul Haq was born at Sarawan near Hapur in Uttar Pradesh; the Urdu article records that his ancestors were Hindu Kayasths of Hapur who converted to Islam in the Mughal era. He was educated at Aligarh under the influence of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, entered the service of Hyderabad State and rose to chief inspector of schools.{cite:wikipedia-abdul-haq-ur}{cite:wikipedia-abdul-haq-en}

## The Anjuman Taraqqi-i-Urdu

Elected secretary of the Anjuman in 1912, Abdul Haq edited the journals Urdu, Science and Hamari Zaban and produced English–Urdu dictionaries, Urdu grammars and a standard Urdu textbook. After retiring from Osmania University in 1930 he lived at Delhi, then migrated to Pakistan in 1948 and continued the Anjuman's work and the Urdu College at Karachi.{cite:wikipedia-abdul-haq-en}

## Death and legacy

Abdul Haq demanded that Urdu be Pakistan's sole national language. He died of bladder cancer at Karachi on 16 August 1961 and was buried in the grounds of the Federal Urdu College; Pakistan Post issued a commemorative stamp in his honour in 2004.{cite:wikipedia-abdul-haq-en}{cite:wikidata-abdul-haq}
