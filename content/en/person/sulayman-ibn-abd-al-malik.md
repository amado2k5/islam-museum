## Overview
[[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik]], one of the Umayyad caliphs. He succeeded [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]], and his caliphate lasted three years, according to the report of the shaykhs of al-Hasan ibn Hammad {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 64}. He made his son Ayyub his heir apparent {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 45}.

## Life before the caliphate
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, calls Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik the seventh caliph of the Umayyad dynasty, reigning from 96 to 99 AH/715 to 717 CE. It says he was probably born in [[place/medina|Medina]] about 55 AH/675 CE, a son of the later caliph [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] and of Wallada, daughter of al-Abbas ibn Jaz of the Banu Abs, a tribe counted within the northern Arab confederation of [[group/ghatafan|Ghatafan]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846}

The article states that almost nothing substantial is known of his first three decades. It judges it likely that he came to Syria early in the [[event/second-fitna|Second Civil War]], with other members of the [[polity/marwanid-dynasty|Marwanid]] branch of the Umayyads. In 81 AH/701 CE he led the pilgrimage. After the death in 85 AH/704 CE of [[person/abd-al-aziz-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan]], the brother of Abd al-Malik, Abd al-Malik named his own sons [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid]] and Sulayman as his successors. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846}

## Governor of Palestine
Under al-Walid, who reigned from 86 to 96 AH/705 to 715 CE, Sulayman served as governor of the military district of Filastin, where he worked to develop [[place/ramla|al-Ramla]] as the new capital of [[place/palestine|Palestine]]. Some time after 90 AH/710 CE he gave refuge to clansmen of the Muhallabids who had fallen out of favour with [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]] and had escaped from prison. Among them was [[person/yazid-ibn-al-muhallab|Yazid ibn al-Muhallab]], whom Sulayman later made governor of Iraq and Khurasan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846}

The article says the reasons are unknown, but that Sulayman's conduct points to some disagreement with al-Hajjaj and with al-Walid. It adds that the sources give no sound grounds for linking this to the efforts, said to have been backed by al-Hajjaj, to set Sulayman aside in favour of [[person/abd-al-aziz-ibn-al-walid|Abd al-Aziz ibn al-Walid]]. It also reminds readers that al-Walid, in trying to name his own son, acted as every earlier Umayyad ruler had done. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846}

## Accession and seat of government
After al-Walid died on 13 Jumada II 96 AH/24 February 715 CE, Sulayman acceded without opposition. According to the article, Syrian sources show that he chose Jerusalem as his main seat. In 97 AH/716 CE he led the pilgrimage, and the article thinks it likely that soon afterwards he moved to [[place/dabiq|Dabiq]] in northern Syria, the supply base for the campaign against the Byzantine empire of 97 to 100 AH/716 to 718 CE. He died at Dabiq on 12 Safar 99 AH/24 September 717 CE. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846}

## Policy and appointments
The article notes that a picture of the reign is hard to form because it was short. In its reading, policy was basically the same as under earlier rulers, although new governors were probably appointed in every province. It sees no bias toward a particular tribal faction in the choices, only a possible aim of closer control through loyal officials and an end to arbitrary conditions under long-established, powerful governors. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846}

It names several such cases: [[person/musa-ibn-nusayr|Musa ibn Nusayr]], the conqueror of Spain, and his sons; the clan of al-Hajjaj, who had himself died in Ramadan 95 AH/June 714 CE; [[person/uthman-ibn-hayyan|Uthman ibn Hayyan al-Murri]] in Medina; [[person/khalid-ibn-abdallah-al-qasri|Khalid ibn Abd Allah al-Qasri]] in [[place/mecca|Mecca]]; and [[person/qutayba-ibn-muslim|Qutayba ibn Muslim]] in Khurasan. Qutayba fell victim to an uprising led by [[person/waki-ibn-abi-sud|Waki ibn Abi Sud]] in Dhu al-Hijja 96 AH/August 715 CE. Waki then made himself governor and was soon dismissed by Yazid ibn al-Muhallab. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846}

## Expansion and the campaign against Byzantium
The article says the expansion of the empire more or less came to a halt, partly because effective opposing forces had appeared. Only the conquests of Jurjan and Tabaristan deserve mention. It argues, however, that the drive to conquer had not weakened, and offers as proof the great campaign against [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]] and the [[polity/byzantine-empire|Byzantine empire]]. It began at the end of 97 AH/716 CE by land under the supreme command of Sulayman's brother [[person/maslama-ibn-abd-al-malik|Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik]], and at the start of 98 AH by sea under [[person/umar-ibn-hubayra|Umar ibn Hubayra al-Fazari]]. It ended in an unsuccessful [[event/siege-of-constantinople-717|siege of the city]], from early summer 98 AH/717 CE to summer 99 AH/718 CE. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 846} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 847}

## Messianic hopes and later images
The article connects the hoped-for conquest of Constantinople and the approach of the year 100 of the Hijra with chiliastic expectations. It says it is hard to judge how widespread and effective messianic ideas then were, or what to make of the fairly numerous references to Sulayman as the expected [[concept/al-mahdi|Mahdi]] in the praise poems of [[person/jarir-ibn-atiya|Jarir]] and [[person/farazdaq|al-Farazdaq]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 847}

It judges that Sulayman's role as the rightly guided ruler who restores justice after oppression rests on a fairly clear contemporary and authentic base. It says the same can hardly be said of his appearances in anecdotal and religious literature. There he is shown on one side as a glutton, a conceited and cruel pleasure-seeker, skilled in Arabic, and on the other as the unjust ruler who, humbled and contrite, falls victim to the sermons of pious figures. The article adds that the accounts of his relations with his successor [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]], the celebrated exception among Umayyad caliphs, are of the same kind. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 847}

## Choice of successor, as the article reads it
The article thinks Sulayman was most probably not bound by the testament of Abd al-Malik in favour of [[person/yazid-ibn-abd-al-malik|Yazid II]] and [[person/hisham-ibn-abd-al-malik|Hisham]], and that like his predecessors he named his own son Ayyub. Ayyub died before his father, about the end of 98 AH/717 CE or the start of 99 AH. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 847}

The sources give much space to reports crediting [[person/raja-ibn-haywa|Raja ibn Haywa]], a court theologian or spiritual counsellor from the time of Abd al-Malik to that of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, with the leading part in securing the nomination and the oath of allegiance to Umar II. The article observes that most of these traditions trace back to Raja himself and that his role at Dabiq at the time of Sulayman's death has very probably been exaggerated. It finds more reasonable a succession of Umar II by traditional patterns, such as seniority and well-founded claims. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 847}

## Governors and officials
Sulayman dismissed Khalid ibn Abd Allah al-Qasri from Mecca and appointed Talha ibn Dawud over it, in the year 97 as printed at the top of the page {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 33}.

## The campaigns
al-Tabari reports that Sulayman equipped armies in the year 97 {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 33}. He sent his brother [[person/maslama-ibn-abd-al-malik|Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik]] to Constantinople, and ordered him to remain there until he conquered it, in the year 98 as printed before the paragraph {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 42}. It is said that when he reached Dabiq he vowed to God that he would not turn back until the army he had sent to Constantinople entered it, according to the report {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 44}.

## Succession
Sulayman pledged allegiance to his son Ayyub ibn Sulayman and made him heir apparent, in the year 98 as printed at the top of the page {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 45}. When Marwan died he pledged to Ayyub, and he held back from Yazid and lay in wait for him, according to the report {cite:tabari-khayyat-part2 scan 45}.

## Notes for researchers
- Reports of the pledge and succession on page 9 have been left out of this entry, because their text is damaged in the OCR. They should be read from the scan before being added.
- The years 97 and 98 are taken from the printed marginal notes as they appear. They have not been reconciled with the dates on other pages.
- Most of the reports are "according to the report": al-Tabari transmits them from named narrators. They have not yet been checked against other sources.
- The references use the scan page number. Reports with OCR damage have been left out.

## His officials in Khalifa ibn Khayyat
[[person/khalifa-ibn-khayyat|Khalifa ibn Khayyat]] closes his account of this reign by naming its governors and office-holders. They are given here in his order; lines the editor restored from later authors are marked as such. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 317–319}

- **Mecca:** [[person/khalid-ibn-abdallah-al-qasri|Khalid ibn Abd Allah al-Qasri]] (confirmed, then dismissed); then Dawud ibn Talha (then dismissed); then Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd Allah (until Sulayman died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 317}
- **Medina:** [[person/uthman-ibn-hayyan|Uthman ibn Hayyan al-Murri]] (dismissed); then [[person/abu-bakr-ibn-hazm|Abu Bakr ibn Hazm]] (from Ramadan 96 AH until Sulayman died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 317}
- **Yemen:** Urwa ibn Muhammad ibn Atiyya al-Sa'di {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Iraq:** [[person/yazid-ibn-al-muhallab|Yazid ibn al-Muhallab]] (all Iraq joined under him in 96 AH); then [[person/al-jarrah-ibn-abdallah-al-hakami|al-Jarrah ibn Abd Allah al-Hakami]] (Yazid's deputy when he went to Khurasan) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 317}
- **Basra:** Sufyan ibn Umayr al-Kindi (appointed by Yazid ibn al-Muhallab, replacing al-Jarrah, in 100 AH (so in the text)); then Abd Allah ibn Bilal al-Kilabi (then dismissed); then Marwan ibn al-Muhallab (until Sulayman died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Kufa:** Harmala ibn Umayr (confirmed by Yazid for some months, then dismissed); then Bishr ibn Hassan al-Mahri (then dismissed; al-Tabari has «Bashir ibn Hassan al-Mahdi»); then Sufyan ibn Harish al-Khawlani (until Sulayman died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Khurasan:** [[person/waki-ibn-abi-sud|Waki' ibn Abi Sud al-Ghudani]] (took charge after Qutayba was killed; dismissed by Yazid); then [[person/mukhallad-ibn-yazid|Makhlad ibn Yazid]] (son of Yazid ibn al-Muhallab, until Sulayman died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Sijistan:** Mudrik [ibn al-Muhallab] (Yazid's brother; then dismissed); then Mu'awiya ibn Yazid [ibn al-Muhallab] (Yazid's son, until Sulayman died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Sind:** [[person/salih-ibn-abd-al-rahman|Salih ibn Abd al-Rahman]] (Sulayman ordered him to audit the family of Abu Aqil); then [[person/habib-ibn-al-muhallab|Habib ibn al-Muhallab]] (over war, later over war and tax); then [[person/yazid-ibn-abi-kabsha|Yazid ibn Abi Kabsha]] (over the tax for less than a month, then died); then Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Kabsha (his brother's deputy, dismissed by Salih); then Imran ibn al-Nu'man al-Kala'i {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Bahrayn:** al-Ash'ath ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Jarud (appointed by Yazid ibn al-Muhallab, expelled by Mas'ud ibn Abi Zaynab in 96 AH) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Ifriqiya:** [[person/abdallah-ibn-musa-ibn-nusayr|Abd Allah ibn Musa ibn Nusayr]] (confirmed, then dismissed in 97 AH); then Muhammad ibn Yazid, mawla of Rayhana bint Abi al-As (some say, 97 AH) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 318}
- **Oman:** Abd al-Rahman ibn Qays al-Laythi (appointed by Salih ibn Abd al-Rahman); then Ziyad ibn al-Muhallab (appointed by his brother Yazid) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Yamama:** Sufyan ibn Amr al-Uqayli; then Nuh ibn Hubayra {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Armenia:** [[person/abd-al-aziz-ibn-hatim-al-bahili|Abd al-Aziz ibn Hatim ibn al-Nu'man]] (Armenia saw no campaign until Sulayman died) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Summer campaign:** Ayyub ibn Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik; then [[person/maslama-ibn-abd-al-malik|Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Police:** Ka'b ibn Hamid al-Absi {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Chancery (letters):** Layth ibn Abi Ruqayya, mawla of Umm al-Hakam bint Abi Sufyan {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Land tax and army:** [[person/sulayman-ibn-sad-al-khushani|Sulayman ibn Sa'd, mawla of Khushayn]] {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Seal:** Nu'aym ibn Abi Salama (a mawla of the people of Yemen) {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Treasuries and storehouses:** Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-Harith, mawla of Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Guard:** Khalid ibn al-Rayyan, mawla of Banu Muharib {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
- **Chamberlain:** Abu Ubayd, his mawla {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 319}
