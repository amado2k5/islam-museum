## Summary
Shaykh Faraz Rabbani (born 1974) is a Canadian scholar and teacher of Pakistani origin, founder and education director of SeekersGuidance (formerly SeekersHub) in Toronto, and one of the pioneers of structured online Islamic education and fatwa question services in the West. Trained in the Hanafi school by scholars in Syria and Jordan, he has spent three decades teaching Islamic law, spirituality and creed to a global English-speaking student body. {cite:themuslim500-faraz-rabbani} {cite:wikipedia-faraz-rabbani-en}

## Early life and education
Rabbani was born in Karachi, Pakistan, in 1974 and raised in Canada, where his family settled. After a period in business and industry — he has spoken of a deliberate decision to leave a commercial career for religious study — he travelled in the late 1990s to Damascus and then Amman to pursue the traditional Islamic sciences. In Jordan he became a close student of the American-born scholar Nuh Ha Mim Keller, studying Hanafi jurisprudence, legal methodology, theology and Sufi discipline, and receiving formal licences (ijazat) to teach and to answer legal questions. {cite:wikipedia-faraz-rabbani-en} {cite:themuslim500-faraz-rabbani}

## Teaching career in the West
Returning to Toronto, Rabbani began teaching in local mosques and homes and moved early onto the internet. He was among the founders of SunniPath, one of the first organised online academies offering graded courses in Islamic law and belief to Western Muslims, and he became known for answering legal and personal questions through online fatwa services — a format he later built into his own institutions. He also co-founded the StraightWay Ethical Advisory, a consultancy applying Islamic ethics to contemporary commercial and professional questions. {cite:wikipedia-faraz-rabbani-en} {cite:themuslim500-faraz-rabbani}

## SeekersGuidance
In Toronto, Rabbani helped launch SeekersHub, which grew into SeekersGuidance — a non-profit educational institution offering free online courses, a question-and-answer service staffed by trained scholars, and local programming. The organisation's model — traditional texts taught online without tuition fees, supported by donations — made it one of the most used Islamic education platforms in the English-speaking world, and Rabbani its principal public teacher. He continues to serve as its education director and senior instructor. {cite:wikipedia-faraz-rabbani-en} {cite:themuslim500-faraz-rabbani}

## Positions and debate
Rabbani teaches within the Sunni, specifically Hanafi, tradition and describes his orientation as mainstream Sunni scholarship with a strong devotional dimension. His public interventions have included early warnings against extremist radicalisation, and commentary on questions of citizenship and civic participation for Western Muslims. Observers also associate him with the loose scholarly current favouring Muslim-Christian-Jewish goodwill and, at times, Sunni–Shia rapprochement — a stance welcomed by some Sunni readers and criticised by others as blurring confessional lines; the museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-faraz-rabbani-en}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 lists him among the Preachers and Spiritual Guides on the strength of SeekersGuidance's reach. His own sources record his school as Hanafi, and this record follows them. {cite:themuslim500-faraz-rabbani}

## Reach and method
SeekersGuidance's catalogue grew from single-subject courses to a multi-level curriculum covering worship, family law, belief and spirituality, with a scholarly answers service handling questions submitted from dozens of countries. Rabbani's own teaching — patient textual exposition aimed at educated laypeople rather than specialists — became a model widely imitated by other Western institutes, and the institution he built remains the clearest example of the migration of traditional madrasa-style instruction onto the internet without abandoning its discipline. {cite:wikipedia-faraz-rabbani-en} {cite:themuslim500-faraz-rabbani}
