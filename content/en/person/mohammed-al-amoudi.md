## Summary
Sheikh Mohammed Hussein Ali Al-Amoudi is a Saudi-Ethiopian billionaire whose MIDROC conglomerate spans oil refining, mining, agriculture, construction and real estate across Sweden, Ethiopia and Saudi Arabia. For years ranked among Africa's richest men, he was detained in Saudi Arabia's 2017 anti-corruption purge and released after more than a year. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-al-amoudi}

## Formation
Born in 1946 in Dessie, in Ethiopia's Amhara region, to a Yemeni father and Ethiopian mother, Al-Amoudi emigrated to Saudi Arabia in the 1960s and built a fortune first in construction and property before expanding into energy and agriculture. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-al-amoudi}

## MIDROC
Through MIDROC — Mohammed International Development Research and Organization Companies — and its sister vehicles he assembled an empire including the Swedish refiner Preem, Moroccan oil-distribution interests, Ethiopian gold mines, vast agricultural projects such as the Saudi Star rice farms in Gambella, and landmark construction work in Saudi Arabia. He has also been a major private investor in Ethiopia, where his companies employ tens of thousands. {cite:muslim500-mohammed-al-amoudi}

## Detention and philanthropy
In November 2017 he was arrested with hundreds of Saudi princes and businessmen in the anti-corruption campaign centred on Riyadh's Ritz-Carlton hotel, and released in 2019 after reportedly reaching a settlement. His philanthropy — hospitals, universities and development projects, particularly in Ethiopia — is frequently cited in profiles. {cite:wikipedia-mohammed-al-amoudi}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as one of the great self-made fortunes of the Muslim world: an Ethiopian immigrant who built in Saudi Arabia and Sweden a conglomerate few private investors anywhere have matched — and whose 2017 detention became a symbol of the kingdom's upheaval. {cite:muslim500-mohammed-al-amoudi}
