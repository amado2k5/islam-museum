# Zeenat Mahal

Zeenat Mahal (1823–1886) was the last Mughal empress consort, the favoured wife of the emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar and, as Padshah Begum from 1840 to 1857, the de facto ruler of the empire {cite:wikipedia-zeenat-mahal-en}. She was born in 1823, at Delhi according to the Urdu article, and married the emperor there on 19 November 1840 {cite:wikipedia-zeenat-mahal-ur}{cite:wikidata-zeenat-mahal}.

She worked to have the emperor's older sons set aside so that her own son, Mirza Jawan Bakht, would be named heir apparent, and she was suspected of involvement in the poisoning of the British Resident Thomas Metcalfe in 1853 {cite:wikipedia-zeenat-mahal-en}. When the rebellion of 1857 came she kept Jawan Bakht away from the rebels, hoping to preserve his claim to the succession {cite:wikipedia-zeenat-mahal-en}.

After Delhi fell she was exiled with her husband to Rangoon, where she died on 17 July 1886; she was buried beside his tomb in Yangon, near the Shwedagon Pagoda {cite:wikipedia-zeenat-mahal-en}{cite:wikipedia-zeenat-mahal-ur}{cite:wikidata-zeenat-mahal}. No source read names a school or branch as her own, and the museum records none for her.
