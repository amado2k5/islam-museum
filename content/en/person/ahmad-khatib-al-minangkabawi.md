# Ahmad Khatib al-Minangkabawi

## Summary
Ahmad Khatib al-Minangkabawi (26 June 1860 – 13 March 1916, debated) was a Minangkabau scholar who rose to be imam and preacher of the Shafi'i school at the Grand Mosque of Mecca. From the sanctuary he taught a generation of Malay and Indonesian students — among them Ahmad Dahlan, Hasyim Asy'ari, Abdul Karim Amrullah and, in the Jeddah years, his nephew Agus Salim — and wrote in Shafi'i fiqh, doctrine and arithmetic. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-id}

## From Koto Tuo to Mecca
He was born at Koto Tuo in West Sumatra; the Indonesian article pairs his birth date with Monday, 6 Dhu al-Hijjah 1276 AH. After early schooling he travelled to Mecca, studied there, married into a scholarly household and was appointed to the Shafi'i imamate of the Grand Mosque, a post he held for the rest of his life. Students from the Malay world gathered around his teaching circle, and through them his books and positions travelled back to the Indies. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-id}

## Teaching, writings and faith
The English article, resting on modern scholarship, calls him an orthodox Sunni Muslim who headed the Shafi'i school at the Meccan mosque; his full name carries the nisba asy-Syafi'i, and his works include Shafi'i fiqh writing such as Ar-Riyadh al-Wardiyyah and a supercommentary on Fath al-Jawwad. The Indonesian article, an independent tradition, records that his students were generally taught Shafi'i fiqh by him and places him in Siradjuddin Abbas's Thabaqat al-Shafi'iyah, the biographical dictionary of Shafi'i scholars. On those two independent text traditions his record documents him as sunni-shafii; the office alone would not have sufficed, and the Ash'ari element of his name is not made a segment because the faith enum has no such compound. He also wrote against certain Naqshbandi practices — grave-visitation and the rabithah — which the articles present as criticism of practices, not as a Sufi affiliation of his own. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-en} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-id}

## Death date
The sources divide on his death. The English article's text, followed by its Arabic and Urdu translations and by the Indonesian article's lead, gives 9 October 1915 at Mecca. The Indonesian article's body text gives 13 March 1916, paired with 9 Jumada al-awwal 1334 AH; Wikidata carries 1916, and the English article's own category files him under 1916 deaths. His record follows the 1916 date and marks it debated, with both readings preserved. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-id} {cite:wikidata-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-khatib-al-minangkabawi-en}
