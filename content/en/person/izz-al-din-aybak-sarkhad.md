## Summary
'Izz al-Din Aybak, lord of Sarkhad; he died in early Jumada I 646 at his place of detention in Cairo and was buried outside Bab al-Nasr in the turbah of Shams al-Dawla — Ibn Khallikan attended the prayer and burial — then he was moved to his own turbah in the madrasa he had built outside Damascus on the upper rise overlooking the great Green Maydan. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Career in the Encyclopaedia of Islam
Izz al-Din Abu'l-Mansur Aybak al-Mu'azzami was a mamluk of al-Malik al-Mu'azzam Sharaf al-Din Isa, who was governor of Damascus (597-615 AH, 1200-1218) and then its sultan (615-624 AH, 1218-1227) after the death of his father al-Malik al-'Adil. In 608/1211-2 Aybak received the town of Salkhad in the Hawran, with the adjacent lands, as a fief, and was appointed majordomo (ustadh-dar). When al-Malik al-Nasir Dawud succeeded his father on the throne of Damascus, Aybak became regent and held the whole political administration. Shortly afterwards al-Malik al-Ashraf, Dawud's uncle, took Damascus; Aybak lost the regency but kept his fiefs in the Hawran. In 636/1238-9 he was still called lord of Salkhad and Zur'a. He was later suspected of treason and lost his standing; he died in Cairo in 646/1248-9, and his remains were taken to Damascus to the mausoleum built for him. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 829}

## Buildings
Aybak built for the districts under him, and as majordomo had charge of khans and fortresses. He erected three Hanafi academies in Damascus and one in Jerusalem. His works include a khan at Salkhad (611/1214-5), a tower in the fortress of Salkhad (617/1220-1), arcades and a minaret in the mosque of Salkhad (630/1232-3), a fort at Kal'at al-Azrak (634/1236-7), a khan at Zur'a (636/1238), a reservoir at Inak (636-637/1238-1240), and a mosque at al-'Ayn (638/1240-1). {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 829}
