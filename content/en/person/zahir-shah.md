## Summary
Mohammad Zahir Shah (15 October 1914 – 23 July 2007) was the last king of Afghanistan, reigning from 8 November 1933 until he was deposed on 17 July 1973. Ruling for almost forty years, he was the country's longest-serving ruler since the foundation of the Durrani Empire, and his reign is remembered as a long era of peace. After twenty-nine years of exile in Italy he returned in 2002 and held the title Father of the Nation until his death. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en}

## Early life and education
He was born in the Deh Afghanan quarter of Kabul into the Musahiban family of the Mohammadzai clan of the Barakzai tribe, the son of Mohammad Nadir Shah and Mah Parwar Begum. The Arabic article gives his birth as 16 October 1914, one day later than the English article's Britannica-backed date, which the museum follows. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-ar}

He was educated at Habibia and Amaniya (later Esteqlal) high schools in Kabul and at a military infantry school, and continued his education in France, studying at the Pasteur Institute and the University of Montpellier, before returning to help his father and uncles restore order. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en}

## Reign
He was proclaimed king on 8 November 1933, aged nineteen, after the assassination of his father. For the first twenty years he ceded effective power to his uncles Mohammad Hashim Khan and Shah Mahmud Khan as prime ministers. Afghanistan joined the League of Nations in 1934 and remained neutral in the Second World War. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en}

After the war he pursued modernisation: a first modern university was founded, and the 1964 constitution made Afghanistan a constitutional monarchy with free elections, a parliament and civil and political rights, including women's suffrage. He was considered a relatively lenient ruler who never signed a political execution warrant. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en}

## Deposition and exile
In July 1973, while he was in Italy for medical treatment, his cousin and former prime minister Mohammad Daoud Khan overthrew the monarchy and proclaimed a republic. From Rome he sent a letter abdicating, saying he respected the will of his compatriots. He lived near Rome for twenty-nine years, first in a small apartment on the Via Cassia and later in a villa in Olgiata, and was barred from returning by the communist governments and then by the wars that followed. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en}

## Return and death
On 18 April 2002, aged eighty-seven, he returned to Kabul and was welcomed by Afghans of all ethnic groups. At the Loya Jirga he accepted the role of head of state if demanded of him but renounced any restoration of the monarchy, and President Karzai gave him the title Father of the Nation. He died on 23 July 2007 in the Arg compound in Kabul, and was buried in the royal mausoleum on Maranjan Hill in eastern Kabul. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en}

## Personal life
He married Humaira Begum in 1931; she died in 2002, shortly after their return. They had eight children. He was known as a quiet, soft-spoken man who liked photography, chess and cigars. {cite:wikipedia-zahir-shah-en}
