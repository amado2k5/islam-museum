## Summary
Umm al-Aswad bint Zayd al-Adawiyya was a devout woman of Banu Adi in [[place/basra|Basra]]; Ibn al-Jawzi gives her entry in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]] (no. 591), noting that [[person/muadha-al-adawiyya|Mu'adha al-Adawiyya]] had nursed her. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.710}

## Mu'adha's charge
From Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami: Mu'adha told her, "Do not spoil my nursing of you by eating what is unlawful; I strove hard while nursing you to eat only what was lawful, so strive to eat only what is lawful, that you may be granted the service of your Lord and contentment with His decree." Umm al-Aswad used to say: "I never ate anything doubtful but that I missed an obligatory prayer or one of my regular devotions." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.710}

## A transmitter of Mu'adha's words
It is from her that Imran ibn Khalid reports Mu'adha's words after Abu al-Sahba' and her son were killed: that she did not love remaining in the world for its pleasures or its breezes, but to draw near to her Lord, hoping He would unite her with them in Paradise. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.705}

## For young visitors
Umm al-Aswad was a girl of Basra nursed by a devout woman named Mu'adha, who told her always to eat lawful food; she later said that doubtful food weakened her worship.
