Osman Đikić (7 January 1879 – 30 March 1912) was a Bosnian poet, dramatist and writer from Mostar who declared himself a Serb of the Islamic faith.{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-en} Expelled from the Mostar gymnasium for nationalist activity, he continued his schooling in Belgrade and Istanbul and graduated from the Trade Academy in Vienna, then worked as a bank clerk in Zagreb, Brčko and Mostar.{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-en}{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-hr} He edited the Mostar paper Musavat from 1907, moved to Sarajevo in 1909 as secretary of the Muslim cultural society Gajret and editor of its journal, and in 1910 founded his own political paper, Samouprava.{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-hr}

His collections Pobratimstvo (1900), Muslimanskoj mladeži (1902) and Ašiklije (1903) mix love, patriotic and religious themes, and he wrote the folk dramas Zlatija, Stana and Muhadžir and several sevdalinkas that are still sung.{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-en}{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-hr} As a folklorist he gathered the folk songs of Mostar and Stolac into the 5,000-verse Hercegovački biser and gave the collection to the Serbian Royal Academy.{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-en} He died of tuberculosis in Mostar on 30 March 1912, aged thirty-three; a brick türbe designed by Aleksandar Deroko was built for his remains in 1936 beside the Karagöz Bey Mosque, destroyed in the Second World War and again damaged in 1992 before being restored.{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-en}{cite:wikipedia-osman-dikic-hr}{cite:wikidata-osman-dikic}
