# Ebüzziya Tevfik

Ebüzziya Mehmed Tevfik (1849–1913) was an Ottoman writer, publisher and calligrapher, a Young Ottoman whose press shaped Tanzimat-era letters. {cite:wikipedia-ebuzziya-tevfik-en}

## Formation and the Young Ottomans

Born at Sultanahmet, Istanbul, on 17 February 1849, the son of a finance-ministry scribe, he lost his father at eight, entered the same office, and educated himself with private tutors. Through Namık Kemal and İbrahim Şinasi he joined the Young Ottomans, and after Şinasi's death the Tasvîr-i Efkâr press passed into his sole custody. His part in the Vatan yahut Silistre affair of 1873 brought exile to Rhodes, where, barred from publishing under his own name, he began signing "Ebüzziya" — "father of Ziya", after his son. {cite:wikipedia-ebuzziya-tevfik-en}{cite:wikipedia-ebuzziya-tevfik-tr}

## Publisher and parliamentarian

From his Matbaa-i Ebüzziya press (1882) he issued the journal Mecmua-i Ebüzziya, almanacs, a Turkish dictionary, and the first Western-style anthology of Ottoman literature, and practised Kufic calligraphy, including the band of the Yıldız Hamidiye Mosque. Exiled to Konya in 1900, he returned at the 1908 revolution and sat in parliament for Antalya. He died at Istanbul on 27 January 1913, the day he last issued Tasvir-i Efkâr, and was buried at Bakırköy Cemetery. {cite:wikipedia-ebuzziya-tevfik-en}{cite:wikipedia-ebuzziya-tevfik-tr}
