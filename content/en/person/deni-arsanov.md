# Deni Arsanov

Deni Arsanov (1851 – 27 December 1917), a Chechen of the Engenoy teip from Zebir-Yurt, was a Sufi sheikh and the founder of one of the largest Vainakh brotherhoods (virds), belonging to the Naqshbandi current of Sufi Islam {cite:wikipedia-deni-arsanov-ru}. No encyclopaedia article on him exists in English; this entry is written from the Russian article.

## Sheikh and peacemaker

In his youth Arsanov was an abrek and took part in the 1877 uprising of Alibek-Khadzhi Aldamov {cite:wikipedia-deni-arsanov-ru}. He founded an Arabic school at Grozny in 1904, kept houses open to the poor at Grozny, Gekhi and Urus-Martan, and was known for reconciling feuding parties {cite:wikipedia-deni-arsanov-ru}. At the Chechen congresses of 1917 he was a leading clerical delegate; the district commissar Eldarkhanov yielded his post to him {cite:wikipedia-deni-arsanov-ru}.

## Death

When Cossack–Chechen clashes in Grozny in December 1917 threatened civil war, Arsanov rode in with 53 murids on a peacemaking mission. On 27 December 1917, at the Groznenskaya stanitsa, Cossacks demanded that he and his murids disarm; he refused, and in the ensuing fight the sheikh and more than thirty of his murids were killed {cite:wikipedia-deni-arsanov-ru}.

## Faith

The Russian article — the only encyclopaedia article on Arsanov in any language — names him in its lead as founder of a brotherhood of the Naqshbandi current, and the scholarship it cites is devoted to him as a Naqshbandi sheikh {cite:wikipedia-deni-arsanov-ru}; Wikidata carries no religion value {cite:wikidata-deni-arsanov}. Because the lane records an affiliation only on two independent texts, his faith segment is recorded as unknown, with the Naqshbandi reading preserved here.
