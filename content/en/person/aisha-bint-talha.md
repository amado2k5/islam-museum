## Overview
'A'isha bint Talha ibn 'Ubaydallah al-Taymiyya, daughter of the Mother of the Faithful 'A'isha's sister Umm Kulthum bint al-Siddiq; her cousin 'Abdallah ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr married her, then after him the amir of Iraq Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr, who gave her a dower of a hundred thousand dinars. She was the most beautiful and noblest woman of her time, and her hadith is included in the Sahih collections. She died around 110 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.2077}

## In Kahhala's A'lam al-Nisa'
Kahhala presents her as A'isha bint Talha ibn Ubayd Allah al-Taymiyya, whose mother was Umm Kulthum bint Abi Bakr al-Siddiq, among the rarest women of her age in beauty and refinement. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.141} He cites *al-Aghani*, from Ibn Ishaq from his father, that she did not screen herself from men, sitting and receiving them as a man would, and that Mus'ab reproached her for it. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.141} At the end of the entry he records her as a hadith transmitter: a group narrated from her, among them her nephew Talha ibn Yahya, Ata' ibn Abi Rabah and al-Minhal ibn Amr, and the six collections carry her reports; Yahya ibn Ma'in called her "trustworthy, a proof", al-Ijli "a Medinan, a Successor, trustworthy", Abu Zur'a al-Dimashqi said people narrated from her for her merit and refinement, and Ibn Hibban listed her among the trustworthy. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.158} He relates that she was learned in the reports, poetry and battle-days of the Arabs and in the stars; visiting Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik, she held her own with the Umayyad elders on every such topic and named every star as it rose or set, saying she had learned the stars from her aunt A'isha, and Hisham gave her a hundred thousand dirhams. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.158} He says she died after the year 100-odd. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.158}
