## Summary
Abu Umar Muhammad ibn al-Abbas ibn Muhammad ibn Zakariyya ibn Hayyuya al-Baghdadi al-Khazzaz was a hadith transmitter who narrated Ibn al-Mubarak's [[work/kitab-al-zuhd-ibn-al-mubarak|Kitab al-Zuhd]] from [[person/yahya-ibn-muhammad-ibn-saaid|Yahya ibn Sa'id]]; through him the book passed to al-Jawhari and then to the narrators of the surviving copy. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 304}

## Standing and death
[[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] quotes Ibn al-Imad calling him "the hadith scholar, the authority", who transmitted from al-Baghandi, Abd Allah ibn Ishaq al-Mada'ini and their generation; al-Khatib said he was trustworthy, wrote all his life and transmitted the great compilations; he died in Rabi' al-Akhir 382 aged eighty-seven. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 101}

## His transmission of Kitab al-Zuhd
Most reports in the book open with the narrator's words "Abu Umar ibn Hayyuya and Abu Bakr al-Warraq informed you", that is, he and [[person/abu-bakr-al-warraq-al-mustamli|Abu Bakr al-Warraq]]. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 2} The opening of part 7 says al-Jawhari read the book to him in Rabi' al-Awwal 382, the year of his death. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 304}

## For young visitors
Ibn Hayyuya was a hadith scholar of Baghdad who spent his life writing down hadith, and he passed Kitab al-Zuhd on to those who came after him.

## Notes for researchers
- In the footnote to the first report the editor holds that "al-Warraq" in the first chain should read "al-Khazzaz", i.e. Ibn Hayyuya. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1}
