## Summary
A Companion who had settled in [[place/mecca|Mecca]] as an ally without roots or clan, having children and family there {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}. When the Prophet resolved to march on Mecca he wrote to Quraysh to tell them; the bearer of [[work/hatib-letter-to-quraysh|his letter]] was overtaken and it was drawn from the coils of her hair — and in the account the Prophet accepted his excuse and mentioned his presence at Badr {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/40-41}.

## The letter
Hatib wrote to Quraysh telling them the Prophet had resolved to march on them, and gave the letter to a woman who set it in the coils of her hair and went out {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/40}. The news came from heaven; the Prophet sent Ali, al-Zubayr and al-Miqdad, who overtook her at [[place/al-khaliqa|al-Khaliqa]] and drew out the letter {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

## The excuse and the pardon
Umar said: let me strike off this hypocrite's head, Messenger of God {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}. Hatib excused himself: there was in me no disbelief nor apostasy — but I am a man with no roots nor clan among the people; my children and family are among them, so I did a thing by which they would protect my family {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

The Prophet told Umar and Abu Bakr: "believe Hatib", then said: "and how do you know, Umar — perhaps God has looked upon the people of Badr and said: do as you wish, I have forgiven you" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}. In the account, the opening of [[passage/quran-60-1|Surat al-Mumtahana]] descended on his affair {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

## For young visitors
A man of Medina's people who feared for his children still in Mecca, so he wrote to its people telling them the army was coming. His letter was discovered; he explained his reason to the Prophet, who accepted his excuse and remembered that he had been one of the people of Badr.
