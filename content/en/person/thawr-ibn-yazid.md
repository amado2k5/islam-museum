## Overview
Thawr ibn Yazid al-Kala'i al-Himsi was a hadith transmitter and jurist whom al-Dhahabi calls the scholar of Homs {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. The sign printed with his entry shows that al-Bukhari and the four Sunan compilers recorded his hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Khalid ibn Ma'dan, Rashid ibn Sa'd, Ata ibn Abi Rabah, Habib ibn Ubayd, Nafi', al-Zuhri, Amr ibn Shu'ayb and others {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. Ibn Ishaq, Sufyan al-Thawri, al-Mu'afa ibn Imran, Ibn al-Mubarak, al-Walid ibn Muslim, Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan, Baqiyya ibn al-Walid, Khalid ibn al-Harith, Abu Asim al-Nabil and others narrated from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. His hadith appears with a short chain in al-Bukhari {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}.

## What the critics said
Yahya al-Qattan said he had seen no Syrian more reliable than Thawr and that he wrote from him in Mecca on tablets; Waki' said Thawr was the most devout man he had seen; Isa ibn Yunus counted him among the most firm; Yahya ibn Ma'in and others called him reliable; Ibn Adi said they declared him reliable and that he saw nothing wrong in his hadith; Abu Hatim called him truthful and a memoriser {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. Al-Dhahabi writes that he was a vessel of knowledge were it not for his innovation {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}.

## The charge of holding qadar
Al-Dhahabi quotes Ahmad that Thawr held the doctrine of qadar, though there was no harm in him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. A story is told that al-Awza'i refused to give him his hand, saying that it was religion, not worldly closeness {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. Isma'il ibn Ayyash says Asad ibn Wada'a banished Thawr, and Abd Allah ibn Salim says they drove him out and burned his house for what he said about qadar {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. Al-Dhahabi judged him devout and pious and thought he had apparently withdrawn the view, citing a report from Abu Zur'a through Munabbih ibn Uthman of his answer to a man who called him a qadari {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}. The museum gives each judgement to the one who made it and decides between none of them.

## His death
Ibn Sa'd and Khalifa say he died in 153 AH, Yahya ibn Bukayr says 155; Ibn Sa'd adds that he died at Bayt al-Maqdis {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص1275}.
