## Brief
Umm Salama — Hind bint Abi Umayya — was a widow of the early Muslim migrations who became a wife of the Prophet. Tradition remembers her intelligence, her counsel at Hudaybiyya, and the hundreds of hadith narrated on her authority.

## Life
She and her first husband, Abu Salama, were among the early Muslims who fled Meccan persecution to Abyssinia in [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia]] {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. Back in Mecca, the couple's move to Medina was blocked by her family — the sira remembers her as the first woman to emigrate with her child — and after Abu Salama died of wounds from [[event/battle-of-uhud]], she married the Prophet about 626 {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

At [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya]] in 628, when the pilgrims were turned back short of Mecca and the men hesitated to abandon the pilgrimage rites, tradition tells that it was Umm Salama who advised the Prophet to act first himself — the men followed {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

She outlived the Prophet by nearly five decades in Medina, answering questions of law and ritual; several hundred hadith stand in her name in the Sunni collections {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Her preserved reports shaped daily Muslim practice — and her role at Hudaybiyya is still cited when Muslims talk about counsel, marriage and who may advise whom.

## For young visitors
When everyone was stuck and upset on a journey, Umm Salama gave the Prophet the advice that worked. People came to her for answers for the rest of her long life.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh relates that the Prophet consoled Umm Salama on the death of her husband Abu Salama and taught her to pray that God reward her in her loss and replace it with something better; she wondered who could be better than Abu Salama, then submitted to God's command, and when her waiting period ended the Prophet married her. He tells it as an example that good may come in what one dislikes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep06 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep06 00:08:30} See: [[concept/al-minha-fi-al-mihna|the gift within the trial]].

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Ish al-Lahza'
[[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] tells the story of Umm Salama's emigration in the nineteenth episode of his programme [[work/ich-allahda|'Ish al-Lahza']], on the moment of oppression: Abu Salama, Umm Salama and their son, whom he names Salama, were among the first emigrants, he says; Banu al-Mughira, Umm Salama's people, held back their daughter, and Banu Abd al-Asad, Abu Salama's people, took the child, so Abu Salama emigrated alone. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep19 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep19 00:21:30} For a whole year, he says, she went out every day to the place where they had been separated and prayed to God, until a man of her husband's people took pity on her, her son was returned to her and she emigrated to her husband. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep19 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep19 00:22:00} He makes her an example of one who, under oppression, has nothing but prayer. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep19 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep19 00:22:00}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the second episode of his programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]] to Umm Salama. He names her Hind bint Abi Umayya, called after her son Salama, and her first husband Abu Salama Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Asad, whose mother Barra bint Abd al-Muttalib was the Prophet's aunt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:01:00} Both believed on hearing the Qur'an from the Prophet, and he counts them among the first emigrants to Abyssinia, "in one report among the first ten". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:03:00} They returned to Mecca when they heard that Umar and Hamza had become Muslim, but the persecution grew worse. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:03:30}

At the emigration to Medina, in his telling, her own clan held her back, her husband's clan took the child Salama, who had been born in Abyssinia, and Abu Salama went on alone. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:05:30} She went out each day to weep on the hills near Mecca until a relative shamed her family; they let her go and her son was given back to her. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:06:30} She set out alone on a camel with the child, and Uthman ibn Talha led her camel all the way to Medina, kneeling it and turning his back whenever she mounted or dismounted; she remembered him as the best of companions. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:09:00}

He says Abu Salama fought at Badr and Uhud, was badly wounded at Uhud, led a raid against Banu Asad some four months later, and died when the wound reopened. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:10:30} Before his death he taught her the prayer said at a calamity, asking God for something better in its place, and she wondered who could be better than Abu Salama. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:12:00} Umar and others proposed and she declined; when the Prophet proposed, she named four obstacles, no guardian in Medina, her age, her strong jealousy and her children, and he answered each one. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:15:00} He says her young son gave her away in marriage. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:15:30}

He tells how at al-Hudaybiya, when the Companions did not rise at the order to leave the state of ihram, she advised the Prophet to go out, speak to no one, shave and sacrifice, and how they all followed him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:23:00} He draws from it that a woman's feeling does not hold back sound judgement, and he recalls the episode already in his opening lecture. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep01 00:06:00} He also relates that she asked why women were so rarely mentioned in the Qur'an, and that a verse then named believing men and believing women side by side. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:24:30} In his account she narrated more than three hundred hadiths, most of them on the Prophet's life at home, and spent her later years raising her children and teaching. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:26:00} In other episodes he shows her rebuking Umar for meddling between the Prophet and his wives, and speaking for the other wives about the gifts brought on Aisha's day. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep10 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep21 00:20:00} He gives her death as 59 AH, under Mu'awiya, at the age of eighty-four. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep02 00:26:30}
