# Asma' Harun

## Summary

Asma' Harun is a Malaysian preacher and author, one of the country's two most-followed celebrity ustazahs in ISEAS's 2025 study, with 1.1 million Instagram followers. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs} A graduate of Al-Azhar University in Egypt, she worked at the Negeri Sembilan State Islamic Religious Department from 2008 to 2017 and leads the Perkampungan Al-Hijrah (Hijrah Village) boarding complex and the Sahabat Hijrah community. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs}

## Family and early life

Asma' Harun descends from a scholarly family in Terengganu: her grandfather was the scholar Awang Abu Bakar and her great-grandfather was Haji Abbas. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs} Her early education was at a PASTI preschool founded by the Islamist party PAS, followed by a school in Kelantan founded by PAS leader Nik Aziz Nik Mat. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs} She studied at Al-Azhar University in Egypt. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs}

## Career

Asma' Harun worked at the Negeri Sembilan State Islamic Religious Department from 2008, resigning in 2017 to preach full-time, according to the ISEAS study. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs} Through her short clips and online lessons she built a wide audience in Malaysia and beyond, with a following in neighbouring Singapore, where she is invited to Islamic festivals. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs}

## Projects and initiatives

She leads Perkampungan Al-Hijrah (Hijrah Village), a modern boarding complex built on the pondok model, and the Sahabat Hijrah community, which collects monthly donations (infaq) supporting her work. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs} Through her platforms she also markets tahfiz and pondok schools and product lines she endorses. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs}

## Positions and criticism

What follows are positions attributed to their sources, not verdicts of the museum. The ISEAS study describes the celebrity-ustazah model as blending preaching with commerce — what critics call penunggang agama, "selling religion" — and records that Asma' Harun criticised women wearing perfume and then promoted a "Wangian Kasturi" perfume, an inconsistency her followers pointed out. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs} The study also records her defence of promoting "The Noor" sandals, her public criticism of the singer Ifa Raziah's dress after umrah, her "10 reasons" campaign against attending a Coldplay concert, and the study's reading of her content as indirectly encouraging votes for a scholar-led party. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs}

## Books

Her books include Kembara Menuju Allah ("Journey Towards Allah") and 150 Tip Menjadi Wanita Dambaan Syurga ("150 Tips to Become a Woman Longed for in Paradise"). {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs}

## Influence and audience

The ISEAS study places Asma' Harun and Norhafizah Musa at the head of Malaysia's celebrity ustazahs as "micro-celebrities" who combine preaching with personal brand-building, with the dated figure of 1.1 million Instagram followers for Asma' Harun at the time of the study. {cite:source/iseas-perspective-2025-81-ustazahs}
