## Summary
Khaled El-Enany is an Egyptian Egyptologist elected in 2025 Director-General of UNESCO — the first Arab and first Muslim to lead the organisation in its eight-decade history. He came to the office from the academic field of Egyptology and from running Egypt's antiquities portfolio as Minister of Tourism and Antiquities. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-el-enany}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1971 in Giza, studied Egyptology at the Faculty of Archaeology of Cairo University where he rose through the academic ranks to professor of Egyptology, and earned his doctorate in conjunction with Paul Valéry University in Montpellier, France, spending years as a visiting researcher and teacher in European institutions. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-el-enany}

## Academic and ministerial career
He chaired Egyptology departments at Helwan University, worked on restoration and heritage-documentation projects, then was appointed Minister of Antiquities in 2016 in the government of Sherif Ismail; in 2019 the portfolio was merged with tourism and he served as Minister of Tourism and Antiquities until 2022. His tenure oversaw the phased opening of the Grand Egyptian Museum, the reopening of the Avenue of Sphinxes in Luxor and the Golden Parade transferring the royal mummies to the National Museum of Egyptian Civilisation — events that drew global media coverage. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-el-enany} {cite:muslim500-khaled-el-enany}

## At UNESCO
In October 2025 the UNESCO General Conference chose El-Enany as Director-General, succeeding Audrey Azoulay, and he took office in November 2025 for a four-year term amid wide Arab and African welcome as the first director from the Arab world and the African-Islamic region. He announced educational and cultural priorities including strengthening global education, protecting endangered heritage and adapting to artificial-intelligence transformations, according to the organisation's statements and his remarks. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-el-enany}

## Recognition
He has received decorations from several governments for heritage service, including French, Italian and Polish honours, and appears in rankings of influential Arab figures. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-el-enany}

## Legacy
His arrival at the head of UNESCO reads as a representational first — an Arab Muslim scholar leading the leading international body for culture and education — and he is credited in Egypt with building bridges between archaeology, public policy and cultural diplomacy; his term will be measured by his ability to safeguard a multi-crisis heritage in an age of conflict and technological change. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-el-enany}
