## Overview
[[person/al-mutawakkil|Al-Mutawakkil ala Allah]], an Abbasid caliph, given the allegiance of the elite at the moment of al-Wathiq's death, and then the allegiance of the common people at the decline of the sun. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part3 scan 98}

## The allegiance
Al-Tabari says that the allegiance of the elite to al-Mutawakkil was at the moment of al-Wathiq's death, and that the common people gave him the allegiance at the decline of the sun. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part3 scan 98}

## His sons and the offices
Al-Mutawakkil appointed his two sons to the posts and districts named in the book, among them prayer, the aides, the judgeship, the grievances and the land-tax. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part3 scan 126}

## Movements
He went out to al-Mada'in in Jumada al-Ula, after the affair of Ibn al-Bu'ayth had ended. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part3 scan 110}

## His secretaries and men
He made Ubayd Allah ibn Yahya ibn Khaqan his secretary. He sent his son al-Mu'tazz to visit Ishaq ibn Ibrahim in his illness, together with [[person/bugha-al-shirabi|Bugha al-Shirabi]]. {cite:tabari-khayyat-part3 scan 135} {cite:tabari-khayyat-part3 scan 131}

## Notes for researchers
- The events here are from the first volume of al-Tabari (the third part in Khayyat's edition), extracted by reading the specified pages. Most of the material has not been put in chronological order because the years are not readable in the scanned text.
- Some reports on garbled pages were left out, among them page 188 (his question to Bugha about the affair), and the page images need to be checked.
- References are to scan page numbers, not to the printed pagination.
- The entry is very partial; a full biography of his caliphate was not written because the other pages have not been read.

## For Sprouts
Al-Mutawakkil ala Allah was an Abbasid caliph, acclaimed in a private pledge at the hour of al-Wathiq's death then by the public at midday. He gave his sons charge of the works including prayer, the guard, justice, grievances and the kharaj, and went out to al-Mada'in after the Ibn al-Ba'ith affair ended.
