## Summary
[[person/umm-sulaym|Umm Sulaym]] bint Milhan al-Ansariyya, mother of [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], servant of [[person/muhammad|the Messenger of God]]; she was famous by her kunya {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2006}. Her name is disputed: said to be Suhayla, Rumayla, Rumaytha, Malika, or al-Ghumaysa' / al-Rumaysa' {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2006}.

## Her Islam and marriage
In the Jahiliyya she married Malik ibn al-Nadr and bore Anas; she became Muslim with the Ansar's forerunners, Malik grew angry and went out to Syria and died there, and she married [[person/abu-talha-al-ansari|Abu Talha]] after him {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2006}.

## Her dower: Islam
Ahmad relates from Anas that Abu Talha proposed to Umm Sulaym before he was Muslim, and she said: "Abu Talha, do you not know your god you worship grows from the earth? ... are you not ashamed to worship a tree? If you become Muslim, I want no dower from you but it" — he came and bore the two testimonies, and she said to Anas: "marry Abu Talha" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2006}. In Ibn Sa'd's account: "her dower was Islam" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}.

## The Prophet in her house
Anas relates that the Prophet used to visit Umm Sulaym and she would offer him what she made for him; he entered no house but Umm Sulaym's apart from his wives' — when it was remarked on he said: "I have mercy on her: her brother and her father were killed with me" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}. Ibn Hajar comments that his entering Umm Haram's house too is because the two sisters lived in one dwelling, and that she used to campaign with the Messenger of God {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}.

## The dagger on the day of Hunayn
Ibn Sa'd narrates with a sound chain that Umm Sulaym took a dagger on the day of Hunayn; Abu Talha said: "Messenger of God, here is Umm Sulaym with a dagger" — she said: "I took it so that if one of the polytheists came near me I would rip his belly" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}.

## Her son's death
In the Sahih: a son of hers by Abu Talha died, and she told the household not to inform Abu Talha before her; when he came and asked she said "he is the calmest he has been" — he thought the boy had recovered; then she adorned herself for him and he slept with her. In the morning she said: "count your son among God's rewards" — he told the Prophet, who said: "may God bless you two in your night" — and she bore Abdullah ibn Abi Talha, and was granted children of whom ten complete Qur'an-reciters read the Qur'an {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}.

## Anas's service
In the Sahih: when the Prophet arrived, Umm Sulaym said: "Messenger of God, here is Anas to serve you" — he was ten years old — and he served the Prophet from his arrival at Medina until he died {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}.

## Narration
She related several hadiths from the Prophet; from her related her son Anas, Ibn Abbas, Zayd ibn Thabit and Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}. Ibn Hajar mentions that Abu Umar took her lineage from Ibn al-Sakan but gave her mother's name as Malika, while Ibn al-Sakan's book has Aniqa — Ibn Fathun pointed this out {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 7)

The Sheikh relates that the Prophet said that when he entered paradise he saw al-Rumaysa in front of him, who is Umm Sulaym, and that Abu Talha came to her as a suitor {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:20:30}. He says that Umm Sulaym told Abu Talha that he was a sensible man who worshipped an idol he carved with his own hands and prostrated to, and the Sheikh comments that whoever knows everything but does not know God is as if he knows nothing {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:21:00}. He mentions that Umm Sulaym told Abu Talha that a man like him is not turned down, and when he asked her what she wanted, she said that if he accepted Islam, that would be her dowry {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:29:00}.

He recounts that Abu Talha accepted Islam, did well in his Islam and married Umm Sulaym, and the Sheikh says that the Prophet saw al-Rumaysa in paradise in front of him and was proud of his Companions as a teacher is proud of his pupils {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:29:30}.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In episode 18 of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] introduces a woman of the Ansar whose son served the Prophet, describing her as a summit of patience and striving, intelligent and wise. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:01:30} He notes that scholars differ on her name: al-Ghumaysa', al-Rumaysa' or al-Rumaytha' bint Milhan, Umm Sulaym al-Ansariyya. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:02:00} In his account, when Islam appeared she was the wife of Malik ibn al-Nadr, an unbeliever, and was about forty when some of the people of Medina embraced Islam; she embraced it on hearing [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]], whom the Prophet had sent to Medina to call people to it. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:02:30} She called her husband, but his heart did not open to it, and she taught her little son [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]] the two testimonies while her husband complained that she was corrupting his son; she argued with him that he worshipped a tree trunk carved by an Abyssinian carpenter, and he objected that the Prophet forbade wine, which he could not do without. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:03:30} Then Malik went trading to Syria, was killed by highwaymen and died an unbeliever, as he says. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:04:00}

He relates that after her waiting period men sought her hand, among them a relative of hers, Zayd ibn Sahl, [[person/abu-talha-al-ansari|Abu Talha al-Ansari]], who was then an unbeliever; she said that such a man is not turned away, but she was a believer and he was not, and if he embraced Islam that would be her dowry, and he did; he attributes the report to the Sunan of [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]] from Anas. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:04:30} This agrees with what this record relates from al-Isaba, that her dowry was Islam. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2006} He draws from it that hers was the most precious of dowries and that every good deed of Abu Talha counts in her favour, citing the hadith that one who guides to good is like the one who does it. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:05:00} He says that Umm Sulaym is counted among the Prophet's maternal aunts by fosterage, a kinship that bars marriage, and that the Prophet used to visit her and she would cook for him; he relates from Anas the story of his little brother Abu Umayr, who grieved over the death of his pet bird, and how the Prophet would joke with him and ask him about the "nughayr", drawing from it that the Prophet had a refined humour and a gentle spirit. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:07:00}

He relates that Abu Talha travelled while his beloved son was ill, and the boy died; she washed him, shrouded him and buried him, and asked that no one tell Abu Talha before she did; when he returned and asked about the boy she said he was calmer than he had been, served him supper and adorned herself for him, and he lay with her. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:08:00} At dawn she put to him the parable of a loan that its owners take back, then told him of his son's death and urged him to be patient; he complained of her to the Prophet, who prayed for blessing on their night. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:09:00} She conceived that night, and in his account labour came on nine months later as they were returning with the Prophet from a campaign near Medina; Abu Talha prayed that he might enter with the Prophet, and her pains eased until they had entered Medina, where she gave birth. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:10:30} She sent the newborn with Anas to the Prophet before he had nursed; the Prophet chewed an 'ajwa date of Medina and rubbed the baby's palate with it, the baby smacked his lips, and the Prophet laughed, mentioning the Ansar's love of dates, and named him Abdallah. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:11:30} He reports a narrator as saying that from this Abdallah came nine children, every one of whom knew the Qur'an by heart, through the blessing of her patience and the Prophet's prayer; the number in the report from al-Isaba given in this record is ten. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:13:00} {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007} He turns the story into advice to women on patience in bereavement and on holding back the news until the husband is at rest, and mentions the hadith that a wailing woman who does not repent will be raised on the Day of Resurrection wearing a garment of tar. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:13:30}

He relates that when the Prophet came to Medina and she found nothing to give him, she offered him her son Anas as a servant, which in his view raised Anas's standing; and from Anas, that the Prophet never reproached him over anything he did or left undone throughout his service, except once, when he sent him on an errand and he lingered playing with the boys, and the Prophet took him gently by the earlobe and told him to go. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:15:00} He relates that Anas was once late on an errand for the Prophet, and when his mother asked him he refused to disclose the secret and she approved; he told the story to a Successor sixty years later and still did not tell him the secret; he draws from it that a mother teaches her children to keep a confidence. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:22:00} He relates that the Prophet napped at their home on a hot day and Umm Sulaym gathered his sweat in a flask to add to her perfume, saying it was the finest of scents; that he drank from the mouth of a waterskin and she cut off its mouth and kept it; and that when he shaved his head in the year of [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|al-Hudaybiyya]] he gave half of it to Abu Talha, and they kept it for blessing; he describes this as legitimate seeking of blessing. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:17:00}

He counts her among the women who went out on campaign: she went with the Prophet to tend the wounded and give water to the thirsty, was present at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] and [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]], and carried waterskins with [[person/aisha|Aisha]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:17:30} He relates that on the day of Hunayn she carried a dagger, and when the Prophet asked her about it she said she had taken it to rip open the belly of any polytheist who came near her, and the Prophet laughed with pleasure; the report is in al-Isaba from Ibn Sa'd, as this record relates. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:18:30} {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2007} As the proof of her glad tidings he gives the hadith in [[work/sahih-muslim|Sahih Muslim]] from Anas that the Prophet entered Paradise, heard footsteps and found it was al-Ghumaysa' bint Milhan, and he notes that in another report he heard footsteps and it was [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:19:00} He lists her family: her brother [[person/haram-ibn-milhan|Haram ibn Milhan]], killed at [[event/incident-of-bir-mauna|Bi'r Ma'una]]; her son [[person/al-bara-ibn-malik|al-Bara' ibn Malik]], martyred in the conquests under Umar; her son Anas, one of the great scholars among the Companions; and her sister [[person/umm-haram-bint-milhan|Umm Haram]], who was also given the glad tidings of Paradise; and he says she lived on in Medina after the Prophet, steadfast to her pledge, until she died. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep18 00:21:00}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Immortal Women"
The second half of the third episode of [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]]'s programme [[work/nesaa-khalidat-program|"Immortal Women" (Nisa' Khalidat)]] is given to Umm Sulaym bint Milhan of the Ansar. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:15:00} He says she became Muslim while her husband Malik ibn al-Nadr was a polytheist away in al-Sham; on his return he accused her of abandoning her religion, and she answered that she had become Muslim. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:15:30} She taught her small son Anas the two testimonies over his father's objection, and Malik died a disbeliever soon after. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:16:00}

When Abu Talha, still a polytheist, proposed, she asked him how a man of sense could worship a tree carved by a craftsman and that fire could burn, and offered to marry him with no dower but his Islam; he accepted, and he says that "they say" no dower was ever nobler. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:18:30} The Prophet often visited the household and rested there at midday, and she gathered his sweat into her perfume. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:19:30} At her request he prayed for Anas to be blessed in wealth and children, and Anas later said he became the richest of the Ansar and saw a hundred and twenty of his own descendants. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:21:00}

He tells the story of her small son Abu Umayr, whose little bird the Prophet joked about. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:21:30} When the boy fell ill and died while Abu Talha was away at his farm, she kept the news from her husband that night and told him in the morning; the Prophet prayed for blessing on their night, and a son, Abd Allah, was born, who in turn had ten sons who all memorised the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:26:00} She also brought Anas to serve the Prophet, and Anas became one of the great narrators of hadith. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:27:30} He closes with the Prophet's dream of hearing her footsteps in Paradise, which he says Imam Muslim narrates. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan nesaa-khalidat/ep03 00:28:00}
