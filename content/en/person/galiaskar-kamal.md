# Galiaskar Kamal

Ğaliäsğar Kamal (Galiaskar Kamal; 6 January 1879 – 16 June 1933) was a Tatar writer, dramatist and playwright, regarded as the founder of modern Tatar drama and a classic of Tatar literature. He was born in Kazan in the family of a furrier craftsman, spent part of his childhood in his mother's village of Nizhnyaya Masra, and studied in the Kazan madrasas Ğosmaniyä and Möxämmädiyä in 1889–1897, alongside a Russian municipal school.{cite:wikipedia-galiaskar-kamal-en}{cite:wikipedia-galiaskar-kamal-ru}

His first play, "The Unlucky Youth", was published in 1900, and Tatar theatre history is counted from the staging of his play "The Pitiful". He worked actively in the Säyyär troupe as both actor and playwright, and after 1905, impressed by the first Russian revolution, his writing widened in scope with plays such as "The First Theater" and "The Bankrupt".{cite:wikipedia-galiaskar-kamal-en}

In June 1923 he was awarded the title Hero of Labour, and soon after that of People's Dramatist of the Tatar ASSR. He died at Kazan in 1933 and was buried in the city's Bratskoye cemetery. The leading Tatar theatre in Kazan, the Tatar Academic Theatre, bears his name today.{cite:wikipedia-galiaskar-kamal-ru}{cite:wikipedia-galiaskar-kamal-tt}

The sources give his birth in dual-dated form, 25 December 1878 by the Julian calendar, carried here as 6 January 1879; for his death the Russian article's infobox, the Tatar article and Wikidata agree on 16 June 1933, while the Russian article's lead gives 8 June, an outlier not followed. None of the sources names a religion or school for him — no article has a religion field and Wikidata carries no religion value — and his madrasa schooling is formation, not attestation, so his faith is recorded as unknown.
