## Overview
Uthman ibn Muhammad ibn Azzuz, that is Abd al-Aziz, ibn Ahmad al-Hintati al-Hafsi, Abu Amr, was one of the kings of the Hafsid state at Tunis. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 821 AH, 1418 CE, and his death as 893 AH, 1488 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 213}. Al-Hintati refers to Hintata, one of the tribes of the Maghreb {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 213}.

## Life and reign
He received the oath of allegiance after the death of his brother al-Muntasir, Muhammad ibn Muhammad, in 839 AH, and took the title al-Mutawakkil ala Allah {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 213}. His mother, named Maryam, belonged to the people called al-Uluj; once he was acknowledged, her kinsmen came to him and he settled them in the suburb next to the Qasba, which has been known ever since as Hawmat al-Uluj {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 213}. Al-Zirikli says his days were not free of disturbances by the Bedouin, after which things became calm and lasted; the Friday sermon was pronounced in his name in Algiers and Tlemcen, and the oath of allegiance came to him from the ruler of Fez {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 213}. According to al-Zirikli he was the last of the Banu Hafs to hold the kingship in unbroken order; his rule lasted fifty-four and a half years and was not troubled until he died in Tunis {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 213}.

## Foundations
Among his works of piety al-Zirikli lists a library in the Zaytuna mosque and a madrasa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 213}.
