## Overview
Al-Malik al-Adil Sayf al-Din Abu Bakr ibn Ayyub, the brother of Saladin. When al-Malik al-Aziz died in Egypt in 595 AH and his son al-Mansur was set up, al-Adil took possession of Egypt, and then established there his son [[person/al-malik-al-kamil|al-Malik al-Kamil]]. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 694}

He died on the seventh of Jumada al-Akhira 615 AH at Alaqin, while he was on the watch at Damietta, aged seventy-five years and some months, and was buried in his mausoleum in Damascus. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 393-394}

Ibn al-Athir relates that he never let his nephew [[person/al-malik-al-afdal-ali|al-Afdal]] hold a kingdom without taking it from him, and that in his lifetime he divided the lands among his sons, so each of them remained firm in his kingdom. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 393-394}

## According to al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that his son al-Kamil was at al-Adiliyya near Damietta, and that when news of his father's death reached him he became sole ruler of the kingdom of Egypt {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.643}.

He says that al-Adil left Marj al-Safar, fell ill and died on the seventh of Jumada II. His son al-Mu'azzam Isa concealed his death, carried him in a litter to Damascus, buried him in its citadel, and later moved him to the al-Adiliyya madrasa there {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.643}.

In the land-tax registers there is a division bearing his name, "the Adili Diwan the Fortunate" {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.291}.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), unsigned, describes al-'Adil I, Abu Bakr Muhammad b. Ayyub, Sayf al-Din ("Sword of the Faith", called Saphadin by the Crusaders), the brother, assistant and spiritual heir of Saladin. He was born in Muharram 540 (June–July 1145) or, by other accounts, in 538 (1143–4), in Damascus or Baalbek. He accompanied Saladin to Egypt in Shirkuh's third expedition in 564 (1169). After Nur al-Din's death in 569 (1174) he governed Egypt during Saladin's frequent absences, and was called "the real sultan of Egypt". He received Aleppo in 579 (1183) with full powers, and in 582 (1186) Saladin let Aleppo go back to al-Zahir. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 216}

He held Karak and Balqa' during the campaigns of 1187–8 and the Third Crusade, took part in the conquest of southern Palestine, and played an important part in the negotiations with Richard Coeur-de-Lion, with whom it was even proposed that he marry Richard's sister Joan. In 588 (1192) he was moved to the Jazira and Diyar Bakr. After Saladin's death in 589 (1193) he defeated al-Afdal, who had been viceroy of Damascus, and was proclaimed sultan of Egypt and Syria in 596 (1200). He received recognition from al-Zahir in 598 (1202) and from the caliph in 604 (1207), and he divided his provinces among his sons, al-Kamil in Egypt, al-Mu'azzam in Damascus, and al-Ashraf and al-Awhad in the Jazira and Diyar Bakr. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 217}

He held Saladin's empire together in the face of new Crusades, patronised religion and learning, made commercial treaties with the Italian states, and kept peace with the local Crusader states by a series of truces. When the Fifth Crusade arrived in 614 (1217), he moved into Syria to help al-Mu'azzam and organise reinforcements for Damietta, fell ill, and died at 'Alikin outside Damascus on 7 Jumada I 615 (31 August 1218). {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 217}
