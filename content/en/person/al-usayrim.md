## Overview
Al-Usayrim was 'Amr ibn Thabit ibn Waqsh ibn Zughba ibn Za'ura' ibn 'Abd al-Ashhal al-Ansari, of [[group/banu-abd-al-ashhal|Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal]], who was killed at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]]; he was the son of the sister of [[person/hudhayfa-ibn-al-yaman|Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman]] — his mother was Lubaba bint al-Yaman — and the brother of Salama ibn Thabit. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 495} Al-Suhayli notes that the name is also read Waqash, with the q vowelled. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 36}

## His story as Ibn Ishaq tells it
Ibn Ishaq relates, from al-Husayn ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Amr ibn Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, from Abu Sufyan the client of Ibn Abi Ahmad, that [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] used to say: "Tell me of a man who entered Paradise without ever having prayed." When people could not name him, they would ask who it was, and he would say: "Usayrim of Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal, 'Amr ibn Thabit ibn Waqsh." {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 14}

Al-Husayn said: I asked Mahmud ibn Labid about al-Usayrim. He said: he used to refuse Islam to his people, but on the day the Prophet went out to Uhud he changed his mind and became Muslim, took his sword and went into the thick of the fighting, and fought until his wounds felled him. Men of Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal looking for their dead on the field came upon him and said, "By God, this is al-Usayrim; what brought him here? We left him rejecting this message." They asked him, "What brought you, 'Amr — concern for your people, or desire for Islam?" He said, "Desire for Islam: I believed in God and His Messenger and became Muslim, then took my sword and went out with the Messenger of God, and fought until I was struck as I have been." Soon after he died in their hands. They mentioned him to the Prophet, who said: "He is one of the people of Paradise." {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 14}

## In al-Isti'ab
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr records that he is the one of whom it was said that he entered Paradise without ever bowing once in prayer to God, as al-Tabari reports, and comments: "This is open to question." {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 495}
