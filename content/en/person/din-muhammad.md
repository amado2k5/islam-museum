Din Muhammad was a judge of the Punjab High Court who sat on the Punjab Boundary Commission, the panel charged in the summer of 1947 with dividing the Punjab between India and Pakistan. He and Muhammad Munir were the Muslim League's two nominees, facing Mehr Chand Mahajan and Teja Singh for the Congress, under the chairmanship of Cyril Radcliffe.{cite:sikh-encyclopedia-punjab-boundary-commission} The commission's membership is likewise carried in the account of the Radcliffe Line.{cite:wikipedia-radcliffe-line-en}

When the commission held its public sittings at Lahore from 21 to 31 July 1947, Radcliffe was attending first to the Bengal commission, and the sittings were chaired by Din Muhammad as the seniormost member.{cite:sikh-encyclopedia-punjab-boundary-commission} Like each of his colleagues he filed his own report, on 5 August, supporting the boundary line proposed by the Muslim League; Radcliffe's award, announced on 16 August, satisfied neither side, and the League resented in particular the loss of the Muslim-majority tehsils of Gurdaspur and of Ferozepur and Zira.{cite:sikh-encyclopedia-punjab-boundary-commission}
