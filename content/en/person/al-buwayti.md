## Overview
Abu Ya'qub Yusuf ibn Yahya al-Misri al-Buwayti, a pupil of [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]]. Ibn Khallikan describes him as the centre of his circle and the most distinguished of them, and says he took the place of al-Shafi'i in teaching and giving fatwas after his death. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.61-64}

## The trial and the prison
Ibn Khallikan relates that he was taken from Egypt to Baghdad in the days of al-Wathiq, during the trial, and was asked to say that the Qur'an was created, but he refused. He was imprisoned and remained in prison and fetters until he died. He relates from al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman that he saw him on a mule with a collar on his neck and fetters on his feet. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.61-64}

Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi relates that whenever he heard the call to prayer on Friday he would wash, put on his clothes and walk to the door of the prison. {cite:wafayat-ayan p.61-64}

## Death
He died in prison in Baghdad in 231 AH according to al-Shirazi; the Gregorian conversion (846 CE) is derived. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981}
