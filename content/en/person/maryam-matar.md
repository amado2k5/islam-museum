## Summary
Dr Maryam Mohamed Fatma Matar is an Emirati geneticist, medical researcher and health-policy pioneer. The Muslim 500 lists her in Science & Technology as a scientist in preventive medicine and genetics who founded and chairs the UAE Genetic Diseases Association; Wikipedia records that she was the first woman to serve as a director-general in the government of Dubai. {cite:muslim500-maryam-matar} {cite:wikipedia-maryam-matar}

## Career in public health
Matar trained in medicine and public health and rose through Dubai's health administration to head the emirate's genetics and public-health programmes, becoming the first woman to hold a director-general post in the Dubai government. Her office pushed premarital screening, newborn testing and genetic-counselling services into the emirate's health policy — issues of direct consequence in a region where consanguineous marriage is common and inherited blood and metabolic disorders are prevalent. {cite:wikipedia-maryam-matar}

## UAE Genetic Diseases Association
In 2004 she founded the UAE Genetic Diseases Association ⁦(UAEGDA)⁩ and has chaired it since, building programmes for community awareness, patient support, screening and research on hereditary conditions including thalassaemia and other disorders common in Gulf populations. The association works with government, universities and international partners, and its conferences have made the UAE a venue for genetic-medicine discussion in the Arab world. {cite:muslim500-maryam-matar} {cite:wikipedia-maryam-matar}

## Public voice
Matar also carries the message into media: she has hosted radio and television programmes on health in the Emirates, arguing for prevention over cure and for public understanding of genetics — an unusual public profile for a scientist, and one the Muslim 500's listing reflects in naming her a founder and communicator as much as a researcher. {cite:muslim500-maryam-matar} {cite:wikipedia-maryam-matar}

## Recognition
Her honours include recognition in Arab and international listings of influential women and scientists, and her role in placing genetic disease on the UAE's public agenda is widely cited. The Muslim 500 counts her among the influential Muslims of Science & Technology. {cite:muslim500-maryam-matar}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Matar stands for the Gulf's health institution-building generation: an Emirati woman who moved from laboratory genetics to running an emirate's health directorate, and then built the civic association that keeps hereditary disease in public view — influence exercised through policy, association and broadcast rather than the laboratory bench alone. {cite:muslim500-maryam-matar} {cite:wikipedia-maryam-matar}

## Genetics as public policy
The context that makes Matar's career legible is demography: the Gulf's populations carry high rates of inherited disorders — thalassaemias, metabolic diseases — amplified by cousin marriage, while genomic medicine was arriving as a tool governments could actually buy. Her achievement was to move the issue from a clinical footnote to a public agenda: screening programmes, premarital testing, a civic association lobbying for awareness. It is the classic pattern of effective public-health advocacy — professional expertise converted into institutions — executed by a woman in a government that had never before appointed one to a director-general's desk. {cite:muslim500-maryam-matar} {cite:wikipedia-maryam-matar}

## The advocate's pattern
Matar's record is the museum's example of the public-health advocate who works inside the system she is trying to change: a physician who took a government post precisely because the post gave her the tools to act on the problem she had diagnosed. The pattern — expertise converted into policy, policy converted into institutions — is the one her Muslim 500 entry records; the museum keeps it as the profile of a woman who made the case for genetics in a region that needed to hear it. {cite:muslim500-maryam-matar}
