## Overview
Abu Ishaq Ibrahim ibn Adham ibn Mansur al-Tamimi al-Balkhi, an ascetic and hadith scholar from Balkh, mentioned in the biographies of ascetics and Sufis. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.424-425}

## Death
The encyclopaedia gives his death as 161 AH (777 CE). This date comes from a single source and needs an additional source. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.424-425}

## In al-Dhahabi's Siyar
Al-Dhahabi records reports of his asceticism: he gathered acorns from oak trees; asked what the believer's miraculous dignity could reach, he said "that he tell the mountain 'move' and it moves" — and the mountain moved, whereupon he said "it was not you I meant". Of his sayings: every unjust king is like a thief, and every impious scholar like a wolf. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.646}

On how his affair began: his father was among the wealthy kings and hunting was made dear to him; then he left all of that and made for the mountain, worshipping God there until he died — the famous story al-Dhahabi narrates with its chain. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house p.646}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
Abu Nu'aym's notice on Ibn Adham — the last notice of volume 7 (no. 394) — opens with verse and reports narrated by his servant Ibrahim ibn Bashshar al-Rattabi and others, including his question to him about the beginning of his affair until he reached what he reached. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/367-368}

He reports that he was of the people of Balkh, his father one of the kings of Khurasan, and hunting was made dear to him; he went out riding his horse, and when he urged it he heard a call from behind him and then from his saddle's brow-strap: "You were not created for this, nor commanded for this." He saw no one; when it repeated, he said a warner had come to him from the Lord of the worlds, and he vowed not to disobey Him after that day. He took off his clothes, took a jubbah and a cloak from his father's grooms and threw his own clothes to them, then travelled to Iraq where nothing lawful was clear to him, asked the shaykhs, and they pointed him to Syria; he worked in al-Mansura and then Tarsus. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/368-369}

Among his reports on lawful earning: he asked the owner of a garden about its pomegranates, they broke one and found it sour, so he told the owner he would eat neither it nor anything else because he had found nothing of grapes for him; and the story of the Christian woman of Ascalon who commended him for good because she saw him eating lunch at supper-time and supper at lunch-time. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/369-372}

He mentions that when he prayed the night vigil he would stand before the house and call out at the top of his voice: "Who wants to grind?" The woman would bring out the bowl and the old man would set up the millstone and grind rent-free until morning. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/372}

On his dress: harvesting was dearer to him than gleaning; in winter he wore fur with no shirt, boots or turban beneath it, in summer two shifts worth four dirhams, he fasted travelling and resident, slept not at night, and ground wheat with one hand. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/373}

Of his renunciation of gifts: a man from Khurasan sent him a garment in which he found a cotton turban on a new shirt; al-Fazari told him: "Thus I found people taking and giving", and turned him away. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/384}

The story of the two dirhams: a man met him on the road and fed him and his companion; when they gave him two gold dirhams and asked who sent them, he said "Ibrahim ibn Adham"; the man knew him as one of the two who had been fed, took the dirhams, and died — shrouded in the two dirhams. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/373-374}

He mentions that he performed the hajj with a company in the year fifty and one hundred; when they entered Mecca they lodged in a house, he placed his staff in a window and hung his bag there, and they would go out when he went in and in when he went out. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/395}

The notice closes with reports of his and his companions' renunciation of four things, among them coverlets and footwear, and the report of a man who looked into his saddlebag and died in Medina. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 7/394-395}
