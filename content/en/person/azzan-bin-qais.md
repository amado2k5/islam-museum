## Summary
Azzan bin Qais Al Busaidi (no birth year in any source – killed at Muttrah in January 1871), Imam of Oman from 1868 to 1871: he deposed his cousin Salim bin Thuwaini and was elected by the ahl al-hall wa'l-'aqd led by Said bin Khalfan al-Khalili, was defeated by Turki bin Said at Dhank in 1869, and was killed at Muttrah, clearing the way for Turki's sultanate. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar}

## Early life
No consulted source gives Azzan a birth year: the English article states it is unknown and places him in the 19th century, the Arabic infobox likewise says "19th century" and gives Muscat as his birthplace, the Urdu categories alone claim an 1836 birth with no support in any text, and Wikidata's birth value is 1900 at century precision, a placeholder for "19th century" rather than a year. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ur} {cite:wikidata-azzan-bin-qais} His father was Qais bin Ahmad, according to the English infobox. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} Azzan married a daughter of Said bin Khalfan al-Khalili, who led the ahl al-hall wa'l-'aqd that chose him as imam. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en}

## Career
In 1868 Azzan deposed his cousin Salim bin Thuwaini, the ruler of Muscat, and was elected imam by the ahl al-hall wa'l-'aqd led by Said bin Khalfan al-Khalili; he took Muscat with the help of Salih bin Ali al-Harthi and declared the imamate. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} His programme, as the Arabic article sets it out, was to restore shura rule in the choice of ruler, to end British interference in Oman, and to reunite Oman and Zanzibar. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} The Urdu article names his expulsion of the Wahhabis from the Buraimi Oasis as his chief achievement, opposing Saudi interference there. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ur} In October 1869 Turki bin Said defeated him at the Battle of Dhank, and in January 1871 Azzan was killed at the Battle of Muttrah, ending his imamate as Turki entered Muscat as sultan. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en}

## Death and legacy
Azzan was killed at Muttrah in January 1871 according to both articles' texts, the Arabic attributing the date to Lorimer's Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf (1915), with the English infobox giving the day as 30 January 1871. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} The Arabic infobox instead gives 1870, its categories carry both 1870 and 1871, and Wikidata gives 1870 at year precision; the record follows the Lorimer-backed reading of the two texts and states the 1870 readings as they stand. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} {cite:wikidata-azzan-bin-qais} His death ended the last elected imamate of that generation in Oman and secured the sultanate of Turki bin Said, which Turki's sons inherited. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section is to be read as a presentation of positions reported in the sources, not as a verdict of the museum.

The record sets out his religion here because it is this group's closest case to the line: the English lead calls him "an Ibadi Imam of Oman", the Arabic text describes the imamate he declared as Ibadi, the English infobox gives his religion as "Ibadi Islam" uncited, and Wikidata carries no religion value. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} {cite:wikidata-azzan-bin-qais} But the two articles mirror each other and rest on the same literature (Lorimer 1915; Hopper 2019), so they form one source family, and what they attest is the Ibadi character of the imamate office he was elected to; under the two-source rule, and by the standard applied to Turki bin Said, an office's character and one family's descriptions are not two independent sources naming the school for the man personally, so the value stays unknown with every reading preserved in the record's faith note. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} The other documentary disagreements are set out in their places: the complete birth-year vacuum (the additions queue carried 1840, which no source supports, so it was dropped), and the contested death year, 1870 against 1871. {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-en} {cite:wikipedia-azzan-bin-qais-ar} {cite:wikidata-azzan-bin-qais}
