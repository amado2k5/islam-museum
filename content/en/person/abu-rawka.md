## Overview
Abu Rawka, whose name was al-Walid, was an Umayyad pretender who rose against the Fatimid caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah; Ibn al-Athir recounts his story in full. He fled when al-Hakim hunted down the Umayyads, reached the Banu Qurra in the Maghreb and summoned them, and they followed him; he took Barqa and his power grew. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.8 p.43}

## War with al-Hakim's armies
Al-Hakim's army under Yinal al-Tawil, about five thousand horse, reached Dhat al-Himam; Abu Rawka sent a commander with a thousand riders to foul the wells and return, so when the caliph's army emerged from the waterless tract weakened and thirsty Abu Rawka met them, many of Kutama joined him, and he routed them, killing Yinal and most of his army. He returned to Barqa greatly feared, his raiders reaching Upper Egypt, then marched there himself when the Egyptians — among them al-Husayn ibn Jawhar, commander of commanders — invited him; al-Hakim mustered about twelve thousand horse and foot under al-Fadl ibn Abd Allah to meet him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.8 p.43-44}
