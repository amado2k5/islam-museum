## Summary
Abdullah ibn [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] ibn Hashim was the father of the Prophet [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]]. Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum describes him as the best, most chaste and most beloved of Abd al-Muttalib's sons to him. He is the "sacrificed one" on whom the arrows fell and who was ransomed with a hundred camels {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/151-155}. He married [[person/amina-bint-wahb|Amina bint Wahb]] and died in Medina at the age of twenty-five {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/156-158}.

## Lineage and mother
His mother was [[person/fatima-bint-amr|Fatima bint Amr]] ibn A'idh ibn Imran ibn Makhzum ibn Yaqzah ibn Murra, so his maternal kin were of [[group/banu-makhzum|Banu Makhzum]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52} {cite:talqih-fuhum-ahl-al-athr p.8-9}. He was one of the ten sons of Abd al-Muttalib that the book counts: [[person/al-harith-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Harith]], [[person/al-zubayr-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Zubayr]], [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]], Abdullah, [[person/hamza|Hamza]], [[person/abu-lahab|Abu Lahab]], [[person/al-ghaydaq-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Ghaydaq]], [[person/al-muqawwim-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Muqawwim]], [[person/dirar-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|Dirar]] and [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52} {cite:talqih-fuhum-ahl-al-athr p.8-9}.

## The sacrifice
When Abd al-Muttalib's sons reached ten, and he knew that they would protect him, he told them of his vow, and they obeyed him. He wrote their names on arrows and gave them to the keeper of [[object/hubal|Hubal]]. The arrow fell on Abdullah {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/151-155}. His father took him, took the knife, and came with him to the [[building/kaaba|Ka'ba]] to slaughter him. But Quraysh, especially his maternal kin of Banu Makhzum and his brother Abu Talib, stopped him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/89-90}. On the advice of a soothsayer, arrows were cast between him and the camels, ten camels at a time, until the number reached a hundred. Then the lot fell on the camels, and they were slaughtered in his place. The details are in [[event/vow-and-lot-of-abd-al-muttalib|the vow and the lot of Abd al-Muttalib]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/151-155}. The book relates the Prophet's saying "I am the son of the two sacrificed ones", meaning [[person/ismail|Isma'il]] and his father Abdullah {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul p.12}.

## Marriage to Amina
Abd al-Muttalib chose for him Amina, daughter of [[person/wahb-ibn-abd-manaf|Wahb ibn Abd Manaf]] ibn Zuhra ibn Kilab. She was then regarded as the most honourable woman of Quraysh in lineage and standing, and her father was the chief of [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]] in lineage and honour. Abdullah consummated the marriage with her in Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/156-158}.

## Death
Shortly after, Abd al-Muttalib sent him to [[place/medina|Medina]] to buy dates for them, and he died there. It is also said that he went out as a merchant to Syria, came back with a Quraysh caravan, fell ill at Medina, and died there. He was buried in the house of al-Nabigha al-Ja'di, aged twenty-five {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/156-158} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/91}. According to most historians, his death came before the Prophet was born; some say it came two months after the birth {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p.45}. Amina composed a lament for him, which the author describes as among the finest laments {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad 1/62}. The details are in [[event/death-of-abdullah-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|the death of Abdullah in Medina]].

## What he left behind
All he left behind was five camels, a flock of sheep, and an Abyssinian slave-girl named Baraka, known as [[person/umm-ayman|Umm Ayman]], who was the Prophet's nurse {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.53} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul-al-najdi p.12} {cite:sahih-muslim 2/96}.

## Notes for researchers
- In the edition the museum read, the book *Mukhtasar Sirat al-Rasul* is sometimes attributed to "Shaykh Abdullah" and sometimes to "Shaykh Abdullah al-Najdi". It is one book.
- The book does not give the year of his birth or of his death. What is in the data card is an approximation derived from his age at death and from the Year of the Elephant.

## For young visitors
Abdullah was the father of the Prophet. His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib wanted to fulfil an old vow, and the lot fell on Abdullah. His people stopped it and ransomed him with a hundred camels. Then he married Amina, and died young in Medina before his son was born.

## For Explorers
Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's father; al-Raheeq calls him the best and dearest of Abd al-Muttalib's sons. His mother was Fatima bint Amr, his uncles Banu Makhzum. He is 'the intended sacrifice': when the sons reached ten and could protect him, Abd al-Muttalib told them his vow; the arrow fell on Abdullah and his father brought him to the Kaaba to offer him, but Quraysh and his Makhzum uncles stopped him. On a diviner's counsel arrows were drawn between him and camels, ten by ten, until a hundred took the lot — hence the Prophet's 'I am the son of the two intended sacrifices', Isma'il and his father. Abd al-Muttalib chose for him Amina bint Wahb of Banu Zuhra, counted the best woman of Quraysh; he married her in Mecca and died at Medina aged twenty-five.

## For Scholars
Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim, the Prophet Muhammad's father; al-Raheeq describes him as the best, most chaste and dearest of Abd al-Muttalib's sons — 'the intended sacrifice' on whom the arrows fell, ransomed with a hundred camels; he married Amina bint Wahb and died at Medina aged twenty-five. His mother was Fatima bint Amr ibn A'idh ibn Imran ibn Makhzum ibn Yaqza ibn Murra so his uncles were Banu Makhzum, and he is one of Abd al-Muttalib's ten sons: al-Harith, al-Zubayr, Abu Talib, Abdullah, Hamza, Abu Lahab, al-Ghaydaq, al-Muqawwim, Dirar and al-Abbas. When the sons reached ten and he knew they could protect him, he told them his vow and they obeyed; he wrote their names on the arrows and gave them to Hubal's keeper, and the arrow fell on Abdullah. His father took him and the blade and brought him to the Kaaba to offer him, but Quraysh stopped him — his Makhzum uncles most of all, and his brother Abu Talib. On a diviner's counsel arrows were drawn between him and the camels, ten by ten, until they reached a hundred and the lot fell on them; and the book reports the Prophet's saying 'I am the son of the two intended sacrifices', meaning Isma'il and his father Abdullah. Abd al-Muttalib chose for him Amina bint Wahb ibn Abd Manaf ibn Zuhra ibn Kilab, then counted the best woman of Quraysh in lineage and standing, her father the lord of Banu Zuhra in lineage and honor; he built with her in Mecca.
