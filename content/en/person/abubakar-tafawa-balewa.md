# Abubakar Tafawa Balewa

Abubakar Tafawa Balewa (December 1912 – 15 January 1966) was a Nigerian politician from Bauchi province, born in the village of Tafawa Balewa to a Gerawa father and a Fulani mother {cite:wikipedia-abubakar-tafawa-balewa-en} {cite:wikipedia-abubakar-tafawa-balewa-ar}. He studied at a madrasa in Bauchi and at Katsina Higher College, became a teacher and then headmaster of Bauchi middle school, and took a teacher's certificate at the University of London's Institute of Education {cite:wikipedia-abubakar-tafawa-balewa-en}.

## Writer and minister

In 1933 Balewa wrote Shaihu Umar, a Hausa novella about a pious Muslim, first published in 1934 and later translated into English by Mervyn Hiskett; it was staged as a play in the 1970s and filmed in 1976 {cite:wikipedia-abubakar-tafawa-balewa-en}. Entering politics, he was elected to the Northern House of Assembly, became a federal minister, and at independence on 1 October 1960 became Nigeria's first prime minister — the only man ever to hold that office {cite:wikipedia-abubakar-tafawa-balewa-en} {cite:wikidata-abubakar-tafawa-balewa}.

## Overthrow and murder

Balewa was overthrown and murdered in the military coup of 15 January 1966, in which his old companion Ahmadu Bello was also killed. His body was discovered at a roadside near Lagos six days later and was buried in Bauchi; the circumstances of his death remain unresolved {cite:wikipedia-abubakar-tafawa-balewa-en}.
