## Overview
Salama al-Qass, a slave-girl of Suhayl ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf al-Zuhri. al-Mas'udi reports that Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik bought her for three thousand dinars, was taken with her, and she came to control his affairs. Ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat composed a verse about her, which al-Mas'udi quotes {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.185}.

## In Kahhala's A'lam al-Nisa'
Kahhala says Sallama al-Qass was a singer born and raised in Medina who learned singing from Ma'bad, Ibn A'isha, Jamila and Malik ibn Abi al-Samh, became expert in song and string-playing and composed much verse; he quotes al-Mada'ini that he never saw a woman combine beauty of face, of singing and of poetry as she did. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.233} Her nickname comes from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Ammar al-Jushami, one of the Qur'an reciters of Mecca, called "the Priest" (*al-qass*) for his devotion, who fell in love with her; it became known, and his nickname passed to her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.233} He relates that the reciter heard her sing and they fell in love, but when she invited him to be alone with her he refused, citing the verse on friends becoming enemies on that Day except the God-fearing [[passage/quran-43-67|(al-Zukhruf 43:67)]], saying he would not have their friendship turn to enmity; he went back to his devotions and wrote verses about her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.233} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.234} She later passed to Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik; Hababa, who knew Sallama's merit in teaching song, slighted her when she saw Yazid's preference, but returned to good terms after Sallama reproached her. Hababa died and Sallama lived long after her, and on Yazid's death she lamented him in elegies Kahhala says brought tears to every eye. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.237}
