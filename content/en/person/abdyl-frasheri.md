## Overview

Abdyl Frashëri (1839 – 23 October 1892) was an Albanian-Ottoman politician and diplomat, the eldest of the three Frashëri brothers and, with his brothers Naim and Sami, a central figure of the Albanian National Awakening.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-sq} He founded the Central Committee for Defending Albanian Rights in Istanbul and was the leading political organiser and foreign spokesman of the League of Prizren, the movement that first demanded Albanian autonomy within the Ottoman Empire.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-ar} In Ottoman state records his name appears as Abdullah Hüsni.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-tr}

## Career

Born at Frashër — the Albanian, English and Arabic articles give 1 June 1839, the German and Turkish articles and Wikidata 17 August 1839 — he spent his youth to the age of eighteen in Frashër and served as a captain in the irregular Albanian contingents his father Halit Bey commanded on the Greek frontier.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-sq}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-de}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-tr} On his father's death in 1859 he became head of the household, and after his mother's death in 1861 he moved the family to Ioannina, where he held clerk and governor posts for some twenty years and studied Arabic, Persian, French and Greek under the müderris Hasan Tahsini.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-sq} He sat as deputy for Yanina in the first Ottoman parliament of 1876–77 and spoke there on 14 January 1878.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} In 1877 he chaired the Albanian Committee of Janina and founded and chaired the Central Committee for Defending Albanian Rights in Istanbul.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} He co-organised the League of Prizren, founded on 10 June 1878 by some eighty delegates, chaired its foreign affairs committee and acted as its foreign minister and spokesman in the European press.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} He led the League's delegation to the European capitals in 1879, meeting Greek representatives in Rome in April 1879, a mission that failed on the Greek side's terms.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} At the Gjirokastër assembly of 1880 he helped frame the autonomy programme, and in early 1881 he served as foreign minister of the League's provisional government under Ymer Prizreni.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} He survived an assassination attempt at Debar in December 1881.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} After the League's suppression he was captured by Dervish Pasha, sentenced to death by a special tribunal, and the sentence was commuted by Sultan Abdülhamid II to prison with hard labour — held at Prizren castle from 1882 to 1885 in the Albanian and English accounts, with the German article adding exile at Balıkesir and Bandırma.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-sq}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-de} Released in 1886 on condition he abandon political activity, he lived in Istanbul under constraint, corresponded with the Italian statesman Francesco Crispi, and died there on 23 October 1892 — the German and Turkish articles and Wikidata give 11 October 1892.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-de} His remains were brought to Albania in 1978, the centenary of the League.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} His son Mit'hat Frashëri (1880–1949) became a writer and politician in independent Albania.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en}

## Positions and criticism

This section reports what the sources attribute to Frashëri and how they assess him; it is not the museum's own judgement.

The articles describe his family as a distinguished Albanian Muslim family affiliated with the Bektashi Sufi order, and record his own wartime approaches to the order's houses — visiting Bektashi tekkes in the south and pressing their babas to join an armed uprising for the League.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-ar} The museum treats the family's affiliation as family background and the tekke visits as the political mobilisation the articles themselves describe; no source attests Abdyl's own adherence, so his faith is recorded as unknown, as his record's faith note sets out.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikidata-abdyl-frasheri} His stated political goal, in the English article's account, was an autonomous Albania inside a confederation with neighbouring peoples or under the guarantee of a European great power.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-en} The German article notes that, unlike his co-founder Ali Pasha of Gusinje, he favoured an independent Albanian state from the outset.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-de} The Arabic article records that he was declared a hero of Albania in the communist era.{cite:source/wikipedia-abdyl-frasheri-ar}
