## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Maymun, Abu al-Ghanaim al-Narsi, was a Qur'an reciter and hadith memoriser from Kufa; his nisba points to a river there. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 424 AH and his death as 510 AH, and prints 1033 and 1166 CE beside them; 510 AH corresponds to 1116 CE on the usual calendar tables (derived), so the printed 1166 looks like a misprint {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 278}.

## Life
He took from the scholars of Kufa and of Baghdad and earned his living by copying books {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 278}. The entry adds a nickname given for the quality of his recitation, but the phrase is garbled in the local text, so it is not repeated here {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 278}. Al-Zirikli reports a saying of his that no one of the people of the Sunna and hadith was left in Kufa except himself {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 278}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists an abridgement he titled Thawab qada hawa'ij al-ikhwan wa-ma ja'a fi ighathat al-lahfan, in manuscript at Dar al-Kutub (no. 20562 b) and in the Chester Beatty library (no. 3490), and al-Hawatif, a fragment of nine leaves inside a manuscript at the Ahmadiyya library in Tunis (no. 5032) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 278}.
