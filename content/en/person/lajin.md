## Overview
A Mamluk sultan. He seized power in Damascus in 696 AH while the sultan was there, and the amirs swore allegiance to him, and no two of them disagreed about him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.731}

The caliph invested him with the black robe and wrote him a decree of appointment. He was then killed in Jumada al-Akhira 698 AH, and [[person/al-nasir-muhammad-ibn-qalawun|al-Malik al-Nasir]] was restored. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.731}

## His restoration of the Mosque of Ibn Tulun
Yeomans relates that Lajin took sanctuary in the abandoned [[building/masjid-ibn-tulun|Mosque of Ibn Tulun]] in 1296 after the assassination of Sultan al-Ashraf Khalil, and vowed to restore it if he survived to take the throne; becoming sultan the following year, he restored it substantially. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.33} The courtyard fountain, the octagonal top shafts of the minaret, the minbar and repairs to the mihrab are attributed to him; whether the whole minaret is his is disputed between Creswell, Behrens-Abouseif and Swelim. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.33} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.34} The fountain is a domed stone cube with five tiers of muqarnas, Mamluk in style, which in Yeomans's view sits well in its ninth-century setting through fine proportion and clarity of form. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.35}
