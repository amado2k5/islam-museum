## Overview
Umar ibn al-Khattab was the second of the Rightly Guided Caliphs (634–644 CE). In his ten years, the first community conquered Syria, Egypt, and most of the Sasanian Empire. In the memory of the Sunna, he came to represent the model of a just Islamic government.

## Life
Umar was a merchant from the [[group/banu-adi|Banu Adi]] in [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]]. At first he was a fierce opponent of the new call. Then he became Muslim around 616 CE, in a conversion that the tradition describes as a strengthening of the community. {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley} He emigrated during the [[event/hijra|Hijra]] and witnessed the first battles. His daughter [[person/hafsa|Hafsa]] was one of the wives of the Prophet.

[[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] chose him as caliph when he died. During his rule, Syria, Egypt and Iraq were conquered, and the emerging state was organized: the diwan and the stipends, the garrison cities, the calendar, and the judges and officials. {cite:watt-1956-medina} In November 644 CE, a Persian captive named Abu Lu'lu'a stabbed him, and he died a few days later in Medina. {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}

## Legacy
The first form of the Islamic state, in how the caliph rules, how stipends are given, and how the judge decides, bears his imprint more than that of any other founder. The dispute over his succession at the Saqifa still divides memory.

## For young visitors
Umar was a feared enemy before he became the second leader of the community. In ten years, the religion that was born in one valley reached three empires.

## For Sprouts
Umar ibn al-Khattab was the second righteous caliph, famous for justice, mercy, helping the needy, and caring for people.

## For Explorers
Umar ibn al-Khattab, titled al-Faruq (the Distinguisher of truth), was the second caliph and a wise leader known for justice, humility, and accountability. During his rule, he established civic administrations, public registries (diwans), and the Hijri calendar, famously walking the streets at night to assist families in need.

## For Scholars
Abu Hafs Umar ibn al-Khattab (c. 40 BH – 23 AH / 584–644 CE), titled al-Faruq, the second Rightly Guided Caliph and a transformative statesman. Embracing Islam in Mecca in the sixth year of prophethood, his leadership strengthened the early community. Assuming the caliphate in 13 AH (634 CE) upon Abu Bakr's nomination, his tenure oversaw historic expansions into Syria, Iraq, Egypt, and Jerusalem, where he issued the landmark Covenant of Umar. He laid the institutional architecture of the Islamic state through the creation of administrative registries (diwans), an independent judiciary, the treasury, and the official institution of the lunar Hijri calendar.

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen

He relates that Umar used to pray to God for martyrdom and for death in the city of God's Messenger, which surprised the Companions; he uses the story to show how closely they clung to the Prophet {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:06:00}.

He mentions that Umar used to ask whoever brought him a matter whether it concerned Muslims in general or himself; if it was personal, he would put out the lamp that the state paid for (needs_source: anecdote-unsourced) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:28:00}. He tells that Umar put out the lamp when the man who sat with him died, because the oil had become the right of the heirs (needs_source: anecdote-unsourced) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:28:30}, and that he took from his grandson a coin from the treasury (needs_source: figure-coin-value; anecdote-unsourced) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:29:00}. He also mentions that Umar ordered the keeper of the treasury to exempt an elderly man among the protected people from the jizya {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:44:00}.

He attributes to Umar the saying that God honoured Muslims with Islam after they had been in weakness, and that seeking honour in anything else would humble them (needs_source: athar-attribution-uncited) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep04 00:36:30}.

He relates that Umar addressed the people when the earth quaked and said to the earth, "be still" (needs_source: athar-umar-unverified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:35:30}. He reads Umar's stance as meaning that the earth shakes because of the deeds of those who forgot God, and lists examples of sins {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:36:00}. He recounts a report that earlier nations had inspired persons (muhaddathun), and then a story of Umar and a man who claimed descent from the Harra of Waqim, whom Umar sent back to his family (needs_source: anecdote-umar) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:20:30}.

He recounts that a young Copt won a race against the son of Amr ibn al-As and was struck by him; the young man complained to Umar, who summoned Amr and his son, ordered the Copt to strike Amr's son, and spoke his well-known words that people were created free (needs_source: anecdote-unsourced) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:28:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:29:00}.

He says that Umar once suggested killing in a certain situation, and the Prophet explained that God softens the hearts of some and hardens the hearts of others; he likened Abu Bakr to Abraham and Jesus, and Umar to Noah and Moses [[passage/quran-14-36|Qur'an 14:36]] [[passage/quran-5-118|Qur'an 5:118]] [[passage/quran-71-26|Qur'an 71:26]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:12:00}.

He relates that Umar slept under a tree after a Persian man told him he was just and so he felt safe, and says that the eye sleeps while the heart stays awake with God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep10 00:37:30}.

He tells of a young man who drank wine away from the road; when he returned home with a bottle of wine, he met Umar on the way (needs_source: anecdote-unattributed) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:22:30}.

He tells of a man who complained to Umar about his wife raising her voice at him, and presents the story as a lesson in patience with bad temper and in noticing the service the wife gives (needs_source: anecdote-garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep14 00:22:00}. He adds that the caliph likewise complained about his wife, describing her as one who washes clothes, cooks and raises the children, and says that patience with bad temper is a path by which Paradise is hoped for (needs_source: anecdote-garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep14 00:23:00}.
