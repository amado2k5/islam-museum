## Brief
Umar ibn al-Khattab was the second caliph (634–644 CE). Under his ten years of rule the early community conquered Syria, Egypt and most of the Sasanian Empire — and, in Sunni memory, the model of just Islamic government took shape.

## Life
A merchant of the clan of [[group/banu-adi|Banu Adi]] of [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]], Umar was at first a stern opponent of the new faith, then embraced it about 616 CE — a conversion the tradition remembers as strengthening the whole community {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He migrated with [[event/hijra]], fought in the early battles, and his daughter [[person/hafsa|Hafsa]] became a wife of the Prophet.

Chosen caliph by the dying [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], Umar presided over the conquests of Syria, Egypt and Iraq, and organized what the early state became: stipends by register (the diwan), garrison cities, a calendar, judges and governors {cite:watt-1956-medina}. In November 644 he was stabbed by a Persian captive, Abu Lu'lu'a, and died days later in Medina {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

:::perspectives
- **Sunni**: Sunni tradition venerates Umar as al-Faruq — the second rightly-guided caliph and the standing example of the just ruler, founder of the early state's institutions {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.
- **Academic**: Historians credit his caliphate with the conquests and the first state institutions, while reading the idealized memory of his justice as partly a later construction {cite:watt-1956-medina}.
- **Twelver Shia**: Twelver Shia tradition is critical of Umar above all for the Saqifa succession that set aside Ali, and for episodes in the relationship with Fatima {cite:momen-1985}.
:::

## Legacy
The Islamic state's first shape — how a caliph rules, how armies are paid, how judges judge — bears his stamp more than any other founder's, and the debate over his succession still divides memory.

## For young visitors
Umar was feared as an enemy, then became the community's second leader. In ten years under him, a religion born in one valley reached three empires.
