## Brief
Safwan ibn Umayya — son of [[person/umayya-ibn-khalaf|Umayya]], the man who tortured Bilal — continued his father's opposition almost to the end, was pardoned on the road to Jeddah, and within weeks was fighting for Islam at Hunayn.

## Life
A chief of Banu Jumah, Safwan helped finance the confederates of the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Trench]] and, the sources record, sent an agent against the Prophet's life. At the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest]] he fled for the coast and a ship out — until the Prophet sent him his own turban as a token of safety, with two months' grace {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He came back, asked for and received pardon, and stood with the Muslim army at [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]] — where the Prophet gave him a valley of livestock, and the tradition records Safwan's words that only a prophet's generosity could make a man love so quickly {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He died in Mecca around 661, an elder of Quraysh.

## Legacy
The son of the man who owned Bilal ends the story as a Muslim elder — the sira's study in pardon as policy.

## For young visitors
Safwan was still running from the Muslims when the Prophet sent him a gift of safety. He came back, and never left.
