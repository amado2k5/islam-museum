## Overview
Sanad ibn Ali, an astrologer and astronomer. Ibn al-Nadim read, in the handwriting of Ibn al-Jahm, that the book "al-Madkhal" was by Sanad ibn Ali, and that he gave it to Abu Ma'shar, who then claimed it as his own {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.384-385}.

Ibn al-Nadim also attributes to him "al-Tis' al-Maqalat fi al-Mawalid" (The Nine Treatises on Nativities) and a book on conjunctions. Sanad commented on the book of Euclid, and Abu Ali read nine treatises of it and part of the tenth {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.384-385}.

## In the introduction to the Cairo edition of the Algebra
The editors of the 1939 Cairo edition of the [[work/al-jabr-wal-muqabala|Book of al-Jabr and al-Muqabala]] observe that the name following Muhammad ibn Musa [[person/al-khwarizmi|al-Khwarizmi]] in Ibn al-Nadim's Fihrist is Sanad ibn Ali, to whom Ibn al-Nadim attributes a book on increase and decrease, a book on algebra and a book on Indian arithmetic. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.23} They report that the orientalist Suter considered the attribution of these books to Sanad a mistake and thought they belonged to al-Khwarizmi; they comment that, if the mistake happened, it must have happened early, in the first copies of the Fihrist, because Ibn al-Qifti (d. 1248 CE) says the same about al-Khwarizmi as Ibn al-Nadim. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.23}
