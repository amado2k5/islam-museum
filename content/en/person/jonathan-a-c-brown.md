Jonathan Andrew Cleveland Brown (born 7 August 1977 in Washington, D.C.) is an American scholar of Islamic studies. Raised Episcopalian, he converted to Islam in 1997 while a student at Georgetown University, graduated in history there in 2000, studied Arabic for a year in Cairo, and completed his doctorate in Islamic thought at the University of Chicago in 2006 with a dissertation on the canonisation of the hadith collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim.{cite:wikipedia-brown-en}{cite:wikidata-brown}

He taught at the University of Washington in Seattle (2006–2010) and then at Georgetown, where he holds the Alwaleed bin Talal Chair of Islamic Civilization and directed the Center for Muslim-Christian Understanding until 2019. His books include Hadith: Muhammad's Legacy in the Medieval and Modern World (2009), Misquoting Muhammad (2014), Slavery and Islam (2019) and Islam and Blackness (2022).{cite:wikipedia-brown-en} He states his legal school in his own words — "the Hanbali school (my school)" — and the biography records him as Sunni following the Hanbali school, so his record enters his faith as Sunni Hanbali, self-identified.{cite:brown-shariah-homosexuality-imanwire-2016}{cite:wikipedia-brown-en}
