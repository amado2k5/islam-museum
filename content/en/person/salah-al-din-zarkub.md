## Overview
Salah al-Din Zarkub of [[place/konya|Konya]] was a goldsmith who became the first deputy of [[person/jalal-al-din-rumi|Jalal al-Din Rumi]] after the disappearance of [[person/shams-tabrizi|Shams-i Tabrizi]]. The Encyclopaedia of Islam describes him as illiterate but distinguished by his handsomeness and pleasant character. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 419} The sources it uses are thin on his own life, so what follows is mostly the story of his appointment and of how the community received it.

## Chosen as deputy
After Shams was murdered in 645/1247, the Rumi article says, Rumi announced that in the year 647/1249 Shams had appeared to him again in the form of one of his disciples, Salah al-Din Zarkub. He appointed the goldsmith as khalifa, and so as the superior of the other disciples, and wished to retire himself from the offices of shaykh and preacher. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 419} The disciples, who had known him from childhood as a goldsmith's apprentice, found the uncultured craftsman harder to accept than the Tabrizi dervish had been. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 419}

## A second crisis
The article says that some of the disciples were driven to plot against him and even to murder him, and that the plot was revealed. When they saw that Rumi threatened to leave them altogether, they asked forgiveness remorsefully. The article suggests that the loyal attitude of [[person/sultan-walad|Sultan Walad]] and Salah al-Din's own modest, pleasant personality helped the community to pass this second crisis. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 419}

## First shaykh of the gathering-room
The Mawlawiyya article reasons that the gathering-room (jama'at-khana) next to Rumi's madrasa was probably used in his lifetime and after his death to train novices, and that a shaykh was found there. It names Salah Zarkub as the first of these shaykhs, followed by [[person/husam-al-din-chalabi|Husam al-Din Chelebi]], Karim al-Din Bektemur and Sultan Walad. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 910}

## Death and succession
For ten years Salah al-Din filled the office of deputy and khalifa. He then fell ill and died, according to the inscription on his sarcophagus, on 1 Muharram 657, which the article gives as 29 December 1258. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 419} His successor was Husam al-Din Chelebi Hasan, whose family came from Urmiya and who was to be the inspirer of the Mathnawi. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 419}

## What is not known
The article does not give his birth date, his father's name, or his burial place, and it names no work by him. It cites Golpinarli's study of the Mevlevi order for the date on the sarcophagus. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 419} The article also notes that in some of Rumi's ghazals the pen-name used is Salah al-Din, but it does not say what that shows about the man himself. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 420}
