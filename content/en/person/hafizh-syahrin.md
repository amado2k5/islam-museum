## Summary
Hafizh Syahrin is a Malaysian motorcycle racer, born in Selangor on 5 May 1994. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en} In 2018 he became the first Southeast Asian to race in the MotoGP category. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en}

## Early life
Born and raised in Selangor, Syahrin began racing pocket bikes at the age of nine and dominated the category, acquiring the nickname "King of Pocket Bikes". {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en} He made his debut in the Malaysian Cub Prix championship in 2007 and progressed through national and Asian road-racing series. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en}

## Career
As a wildcard at the 2012 Malaysian Grand Prix, Syahrin led his home Moto2 race in the middle stages and was classified third after Anthony West's results were annulled for doping. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en} Riding full-time for Petronas Raceline Malaysia in Moto2 from 2014, he took further podiums at San Marino — second, after Dominique Aegerter's disqualification — and Japan in 2017, finishing tenth that season. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en} Signed by Monster Yamaha Tech3 for 2018 after Jonas Folger withdrew for health reasons, he raced two MotoGP seasons, on Yamaha machinery and then on a factory-spec KTM, with a best result of ninth place in Argentina. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en} After two further Moto2 seasons he moved to the Superbike World Championship, racing for MIE Honda in 2022–2023 and in JDT Racing Team colours on a Ducati in 2024. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en}

## Significance
Syahrin's arrival in MotoGP in February 2018 made him the first Malaysian, and the first Southeast Asian, to compete in the premier category of Grand Prix motorcycle racing. {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-ms} {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en} He is nicknamed "El Pescao". {cite:wikipedia-hafizh-syahrin-en}
