## Overview
Khayr al-Din al-Tunisi, a Tunisian and Ottoman statesman, born in the land of the Circassians in 1238 AH (1822 CE) and died in 1308 AH (1890 CE). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## Public life
He presided over the Tunisian government, and wrote on political reform. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes Khayr al-Din al-Tunisi, also known as Khayr al-Din Pasha, as a Tunisian Islamic modernist and grand vizier. It gives his death as 1890, which agrees with the record above, and says he was prime minister of Tunisia in 1873 and brought in economic and administrative reforms to meet the country's financial crisis. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1284}

It says he was appointed grand vizier of the [[place/ottoman-empire|Ottoman]] state in 1878 but was dismissed soon afterwards for supporting parliamentary government. He was among the early Muslim thinkers who worried about the community's decline and how to revive it; his book Aqwam al-Masalik fi Ma'rifat Ahwal al-Mamalik argued for constitutional government, the parliamentary system and modernisation of education, and in 1875 he founded the Sadiqi College, which trained successive generations of Tunisia's modernising elite. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1284} The entry says he served under Abd al-Hamid II, which is its own wording. The entry is short and this record does not rest on it for any disputed judgement.
