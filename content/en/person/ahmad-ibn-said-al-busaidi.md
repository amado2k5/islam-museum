## Overview
Ahmad ibn Said ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Busaidi al-Azdi al-Umani, given the title al-Mutawakkil ala Allah, founded the Busaidi state of Oman that still continues and is the forefather of its kings, who are of the Ibadi school, as al-Zirikli states. He gives his death as 1196 AH, 1782 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}.

## His works
In his early life he was one of the brave commanders and governors; Sayf ibn Sultan employed him, liked his conduct, appointed him over Sohar, then made him the sword of his state and the seat of his strength and entrusted all affairs to him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}. When rule passed to Sultan ibn Murshid, Ahmad stayed on in Sohar, and Sultan died while with him, in 1155 AH, in his war with the Persians, who had pushed far into Omani lands. Ahmad fought them and drove them out, killing many by a stratagem he devised; the land submitted to him, its people loved him, and the rule of the Ya'rubids passed to him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}. In his days Bal'arab ibn Himyar claimed the imamate, and Ahmad killed him in 1167 AH; the state was clear for him and he was given the oath of allegiance as imam in that year. The rule of Oman and Muscat came to him and continued until he died {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 111}.
