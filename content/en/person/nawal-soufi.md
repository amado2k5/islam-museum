## Summary

Nawal Soufi ⁦(born 1988)⁩ is a Moroccan-Italian social worker and humanitarian activist known in Italy as 'Lady SOS' and across the Arab world as the 'Angel of the Refugees': a Sicilian-raised rescuer whose mobile phone became a relay line between migrant boats in distress in the Mediterranean and the Italian coastguard. {cite:wikipedia-nawal-soufi} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 183}

## Early life

Born in Morocco to Moroccan parents, she was only weeks old when the family moved to Catania, the city at the foot of Etna in Sicily. She began her social work at fourteen, helping Moroccan immigrants and homeless Italians in their daily needs, and continued while studying political science and international relations, working part-time as an interpreter in Sicilian courts and prisons. {cite:wikipedia-nawal-soufi}

## Syria and the sea

Moved by the Arab Spring and the Syrian war, she travelled to Syria in December 2012 at the head of a humanitarian convoy that assisted some eight hundred families, and she began helping Syrians trying to reach Italy. {cite:wikipedia-nawal-soufi}

She became publicly known in 2013, when a call reached her from a boat lost in the Mediterranean carrying hundreds of Syrian refugees in danger of drowning; her relay of the distress to the authorities contributed to their rescue. From then on aid to migrants at sea became her daily occupation: boats in distress called her number, and she passed positions and appeals to the coastguard and rescue services. The English Wikipedia article credits her with contributing to the saving of more than two million migrants — a figure the museum repeats only as the article's own claim, since such totals are by nature approximate. {cite:wikipedia-nawal-soufi}

## Recognition

For documentaries she made in Syria and Libya in 2014 she received the 'Pioneer Woman' and 'Border Woman' awards at the Marzamemi International Frontier Film Festival. In 2016 she was awarded the European Citizens' Prize, and in 2017 she won the Arab Hope Maker ⁦(صناع الأمل)⁩ award in Dubai — a distinction chaired by Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum promoting Arabs working for positive change — where her work earned her the title the coverage made famous: the refugees' angel, 'Mama Nawal'. A book about her, Nawal: Angel of the Refugees, appeared in Italy in 2016. {cite:wikipedia-nawal-soufi} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 183}

## Assessment

Soufi's significance in the record is the person-to-person form of humanitarian work in a decade of mass Mediterranean crossings: not an agency or a fleet but a phone number, an interpreter's fluency and a reputation passed between boats. Her listing in The Muslim 500 places her among the edition's social-issues figures. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 183}
