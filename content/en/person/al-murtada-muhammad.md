## Overview
Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn al-Husayn ibn al-Qasim ibn Ibrahim al-Alawi al-Talibi, called al-Murtada, is described by al-Zirikli in al-A'lam as a Zaydi imam, a jurist learned in legal theory, from Sa'da in Yemen. The Zaydis are a Shi'i school, so this is al-Zirikli's description of him, not the museum's position. Al-Zirikli gives his birth as 278 AH, 891 CE, and his death as 310 AH, 922 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 135}.

## Lineage and imamate
He was the son of al-Hadi, who fought the Qarmatians and their leader Ali ibn al-Fadl. Al-Zirikli says he took up the office after his father's death and was addressed as al-Murtada li-Din Allah; it lasted about six months and he then withdrew {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 135}.

## Death and works
He died in Sa'da and was buried beside his father {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 135}. His books include al-Idah, al-Nawazil and Jawab masa'il Mahdi, all on jurisprudence {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 7 p. 135}.
