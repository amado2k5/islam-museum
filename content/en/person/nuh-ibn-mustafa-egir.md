## Overview
Nuh ibn Mustafa al-Rumi al-Hanafi, resident of Egypt, is described by al-Zirikli as a jurist and Sufi {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}. He was born and educated in Amasya and was mufti of Konya; he then settled in Cairo and died there {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}. The entry gives his death as 1070 AH, 1660 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}.

## His works
Al-Zirikli names among his books, all in manuscript unless stated otherwise: «al-Qawl al-Dall ala Hayat al-Khidr wa Wujud al-Abdal» (on the life of al-Khidr and the existence of the abdal); «Tarikh Misr» (History of Egypt), of which a copy is in Paris (6036), noted by Brockelmann; «al-Sayf al-Mujzim fi Qital man Hatak Hurmat al-Haram», in six chapters; «Sharh Du'a al-Qunut»; and «Nata'ij al-Nazar», a gloss on jurisprudence {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}. He also left three collections of treatises: the first holds twenty on jurisprudence, Sufism, tawhid, virtues and terminology, the second five on various jurisprudential studies, and the third sixty-seven {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}. The entry further lists a gloss on «al-Durar wa al-Ghurar»; «al-Durr al-Munazzam fi Manaqib al-Imam al-A'zam», a manuscript in the Ahmadiyya library in Tunis (3860); and a treatise on the difference between the hadith qudsi, the Qur'an and the prophetic hadith, noted by Taymur {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}.
