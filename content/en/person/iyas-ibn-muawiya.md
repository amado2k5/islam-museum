## Summary
Abu Wathila Iyas ibn Mu'awiya ibn Qurra al-Mazni — eloquent of tongue, counted a byword for intelligence and quickness; true in physiognomy, seasoned in affairs, famous for extreme cleverness — proverbs were coined on him; 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz had appointed him qadi of Basra. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] counts Iyas ibn Mu'awiya among the most perspicacious men of his time and says that al-Mizzi gives stories of his firasa in his entry in Tahdhib al-Kamal. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:18:00} Among them: a dihqan asked him how an intoxicant can be forbidden when dates, kushuth and water are each lawful on their own; Iyas answered that dust, water and straw thrown at a man separately would not hurt him, but mixed and dried into a clod they could kill him. Al-Heweny calls this a rational proof for a rational demand, and explains kushuth as a rootless plant that clings to branches, used in making nabidh. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:21:30}

He relates that Iyas, as a young man in Syria in the time of Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, brought a case against an old man who was a friend of the judge. Told "are you not ashamed?", he said, "the truth is greater than he"; told "I do not think you will say anything true until you leave", he said, "there is no god but God". Abd al-Malik ordered that he be given what he asked and sent out of Syria. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:23:00}
