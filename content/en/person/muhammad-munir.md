# Muhammad Munir

## Summary
Muhammad Munir (1895–1979) was a Pakistani judge who served as the second Chief Justice of Pakistan from 29 June 1954 to 2 May 1960. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} {cite:wikidata-muhammad-munir} His judgment in *Federation of Pakistan v. Maulvi Tamizuddin Khan* founded the doctrine of necessity in Pakistani constitutional law. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} The Urdu article confirms the tenure and his place in the succession of chief justices. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-ur} No Arabic Wikipedia article exists for him, so this entry rests on the English and Urdu articles and Wikidata.

## Early life and the bar
He was born in 1895 at Amritsar into a Kakkyzai Pashtun family. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} He graduated in English literature from Government College University, Lahore, and took his law degree at the University Law College of Punjab University. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} He began practice at Amritsar in 1921 and moved to Lahore the following year. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en}

## The bench
He became an assistant advocate-general of Punjab in 1937 and the first president of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal of British India in 1940, and was elevated to the Lahore bench in 1942. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} In 1947 he and Justice Din Muhammad represented the All-India Muslim League on the Punjab Boundary Commission, and in 1948 he chaired the Pakistan Pay Commission. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} He became Chief Justice of the Lahore High Court in 1949. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en}

## Chief Justice of Pakistan
Appointed by Governor-General Malik Ghulam Muhammad, he succeeded Abdul Rashid as Chief Justice of Pakistan on 29 June 1954 and held the office until 2 May 1960, when Muhammad Shahabuddin followed him. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-ur} In the Tamizuddin Khan case he invoked the doctrine of necessity to validate the dissolution of the Constituent Assembly on 24 October 1954 — a judgment the English article notes was widely criticised as a blow to democracy in Pakistan. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} He also validated the 1958 military coup on the same doctrine, and chaired the Delimitation Commission from June 1956 to July 1958. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en}

## Later writing and death
In retirement he wrote *From Jinnah to Zia* (1980), arguing that Jinnah had stood for a secular state — a claim the English article records as drawing objections from contemporaries who said Jinnah's speeches had been quoted out of context. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} He died in 1979; the Urdu article's lead and Wikidata give 26 June 1981 instead, and the conflict is recorded in the museum's data. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-munir-ur} {cite:wikidata-muhammad-munir} No consulted source names a religion or school for him, and the museum records his affiliation as unknown.
