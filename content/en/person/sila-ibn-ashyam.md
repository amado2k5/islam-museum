## Overview
Silat ibn Ashyam Abu al-Sahba' al-Adawi, husband of Mu'adha al-Adawiyya. He was killed as a martyr with Yazid ibn Ziyad at Janza in the battle of Kabul {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
In a reflection answering those who presume upon their deeds before God, Ibn al-Jawzi sets Sila ibn Ashyam against the presumptuous worshipper: the lion fled from him, and in his night prayer he would say, "My Lord, save me from the Fire; would one like me ask for Paradise?" {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:27:00} [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] introduces him: his kunya was Abu al-Sahba', his wife was Mu'adha al-Adawiyya, well known in narration, he narrated only one hadith, from Ibn Abbas, living in seclusion and renunciation and narrating little, and he and his son were killed in battle with the Turks in Sijistan in the year 62 of the Hijra. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:28:00} That date is the one al-Heweny gives.

He narrates, by way of Hammad ibn Salama from Thabit al-Bunani, that Sila was on campaign with his son and told him to go forward so that he might count him with God; the son advanced and was killed, then Sila was killed; and that Mu'adha told the women who gathered at her house: welcome, if you have come to congratulate me; otherwise go back. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:29:30} He quotes al-Dhahabi that the story of the dates is an established miracle with more than one chain: in a time of flood Sila found no food, a non-Arab offered him bread with pig fat, which he refused, and then a basket of fresh dates wrapped in a white cloth fell behind him at a season when there were no fresh dates; he ate and carried the stones away. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:30:30}

The story of the lion is told by Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak, and through him by Abu Nu'aym in Hilyat al-Awliya', from Hammad ibn Ja'far ibn Zayd from his father: Ja'far ibn Zayd went on campaign to Kabul with Sila in the army; Sila went into a thicket to pray, a lion came, Ja'far climbed a tree, and when Sila had finished he told the lion to seek its provision elsewhere, and it turned away; then Sila prayed that God save him from the Fire. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:44:30} Al-Heweny notes the critics' disagreement over Hammad ibn Ja'far and says such stories are treated leniently because they are not hadith attributed to the Prophet that bind all people; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:46:30} and he sees Sila's "would one like me ask for Paradise?" as a passing state, not a rule, since the Prophet often asked God for Paradise. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:47:00}

## In Ibn al-Mubarak's Kitab al-Zuhd
In the chapter [[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Ibn al-Mubarak]] gives to Amir ibn Abd Qays and Sila, he reports from Ja'far ibn Zayd al-Abdi the story of the Kabul campaign: he watched Sila at night and saw him praying in a thicket; a lion came and he did not break off his prayer, then told it to seek its provision elsewhere. In the same report his mule strayed with its load and returned when he prayed, and he and Hisham ibn Amir charged the enemy and broke them. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 295} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 296} The editor notes that Abu Nu'aym gave the first part of the report in the Hilya. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 296}

He reports from Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid ibn Jabir as something that "reached him", that is, without a connected chain, that the Prophet said there would be in his community a man called Sila ibn Ashyam through whose intercession so many would enter paradise. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 297} Through Humayd ibn Hilal he reports from Sila himself the story of his journey through the villages of Nahr Tira at the time of the floods: he refused food containing pig fat, and then a basket of fresh dates fell behind him; Awf ibn Dalham says he saw the cloth with Sila's wife, wrapped around her Qur'an, until it was lost. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 297} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 298}

He reports from Abu Wa'il that he asked Abu al-Ala' Sila whether the plague had struck his family, and Sila said he feared more that it would pass them by than that it would strike them. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 307}
