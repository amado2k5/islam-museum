## Overview
Muhammad Zahid ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Kawthari was a Hanafi jurist of Circassian origin who also busied himself with literature and biography. Al-A'lam gives his years as 1296 AH, 1879 CE, to 1371 AH, 1952 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 128}.

## His life
He was born and raised in a village in the district of Duzce, east of Istanbul, studied jurisprudence at the Fatih mosque there, taught in it, and became head of the teaching council. The Unionists persecuted him during the First World War because he opposed their plan to put modern sciences in place of the religious sciences in most class hours. When the Kemalists took power and, in al-Zirikli's phrase, openly professed atheism, there was an intention to arrest him, and he boarded a ship to Alexandria in 1341 AH, 1922 CE. He moved for a time between Egypt and Syria, then settled in Cairo as an employee of Dar al-Mahfuzat, translating its Turkish documents into Arabic, and died in Cairo {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 128}.

## Languages and annotations
He was fluent in Arabic, Turkish, Persian and Circassian, with a slight accent in his Arabic. He wrote many annotations on works printed in his day, in jurisprudence, hadith and the study of narrators {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 128}.

## His works
Al-Zirikli names, all printed: Ta'nib al-Khatib ala ma saqahu fi tarjamat Abi Hanifa min al-akadhib, where the Khatib means the author of Tarikh Baghdad; al-Nukat al-tarifa fi al-tahaddduth an rudud Ibn Abi Shayba ala Abi Hanifa; al-Istibsar fi al-tahaddduth an al-jabr wa al-ikhtiyar; and treatises on the lives of Imam Zufar, Abu Yusuf the judge, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Shaybani, al-Badr al-Ayni, the two imams al-Hasan ibn Ziyad and Muhammad ibn Shuja', and al-Tahawi. About a hundred articles of his were gathered by al-Sayyid Ahmad Khayri in Maqalat al-Kawthari (printed), and some scholars criticised him in Al-Kawthari wa ta'liqatuh (printed) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 128}.
