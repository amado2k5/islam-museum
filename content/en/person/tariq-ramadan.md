## Summary
Tariq Ramadan (born 26 August 1962) is a Swiss philosopher, writer and scholar of Islam, long among the most prominent and most contested Muslim intellectuals in Europe. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

He held the chair in Contemporary Islamic Studies at St Antony's College, Oxford, was a visiting professor at Hamad Bin Khalifa University in Qatar and directed the Research Center of Islamic Legislation and Ethics in Doha, and wrote and lectured on Islam, Muslim identity in Europe and reformist thought. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

His public career was overtaken from 2017 by sexual-assault allegations, which he denied; Swiss and French courts have since convicted him, as the legal section of this record sets out in attributed detail. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

## Early life and family
He was born in Geneva, Switzerland, on 26 August 1962, to an Egyptian Muslim family: his father was Said Ramadan, a prominent figure of the Muslim Brotherhood exiled from Egypt by Gamal Abdel Nasser, and his mother was Wafa al-Banna, the eldest daughter of Hassan al-Banna, who founded the Muslim Brotherhood in Egypt in 1928; the liberal reformer Gamal al-Banna was his great-uncle. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en} {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-ar}

He married in 1986 and is the father of four children; his wife, born in Brittany, France, converted from Catholicism to Islam and took the name Iman, and the article records that the couple live separately. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

## Education and academic career
He took an MA in French literature and a PhD in Arabic and Islamic studies at the University of Geneva, and wrote a further PhD dissertation on Friedrich Nietzsche, titled Nietzsche as a Historian of Philosophy. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

He taught at the Collège de Saussure in Lancy and has claimed a lectureship at the University of Fribourg from 1996 to 2003, which the university publicly denied in 2018; in 2004 he accepted a tenured chair at the University of Notre Dame in the United States, which he never took up after his visa was revoked, and from October 2005 he taught at St Antony's College, Oxford, on a visiting fellowship. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

In 2007 he won the professorship in Islamic studies at Leiden and then declined it amid severe criticism from academics and politicians who called him a radical Islamist and a "wolf in sheep's clothing"; he was also a guest professor at Erasmus University Rotterdam until the city and the university dismissed him in August 2009 over the programme Islam & Life that he hosted on Iran's Press TV, a dismissal he called Islamophobic — the Rotterdam district court ruled in 2012 that the university, though not the municipality, had acted carelessly in dismissing him at short notice. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

From September 2009 he was appointed to the chair in Contemporary Islamic Studies at Oxford; he founded and presided over the European Muslim Network in Brussels, advised the European Union and, in September 2005, joined a task force of the British government. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

As of 2009 he was, by his own account as the article reports it, persona non grata in Tunisia, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Libya and Syria, because of his criticism of their undemocratic regimes, and he is considered persona non grata in Israel. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

## The American visa affair
In February 2004 he accepted the Henry R. Luce chair at Notre Dame's Kroc Institute; a visa granted on 5 May was revoked on 28 July, with the State Department later citing the Patriot Act's ideological-exclusion provision and then concluding that donations totalling $940 that he had made between 1998 and 2002 to two Palestinian charities — designated by the US Treasury in 2003 as terrorist fundraising organisations for alleged links to Hamas — constituted material support to a terrorist organisation, on the ground that he "reasonably should have known" of their activities. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

The American Civil Liberties Union sued on behalf of three scholarly bodies in 2006; a district judge upheld the government in 2007, but on 17 July 2009 a federal appeals panel reversed, holding that the government was required to confront him with the allegation and give him the opportunity to show he neither knew nor should have known. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

The ban was thereafter lifted by the Obama administration, and on 8 April 2010 he spoke at Cooper Union in New York, his first public appearance in the United States since the exclusion began. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

## Views
This section reports his positions as the sources present them; it is not a verdict by the museum.

His work addresses Islamic theology and the place of Muslims in the West and in Muslim-majority countries; he prioritises interpretation of the Qur'an's meaning over literal reading, and has at times described himself with the construction "Salafi reformist". {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

He rejects the division of the world into dar al-Islam and dar al-harb as unmentioned in the Qur'an, holds that the Islamic message is a commitment to universalism and to the welfare of non-Muslims, opposes all capital punishment while arguing the Muslim world should abolish it from within, and condemns suicide bombing and terrorism as never justifiable. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

In a televised debate in 2003 Nicolas Sarkozy accused him of defending the stoning of adulterers; Ramadan replied that he opposed corporal punishments, stoning and the death penalty, and called for a moratorium on such practices to open debate among scholars — a position he defended on the ground that the texts involved are taken seriously by Muslims and that condemnation alone changes nothing. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

## Reception and criticism
This section sets out judgements as the sources attribute them; it is not a verdict by the museum.

An article in The American Prospect praised his corpus as "a steady and unyielding assault on Muslim insularity, self-righteousness, and self-pity", and Foreign Policy's readers listed him among the world's top hundred global thinkers in 2008, 2009, 2010 and 2012. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

Caroline Fourest's book Frère Tariq concluded, from her analysis of his books, interviews and recordings, that he is "the political heir of his grandfather", whose discourse he repeats, and accused him of saying one thing to Muslim followers and another to Western audiences; other French critics quoted in the article called him a fascist and a "small Le Pen" and accused him of antisemitism and of misusing the alter-globalisation movement. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

Ramadan denies contacts with terrorists, the charges of antisemitism and the charge of double talk, attributing them to misinterpretation and to journalists repeating uncorroborated propaganda: "There is double-talk, yes, but there is also double-hearing. That is what I want to challenge." {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

## The allegations and the convictions
This section records the proceedings exactly as the English article reports them at the revision checked (15 October 2026). It is not a verdict by the museum: allegations are allegations, his denials are recorded with them, and only the courts' outcomes are stated as outcomes, with their appeal status.

In October 2017 the secular activist Henda Ayari filed a complaint at Rouen alleging that Ramadan had sexually assaulted her in a Paris hotel — an incident she had described in her 2016 book without naming him — and a second woman, a disabled French convert known in reports as "Christelle", filed a complaint days later alleging rape in 2009; a third woman alleged pornographic messages and blackmail, and four Swiss women came forward in November 2017 alleging that he had molested them as teenagers. Ramadan denied the accusations, and his lawyer announced a counter-suit for defamation. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

On 7 November 2017 the University of Oxford announced that, by mutual agreement and with immediate effect, he had taken a leave of absence, the statement noting that an agreed leave of absence implied no acceptance or presumption of guilt; in January 2018 he was denied entry to Qatar. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

On 31 January 2018 French police took him into custody, and after two days he was formally charged with two counts of rape and held at Fleury-Mérogis prison; further complainants followed in March and April 2018, including a Geneva woman who alleged a sexual assault with aggravating cruelty in September 2008, while a complaint from Washington, DC produced no charges. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

In the course of 2018 he admitted a consensual sexual relationship with the third complainant and, later, consensual sex with Ayari and Christelle, while admitting in June 2018 to five extramarital affairs and to conduct inconsistent with his principles; the judges that month cleared him of the third accusation as a consensual affair. A further allegation from Lyon followed in August 2019, and in February 2020 he was formally charged with raping two more women. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

In September 2019 he described the allegations, the indictment, his custody and the media coverage as "state racism" and compared his case to the Dreyfus affair — a comparison that drew indignation, among others from the editor Laurent Joffrin, who called it "ridiculous". {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

**The Swiss case.** A Geneva court acquitted him in May 2023 of the charges arising from the 2008 incident; on appeal, the Swiss appeals court convicted him in September 2024 of rape and sexual coercion and sentenced him to three years' imprisonment, two of them suspended. His appeal to the Federal Supreme Court of Switzerland was rejected in August 2025, making the Swiss conviction final. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

**The French case.** In March 2026 the Paris criminal court convicted him of rape and of rape of a vulnerable person, for offences the article dates to 2009–2016 against three women, and sentenced him to eighteen years' imprisonment, with eight years of judicial supervision, a ban on contact with the victims and a permanent ban from French territory after the sentence; he was tried in absentia after failing to appear — a medical assessment had found him fit to stand trial — and the court issued an arrest warrant. The article records that this judgment is not final and may be appealed. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

**His health.** Since his first detention he has been hospitalised several times for multiple sclerosis; his lawyer has said doctors consider his condition incompatible with detention, while the courts, after multiple medical examinations, maintained his detention as compatible with it. {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}

## Books
He has authored around thirty books and several hundred articles in French and English, among them, in English: To Be a European Muslim (1999), Western Muslims and the Future of Islam (2004), In the Footsteps of the Prophet (2007), Radical Reform: Islamic Ethics and Liberation (2008), What I Believe (2009), Islam and the Arab Awakening (2012) and Islam: The Essentials (2017). {cite:wikipedia-tariq-ramadan-en}
