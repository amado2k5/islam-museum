## Summary
Abu al-Nadr Muhammad ibn al-Sa'ib al-Kalbi of Kufa, the exegete, genealogist and reporter of history, was the father of [[person/hisham-ibn-al-kalbi|Hisham ibn al-Kalbi]]. [[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] says in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] that he narrated from [[person/amir-al-shabi|al-Sha'bi]] and others, and that his son Hisham and Abu Mu'awiya narrated from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 556} [[person/ibn-al-nadim|Ibn al-Nadim]] says his son Hisham learned from him, and that he found in Ibn al-Kufi's hand that he was one of the genealogists. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 299-300}

## His memory, in his own telling
Al-Dhahabi quotes Abu Mu'awiya: al-Kalbi said, I memorised what no one else memorised — I learned the Qur'an by heart in six or seven days — and I forgot what no one else forgot: I gripped my beard to trim what lay below my fist and cut what lay above it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 556} He relates through Yahya ibn Ya'la from his father that Ya'la used to read the Qur'an with him, and heard him say that he had fallen ill and forgotten what he knew, then went to the family of Muhammad, who spat into his mouth, and he remembered what he had forgotten; Ya'la said: then I will narrate nothing from you after this, and left him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 557}

## "From Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas"
His Mizan entry turns on his exegetical chain from Abu Salih from [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]]. Al-Dhahabi quotes Sufyan: al-Kalbi told me that Abu Salih said to him, look at everything you have narrated from me from Ibn Abbas and do not narrate it; and, through al-Bukhari from Ali ibn al-Madini from Yahya from Sufyan: al-Kalbi told me, everything I have told you from Abu Salih is a lie. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 556} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 557} Ibn Hibban says he narrated tafsir from Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas, although Abu Salih never saw Ibn Abbas, and al-Kalbi heard from Abu Salih no more than a word here and there. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 559} Al-Dhahabi then lists a series of hadiths he attributed to the Prophet through this chain as examples of his objectionable reports. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 557} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 558}

## The critics' verdicts
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: abandoned and suspected (al-Qattan, Ibn Mahdi, Ibn Ma'in, al-Juzjani, al-Daraqutni, Ibn Hibban, Ahmad)**: al-Bukhari said: Abu al-Nadr al-Kalbi — Yahya and Ibn Mahdi abandoned him; Ibn Ma'in said: not trustworthy; [[person/ibrahim-ibn-yaqub-al-juzajani|al-Juzjani]] and others: a liar; [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] and others: abandoned; [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]]: his doctrine and the plainness of his lying are too evident to need elaborate description, and it is not permitted even to mention him in books, let alone cite him as proof; Ahmad ibn Zuhayr asked [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] whether one may read al-Kalbi's tafsir, and he said no. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 557} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 559}
- **Sunni: accepted in exegesis, not in hadith (Ibn Adi; reported from al-Thawri)**: Ibn Adi said: Sufyan, Shu'ba and others narrated from al-Kalbi and accepted him in exegesis, but in hadith he has objectionable reports, especially from Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas; Ya'la ibn Ubayd reports [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|al-Thawri]] as saying: beware of al-Kalbi — and when told that he himself narrated from him, he said: I know his truth from his lies. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 557} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 558}
:::

## The doctrine attributed to him
Al-Dhahabi quotes Yazid ibn Zuray' describing him as a Saba'i, al-A'mash saying: beware of these Saba'is, for I found that people called them the liars; Ibn Hibban explaining the term as those who say that Ali did not die and will return to the world; and Hammam reporting him as saying: I am a Saba'i. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 557} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 558} These are the words of his critics among the hadith scholars as al-Dhahabi transmits them; the Mizan does not give his own account or his followers' account of his beliefs.
