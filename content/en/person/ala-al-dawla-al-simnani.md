## Overview
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Simnani, titled Ala al-Dawla and Rukn al-Din, was a researcher among the Sufi scholars and a Shafi'i. He was born at Simnan, between al-Rayy and Damghan, and died in Baghdad; al-Zirikli gives his birth as 695 AH, 1261 CE, and his death as 736 AH, 1336 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 223}.

## Life
Al-Zirikli reports that he disparaged and declared [[person/ibn-arabi-muhyi-al-din|Ibn al-Arabi]] a disbeliever; this is al-Zirikli's report of his stance, not a judgement of the museum {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 223}. It is said that his writings exceed 300, and that he was generous, spending all the income of his estates, about ninety thousand a year, in charity {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 223}. He associated with the Tatars (Mongols) in the early days of their rule, then withdrew, and lived in Tabriz and Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 223}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists among his surviving books: al-Falah li-Ahl al-Salah, in manuscript in the Chester Beatty library; al-Urwa li-Ahl al-Khalwa, in manuscript in Dar al-Kutub; Safwat al-Urwa, in manuscript in the Lala Li library (no. 1432) and Dar al-Kutub, which treats the legal proprieties and protects the retreats of the Sufis from the ecstatic utterances and idle talk attributed to them; and Tuhfat al-Salikin, in manuscript in the Fatih library (no. 2567) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 223}.
