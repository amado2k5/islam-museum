## Summary
Iraqi calligrapher (1917–1973), counted among the greatest calligraphers of the twentieth century, reaching the summit in thuluth and naskh. He wrote Qur'an manuscripts, mosque inscriptions and Iraqi currency designs, and authored Qawa'id al-Khatt al-Arabi (Rules of Arabic Calligraphy), now a standard reference. {cite:wikipedia-hashim-al-baghdadi}

## Life and formation

Hashim Muhammad al-Baghdadi was born in Baghdad in 1917 and was drawn to calligraphy from childhood, apprenticing under the leading Iraqi and resident Turkish masters and forming himself on the Hamid al-Amidi school's discipline of letterform. {cite:wikipedia-hashim-al-baghdadi}

He excelled until he became teacher to generations of calligraphers, led the Iraqi calligraphers' association founded in Baghdad, and worked for state institutions designing panels, stamps and currency. {cite:wikipedia-hashim-al-baghdadi}

## Works

His works include inscriptions for Iraqi and foreign mosques, among them al-Azhar mosque in Cairo and mosques of Baghdad; masterfully penned Qur'an manuscripts; and designs for Iraqi banknotes and official seals. {cite:wikipedia-hashim-al-baghdadi}

He authored Qawa'id al-Khatt al-Arabi (Rules of Arabic Calligraphy), setting out the principles of thuluth and naskh in pedagogical order, making it one of the most-taught primers in the Arab East and the Muslim world. {cite:wikipedia-hashim-al-baghdadi}

## Legacy

Al-Baghdadi is described as the reviver of Arabic calligraphy in modern Iraq, joining the precision of the Ottoman school to a distinctly Iraqi grace; he left schools of students across Iraq, Turkey and the Arab world. {cite:wikipedia-hashim-al-baghdadi}

He died in Baghdad in 1973 of a heart attack at the height of his powers; calligraphy museums and manuscript collections preserve his work, and his rules are still taught today. {cite:wikipedia-hashim-al-baghdadi}
