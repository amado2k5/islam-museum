## Summary
Salman bin Hamad Al Khalifa (1894–1961) was Hakim (ruler) of Bahrain from the death of his father, Hamad bin Isa, on 20 February 1942 until his own death on 2 November 1961. Raised as heir under his father, he devoted his two decades of rule to health and education — occupational clinics for pearl divers, expanded hospitals, and free health care for citizens and visitors in his final year — while personally auditing the state's budgets and oil revenues. He is not the present Crown Prince of the same name (born 1969). {cite:wikipedia-salman-bin-hamad-al-khalifa-en} {cite:wikidata-salman-bin-hamad-al-khalifa}

## Early life and succession
Salman was born on 10 October 1894 — the English biography's prose gives 1895, a variant the data record preserves while following the lead, the Arabic article and Wikidata — and was raised under his father's care, studying the Qur'an and religious subjects and other fields with tutors his father provided. Approved as heir by his father and the notables of the Al Khalifa house, he represented his father on many official occasions. On Hamad's death on 20 February 1942 he succeeded as Hakim; his accession was celebrated officially on 6 April 1942. {cite:wikipedia-salman-bin-hamad-al-khalifa-en} {cite:wikipedia-salman-bin-hamad-al-khalifa-ar}

## Reign
Salman's ruling passion was public welfare. Familiar with the diseases of the pearl trade, he promoted reforms of divers' labour conditions against the resistance of the traditional elite and pushed funding for hospitals, arguing that health was possible in the end only through education; in the last year of his reign the government offered free health care to citizens and visitors alike. He reviewed the annual budget personally and pressed the advisers over the oil revenue quota system and the duty-free refining of foreign oil, which he saw as a threat to the country's long-term fortunes; Gulf Air was founded in his term on the basis of a 1914 land-law provision he had noticed. In 1948 he organised repeated donation drives for Palestinians displaced by the war — a committee that raised 66,000 rupees, a merchants' conference that collected 77,000 rupees in under fifteen days, and a personal donation of 100,000 rupees to UNRWA. His uncle Abdullah bin Isa Al Khalifa led a parallel drive. {cite:wikipedia-salman-bin-hamad-al-khalifa-en}

## Death and succession
Salman kept a demanding public schedule into old age, rising early to receive citizens' petitions at his office in the Bab al-Bahrain before turning to interviews, meetings and correspondence, and refused his doctors' advice to rest. He died on 2 November 1961 at his Safra residence, after an illness of about two years, and was succeeded in December 1961 by his son Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa. His filiation is recorded once, in his own data record, as a child of Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa, whose record this museum built in the same wave. {cite:wikipedia-salman-bin-hamad-al-khalifa-en} {cite:wikipedia-salman-bin-hamad-al-khalifa-ar}
