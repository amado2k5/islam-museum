## Summary
Qays ibn al-Rabi' al-Asadi of Kufa is described by [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] as one of the vessels of learning, truthful in himself but of poor memory. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 393} He is a case where his teachers' praise of his zeal for hadith sits beside the critics' reservations about his memory.

## Qays the wanderer
Qurad said he heard [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]] say: we never came to a shaykh in Kufa without finding that Qays had got there before us; we called him "Qays the wanderer". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 393} [[person/sharik-ibn-abdallah|Sharik]] said no one grew up in Kufa more eager for hadith than Qays, and on the day of his burial is reported to have said: he has left no one like him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 394} Shu'ba used to say "keep to Qays" and objected to Yahya al-Qattan's criticism of him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 394} When people in [[person/abu-dawud-al-tayalisi|Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi]]'s session in Basra said they had no need of him, Abu Dawud said: write, for I hold seven thousand of his hadiths rattling in my breast. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 394}

## The critics divided
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: praise and acceptance**: Shu'ba praised him; Abu Hatim said his place is truthfulness though he is not strong; Ibn Adi concluded that most of his narrations are sound and that the right view is Shu'ba's — no harm in him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 393} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 396}
- **Sunni: weakening**: Ibn Ma'in said weak, once "his hadith is not to be written"; [[person/waki-ibn-al-jarrah|Waki']] and [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ibn al-Madini]] weakened him; [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]] said abandoned; [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] said weak; asked why his hadith was left, [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said: he inclined to Shi'ism, made many mistakes and has objectionable hadiths. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 393}
- **Sunni: a distinction by age (Ibn Hibban)**: having sifted his reports as given by early and late students, he found him truthful and trustworthy when young; when he grew old his memory worsened and he was tried with a bad son who slipped material into his hadith. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 394}
:::

## The son who was his bane
Affan said: when I came to Kufa we went and sat with him, and his son kept prompting him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 394} Ibn Numayr said: he had a son who was his bane; hadith scholars examined his books, found his hadith objectionable and thought his son had altered them. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 394} Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna reported that Shu'ba and Sufyan narrated from him, that Yahya al-Qattan and [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-mahdi|Ibn Mahdi]] did not, and that Ibn Mahdi narrated from him and then stopped. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 395}

## Governor of al-Mada'in
Muhammad ibn Ubayd al-Tanafisi said Abu Ja'far appointed him over al-Mada'in, reported harshness in his punishments and that a man died under a prescribed penalty he applied, after which his standing faded; he added that Qays was in their eyes no lower than Sufyan. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 395} Among his objectionable reports al-Dhahabi cites one about two amulets of al-Hasan and al-Husayn, which he calls very objectionable. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 395}
