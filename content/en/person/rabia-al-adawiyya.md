## Summary
Rabi'a bint Isma'il al-Adawiyya was a great Sufi and famous devout woman of [[place/basra|Basra]]. [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] says she reached a high station in the fine points of the Sufi path and that the leading Sufis of her time consulted her on them. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.434}

## With Sufyan al-Thawri
Kahhala relates that [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]] used to say, "Take us to the teacher of manners; I find no one to rest with once I leave her", and that when he said in her presence "O my sorrow!" she told him: "Do not lie — say, O how little my sorrow!" {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.434}

## Her insight and sayings
He quotes Ibn al-Jawzi that she was perceptive, and that a sign of her understanding was her saying: "I ask God's forgiveness for how little truth there is in my saying, I ask God's forgiveness." {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.435} Among her sayings: the lover of God does not cease his moaning and longing until he rests with his Beloved; and among her counsels: hide your good deeds as you hide your bad ones. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.435} A man once brought her forty dinars to help with her needs; she wept and said she was ashamed to ask this world of the One who owns it, so how could she want it from one who does not? {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.435} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.436}

## Her devotion
She wept much and prayed through the night, taking a light sleep at her prayer place when dawn broke; the place where she prostrated was like a pool from her tears, she trembled when death was mentioned, and kept her shroud ready. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.435} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.436} By the age of eighty she had become "like a worn-out water-skin", nearly falling when she walked. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.436}

## How scholars have seen her
Kahhala cites Massignon that Rabi'a al-Adawiyya and Rabi'a al-Qaysiyya were ascetics of the Basran school whose zeal for renunciation led them to treat various mystical states, and that researchers on sainthood regard Rabi'a as the greatest woman saint. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.435} Mustafa Abd al-Raziq disagrees, finding it forced to credit her with taking up fine questions of law and theology, but calls her the first to lay down the principles of love and sorrow in the structure of Islamic Sufism, so that what Sufi literature later poured out on these two themes is "a breath of her breath". {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.435}

## Death
When death came she told her servant Abda not to announce it and to shroud her in the hair cloak she wore for night prayer, and Abda did so. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.436} Kahhala says she was buried in [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]] in 135 AH; his note adds that *al-Nujum al-Zahira* gives 180 AH, and another report 185 AH. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.436}

## For young visitors
Rabi'a al-Adawiyya was a woman of Basra famous for her love of God, her long prayers and her tears; great scholars came to visit her and ask her questions.

## Research notes
- Her date of death is disputed (135, 180 or 185 AH), and burial in Jerusalem is as Kahhala gives it; many reports about her come from later sources, and the museum gives them as attributed to Kahhala and his sources (Ibn Khallikan, Sifat al-Safwa, al-Sha'rani and others).
