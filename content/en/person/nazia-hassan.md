Nazia Hassan (3 April 1965 – 13 August 2000) was a Pakistani singer and songwriter, known as the "Queen of South Asian Pop". Born in Karachi and brought up between Karachi and London, she shot to fame at fifteen when she sang "Aap Jaisa Koi" for the Indian film Qurbani (1980).{cite:wikipedia-nazia-hassan-en}{cite:wikipedia-nazia-hassan-ur}{cite:wikidata-nazia-hassan}

With her younger brother Zoheb she formed the duo Nazia and Zoheb, whose records sold over 65 million copies worldwide and overturned the South Asian music industry in the 1980s.{cite:wikipedia-nazia-hassan-en} Alongside music she studied law in London and worked in philanthropy. She died of lung cancer in London aged 35, and was buried at Hendon Cemetery after a funeral prayer at Hendon Mosque; Pakistan awarded her the Pride of Performance posthumously in 2002.{cite:wikipedia-nazia-hassan-en}{cite:wikipedia-nazia-hassan-ar}
