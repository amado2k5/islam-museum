## Overview
Abd al-Qadir ibn Mustafa al-Maghribi al-Tarabulsi was deputy president of the Arab Academy of Damascus and one of the scholars of language and literature. His family came from Tunisia, from a house called Darghuth, and he was born in Latakia in 1284 AH (1868 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}. He grew up in Tripoli in Syria, read with his father and with some scholars of Damascus and Constantinople, became known as al-Maghribi, and was in contact with Jamal al-Din al-Afghani and Muhammad Abduh {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}.

## Journalism and teaching
Muhammad Abduh encouraged him to travel to Egypt, and he went there in 1905, shortly before Abduh died, and turned to journalism, writing a great deal for the major newspapers {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}. When the Ottoman constitution was proclaimed in 1908 he returned to Tripoli and issued the newspaper "al-Burhan", which he closed when the First World War began in 1914 {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}. He taught at the Salahiyya college in Jerusalem, then settled in Damascus and edited the newspaper "al-Sharq" until the war ended {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}. When the Arab Academy was founded he was among its members and then its deputy president; he was appointed lecturer in Arabic and its literature at the Syrian university, became a member of the academies of Egypt and Iraq, and over twenty years gave a large number of public lectures in the academy's hall in Damascus {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}.

## His works
His first book was "al-Ishtiqaq wa-al-Ta'rib", printed in 1908. His other printed books include "al-Bayyinat", a collection of his articles in two parts; "al-Akhlaq wa-al-Wajibat"; "Mudhakkirat Jamal al-Din al-Afghani"; "Muhadarat"; "Tafsir Juz' Tabarak"; and "'Ala Hamish al-Tafsir". Some of his writings remain in manuscript {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}.

## Death
Al-Zirikli describes him as tireless despite his age and as one who sought out the witty remark in his talk, lectures and articles. He was struck by a car in Cairo and treated in a hospital there for nearly three months, then travelled to Damascus, where he did not live long and died in 1375 AH (1956 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 4 p. 10}.
