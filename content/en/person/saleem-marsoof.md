## Summary
Justice Saleem Marsoof is a Sri Lankan jurist and international arbitrator who served on the Supreme Court of Sri Lanka and briefly acted as Chief Justice. Within the country's Muslim community he is known as the chair of the committee tasked with reforming the Muslim Marriage and Divorce Act — the post most identified with his name in public life. {cite:wikipedia-saleem-marsoof}

## Judicial career
Trained as a lawyer, Marsoof rose through the judiciary to the Court of Appeal, which he headed as its President, and then to the Supreme Court, where he sat on benches deciding constitutional and fundamental-rights questions. He served as Acting Chief Justice, placing him among the most senior Muslim jurists in Sri Lanka's history. {cite:wikipedia-saleem-marsoof}

## Muslim law reform
His most debated public role came as chair of the committee appointed to review and reform Sri Lanka's Muslim personal law — the colonial-era statute governing marriage, divorce and related matters for the island's Muslims. The committee's deliberations, over questions such as the minimum marriage age, the qualifications of qazis and the place of women in the Quazi court system, ran into deep disagreement between reformers and conservative scholars, and its report remains a reference point in a continuing argument inside the community. The museum records the dispute attributed: supporters credit the committee with opening questions long closed; critics held that it moved beyond its mandate. {cite:muslim500-saleem-marsoof}

## Arbitration and scholarship
In parallel with the bench, Marsoof built an international practice in arbitration and mediation, and has taught and published on law, serving in academic and professional bodies in Sri Lanka and abroad. {cite:wikipedia-saleem-marsoof}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as the leading Muslim jurist of his generation in Sri Lanka — a figure whose influence runs through both the state's highest courts and the community's own legal debates. {cite:muslim500-saleem-marsoof}
