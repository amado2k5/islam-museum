## Overview
Jibril is the angel charged with revelation, known among Muslims as "the Trustworthy" and "the Holy Spirit" who brought the Qur'an down upon the Prophet. He is the one who came to the Prophet in the form of a Bedouin asking about Islam, faith and excellence and the signs of the Hour {cite:sahih-muslim}, who ascended with him on the night of the Mi'raj from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque then to what is beyond them to the Lote-tree of the Utmost Boundary {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}, and who reviewed the Qur'an with him on a night of each Ramadan {cite:sahih-muslim}.

## The name and its meaning
"Jibril" with a fatha on the jim, a sukūn on the single ba', and a kasra on the ra'; it is also narrated as "Jibrā'īl" and "Jibrīl". The scholars differed on its derivation: some derived it from "jabr" meaning strength and servanthood, saying "Jibrā'īl" means the servant of the Mighty God; others said it is a non-Arabic or Aramaic name. The more common view among the exegetes is that they read it "Jibrā'īl" with the single ba' {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## In the Qur'an
- He is named explicitly in the verse of enmity: {whoever is an enemy to God and His angels and His messengers and Jibril and Mīkāl} [[passage/quran-2-98|Al-Baqara 98]], and he is mentioned with Mika'il in one place; in many other places he is mentioned by his description, not his name.
- His Qur'anic descriptions: "the Holy Spirit" who brought the Qur'an down upon the Prophet's heart {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-26-193|Al-Shu'ara' 193]], [[passage/quran-81-19|At-Takwir 19]], [[passage/quran-81-20|At-Takwir 20]], [[passage/quran-81-21|At-Takwir 21]], [[passage/quran-69-4|Al-Qari'a 4]], [[passage/quran-69-5|Al-Qari'a 5]].
- Scenes of the Mi'raj: [[passage/quran-17-64|Al-Isra' 64]] ("and nothing is hidden from my Lord" — said to be Jibril, said to be another), and he is mentioned in the context of "We sent it down on the Night of Decree" [[passage/quran-97-4|Al-Qadr 4]] with "and the Spirit", as will come in [[concept/malaikat-laylat-al-qadr|the angels of the Night of Decree]].
- "It is not for any human to speak to God except by revelation or from behind a veil, or He sends a messenger who reveals by His permission what He wills" [[passage/quran-42-51|Al-Shura 51]] — in the tafsir this messenger is Jibril.
- "Those who said: our Lord is God, then remained steadfast, the angels descend upon them: do not fear and do not grieve, and give good tidings of Paradise" [[passage/quran-41-30|Fussilat 30]] — in a group's view the ones meant are Jibril and those with him.

## In hadith and sira
- **The hadith of faith**: a man came to the Prophet and asked about Islam, faith, excellence, the Hour and its signs; the Prophet said: "This is Jibril, who has come to you to teach you your religion"; Umar related that they wondered at his asking and his affirming, for they knew he was Jibril who used to bring them revelation {cite:sahih-muslim}.
- **The first revelation**: he came to him in the cave of Hira' in the form he saw — a man on the ground at the distance of two bows — and said "Read", and he hesitated three times, then God sent back to him the first verses {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.
- **The Mi'raj**: he accompanied him in the Isra' and Mi'raj as far as the Lote-tree of the Utmost Boundary, ascended with him to where God willed, and saw of the signs what is not narrated {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}; in some narrations it is mentioned that he came to him in the form in which he was created.
- **Ramadan**: he used to meet him every night of Ramadan and review the Qur'an with him; "when Jibril met him, the Messenger of God was at his most generous with good" {cite:sahih-muslim}.
- **Uhud**: he stood beside him on the day of Uhud when there were ten thousand among the raiders; Jibril said to him: "By God, you have not been defeated" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.
- **His form among people**: the Prophet used to tell that he saw Jibril in the form in which he was created twice — in a narration "a man with white hair at both temples" — between the heavens and earth {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}.
- **The opening of the chest**: it is mentioned in narrations that he opened the Prophet's chest, took out his heart and washed it in a golden basin with Zamzam water {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.
- **The trustworthiness**: he is the one named "the Trustworthy" in the Qur'an and hadith, and God sent him to the intimate friends Ibrahim, Musa and Isa in the Qur'anic accounts {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-19-17|Maryam 17]] ("our spirit" — in a view), [[passage/quran-11-69|Hud 69]] (the guests of Ibrahim).

## His attributes and rank
- "The Holy Spirit" {cite:tanzil-quran}, and "mighty" in "it is the word of a noble messenger * of strength" [[passage/quran-81-19|At-Takwir 19]] — in the tafsir the one of strength is Jibril.
- He is the chief of the angels in the view of the majority of exegetes, the noblest of them in rank {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}; some of the early scholars counted him among the "Most High Assembly".
- It is narrated from him that he said "I am the servant of God and His messenger and your messenger" in his various forms, and that he neither eats nor drinks as in his dialogue with the Prophet {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.
- In books of tafsir it is mentioned that God chose him to bear revelation because of his trustworthiness and high standing {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997}.

## With the exegetes
Ibn Kathir details the places of the descent of the Spirit, mentioning that "the Holy Spirit" is Jibril in the majority view, and in another view another angel or the Spirit named Jibril {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}. Some exegetes hold that "the Spirit" in [[passage/quran-97-4|Al-Qadr 4]] and [[passage/quran-78-38|An-Naba 38]] is Jibril, which is the view of most of them as explained in the tafsir lessons {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:12:30}.

## In books of sira and description
Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham mention that Jibril descended upon the Prophet with revelation on occasions of the sira: the pigeon at the Hijra, the pledge under the tree, the command of the raids, and the Qur'an being recited over him {cite:ibn-hisham-sira} {cite:al-sira-al-nabawiyya-nadwi-ibn-kathir-2004}. The books of Shama'il gather the attributes of Jibril and his rank {cite:shamail-kushak-2012} {cite:shamail-bajuri-awwama-2001}.

## Notes for researchers
- The name is written in the tradition in several forms: Jibril, Jibrā'īl, Jibrīl; and the mushaf fixes the spelling with the single ba'.
- "The Trustworthy" is a Qur'anic attribute of his, and "the Holy Spirit" is his best-known title.
- Some of what is related about Jibril of details of his states and the scenes of the Mi'raj is long, and is gathered by books of Shama'il and sira.
- His relation to Mika'il: partners in bearing revelation and the Most High Assembly in the exegetes' view, and Mika'il is at [[person/mikail|his page]].
