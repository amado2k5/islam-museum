Mohamed Lasram (1866–1925) was a Tunisian nationalist from a family of the great Tunis notability, of Kairouan origin and descent from a Yemeni tribe, whose members were rich landowners forming a near-hereditary dynasty of Ministers of the Pen from the second half of the 18th century.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-fr}{cite:wikidata-mohamed-lasram} He studied at the Zaytuna and also at Sadiki College, then spent two years in France.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-fr} A member of the Young Tunisians movement, he wrote articles in various journals in which he encouraged the generalisation of education to both sexes, the protection of the habous endowments, the engagement of Tunisians in political life and a rapprochement between French and Tunisians.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-fr}{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-ar}

He worked first as a teacher at the Alaoui College and then became head of the forestry administration.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-fr} Dean of the Khaldounia association between 1900 and 1909, he took part in the colonial conference organised at Marseille in September 1906 and in the North African conference held from 6 to 10 October 1908, in the company of Abdeljelil Zaouche, Sadok Zmerli and Khairallah Ben Mustapha.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-fr}{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-ar} He died on 3 March 1925.{cite:wikipedia-mohamed-lasram-ar}{cite:wikidata-mohamed-lasram}
