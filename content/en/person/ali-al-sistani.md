## Summary

Ali al-Husseini al-Sistani (born 1930 in Mashhad, Iran) is a Grand Ayatollah and the senior marja' of the Najaf hawza, the most widely followed authority of Twelver Shia Muslims in Iraq and beyond. He combines decades of seminary teaching with an authority of legal opinion that, after 2003, became a factor in Iraqi national politics itself.{cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sistani}

## Life and formation

He was born in Mashhad into a scholarly sayyid family, began seminary studies there, moved to Qom in 1949 to study under Grand Ayatollah Hossein Borujerdi, then to Najaf in 1951 where he attended the advanced lectures of Abu al-Qasim al-Khoei and Mohammad Hussein al-Gharawi. He remained in Najaf as teacher and researcher, rarely leaving the city.{cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sistani}

## The marja'iyya and public role

After al-Khoei's death in 1992 the marja'iyya passed effectively to al-Sistani, and his offices and representatives grew into a network stretching from Najaf to Asia and the West. In post-2003 Iraq he called for direct elections and an Iraqi-written constitution while publicly declining to meet political officeholders; in June 2014 he issued the "sufficient jihad" fatwa calling for volunteers in the security forces after armed militants seized Iraqi cities, a ruling observers described as changing the course of the war.{cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sistani}

His works include the practical treatise Minhaj al-Salihin, the collection Al-Fatawa al-Muyassara, and advanced research lectures in jurisprudence and legal theory taught within the hawza.{cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sistani}

## Method and legacy

His method joins traditional seminary authority to timed interventions at major national junctures, while he publicly abstains from seeking political office. Supporters describe him as a guardian of Iraq's stability; others criticise his religious influence in the public sphere — the museum records both positions without a verdict.{cite:wikipedia-ali-al-sistani}
