## Overview
Waraqa ibn Nawfal, a cousin of Khadija: an elderly Meccan Christian who read books, and the first to tell Muhammad that what had come to him in the cave was prophethood.

## Life
An elder of Quraysh whose sight failed in his later years. He "read books": the tradition says he used to write in Hebrew and copy from the Gospel in Arabic {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

When the Prophet returned trembling from [[event/first-revelation|Hira]], Khadija took him to Waraqa. His judgement, the turning point of the narrative, was that what had been sent down to Moses had been sent down to Muhammad: the great Namus. Then came the warning: "I wish I had been a young man then, when your people drive you out" {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He died shortly afterwards, before the call was announced openly {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Waraqa is a bridge between Islam and the older scriptures: a Christian elder inside the story who names what is happening before the new community has a name for it.

## For young visitors
The first person to understand what was happening to Muhammad was an old Christian who had spent his life reading books, and he told him so.

## In The Sealed Nectar (p.67-68)
A hadith from Aisha, which the book cites, describes him as Khadija's cousin: a man who had become a Christian in the pre-Islamic period, who used to write the Hebrew script and copy from the Gospel in Hebrew as much as God willed, a great old man who had gone blind {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.67} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/2-3}. When he heard what the Prophet had seen, he said it was the Namus that God had sent down to Moses. He wished he were alive when his people drove him out. He told him that no man had come with the like of what he brought without being opposed, and that if he lived to see that day he would support him with a mighty support. He did not last long after that and died ([[event/first-revelation|the beginning of revelation]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.67} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.68} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/2-3}. In Ibn Hisham's version, Khadija went to him and he said that the great Namus that used to come to Moses had come, and that Muhammad was the prophet of this community. Then he met the Prophet after his stay had ended and said the same to him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.68} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/237-238}.

## In Abla al-Kahlawi's programme 'In the Love of al-Mustafa'
[[person/abla-al-kahlawi|Abla al-Kahlawi]] introduces Waraqa ibn Nawfal in her programme [[work/fi-hub-al-mustapha|'In the Love of al-Mustafa']] as Khadija's cousin, a man who read the revealed scriptures, reflected and had a measure of wisdom. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:06:00} She relates that before her marriage Khadija told him her dream of the sun coming out of her house, and that he said a light would come from it and thought it would be the awaited prophet. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:06:30} When the matter of the revelation was put to him, she says, he swore that this was the namus that had come to Moses and Jesus before, and told the Prophet that his people would drive him out; the Prophet, in innocence and simplicity, asked in surprise whether they would really expel him, and Waraqa answered that no one had ever brought what he brought without being treated as an enemy. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:08:00} She sees in this that the Prophet knew from the start that there would be a hijra, though it was deferred. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:08:00}
