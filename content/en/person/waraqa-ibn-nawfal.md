## Brief
Waraqa ibn Nawfal was Khadija's elderly cousin — a Meccan Christian who could read scripture — and the first person to tell Muhammad that what had come to him in the cave was prophecy.

## Life
An old man of Quraysh, blind in his last years, Waraqa had "read the scriptures" — the tradition says he could write Hebrew or transcribe the Gospel in Arabic {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

When the Prophet returned from [[event/first-revelation|Hira' shaken]], Khadija brought him to Waraqa. The old man's verdict — the tradition's hinge — was that what had descended on Moses had descended on Muhammad: the great *namus* (law). And then the warning: "Would that I were young when your people drive you out" {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He died soon after, before the preaching became public {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Waraqa is the tradition's bridge between Islam and the older scriptures: a Christian elder inside the story who names what is happening before the new community itself has a name.

## For young visitors
The first person to understand what was happening to Muhammad was an old Christian man who had spent his life reading scripture — and he told him so.
