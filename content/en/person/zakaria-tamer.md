# Zakaria Tamer

Zakaria Tamer (born 2 January 1931) is a Syrian short-story writer and journalist, one of the most widely read and translated authors of modern Syrian literature and one of the foremost writers of children's stories in Arabic. His stories are renowned for their relative simplicity on the one hand and the complexity of their possible references on the other, and often carry a sharp, satirical edge against political and social oppression. {cite:wikipedia-zakaria-tamer-en} {cite:wikipedia-zakaria-tamer-ar}

## Early life
He was born in the al-Basha district of Damascus. Forced to leave school in 1944, at thirteen, to help provide for his family, he was apprenticed to a blacksmith as a locksmith in a factory in the al-Basha district. As an autodidact he spent many hours reading, became interested in politics, and continued his education at night school; as he later said in an interview, his reading provoked him "to create a voice which [he] hadn't been able to find [there]". His intention was to represent in his writing the very poor majority of men and women in Syria, with their joyless and restricted existence. {cite:wikipedia-zakaria-tamer-en}

## Career in Syria
His first stories were published in Syrian journals in 1957. His first manuscript was noticed by Yusuf al-Khal, editor of the magazine Shi'r, who published it as his first collection, The Neighing of the White Steed (1960). Tamer then left blacksmithing for work as a government official and as editor of several journals, including the cultural periodicals Al-Mawqif al-Adabi and Al-Marifa and the children's magazine Usamah. He was instrumental in establishing the Syrian Writers Union in 1968 and was its vice-president for four years. In 1980 he was dismissed from editing Al-Marifa, published by the Ministry of Culture, after publishing extracts from Abd al-Rahman al-Kawakibi's The Characteristics of Despotism (1900), which denounced tyranny and called for freedom; he then decided to leave Syria for London. {cite:wikipedia-zakaria-tamer-en}

## London years
In London he was managing editor of Al-Dustoor magazine (1981–1982), culture editor of Al-Tadhamon (1983–1988), managing editor of Al-Naqid (1988–1993), and culture editor at Riyadh Al Rayes Publishing House, while writing satirical columns for Arabic newspapers. His eleven collections of short stories include The Thunder (1970), Damascus Fire (1973), Tigers on the Tenth Day (1978), Noah's Summons (1994) and Sour Grapes (2000), whose English translation appeared in 2023. In January 2012 he opened a Facebook page, Al-Mihmaz ("The Spur"), publishing daily articles, most recently on the Syrian uprising. His awards include the Sultan Bin Ali Al Owais Cultural Foundation prize (2001), the Syrian Order of Merit (2002), the Blue Metropolis Montreal International Literary Prize (2009) and the Mahmoud Darwish Award for Freedom and Creativity (2015). {cite:wikipedia-zakaria-tamer-en}
