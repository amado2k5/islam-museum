## Summary
Abu Sa'id Ahmad ibn Isa al-Kharraz, the Baghdadi Sufi, has an entry in [[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]]'s [[work/tarikh-dimashq|Tarikh Dimashq]] because he passed through Sidon on the Damascus coast in his wanderings. He narrated from Ibrahim ibn Bashshar al-Khurasani, the companion of Ibrahim ibn Adham, and from Muhammad ibn Mansur al-Tusi; Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Misri the preacher, Abu Ja'far al-Saydalani, Ali ibn Hafs al-Razi, Abu Muhammad al-Jariri the Sufi, Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn al-Hasan al-Daqqaq, Muhammad ibn Ali al-Kattani and Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Muqatil narrated from him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 129} The editor explains the name: it refers to stitching leather goods such as water-skins, mats and straps. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 129} The Mawsu'at A'lam al-Ulama' wa-l-Udaba' likewise says he was called al-Kharraz after this trade. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## His standing among the Sufis
Ibn Asakir quotes [[person/al-sulami|Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami]]: he was the imam of the Sufis in every branch of their sciences, of Baghdadi origin, with well-known wonders and marvels at the start of his path, and the finest speaker among them except [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]], who is the imam; and it is said that the first to speak of the science of annihilation and subsistence (fana' and baqa') was Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 130} He quotes [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib al-Baghdadi]]: he was among those known for scrupulousness, self-watchfulness, careful attention and spiritual effort, and he narrated a little. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 130} [[person/al-qushayri-abu-l-qasim|Al-Qushayri]] says he kept company with [[person/dhu-al-nun-al-misri|Dhu al-Nun al-Misri]], al-Nibaji, Abu Ubayd al-Busri, [[person/sari-al-saqati|al-Sari]], [[person/bishr-al-hafi|Bishr]] and others. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 130} Ibn Makula gives the spelling of his name (kha', then ra', then zay) and says he wrote books. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 130}

He relates that Abu Bakr ibn al-Tarsusi said: Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz is the moon of the Sufis; and that al-Junayd said: were God to hold us to the reality of Abu Sa'id's state, we would perish — explained as his having spent years stitching without ever missing the remembrance of God between two stitches. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 131} Al-Murta'ish said: all creatures are dependants of Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz when he speaks of the realities. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 131} The Muntazam counts him among those known for spiritual effort, scrupulousness and self-watchfulness. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi v. 12 p. 280}

## Sayings
Among the sayings Ibn Asakir gathers: every inward meaning that contradicts the outward is false; and that he kept company with the Sufis without ever quarrelling with them, because he took their side against himself. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 130} Being occupied with a past moment wastes a second moment; whoever thinks he arrives by spending his effort is labouring in vain, and whoever thinks he arrives without spending it is merely wishing. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 132} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 133} Asked whether the knower reaches a state where weeping leaves him, he said yes: weeping belongs to the times of their journey to God, and when they alight at the realities of nearness it leaves them. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 131} Knowledge is what puts you to work, and certainty is what carries you. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 134} The sins of those brought near are the good deeds of the pious; the farthest from God is the one who claims allusion and nearness; the least a traveller needs is four things — knowledge to guide him, remembrance to keep him company, scrupulousness to restrain him and a self to carry him; and contentment before the decree is entrusting, contentment with the decree is surrender. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 137} Renunciation is that the heart neither desires what is desired in this world nor rests in what it has of it. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 141}

He also gives his reflections on the meaning of [[passage/quran-55-60|Surat al-Rahman 60]], on the reward of whoever cuts himself off from people, and his reading of God's treasuries in [[passage/quran-63-7|Surat al-Munafiqun 7]]: on earth they are hearts, which God sweeps clean of association, hypocrisy and deceit and in which He makes a tree grow that bears contentment, love and gratitude, linked to the image of [[passage/quran-14-24|Surat Ibrahim 24]]; al-Khatib transmitted this through al-Hawwariyya, who in Tarikh Baghdad is Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz's sister. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 138} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 139}

## Reports of his austerity
Ibn Asakir gives, with chains, reports of his self-denial, among them his own account: he inherited some money and took a weighed daily ration from it while keeping to seclusion, then reduced it to one meal every two nights; when the inheritance ran out he gathered what fell from the vegetable-sellers, then sewed shirts for the poor of a village for a few days' wage, then stayed in the cemeteries eating from a lote-tree whose fruit was free to all, and he says that his food later became bones and then clay. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 135} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 136} He relates that al-Kharraz spoke in Mecca on a question of knowledge that people objected to; the governor sent word that he should leave the city, and he left, refusing to let his companions intercede for him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 136} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 137}

Abu al-Qasim ibn Mardan relates that a young man from Nihawand asked him about the reality of trust in God, and he answered: that you do not take a pass from Hamula — and the young man had in fact taken a pass from Hamula, the chief of Nihawand. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 133} Reports of wonders are also told of him — two wild beasts climbing onto his shoulders in the desert of Mosul while he kept his heart from turning round, a voice that offered him the choice between a means and strength, and his burying himself in the sand until he was carried to the way-station; Ibn Asakir gives them as transmitted reports with their chains, and the museum does not assert them. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 133} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 134} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 142} He also relates that al-Kharraz saw in a dream his son who had died before him and asked him for counsel, and after it did not wear a shirt for thirty years. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 131}

## Death
Ibn Asakir relates from [[person/ruwaym-ibn-ahmad|Ruwaym]] that he was present at his death and heard him reciting verses on the longing of the knowers' hearts. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 142} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 143} The year is disputed:

- **277 AH (al-Qushayri; preferred by al-Sulami; judged nearer by al-Khatib)**: al-Qushayri said he died in 277; Abu Bakr ibn Abi al-Ajuz, asked, said 247 or 277, and al-Sulami said: I think this (277) is more correct; al-Khatib said: the first view is certainly false, and the second is nearer the truth if it is reliably preserved. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 130} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 143}
- **286 AH (Abu al-Qasim ibn Mardan)**: he said: I kept company with Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz for fourteen years, and he died in 286. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 143} The Mawsu'at A'lam al-Ulama' wa-l-Udaba' follows this date. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}
