## Summary

Hussein Dey, born Hüseyin bin Hüseyin, was the twenty-seventh and last Dey of the Regency of Algiers, ruling from 1 March 1818 until the French invasion of 1830 {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. He is remembered above all for the fly-whisk incident of 1827, the diplomatic slight that France used as the pretext for blockading and then invading Algiers {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}.

## Early life and rise

Hussein was born at İzmir or at Urla in the Ottoman Empire {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. The Arabic article adds that his father was an artillery officer, that he received a special training and was sent to Istanbul to study at a private school as a common soldier, and that in his youth he traded in tobacco, which earned him the nickname Khodja {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar}. The English article records that he went to Istanbul and joined the Cannoneers, rose quickly to the rank of Odabaşı, and then, forced by rivalries to flee, made his way to the Regency of Algiers, a country known for accepting fugitives, where he joined the Odjak and became a Janissary {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. In 1815 he was appointed Khodjet al-khil, the minister charged with raising and commanding the dey's cavalry and with raising taxes, and he commanded the cavalry as a relief force during the British bombardment of Algiers in 1816 {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. In 1818, following the death of the previous dey, the Divan of Algiers elected him ruler {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}; the Arabic article adds that the dying Omar Pasha had designated him in his will, and that the Sublime Porte confirmed the appointment by firman of Sultan Mahmud II {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar}.

## Rule

Hussein Dey succeeded as dey of Algiers in March 1818 {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. He enacted measures such as ensuring freedom of religion for the Jews, and under threat of renewed British military action he freed European Christian slaves {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. In 1824 he expelled the British consul who was pressing him to renew the treaty of 1816 against the enslavement of European Christians; the British fleet withdrew and returned two weeks later, and the treaty was renewed under threat of bombardment {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. The Arabic article records that he reorganised the administration and reformed the army, especially the fleet, building a shipyard and equipping it, and that economic life improved markedly under him {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar}.

## The fly-whisk incident and the invasion

In the 1790s France had contracted to buy wheat for its army from two Jewish merchants of Algiers, Bakri and Busnach, and had fallen into arrears; the merchants owed their own debts to the dey and could not pay until France paid them {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. After a contentious meeting on 29 April 1827 in which the French consul Pierre Deval refused satisfactory answers, the dey struck him with his fly whisk {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. Charles X demanded an apology and then blockaded Algiers; when the dey answered a demand to send an ambassador by firing cannon at a blockading ship, France resolved on stronger action {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. On 14 June 1830, 34,000 French soldiers landed at Sidi Fredj, west of Algiers, and entered the city on 5 July after a three-week campaign {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. Hussein Dey agreed to surrender in exchange for his freedom and the retention of his personal wealth; this ended the Deylik and began French rule in Algeria {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. The Arabic article details the resistance he organised over twenty-one days, through the battles of Staouéli and the defence of the Casbah {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar}.

## Exile and death

On 15 July 1830 Hussein Dey left Algiers with his family, his harem and his personal fortune aboard the French ship *Jeanne d'Arc* {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. His request to live in France was refused by Charles X, and he settled in Italy — Naples in the English article's account, Livorno in the Arabic article's, which adds that he stayed three years, from 1830 to 1833, before settling finally at Alexandria from September 1833 {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en} {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar}. He died at Alexandria in 1838 {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}. A suburb of Algiers bears his name, and the football club NA Hussein Dey is based there {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en}.

## Faith

No source read names a legal school for the man himself; the English and Arabic articles' infoboxes file him as Sunni Islam, and Wikidata carries only the generic value Islam {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-en} {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar} {cite:wikidata-dey-hussein}. His documented practice is stated in full in his record: the Arabic article records that he had memorised the Qur'an, was known from his youth for his religious learning and observance, served as an imam, was respected by the people of Algiers for his piety, and issued no judgement without referring back to the ulama {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar}. The Regency he ruled was an Ottoman province whose Odjak establishment followed the Hanafi rite while most of its Algerian subjects were Maliki; that institutional setting is context, not a naming of the dey's own school, so his faith is recorded as unknown.

## Notes for researchers

His birth year is disputed: the English article gives 1764/65, while the Arabic and Urdu articles and Wikidata give c. 1773 {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ur} {cite:wikidata-dey-hussein}; his record builds 1765 with the debate preserved, and the birth-place edge follows İzmir, on which the Arabic article and Wikidata agree with one of the English article's two candidates. His three Italian years are placed at Naples by the English article and at Livorno by the Arabic article {cite:wikipedia-dey-hussein-ar}. Wikidata's death day of 30 October 1838, repeated in the Arabic and Urdu infoboxes from the same web source, is a single structured value and is not adopted; the articles' texts support only the year 1838 at Alexandria {cite:wikidata-dey-hussein}.
