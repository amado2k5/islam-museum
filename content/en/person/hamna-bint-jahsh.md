## Summary
[[person/hamna-bint-jahsh|Hamna]] bint Jahsh al-Asadiyya, sister of Umm al-Mu'minin Zaynab bint Jahsh and her siblings {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1849}.

## Her marriage and children
She was wife of [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]], who was killed at Uhud leaving her; [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha ibn Ubaydullah]] then married her and she bore him Muhammad and Imran — her mother and her sister Zaynab's being [[person/umayma-bint-abd-al-muttalib|Umayma bint Abd al-Muttalib]] {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1849}.

## The day of Uhud
Abu Umar says she was among the women who pledged, and witnessed [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], giving the thirsty water and carrying the wounded and treating them {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1849}.

## The istihada hadith
Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi relate the istihada hadith from Hamna. Abu Ishaq al-Shaybani and Abu Bishr differed on the story's heroine's name; some reconcile the accounts by saying both women suffered istihada, while al-Waqidi denied Hamna ever had it at all {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1849}.

## Of her affairs
Ibn Sa'd said the Prophet fed her thirty wasqs from Khaybar; she is the mother of Muhammad ibn Talha, known as al-Sajjad {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1849}.
