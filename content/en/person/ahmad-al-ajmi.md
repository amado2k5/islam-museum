## Summary
Ahmad ibn Ali Al-Ajmi is a Saudi Qur'an reciter born in Khobar in 1968, among the most widely heard reciters in the world; The Muslim 500 lists him among influential Qur'an reciters. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-al-ajmi} {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Formation
He memorised the Qur'an and studied under senior reciters in the Eastern Province, then took ijaza in the canonical readings; he began leading prayers in Khobar mosques while still young. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Ministry
He has served as imam at prominent mosques in Khobar and Jeddah — including Al-Amir Sultan Mosque — and has led tarawih at the Grand Mosque in Makkah; recordings of his prayers circulate globally. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Style and reach
His recitation is marked by a resonant, emotive voice and precise tajwid; complete mus'haf recordings in his voice are among the most streamed and broadcast of any living reciter, carried by satellite religious channels and apps worldwide. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Assessment
Listeners rank him with the leading Saudi reciters of his generation for the blend of technical discipline and emotional force; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi} {cite:muslim500-ahmad-al-ajmi}


## Broadcast recitation
His recorded mushaf is among the most-distributed Qur'an recordings globally — included in apps, television loops and Islamic-radio programming across the Arab world, South Asia and Southeast Asia. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Style
Reciting in Hafs 'an 'Asim with a clear, unhurried delivery, he is among the reciters most recommended to students for tajweed modelling and memorisation. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Imamate
His imamate in Khobar mosques and his touring recitations at Islamic conferences and Ramadan programmes across the Muslim world have kept him among the busiest reciters of his generation. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi} {cite:muslim500-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Origins
Born Ahmad bin Ali al-Ajmi in Khobar in 1968, he memorised the Qur'an and rose through the Saudi recording industry in the 1990s to become one of the kingdom's most-recorded reciters. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## The recording phenomenon
His mushaf recordings were an industry event: cassette and CD sets distributed across the Muslim world at volumes press profiles describe as among the largest of any reciter, with a clean Hafs delivery built for memorisation listening. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## Standing
Within the Gulf recitation tradition he occupies the position of a classic recording reciter — alongside al-Ajmy's generation of studio-era voices — whose catalogues remain the standard references for students worldwide. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi} {cite:muslim500-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## The mushaf recording
Ahmad Al-Ajmi's mushaf recordings are among the most-distributed Qur'an recordings globally: cassette and CD sets distributed across the Muslim world at volumes press profiles describe as among the largest of any reciter, included in apps, television loops and Islamic-radio programming across the Arab world, South Asia and Southeast Asia. His clean, unhurried Hafs 'an 'Asim delivery — built for memorisation listening — made his recordings the standard reference for students learning tajweed and memorisation. {cite:wikipedia-ahmad-al-ajmi}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists him among the most influential Muslims in arts, citing the global circulation of his mushaf recordings and his position as one of the reciters most recommended to students for tajweed modelling. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-al-ajmi}



## Standing in the field

Within Qur’an recitation, Ahmad Al-Ajmi stands as a Saudi reciter whose mushaf recordings rank among the most distributed globally — a position that explains the scale of public attention the career has attracted. The Muslim 500's listing places Ahmad Al-Ajmi among the most influential Muslims of the present generation in this field, reflecting both the size of the audience the work reaches and the extent to which the figure has come to represent it beyond specialist circles. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-al-ajmi}
