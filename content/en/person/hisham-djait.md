## Summary

Hisham Djait (1935–2021) was a Tunisian historian and professor of Islamic history, known for critical readings of early Islamic political history that joined Western historical method to command of the Arabic sources.{cite:wikipedia-hisham-djait}

## Life

Born in Tunis into a religious scholarly family, he studied history in Tunis and Paris, taught for many years at the University of Tunis, and was a member of European and American academies.{cite:wikipedia-hisham-djait}

## Works

His book Al-Fitna (The Great Fitna: Religion and Politics in Early Islam, 1989) is among the broadest modern analyses of the first civil war and the caliphates of Ali and Mu'awiya. He also wrote on the life of Muhammad and on Europe's relation with Islam and the Islamic culture of Kairouan.{cite:wikipedia-hisham-djait}

## Method and standing

He argued for distinguishing faith from history when studying Islam's origins — a stance welcomed by academics and objected to by some religious scholars. He is counted among the most important contemporary Arab historians of the critical school.{cite:wikipedia-hisham-djait}
