## Summary
The hadith master Abu Muhammad Yahya ibn Muhammad ibn Sa'id ibn Katib al-Hashimi al-Baghdadi transmitted Ibn al-Mubarak's [[work/kitab-al-zuhd-ibn-al-mubarak|Kitab al-Zuhd]] from [[person/husayn-ibn-al-hasan-al-marwazi|al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan al-Marwazi]]; he was born in 228 and died in Dhu al-Qa'da 318, as [[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] summarises from al-Dhahabi. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 100}

## Teachers and students
He heard from Luwayn, Ahmad ibn Mani', the judge Sawwar ibn Abd Allah, Harun ibn Abd Allah al-Hammal and "countless" others; from him transmitted Abu al-Qasim al-Baghawi, though senior to him, [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]], Abu Tahir al-Mukhallis and many others. He had two brothers, Yusuf and Ahmad. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 100}

## Standing
Al-Daraqutni called him "trustworthy, firm, a hadith master"; Abu Ali al-Naysaburi said none of his peers in Iraq matched his understanding; al-Khatib said he held a place in learning and wrote works on Sunna and legal rulings; al-Dhahabi said he had solid views on narrators and hidden defects. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 100}

## His link to Kitab al-Zuhd
In al-A'zami's text Ibn Sa'id narrates the book "on Thursday, six days into Rabi' al-Awwal of the year 315, at the door of his house", while the opening of part 7 says he read it out himself at his house in Dhu al-Qa'da 309. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 304} The book contains "additions" of his from his own teachers, not through Ibn al-Mubarak, which the editor marks, and a remark of his after the reports on [[person/uwais-al-qarni|Uways]] declaring their chains sound. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 127} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 294}

## For young visitors
Ibn Sa'id was a hadith scholar of Baghdad who read Kitab al-Zuhd to his students at the door of his house, and they passed it on to those after them.

## Notes for researchers
- The two dates of hearing (309 and 315) are explained by the editor in the footnote to the first report.
