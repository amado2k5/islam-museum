Yusuf ibn Ismail al-Nabhani (1849–1932) was a Palestinian judge, jurist and poet from Ijzim in the Haifa district, remembered as a leading defender of the Ottoman Caliphate and of Sufism.{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-nabhani-en}{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-nabhani-ar} Trained at al-Azhar in Cairo, he served as a judge in Ottoman service, edited the periodical al-Jawa'ib in Istanbul, and presided over Beirut's Court of Rights for some twenty years from 1888.{cite:azhar-memory-yusuf-al-nabhani}

Al-Nabhani was initiated into several Sufi orders — among them the Idrisiyya, Shadhiliyya, Naqshbandiyya, Qadiriyya, Rifa'iyya and Khalwatiyya — taking each path from a named shaykh.{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-nabhani-ar}{cite:azhar-memory-yusuf-al-nabhani} His dozens of books, including Jami' Karamat al-Awliya' and Shawahid al-Haqq, made him the era's most prolific Arabic writer in praise of the Prophet and in defence of the saints.{cite:wikipedia-yusuf-al-nabhani-en}{cite:wikidata-yusuf-al-nabhani}
