## Summary
Mi Guang Jiang, known religiously as Haji Noor Deen, is a Chinese Muslim calligrapher born in Shandong province in 1963, regarded as the modern master of Sini — the distinctively Chinese style of Arabic calligraphy. The Muslim 500 lists him among influential figures in Muslim arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-haji-noor-deen} {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## Formation
A member of the Hui, China's native Muslim ethnicity, he studied Arabic calligraphy in China before travelling to Egypt in the 1990s, where he trained in classical Arabic scripts and earned certification; he later settled in the United States. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## The Sini art
Chinese Muslims have written Arabic with the brush for centuries, developing elongated, tapered forms governed by Chinese stroke logic. Noor Deen codified this heritage: his works render Qur'anic titles, the shahada in abstracted form, and Arabic poetry in compositions that read simultaneously as Chinese painting and Arabic script. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## Career
His calligraphy has been exhibited and collected by museums including the British Museum and displayed in the United States, the Middle East and East Asia; he lectures, teaches workshops and has received honours from Arab and Islamic cultural bodies. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## Assessment
He is credited with rescuing Sini calligraphy from obscurity and establishing it as a bridge between Islamic and Chinese aesthetics; scholars of Islamic art treat him as the tradition's modern authority. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen} {cite:muslim500-haji-noor-deen}


## Style and method
His signature 'Chinese-style' script stretches Arabic letters vertically like Chinese characters, applying the pressure-release rhythm of the brush to Arabic letterforms; he also works in the classical thuluth, naskh and kufi styles in which he holds ijaza certification. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## Teaching
He teaches through workshops and an academy presence across China, the United States and online, training a generation of students — Muslim and non-Muslim — in the union of Chinese brushwork and Arabic script, and has been received by Arab cultural ministries. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## Collections
His pieces have entered major collections — among them the British Museum — and been exhibited across the Middle East, Southeast Asia, North America and Europe, frequently commissioned for mosques and Islamic cultural institutions. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen} {cite:muslim500-haji-noor-deen}

## Origins
Born Mi Guangjiang in 1963 into a Hui family in Shandong, he studied Chinese calligraphy and painting before moving to Egypt in the 1990s to study Arabic calligraphy formally at the traditional schools of Cairo. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## Recognition
He became the first Chinese member of the Association of Egyptian Calligraphers and has been received by Arab ministries and cultural institutions; coverage of his work frames him as the leading bridge between Chinese and Arabic calligraphic traditions. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## The Sini script
His work popularised the centuries-old 'Sini' style — the distinct Chinese-Islamic script tradition of Hui mosques — for a new global audience, while his own vertical compositions push it into contemporary-art territory. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen} {cite:muslim500-haji-noor-deen}

## The Sini tradition
Haji Noor Deen's work belongs to the 'Sini' or 'Chinese-style' calligraphic tradition — the distinct script art that developed in China's Hui Muslim communities over centuries, in which Arabic letters are stretched vertically like Chinese characters and executed with the pressure-release rhythm of the Chinese brush. His contribution has been to carry the tradition to international attention: exhibited in major museums, taught to students across continents, and received by Arab cultural ministries as the leading practitioner of a form that joins two civilisational calligraphic arts. {cite:wikipedia-haji-noor-deen}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists him among the most influential Muslims in arts, citing his position as the leading contemporary practitioner of Chinese-style Arabic calligraphy and his role in bringing the tradition to global museum and exhibition audiences. {cite:muslim500-haji-noor-deen}
