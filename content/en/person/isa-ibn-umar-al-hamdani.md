## Overview
Isa ibn Umar al-Hamdani al-Kufi, Abu Umar, is described by al-Dhahabi as the imam, the Qur'an reciter and the devout; he says he was known as al-Hamdani but was in fact one of the clients of Banu Asad {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2991}. The sign printed with his entry points to al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2991}.

## Teachers and students
He took the readings by direct recitation from Talha ibn Musarrif, Asim ibn Bahdala and al-A'mash; al-Kisa'i, Ubayd Allah ibn Musa, Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hammad and others read to him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2991}. He narrated hadith from Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, Hammad the jurist and Amr ibn Murra, and Ibn al-Mubarak, Waki', Abu Nu'aym, al-Firyabi, Khallad ibn Yahya and many others narrated from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2991}.

## What the critics said
Al-Dhahabi says Ibn Ma'in and others held him trustworthy and that he was the reciter of Kufa in his time, and quotes Sufyan al-Thawri as saying there was no better reciter there than he {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2992}.

## His death
Al-Dhahabi quotes Mutayyin that he died in 156 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2992}.
