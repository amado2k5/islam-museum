# Bekir Çoban-zade

Bekir Vaap oğlu Çoban-zade (27 May 1893 – 13 October 1937) was a Crimean Tatar poet and professor of Turkic languages, one of the founders of Turkology in the Soviet East. He was born at Qarasuvbazar in Crimea, the son of a shepherd — çoban — from whom the surname, "son of a shepherd", derives. Sent by his town's Muslim charitable society to Istanbul, he studied at the Galatasaray Lyceum, then took his doctorate at the Pázmány Péter University in Budapest, having reached Hungary as a prisoner of war.{cite:wikipedia-bekir-coban-zade-en}{cite:wikipedia-bekir-coban-zade-ru}

Back in Crimea he taught Crimean Tatar language and literature at Simferopol and in 1922 took the chair of Turkology at the Crimean University. In 1924 he moved to Baku as professor of Turkology at Baku State University, where he led the All-Union committee for the new Turkic alphabet and wrote some 150 scholarly works on the Turkic languages and their literatures, many of them the first scientific treatments of their subjects in Azerbaijani.{cite:wikipedia-bekir-coban-zade-ru}

In January 1937 Çoban-zade was arrested; after a twenty-minute trial he was condemned, and he was executed on 13 October 1937. Some twenty years later, on his wife's appeal, a Soviet military court reversed the verdict and declared the charges baseless.{cite:wikipedia-bekir-coban-zade-en}

The English and Russian articles agree on his dates and career, and Wikidata carries both forms of his birth date. None of the sources names a religion or school for him — the English article files him only under the category "Muslims from the Russian Empire", a category filing and not attestation — so his faith is recorded as unknown.
