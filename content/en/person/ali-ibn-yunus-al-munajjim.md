## Summary
Abu'l-Hasan 'Ali b. Abi Sa'id 'Abd al-Rahman b. Ahmad b. Yunus b. 'Abd al-A'la al-Sadafi, the Egyptian astronomer; author of the *Zij al-Hakimi* known as the Zij of Ibn Yunus — a great zij that Ibn Khallikan saw in four volumes, in which the computation and its working were set down with unsurpassed exactness, the longest of all zijes he had seen; al-'Aziz of Egypt commissioned it and began it for him. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His gift
He specialised in the science of the stars, commanding in all the sciences, excellent in poetry; of his absent-mindedness it is told that al-Hakim said: he came in to me one day with his sandal in his hand, kissed the ground, sat down — and left the sandal beside him. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## Extension: observations, tables and attribution limits

The earlier text and attribution remain intact. This extension adds King's scholarly biography, Muammer Dizer's entry and MacTutor. The latter individually calls him an Islamic mathematician, supplying Muslim identity evidence; Sunni is separately the museum's frame, not inferred from family or Fatimid patronage. King and Dizer regard birth as unknown, so MacTutor's header950 is not adopted as certain. The earlier death record is preserved {cite:ibn-yunus-mactutor summary} {cite:ibn-yunus-king-bea biography} {cite:tdv-ibn-yunus-dizer biography}.

He lived in Fustat, witnessing Cairo's foundation as a young man in969, with support from al-Aziz and later al-Hakim and recorded observations until1003. Instrument and site descriptions do not establish the popular equipped-observatory picture. Dizer distinguishes reports about the Muqattam palace and other locations from an unproven hypothesis. His education and teachers remain unknown {cite:ibn-yunus-king-bea observations and observatory caution} {cite:tdv-ibn-yunus-dizer biography and instruments}.

The Hakimi Zij lists his observations and predecessors', comparing some with Mumtahan computations. Subjects include calendars and conversions, planets, eclipses, equinoxes and spherical quantities. Careful recording of some observations does not explain how every parameter was obtained: King emphasizes that gap. Orthogonal projections as a source of some formulas remain a scholarly inference, not a derivation the author wrote in modern symbols {cite:ibn-yunus-king-bea Hakimi Zij} {cite:tdv-ibn-yunus-dizer Zij contents}.

His [[invention/ibn-yunus-solar-time-tables|solar-time contribution]] belongs to another, layered corpus. Scholarship assigns him an important part without determining his share in every surviving table. King describes time since sunrise, time until midday and azimuth sets, some prayer-related tables and later additions. Neither every cell nor separate lunar tables are automatically his timekeeping contribution {cite:ibn-yunus-king-bea timekeeping corpus} {cite:ibn-yunus-mactutor lunar and solar tables}.

TDV lists writings on sine, declination, equations and the meridian, an astrological Sirius work, and a twelve-lamp chandelier treatise uncertainly attributed between him and Yunus al-Asturlabi. A title does not prove that he built every device. MacTutor rejects evidence for pendulum use; the uncertain lamp device is not made a verified invention here {cite:tdv-ibn-yunus-dizer works2–8} {cite:ibn-yunus-mactutor pendulum discussion}.

Scholarship reports later lunar research using observations and Yemeni/Syrian timekeeping inspired by the Cairo corpus. Stories about absent-mindedness, astrology, predicted death and the sale of papers remain attributed accounts, not personality judgements or validation of astrology. Manuscripts and every cited reference were not read here. Neither the old zij description nor Wafayat receives a new reading certificate from this extension {cite:ibn-yunus-king-bea reception and anecdotes} {cite:tdv-ibn-yunus-dizer biography and reception}.
