## Summary
Taha Abdurrahman is a Moroccan philosopher counted among the deepest voices of contemporary Arabic philosophy; his project critiques Western modernity with its own tools and builds an ethical Islamic modernity. {cite:wikipedia-taha-abdurrahman}

## Life and upbringing
Born in 1944 in El Jadida, Morocco, he memorised the Quran young, studied Islamic sciences then philosophy at Mohammed V University to a doctorate, specialising in logic and language. {cite:wikipedia-taha-abdurrahman}

## Career and works
He worked as a university professor and adviser in Islamic organisations. His books include al-Lisan wa-l-Mizan on logic, Tajdid al-Manhaj on evaluating the heritage, Su’al al-Akhlaq proposing an Islamic grounding for ethics, and Ruh al-Hadatha crystallising the “trusteeship paradigm” as an alternative to a modernity detached from values. {cite:wikipedia-taha-abdurrahman}

## Method and contribution
He bends the tools of formal logic to serve the Arab-Islamic heritage and holds that trusteeship — not abstract reason — grounds human existence. Many researchers read his project as a way beyond cultural coloniality, while secular readings object that it imports religion into philosophy; the museum attributes these positions to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-taha-abdurrahman}
