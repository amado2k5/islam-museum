## Summary
He was on Umara ibn Hazm's camel; Ibn Ishaq describes him as a hypocrite. A saying about the straying of the Prophet's she-camel is attributed to him — the Prophet told them where the she-camel was — and people differ on his repentance. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/166}

## In the Tabuk expedition
Ibn Ishaq names him "Zayd ibn al-Lusayt al-Qaynuqa'i"; he was on Umara ibn Hazm's mount, and Ibn Ishaq describes him as having been a hypocrite. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/166} Ibn Hisham said it is also said "ibn Lusayb" with a b. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/166} A saying about the straying of the Prophet's she-camel is attributed to him; the Prophet told them of the saying and of the she-camel's place, and it was found where he described. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/166-167} Ibn Ishaq said some people claim he repented after that, while others said he never ceased to be suspected of evil until he perished. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/167}

## Tribe
Ibn Ishaq's lineage for him: of Banu Qaynuqa'. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/166}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- Ibn Hisham said: "and it is said ibn Lusayb (with a b)" (p. 166).
- People differ on his repentance (p. 167): "some people claimed Zayd repented", "others said he never ceased to be suspected of evil until he perished".
