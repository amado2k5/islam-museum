Kaddour Benghabrit (1 November 1868 – 24 June 1954), born Abdelkader Ben Ghabrit at Sidi Bel Abbes into a prominent Andalusian family of Tlemcen, was educated at the Thaalibiya madrasa in Algiers and the University of al-Karaouine in Fez, and began his career in the Algerian judiciary.{cite:wikipedia-kaddour-benghabrit-en}{cite:wikipedia-kaddour-benghabrit-fr}

From 1892 he served the French legation at Tangier as interpreter, becoming a trusted liaison between North Africa and the Quai d'Orsay; he interpreted the negotiations that produced the Treaty of Fes in 1912 and became the Sultan of Morocco's head of protocol.{cite:wikipedia-kaddour-benghabrit-en} In 1916 he headed the French mission to Mecca to facilitate the Hajj and to win the Sharif Hussein to the Allied side, and in 1917 he founded the Society of Habous and the Holy Places of Islam.{cite:wikipedia-kaddour-benghabrit-en}

Through that society he founded the Muslim Institute of the Grand Mosque of Paris, opened in 1926, and remained its rector until his death. During the German occupation he sheltered Jews at the mosque and had Muslim identity certificates issued to them, saving hundreds of lives. Dubbed "the most Parisian of Muslims", he held the Grand Cross of the Legion of Honour and was buried north of his mosque, according to the Maliki rite.{cite:wikipedia-kaddour-benghabrit-en}
