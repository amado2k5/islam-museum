## Şeyhi

Şeyhi — in his own name Yusuf Sinan, and famous in his lifetime as Hekim Sinan, "Sinan the physician" — is counted among the founders of classical Turkish literature {cite:tdv-seyhi}. He was born at Kütahya, then the capital of the Germiyan beylik, the son of Ahmed Mecdüddin {cite:tdv-seyhi}. No early source gives his birth year: Latifi places the birth in the reign of Murad I, and modern scholarship reasons from a fixed point in his career — that in 818/1415 he treated the eye of Sultan Mehmed I and was already famous under his physician's name — that he must then have been about forty to forty-five years old, which puts his birth between 1371 and 1376 {cite:tdv-seyhi}. The dates that circulate for him apart from that range, such as the 1350 carried by Wikidata, are supported by no text {cite:wikidata-seyhi}.

### Formation and the Ankara affiliation

He began his studies at Kütahya, taking lessons from the divan poet Ahmedi and other scholars, then travelled to Iran, where he studied literature, Sufism and medicine and was a fellow student of al-Sharif al-Jurjani {cite:tdv-seyhi}. Latifi reports that on the return journey he stopped at Ankara and bound himself, in the Sufi manner of intisab, to Haci Bayram Veli, meeting the poet Nesimi at Bursa on the same journey; later scholarship has accepted the inference that he took his pen name Şeyhi from that affiliation, though whether he ever practised sheikhdom himself is not known {cite:tdv-seyhi}. The Turkish article carries the affiliation in a stronger form, as his becoming Haci Bayram's khalifa {cite:wikipedia-seyhi-tr}. Back home he practised as an eye-doctor, a craft in which the sources call him masterly {cite:tdv-seyhi}.

### Physician to beys and sultans

At Kütahya he entered the service of the Germiyan bey Yakub II as companion and personal physician {cite:tdv-seyhi}. His contact with the Ottoman house began under Emir Süleyman Çelebi, and after the treatment of Mehmed I's eye in 1415 he became the first chief physician of the Ottoman state {cite:tdv-seyhi}. In 831/1428 he acted as host to Yakub II on the bey's visit to Edirne, and when Yakub died the following year he wrote his elegy — the poem that anchors his own death after 1429 {cite:tdv-seyhi}. He also met Murad II, at whose wish the tezkire tradition says the great mesnevi was undertaken, but he did not remain long at the Ottoman court {cite:tdv-seyhi} {cite:wikipedia-seyhi-tr}.

### The works

His Divan — fifteen kasides, four terciibends, two terkibibends, a mesnevi, two müstezads and 202 ghazals — stands among the earliest divans arranged in Anatolia {cite:tdv-seyhi}. The work that made his name is the Hüsrev ü Şirin, 6,944 couplets on the TDV entry's count, the second Turkish telling of Nizami's romance, which began as a translation at Murad II's wish and grew by his additions into a work of his own; he died with it unfinished, and his nephew Cemali completed it {cite:tdv-seyhi} {cite:wikipedia-seyhi-tr}. Beside it stands the Harname, 126 couplets presented to Murad II, in which a worn-out donkey envies the fat oxen, goes looking for horns and comes home without ears or tail — the most celebrated piece of satire in early divan literature {cite:tdv-seyhi}. Not everything attached to his name is his: the medical compilation Kenzü'l-menafi, ascribed to him in Osmanlı Müellifleri, fails as his work on style, content and language, and the Habname and Neyname attributed to him in the same compilation have never been found {cite:tdv-seyhi}.

### Death and the two Şeyh Sinans

His last years are obscure; in all likelihood he returned to Kütahya, died there, and was buried at Dumlupınar outside the city, where his tomb became a place of visitation {cite:tdv-seyhi}. Of the death dates in the older compilations — 826/1423, 829/1426, 855/1451 — none rests on evidence; the elegy for Yakub Bey proves only that he outlived 1429, and the year now accepted, following Timurtaş, is 1431 {cite:tdv-seyhi} {cite:wikipedia-seyhi-tr}. One later identification made the poet into a Şeyh Sinan attached to Emir Sultan of Bursa, who went to Alaşehir in 833/1430, built the Şeyh Sinan mosque there in 870/1465-66 and died in 887/1482; the TDV entry examines that case and rejects it on the latest documents as a conflation of two different men {cite:tdv-seyhi}.
