## Summary
Fath al-Din Abu al-Fath Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Muhammad Ibn Sayyid al-Nas al-Ya'muri, of Andalusian family and Egyptian residence, was a Shafi'i hadith master, man of letters and poet, author of [[work/uyun-al-athar|Uyun al-Athar fi Funun al-Maghazi wa-l-Shama'il wa-l-Siyar]]. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.4} {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.6}

## Birth, upbringing and teachers
He was born in [[place/cairo|Cairo]] in Dhu al-Qa'da — or Dhu al-Hijja — 661 AH, or by another account in 671, and grew up in a learned household. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.4} He heard hadith from al-Izz al-Harrani, Ghazi al-Halawi, Ibn al-Anmati and others, studied Shafi'i law, took hadith from his father and from [[person/ibn-daqiq-al-id|Taqi al-Din Ibn Daqiq al-Id]], whom he attended for many years and with whom he read legal theory, and read grammar with al-Baha' Ibn al-Nahhas. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.4} He reached Damascus a few nights after the death of Ibn al-Bukhari and so missed him, and heard there from Ibn al-Mujawir, Muhammad ibn Mu'min and al-Taqi al-Wasiti. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.4}

## Posts and standing
He taught hadith at the Zahiriyya madrasa in Cairo and preached at the Khandaq mosque, as the editor of the Sharqawi edition reports from Ibn Taghri Birdi. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.5} Al-Suyuti called him one of the leading hadith masters, a critic in the field, expert in narrators, hidden defects and chains; Ibn Kathir said he excelled in hadith, law, grammar and sira and had fine poetry and a Salafi creed; al-Safadi praised his memory, his range in letters and composition and his beautiful hand. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.4} {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.5} Al-Dhahabi wrote in his specialised dictionary that he was one of the imams of this discipline, copied much in his fine hand, and compiled, authored, corrected and identified defects. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.6}

## Works
- [[work/uyun-al-athar|Uyun al-Athar]], which he abridged in a few quires under the title Nur al-Uyun. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.6}
- Bushra al-Labib fi Dhikra al-Habib, poems in praise of the Prophet, some of them printed in Europe in the nineteenth century. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.6}
- Al-Nafh al-Shadhi fi Sharh Jami' al-Tirmidhi, which reached the Book of Prayer and was left unfinished; the hadith master Abu al-Fadl al-Iraqi completed it. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.6} The editors of the Khatrawi edition say he first titled it al-'Arf al-Shadhi and changed the title at the suggestion of his student Salah al-Din [[person/al-safadi|al-Safadi]]. {cite:uyun-al-athar-khatrawi-v1 p.11}
- Tahsil al-Isaba fi Tafdil al-Sahaba, a large volume against the sale of slave mothers of their masters' children (umm walad), and al-Maqamat al-'Aliyya fi al-Karamat al-Jaliyya. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.6}

## Death
He died suddenly on Saturday 11 Sha'ban 734 AH; his funeral was well attended and he was buried in [[place/al-qarafa|al-Qarafa]] beside Ibn Abi Hamza. {cite:uyun-al-athar-sharqawi-1993 p.6}
