## Overview
Ahmad ibn Abi Tahir Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Isfara'ini, the jurist, shaykh of Iraq and imam of the Shafi'is to whom the leadership of the school came; he reached Baghdad as a boy and studied under Ibn al-Marzuban and Abu'l-Qasim al-Dariki — his lecture notes ran to about fifty volumes and seven hundred jurists attended his class, and they said "had al-Shafi'i seen him he would have rejoiced"; born in 344, he gave fatwas at seventeen. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.178-179}

## Testimonies and death
Ibn al-Jawzi places his teaching circle in the mosque of 'Abdallah ibn al-Mubarak at the head of the Rabi' allotment; Abu'l-Husayn al-Quduri said "I never saw a better jurist among the Shafi'is than Abu Hamid," and al-Saymari "the cleverest jurist I ever saw"; he died on Saturday night with eleven nights left of Shawwal 406, and Abu 'Abdallah ibn al-Muhtadi, khatib of the Mansur mosque, led the funeral prayer in the open field in a grand assembly. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi}
