## Summary
Bahauddeen Muhammed Jamaluddeen Nadwi is an Indian Islamic scholar from Kerala, the founding vice-chancellor of Darul Huda Islamic University in Malappuram district, and a senior figure in the Samastha tradition of Kerala's Sunni scholars. {cite:dhiu-bahauddeen-nadwi}

## Life and upbringing
He was born on 22 April 1951 (15 Rajab 1370 AH) at Kooriyad near Kottakkal in Malappuram district, into a family of religious scholars. His father, Muhammed Jamaluddeen Musliyar, was a Sufi scholar, and his maternal grandfather Sheikh Zainuddin bin Ahmed, known as Thenu Musliyar, was a widely followed Sufi of twentieth-century Malabar. After early schooling he entered Jamia Nooriyya Arabiyya in 1972 and completed its course with the honorary title Moulawi Fazil al-Faizy; he also took the Afzal al-Ulama degree from the University of Calicut in 1979 and a government diploma in Arabic teaching. {cite:dhiu-bahauddeen-nadwi}

## Career
At about thirty he moved to northern India and studied at Darul Uloom Nadwatul Ulama in Lucknow, taking the Alimiyya degree and a Fazeelath (master's) degree in Islamic studies, followed by a master's in Arabic literature from Aligarh Muslim University in 1987 and an advanced diploma in Islamic preaching at al-Azhar University in Cairo. In 1993 he earned a doctorate from the Department of Arabic Language and Literature at the University of Calicut, followed by a post-doctoral fellowship. {cite:dhiu-bahauddeen-nadwi}

He is counted among the six founding figures of Darul Huda Islamic Academy, where he served as vice-principal; when the academy was upgraded in 2009 into Darul Huda Islamic University — described by the institution as the first Islamic university in South India — he was appointed its first vice-chancellor, designing a curriculum that joined Islamic higher education with modern disciplines. He also serves on the supreme council of Samastha Kerala Jam'iyyathul Ulama and became president of the Samastha Kerala Jam'iyyathul Mu'allimeen central council in 2020, and holds membership in the International Union of Muslim Scholars. {cite:dhiu-bahauddeen-nadwi}

## Contributions and legacy
Nadwi has authored and translated books across the Islamic disciplines, lectures internationally, and directs the Malayalam daily Suprabhaatham; he has edited the monthly Thelitcham since 1998. Within the Muslim 500 he is listed as one of the influential scholars of the Muslim world, his significance resting on institution-building: joining Kerala's madrasa tradition to a university model for a generation of South Indian students. {cite:dhiu-bahauddeen-nadwi}
