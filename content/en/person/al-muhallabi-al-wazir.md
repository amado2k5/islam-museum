## Overview
Abu Muhammad al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Muhallabi, vizier of [[person/muizz-al-dawla|Mu'izz al-Dawla]]. Al-Suyuti says that after what the Shi'a had written of cursing on the doors of the mosques was erased, he advised Mu'izz al-Dawla to write in its place: "May God curse the wrongdoers against the family of the Messenger of God", and that they only expressed the curse on Mu'awiya. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.619}

## His coming to Baghdad
Ibn al-Athir relates that he came to Baghdad before Mu'izz al-Dawla, met Ibn Shirzad in the place where he was hiding, and then met al-Mustakfi. Al-Mustakfi showed his pleasure at the coming of Mu'izz al-Dawla, and informed him that he had hidden from the Turks so that they would scatter, and the affair would come to Mu'izz al-Dawla without fighting. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.336}

## In Miskawayh's Tajarib al-umam
[[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] was al-Muhallabi's boon companion and says that al-Muhallabi told him most of what happened in his time "through long companionship and frequent sittings", making him one of his sources for the years after 340 AH. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.11} The book's editor notes that al-Muhallabi became Mu'izz al-Dawla's secretary in 339 and was given the title of vizier in 345. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v1 p.12} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.199}
- **His beating in 341 AH:** Miskawayh relates that Mu'izz al-Dawla, angered with al-Muhallabi over the affair of Tazadh, seized him and gave him a hundred and fifty lashes, pausing to upbraid him and then resuming, until he was said to be near death; he had him taken home under guard, and later robed him again and restored him, finding no one to take his place. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.180} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.181}
- **His forbearance:** Miskawayh says Mu'izz al-Dawla was "sharp, quick to anger and foul-mouthed", constantly abusing his viziers, and al-Muhallabi bore it like a man who did not care: "I was his boon companion at the time and saw no trace in him of what he heard". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.181}
- **His death:** among the events of 352 AH Miskawayh records that his illness worsened in mid-Sha'ban while on an expedition towards Oman; he was brought back to al-Ubulla out of his mind, carried in a litter borne by forty men, and died on Saturday, three days before the end of Sha'ban, in the afternoon, at Zawata; his coffin reached Baghdad on Wednesday 5 Ramadan. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.239}
- **After his death:** he relates that Mu'izz al-Dawla seized his estate and arrested his family, his son and anyone who had ever called on him, and that they were mulcted "down to the muleteers and boatmen" who served his household, "and people found it hideous and held it against Mu'izz al-Dawla"; his vizierate had lasted thirteen years and three months. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.239}
