Mahmud Gami (c. 1765–1855) was a Kashmiri Sufi poet of Doru Shahabad in Anantnag, born at Aroora — the village now called Mahmudabad after him — and buried there on his death in 1855.{cite:wikipedia-mahmud-gami-ks}{cite:wikidata-mahmud-gami} Popularly known as the Jami of Kashmir, he chose his takhallus Gami, the Kashmiri biography says, to rhyme with Jami and Nizami, the Persian masters whose forms he naturalised.{cite:wikipedia-mahmud-gami-ks}{cite:wikipedia-mahmud-gami-en}

He is credited with introducing the Persian mathnavi and ghazal into Kashmiri. His Kulliyat, published by the Jammu and Kashmir Academy of Art, Culture and Languages, gathers 112 vatsuns and ghazals, nineteen nazms, na'ats in praise of Muhammad, and eleven mathnavis adapted from Persian literature — Yusuf-Zuleikha from Jami, Lael Majnun and Shirin-Khusrao from Nizami, and Sheikh Sana'n from Attar's Manteq-ut-Tair.{cite:wikipedia-mahmud-gami-en} The Kashmiri biography styles him an eighteenth-century Sufi poet and dates the beginning of Kashmir's line of Sufi poets from him; the Kashmir Times likewise presents him as Dooru's mystic saint-poet, whose grave is still visited and whose couplets are sung at gatherings.{cite:wikipedia-mahmud-gami-ks}{cite:kashmir-times-gami-rasul-triad} Mufti Mudasir's Yusuf's Fragrance (2022) gave English readers their first substantial translation of his verse.{cite:wikipedia-mahmud-gami-en}
