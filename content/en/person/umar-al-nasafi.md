## Overview
Umar ibn Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Ismail, Abu Hafs, Najm al-Din al-Nasafi, is described by al-Zirikli as a scholar of Qur'anic exegesis, letters and history and one of the Hanafi jurists. He was born at Nasaf, to which his name points, and died in Samarkand. Al-Zirikli gives his birth in 461 AH, 1068 CE, and his death in 537 AH, 1142 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}.

## Life
Al-Zirikli says he was given the title "mufti of the two worlds of men and jinn" (mufti al-thaqalayn), and that he is a different man from the exegete al-Nasafi, Abd Allah ibn Ahmad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}. He reports that it was said he had about a hundred works {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli names, in exegesis, "al-Akmal al-Atwal" and "al-Taysir fi al-tafsir", both in manuscript {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}. He also names "al-Mawaqit"; "Ta'dad shuyukh Umar", on his teachers; and "al-Ish'ar bi-l-mukhtar min al-ash'ar", in twenty parts {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}. In Hanafi law he lists "Nazm al-Jami al-Saghir", in manuscript, "Qayd al-Awabid", a legal poem in manuscript, and a poem on points of disagreement ("Manzumat al-Khilafiyyat"), in manuscript {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}. In history he lists "al-Qand fi ulama Samarqand", in twenty parts, and a history of Bukhara {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}. His "Talabat al-Talaba", on the technical terms of jurisprudence, is printed, as is his "al-Aqa'id" (creeds), known as the creed of al-Nasafi {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 60}.
