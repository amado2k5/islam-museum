Abu Muslim Salama ibn Amr ibn al-Akwa al-Aslami was a distinguished Companion, known for exceptional courage and speed on foot, to the point that it was said he could outrun horses. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He witnessed the Pledge of Ridwan and pledged allegiance to the Prophet three times: at the beginning of the people, in the middle, and at the end. His rare bravery appeared in the raid of Dhi Qarad, when he pursued the raiders of Ghatafan alone on foot until he rescued the Prophet's milch camels. He witnessed the conquest of Khaybar and the battles after it, and he was among those who gave legal opinions in Medina. {cite:sahih-muslim}

In the last part of his life, after the killing of Uthman, he settled in al-Rabadha. He later came to Medina, where he died in the year 74 AH at about eighty years of age. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## In the raid on Banu Fazara
Ibn Ishaq reports that Salama ibn Amr ibn al-Akwa took a girl, the daughter of Umm Qirfa, during [[event/raid-of-zayd-on-banu-fazara-and-umm-qirfa|the raid of Zayd ibn Haritha on Banu Fazara]]. The Prophet asked him for her, and he gave her to Hazn ibn Abi Wahb. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/266}

## In Khalid Muhammad Khalid's Rijal Hawl al-Rasul
[[person/khalid-muhammad-khalid|Khalid Muhammad Khalid]] titles his chapter on him in [[work/rijal-hawl-al-rasul|Rijal Hawl al-Rasul]] "Hero of the foot soldiers", and opens it with his son Iyas's words: "My father never told a lie." He describes him as one of the Arabs' noted archers, outstanding in courage and generosity, and one of the men of the [[event/bayat-al-ridwan|Pledge of Ridwan]], who pledged under the tree to fight to the death and pledged again when the Prophet called him. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.553} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.554} He quotes him: "I went on seven campaigns with the Messenger of God and nine with Zayd ibn Haritha." {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.555}
The author likens his way of fighting on foot — falling back when attacked and attacking when the enemy withdrew or rested — to some forms of guerrilla warfare; by it he pursued alone the force that raided under [[person/uyayna-ibn-hisn|Uyayna ibn Hisn]] in the [[event/expedition-of-dhu-qarad|campaign of Dhu Qarad]] until the Prophet caught up, saying that day, "The best of our foot soldiers is Salama ibn al-Akwa'." {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.555} He tells of his grief for his brother Amir ibn al-Akwa' at Khaybar, whose own sword rebounded on him, when some said he had lost the reward of martyrdom, and the Prophet told Salama that Amir had a double reward. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.555} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.556} He gave to anyone who asked by the face of God, saying, "If one does not give for the face of God, for what will he give?" After Uthman was killed he left for [[place/al-rabadha|al-Rabadha]] rather than fight a Muslim, and lived there until he visited Medina in 74 AH and died there on the third day. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.556} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.557}
