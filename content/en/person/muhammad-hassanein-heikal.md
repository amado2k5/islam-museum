## Summary

Mohamed Hassanein Heikal (1923–2016) was the most famous Arab journalist of the twentieth century, editor-in-chief of Al-Ahram for seventeen years and a writer who documented the Nasser and Sadat eras from inside.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-hassanein-heikal}

## Life

Born in Cairo, he began in journalism as a war correspondent in the 1940s, grew close to Gamal Abdel Nasser after the 1952 revolution, becoming media adviser and informal spokesman of the regime, and headed Al-Ahram's editorship from 1957 to 1974.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-hassanein-heikal}

## Books and method

He wrote a series of memoir-histories of Egyptian and Arab politics: Autumn of Fury on the Sadat era, The Cairo Documents, and The American Empire and the Preemptive Strikes. His method mixes journalism with document and personal testimony, and his accounts provoked debate over accuracy and position.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-hassanein-heikal}

## Standing

He is described as the maker and foremost witness of modern Arab political journalism, and criticised for closeness to power and selectivity of narrative. He died in Cairo in 2016; his books remain among the most widely read on the region's modern history.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-hassanein-heikal}
