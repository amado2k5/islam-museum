## Summary

Sultana Taj ul-Alam Safiatuddin Syah, born Putri Sri Alam about 1612 — the Indonesian article notes the year comes from later historiography, with no contemporary birth record, so it is an estimate — was the fourteenth ruler of Aceh, reigning from 1641 to 1675 as the first of four women to hold the position in succession.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en}{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-id} She was the daughter of Sultan Iskandar Muda and the wife of his successor Iskandar Thani — not Iskandar Thani's daughter.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en}{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-ar}

## Marriage and accession

Her father conquered Pahang in 1617 and the defeated sultan's son, the future Iskandar Thani, was brought as a prisoner to Aceh and raised as Iskandar Muda's foster son; in 1619, aged nine, he was married to Putri Sri Alam, and the couple were given the Sri Wareuna palace next to the sultan's.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en} After Iskandar Muda's death her husband succeeded, then died on 15 February 1641; his death created grave disturbances, and after three days it was agreed that his widow would be enthroned — the Dutch report places the enthronement about 18 February, while the Bustan al-Salatin places her accession on her husband's death day itself.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en}{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-id}

## Rule

Putri Sri Alam took the title Sultana Taj ul-Alam Safiatuddin Syah — "world crown, pure-of-faith" — and governed, as the Indonesian article describes it, by negotiation and consultation with the nobility, the ulama and the commanders, rather than by her father's military centralisation.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en}{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-id} Acehnese power outside the heartland contracted under her: Johor and Pahang were given up while tin-producing Perak was retained, VOC ships blockaded Aceh until the 1659 treaty granting the Company half of Perak's tin and a trade monopoly that was not fulfilled, the Painan treaty of 1663 led to the expulsion of the Acehnese from the west coast, and Deli fell away in 1669.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en}

## Learning and legacy

Indigenous and European writers praised her: the chronicle Bustanus Salatin characterised her as virtuous and pious, and a Dutch witness called her rule "good-natured but awe-inspiring".{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en} She inherited a tradition of Islamic scholarship at court: she was less favourable to Nuruddin ar-Raniri than her predecessor and he left royal employ in 1644, while the major writer of her reign was Abdurrauf of Singkil, who wrote on Shafi'i jurisprudence and mysticism and composed the fiqh work Mir'at al-Tullab at her request about 1663; a proliferation of Islamic literature and learning marked her reign and those of the three queens who succeeded her.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en}{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-id} She died on 23 October 1675 leaving no children, and was succeeded by another queen, Sultan Nurul Alam Naqiatuddin Syah.{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-en}{cite:wikipedia-taj-al-alam-ar}
