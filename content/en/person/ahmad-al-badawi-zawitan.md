## Overview
Ahmad al-Badawi ibn Ahmad Zawitan al-Darqawi, Abu al-Abbas, was a Moroccan Sufi from Fez, who died in 1275 AH (1859 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}. He kept a shop in the spice-sellers' market, left it, devoted himself to learning, and became fond of the books of the Sufis {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}.

## His works
He composed "al-Rasa'il al-Kubra", which he named "al-Munajat al-Fardiyya"; the author of "al-Salwa" says he came across them in a large volume and counted them among the finest and most precious epistles {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}. He also wrote "al-Rasa'il al-Sughra", a manuscript in Rabat, and five epistles in a manuscript collection, the first of which he addressed to the people of Meknes {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 84}.
