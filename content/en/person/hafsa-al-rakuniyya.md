## Summary
Hafsa bint al-Hajj al-Rakuniyya was a poet and woman of letters of [[place/granada|Granada]], endowed with beauty, lineage, wealth, ready wit and quickness of mind, as [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] presents her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.271}

## Her verse among her contemporaries
Kahhala cites Ibn Dihya that Hafsa was of the nobility of Granada, "soft-voiced in verse, delicate in poetry and prose". {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.271} Among her poems are lines she improvised before the Commander of the Faithful [[person/abd-al-mumin-ibn-ali|Abd al-Mu'min ibn Ali]], asking him to grant her a decree on which his own hand would write "Praise be to God alone"; Abu al-Khattab quoted two love verses of hers in *al-Mutrib*. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.271}

## With Abu Ja'far ibn Sa'id
Kahhala relates that Abu Ja'far ibn Sa'id asked to meet her and she put him off for about two months; he wrote her verses complaining of his waiting, and she answered with verses faulting his versification and his claim to lead in love. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.271} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.272} Their exchanges and meetings in his garden known as al-Kamama follow, and Abu Ja'far swears he never saw or heard of anyone like Hafsa. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.273} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.275}

## Praise poems and writing
She congratulated the sayyid Abu Sa'id, ruler of Granada, on a feast day in verses addressing him as "son of the caliph and of the approved imam"; al-Mallahi reports in his history that a lady of Granada's notables asked her for something in her own hand, and she wrote her verses apologising for her poor script. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.274} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.275}

## For young visitors
Hafsa was a poet from Granada in al-Andalus who could make up verses on the spot — even in front of the ruler.
