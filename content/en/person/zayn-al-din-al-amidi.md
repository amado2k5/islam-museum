## Overview
Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Yusuf ibn al-Khidr, Zayn al-Din al-Amidi, was the first to make the 'marking letters' (al-huruf al-tariza); of Amid (Diyarbakir) by origin, he lived at Baghdad and died there in 714 AH (1314 CE), one of the chief Hanbalis in fiqh, piety, honesty and presence. {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.257}

Blind from childhood, he was a marvel of discernment, sharpness of mind and dream-interpretation, and knew many languages including Persian, Turkish, Mongol and Greek. He traded in books and amassed many; whenever he bought a book he would twist a paper into one or more alphabet letters for the price in abjad reckoning, paste it at the book's edge under a fixing leaf, and when the price slipped his mind he would touch the paper letters and recall it. He wrote books including Jawahir al-Tabsir fi 'Ilm al-Ta'bir. {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.257}
