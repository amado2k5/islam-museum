## Overview
Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, Abd Allah ibn Qays al-Ash'ari, a Companion of the Ash'aris and an ally of the Banu Utba ibn Rabi'a ibn Abd Shams. He was among those whom the two ships brought from Abyssinia to the Prophet at Khaybar. See [[event/return-of-abyssinian-emigrants|the return of the emigrants from Abyssinia]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

He became known for his jurisprudence and his recitation of the Qur'an, and he was appointed over Basra and Kufa in the caliphate of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Before Siffin
Al-Mas'udi relates that before Siffin he used to say that this community would not stop facing trials until they appointed two arbiters. Suwayd ibn Ghafla advised him not to be one of them. He then accepted an arbiter on behalf of the people of Iraq, and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] did not accept him, but al-Ash'ath and his group insisted. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 682-683}

## For Sprouts
A noble companion from Yemen who had a beautiful voice reciting the Qur'an and was a wise teacher and judge.

## For Explorers
Abu Musa al-Ashari was an esteemed companion who came from Yemen with his people to accept Islam. Blessed with an exceptionally melodious voice in reciting the Qur'an, the Prophet praised him saying he had been given a flute from the flutes of the family of David. He served as a wise governor and judge in Basra and Kufa.

## For Scholars
Abu Musa Abd Allah ibn Qays al-Ashari (d. c. 44 AH / 665 CE), a prominent companion, jurist, judge, and military commander originating from the Ash'ar tribe of Yemen. After migrating to Abyssinia, he arrived in Medina during the Khaybar expedition in 7 AH, earning high prophetic praise for the Ash'arite virtues of mutual solidarity. Acclaimed for his melodious and moving recitation of the Qur'an, likened by the Prophet to the melodic pipes of the house of David, he was appointed governor of Basra and later Kufa under Caliphs Umar and Uthman, serving as an esteemed judge and later representing Caliph Ali during the historic arbitration proceedings.
