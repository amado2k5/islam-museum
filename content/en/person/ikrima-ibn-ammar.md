## Summary
Ikrima ibn Ammar, Abu Ammar al-Ijli of al-Yamama, has an entry in [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]]'s [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] marked with the sigla of Muslim and the four Sunan: he narrated from al-Hirmas ibn Ziyad and also from [[person/tawus-ibn-kaysan|Tawus]], [[person/salim-ibn-abd-allah-ibn-umar|Salim]], [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata]] and [[person/yahya-ibn-abi-kathir|Yahya ibn Abi Kathir]], and [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya al-Qattan]], [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-mahdi|Ibn Mahdi]], Abu al-Walid and many others narrated from him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 90} Asim ibn Ali said that his prayers were answered. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 91}

## The critics' verdicts
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: trustworthy and firm (Ibn Ma'in, Ali ibn al-Madini)**: Ya'qub ibn Shayba reports from several who heard [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]] say: trustworthy and firm; Abu Hatim reports Ibn Ma'in as saying: he was unlettered but had a sound memory; [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ali ibn al-Madini]] said: among our colleagues Ikrima ibn Ammar was trustworthy and firm; [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]] said: Muslim cited him often in corroboration. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 90} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 91}
- **Sunni: truthful but errs, weak from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir (Abu Hatim, al-Qattan, Ahmad, al-Bukhari)**: [[person/abu-hatim-muhammad-al-razi|Abu Hatim]] said: truthful, sometimes mistaken; Yahya al-Qattan: his hadiths from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir are weak; [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]]: weak in hadith, though his hadith from Iyas ibn Salama was sound, and his hadiths from Yahya are weak, not sound; [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]]: he had no written book, so his hadith from Yahya became confused. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 90} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 91}
:::

Al-Dhahabi relates that Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi was with [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan]] at Ikrima ibn Ammar's, and when Ibn Mahdi wanted to take the book to copy, Sufyan told him not to hurry and made Ikrima confirm each hadith as something he had heard; Ibn Mahdi added that Sufyan's handwriting was poor. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 91}

## Against the Qadariyya
Al-Dhahabi quotes [[person/sulayman-ibn-harb|Sulayman ibn Harb]]: Ikrima ibn Ammar came to us from al-Yamama, and I saw him on a rooftop arguing with the people of [[concept/qadar|qadar]] about qadar — to which al-Dhahabi adds: and Basra is the nest of the Qadari doctrine. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 91} Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh heard him tell people: I adjure any man who holds the Qadari view to get up and leave, for I will not narrate to him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 91}

## His reports from al-Hirmas
Al-Dhahabi gives a series of his reports from al-Hirmas ibn Ziyad: that as a small boy riding behind his father he saw the Prophet addressing people from his she-camel al-Adba' at Mina on the Day of Sacrifice, touching the Corner with a crooked staff, and that he came to the Prophet as a boy to pledge allegiance and the Prophet did not take his pledge. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 91} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 92} He then lists hadiths found objectionable in his narration, and ends by noting that Muslim included in his Sahih an objectionable core report of his from Simak al-Hanafi from Ibn Abbas about the three things Abu Sufyan asked for. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 92} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 93}
