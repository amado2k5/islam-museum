## Summary

Assia Djebar (1936–2015), real name Fatima-Zohra Imalayen, was an Algerian novelist and filmmaker, the first North African admitted to the Académie française, whose works made Algerian women's voices a subject for literature and history.{cite:wikipedia-assia-djebar}

## Life

Born in Cherchell, she studied history in Algiers and Paris, published her first novel young, then paused publishing for years to work in history and cinema — directing the film La Nouba des femmes du Mont Chenoua (1977) — before returning with her major works and settling to teach in the United States.{cite:wikipedia-assia-djebar}

## Works

Her novel Fantasia (1985) weaves the history of French colonialism with women's lives; Women of Algiers in Their Apartment (1980), Far from Medina and So Vast the Prison (1995) follow women's memory through colonialism and the Algerian civil war.{cite:wikipedia-assia-djebar}

## Standing

The Académie française elected her in 2005 and she won international honours including the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade (2000). She remains central to discussions of women's writing and postcolonial Francophone literature.{cite:wikipedia-assia-djebar}
