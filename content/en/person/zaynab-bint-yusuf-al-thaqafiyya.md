## Summary
Zaynab bint Yusuf ibn al-Hakam al-Thaqafiyya was the sister of [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]]; [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']] describes her as a woman of intelligence, judgement and resolve. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.128}

## With al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba
She was married to [[person/al-mughira-ibn-shuba|al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba]], who one morning saw her picking her teeth, mocked her and divorced her; she told him he was a poor husband for a free woman, and that what he had seen was only a splinter of her tooth-stick caught between two of her teeth. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.128}

## Al-Numayri's poem
The love poet Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Numayr al-Thaqafi, an Umayyad-era poet born and raised at [[place/taif|Ta'if]], was enamoured of her and composed a poem picturing her among women on the lesser pilgrimage, passing Nu'man and Fakhkh as they called out the talbiya. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.128} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.129} The poem reached [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]], who wrote to al-Hajjaj to ignore the man and not mention him: if he drew him near or rebuked him he would encourage him, and if he punished him he would seem to confirm the poem. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.129} Al-Numayri then fled to Abd al-Malik for protection; the caliph had him recite the poem, laughed at his description of his "caravan", and wrote to al-Hajjaj that he had no claim on him. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.129} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.130}

## Al-Hajjaj and the poem
Kahhala relates that al-Hajjaj swore he would hear the poem, and as al-Numayri recited he commented line by line: he called him a liar for saying she wore perfume when she went out, agreed that she was a frequent pilgrim and faster who went out veiled at nightfall — "that is how a free Muslim woman behaves" — and granted him safety. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.130} When Zaynab died, al-Numayri mourned her in an elegy. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.130}

## For young visitors
Zaynab was the sister of the governor al-Hajjaj; she was clever and firm, and when a poet praised her in a poem her brother was angry at first, but on hearing it found it described a good woman.
