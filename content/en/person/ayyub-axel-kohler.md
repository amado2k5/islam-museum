## Summary
Ayyub Axel Köhler is a German geophysicist and Muslim community leader, chairman of the Central Council of Muslims in Germany (ZMD) from 2006 to 2010 — the first German-born convert to head a major German Muslim umbrella body. {cite:wikipedia-ayyub-axel-kohler} {cite:muslim500-ayyub-axel-kohler}

## Life and formation
He was born Axel Köhler on 3 April 1938 in Stettin (now Szczecin, Poland), displaced with his family to East Germany, and fled to the Federal Republic in 1956. He studied geophysics at Freiburg and Kiel, took his doctorate at Cologne in 1968, and converted to Sunni Islam in 1963 as a student, taking the name Ayyub — the Arabic form of Job. {cite:wikipedia-ayyub-axel-kohler}

## Professional life
He worked briefly at the German aerospace research establishment, taught as assistant professor at the University of Tehran from 1970, and from 1973 to his 1999 retirement worked at the German Economic Institute in Cologne on economics and ecology, meanwhile advising the International Association of Islamic Banks. {cite:wikipedia-ayyub-axel-kohler}

## Muslim institution-building
A co-founder of the Islamischer Arbeitskreis that became the ZMD in 1994, he was its general secretary from 2001, then chairman 2006–2010, and helped found the Koordinierungsrat der Muslime (KRM) coordinating Germany's Muslim umbrella bodies. He also sat as a liberal FDP councillor in Cologne-Nippes. His line — loyalty to the German constitution, an "Islamic charter" framework, condemnation of violence — made him a spokesman for a settled, German-rooted Islam; conservative critics within the community found his profile more political than theological. The museum records the assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-ayyub-axel-kohler} {cite:muslim500-ayyub-axel-kohler}
