## Summary
Of Ashja', an ally of Banu Salama; he was among the band who mocked the Tabuk army then excused themselves. Ibn Ishaq mentions he renamed himself Abd al-Rahman and asked God to be killed a martyr in a place unknown — and he was killed on the day of al-Yamama and no trace of him was found. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/168}

## In the Tabuk expedition
Ibn Ishaq names him "a man of Ashja', an ally of Banu Salama, called Mukhashin ibn Humayyir". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/168} He says the man wished no Quran would descend on them even were each flogged a hundred lashes, and that he afterwards said to the Prophet: my name and my father's name have shamed me. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/168-169} And that it is he who was pardoned in the verse — so he renamed himself Abd al-Rahman and asked God to kill him a martyr where none would know his place; he was killed on the day of al-Yamama and no trace of him was found. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/169}

## Tribe
Ibn Ishaq gives his lineage: of Ashja', ally of Banu Salama. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/168}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- Ibn Hisham said: "and it is said Mukhashshi" instead of "Mukhashin" (p. 168).
- Ibn Ishaq's saying that he is the one pardoned in the verse is an interpretation the museum ascribes to him (p. 169).
