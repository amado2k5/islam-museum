## Overview
Nur Allah ibn Sharif al-Din Abd Allah ibn Diya al-Din Nur Allah ibn Muhammad Shah al-Mar'ashi al-Tustari, also written al-Shushtari, was of the line of Imam Zayn al-Abidin. Al-Zirikli calls him a mujtahid among the Imami scholars, that is, of the Twelver Shi'a. He was styled al-Qadi Diya al-Din and came from Tustar. Al-Zirikli gives his years as 956 to 1019 AH, 1549 to 1610 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}.

## In India and his death
He travelled to India, where Sultan Akbar Shah appointed him chief judge at Lahore on condition that his rulings not depart from the four Sunni schools. He kept the post until he showed otherwise, and was killed under the lash in the city of Akbarabad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}.

## His works
Al-Zirikli says he left ninety-seven books and treatises, whose names are given by the author of Shuhada' al-Fadila. The best known is Ihqaq al-Haqq, printed, which the same author says is what caused his killing. Others are Majalis al-Mu'minin, printed, on the famous men of the Shi'a; Masa'ib al-Nawasib, in manuscript; a gloss on the Qur'an commentary of al-Baydawi, in manuscript; al-Husn wa-l-Qubh, in manuscript; and Tadhhib al-Akmam fi Sharh Tahdhib al-Ahkam, in manuscript {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 8 p. 50}.
