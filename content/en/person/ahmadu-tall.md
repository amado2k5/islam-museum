## Overview

Ahmadu Tall (21 June 1836 – 15 December 1897), whose full name the English article gives as Ahmad al-Madani al-Kabir at-Tijani, was the eldest son of al-Hajj Umar Tall and his successor as ruler of the Toucouleur Empire in West Africa.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-ar} He conquered Ségou for his father in 1861, inherited a fragmenting empire in 1864, and defended it against the French advance for nearly three decades before going into exile in the east.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en}

## Career

He was born during his father Umar Tall's stay in the Sokoto Caliphate — the French article names Sokoto as the birthplace, with a citation-needed flag — the eldest son of Umar and of Aisha, a Hausa slave, and his half-brothers looked down on him for his mother's slave status.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-fr} He was raised at Dinguiraye, his father's capital, and in 1859 his father named him Amir al-Muminin and Laamdo Dioulbé.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en} On 10 March 1861 he conquered Ségou, the heart of the Bambara Empire, and was left there as its Almami while his father campaigned in Massina, where Umar died in 1864.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en} Ahmadu succeeded him at Ségou, but the empire never functioned as a coherent whole again: his brother Tidiani Tall ran a quasi-state at Bandiagara, Habibou held Dinguiraye and Mokhtar Kaarta, and rebellions followed — Habibou and Mokhtar in 1869, and Muntaga of Nioro in 1885, who blew up his own powder magazine rather than surrender.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en} He negotiated with France from strength that was ebbing: Gallieni's mission produced the Treaty of Nango in 1880, which Paris never ratified, and the Treaty of Gory of 12 May 1887 made the empire a nominal French protectorate, a delaying tactic both sides understood as such.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en} Colonel Archinard's offensive took Koundian in February 1889 and Ségou itself in 1890; Ahmadu's last stand at Youri was destroyed by machine guns on 3 January 1891.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-fr} He moved east to Bandiagara, forcing his brother Mounirou to yield the city; the French took Djenne and Mopti and then Bandiagara itself on 29 April 1893, installing his son Aguibou as puppet sultan of Massina on 4 May.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en} Ahmadu refused Archinard's offer of a comfortable retirement and fled east; his ally Alboury Ndiaye of Jolof was killed fighting for him, and he settled at Maykouki near Sokoto, where he died on 15 December 1897.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-fr} The French article dates his rule 1864–1890, the English article's titles 1864–1893 — a framing difference recorded in his record.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-fr}{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en}

## Positions and criticism

This section reports what the sources attribute to Ahmadu Tall and how they assess him; it is not the museum's own judgement.

The English article's legacy section holds both sides: he resisted colonial encroachment into West Africa for some thirty years and kept the Toucouleur Empire a significant regional power, "however" he and his father "were themselves imperialists imposing the rule of a Toucouleur minority on an often unwilling local population".{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en} The same article records that he is remembered fondly by the Bambara of Ségou, who had every reason to resent a conqueror, and cites the Diafunu traditions describing him as "very calm, sober, pensive, and ascetic".{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en} On his religion the sources are silent about the man himself: his father's Tijani sheikhdom belongs to his father's record, and no fetched source attests an affiliation of Ahmadu's own, so the museum records his faith as unknown, as his record's faith note sets out.{cite:source/wikipedia-ahmadu-tall-en}{cite:source/wikidata-ahmadu-tall}
