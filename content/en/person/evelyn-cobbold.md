Evelyn Cobbold (17 July 1867 – 25 January 1963), born Evelyn Murray in Edinburgh, eldest daughter of the 7th Earl of Dunmore, spent her childhood winters in Algiers and Cairo among Muslim nurses and grew up fluent in Arabic, feeling, as she later wrote, "a little Moslem at heart".{cite:wikipedia-evelyn-cobbold-en}{cite:facey-taylor-cobbold-introduction}

She affirmed her Islam by 1915 and took the name Zainab, calling Islam "the religion of common sense"; no writing of hers names a school, and her biographers judge only that she would certainly have claimed to be Sunni, while adding that she cannot be pinned down more precisely.{cite:wikipedia-evelyn-cobbold-en}{cite:facey-taylor-cobbold-introduction} In 1933, at sixty-five, she became the first British-born Muslim woman to perform the Hajj, travelling to Mecca with King Abd al-Aziz's permission and recording the journey in Pilgrimage to Mecca (1934), the first Hajj account by an Englishwoman.{cite:wikipedia-evelyn-cobbold-en}

She died at Inverness in 1963 and was buried, as she had stipulated, on a hillside of her Glencarron estate facing Mecca, the imam of the Shah Jahan Mosque at Woking travelling up through snow to perform her funeral prayer.{cite:wikipedia-evelyn-cobbold-en}
