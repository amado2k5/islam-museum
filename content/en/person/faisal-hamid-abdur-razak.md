## Summary
Shaykh Faisal Hamid Abdur-Razak is a Canadian Muslim scholar, preacher and author, best known as the founder and longtime head of the Islamic Forum of Canada in Brampton, Ontario. He has served on the Government of Canada's Interfaith Committee, led interfaith delegations to the Vatican, and his organisation broadcasts religious teaching around the clock through a continuous livestream service. {cite:themuslim500-faisal-hamid-abdur-razak}

## Background and formation
Public materials associate Shaykh Faisal with the traditional Sunni learning of the Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a current of the Indo-Pakistani diaspora, and with a devotional orientation that honours the Prophet's family (Ahl al-Bayt), a combination common in the Barelvi and allied scholarly networks of South Asian Canada. He settled in the Toronto area and built his community work around the western suburb of Brampton, which over the course of his career became home to one of the largest concentrations of South Asian Muslims in North America. {cite:themuslim500-faisal-hamid-abdur-razak}

## The Islamic Forum of Canada
The Islamic Forum of Canada, the organisation he founded and has led for decades, operates a mosque and community centre in Brampton and runs educational, youth and social programmes for the city's large Muslim population. Its media arm streams continuous religious programming — lectures, recitation and devotional content — which the Muslim 500 profile describes as a twenty-four-hour livestream broadcast reaching viewers well beyond the local congregation. {cite:themuslim500-faisal-hamid-abdur-razak}

## Interfaith work
Shaykh Faisal's public career has leaned heavily on interfaith activity. He served on the Government of Canada's Interfaith Committee, a consultative body that brought religious leaders into contact with federal officials, and he led Muslim-Christian delegations to the Vatican, meeting Vatican officials in the course of Catholic–Muslim dialogue initiatives. Within Canada he is a regular presence at multi-religious commemorations and civic ceremonies. {cite:themuslim500-faisal-hamid-abdur-razak}

## Context
The world in which Shaykh Faisal's institution grew is itself notable: Canada's Muslim population expanded rapidly through South Asian immigration from the 1970s onward, and the Greater Toronto Area became the centre of that growth. Organisations like the Islamic Forum of Canada supplied the prayer space, schooling, marriage and funeral services and public representation that the new communities required, while the federal and provincial governments built interfaith consultative structures to engage them. Shaykh Faisal's career — congregational leadership, plus service on a government interfaith committee and delegations to the Vatican — is a compact example of how that generation of immigrant religious leaders moved between mosque, media and state. {cite:themuslim500-faisal-hamid-abdur-razak}

Alongside the institutional work, his public profile rests on a consistent presence at state and civic occasions — consultations in Ottawa, commemorations in Ontario, and the Vatican visits — which together made him one of the more visible faces of Canada's South Asian Muslim community in its dealings with both government and church. {cite:themuslim500-faisal-hamid-abdur-razak}
