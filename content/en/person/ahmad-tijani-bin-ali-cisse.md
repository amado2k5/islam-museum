## Summary
Shaykh Ahmad Tijani bin Ali Cisse is a Senegalese Islamic scholar and the spiritual leader of the Tijaniyya, one of the largest Sufi orders in the world, with tens of millions of followers concentrated in West Africa and a wide diaspora. He serves as imam of the Grand Mosque of Medina Baye in Kaolack, Senegal. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-tijani-cisse}

## Life and family
He was born in 1955 in Kaolack into the Cisse family, a line of Tijani scholarship. The seat at Medina Baye descends through the circle of Shaykh Ibrahim Niass (1900–1975), the Kaolack shaykh whose preaching spread the Tijaniyya across West Africa and beyond; Cisse's father, Shaykh Ali Cisse, was a close disciple of Niass. Ahmad Tijani studied the religious sciences in Senegal and continued his education in Egypt, where he studied at Al-Azhar University. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-tijani-cisse}

## Leadership
In 2010, after the death of Shaykh Hassan Cisse, Ahmad Tijani Cisse assumed the general leadership of the Tijaniyya from Medina Baye. The Kaolack branch of the order draws disciples from Senegal, Nigeria, Mauritania, Ghana and far beyond, and the annual gatherings he presides over are among the largest religious events in West Africa. His influence is spiritual rather than political: he leads prayers, teaching sessions and the order's litanies, and receives visiting delegations of scholars and officials. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-tijani-cisse}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 has listed him among the world's most influential Muslims for many years, ranking the Tijaniyya's head among the leading spiritual guides of the Muslim world. The museum presents the order's internal history — the Niass succession and the place of the Cisse family — as it is described by the order's own tradition and reference works, without adjudicating between the order's branches. {cite:muslim500-ahmad-tijani-cisse}
