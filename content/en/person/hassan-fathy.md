## Summary
Egyptian architect (1900–1989), pioneer of mud-brick architecture and appropriate technology in the twentieth century. He designed New Gourna village to house farmers with local materials and traditional building, authored Architecture for the Poor — a world reference in sustainable architecture — and won the Aga Khan Award for Architecture in 1980. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-fathy}

## Life and formation

Hassan Fathy was born in Alexandria in 1900 and graduated in architecture from the Higher School of Engineering (later Cairo University) in 1926, working in design and teaching before turning to his great project: housing the poor with architecture that respects environment and local culture. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-fathy}

His early buildings were inspired by the Egyptian countryside and the Nubian traditions of mud-brick and vault building; he combined his academic training with the craft of local masons and taught at the Faculty of Fine Arts in Cairo. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-fathy}

## Works and project

His best-known project is New Gourna village near Luxor, designed in the 1940s to rehouse the people of old Gourna in mud-brick homes with vaults and ventilation openings, built with the residents' training and participation; he later built in Egypt and abroad, including the Dar al-Islam mosque in New Mexico. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-fathy}

He published his experience in Architecture for the Poor (1969), translated into many languages and now a founding text of sustainable architecture and 'appropriate technology', a concept he championed against the blind import of industrial models. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-fathy}

## Legacy

In his lifetime his ideas met limited acceptance in Egypt itself and New Gourna remained unfinished, but the architectural world turned to him from the 1970s: he received the Aga Khan Award for Architecture in 1980 and the Right Livelihood Award. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-fathy}

He died in Cairo in 1989; today his work is read in sustainable-architecture curricula worldwide, and Gourna is studied as a model of building that balances climate, society and local economy. {cite:wikipedia-hassan-fathy}
