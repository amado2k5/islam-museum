## Summary
Iskandar Muda (c. 1583 – 27 December 1636) was the twelfth sultan of Aceh, under whom the sultanate achieved its greatest territorial extent and held sway as the strongest power and the wealthiest state in the western Indonesian archipelago and the Straits of Melaka. “Iskandar Muda” means “young Alexander”, and his conquests were often compared to those of Alexander the Great. {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-en}

## Early life
He was born at Banda Aceh in about 1583 — the English article derives the year from his being 32 (Muslim) years old in 1613, while the Indonesian article shows modern works variously using 1583, 1588, 1590, 1591 and 1593. His father was Mansur Syah, son of Sultan Abdul Jalil, son of the third sultan Alauddin al-Qahhar; his mother Puteri Raja Inderabangsa was a daughter of the tenth sultan, Alauddin Ri'ayat Syah Sayyid al-Mukammal, so he united the dynasty's two branches. Known in youth as Perkasa Alam, he fell out with his uncle Sultan Ali Ri'ayat Syah III about 1605, was imprisoned, then released when the Portuguese invaded in 1606 and distinguished himself in the fighting. When Sultan Ali died suddenly on 4 April 1607, Perkasa Alam secured the throne the same day. {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-en} {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-id}

## Conquests
His strength rested on a navy of heavy galleys, cavalry on Persian horses, an elephant corps, conscript infantry and more than 2,000 cannon. He conquered Deli (1612), Aru and Johor (1613), defeated a Portuguese fleet at Bintan (1614), took Pahang (1617), Kedah (1619) and Perak (1620), and sacked Johor again in 1623. In 1629 he sent several hundred ships against Melaka in the sultanate's greatest effort; the mission was a devastating failure — Portuguese sources claim all his ships were destroyed with 19,000 men — and afterwards he launched only two more expeditions, in 1630/1 and 1634, to suppress revolts in Pahang and firmly establish Islam there. Aceh kept northern Sumatra but never won supremacy in the Straits. {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-en}

## State, economy and court
The pepper trade was the sultanate's economic foundation, and Iskandar Muda was a centralising ruler: he monopolised foreign trade through the court, broke the independence of the orang kaya elite, created a new nobility of war leaders holding districts in feudal tenure, replaced princes with reporting royal officials, kept a palace guard of 3,000 women, and passed legal reforms creating a network of courts using Islamic jurisprudence — a system that became a model for other Islamic states in Indonesia. His reign was also marked by notorious severity, including mutilating punishments reported by a French visitor of the 1620s and the execution of his own crown prince, Meurah Pupok, after which he named his son-in-law Iskandar Thani as successor. Aceh became a centre of Islamic scholarship: he favoured the Sufi tradition of Hamzah Pansuri and Syamsuddin of Pasai, who resided at his court, and the chronicle Hikayat Aceh, probably written in his reign, celebrated his youth. {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-en} {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-id}

## Death and legacy
Iskandar Muda died at Banda Aceh on 27 December 1636; the Indonesian article pairs the date with 29 Rajab 1046 AH in the royal chronology. He was the last sultan of Aceh who was a direct male-line descendant of the founder Ali Mughayat Syah, and with him the founding House of Meukuta Alam died out. Among the Acehnese he is revered as a hero and symbol of Aceh's past greatness, remembered by the posthumous title Po Teuh Meureuhom. {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-en} {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-id} {cite:wikidata-iskandar-muda}

## Religion
Two infoboxes name his branch — the English article gives his religion as Sunni and the Indonesian as Sunni Islam — while Wikidata's religion value is simply Islam, and no source read for this record names a legal school for him. What the sources document is a reign of conspicuous Muslim kingship: courts applying Islamic jurisprudence, a court that drew eminent Islamic scholars to Aceh, and final campaigns undertaken in part to establish Islam in Pahang. {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-en} {cite:wikipedia-iskandar-muda-id} {cite:wikidata-iskandar-muda}
