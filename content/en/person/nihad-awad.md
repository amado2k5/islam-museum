## Summary
Nihad Awad is the co-founder and National Executive Director of the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR), the largest Muslim civil-rights and advocacy organisation in the United States. A Palestinian refugee by birth, he has led CAIR since its founding in 1994, making him among the longest-serving leaders of any American Muslim institution. {cite:wikipedia-nihad-awad}

## Formation
Born in 1960 in a refugee camp near Amman, Jordan, Awad studied in Italy and then at the University of Minnesota, where he trained as a civil engineer. He turned to advocacy work in the early 1990s, serving briefly as public-relations director of a Muslim charitable body before co-founding CAIR. {cite:wikipedia-nihad-awad}

## CAIR
Founded in Washington in 1994, CAIR grew under Awad's direction into a national organisation with dozens of chapters, handling employment-discrimination and hate-crime cases, publishing annual reports on anti-Muslim incidents, lobbying on civil-liberties questions and organising Muslim voter engagement. After 2001 the organisation's casework expanded sharply as American Muslims faced new scrutiny. {cite:wikipedia-nihad-awad}

## Standing and controversy
Supporters describe Awad as the builder of the institution that made Muslim civil rights a permanent subject in American public life. CAIR has also attracted sustained political controversy — critics have questioned its origins and associations, accusations the organisation denies — and Awad's own statements on Middle East politics have periodically drawn criticism. The museum records the dispute attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-nihad-awad}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles him as the most durable institutional leader in American Muslim public life — a figure whose organisation set the template for Muslim civil-rights advocacy in the United States. {cite:muslim500-nihad-awad}
