## Summary
Zubayr ibn al-Awwam ibn Khuwaylid al-Asadi, one of the first to embrace Islam. His mother was [[person/safiyya-bint-abd-al-muttalib|Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib]], the Prophet's aunt {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}.

## His role at Badr
He was one of the two horsemen of the Muslim army; the Muslims had only two horses. He was given command of the right wing {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.205}. He went out on a reconnaissance patrol with [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]]. They captured two boys who were water-carriers for Quraysh. From the camels slaughtered, the Prophet estimated the strength of the army as between nine hundred and a thousand {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.210-211}.

## After Badr
In the Sunni narration he is one of the ten given the glad tidings of Paradise. He was killed in 36 AH after leaving the Battle of the Camel {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad}.

## In the secret call (The Sealed Nectar, p.76)
The book counts him among those who embraced Islam through the call of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] during the years of the [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|secret call]]. He was one of the eight who came before the people and formed the first rank and vanguard of Islam {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. The book names him with his Asadi lineage {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## For Sprouts
Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam was one of the earliest Muslims; his mother Safiyya was the Prophet's aunt. At Badr he was one of the army's only two horsemen, and Sunnis count him among the ten promised Paradise. He died in 36 AH after withdrawing from the Battle of the Camel.

## For Explorers
Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam ibn Khuwaylid al-Asadi was one of the first to enter Islam, called by Abu Bakr in the years of the secret call; his mother Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib was the Prophet's aunt. At Badr he was one of the Muslim army's two horsemen and held the right wing; he rode out on the scouting patrol with Ali and Sa'd that captured two camel-keepers of Quraysh. Sunnis count him among the ten promised Paradise, and al-Sallabi says he was the first to draw a sword for God's sake. His estate left lands and houses but no dinar or dirham. He died in 36 AH after withdrawing from the Battle of the Camel.

## For Scholars
Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam ibn Khuwaylid al-Asadi was of the first vanguard — one of the eight who preceded the people into Islam through Abu Bakr's call — and his mother Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib was the Prophet's aunt. At Badr he was one of the army's two horsemen, commanding the right wing, and rode on the scouting patrol with Ali and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas that captured two of Quraysh's camel-keepers, giving the estimate of the enemy army. Al-Sallabi makes him the first to draw his sword for God's sake, and the Prophet's joining of both parents to him at the Trench is recorded; his estate held lands and properties — al-Ghaba, eleven houses in Medina, and houses in Basra, Kufa and Egypt — but no dinar or dirham, with a rebuttal of Will Durant's claim of ten thousand slaves. He died in 36 AH after withdrawing from the Camel battle; al-Sallabi names Amr ibn Jurmuz in Wadi al-Siba'.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
In his explanation of the hadith of the cave, [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] tells the story of al-Zubayr's debt and says al-Bukhari alone of the Six Books narrates it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:10:00} He says al-Zubayr was the Prophet's disciple (hawari); that on the day of the Camel he told his son [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abd Allah]] that none would be killed that day but a wrongdoer or one wronged, that he expected to be killed wronged, that his greatest worry was his debt, and that if Abd Allah could not pay it he should seek the help of his Master, meaning God. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:11:00} He says al-Zubayr held no governorship and collected no land tax, that his property was eleven houses in Medina, two in Basra, one in Kufa and one in Egypt, with land at al-Ghaba, and that his debt came to two million two hundred thousand. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:12:30}

Al-Heweny explains the debt: people would bring him their money as deposits, and he would say he could not guarantee it and make it a loan to him instead, to protect his honour if it were lost. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:13:00} He relates that Hakim ibn Hizam asked about the debt and Abd Allah hid its size, and then offered help once he knew; that Abd Allah called out at four Hajj seasons that anyone owed money by al-Zubayr should come to him; and that he then divided what remained, each of al-Zubayr's four wives receiving one million one hundred thousand; al-Bukhari placed it under the chapter "the blessing in the warrior's wealth, alive and dead". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:17:30} In the lesson on the migration to Abyssinia he narrates from Umm Salama's hadith that al-Zubayr, one of the youngest of them, crossed the Nile on an inflated waterskin to watch the Negus's battle with the man who contested his throne and bring them news. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:17:00}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the twenty-eighth episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] introduces al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam as the son of the Prophet's paternal aunt and his disciple (hawari), who fought in every campaign and took part in the conquests in the days of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] and [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]], and one of the early converts, emigrants and veterans of Badr. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:01:00} He says al-Zubayr fought at [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|al-Yarmuk]] and in the [[event/conquest-of-egypt|conquest of Egypt]], and tells his audience that every Egyptian owes him a debt, because Islam reached the people of Egypt through him and those with him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:01:30} He lists the ties between him and the Prophet: he was the son of the Prophet's aunt; his father was the brother of [[person/khadija|Khadija]], the Prophet's wife; they shared the brotherhood of faith; and he grew up an orphan, as the Prophet had. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:02:00} He says his maternal grandfather was [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]], lord of Mecca, his father al-Awwam ibn Khuwaylid a rich merchant of Banu Asad, and his mother [[person/safiyya-bint-abd-al-muttalib|Safiyya]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:02:30}

He relates that after his father died his mother did not remarry and raised him to horsemanship and the making of arrows, beating him when he idled as children do; one of his uncles told her she beat him like someone who hated him, and she answered in verse that she beat him so that he would grow up sharp, rout armies and bring back the spoils. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:02:30} He counts him the fifth person to accept Islam. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:04:00} He relates that one of his uncles used to roll him in a mat and smoke him until he nearly choked, ordering him to renounce Islam, and that he refused, saying there was no going back to unbelief after faith. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:04:30} He says al-Zubayr emigrated to [[place/abyssinia|Abyssinia]] twice, and that he became Muslim at fifteen and emigrated at eighteen. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:04:30} He relates from [[person/ibn-hisham|Ibn Hisham]] in the Sira, in the report of [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama]], that war broke out between [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]] and his enemies on the far bank of the Nile and the Muslims were anxious; al-Zubayr, the youngest of them, volunteered, they blew up a waterskin for him, he put it under his chest and swam the Nile on it, witnessed the battle and came back with the good news of the Negus's victory. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:05:30}

He relates from [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]], through [[person/urwa-ibn-al-zubayr|Urwa]], that al-Zubayr bore three sword wounds on his shoulder, two from Badr and one from al-Yarmuk, into which Urwa used to put his fingers, and from [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]], through Ali ibn Zayd, that his chest bore scars like eyes from the many spear thrusts and arrows. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:07:00} He relates from al-Hakim in [[work/al-mustadrak-ala-al-sahihayn|al-Mustadrak]] that when it was rumoured that the Prophet had been killed or seized, al-Zubayr rushed out with his sword; the Prophet met him and asked him what he was doing, and he said he would have struck whoever had taken him, and the Prophet prayed for him and for his sword. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:07:30} He relates that al-Zubayr said that [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha]] named his sons after prophets though he knew there would be no prophet after Muhammad, whereas he named his after martyrs in the hope that they would be granted martyrdom: Abd Allah after [[person/abdullah-ibn-jahsh|Abd Allah ibn Jahsh]], al-Mundhir after [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-amr-companion|al-Mundhir ibn Amr]], Urwa after [[person/urwa-ibn-masud|Urwa ibn Mas'ud]], Hamza after [[person/hamza|Hamza]], Ja'far after [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far]], Mus'ab after [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]], Ubayda after [[person/ubayda-ibn-al-harith|Ubayda ibn al-Harith]], Khalid after [[person/khalid-ibn-said-ibn-al-as|Khalid ibn Sa'id]] and Amr after [[person/amr-ibn-said-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn Sa'id ibn al-As]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:08:00} He cites [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] as saying that the bravest of people was al-Zubayr, and [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|al-Thawri]] as saying that the champions of the Companions were Hamza, Ali and al-Zubayr. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:09:00} He relates that al-Zubayr fought a man in single combat and split him in half with his sword; people marvelled at the sword, and he told them to marvel rather at the hand that held it, from which the speaker draws the lesson that a weapon does not fight on its own. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:09:30}

He says that at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] there were no horsemen in the Muslim army but al-Zubayr and [[person/al-miqdad-ibn-amr|al-Miqdad]], and that al-Zubayr wore a yellow turban that day and Gabriel came down in his likeness wearing a yellow turban; that the Muslims had only two horsemen at Badr is also given in this record from The Sealed Nectar. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:10:00} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.205} He relates from al-Bukhari, in al-Zubayr's own report, that at Badr he met Ubayda ibn Sa'id ibn al-As, armoured so that only his eyes could be seen, and speared him in the eye, killing him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:10:30} He says that al-Zubayr killed his uncle Nawfal ibn Khuwaylid that day, who had come out as an unbeliever intent on killing the Prophet and his Companions, and he cites the hadith that makes love for God's sake and hatred for God's sake the firmest bond of faith. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:10:30} He relates from [[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]], in his Tahdhib, that at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] the Prophet saw a man cutting down the Muslims and told al-Zubayr to go after him; al-Zubayr leapt onto the man's horse, brought him down, and the two rolled on the ground until al-Zubayr stabbed him in the chest and the Muslims cried "God is greatest". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:12:00} He relates that at the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Trench]], when news of Banu Qurayza's betrayal reached the Prophet, he asked three times who would bring him word of them, and each time al-Zubayr said "I" and no one else did; the Prophet said that every prophet has a disciple and that his disciple is al-Zubayr, and the speaker explains hawari as a sincere friend and helper. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:12:30}

He relates that at al-Yarmuk al-Zubayr fought under [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid]] against the Byzantines with his son [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abd Allah]] behind him on his horse, teaching him horsemanship. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:13:00} He reports from [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] that a band of champions asked to charge the Byzantines with him; he told them they would not hold firm, and when they charged they held back while he went on, broke through the Byzantine ranks and came out on the other side, and that this happened twice. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:13:30} For the conquest of Egypt he relates that [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] set out for it with three thousand five hundred men, met great Byzantine forces and asked for reinforcements, and Umar sent four thousand under four commanders, each worth a thousand men: al-Zubayr, [[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]], Maslama ibn Mukhallad and Kharija ibn Hudhafa. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:14:30} He relates that the siege of the [[event/siege-of-babylon-fortress-640|Babylon fortress]] lasted seven months, that al-Zubayr declared he had given himself to God, a ladder was made for him, and he climbed it, stormed the fortress and fought at its gate; the Muslims cried "God is greatest", the Byzantines fled, and the fortress fell at his hands. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:15:00}

He says al-Zubayr married four women, the best known of them [[person/asma-bint-abi-bakr|Asma' bint Abi Bakr]] and [[person/atika-bint-zayd|Atika bint Zayd]], the sister of [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Sa'id ibn Zayd]], and that he was intensely jealous. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:16:00} He relates from Asma' that when he married her he had no property and no servant apart from his horse; she fed the horse, ground date stones for his water-camel and carried them on her head from land the Prophet had granted him two-thirds of a farsakh away, about five kilometres by the speaker's estimate. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:16:00} One day, he relates, she met the Prophet with the date stones on her head and he made his camel kneel to carry her behind him, but she felt shy, remembering al-Zubayr's jealousy, and the Prophet went on; when she told al-Zubayr, he said that her carrying the date stones weighed on him more than her riding with the Prophet would have, and later Abu Bakr sent her a servant, and it was as if he had set her free. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:17:00} The lesson he draws is that Asma' respected her husband's feelings even though the Prophet was close to her family, and he praises the kind words al-Zubayr gave her. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:18:00}

He relates from Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr that his father charged him to pay off his debt and to seek the help of his Master in it; when Abd Allah asked who his Master was, he said God, and whenever Abd Allah ran into difficulty over the debt he called on al-Zubayr's Master and it was paid. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:22:30} He explains that the debt came from deposits people left with al-Zubayr, which he took as loans rather than trusts for fear that they might be lost, and that he left no dinar or dirham, only two plots of land; that he left neither dinar nor dirham is also given in this record from al-Bukhari as al-Sallabi cites him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:23:00} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 88} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 90} He relates that his son refused to divide the inheritance until the debt was paid, announced at the pilgrimage season for four years that anyone with a claim on al-Zubayr should come forward, and then distributed what remained, which the speaker counts as a blessing that came from al-Zubayr's seeking the help of his Master. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep28 00:23:30}
