## Summary
Oumou Sangaré (born 1968 at Bamako) is a Malian singer of the Wassoulou region south of the Niger River, called "The Songbird of Wassoulou", of Fulani descent and the daughter of the singer Aminata Diakité. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-ar} She sang in the streets as a child to support her family after her father left, won an inter-kindergarten singing competition at five, and her first album Moussolou (1990) made her a star across West Africa; she is an FAO goodwill ambassador, a 2011 Grammy winner, and her songs carry social criticism, above all on the status of women. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en}

## Career
She was born at Bamako; the English and Arabic articles give her birth as 25 February 1968, the French article gives 2 February 1968, and Wikidata carries two claims, 12 and 25 February — this record follows 25 February and records the dispute. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-ar} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr} {cite:wikidata-oumou-sangare} Her father was Sidiki Sangaré and her mother the singer Aminata Diakité, both from the Wassoulou region; in 1970 her father took a second wife and moved to Abidjan, leaving the family in Bamako, and Oumou left school early and sang in the streets to help her mother. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr}

In 1973, aged five, she won an inter-kindergarten singing competition in Bamako and performed before several thousand people at the Omnisport stadium — the French article gives the audience as 3,000 and recalls that she had to stand on a table at the centre of the stage to reach the microphone — and at sixteen she toured with the percussion group Djoliba in France, Germany, the Netherlands, the Caribbean and elsewhere, then returned to Bamako and formed her own group. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr}

## Works
She recorded her first album, Moussolou ("Women"), for the Malian producer Abdoulaye Samassa with the maestro Amadou Ba Guindo; it sold more than 200,000 copies in Africa by the English article's account — the French article gives more than 100,000 cassettes sold in Mali within a week — and with Ali Farka Touré's help she signed with the English label World Circuit, which re-released it; at twenty-one she was already a star. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr} Her later albums include Ko Sira (1993), Worotan (1996), Seya (2009), nominated for a Grammy, Mogoya (2017) and Timbuktu (2022). {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en}

Since 1990 she has performed at major world venues; she contributed vocals to "Imagine" on Herbie Hancock's The Imagine Project (2010), which won her the 2011 Grammy for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals; Beyoncé sampled her song "Diaraby Nene" in "Mood 4 Eva" for The Lion King (2019); and in 2022 she took her first acting role, in Maïmouna Doucouré's film Hawa. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr} Her honours include the IMC-UNESCO International Music Prize (2001), Commander of the French Ordre des Arts et des Lettres (1998), appointment as FAO goodwill ambassador on 16 October 2003, the WOMEX Artist Award (2017), the Aga Khan Music Award (2019) and the Rolf Schock Prize (2024). {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr}

## Positions and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**Women's rights and business.** The sources describe her as an advocate for women's rights around the world, opposed to child marriage and polygamy — the French article adds excision — whose songs include social criticism especially of women's low status, many of them about love, marriage and freedom of choice in marriage. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-ar} She is also a businesswoman: she owns the Hotel Wassoulou in Bamako — thirty rooms by the English article, thirty-seven by the French — and has invested in agriculture and automobiles, launching the "Oum Sang" car; she has said she helped build the hotel herself to show women that work can make their lives better, and that she does not want to be a politician because an artist is free to say what she thinks. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr}

**Her faith.** None of the three articles, and not Wikidata either, states a religion, legal school or order for Oumou Sangaré; her Fulani descent, her Wassoulou upbringing and the traditional repertoire she sings are background and work, not an attestation of a personal school, so the museum records her faith as unknown. {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-en} {cite:wikipedia-oumou-sangare-fr} {cite:wikidata-oumou-sangare}
