## Summary
Shibl al-Madri was watched over with gentleness and made whole. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 531, sequence 532, on pages 161 and 162. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/161} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/162}

## The two kites and the meat
Abu Musa al-Tawil al-Basri relates that Shibl al-Madri craved meat, and when he took it to cook, a kite swooped down and snatched it from him. He vowed a fast and returned to the mosque. Another kite came and fought the first for the prey; a piece fell and landed in the lap of Shibl's wife beside his house, and she cooked it. When Shibl came home to break his fast, she brought him the meat, and he asked where it came from. She told him about the two kites and their struggle. Shibl wept and said: praise God, who has not forgotten Shibl, though Shibl forgets Him. A manuscript variant in the margin gives the nisba as al-Rawzi; it is noted and not preferred here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/161} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/162}
