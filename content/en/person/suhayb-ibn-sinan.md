Suhayb ibn Sinan, known as Suhayb al-Rumi, was among the earliest Muslims persecuted at Mecca; it is related that when [[event/islam-of-umar|Umar accepted Islam]] the Muslims came out openly, sat in circles around the House, circumambulated it, and repaid some of the harshness they had suffered. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## In al-Isaba
Ibn Hajar gives him his own entry in al-Isaba, no. 4175: "Suhayb ibn Sinan ibn Malik — it is also said Khalid ibn Abd Amr ibn Uqayl, and it is said Tufayl ibn Amir ibn Jandala ibn Sa'd ibn Khuzayma ibn Ka'b ibn Sa'd ibn Aslam ibn Aws ibn Zayd Manat ibn al-Namir ibn Qasit, al-Namiri, Abu Yahya — and his mother was of Banu Malik ibn Amr ibn Tamim; he was called al-Rumi ('the Byzantine') because the Byzantines had taken him captive as a small child". {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.705}

## His Islam and emigration
Ibn Sa'd records that he and Ammar accepted Islam in the house of al-Arqam, that he was among the downtrodden who were tortured for God, and that he emigrated to Medina with Ali ibn Abi Talib among the last of that year's emigrants, arriving in mid-Rabi al-Awwal; he witnessed Badr and the campaigns after it. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.705}

## The occasion of "and of the people is he who sells himself"
Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Abi Khaythama relate from Hammad from Ali ibn Zayd from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab that when he emigrated a band of polytheists followed him, and he said: "Men of Quraysh, I am among your best archers, and you will not reach me until I have shot every arrow I have and then struck you with my sword; if you want my property, I will show you where it is." They made a pact, he directed them, they took his property and turned back; when he reached the Prophet he said to him, "The sale profited," and God sent down "and of the people is he who sells himself seeking God's pleasure" (2:207). Ibn Sa'd also transmits it by another route from Abu Uthman al-Nahdi. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.705}

## His character and death
Al-Humaydi and al-Tabarani transmit through his own family that he said: "The Prophet never witnessed an occasion but I was present at it, never gave a pledge but I was present at it … and I never put him between me and the enemy until he died." {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.705}

When Umar died he instructed that Suhayb pray over him and lead the people in prayer until the Muslims agreed on an imam, as al-Bukhari records in his Tarikh. Suhayb died in year thirty-eight; it was also said year nine. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.706}
