## Overview
Buraydah ibn al-Husayb ibn 'Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn al-A'raj ibn Sa'd ibn Razah ibn 'Adi ibn Sahm ibn Mazin ibn al-Harith ibn Salaman ibn [[group/aslam|Aslam]] ibn Afsa ibn Haritha ibn 'Amr ibn 'Amir. His kunya is given variously as Abu 'Abd Allah, Abu Sahl, Abu al-Husayb and Abu Sasan; Abu 'Abd Allah is the best known. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94}

## Conversion
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says he became Muslim before Badr but was not present at it: when the Prophet, emigrating from Mecca to Medina, reached al-Ghamim, Buraydah came to him and accepted Islam with those who were with him, some eighty households; the Prophet led the evening prayer and they prayed behind him, and Buraydah went back to his people's land having learned some of the Qur'an that night. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94} Ibn Hajar likewise reports from Ibn al-Sakan that he became Muslim when the Prophet passed him at al-Ghamim on the Hijra and stayed where he was until Badr and Uhud had passed; it is also said he became Muslim after the Prophet's return from Badr. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 141}

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr also gives, with his chain, 'Abd Allah ibn Buraydah's report from his father that the Prophet took no bad omens but welcomed good ones, and that Buraydah rode out with seventy riders of his family of Banu Sahm and met the Prophet, who asked his name; on hearing "Buraydah" he said to Abu Bakr, "Our affair has cooled (barada) and come right"; on hearing "of Aslam", "We are safe (salimna)"; and on hearing "of Banu Sahm", "Your arrow (sahm) has come out." {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94} The editor notes that its chain is very weak, and that part of it is missing from the surviving copies and was supplied from *al-Tamhid*. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94}

## Campaigns and residence
He came to the Prophet after Uhud and took part in his campaigns; he was at [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|al-Hudaybiya]] and was among those who took [[event/bayat-al-ridwan|the Pledge of Ridwan]] under the tree. He lived in [[place/medina|Medina]], then moved to [[place/basra|Basra]], and from there went out to [[place/khurasan|Khurasan]] on campaign. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94} *Al-Isaba* adds that, as both *Sahih*s report from him, he went on sixteen campaigns with the Prophet, that he settled in Basra when it was conquered, campaigned in Khurasan in 'Uthman's time and then settled in Merv. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 141} Al-Baladhuri lists him among those whom [[person/al-rabi-ibn-ziyad-al-harithi|al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad]] moved to Khurasan in 51 AH; see [[event/rabi-khurasan-transplant|al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad's fifty-thousand transplant]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## His name
Ibn Hajar reports from Abu 'Ali al-Tusi, the companion of Ibn al-Mubarak, that his name was 'Amir and Buraydah a nickname, and that Buraydah's hadiths are many and his merits well known. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 141}

## Death
He died at [[place/merv|Merv]] during the rule of [[person/yazid-ibn-muawiya|Yazid ibn Mu'awiya]], and his descendants remained there. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94} Ibn Sa'd gives the year as 63 AH. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 141} Al-Bukhari relates in his *Tarikh* from 'Abd Allah ibn Muslim al-Sulami of Merv that he heard 'Abd Allah ibn Buraydah say: "My father died at Merv and his grave is at al-Jussayn; he is the leader and light of the people of the East," citing the hadith "Any of my Companions who dies in a land will be their leader and light on the Day of Resurrection"; the editor judges its chain weak. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94}
