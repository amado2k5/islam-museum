## Summary
Mehmed Süreyya Bey was an Ottoman historian and official of the Ministry of Education, author of the [[work/sicill-i-osmani|Sicill-i Osmani]]. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 5} He was born in Istanbul in 1845; his father Mehmed Hüsnü Bey held various state posts, and his father's maternal ancestor was Ibrahim Pasha, killed at Belgrade around 1750. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 5} Most of what is known of his schooling and career comes from the *sicill-i umumi* registers in the Prime Ministry Archive, his record being in register no. 113. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 5}

## Education and career
He finished the Darülmaarif rüşdiye school and took private lessons in Arabic, Persian and French. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 5} {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 6} He began his first official post in Dhu al-Qa'da 1279 AH (April–May 1863) as a probationer in the translation office of the Sublime Porte, and in those years wrote for the newspaper *Ceride-i Havadis*. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 6} On 7 Muharram 1304 (6 October 1886) he was appointed a member of the Grand Council of Education (*Meclis-i Kebir-i Maarif*) in the Ministry of Education, where he received successive ranks. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 6}

## How he worked
Akbayar quotes the poet Halil Edib, his colleague at the ministry, that he had read almost every printed and manuscript work on history and biography in the libraries. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 8} He came to work covered in dust in summer and mud in winter from his rounds of the cemeteries reading gravestones, earning the nickname "Ragged Süreyya Bey"; summer and winter he wore an old overcoat with pockets full of notes, and he never married. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 8}

## His works
Only the Sicill-i Osmani and the *Nuhbetü'l-Vekayi* were printed; the latter lists appointments and deaths of state officials between 1247 and 1272 AH, and only part of it was printed. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 6} {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 7} Bursalı Mehmed Tahir Bey names unprinted works: a two-volume *Tekmile-i Sicill-i Osmani*, a *Zeylü'z-Zeyl*, a nine-volume history covering 1808–1876, a thirty-part dictionary of Arabic, Persian, Ottoman, Chagatai and Tatar, a four-volume *Mir'at-i Tarih-i Islam*, and four unpublished novels; his family is reported to have said that all of them burned in the Cihangir fire. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 7}

## His death
He fell ill in 1907 and died on 30 Kanunuevvel 1324 (Rumi calendar), that is 12 January 1909, and was buried in the Karacaahmet cemetery. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 6} Halil Edib mourned him in a long article, and an obituary in the *Nevsal-i Osmani* said that no one like him in biography had arisen since İsmet Efendi. {cite:sicill-i-osmani vol. 1 PDF p. 6}
