## Summary
Wan Azizah Wan Ismail is a Malaysian ophthalmologist who became a politician by circumstance and a symbol by endurance: the founder-president of the People's Justice Party (Keadilan), Malaysia's first female leader of the opposition, and the country's first female deputy prime minister, serving from 2018 to 2020. She is the wife of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. {cite:wikipedia-wan-azizah-wan-ismail}

## Life and medicine
She was born on 3 December 1952 in Singapore and trained in medicine at the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland, specialising in ophthalmology. She worked as a government eye surgeon for fourteen years, marrying Anwar Ibrahim in 1980 and raising six children, largely outside politics until the crisis of 1998. {cite:wikipedia-wan-azizah-wan-ismail}

## The Reformasi years
When Anwar Ibrahim was dismissed as deputy prime minister and imprisoned in 1998, Wan Azizah founded the Reformasi movement and then the National Justice Party (later PKR), winning the Permatang Pauh seat her husband had held. She kept it across the 1999 and 2004 elections — the only opposition MP returned in 2004 — and twice vacated the seat so that Anwar, once freed, could return to parliament through by-elections she ceded to him. {cite:wikipedia-wan-azizah-wan-ismail}

## Deputy prime minister
After the Pakatan Harapan victory of May 2018 she was sworn in as Malaysia's first female deputy prime minister, also taking the women and welfare portfolio, and held office until the coalition's collapse in February 2020. She had earlier served a term as menteri besar of Selangor (2014–2015), again standing in during her husband's legal battles. {cite:wikipedia-wan-azizah-wan-ismail}

## Assessments
Supporters describe her as the steady custodian who kept the reform movement alive through two decades of Anwar's trials, noted for a soft-spoken persistence rather than oratory. Critics argue her career has been defined by proxy politics — keeping seats warm — more than by independent policy.
