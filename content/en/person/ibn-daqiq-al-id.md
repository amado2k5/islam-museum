## Overview
The shaykh Taqi al-Din Muhammad ibn Ali, known as Ibn Daqiq al-Id, a Shafi'i scholar, who died in 702 AH. The conversion to the Gregorian calendar is derived. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 585}

He compiled *al-Ilmam*, which gathers the hadith texts relating to rulings, stripped of their chains of transmission, and then wrote a commentary on it that he called *al-Imam*. *Kashf al-Zunun* says it was said that no greater work of this kind had been written, but that he did not complete it. Al-Biqa'i says that he completed it, but that after his death only a little of it was found, and it is said that some envious person destroyed it. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 585}

He is credited with [[work/al-ilmam-fi-ahadith-al-ahkam|al-Ilmam fi Ahadith al-Ahkam]].

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Wahb, Ibn Daqiq al-Id: of a family from Manfalut, he grew up in Qus, was first a Maliki and then a Shafi'i, and was born at sea near Yanbu' in 625. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/91} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/92} His works include al-Ilmam, al-Imam and al-Iqtirah. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/92} He became chief judge of Egypt in Jumada al-Ula 695 and held the office until his death in Safar 702. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/94}

## In al-Dhahabi's Dhayl Tarikh al-Islam
[[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry for "the chief judge, shaykh al-Islam Taqi al-Din Abu al-Fath Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Wahb ibn Mati' ibn Abi al-Ta'a al-Qushayri, the Egyptian of Manfalut, a Maliki and then a Shafi'i, judge of the Egyptian lands", and says he was born in the desert of 'Aydhab in Sha'ban 625. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 24} The editor's footnote quotes Yaqut that 'Aydhab is a small town on the shore of the Red Sea and Ibn Kathir that he was born on the coast of Yanbu' in the Hijaz; this differs from Ibn Hajar's "at sea near Yanbu'". {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 24}

He studied fiqh under his father, the Maliki Majd al-Din Ibn Daqiq al-Id, and heard hadith from Ibn Rawwaj, Ibn al-Jummayzi, Sibt al-Silafi, Ibn al-Muqayyir, the hafiz al-Mundhiri, Ibn Abd al-Da'im, Abu al-Baqa' al-Nabulsi and others. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 24} Al-Dhahabi lists among his writings al-Ilmam and a commentary on al-Umda, and says he excelled in the principles and the branches of law and in telling sound reports from weak ones. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 24} He also names a collection of forty reports about what is related from the Lord of the worlds and a set of forty nine-link hadiths. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 25}

Al-Dhahabi describes him as quick-minded and sound in thought, constant in worship, thoroughly scrupulous, and sparing in speech about what did not concern him; he had studied under al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam, accepted the judgeship only after reluctance and hesitation, resigned from it more than once, and was treated with respect by the rulers. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 24} {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 25} He says he did not sleep at night but spent it in study, writing, worship and remembrance, that he was extremely anxious about water and ritual purity, and that he neither backbit anyone nor listened to backbiting. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 25} He also relates that in Egypt he announced, on the day itself, the defeat of the Tatars at Homs in 680. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 25}

Al-Dhahabi names those who narrated from him, among them the judges Alam al-Din al-Akhna'i and Ala' al-Din al-Qunawi and the hafizes al-Mizzi, al-Birzali, al-Ya'mari and al-Halabi, and says that Ibn Daqiq al-Id dictated a hadith to him and that he heard a part from him in his own wording. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 25} He died in Safar 702 aged seventy-seven; people mourned him and his bier was carried on heads, and Qutb al-Din al-Halabi and Fath al-Din al-Ya'mari compiled biographies of him. {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 25} {cite:dhayl-tarikh-islam-bawazir p. 26}
