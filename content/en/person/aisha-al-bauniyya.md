## Overview
A'isha bint Yusuf ibn Ahmad ibn Nasir al-Ba'uniyya, Umm Abd al-Wahhab: a poetess, woman of letters and jurist, named after Ba'un in the villages of Ajlun east of the Jordan; she was born in Damascus and died there in 922 AH (1516 CE). She studied language and letters, travelled to Egypt in 919 AH where she praised al-Maqarr al-Ashrafi in a qasida and returned, and visited Aleppo in the year at the end of which she died. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.240}

Her works include the printed Badi'iyya, which she herself expounded in a fine commentary; al-Fath al-Haqiqi min Manah al-Talaqqi, terminology she coined in the Sufi manner; al-Malamiḥ al-Sharifa fi al-Athar al-Latifa of Sufi allusions; Durr al-Gha'is fi Bahr al-Khasa'is, a ra'iyya ode; al-Isharat al-Khafiyya fi al-Manazil al-Aliyya, an urjuza on Sufism; Fayd al-Fadl; and al-Mawrud al-Ahna fi al-Mawlid al-Asna, printed as Mawlid al-Nabi of al-Ba'uniyya. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.240}

## In Kahhala's A'lam al-Nisa'
Kahhala presents her as A'isha bint Yusuf ibn Ahmad ibn Nasir al-Din al-Ba'uniyya of Damascus, an eminent scholar, woman of letters and major poet, versed in Sufism, who took the path under Isma'il al-Khwarazmi and then under his successor Muhyi al-Din Yahya al-Urmawi, and then went to Cairo, where by his account she gained a large share of learning and was licensed to give fatwas and to teach. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.200} Her works include *al-Fath al-Haqqi min Minah al-Talaqqi*, Sufi poems and mystical knowledge; *Durr al-Gha'is fi Bahr al-Mu'jizat wa-al-Khasa'is*, a badi'iyya ode rhyming in *ra'* with her own commentary; *al-Isharat al-Khafiyya fi al-Manazil al-Aliyya*, a rajaz poem abridging al-Harawi's *Manazil al-Sa'irin*; and another rajaz poem summarising al-Sakhawi's *al-Qawl al-Badi'* on invoking blessings on the Prophet. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.200} He quotes her saying she had memorised the whole Qur'an by the age of eight. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.200} She travelled to Cairo in 919 AH and lost some of her writings on the road; the head of the chancery Abu al-Thana' Mahmud al-Halabi honoured her and lodged her with his household, and she praised him in an ode; she also wrote a poem describing Damascus, and died there in 922 AH. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.201}
