## Summary
Ahmed III, the twenty-third Ottoman sultan, son of Mehmed IV, was born in 1084/1673 and reigned from 1115/1703. His reign divides into a first phase of political instability and wars and a second phase, the lâle devri, of taste for the arts and for European models.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.) describes Ahmed III as the twenty-third Ottoman sultan, son of Mehmed IV. He succeeded his brother Mustafa II on 10 Rabi' II 1115/21 August 1703, when Mustafa abdicated after a Janissary rising. The leaders of the rising were soon removed, and the sultan made Istanbul the habitual seat of the court again. Many people suspected of involvement were dismissed, banished or executed in the following years. He dismissed the 700 bostandis of the palace service and replaced them with devshirme conscripts, the last use of the devshirme, and later sharply reduced the Janissary establishment. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 290}

For the first half of a reign of twenty-seven years he lived in dread of "revolutionaries" (fitnecilar), and for three years he could not find a capable grand vizier until Corlulu 'Ali Pasha was appointed in Muharram 1118/May 1706. A palace camarilla, headed by the valide sultan, the kizlar agasi and the sultan's favourite, later Silahdar Damad 'Ali Pasha, shaped policy for the next eight or nine years. Ministers from outside the palace service were distrusted. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 290}

The Ottoman government was drawn into war with Russia through Charles XII of Sweden, who took refuge at Bender in 1709 after his defeat at Poltava. Under pressure from Charles and from France and Venice, war was declared on 20 November 1710. The Russian army was trapped on the Pruth and forced to make the treaty of the Pruth, by which Peter gave up Azov and the other fortresses. Charles was removed by force from Bender in 1714. The Ottoman war with Russia was renewed three times before the treaty of Adrianople of June 1713 settled peace for twenty-five years. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 290}
