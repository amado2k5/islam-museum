## Overview
Sasa'a ibn Najiya ibn Aqal al-Mujashi' al-Tamimi, of the [[group/banu-tamim|Banu Tamim]]. He was an orator in the pre-Islamic period, and the grandfather of the poet [[person/farazdaq|al-Farazdaq]] {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 349}.

## Some of his reports
According to Ibn al-Athir, he called on his people to fall upon the wealth of Wahraz and to plunder the passers-by through their lands on their way to Kisra {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 349}.

The sources report that he was known as "the preventer of burying alive" (mani' al-wa'ida): he bought a little girl whose father, of the Banu al-Hujaym, wanted to bury her alive, and then he saved some hundred girls in the same way. Al-Farazdaq composed verse in pride of this {cite:ansab-al-ashraf j.12 p. 61-62}.

The same report says that he went to the Prophet as a delegation and embraced Islam, and asked him whom he should treat with kindness. The Prophet answered: your mother, then your father, then your brother, then the nearest and then the next nearest {cite:ansab-al-ashraf j.12 p. 61-62}.
