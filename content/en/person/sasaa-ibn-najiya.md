## Overview
Sasa'a ibn Najiya ibn Aqal al-Mujashi' al-Tamimi, of the [[group/banu-tamim|Banu Tamim]]. He was an orator in the pre-Islamic period, and the grandfather of the poet [[person/farazdaq|al-Farazdaq]] {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 349}.

## Some of his reports
According to Ibn al-Athir, he called on his people to fall upon the wealth of Wahraz and to plunder the passers-by through their lands on their way to Kisra {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 349}.

The sources report that he was known as "the preventer of burying alive" (mani' al-wa'ida): he bought a little girl whose father, of the Banu al-Hujaym, wanted to bury her alive, and then he saved some hundred girls in the same way. Al-Farazdaq composed verse in pride of this {cite:ansab-al-ashraf j.12 p. 61-62}.

The same report says that he went to the Prophet as a delegation and embraced Islam, and asked him whom he should treat with kindness. The Prophet answered: your mother, then your father, then your brother, then the nearest and then the next nearest {cite:ansab-al-ashraf j.12 p. 61-62}.

## In al-Hakim's Mustadrak
[[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|Al-Hakim]] quotes Abu 'Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna for his lineage, Sa'sa'a ibn Najiya ibn 'Iqal ibn Muhammad ibn Sufyan ibn Mujashi' ibn Darim, grandfather of al-Farazdaq ibn Ghalib, and for his coming to the Prophet in a delegation. {cite:mustadrak-ilmiyya-ata-v3 p. 707}

He gives, with his chain through al-Tufayl ibn 'Umar al-Rib'i, Sa'sa'a's story in full. He came to the Prophet, accepted Islam and learned some verses of the Qur'an, then asked whether there was reward for deeds he had done before Islam. He told how two of his she-camels had strayed and he went looking for them until he reached two tents in open country. In one he found an old man; as they talked, a woman called from the other tent that she had given birth to a girl, and the man told her to bury it. Sa'sa'a said he was not buying the child's person but her life, that he not kill her, and bought her with his two camels, their young and his own camel. {cite:mustadrak-ilmiyya-ata-v3 p. 707}

He then reflected that this was a noble deed no Arab had done before him, and by the time Islam appeared he had saved three hundred and sixty girls from burial, buying each for two pregnant camels and a male camel. He asked the Prophet: "Is there reward for me in that?" The Prophet said: "Its reward is yours in full, since God has favoured you with Islam." The narrator cites [[person/farazdaq|al-Farazdaq]]'s line on his grandfather who stopped the women who buried girls and so saved the buried. Al-Dhahabi passes over it without comment in the *Talkhis*. {cite:mustadrak-ilmiyya-ata-v3 p. 707-708}

Another of his reports is that he asked the Prophet about surplus he set aside for misfortune and for travellers, and was told: "Your mother and your father, your sister and your brother, the nearest to you, then the next nearest." {cite:mustadrak-ilmiyya-ata-v3 p. 708}

The reports differ on the number of girls: three hundred and sixty here, about a hundred in al-Baladhuri, as above. {cite:mustadrak-ilmiyya-ata-v3 p. 707} {cite:ansab-al-ashraf j.12 p. 61-62}
