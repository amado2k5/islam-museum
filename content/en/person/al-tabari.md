## Overview
Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari al-Amuli, born in Amul in 224 AH and died in Baghdad in 310 AH. Ibn al-Nadim counts him among those skilled in the sciences of the Qur'an, grammar, poetry, language and jurisprudence. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.326-327}

## His learning
He took hadith from shaykhs including Muhammad ibn Humayd al-Razi, Abu Kurayb and Hannad ibn al-Sari. He read jurisprudence under Dawud ibn Ali, took the jurisprudence of al-Shafi'i from al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman in Egypt, and the jurisprudence of Malik from Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.326-327}

## His works
He had a jurisprudential school that he chose for himself. Among his books are "al-Tarikh", which he dictated up to 302 AH, "al-Tafsir", "al-Qira'at", "Tahdhib al-Athar", and "Ikhtilaf al-Fuqaha'". {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.326-327}

## His death and the dispute about it
Ibn al-Athir says that he was buried at night in his house, because the common people gathered and prevented his burial by day. He says that some Hanbalis were fanatical against him, and the cause was his book on the disagreements of the jurists, in which he did not mention Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and his saying that he was a hadith scholar and not a jurist. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.99-100}

Ibn Khuzayma, on reading his commentary, said: "I know of no one on the face of the earth more learned than Abu Ja'far." Al-Khatib said he was one of the imams of the scholars, and that rulings were referred to his word. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.99-100}

## In the 'Al-Mubdi'un' programme (part 1 of 2)
Tareq al-Suwaidan dedicates two episodes to al-Tabari, presenting him as Persian-born yet master of Arabic until he wrote the greatest of exegeses, living 224–310 AH in an age of active codification when the six books were gathered {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:10:00}.
He pauses on a wider observation: our history was written politically and militarily while civilizational history lies fragmented; political stability usually pairs with civilizational output though not always — and the great mosques like the Umayyad and Baghdad's served as universities through their corner circles {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:06:30}.
Of his life: he memorised the Qur'an at seven and began writing hadith at nine; his father, seeing his brilliance, freed him entirely from seeking livelihood {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:12:00}.
The surname Abu Ja'far was agreed by the historians though he never married and had no son — he took it in observance of the sunna of kunyah {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:09:30}.
His travels: beginning in Tabaristan's cities, then Rayy, then Baghdad — seeking Imam Ahmad, whom he missed by his death in 241 AH — he stayed taking hadith and the fiqh of every school, then Basra, Wasit and Kufa, and returned to Baghdad with accumulated questions, focusing on Qur'an and history, taking Shafi'i fiqh from al-Za'farani and teaching {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:18:30}.
Then to Syria for the Syrian readings; to Beirut, al-Awza'i's centre that many overlook; to Egypt — then on the Maliki school, so he studied under Abdallah ibn Wahb, Malik's direct student — and back to Egypt in 256 AH learning the readings of Hamza and Warsh and prosody {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:21:00}.
Returning to Tabaristan he could not settle, and came back to Baghdad for the rest of his life, teaching and writing — the rare creator who excelled in more than one field: exegesis, history and fiqh, with complete character, worship and balance {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep13 00:23:00}.
