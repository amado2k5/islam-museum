## Biography
Ibn al-Abbar, Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Ahmad al-Quda'i al-Andalusi al-Balansi, traditionist, littérateur, historian and poet; he belonged to a family originating from Onda, the Spanish patrimony of the Quda'a {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 705}.

He was born in Rabi' II 595 (February 1199) at Valencia, where he spent his youth studying under teachers he names in his Mu'jam; for more than twenty years he was the disciple of the most learned traditionist in Spain, Abu al-Rabi' ibn Salim, who persuaded him to complete the Sila of Ibn Bashkuwal — producing the Takmila li-Kitab al-Sila {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 705}. Ibn al-Imad calls him the katib-adib, one of the imams of hadith, who mastered the readings, devoted himself to the tradition, excelled in rhetoric, verse and prose, and possessed majesty and standing {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 303}.

After journeys in the Peninsula fi talab al-'ilm he acted as secretary to the Mu'minid governors of Valencia; when James I of Aragon besieged the city in Ramadan 635 he was sent with a deputation to the Hafsid sovereign Abu Zakariyya' of Ifriqiya, reciting before him on 4 Muharram his qasida rhyming in sin which painted a moving picture of his besieged city {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 705}; the sultan hastened to aid its people, loading fleets with money, provisions and clothing {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 464}. Ibn al-Abbar returned to Valencia but left days after its capture on 17 Safar 636 (28 September 1238), stopped at Bougie, then went to Tunis, where Abu Zakariyya' appointed him chief of his chancellery. Ordered to leave the authentication blank for the sahib al-'alama to fill in, he did not; he was dismissed and confined to his house, then pardoned and reinstated {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 705}.

## His works
Author of some fifteen works in about forty-five volumes, of which preserved: the Takmila li-Kitab al-Sila finished at Tunis; the Mu'jam fi ashab al-qadi al-imam Abi 'Ali al-Sadafi; al-Hulla al-siyara (biographies of poets); Tuhfat al-kadim on the poets of Spain; I'tab al-kuttab ("The secretaries' contentment"); and Durar al-simt fi khabar al-Sibt, on the family of the Prophet and the 'Alids {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 705-706}.

## His killing
When Abu Zakariyya' died, his successor al-Mustansir kept Ibn al-Abbar close, but his attitude so irritated the sovereign and his courtiers that he was ordered to be tortured; his writings were confiscated and a satire against the amir was found among them, enraging him further. He ordered Ibn al-Abbar killed by lance-thrusts on the morning of 20 Muharram 658 (6 January 1260), and the next day his corpse was burnt together with his books, poems and diplomas {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 705}. Ibn al-Imad records that the lord of Tunis had him killed unjustly on 20 Muharram at the age of sixty-three {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 303}.
