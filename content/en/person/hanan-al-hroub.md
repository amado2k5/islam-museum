## Summary

Hanan al-Hroub is a Palestinian primary-school teacher who in March 2016 became the second winner of the Global Teacher Prize, the Varkey Foundation's one-million-dollar award for an exceptional teacher. She was honoured for a method she built herself to teach children traumatised by violence in her own environment — the refugee camps and checkpoints of the occupied West Bank — summed up in her slogan 'No to Violence'. {cite:wikipedia-hanan-al-hroub} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 184}

## Life and formation

She was born in Bethlehem and grew up in the Dheisheh refugee camp nearby, where she saw violence at close quarters. She was studying English at university intending to become a translator when, in October at the outbreak of the Al-Aqsa Intifada, her children — nine-year-old twins and a six-year-old son — were shot at by Israeli soldiers while her husband was shot in his car at an army checkpoint. The trauma her children carried home turned her toward education. {cite:wikipedia-hanan-al-hroub}

## The method

Al-Hroub teaches Palestinian children, many of them in refugee camps, and reports that when she began, 'the violence that kids were seeing in the streets, they were carrying into the classroom.' Her response was a play-based pedagogy of her own design: games and activities aimed at resolving violence and tension, rebuilding trust and cooperation rather than punishing behaviour. Within months, she reports, her students' behaviour and attitude improved markedly. The approach is set out in her book We Play and Learn. {cite:wikipedia-hanan-al-hroub}

## The Global Teacher Prize

In March 2016, at the Varkey Foundation's Global Education and Skills Forum in Dubai, she was named the prize's second winner — the first had been the American teacher Nancie Atwell in 2015 — chosen from a field of thousands of nominated teachers worldwide. The award was marked by a video message from Pope Francis. She said her students were 'the true prize winners', and described her work's aim as enrichment rather than mere instruction. {cite:wikipedia-hanan-al-hroub}

## The controversy

Her win drew press attention to her husband's past: Omar al-Hroub had served ten years in an Israeli prison, convicted as an accomplice in a lethal 1980 attack in Hebron, and on release had endorsed the Oslo interim accord and the two-state solution, later serving as a deputy cabinet minister in the Palestinian Authority. The Varkey Foundation stated that 'the judging process examines the qualities and achievements of the candidates themselves only' and the prize stood. The museum records the controversy attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-hanan-al-hroub}

## Assessment

Al-Hroub's listing rests on the case she made to a global audience: that in a landscape of conflict the classroom can be engineered against violence, and that a teacher's method — not a programme or a ministry — can carry that engineering. {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 184} {cite:wikipedia-hanan-al-hroub}
