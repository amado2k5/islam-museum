## Summary
Abu al-Fadl Abbas ibn Isma'il al-Tamdi was known for worship and sweetness of character in a firm station, and for broad and useful knowledge. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 682, sequence 683, on pages 398 to 400. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/398} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/400}

## His grief at his lost khatmas
When he fell ill for several days and was found grieving, he said: this illness has left me weak and has cost me thirty khatmas of my monthly recitation. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/399}

## Ibn al-Mubarak's saying
Abd Allah ibn Kawthar al-Isbahani relates at Mecca from Abbas al-Tamdi, from Husayn ibn al-Faraj, from Ibn al-Mubarak: if virtue lies in the congregation, safety lies in solitude. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/399}

## A report of God's address to the child of Adam
Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Sufi relates from Abbas ibn Isma'il, from Makki ibn Ibrahim ibn Musa ibn Ubayda al-Rabadhi, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, that he read in the Torah, or in the scrolls of Abraham, an address from God to the child of Adam: God reminds him that He made him from clay, then a drop, a clot, a lump, bones, and clothed the bones with flesh; that He lightened the burden of the mother's womb, put mercy in the hearts of his parents, and fed him the fruits of summer and winter; and then calls him to pray and to seek forgiveness, for He is near and answers, and forgiving and merciful. The wording of the address is not given here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/399} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/400}
