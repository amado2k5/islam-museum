## Summary
Namrud in the commentators and chroniclers is the tyrant king of Babylon who disputed with Ibrahim over his Lord and cast him into the fire; the Qur'an does not name him — the commentators name him as the arguer of al-Baqara's story.

## In the texts
The Qur'an names only the arguer: «have you seen the one who disputed with Ibrahim over his Lord, because God had given him kingship?» — and for al-Tabari and the commentators this is Namrud ibn Canaan, king of Babylon, the first to wear a crown and to tyrannise in the earth {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997}.

## In the Sira and history
The chroniclers place him among Babylon's kings after the Flood: he built the Tower of Babylon reaching for heaven on one report; disputed with Ibrahim out of kingship and power and cast him into the fire which God saved him from; then God killed him with a gnat that entered his brain, on the famous account {cite:tarikh-al-tabari} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997}.

## For younger visitors
Namrud is Babylon's tyrant king; he argued with Ibrahim and threw him into the fire, and God saved him.

## Notes for researchers
The name 'Namrud' comes to the chroniclers through the Isra'iliyyat; the Qur'an does not name him, so attribute to him only what the tafsirs and reports carry.
