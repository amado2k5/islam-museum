## Overview
Tahir ibn Abd Allah ibn Tahir al-Tabari, Abu al-Tayyib, was a judge and one of the notable Shafi'is. Al-Zirikli dates his birth to 348 AH and his death to 450 AH, that is 960 and 1058 CE as printed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 222}. Al-Safadi describes him as the Shafi'i jurist, trustworthy and truthful, versed in the principles and the branches of law, a careful verifier of good character and sound in the school's positions {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 230}.

## Life
He was born in Amul in Tabaristan, settled in Baghdad and became judge of the Rub' al-Karkh quarter {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 222}. Al-Safadi says he studied law at Amul under al-Zajjaji, a pupil of Ibn al-Qass, read with Abu Sa'id al-Isma'ili and Abu al-Qasim ibn Kajj at Jurjan, then travelled to Nishapur, met Abu al-Hasan al-Masarjisi and studied under him for four years; he then came to Baghdad, attended the circle of Shaykh Abu Hamid al-Isfarayini and read with him, and Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi in turn read with al-Tabari {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 231}. Al-Safadi quotes Abu Ishaq as saying he had seen no one among those he had met more complete in independent reasoning, firmer in verification or better in analysis {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 231}. Al-Safadi adds that al-Tabari wrote a commentary on al-Muzani's Mukhtasar and on Ibn al-Haddad's Furu', and composed many books on principles, the school, disputed questions and dialectic; he made Baghdad his home and became judge of the Rub' al-Karkh after the death of Abu Abd Allah al-Saymari, holding the post until he died in Baghdad {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 231}. Al-Safadi also quotes al-Khatib as saying he attended him and took notes of law from him for years {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 230}.

## Death
Al-Safadi gives his birth at Amul in Tabaristan in 348 AH and his death in 450 AH at the age of one hundred and two, with his mind and understanding unimpaired, still giving legal opinions among the jurists and correcting their errors {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 231}. He reports that the judge Abu Bakr ibn Bakran al-Shami said to him in his old age that he had enjoyed his faculties, and he answered that it was only natural, since he had never disobeyed God with any one of them, or words to that effect {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 230}. He also reports that several people heard him say he had seen the Prophet in a dream and asked whether the hadith beginning "May God make radiant a man who heard my words" was sound as transmitted, and was told that it was {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 230}. Among the anecdotes al-Safadi gives is that he and his brother shared one shirt and one turban, so that when one went out the other stayed at home {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 231}.

## Works
Al-Zirikli lists among his books a commentary on al-Muzani's Mukhtasar in eleven parts on law, in manuscript, a reply on listening to music and song, in manuscript in the Rabat library, and al-Ta'liqa al-Kubra on Shafi'i branch law, in manuscript, with a copy in Istanbul; he adds that he also composed verse {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 222}. Al-Safadi reports a verse exchange between him and Abu al-Ala al-Ma'arri when al-Ma'arri came to Baghdad and lodged at Suwayqat Ghalib: the judge sent verses posing a riddle, al-Ma'arri dictated his answer on the spot to the messenger, and the judge replied in verse {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 231} and {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 16 p. 232}.
