Al-Ala' ibn Abdullah ibn Imad al-Hadrami, a distinguished Companion, jurist and one of the Muslim commanders; his lineage goes back to Hadramawt, and he was an ally of Harb ibn Umayya at Mecca. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

In late 6 AH the Prophet sent him with his letter to al-Mundhir ibn Sawa al-Tamimi, lord of Bahrayn, calling him and his people to Islam. Al-Mundhir became Muslim, and with him the Arab people of Bahrayn; its Magians and Jews entered into the jizya tax and a covenant. {cite:zad-al-maad}

The Prophet appointed him governor of Bahrayn and gave him charge of collecting its alms. He was one of the great governors among the Companions, known for answered prayers and for a clear wonder of crossing the sea during the Ridda wars. He remained governor of Bahrayn until he died in 21 AH, in the caliphate of Umar. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}


## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
Ibn Ishaq reports that before the conquest of Mecca the Prophet sent al-Ala' ibn al-Hadrami to [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-sawa|al-Mundhir ibn Sawa al-Abdi]], who became Muslim and whose Islam was good. He also reports that al-Mundhir died after the Prophet and before the Ridda of the people of [[place/bahrayn|Bahrayn]], and that al-Ala' was then the Prophet's governor over Bahrayn; see [[event/islam-of-al-mundhir-ibn-sawa|the Islam of al-Mundhir ibn Sawa]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/222}
