# Rasuna Said

## Summary
Rasuna Said (14 September 1910 – 2 November 1965) was a Minangkabau orator and nationalist from Maninjau, West Sumatra, remembered for speeches that joined the case against Dutch colonial rule to the case for women's education. She worked in Islamic schools, spoke for the Persatuan Muslimin Indonesia (Permi), was imprisoned by the colonial authorities, and after independence sat in national representative bodies; Indonesia named her a National Hero in 1974. {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-en} {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-id} {cite:wikidata-rasuna-said}

## Maninjau and education
She was born at Maninjau to a family the articles describe as devout Muslims: her father, Haji Muhammad Said, was a merchant and independence activist, and several uncles were educators. Family background is not personal affiliation, and the record says exactly that. She studied at local religious schools, at the Diniyah Putri girls' school in Padang Panjang, where she also worked as an assistant teacher, and in the orbit of Sumatera Thawalib. The Indonesian article adds that she deepened her religious study under Abdul Karim Amrullah, called Haji Rasul; that sentence stands in a paragraph the article itself marks as needing a citation, so it is reported here as the article's claim and no student edge is built on it. {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-en} {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-id} {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-ar}

## Oratory, Permi and prison
In the 1930s she toured West Sumatra giving fiery speeches for Permi, attacking colonial policy and the exclusion of women from schooling, and appealing to the Qur'an in public argument against colonialism. The colonial government watched her closely; in 1932 she was imprisoned. Public oratory and party affiliation are not a school of Islam, and no article names one for her. {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-en} {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-id}

## After independence and death
After 1945 she remained in public life as a representative and advocate for women's education. She died of breast cancer in Jakarta on 2 November 1965 and was buried in the Kalibata Heroes' Cemetery. Jakarta later carried her name on one of its main avenues. {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-en} {cite:wikipedia-rasuna-said-id} {cite:wikidata-rasuna-said}
