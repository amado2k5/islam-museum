## Summary
Husain Shah Sharqi was the sixth and last sultan of Jaunpur, the Sharqi kingdom of northern India. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} A son of Mahmud Shah, he succeeded his brother Muhammad Shah in 1458 and reigned as an ambitious expansionist until his long war with Bahlul Lodi of Delhi cost him his kingdom; he spent the last quarter-century of his life in exile in Bihar and died in 1505. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} Wikidata independently carries his death year as 1505. {cite:wikidata-husain-shah-jaunpur} No source consulted gives his birth year — the article files him under "Year of birth unknown" — and the museum's queue figure of 1440 is attested nowhere, so this record carries no birth date. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} {cite:wikidata-husain-shah-jaunpur}

## Accession and first campaigns
Husain began by signing a four years' peace treaty with Bahlul Lodi in 1458. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} At the opening of his reign he invaded Orissa, whose ruler Kapilendra Deva presented him with thirty elephants and a hundred horses, and in 1466 he sent a large army against Gwalior, where after a long siege Raja Kirti Singh agreed to become a vassal of Jaunpur and pay tribute. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en}

## The war with Delhi
In 1478 Husain reached the banks of the Yamuna with a very large army; Bahlul Lodi tried to secure peace by offering to retain only Delhi and govern it as Husain's vassal, but Husain rejected the offer. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} Bahlul crossed the Yamuna and defeated him; Husain made a truce, again captured Etawah and marched on Delhi, and was again defeated, and in March 1479 he came to the Yamuna a third time and was defeated again, losing the parganas of Kampil, Patiali, Shamsabad, Suket, Koil, Marhara and Jalesar. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} After further defeats Bahlul appointed Mubarak Khan to Jaunpur; Husain reassembled his forces, expelled Mubarak Khan and re-occupied the city, until Bahlul drove him out again, and in 1486 Bahlul placed his own son Barbak Shah Lodi on Jaunpur's throne. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} The article's infobox still prints his reign as running to 1494, a figure its own narrative does not support. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en}

## Exile and death
Husain fled to Kahalgaon in Bihar, where Sultan Alauddin Husain Shah of Bengal granted him asylum. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} He spent his last days there, still minting his own coins, and his son Jalal Khan was married to a daughter of his Bengali host. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} He died in 1505 and was buried at Jaunpur. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} {cite:wikidata-husain-shah-jaunpur}

## The man
Husain was interested in art and music, and is credited with a significant contribution to the development of khayal singing. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en} It was during his rule that Muhammad Jaunpuri, the claimant to be the Mahdi, appeared at Jaunpur; the article says Husain admired him, and flags that claim itself as needing a citation — no source consulted names a legal school for the sultan. {cite:wikipedia-husain-shah-jaunpur-en}
