## Summary
Khurram Parvez (born 1977) is a Kashmiri human rights defender based in Srinagar, programme coordinator of the Jammu and Kashmir Coalition of Civil Society (JKCCS) and chairperson of the Asian Federation Against Involuntary Disappearances (AFAD). Detained since November 2021 under India's Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, he was awarded the Martin Ennals Award for Human Rights Defenders in 2023. {cite:wikipedia-khurram-parvez-en} {cite:themuslim500-khurram-parvez}

## Early life
Parvez was born in Srinagar in 1977 and raised in the Kashmir Valley during the years in which the armed conflict between Indian forces and insurgents, and the violence surrounding it, reshaped civilian life. He studied electronics and communications, but the experience of the conflict turned him towards documentation of its human cost — extrajudicial killings, torture, enforced disappearances and the unidentified graves that became the focus of much of his later work. {cite:wikipedia-khurram-parvez-en}

## JKCCS and documentation work
As programme coordinator of JKCCS, Parvez organised fact-finding missions, published reports and built victim-centred archives on disappearances and unmarked graves in Kashmir, pressing for forensic identification and accountability. The coalition's reports — on mass graves in frontier districts, on enforced disappearances numbering in the thousands in its accounting, and on alleged torture — made it the most prominent documentation body of Kashmir's civil society. Indian authorities dispute its characterisations; the organisation presents its findings as victim testimony and official-record analysis. {cite:wikipedia-khurram-parvez-en}

## Personal cost and earlier detentions
In 2004, while monitoring election-related violence, Parvez was badly injured by a landmine explosion that killed a colleague and cost him a leg; he continued his work on a prosthetic limb. He was detained in 2011, and in 2016 was prevented from travelling to a UN Human Rights Council session in Geneva and held for weeks under public-safety legislation. In November 2021 the National Investigation Agency arrested him under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act on charges including terror-funding and conspiracy — charges he and rights organisations deny — and he has remained in custody since, with bail applications repeatedly contested. {cite:wikipedia-khurram-parvez-en}

## International recognition
Parvez was awarded the Rafto Prize for Human Rights in 2017 and the Martin Ennals Award in 2023, the jury describing him as a voice of the disappeared. UN special rapporteurs and international rights organisations have called for his release, characterising his detention as reprisal for documentation work; the Indian government rejects that characterisation and treats the prosecution as a counter-terrorism matter. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-khurram-parvez-en} {cite:themuslim500-khurram-parvez}

## Context
Parvez's work belongs to a documented tradition of Kashmiri civil-society documentation that grew out of the conflict years of the 1990s and 2000s: local groups collecting testimonies where formal investigations were absent or stalled, and seeking international attention when domestic remedies appeared exhausted. His case became emblematic of the wider question of how far documentation itself is treated as a security matter — a debate in which his supporters cite the prizes he received in custody and the rapporteurs' appeals, and his prosecutors cite the statutory charges pending against him. {cite:wikipedia-khurram-parvez-en}

His 2023 Martin Ennals Award, received in absentia, was described by its jury as honouring not only him but the wider documentation work of Kashmiri civil society; colleagues collected it on his behalf while he remained in Tihar Jail — a ceremony widely reported as a tribute to the documentation tradition he had come to personify. {cite:wikipedia-khurram-parvez-en}
