## Overview
Jafar ibn Ulba ibn Rabi'a al-Harithi, Abu Arim, was a love poet of little verse, a mukhadram who lived under both the Umayyad and the Abbasid states, and a horseman spoken of among his people. Al-Zirikli dates his death to 145 AH, that is 762 CE as printed, and prints no year of birth; he is one of the poets of Abu Tammam's Hamasa {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 125}.

## Life
He is the author of the verses that include the line "My heart goes with the Yemeni riders, riding up, a led mount beside them, while my body is bound in Mecca" {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 125}. He lived at Najran, and was imprisoned there on suspicion of taking part in the killing of a man of Banu Uqayl called Khushayna {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 125}.

## Death
Al-Zirikli says he was put to death in retaliation, naming al-Sari ibn Abd Allah al-Hashimi, al-Mansur's governor of Mecca, in that connection, though the printed wording at this point is unclear. He adds that it was also said that the man who killed him was a man of Banu Uqayl named Rahma ibn Tawaf {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 125}.
