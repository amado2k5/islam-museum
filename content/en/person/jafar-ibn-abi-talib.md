## Brief
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib was the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother — the man who stood before an African king and answered for a hunted religion, and who died holding its standard at Mu'ta.

## Life
When the persecution in Mecca grew dangerous, the Prophet sent a group of Muslims to seek refuge in Abyssinia — and Ja'far, son of [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]], led them, in [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|the first migration]] about 615 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

When Meccan envoys came to the court of [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]] demanding the refugees back, tradition tells that Ja'far answered for them: describing what his people had been, what the Prophet taught, and reciting the Qur'anic passage on Mary. The king wept, and gave them refuge {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

Ja'far returned to Medina only in 628. Months later, at [[event/battle-of-mutah|Mu'ta]], he took the standard when [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] fell, held it until both his hands were cut off, and was killed — September 629 CE {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

## Legacy
His day in the Negus's court is the tradition's first scene of Islam explained to a foreign ruler — a small Muslim voice, far from home, that carried. In memory he is Ja'far al-Tayyar, "the Flyer," wings in place of the arms he lost.

## In Ahmad ibn Hanbal's Fada'il al-Sahaba
[[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] gives him a short chapter: from al-Sha'bi, that the Prophet sent to Ja'far's wife to bring him Ja'far's sons and prayed, "O God, Ja'far has gone ahead to the best reward; be his successor among his offspring" (no. 1690, *mursal* with trustworthy narrators according to the editor [[person/wasi-allah-abbas|Wasi Allah Abbas]]); from an unnamed man, that the Prophet said, "I saw him in Paradise, his two wings stained with blood" (1691, weak for a break and an unknown transmitter); and from Ibn Shihab and Ubayd Allah ibn Aslam his words to Ja'far, "You resemble me in form and character" (1692–1693, both weak as *mursal*, the second also because of Ibn Lahi'a). {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1125} {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1126} {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1127}

## For young visitors
Ja'far had to stand before a king and explain his whole religion. He did it so well that the king protected all the refugees — and Ja'far died years later holding the army's flag.

## For Sprouts
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib was the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother, and one of the earliest Muslims. He emigrated with the Muslims to Abyssinia to escape harm and stood answering for their faith before its king, the Negus. He returned with his family in the year Khaybar fell and was martyred carrying the flag at Mu'ta in 629 CE.

## For Explorers
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother, was said to resemble the Prophet in body and character, and was among the earliest Muslims. He led the second emigration to Abyssinia and stood before the Negus answering for their new faith, reciting from the sura of Mary before the court — where the Negus came to Islam in the account — when Amr ibn al-As arrived to demand their return. He stayed about ten years and came back in 7 AH on the day Khaybar fell; the Prophet said he did not know which gladdened him more. At Mu'ta in 8 AH he was the second of the three commanders, carried the flag until both hands were cut and held it with his stumps — so Muslims call him Ja'far the Flyer, the One of Two Wings.

## For Scholars
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib ibn Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim, the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother, was the one most like the Prophet in form and character as the sira describes, and among the earliest Muslims. When harm intensified in Mecca he emigrated to Abyssinia at the head of the second emigration around 615 CE, becoming the community's leader and spokesman before the Negus: when Amr ibn al-As and al-Walid ibn al-Mughira came as Quraysh's envoys demanding the emigrants' return, the Negus asked Ja'far about the new religion, and he explained monotheism, truthfulness and family ties, then recited from the sura of Mary until the Negus and his bishops wept, so he protected them in his land as Ibn Hisham tells it — with Ibn Ishaq's further account of what Ja'far said about Jesus. He stayed in Abyssinia until returning in 7 AH, arriving on the very day Khaybar fell: 'I do not know which gladdens me more,' the Prophet said, 'the conquest of Khaybar or the arrival of Ja'far'. Made second commander after Zayd at Mu'ta, he bore the flag fighting until his hands were cut — fixing his name among Muslims as Ja'far al-Tayyar, Dhu al-Janahayn — and al-Bukhari records the Prophet saying God gave him two wings to fly with in Paradise.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
In two lessons of [[work/madrassat-al-hayat|Madrasat al-Hayat]], [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] narrates the speech of Ja'far ibn Abi Talib before [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]], from Umm Salama's hadith, which he says Ibn Ishaq gives in the Sira and Ahmad in the Musnad; in the second lesson he describes it as being in the Musnad with a good (hasan) chain. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:27:30} He says Ja'far went in the second migration to Abyssinia, in which the emigrants were some eighty-odd men and women, the first migration having been twelve men and four women. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:19:00}

In his telling Ja'far said: we will say what our Prophet told us, whatever comes of it; and he described his people's state in the Jahiliyya, worshipping idols, committing indecencies, the strong devouring the weak, until God sent a messenger whose lineage they knew, who commanded them to truthfulness, prayer, charity, chastity and the oneness of God. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:33:30} He says Ja'far recited the opening of Kaf Ha Ya Ayn Sad, and the Negus and his bishops wept, and that he said of Jesus that he was God's servant and messenger, His spirit and His word cast into Mary. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:15:00} Al-Heweny makes Ja'far's speech the first of three descriptions of the Jahiliyya by Companions, with that of [[person/al-mughira-ibn-shuba|al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba]] before Khosrow's interpreter, in al-Bukhari's Book of the Jizya, and that of al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad in al-Adab al-Mufrad with a sound chain. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:36:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:39:00}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the twenty-sixth episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] introduces Ja'far ibn Abi Talib as a man of the two emigrations, to [[place/abyssinia|Abyssinia]] and then to [[place/medina|Medina]], the Prophet's cousin, and among the people most like him in appearance and in character. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:01:00} He says Ja'far was the elder brother of [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], ten years his senior, calls him Ja'far al-Tayyar, "the one of the two wings", and counts him among the early converts, one of the first twenty-five to declare their Islam by his count. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:02:00} He cites the Prophet telling Ja'far that he resembled him in appearance and character, and pairs it with the saying of [[person/aisha|Aisha]] that the Prophet's character was the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:12:30}

In his account, when Mecca became unbearable for the Muslims the Prophet told Ja'far to emigrate with some of the Companions to Abyssinia, because its king was just and no one was wronged in his presence, and he set out with his wife [[person/asma-bint-umays|Asma' bint Umays]] and eighty others. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:02:30} He dwells on the hardship of that emigration: leaving homeland, family and property for an unknown fate, without work or kin in a foreign land. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:03:00} He then relates, in a report he attributes to [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] in the Musnad from [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama]], that they lived in Abyssinia under the best protection and safe in their religion, until Quraysh sent [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-rabia|Abd Allah ibn Abi Rabi'a]] with many gifts for the patriarchs, so that they would urge [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]] to hand the Muslims over. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:03:30} He says Amr had known the Negus before he became king; the two envoys entered, prostrated to him and asked for the return of people who had left their own people's religion without entering his, the patriarchs backed them, and the Negus refused until he had heard the Muslims. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:04:30}

He tells how the Muslims came in led by Ja'far as their spokesman, greeted the king and did not prostrate; asked why, Ja'far said they prostrate to God alone, and the speaker praises his dignity and courage in that moment. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:05:30} He summarises Ja'far's speech: they had been idolaters who ate carrion, harmed their neighbours and made lawful what was sacred, until God sent among them a prophet whose truthfulness and trustworthiness they knew, who called them to worship God alone, to keep the ties of kinship, to treat neighbours well, to pray and to fast. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:06:30} When the Negus asked him for something of what the Prophet had brought, Ja'far recited the opening of Surat Maryam, and the Negus and his bishops wept until their beards were wet; the Negus said that this and what Moses brought come from a single lamp-niche, and granted them safety; Ja'far's recitation from Surat Maryam, the king's weeping and his protection of the emigrants also appear in Ibn Ishaq's account cited in this record. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:07:00} {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} He then relates that Amr returned the next day saying that the Muslims said something grave about Jesus; the Negus asked them, and Ja'far answered that Jesus is God's servant and messenger, His word cast to Mary and a spirit from Him, whereupon the Negus picked up a twig and said that what Ja'far had said did not go beyond that by the width of this twig. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:07:30} He relates that the Negus told the Muslims in the Abyssinian tongue that they were safe in his land and that whoever insulted them would be fined, and returned Amr's gifts, saying that God had taken no bribe from him when He restored his kingdom to him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:09:00} The lesson he draws is that Ja'far presented his religion as it was revealed, without flattery or dilution, and did not sell his faith for worldly gain. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:09:30}

He relates that Ja'far reached Medina in the year 7, on the day [[place/khaybar|Khaybar]] was taken, and that the Prophet embraced him, kissed him between the eyes and said he did not know which pleased him more, the conquest of Khaybar or Ja'far's arrival (see [[event/jafar-arrival-at-khaybar|Ja'far's arrival at Khaybar]]). {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:11:00} He says Ja'far stayed in Medina only some months, less than a year, and then went out with the army of Mu'ta rather than resting after his years abroad. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:12:00} He says people called him "father of the poor" for his generosity, and relates from [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]], through [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], that after the Prophet no one was kinder to the poor than Ja'far: he fed them whatever was in his house, even bringing out to them an empty skin of butter or honey, which they split open and licked clean. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:13:00} He remarks that the testimony of Abu Hurayra, himself a poor man, carries weight, because the poor are the best witnesses to a generous man. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:14:00}

At [[event/battle-of-mutah|Mu'ta]], he relates, the Prophet put [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] in command, then Ja'far if Zayd fell, then [[person/abd-allah-ibn-rawaha|Abd Allah ibn Rawaha]] if Ja'far fell, and he says the army numbered three thousand Muslims facing a hundred thousand Byzantines on the edge of Jordan. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:15:00} He tells that God brought the battlefield into the Prophet's sight, and from the pulpit the Prophet told the Companions what was happening moment by moment: Zayd took the banner and was killed, then Ja'far, then Ibn Rawaha, asking them to seek forgiveness for each, and then "a sword of God's swords" took it and God gave him victory, meaning [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:15:30} He relates that Ja'far's right hand was cut off, so he took the banner in his left, which was cut off too, so he clasped it with his upper arms and bent his chest over it until he was killed, and God gave him in place of his arms two wings to fly with in Paradise, which is why he is called al-Tayyar; his bearing the banner until his hands were cut off is also told in this record from Ibn Ishaq. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:16:30} {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} He says the Prophet testified to his martyrdom, and cites the verse that tells that those killed in God's way are alive with their Lord and provided for. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:17:30} He reports from [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]], who was with them at Mu'ta, that he saw ninety-some wounds on Ja'far's body, from sword, spear and arrow, none of them in his back, which the speaker takes as proof that he never turned away. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:18:00} He relates from al-Bukhari that whenever Ibn Umar met [[person/abdullah-ibn-jafar|Abd Allah ibn Ja'far]] he greeted him as "son of the one of the two wings". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:18:30} He relates that the Prophet came to Ja'far's house and stroked the heads of his children, and ordered that food be prepared for Ja'far's family, since grief had come upon them, noting that part of this report is disputed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:19:00} He closes by saying that Ja'far was buried at Mu'ta, on the edge of Jordan. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep26 00:19:30}
