## Brief
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib was the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother — the man who stood before an African king and answered for a hunted religion, and who died holding its standard at Mu'ta.

## Life
When the persecution in Mecca grew dangerous, the Prophet sent a group of Muslims to seek refuge in Abyssinia — and Ja'far, son of [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]], led them, in [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|the first migration]] about 615 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

When Meccan envoys came to the court of [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]] demanding the refugees back, tradition tells that Ja'far answered for them: describing what his people had been, what the Prophet taught, and reciting the Qur'anic passage on Mary. The king wept, and gave them refuge {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

Ja'far returned to Medina only in 628. Months later, at [[event/battle-of-mutah|Mu'ta]], he took the standard when [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] fell, held it until both his hands were cut off, and was killed — September 629 CE {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

## Legacy
His day in the Negus's court is the tradition's first scene of Islam explained to a foreign ruler — a small Muslim voice, far from home, that carried. In memory he is Ja'far al-Tayyar, "the Flyer," wings in place of the arms he lost.

## For young visitors
Ja'far had to stand before a king and explain his whole religion. He did it so well that the king protected all the refugees — and Ja'far died years later holding the army's flag.

## For Sprouts
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib was the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother, and one of the earliest Muslims. He emigrated with the Muslims to Abyssinia to escape harm and stood answering for their faith before its king, the Negus. He returned with his family in the year Khaybar fell and was martyred carrying the flag at Mu'ta in 629 CE.

## For Explorers
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother, was said to resemble the Prophet in body and character, and was among the earliest Muslims. He led the second emigration to Abyssinia and stood before the Negus answering for their new faith, reciting from the sura of Mary before the court — where the Negus came to Islam in the account — when Amr ibn al-As arrived to demand their return. He stayed about ten years and came back in 7 AH on the day Khaybar fell; the Prophet said he did not know which gladdened him more. At Mu'ta in 8 AH he was the second of the three commanders, carried the flag until both hands were cut and held it with his stumps — so Muslims call him Ja'far the Flyer, the One of Two Wings.

## For Scholars
Ja'far ibn Abi Talib ibn Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim, the Prophet's cousin and Ali's brother, was the one most like the Prophet in form and character as the sira describes, and among the earliest Muslims. When harm intensified in Mecca he emigrated to Abyssinia at the head of the second emigration around 615 CE, becoming the community's leader and spokesman before the Negus: when Amr ibn al-As and al-Walid ibn al-Mughira came as Quraysh's envoys demanding the emigrants' return, the Negus asked Ja'far about the new religion, and he explained monotheism, truthfulness and family ties, then recited from the sura of Mary until the Negus and his bishops wept, so he protected them in his land as Ibn Hisham tells it — with Ibn Ishaq's further account of what Ja'far said about Jesus. He stayed in Abyssinia until returning in 7 AH, arriving on the very day Khaybar fell: 'I do not know which gladdens me more,' the Prophet said, 'the conquest of Khaybar or the arrival of Ja'far'. Made second commander after Zayd at Mu'ta, he bore the flag fighting until his hands were cut — fixing his name among Muslims as Ja'far al-Tayyar, Dhu al-Janahayn — and al-Bukhari records the Prophet saying God gave him two wings to fly with in Paradise.
