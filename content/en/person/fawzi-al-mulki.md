Fawzi al-Mulki (1910 – 10 January 1962) was a Jordanian politician and diplomat from Irbid, educated at the American University of Beirut and at Cambridge, where he read veterinary medicine and then law, political science and economics.{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-ar}{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-en} Returning to Jordan in 1943, he rose through the veterinary service and wartime economic administration to the posts of consul-general in Cairo, minister to Egypt and foreign minister, and was minister of defence in Tawfiq Abu al-Huda's wartime cabinet.{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-en}{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-ar}

Posted to Paris in 1951 and then to London, where he befriended the young King Hussein, he was appointed prime minister on 5 May 1953 — the first of Hussein's reign — and held office until 2 May 1954, when his liberal policies provoked riots and his dismissal.{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-en} He served again as foreign minister in 1956 and ended his career as Jordan's permanent representative to the United Nations, dying in post on 10 January 1962.{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-ar}{cite:wikidata-fawzi-al-mulki} His son Hani al-Mulki would also become prime minister of Jordan.{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-en}{cite:wikipedia-fawzi-al-mulki-ar}
