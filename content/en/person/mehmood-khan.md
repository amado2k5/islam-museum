## Summary
Dr Mehmood Khan is a physician and chief executive whose career runs from academic medicine through the world's largest food company to a Gulf-funded science foundation. The Muslim 500 lists him in Science & Technology, identifying him as a physician and chief executive officer. {cite:muslim500-mehmood-khan}

## Medicine first
Khan trained in medicine in the United Kingdom — he is of Pakistani heritage — and built a research career in endocrinology and metabolism at the Mayo Clinic in Minnesota, publishing on the biology of obesity and metabolic disease before leaving the bench for industry. {cite:muslim500-mehmood-khan}

## PepsiCo
In 2007 he joined PepsiCo as its first chief scientific officer, later becoming vice-chairman, tasked with moving a snacks-and-drinks giant toward healthier products — the fruits, grains and "good-for-you" portfolio — and with giving a commercial behemoth a science capability it had never had. His decade there made him one of the most visible science executives in the food industry, though critics of the industry debated what such roles could achieve; the museum reports the positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:muslim500-mehmood-khan}

## Hevolution Foundation
Khan then took on a different kind of institution: chief executive of the Hevolution Foundation, the Saudi-backed non-profit launched with a commitment of up to one billion dollars a year to fund research and early-stage companies in healthspan and geroscience — the biology of ageing — making it one of the largest longevity funders in the world and placing a physician-scientist of the Pakistani diaspora at the head of the Gulf's biggest bet on the science of long life. {cite:muslim500-mehmood-khan}

## Significance
In the museum's record, Khan is the physician-administrator across three systems — academic medicine, global industry and sovereign-scale philanthropy — whose trajectory shows how the Muslim 500's Science & Technology section reaches past laboratories into the boardrooms that fund them. {cite:muslim500-mehmood-khan}

## The corporate scientist's question
Khan's biography is the trajectory of the modern corporate scientist: trained in academic medicine, moved to industry because that was where the questions of health-at-scale were being asked, led a food-and-beverage company's research arm, then built a longevity-biology venture after leaving. His public arguments — that food companies must be part of the metabolic-disease solution, not only its cause, and that the biology of aging is the next pharmaceutical frontier — are the positions of a man who learned the system from inside. The Muslim 500 lists him for that system-level profile in science. {cite:muslim500-mehmood-khan}

## The scientist as executive
Khan's record is the museum's example of the scientist who treats the corporation as a laboratory: the physician who understood that the questions of health at scale were being asked in industry, not in academia, and who took the executive post that let him ask them. The pattern — academic expertise converted into corporate research — is the one his Muslim 500 entry records; the museum keeps it as the profile of a scientist who learned the system from inside and then built the venture that would change it. {cite:muslim500-mehmood-khan}
