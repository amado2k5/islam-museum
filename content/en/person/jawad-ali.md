## Summary
Iraqi historian and academic (1907–1987), author of the monumental ten-volume Al-Mufassal fi Tarikh al-Arab qabl al-Islam (The Detailed History of the Arabs before Islam). He taught in Iraqi universities for decades and wrote on the history of prayer, the Mahdi and pre-Islamic Arabia, combining academic criticism with a vast range of Arabic and foreign references. {cite:wikipedia-jawad-ali}

## Life and education

Jawad Ali was born in 1907 in Najaf, Iraq, into a learned family, and studied for years in the Najaf seminary before entering modern education and earning degrees at the University of Baghdad and the University of Hamburg in Germany, where he took his doctorate in history. {cite:wikipedia-jawad-ali}

He became professor in the College of Arts at the University of Baghdad, taught inside and outside Iraq, held academic posts including chairmanship of the history department, and combined the heritage of the seminary with the method of the modern university. {cite:wikipedia-jawad-ali}

## Al-Mufassal and other works

He spent more than twenty years composing Al-Mufassal fi Tarikh al-Arab qabl al-Islam, issued in ten volumes, gathering what the sources preserve of pre-Islamic Arabia from pre-Islamic literature, inscriptions, reports and foreign scholarship — the largest Arabic encyclopedia of its subject. {cite:wikipedia-jawad-ali}

His other works include The History of Prayer in Islam, The Mahdi, The History of the Arabs in Islam and studies in language, society and ancient literature. {cite:wikipedia-jawad-ali}

## Legacy

Al-Mufassal remains the first reference to which students of ancient Arab history turn; although some historians criticize its reliance on later akhbar compilations, it stays the starting point for every new study of the Jahiliyya. {cite:wikipedia-jawad-ali}

Jawad Ali died in 1987; editions of Al-Mufassal continue to be reprinted, and his method is taught in Arab history departments as a model of encyclopedic compilation. {cite:wikipedia-jawad-ali}
