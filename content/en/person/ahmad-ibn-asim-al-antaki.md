## Summary
Ahmad ibn Asim al-Antaki was an ascetic of [[place/antioch|Antioch]]. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 449 in the ninth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], describing him as one who broke desire and cut short the evils of the self, who kept long vigil and was harsh with those who blamed him. Most of the notice is related by [[person/ahmad-ibn-abi-al-hawari|Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari]] from him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/281-284}

## Self-examination and striving
He said that every soul is held in pledge by its deeds, and that the pledges are released only after the debts are paid; when the pledges are closed, the debts are confirmed, and their owners then deserve prison. He advised the seeker to turn to God against the evils of the self and the opposition of desire, and to fight the enemy in need of Him, afraid of His punishment and hopeful of His reward. He advised continual reckoning of the self, not to boast before people of one's piety, and to be ashamed before God lest He let you go to waste before the keepers of Hell feel shame toward you. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/280-281} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/282}

## Truthfulness and lying
He said that between you and the rank of truthfulness lies the hurdle of lying, and that you cross it with a fear that holds you back and with sincere supplication. He advised that a person who finds his self loving falsehood should count it among the liars, and confess to God its lying. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/281}

## Knowledge and modesty
He said he wished not to die before knowing his Lord, and that knowledge is not mere acknowledgement, but the knowledge which, when you have it, makes you ashamed. He said that all good lies in two things: that the world be turned away from you, and that you be given contentment, and that people's faces be turned from you, and that you be given satisfaction. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/282}

## What benefits and what harms
He listed what benefits: truthfulness when it removes lying from its places; trust when one relies on the guarantee; wealth when it removes poverty and the fear of poverty; poverty when its bearer is dignified and content. Among the beneficial he counted knowledge that turns back ignorance; among the harmful, speech when silence would be better. He said that the first of rights is that a person bind himself to fulfil what God has laid on him, even against his desire, then his parents and children, then the nearest. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/283}

## Hidden sins
Asked what is the best means of drawing near to God, he said: abandoning His hidden sins, since with them the outward is also set aside. Asked which sin is most harmful, he said: a sin you do not know to be a sin; and more harmful still is what you took for obedience while it is disobedience to God. Asked which sin is most useful for repentance, he said: to keep it before your eyes and weep over it until you leave the world, and never return to its like; that is sincere repentance. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/284}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya (continued)
Abu Nu'aym continues Ahmad's sayings on pages 285–290 of the ninth volume. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/285-290}}

**His lament over the age.** He said he had lived to see a time when Islam had become strange and the description of truth had become strange. If he sought a scholar, he found him beguiled by the world and loving honour and leadership; if he sought a worshipper, he found him ignorant in his worship and overcome by Iblis. He counted among the people of his time those who adorn themselves with talk in gatherings, grow proud in anger, and flatter one another in the race for this world. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/286-287}}

**Remedies for the self.** He set out remedies for diseases of the heart: greed is checked by contentment; short hope brings the sweetness of asceticism; true despair of what people hold cuts the causes of covetousness; surrender to God brings the heart's rest; knowing the self shuts the door on conceit; a heart at rest with little mixing and no seeking gives the body rest. He counted gatherings of the mindful among the wise as a cause of a softened heart. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/288}}

**What nothing equals.** He listed things that other things do not equal in worth or in harm: no light like the light of certainty, no knowledge like knowing the self, no justice like fairness, no tyranny like following desire, no struggle like struggling with the self, no defeat like being mastered by desire. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/289}}

**Sayings of Abu Abd Allah al-Antaki.** When desire comes upon people, it seats the seeker and blinds the wise: the wise does not know his disease and the seeker does not seek its cure. Whoever seeks help from God is helped; whoever keeps himself from sin is kept from sin; whoever gives himself up to his self is cut off from obedience, desire overcomes him, and he is led on the road to ruin. "The one deprived is he who is denied asking, for asking is the key to answer." {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/290}}

**From his letters and sayings on pages 291–296.** He said that whoever does not weaken his certainty does not weaken his fear; whoever shows a sense of safety grows heedless; and whoever grows heedless hardens of heart, so no admonition reaches him. He related that a man wrote to his brother advising him to seek what concerns him and leave what does not; the same letter holds that backbiting is among the worst of evils in this life and the next, and that it is disliked in the heart before it is spoken. He said that a servant is not enough who makes a claim and does not make it true in his deeds, nor one who gives any part of his heart to other than his Lord; he must busy himself with purifying his conscience. {{cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/291-296}}
