# Pir Baba

## A Sayyid from across the Oxus

Sayyid Ali Tirmizi — Pir Baba, "the holy father", as the Pashtuns came to call him — was born about 908 AH (1502 CE). The two encyclopedia articles disagree on where: the English article gives Fergana, in present-day Uzbekistan; the Simple English article gives Kunduz, at Tirmiz in Khurasan. The English article calls him a Hussaini Sayyid; devotional genealogies name his father, Sayyid Qanbar Ali, and say the family came to India in Babur's time, reaching Delhi in 943 AH/1536 CE. The record preserves the readings rather than choosing between them. {cite:source/wikipedia-pir-baba-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-pir-baba-simple}

## Discipleship and the road to Buner

His formation was Sufi. The English article calls him "a Sufi Pir" and "a stalwart of the Chishti, Kabrawi, Sarwardi, and Qadri Sufi order[s]", a disciple of Salaar Roomi; the Simple English article, in its own words, calls him "a Sufi Muslim saint of the Kubrawiya order". The devotional tradition fills in the mechanism: he became the murid and khalifa of Khwaja Salaar Ataullah Chishti Roomi of Ajmer — the sources also name a teacher, Syed Ahmad Noor — and was directed to carry the teaching east, into the hills of Buner, in present-day Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. {cite:source/wikipedia-pir-baba-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-pir-baba-simple}

## Among the Yusufzai

In Buner he settled among the Yusufzai Pashtuns, and his mission is remembered as the turning of the valley to Islam. The devotional tradition says he married Bibi Mubarika, called Pir Abai, a sister of Daulat Khan, the sardar of Buner. With his disciple Akhund Darweza — the chronicler of the Yusufzai, author of the Tazkira and the Makhzan — Pir Baba opposed Bayazid Ansari, known as Pir Roshan, and the Roshnai movement his followers carried into the Pashtun hills, arguing it a bid'ah, an innovation outside the faith. {cite:source/wikipedia-pir-baba-en}

## Death and shrine

He died in Rajab 991 AH (1583 CE) at Buner and was buried at Pacha Killay village, where his shrine still stands; a local-history thesis gives 1575 instead, and one devotional page misprints the Gregorian year of 991 AH as 1593, and the record preserves both minority readings. His two sons are remembered in the tradition: Sayyid Mustafa, buried in Kunar in Afghanistan, and Sayyid Habib, buried beside his father. The shrine remains a place of visitation — in 2009 the Taliban closed it temporarily, and in 2010 it was bombed. {cite:source/wikipedia-pir-baba-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-pir-baba-simple}
