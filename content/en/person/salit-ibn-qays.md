## Overview
Salit ibn Qays ibn Amr al-Ansari, sent by Umar with Abu Ubayd to Iraq. al-Baladhuri reports that Umar said to him: "Were it not for a haste in you, I would have appointed you. But war is a cruel thing, and only a steady man is fit for it" {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}.

## The Battle of the Bridge
On the day of the Bridge, he told Abu Ubayd that he had forbidden him to cut the bridge to the enemy. He advised him to withdraw, and to write to the Commander of the Faithful asking for reinforcements, but Abu Ubayd refused. Salit fought until he was killed {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}.

His death is reported at the end of Ramadan of the year 13 AH. This date rests on al-Baladhuri alone (pp.351-352), and it needs an additional source.

## In al-Mas'udi
Sulayt ibn Qays, one of the people of Badr, whose reports appear in al-Mas'udi's account of the Battle of the Bridge {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.604}.

## His counsel
al-Mas'udi reports that Umar associated him with Abu Ubayd and instructed him to consult him and not to oppose him. His view was that the Muslims should not cross the Euphrates until the enemy crossed to them, and that the bridge should not be cut, so that it would remain a refuge for them if they were defeated {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.604}.

When Abu Ubayd said to him, "You have been cowardly", he answered that he was bolder of spirit and of tribe than he was, and that he had only given his opinion. He said that he would hear and obey, out of dislike for disobeying the obedience {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.604}.
