## Summary
A Companion of Banu Abd al-Dar; he emigrated to Abyssinia with his wife and two sons, and came in the two ships. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/5}

## In the Abyssinian emigrants chapter
Ibn Ishaq names him of Banu Abd al-Dar in the two ships, with his sons Amr and Khuzayma and his wife Umm Harmala bint Abd al-Aswad, who perished in the land of Abyssinia. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/5} He says the two sons were his by Umm Harmala. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/5}

## Tribe
He belongs, per Ibn Ishaq, to [[group/banu-abd-dar|Banu Abd al-Dar]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/5}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- Ibn Ishaq counted Jahm, his two sons and his wife as one "man" in the total of sixteen; so the sons should not be counted as separate men (p. 5).
