Omid Safi (born 1970 in Jacksonville, Florida) is an Iranian-American scholar of Islam. Born in the United States and raised in Iran, he migrated with his family from Tehran to the United States in 1985, and took his doctorate at Duke University. He taught philosophy and religion at Colgate University (1999–2004), then at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, and joined Duke University, where he directed the Duke Islamic Studies Center from 2014 to 2019.{cite:wikipedia-safi-en}{cite:wikidata-safi}

A specialist in Islamic mysticism (Sufism), contemporary Islamic thought and medieval Islamic history, he edited Progressive Muslims: On Justice, Gender, and Pluralism (2003), defining the progressive Muslim movement he helped to lead, and wrote The Politics of Knowledge in Premodern Islam (2006), Memories of Muhammad: Why the Prophet Matters (2009) and Radical Love: Teachings from the Islamic Mystical Tradition (2018).{cite:wikipedia-safi-en} His books and columns are first-person Muslim writing, but no source names a legal school for him or attests membership of a Sufi order — studying and teaching Sufism is not initiation — and his record accordingly leaves his faith unknown.{cite:wikipedia-safi-en}{cite:wikidata-safi}
