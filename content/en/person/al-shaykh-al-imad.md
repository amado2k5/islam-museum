## Overview
Al-Shaykh al-'Imad Abu Ishaq Ibrahim ibn 'Abd al-Wahid al-Maqdisi the Hanbali, brother of the hafiz 'Abd al-Ghani; born at Jamma'il in 543 AH, he emigrated in 551 with his relatives, heard hadith at Baghdad and Mosul, memorised al-Khiraqi and Gharib al-'Azizi, gave lessons, debated, and read the canonical readings under Abu'l-Hasan al-Batayihi. He devoted himself to teaching Qur'an and fiqh and was pious, humble, fasting and night-praying; al-Muwaffaq said "I never left him save to travel, and I never knew him to disobey God." {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## His stations and death
He held the Mountain when al-Muwaffaq was in town and the town when al-Muwaffaq went up, taught at the Umayyad mosque from dawn to night, and was fervent in commanding right and forbidding wrong; his works include the Book of Distinctions in legal questions. He died suddenly on Thursday night, 17 Dhu'l-Qa'da 614, and the mosque could not contain his funeral. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## In Ibn Rajab's Dhayl Tabaqat al-Hanabila
Ibn Rajab gives him a long entry. He records his birth at Jamma'il in 543, notes that al-Mundhiri gives 544, and says he emigrated to Damascus with his community in 551 when the Franks took their land {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 93-94}. He travelled to Baghdad twice, the first time with Shaykh al-Muwaffaq in 569, and studied law there under [[person/ibn-al-mani|Ibn al-Manni]] until he excelled, debated and gave legal opinions; he then returned to Damascus and gave himself to teaching {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 94}.

Among the devotions Ibn Rajab reports, he sought out the times and places where prayer is answered, and kept up supplication on Wednesdays between the noon and afternoon prayers at the martyrs' graves by Bab al-Saghir {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 100}. Ibn Rajab adds that the preacher Abu Muhammad al-Buzuri included him in his classes of Ibn al-Manni's students and praised him, and that Abd al-Razzaq al-Ras'ani often cited him in his Qur'an commentary with great respect {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 103}.

On his death, Ibn Rajab quotes al-Diya' that he died on the night of Thursday at the time of the night prayer, 16 Dhu al-Qa'da 614, while al-Mundhiri says the 17th. He had prayed the sunset prayer in the mosque that evening while fasting and broke his fast on a little food; when death came he faced the qibla and recited the testimony of faith. Al-Diya' describes the mosque as crowded as on a Friday, al-Muwaffaq leading the funeral prayer, a crush around the bier that nearly killed some people, and the judges and notaries walking out with it {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 103-104}. Ibn Rajab also gives Sibt Ibn al-Jawzi's account of the crowd stretching from the Cave of Blood to Bab al-Faradis, and several dreams reported about him after his death, each attributed to its narrator {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 104-105}. He narrated a great deal; al-Diya' and al-Mundhiri heard from him, and Ibn Khalil and Ibn al-Bukhari transmitted from him {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 105}.
