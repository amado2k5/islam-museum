## Summary
Abd Allah ibn Muhayriz al-Jumahi al-Qurashi of Syria, given the kunya Abu Muhayriz by Damra, has an entry in al-Bukhari's [[work/al-tarikh-al-kabir|al-Tarikh al-Kabir]]: he heard [[person/abu-mahdhura|Abu Mahdhura]] and [[person/abu-said-al-khudri|Abu Sa'id al-Khudri]], and [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]], Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Habban, [[person/makhul-al-dimashqi|Makhul]] and his son Abd al-Rahman heard him; al-Hasan reported from Damra that he died in the reign of [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]]. {cite:tarikh-kabir-hyderabad-3-1 p. 193}

## An orphan in Abu Mahdhura's care
Al-Bukhari relates through Ibn Jurayj from Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd al-Malik that Ibn Muhayriz was an orphan in Abu Mahdhura's care when Abu Mahdhura fitted him out for Syria, and that he said: uncle, I am leaving for Syria and fear I shall be asked about your call to prayer — so tell me about it. The editors note that al-Nasa'i gives the hadith in full through Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj. {cite:tarikh-kabir-hyderabad-3-1 p. 194}

## The report of Mu'adh and Umar
Al-Bukhari also gives, through Makhul from Ibn Muhayriz, a report that [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh]] told [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] of a sequence of coming events: the flourishing of [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]] is the ruin of Yathrib, and the coming of the great battle is the conquest of [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]]; then Mu'adh struck Umar's shoulder and said: it is true. {cite:tarikh-kabir-hyderabad-3-1 p. 193}

## His renunciation in al-Zuhd
[[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] gathers reports of him in al-Zuhd. He went into a cloth-merchant's shop to buy something, and a man told the seller: do you know who this is? This is Ibn Muhayriz — and he stood up and said: we came to buy with our dirhams, not with our religion. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 460} A man kept him company in the rearguard in Byzantine territory, and when the man was about to leave him Ibn Muhayriz counselled him: if you can know and not be known, do so; if you can ask and not be asked, do so; and if you can walk to others and not have others walk to you, do so. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 468} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 469}

Among his sayings there: whoever walks in front of his father has been undutiful to him, unless he walks ahead to clear something harmful from his path; and whoever calls his father by his name or kunya has been undutiful, unless he says "father". {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 469} He would bring [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik]] a letter of counsel, have him read it and not leave it in his hand; he grew angry when praised to his face; he said: I would rather have leprosy on my skin than wear a silk garment; and al-Awza'i reported from one who heard him that whoever stands guard for a night in God's path earns a qirat for every person and animal. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 469}

Ahmad relates that Abd al-Malik sent him a slave-girl, whereupon he left his house and would not enter it; when Abd al-Malik was told that he had driven Ibn Muhayriz from his own home, he sent for her and took her back. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 469} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 470} On campaign, the expense he liked best was fodder for the mounts. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 470}
