## Summary

Salma Khadra Jayyusi (1925–2023) was a Palestinian poet, critic and translator, born in Safed, who devoted her later decades to introducing modern Arabic literature to the English-speaking world.{cite:wikipedia-salma-khadra-jayyusi}

## Life

She studied at the American University of Beirut and the University of London, worked in university teaching and journalism across Jordan and the Arab world, then settled in the United States, where in 1980 she founded PROTA, the Project of Translation from Arabic, which issued massive anthologies of Arabic poetry, fiction and drama.{cite:wikipedia-salma-khadra-jayyusi}

## Works

Her best-known critical book is Trends and Movements in Modern Arabic Poetry (1977), a standard study of the modern Arabic poetic renaissance. She also published poetry collections and translations, and the anthology Modern Arabic Poetry, which gathered poets of successive generations.{cite:wikipedia-salma-khadra-jayyusi}

## Standing

She is often described as the most important bridge between contemporary Arabic literature and the Western academy, and received Arab and international honours; her work and project feature in a broad debate on the representation of Arabs in Western culture.{cite:wikipedia-salma-khadra-jayyusi}
