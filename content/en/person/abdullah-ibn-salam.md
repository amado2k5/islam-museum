## Brief
Abdullah ibn Salam was a learned rabbi of Banu Qaynuqa' who examined the Prophet on his arrival in Medina, declared him genuine, and converted — the most famous Jewish convert of the era.

## Life
The tradition gives him a name before Islam, al-Husayn ibn Salam, and a scene at the Prophet's arrival: questions put and answered to a scholar's satisfaction. He declared his Islam publicly — the tradition adding that his own community, hearing him, called him a liar where moments before they had called him their master {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He is counted among the companions, and in the hadith tradition among those named as promised paradise. He died at Medina around 663 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

:::perspectives
Sunni tradition holds him as the model sincere convert: a scholar of the older scripture who recognized the Prophet and bore witness openly, against his community's disapproval.

Historians note that the account also serves apologetic ends — a Jewish scholar validating the Prophet — while accepting that conversions from Medina's Jewish tribes did occur.
:::

## For young visitors
