## Brief
Abdullah ibn Salam was a learned rabbi of the Banu Qaynuqa who tested the Prophet when he arrived in Medina, testified to his truthfulness and embraced Islam — the most famous Jew of his age to do so.

## Life
Tradition gives him a pre-Islamic name, al-Husayn ibn Salam, and a scene on the Prophet's arrival: questions put to him and answered to a scholar's satisfaction. He then declared his Islam openly. Tradition adds that when his people heard of it they called him a liar, though only moments before they had been calling him "our lord and the son of our lord" {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

He is counted among the Companions, and in the hadith tradition he is among those given the good news of Paradise. He died in Medina around 663 CE {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## For young visitors
A Jewish scholar tested the Prophet with his hardest questions — and came away convinced.

## His conversion on the Prophet's arrival (The Sealed Nectar p.181–182)
He heard of the Prophet's arrival and came to him with questions that only a prophet could answer, and he believed at once. He then told the Prophet that the Jews were a people of slander. The Prophet sent for them; when they learned of his conversion they said, "our worst man and the son of our worst man," and when he asked them about his Islam they said he had lied ([[concept/medina-society-at-hijra|Medina's society at the Hijra]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.181} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.182} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/459}.

## From the series Qissa wa 'Ibar
In the fifth episode Omar Abdelkafy relates that Abdullah ibn Salam was one of the leading Jews, that on seeing the Prophet he said he knew him from what he had read in the Torah better than he knew his own son, and that, fearing his people would slander him if he became Muslim, he asked that they be questioned about him first: they praised him and his father, and when he testified that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His messenger, they reviled him. He tells it as evidence that the first thing truth meets is denial. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:04:00}

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] mentions Abd Allah ibn Salam twice in [[work/madrassat-al-hayat|Madrasat al-Hayat]]. In the first lesson he narrates from Sahih Muslim his dream of a garden with a pillar reaching the sky and a ring at its top; a servant lifted him and he woke with the ring in his hand. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:34:00} In another version in Muslim he tried to climb a mountain and kept falling, and was told that this was the station of the martyrs, which he would not reach, and that he would die holding the firmest handhold. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:38:00} In the reflection on the friends of God he says the Prophet interpreted the garden as Islam, the pillar as its pillar and the ring as the firmest handhold, and told him he would die upon Islam; al-Heweny says he is certain Ibn Salam is one of the friends of God. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:26:00}

He relates that Ibn Salam had many slaves yet carried his own goods on his shoulder in the market, citing the hadith that no one with an atom's weight of pride in his heart enters Paradise, and he attributes the story to al-Hakim's Mustadrak, al-Tabarani and others. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:28:00}

## In Ahmad Farid's lessons 'The Boat'
In [[work/the-boat-ahmad-farid|'The Boat']] [[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] tells Abdallah ibn Salam's story as an example of how those who lived in the Prophet's time saw the signs of truthfulness in his face. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep11 00:01:30} He describes him as a rabbi of the Jews who, when the Prophet arrived in Medina, was in his orchard gathering fruit, went with what he had gathered, looked at the Prophet's face and said that it was not the face of a liar. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep11 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep11 00:02:00} He then asked the Prophet to call the Jews and ask them about him: they called him their best and the son of their best, their most learned and the son of their most learned, and said he would never become Muslim; when he came out and bore witness, they called him their worst and most ignorant. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep11 00:02:30}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In episode 10 of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']] [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] tells the life of Abd Allah ibn Salam, introducing him as a Medinan of the Children of Israel, a descendant of Joseph son of Jacob, and a rabbi revered by his people, who became Muslim without hesitation when the Prophet emigrated {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:01:30}. He says his name before Islam was al-Husayn and the Prophet named him Abd Allah, and that he was given glad tidings of Paradise more than once {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:02:30}; he cites a hadith he calls sound and attributes to the two Shaykhs, on the three who are given their reward twice, among them a man of the People of the Book who believed in his own prophet and then in Muhammad {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:02:00}. In his view, the description of a final prophet from the Torah, which the Jews of Medina spoke of before the mission, was among the main reasons its people hastened to Islam {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:03:00}, and he says Ibn Salam read in the Torah the description of that prophet and his Companions and prayed for a long life so as to meet him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:04:30}.

He relates from him, in a report he attributes to Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi, that when the Prophet arrived in Medina people rushed out to him, Ibn Salam among them, and on seeing his face he knew it was "not the face of a liar" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:05:00}. He then came to him, in a report he attributes to al-Bukhari in the chapters on the merits of the Ansar and on the prophets, asking about three things only a prophet would know: the first sign of the Hour, the first food of the people of Paradise, and why a child resembles its father or its mother; the Prophet said Gabriel had just told him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:06:30}. The answers were a fire coming out of the east that drives people to the west, the extra lobe of the fish's liver, and that resemblance follows whichever parent's fluid comes first {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:07:30}. The episode of the questions is also given in The Sealed Nectar {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.181} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/459}. Ibn Salam bore witness to his prophethood and then asked him to question his people about him before they learned of his conversion; they praised him as their chief and son of their chief, their scholar and son of their scholar {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:08:00}. When asked what they would say if he became Muslim they sought refuge from the thought; Ibn Salam came out and declared his faith, and they called him their fool and son of their fool {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:09:00}.

He gives another version, which he attributes to al-Bayhaqi in Dala'il al-Nubuwwa and to [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] in al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, noting a similar report in the book of the prophets in Sahih al-Bukhari: Ibn Salam was at the top of a palm tree working on it, with his aunt Khalida bint al-Harith sitting below, when a man brought news that the Prophet had arrived and stopped at [[place/quba|Quba']]; he cried out "God is greatest", and his aunt rebuked him, saying he would have done no more had it been Moses son of Imran; he answered that this was Moses' brother, sharing one religion with him, and then he, his aunt and his household became Muslims {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:09:30}.

He lists the glad tidings given him. In a report he attributes to Ahmad and al-Hakim, with al-Hakim and al-Dhahabi grading it sound, from [[person/musab-ibn-sad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Mus'ab ibn Sa'd]] from his father, the Prophet ate from a bowl, left some over and said a man of Paradise would come along that path and eat it; [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd]] hoped it would be his brother [[person/umayr-ibn-abi-waqqas|Umayr]], whom he had left doing his ablutions, but it was Abd Allah ibn Salam who came and ate it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:11:30}. He cites al-Hakim's Mustadrak, through Yazid ibn Umayra, that [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] on his deathbed named four men to seek knowledge from: [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]], [[person/abu-al-darda|Abu al-Darda']], [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman al-Farisi]] and Abd Allah ibn Salam, saying he had heard the Prophet call Ibn Salam the tenth of ten in Paradise {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:13:00}. He cites [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]]'s Tafsir from Ibn Abbas that the verse praising an upright group among the People of the Book who recite God's verses through the night in prostration was revealed about Abd Allah ibn Salam, Tha'laba ibn Sa'ya and a third man with them {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:14:00}. He reports a son of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas as saying he never heard the Prophet say of anyone walking the earth that he was of the people of Paradise except Abd Allah ibn Salam, noting himself that other Companions heard it said of others, and adds that the verse mentioning a witness from the Children of Israel was revealed about him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:16:30}.

He relates from Qays ibn Ubad that in the mosque of Medina he saw a man bearing the marks of humility, of whom people said he was of the people of Paradise; Qays followed him home, and the man said no one should say what he does not know, and then told him a dream {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:15:00}: a green garden with an iron pillar in its middle, its foot in the earth and its top in the sky, with a handhold at the top; he climbed, took hold of it and woke with it in his hand, and the Prophet interpreted the garden as Islam, the pillar as its pillar and the handhold as the firmest handhold, and told him he would remain a Muslim until he died {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:15:30}. In a report he attributes to Sahih Muslim from Ibn Salam, he relates that Ibn Salam, though God had made him well off, walked through the market carrying a bundle of firewood on his back, and when asked said he wanted to drive away pride, citing the hadith that warns whoever has a mustard seed's weight of arrogance in his heart {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:17:30}. From Ibn Abi al-Dunya's book on reliance on God, through [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]], with a chain he calls sound, he reports that Salman and Ibn Salam agreed that whoever died first would tell the other what he met; one died and appeared to the other in a dream, telling him to rely on God and rejoice, for he had seen nothing like reliance on God {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:18:30}. He defines reliance on God as taking every means and leaving the outcome to God, accepting the decree when it falls short of one's hopes {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:19:30}. He cites [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]]'s Siyar A'lam al-Nubala' that Ibn Salam was present at [[event/battle-of-nahawand|Nahawand]] and asked, if no mount could be found for him, to be carried and set down between the battle lines {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:20:30}, and says he died in his bed in Medina after a long life as a scholar, a worshipper and a sincere counsellor of the Muslims {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep10 00:21:30}.
