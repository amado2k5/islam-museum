## Summary
[[person/umm-al-fadl-lubaba|Umm al-Fadl]], wife of [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib]]; her name is Lubaba bint al-Harith al-Hilaliyya — Lubaba the Elder, sister of Lubaba the Younger {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2026}.

## Her Islam
She became Muslim before the hijra, as it is said — or it is said after it {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2026}. Ibn Sa'd said: "Umm al-Fadl was the first woman to believe after Khadija", and she related from the Prophet {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2026}.

## The believing sisters
Al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar and others relate from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet said: "the four sisters are believers: Umm al-Fadl, Maymuna, Asma' and Salma" — [[person/maymuna-bint-al-harith|Maymuna]] is the Mother of the Believers, Umm al-Fadl's full sister, and Asma' and Salma are their sisters by their father, daughters of Umays al-Khath'amiyya {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2026}. Al-Waqidi mentions another account from Kurayb naming Lubaba, Izza, Asma' and Salma {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2026}.

## Her mother's kin-by-marriage
It was said of Umm al-Fadl's mother, the old woman of al-Harsh: "the noblest of people in kin-by-marriage" — Maymuna was the Prophet's wife; al-Abbas married her sister Lubaba; Hamza married her sister Salma; and Ja'far ibn Abi Talib married her sister Asma', whom Abu Bakr al-Siddiq then married, then Ali {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2027}.

## Her dream and her nursing of al-Hasan
Ibn Sa'd narrates with a good chain from Simak ibn Harb that Umm al-Fadl said: "Messenger of God, I saw that a limb of your limbs was in my house" — he said: "Fatima will bear a boy and you will nurse him with Quthm's milk" — so she bore [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|Hasan]] and Umm al-Fadl took him. In the account he urinated on the Prophet and she pinched him so he cried; he said: "you have hurt me in my son", then called for water {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2026}. Qabus ibn al-Mukharriq's account is similar, adding that she nursed him until he was grown; the Prophet brought him, sat him on his lap and he urinated, so she struck him between the shoulders — he said: "you have pained my son, God have mercy on you" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2027}.

## The day of Arafa
In the Sahih: the people doubted whether the Prophet was fasting on the day of Arafa, so Umm al-Fadl sent him a cup of milk and he drank at the standing-place — and they knew he was not fasting {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2027}.

## Her lineage, merit and death
Abu Umar said she was among the blessed childbearing women and the Prophet used to visit her; Ibn Hibban said she died in Uthman's caliphate before her husband al-Abbas {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2027}. From her related her sons Abdullah and Tamam, her client Umayr ibn al-Harith, her son's client Kurayb, Abdullah ibn Abbas and Abdullah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2026}.
