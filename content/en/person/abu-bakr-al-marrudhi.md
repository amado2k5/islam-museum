## Overview
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hajjaj, Abu Bakr al-Marrudhi, was a scholar of fiqh and hadith and one of the most eminent companions of Imam [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]]. Al-Zirikli gives his death in Baghdad in 275 AH, 888 CE, with no year of birth, and says his name points to [[place/marw-al-rudh|Marw al-Rudh]] in Khurasan {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 205}. Al-Safadi writes his name as al-Marwazi {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 7 p. 256}.

## His life
Al-Zirikli says he was among Imam Ahmad's most eminent companions and was devoted to his service; the Imam took comfort in him and told him that whatever he said was on the Imam's own tongue and that the Imam had said it. He transmitted many questions (masa'il) from Ahmad, is described as the author of many works, and died in Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 205}. Al-Safadi adds that his father was from Khwarazm and his mother from Marw, that he carried much learning from Ahmad and stayed with him until he died, and that he wrote on hadith, the sunna and fiqh. He was the man who closed Ahmad ibn Hanbal's eyes and washed his body. He died on the sixth of Jumada al-Ula in 275 AH and was buried beside Imam Ahmad {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 7 p. 256}.
