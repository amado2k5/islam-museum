## Summary
Mohammad bin Abdulkarim al-Issa is a Saudi scholar and former minister of justice who has been secretary-general of the Muslim World League (Rabita) since 2016, becoming the most travelled institutional spokesman of Saudi religious diplomacy — interfaith delegations, Holocaust commemoration visits and a discourse of moderation promoted by the kingdom. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-al-issa}

## Life and formation
Born in 1965 in Riyadh, he studied Sharia at Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University, earning master's and doctoral degrees, and worked as a judge and in the ministry of justice, rising to vice-minister before King Abdullah appointed him minister of justice in 2009 — a post he held until 2015. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-al-issa}

## Muslim World League
Taking over the Mecca-based league in August 2016, he rebranded it around "moderate Islam": memoranda with the Vatican and World Council of Churches, a 2019 "Charter of Mecca" on pluralism signed by scholars of multiple traditions, joint prayers with Christian and Jewish leaders, and a 2020 visit to Auschwitz — the first by a senior Saudi religious official — that drew wide attention. {cite:wikipedia-mohammad-al-issa}

## Assessments
Supporters describe him as the most effective public face of a Saudi effort to project tolerant Islam, citing unprecedented interfaith gestures; critics note that the league's moderation discourse tracks official Saudi policy and that its tolerance is projected abroad more than debated at home.
