## Summary
Badruddin Ajmal is an Indian businessman, theologian and politician who combined three fields rarely held by one person: the perfume empire he built between India and the Gulf, the Deobandi religious formation that made him a reference for the Muslims of Assam, and parliamentary politics led by a party he founded himself. He is counted among the wealthiest Indian Muslims and among the largest funders of Islamic education and charity. {cite:wikipedia-badruddin-ajmal}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1956 in Hojai, Assam, into a family of modest agricultural background, studied the Islamic sciences at Darul Uloom Deoband, the most prestigious Deobandi seminary in India, then moved as a young man to Mumbai where he entered the agarwood and perfume trade with his brothers in the 1950s-70s generation of the family business. {cite:wikipedia-badruddin-ajmal}

## The Ajmal perfume empire
From a small Mumbai shop the family venture grew into Ajmal Perfumes, one of the best-known attar and oudh houses in the world, with markets stretching from India to the Gulf states, the Middle East and Europe, and factories and agarwood plantations in Assam and abroad. The company is among the few family concerns to turn the traditional oudh trade into a global brand, and he is personally credited with leading its Gulf expansion. {cite:wikipedia-badruddin-ajmal} {cite:muslim500-badruddin-ajmal}

## Politics and public work
In 2005 he founded the All India United Democratic Front (AIUDF) in Assam, declaring the defence of Muslim minority interests and the marginalised riverine belt, and led it to become a parliamentary force in the Assam assembly and in the union parliament; he has been elected to the Lok Sabha for the Dhubri constituency. His name is also tied to the Ajmal Foundation, which runs dozens of religious and secular schools, colleges and hospitals in Assam and beyond, funded by a large share of company profits. {cite:wikipedia-badruddin-ajmal}

## Positions and debate
He is described in media as one of the most prominent Muslim voices in Indian politics, and draws criticism from political opponents who accuse his party of communal politics, and from secular parties competing with him for Muslim votes; some of his press statements have caused national controversy and are documented in Indian reporting. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-badruddin-ajmal}

## Legacy
He created a singular model in contemporary India: perfume wealth funding a wide educational and charitable network, and Deobandi religious leadership converted into an influential regional party — making him to supporters a symbol of Assam's Muslim political representation, and to critics an example of identity politics; in either reading he remains one of the most present figures of the Indian Muslim scene. {cite:wikipedia-badruddin-ajmal}
