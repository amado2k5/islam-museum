## Summary
Nur al-Din Mulla Ali ibn Sultan Muhammad al-Qari of Herat, later of Mecca, a Hanafi with the kunya Abu al-Hasan, was a hadith scholar, jurist, exegete and Qur'an reciter; born in [[place/herat|Herat]], he lived in [[place/mecca|Mecca]] for more than forty years and died there in 1014 AH, as Lu'lu'a al-Qwayfili sets out in the study opening her edition of [[work/jam-wasail-shamail|Jam' al-Wasa'il fi Sharh al-Shama'il]]. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.25} {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.28} {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.36}

## Name and nisbas
This is the name he gave himself in his works. "Al-Qari" softens "al-qari'" (reciter), a title he earned by his skill in the Qur'anic readings, which he pursued alongside the other sciences; "al-Harawi" refers to Herat, one of the chief cities of Khurasan, where he was born, raised and began his studies; and "al-Makki" to his living beside the Ka'ba for over forty years and making Mecca his home. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.25}

## Early life and move to Mecca
His date of birth is unknown, as he wrote no autobiography. In Herat — then capital of the Timurid state and a centre of civilisation — he learned the Qur'an by heart with its recitation and the basics of the sciences, then travelled to Mecca to settle; the date is not recorded, but the editor places his arrival between 952 and 973 AH, since he studied with Ibn Hajar al-Haytami, the earliest of his teachers to die. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.28}

## Teachers and students
His teachers included [[person/ibn-hajar-al-haytami|Ibn Hajar al-Haytami]], [[person/ali-al-muttaqi-hindi|Ali al-Muttaqi al-Hindi]], Muhammad al-Hindi Qutb al-Din, the Meccan Shafi'i exegete Atiyya ibn Ali al-Sulami (d. 982 AH) and the Egyptian astronomer-timekeeper Shihab al-Din Ahmad ibn Badr al-Din al-Abbasi (d. 992 AH). {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.29} Among his students were [[person/abd-al-qadir-al-tabari-makki|Abd al-Qadir ibn Muhammad al-Tabari al-Makki]] (d. 1032 AH), the Meccan Hanafi Abd al-Rahman ibn Isa al-Murshidi (killed 1037 AH) and the Meccan Hanafi Muhammad ibn Farrukh (d. 1061 AH). {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.29} {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.30}

## Standing and criticisms
The editor quotes al-Isami's praise of him as "combining the rational and transmitted sciences, steeped in the Prophetic Sunna", al-Muhibbi's "one of the foremost in learning, unique in his age", al-Zirikli, the dictionaries of printed books and of authors, and *al-Ta'liqat al-Saniyya*, which says he reached "the rank of renewer at the turn of the millennium". {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.31} {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.32} She notes criticisms: a fondness for figurative interpretation and leniency in grading hadith; objections to some imams, notably the Shafi'is, and to Malik on leaving the arms unclasped in prayer; and the attribution to him of the view that the Prophet's parents died as unbelievers — which she answers as the result of a copyist's error in the copy of *al-Fiqh al-Akbar* he used. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.33} In her assessment he agreed with the early generations in holding the divine names to be fixed by revelation and not limited in number, agreed with the Maturidis in counting eight essential attributes, and had a Sufi leaning. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.27}

## Works
She says he wrote prolifically, most of all late in life, as author, commentator and abridger; she cites Qutlay's count of 148 known works and another estimate of over 125, from works of more than ten volumes to short treatises. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.32} {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.34} They include six works on the sira and the Prophet's qualities, among them *Zubdat al-Shama'il* and *Jam' al-Wasa'il*; commentaries such as *Sharh Sharh Nukhbat al-Fikar*, a commentary on *al-Shifa* and [[work/mirqat-al-mafatih|Mirqat al-Mafatih, on Mishkat al-Masabih]]; in creed, a commentary on *al-Fiqh al-Akbar*; in exegesis, *Anwar al-Qur'an wa-Asrar al-Furqan* and a gloss on the Jalalayn; in recitation, *al-Minah al-Fikriyya* on Ibn al-Jazari's *Muqaddima*; in biography, *Manaqib al-Imam al-A'zam*; and in devotions, *al-Hizb al-A'zam* and *al-Hirz al-Thamin*. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.34} {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.35}

## Death
He died in Mecca in Shawwal 1014 AH and was buried in the al-Ma'la cemetery; al-Laknawi insists the year is 1014, not 1016, 1044 or 1010. When the news reached the scholars of Egypt, they prayed the funeral prayer in absentia for him in al-Azhar in a vast gathering. {cite:jam-wasail-thesis-qwayfili p.36}

## For young visitors
Mulla Ali al-Qari was a scholar born in Herat, in Afghanistan, who lived in Mecca for more than forty years and wrote over a hundred books, including a commentary on a book about the Prophet's qualities.
