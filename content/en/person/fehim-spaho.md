Fehim Spaho (13 February 1877 – 14 February 1942) was a Bosnian scholar, writer and translator from Sarajevo, the elder brother of Mehmed Spaho, the leader of the Yugoslav Muslim Organization.{cite:wikipedia-fehim-spaho-hr}{cite:wikipedia-fehim-spaho-en} Trained at Sarajevo's Sharia Judges' School, he passed the qadi examination in 1897, served as a qadi and government official, headed the Ministry of Faiths in Belgrade from 1919 to 1920, and from 1936 presided over the Supreme Sharia Court.{cite:wikipedia-fehim-spaho-bs}

Appointed reis-ul-ulema — Grand Mufti of Yugoslavia — by royal decree on 26 April 1938 and inaugurated at Sarajevo's Emperor's Mosque on 9 June, he held the office through the establishment of the Independent State of Croatia until his death, interceding for threatened Orthodox churchmen and signing his name at the head of the Sarajevo Resolution's list when its signatories were threatened.{cite:wikipedia-fehim-spaho-en}{cite:wikipedia-fehim-spaho-bs} Alongside his official career he wrote studies of Ottoman documents and Bosnian religious life and translated prolifically from Turkish, Arabic and Persian, including the Thousand and One Nights and several Turkish novels.{cite:wikipedia-fehim-spaho-en}{cite:wikipedia-fehim-spaho-bs}
