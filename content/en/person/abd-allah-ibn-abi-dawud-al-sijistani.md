## Summary
Abu Bakr Abd Allah ibn Sulayman ibn al-Ash'ath al-Sijistani, son of the imam [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]], author of the Sunan, is introduced by [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]] as "the trustworthy hafiz, author of works". {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 42} He says he was one of the great hafizes and leading figures, that he was born in 230 AH, and that his father took him travelling, so that he met the great masters and heard Isa ibn Hammad, the companion of al-Layth ibn Sa'd, and men of his generation. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 43} [[person/ibn-al-nadim|Ibn al-Nadim]] counts him among the eminent hadith scholars and jurists, trustworthy, and gives his death as 316 AH. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 324-325}

## His memory
Al-Dhahabi quotes [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib]]: the hafiz Abu Hashim al-Khallal said that Abu Bakr had a better memory than his father Abu Dawud. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 43} He relates from Abu Bakr ibn Shadhan that Ibn Abi Dawud went to Sijistan, where people asked him to narrate; he said he had no book with him, and dictated to them thirty thousand hadiths from memory; and he relates from Abu Ali al-Naysaburi a report of his narrating tens of thousands of hadiths from memory in Isfahan. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 43}

## His works
Ibn al-Nadim attributes to him a Tafsir, which he composed when [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]] composed his, al-Masabih fi al-Hadith, al-Masahif, Nazm al-Qur'an, Fada'il al-Qur'an, Shari'at al-Tafsir, al-Nasikh wa-l-Mansukh and al-Ba'th wa-l-Nushur. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 324-325}

## What was said of him
Al-Dhahabi includes him in the Mizan in order to set out what was said against him and then answer it; the entry gives two positions among Sunni critics:

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: a trustworthy, accepted hafiz (al-Dhahabi, al-Daraqutni, Ibn Adi)**: al-Daraqutni said: trustworthy, except that he often errs when speaking on hadith; Ibn Adi said: known for his pursuit of learning, most of what he wrote he wrote alongside his father, and he is accepted by the hadith scholars; al-Dhahabi calls him the trustworthy hafiz. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 42}
- **Sunni: his father's word and those who followed it (Abu Dawud, Ibn Sa'id, Ibrahim al-Isbahani)**: Ibn Adi relates with his chain from Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn al-Junayd that he heard Abu Dawud say: my son Abd Allah is a liar, and that Ibn Sa'id said: what his father said about him is enough for us; he also relates that Ibrahim al-Isbahani called him a liar. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 42}
:::

Al-Dhahabi relates that he was nearly executed in Isfahan over a report he narrated from al-Zuhri from Urwa concerning Ali — a report al-Dhahabi notes he did not give with a full chain, so that it is broken — and that Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Hafs al-Hamadhani rescued him from Abu Layla, the governor of Isfahan. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 42} Ibn Adi relates that early on he was linked to some hostility towards Ali (nasb), so [[person/ali-ibn-al-furat|Ibn al-Furat]] banished him from Baghdad; then [[person/ali-ibn-isa|Ali ibn Isa]] brought him back, and he narrated and made known the merits of Ali; he is quoted as saying: I forgive everyone except whoever accuses me of hating Ali. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 42}

## His quarrels with Ibn Sa'id and Ibn Jarir
Al-Dhahabi says he was strong-willed, and that there was enmity between him and [[person/yahya-ibn-muhammad-ibn-saaid|Ibn Sa'id]] and Ibn Jarir. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 42} He and [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] relate that the vizier Ali ibn Isa brought him and Ibn Sa'id together to reconcile them, in the presence of the judge Abu Umar; the vizier called him "a false shaykh", and he answered: the false shaykh is the one who lies about the Messenger of God; then he said he would never take anything from the vizier's hand, and when [[person/al-muqtadir-billah|al-Muqtadir]] heard of it he began weighing his stipend with his own hand and sending it on a platter by a servant. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 42-43} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 595} Al-Dhahabi adds that Ibn Abi Dawud and his followers, who were very many, rose against Ibn Jarir and accused him of the innovation concerning the "utterance" (lafz) of the Qur'an, and that Ibn Jarir wrote a sound creed disowning what was said of him and was pained by it. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2 p. 43}
