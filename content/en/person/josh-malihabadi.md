# Josh Malihabadi

**Josh Malihabadi** (5 December 1898 – 22 February 1982), born **Shabbir Hasan Khan**, was a leading Urdu poet of the subcontinent, titled **Shayar-e-Inqalab** (Poet of the Revolution) for his poem 'Hussain aur Inquilab'.{cite:wikipedia-josh-malihabadi-en}{cite:wikipedia-josh-malihabadi-ar}

## Early life and formation

Josh was born at Malihabad near Lucknow to an Urdu-speaking Muslim family of Afridi Pashtun origin. He began composing poetry at eleven, studied at Lucknow and at Rabindranath Tagore's Visva-Bharati, and entered the service of Hyderabad State as a translator.{cite:wikipedia-josh-malihabadi-en}

## Exile and Aaj-Kal

Exiled from Hyderabad State in 1925 over a poem against the Nizam, Josh moved to Delhi and founded the magazine Kaleem. In the 1940s he edited the government Publications Division's magazine Aaj-Kal, gathering Delhi's Urdu literary circle around it, and left over its pro-British wartime line.{cite:wikipedia-josh-malihabadi-en}

## Migration and legacy

Josh migrated to Pakistan in 1956 despite Jawaharlal Nehru's opposition, worked at the Board for the Advancement of Literature in Lahore and edited the daily Jang; Pakistan awarded him the Hilal-i-Imtiaz posthumously in 2013. He died at Islamabad on 22 February 1982.{cite:wikipedia-josh-malihabadi-en}{cite:wikidata-josh-malihabadi}

## A note on affiliation

No edition of Josh's articles names a branch or school in its text: the English article gives his Muslim family of Afridi origin, and the Shia reading stands only in Wikidata's single structured value and in the Urdu article's 'Pakistani Shia' category, a filing. His Husayn elegies are treated by the articles as literary works.{cite:wikipedia-josh-malihabadi-en}{cite:wikipedia-josh-malihabadi-ur}{cite:wikidata-josh-malihabadi}
