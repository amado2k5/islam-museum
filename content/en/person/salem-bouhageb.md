Salem Bouhageb (1827–1924) was a Tunisian reformer, jurisconsult and poet, born at Bembla, near Monastir, and considered one of the first Tunisian reformers.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr}{cite:wikidata-salem-bouhageb} He settled at Tunis in 1852 and followed the teaching of great imams such as Sidi Brahim Riahi and of shaykhs al-Islam such as Mohamed Belkhodja, Mohamed Béchir Ennaifer and Mohamed Bayram IV, obtaining ijazas at the Zaytuna in Arabic language and grammar, linguistics, literature, jurisprudence, theology, history and religions, and also studying logic, philosophy and mysticism; at the end of 1860 he obtained the title of alim.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr} He then taught in his turn, above all at the Zaytuna and the Khaldounia for some seventy years, influencing numerous disciples: the nationalists Béchir Sfar, Abdelaziz Thâalbi and Ali Bach Hamba, the intellectuals Mohamed Nakhli and Mahmoud Messadi, and the scholars Mohamed Snoussi and Mohamed Tahar Ben Achour.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr}{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-en}

Bouhageb was a disciple of Kheireddine Pacha, who in 1857 chose him to take part in creating the municipality of Tunis, charged with preparing the files of its administrative committee; he became the municipal council's chief secretary in 1858 and sat on reform commissions for the civil service, regional administration, justice and teaching.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr} He travelled to Italy in 1864 in Kheireddine's official delegation and to Constantinople in 1871, took part in writing Kheireddine's celebrated book on the surest way to know the state of nations, and in 1875 helped create Sadiki College, proclaiming at its inauguration that Islam is not incompatible with science and modernity.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr} As a member of the judicial councils he was charged with the application of the Fundamental Pact in 1901 and the promulgation of the Code of Obligations and Contracts in 1906.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr}

Named imam of the Soubhan Allah mosque at Bab Souika in 1884, Bouhageb was the first to renew the content of the Friday sermon, treating the people's social problems from the pulpit, and crowds pressed to hear him.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-ar}{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-ur} On 15 May 1897 he pronounced the inaugural discourse of the Khaldounia, an exegesis in which he showed that God made science the means of Adam's succession on earth, and he taught there for years afterwards.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr} He took the muftiate in 1905, on the Arabic biography's dating, and the headship of the Maliki shura council in 1919, rising to chief Maliki mufti of the country; his Friday sermons were published in 1913.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-ar}{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr} He died at La Marsa on 14 July 1924 and was buried at the Djellaz cemetery after national funerals held the next day in the presence of the bey of Tunis.{cite:wikipedia-salem-bouhageb-fr}{cite:wikidata-salem-bouhageb}
