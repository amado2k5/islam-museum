Abu Jabir Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Haram al-Sulami al-Khazraji, chief of Banu Salama of the Ansar and father of the notable Companion Jabir ibn Abdullah. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He came to Mecca in the pilgrimage season with his people's delegation; the Muslim elders of the Ansar called him to Islam before the great Pledge of al-Aqaba and told him of the Prophet's affair, so he became Muslim, witnessed the second Pledge of al-Aqaba, and the Prophet chose him as one of the twelve leaders. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He witnessed Badr, then was martyred on the day of Uhud — the first Muslim casualty in the battle. Of his merit it is told that God gave him life and spoke to him face to face with no veil between, saying: "my servant, ask of me and I will give you"; he said: Lord, give me life again to be killed for You a second time. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy relates that Abdullah ibn Amr al-Ansari, the father of Jabir, spoke to his son on the day of battle about going before him; after he was martyred the Prophet comforted Jabir that God had never spoken to anyone except from behind a veil save his father, to whom He spoke face to face, and that he had wished to be sent back to the world to be killed again, and was told that it had already been decreed that the dead do not return to it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep05 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep05 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep05 00:08:30}

## In Rijal Hawl al-Rasul
[[person/khalid-muhammad-khalid|Khalid Muhammad Khalid]] heads his chapter on him in [[work/rijal-hawl-al-rasul|Rijal Hawl al-Rasul]] "the shade of the angels," recording that he was one of the seventy who gave the second pledge of Aqaba and that the Prophet made him naqib over his people of Banu Sakuma. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.479} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.480}

He recounts that he foresaw his death before the battle of Uhud and summoned his son [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abdullah]], charging him to settle his debt and look after his brothers, saying: by God I leave no one after me dearer to me than you after God's Messenger. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.480} When the battle came he fought a farewell fight and died a martyr; Jabir stood weeping over him and the Prophet came and comforted him: weep or do not weep — the angels shade him with their wings. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.481}

The book relates that the Prophet told Jabir that God had spoken to His servant face to face with no veil — "my servant, ask of me and I will give you" — and he asked to be returned to the world to be killed a second time, and was told it had already been decreed that they do not return to it; God then sent down the verse on the honour of the martyrs with which the book closes the chapter, holding that those killed in God's path are not dead but alive with their Lord, provided for. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.481} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.482}

It records the Prophet's order that Abdullah ibn Amr and Amr ibn al-Jamuh be buried in one grave, "for they were two loving, kindred friends in this world," and that Jabir, forty-six years later when Mu'awiya channelled a spring into the cemetery ground and the martyrs' remains were moved, found the two bodies as if asleep, untouched by the earth. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.482} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.489}
