## Summary
Badr al-Jamali, amir al-juyush; an Armenian by birth bought by Jamal al-Dawla Ibn 'Ammar, raised as his slave and advanced by his master; al-Mustansir of Egypt appointed him his deputy at Tyre — some say Acre. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His coming and monuments
When al-Mustansir's state weakened he summoned him; he braved the winter sea and reached Cairo on the eve of Wednesday, two nights left of Jumada II 466, was given the direction of affairs — and the state stood upright at his coming. He was vizier of sword and pen, over the chief judgeship and the da'is; he built the mosque at the Alexandrian port in the perfumers' market and the Mashhad al-Ra's at Ascalon. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## The walls of Cairo in Yeomans
Yeomans writes that Badr was an Armenian slave who rose to govern Damascus and Acre, and that the caliph al-Mustansir secretly invited him in 1073 to restore order after years of plague and famine had left Egypt in anarchy and the caliph a puppet of his Turkish and Berber troops. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.46} Arriving with his Armenian and Syrian bodyguard, he rounded up and executed the leading palace officials and Turkish commanders in a single night, took control of the army, the mission and the bureaucracy, and became Egypt's de facto ruler. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.46}

Cairo's mud-brick wall had crumbled by 1046, and Badr rebuilt it in brick and stone between 1087 and 1092; Yeomans sees his building programme as one with his reconstruction of the country. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.46} His work includes [[building/bab-al-nasr|Bab al-Nasr]], [[building/bab-al-futuh|Bab al-Futuh]] and [[building/bab-zuwayla|Bab Zuwayla]], which al-Maqrizi says were built by three Armenians from Edessa; he and al-Hakim before him added the corner salients that folded the [[building/mosque-of-al-hakim|Mosque of al-Hakim]] into the fortifications. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.46} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.60}
