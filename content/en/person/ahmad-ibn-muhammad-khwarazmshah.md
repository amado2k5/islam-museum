## Summary
Ahmad ibn Muhammad, grandson of Iraq ibn Mansur, was one of the Afrighid Khwarazmshahs. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 48} Shohruxmirzo Ismoilov says his dirham of 959 is the first Islamic-type silver dirham struck by a ruler of Khwarazm, the first Khwarazmshah coin with Qur'anic verses, and the first coin to bear the title "Khwarazmshah" in Arabic. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 10}

## The single dirham
It was struck at Khwarazm in 348 AH (959 or 960), with the word "three hundred" left out of the date. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 48} The obverse carries the profession of God's oneness, with a verse from [[passage/quran-30-4|al-Rum 30:4]] in the outer margin; the reverse reads "To God; Muhammad is the Messenger of God; Ahmad ibn Muhammad Khwarazmshah", with the wording of [[passage/quran-9-33|al-Tawba 9:33]] in its margin. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 48} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 49} The Swedish History Museum holds two specimens, ShM 9136 (3.85 g, 30 mm) and ShM 16840 (3.28 g, 29 mm). {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 49}

## His fulus
All his other coins are copper fulus, repeating the al-Rum verse and alternating the titles "shah" and "Khwarazmshah", weighing about 2 g. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 49} The latest is dated 367/977, or 366/976 according to M. Fedorov. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 49} Among the rarest is a type showing a cat-like animal facing left, which the author compares with the coins of the Tudun dynasty of Chach in the sixth and seventh centuries. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 50}

## Under the Samanids
The author holds that Ahmad ibn Muhammad led a revolt against the Samanids before 943 and another in 959, the year to which the single silver dirham belongs, while dependence on the Samanids continued. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 51}
