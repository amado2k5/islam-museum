## Summary
Muhsin Yazıcıoğlu (31 December 1954 – 25 March 2009) was a Turkish politician and member of the Parliament of Turkey. He was the leader and founder of the Great Unity Party (BBP), a right-wing nationalist-Islamist political party. His name remained prominent in Turkish public life because of the circumstances of his death in a helicopter crash in 2009, which stayed disputed and under investigation. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

## Early life and education
Yazıcıoğlu was born in 1954 in a small village named Elmalı in Şarkışla, in Sivas Province. He studied in Şarkışla from primary school to high school, and was then educated at the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine of Ankara University. He was a member of the ultranationalist Grey Wolves. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

After the 1980 Turkish coup d'état, Yazıcıoğlu was arrested for being one of the leaders of the Grey Wolves. He stayed for seven years in prison until his release in 1987. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

## Political career
In the following years, Yazıcıoğlu was involved in politics as a member of the far-right Nationalist Movement Party. In 1991, he and some of his fellows left this movement and established a new political party called the Great Unity Party, with a more Islamist ideology. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

His party has been represented in the Parliament only via electoral coalitions with popular parties. At the 2002 legislative elections, the party won 1.1% of the popular vote and no seats; in the 2007 elections Muhsin Yazıcıoğlu was elected as an independent. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

## Death in the helicopter crash
Yazıcıoğlu died on 25 March 2009 in a helicopter crash described as suspicious, in the southern Turkish province of Kahramanmaraş, after a political rally there, on the way to the next rally in Yozgat, just four days before the local elections. The pilot and three other passengers also died in the accident. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

After the crash, İsmail Güneş, who was one of the passengers, called the Turkish emergency service number 112 and was able to talk to the dispatcher clearly. Güneş made several calls to emergency services, but the helicopter wreck was not found in time. Later, the officials involved were investigated and charged with several crimes including negligence of duty, with the lack of and delayed response being blamed on the Gülen movement having infiltrated government institutions, as the article reports it. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

Yazıcıoğlu received a state funeral to which most of the party leaders attended. Thousands of people joined his funeral ceremony in Kocatepe Mosque in Ankara. After his death, in the 2009 local elections the BBP's candidate was elected as the new mayor of Sivas. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section should be read as a presentation of differing positions reported in the sources, not as the museum's verdict on them.

The article describes him as the leader of a right-wing nationalist party with an Islamist ideology, formed out of the Grey Wolves and Nationalist Movement Party milieu, from which he split in order to build a more Islamist discourse. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en} The largest controversy around his name concerns not his discourse but his death: the article calls the helicopter crash "suspicious", reports the investigations and charges against officials over the delayed rescue of the passengers, and reports the attribution of that delay to the Gülen movement's infiltration of state institutions as presented in the investigations, without the article settling the cause of the crash. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}

## Influence and audience
The Great Unity Party he founded remained present in Turkish politics after his death, and his name stayed tied in Turkish public memory to the case of his death and to demands that its circumstances be uncovered, which kept the case alive in the media and in investigations for years. {cite:wikipedia-muhsin-yazicioglu-en}
