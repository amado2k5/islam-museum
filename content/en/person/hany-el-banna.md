## Summary
Hany El-Banna is an Egyptian-British physician who co-founded Islamic Relief Worldwide in 1984 from a first donation of twenty pence and, over four decades, grew it into the largest Western-based Muslim relief and development organisation, working in more than forty countries. He is counted among the architects of modern institutional Muslim humanitarianism. {cite:wikipedia-hany-el-banna}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1950 in Egypt, studied medicine at Cairo University, then moved to Britain to specialise in endocrinology, settling in Birmingham as a practising physician. {cite:wikipedia-hany-el-banna}

## Founding Islamic Relief
In 1984, while a medical trainee in Britain, he and fellow students collected a first symbolic donation of twenty pence in response to the famine in Sudan and East Africa; from that gesture Islamic Relief was born, headquartered in Birmingham. He led it as president and international president until 2014, expanding it from seasonal fundraising into emergency relief, sustainable development, orphan sponsorship and water, health and education programmes, with partnerships including UN agencies and the European Union. {cite:wikipedia-hany-el-banna} {cite:muslim500-hany-el-banna}

## After Islamic Relief
After retiring from the presidency he founded a humanitarian leadership centre and the World Congress of Muslim Philanthropists, sat on advisory boards of international humanitarian organisations, and wrote memoirs of the experience of building the organisation. {cite:wikipedia-hany-el-banna}

## Scrutiny and transparency
The organisation he founded faced in later years political and media scrutiny in some countries over funding sources and alleged associations; Islamic Relief denied the accusations and underwent independent audits that preserved its international partnerships, and El-Banna has said in statements that building trust was a permanent challenge. The museum reports these matters attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-hany-el-banna}

## Recognition and legacy
He was appointed OBE for services to humanitarian causes and has received Arab and international humanitarian awards. His legacy is bound to a single institution: without the twenty-pence donation and his persistence there would be no British platform of Islamic relief serving millions yearly, and his model is taught in the management of faith-based non-profits. {cite:wikipedia-hany-el-banna}
