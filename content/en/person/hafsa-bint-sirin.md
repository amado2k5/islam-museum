## Summary
Hafsa bint Sirin, Umm al-Hudhayl, was a devout woman of [[place/basra|Basra]] and sister of [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Muhammad ibn Sirin]]; Ibn al-Jawzi gives her entry among the women ascetics of Basra in [[work/sifat-al-safwa|Sifat al-Safwa]] (no. 585), taking most of her reports from Hisham ibn Hassan. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706} She had read the Qur'an by the age of twelve and died at ninety. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706}

## Her prayer room and devotion
She would enter the prayer room of her house and pray the five prayers there, staying until the sun was high and she had prayed, then come out for her ablution and her sleep, returning when the next prayer came; Mahdi ibn Maymun said she stayed in her prayer room for thirty years, leaving only for a need or a midday rest. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706} She is reported to have read half the Qur'an each night and to have fasted continually except on the two Eids and the days of Tashriq, and to have kept her shroud ready, wearing it when she entered ihram for the Hajj and in the last ten nights of Ramadan. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.707} Hisham said: "I saw al-Hasan and Ibn Sirin, and I never saw anyone I thought more intelligent than Hafsa." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.707}

## Her knowledge of the readings
From Hisham: when a point of Qur'anic reading puzzled Ibn Sirin he would say, "Go and ask Hafsa how she reads it." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706} From Asim al-Ahwal: they would visit her when she had veiled her face with her outer garment, and remind her of the allowance for older women in [[passage/quran-24-60|al-Nur 60]]; she would ask what followed, they would say that restraint is better for them, and she would answer, "That is keeping the outer garment." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706} She used to tell young people: "Take from yourselves while you are young, for I have seen that deeds are done only in youth." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706}

## Her son al-Hudhayl
From Hisham: her son al-Hudhayl gathered firewood in summer and peeled it, and split reeds, and in winter set the brazier behind her in her prayer room and burned them in a fire whose smoke did no harm and kept her warm; he sent her milk from his camel each morning, and when she said she was fasting he said, "The best milk is what has spent the night in the camel's udder — give it to whom you like." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.707} When he died she bore it patiently, yet felt a lump in her throat that would not go, until one night she read in Surat al-Nahl [[passage/quran-16-96|"what is with you runs out, and what is with God remains"]], repeated it, and God took away what she felt. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.706}

## Other reports
From Umm Sulaym bint Sirin: Hafsa's room was sometimes lit for her; from Hisham: she lit her lamp at night and stood in her prayer room, and sometimes the lamp went out and the room stayed lit for her until morning. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.707} After her Ibn al-Jawzi lists her sister Karima bint Sirin, who stayed fifteen years in her prayer room, leaving it only for a need. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.707}

## For young visitors
Hafsa was the sister of the scholar Ibn Sirin. She knew the Qur'an by twelve, her brother sent people to ask her how to read it, and her son lit a fire behind her in winter to keep her warm while she prayed.
