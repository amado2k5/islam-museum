## Summary
Shavkat Miromonovich Mirziyoyev has been president of Uzbekistan since December 2016, when he succeeded Islam Karimov after thirteen years as his prime minister. His presidency is associated with a programme of economic liberalisation and a partial opening in religious affairs presented under the slogan of a "New Uzbekistan". {cite:wikipedia-shavkat-mirziyoyev}

## Life and career
He was born on 24 July 1957 in the Zaamin district of Jizzakh region, trained as a mechanical engineer at the Tashkent Institute of Engineers of Irrigation and Agricultural Mechanisation, and worked as an academic before entering administration. He served as district and regional governor (hokim) in Jizzakh and Samarkand, and was appointed prime minister in 2003. {cite:wikipedia-shavkat-mirziyoyev}

## Presidency
After Karimov's death in September 2016, Mirziyoyev became acting president and won the December 2016 election; he was re-elected in 2021 and, after a constitutional referendum resetting term limits, again in 2023. His government liberalised the currency in 2017, eased visa and media restrictions, released a number of religious and political prisoners, reopened cooperation with neighbours, and created new religious-education institutions while keeping the state security apparatus largely intact. Observers describe the changes as real but managed from above; the museum reports this characterisation attributed. {cite:wikipedia-shavkat-mirziyoyev}

## Islam and the record
Mirziyoyev's government has restored and promoted Islamic heritage — new complexes at Samarkand, Tashkent's Islamic Civilisation Centre, state-backed imam training — while retaining tight registration of religious activity. Reference sources list him as a Sunni Muslim. The Muslim 500 has placed him among the most influential Muslims and named him its Man of the Year in an earlier edition for his reform programme. {cite:wikipedia-shavkat-mirziyoyev}
