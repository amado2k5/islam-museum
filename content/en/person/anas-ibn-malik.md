## Summary
[[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]] ibn al-Nadr al-Ansari al-Khazraji, Abu Hamza, "the Messenger of God's servant and one of the most copious transmitters from him" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 100}.

## Serving the Prophet
It is sound from him that he said: "the Prophet came to Medina when I was ten", that his mother [[person/umm-sulaym|Umm Sulaym]] brought him to the Prophet on his arrival saying "this is Anas, a boy to serve you", and he accepted him; the Prophet gave him the kunya "Abu Hamza" after a herb he used to shun, and jested with him saying "O you of the two ears" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 100}. Al-Tirmidhi relates from Abu al-Aliya, when Abu Khalda asked him did Anas hear from the Prophet: "he served him ten years, and the Prophet prayed for him" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 100}.

## Badr
Muhammad ibn Abdullah al-Ansari relates that Anas went out with the Prophet to [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] as a boy serving him, and said to one who asked: "and where would I miss Badr, may you have no mother?" Ibn Hajar remarks that they did not list him among the men of Badr because he was not of fighting age {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 100}.

## The prayer for him
The accounts tell that Umm Sulaym said: "Messenger of God, pray God for Anas" — he said: "O God, multiply his wealth and children and bless him in them." Anas said: "I buried of my own loins, besides my grandchildren, one hundred twenty-five; and my land bears fruit twice a year" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 101}. In Ja'far ibn Sulayman's account from Thabit: "and admit him to Paradise" — Anas said: "two I have seen, and the third I hope for" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 101}.

## After the Prophet
He stayed in Medina after the Prophet, witnessed the conquests, then settled in [[place/basra|Basra]] and died there {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 101}. Ali ibn al-Madini said he was the last Companion to die in Basra {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 101}. Bukhari said from Musa ibn Anas that he campaigned with the Prophet on eight expeditions {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 101}.

## Relics of the Prophet
Ibn al-Sakan relates from Thabit that Anas said: "this is a hair of the Messenger of God's — lay it beneath my tongue", and was buried with it under his tongue {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 101}.

## His death
His year of death is disputed: Shu'ayb ibn al-Habhab said year 90; Sa'id ibn Ufayr, al-Haytham ibn Adi and Mu'tamar said year 91; Abdullah ibn Zayd al-Hudhali attended him in year 92 in Ibn Sa'd's account from al-Waqidi; Abu Nu'aym al-Kufi, al-Mada'ini and Khalifa said year 93, al-Mada'ini and Khalifa adding that he was one hundred three — and other views exist {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 101}.

## For Sprouts
A kind companion who served the Prophet in Medina as a youth for ten years, sharing stories of gentleness and care.

## For Explorers
Anas ibn Malik was an Ansari companion brought by his mother Umm Sulaym to serve the Prophet when he arrived in Medina as a ten-year-old boy. He lived with the Prophet for ten years, recalling that the Prophet never rebuked him or spoke harshly, praying for blessings in his family, wealth, and lifespan, becoming one of the major narrators of Hadith.

## For Scholars
Abu Hamza Anas ibn Malik (c. 10 BH – 93 AH / 612–712 CE), the dedicated companion who served the Prophet throughout the Medinan period and stands as the third most prolific narrator of Hadith after Abu Hurayra and Ibn Umar. Entrusted to the Prophet by his mother Umm Sulaym at age ten, he resided in close proximity to the household for ten years, faithfully preserving detailed observations of prophetic worship and domestic ethics. Blessed by the Prophet's prayer for abundance and long life, he settled in Basra, surviving as one of the last living companions and teaching seminal scholars including al-Hasan al-Basri and Ibn Sirin.
