## Overview
Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib, known as al-Nafs al-Zakiyya (the Pure Soul). He was the son of [[person/abdallah-ibn-al-hasan|Abdallah ibn al-Hasan]] and the brother of [[person/ibrahim-ibn-abdallah-al-hasani|Ibrahim ibn Abdallah]], and the leader of [[event/nafs-al-zakiyya-revolt|the revolt of al-Nafs al-Zakiyya]]. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424-425}

## The revolt and its end
Muhammad and his brother Ibrahim rose in 145 AH, and al-Mansur overcame them and killed them, together with a great many of the House of the Prophet. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424-425}

Al-Suyuti says that al-Mansur was the first to sow strife between the Abbasids and the Alids, when they had been one thing before. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424-425}

## The position of the scholars
Al-Suyuti says that al-Mansur harmed scholars who had gone out with them or had given legal opinions in favour of the revolt, among them Abu Hanifa, Abd al-Hamid ibn Ja'far and Ibn Ajlan. He says that Malik gave a legal opinion permitting going out with him, and said that the oath of allegiance had been given under compulsion, and that a compelled man has no oath. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424-425}

## After his death
His brothers and sons scattered through the lands calling to his imamate. His son Ali was killed in Egypt, his son Abdallah in Sind, and his son al-Hasan was imprisoned in Yemen, where he died in prison. His brother Musa went to al-Jazira, Yahya to al-Rayy and then Tabaristan, and Idris to the Maghrib. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.285}
