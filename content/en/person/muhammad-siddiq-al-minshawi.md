# Muhammad Siddiq al-Minshawi

## Summary

Muhammad Siddiq al-Minshawi (20 January 1920 – 20 June 1969) was an Egyptian Qur'an reciter born at al-Munsha'ah in Sohag, Upper Egypt, into a family that had practised recitation for generations. He was accepted as an Egyptian Radio reciter in 1953, recorded the complete Qur'an in the murattal style in the recitation of Hafs, and travelled widely in the Muslim world. His voice — hushed, exact and touched with sorrow — earned him the epithet al-Sawt al-Baki, "the Weeping Voice", and he is grouped with Abdul Basit Abd us-Samad, Mustafa Ismail and Mahmoud Khalil al-Hussary among the four great Egyptian reciters of the twentieth century.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-en}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-ar}

## Early life

He was born on 20 January 1920 — the Arabic article pairs the year with 1338 AH — at al-Munsha'ah in the Sohag Governorate. His father Siddiq al-Minshawi, his grandfather Tayb and his great-grandfather were all reciters; his brothers Ahmad and Mahmud also recited, and the family gave its name to a distinctive school of recitation, the Minshawi school. His father, himself a protégé of Muhammad Rifat and Muhammad Salamah, was the central influence on his art.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-ar}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-en}

He completed his memorisation of the Qur'an at the age of eight. In 1927 he travelled to Cairo with his uncle, the reciter Ahmad al-Sayyid, and memorised a quarter of the Qur'an there before returning to al-Munsha'ah, where he completed his memorisation and study under Muhammad al-Namki, Muhammad Abu al-'Ala and Rashwan Abu Muslim, who took no fee for teaching; the English article adds instruction in tajwid under other teachers, among them Ibrahim al-Su'udi.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-ar}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-en}

## Career

He began reciting in the 1930s, touring religious evenings with his father and uncle. His solo turning point came at an evening in Sohag in 1952, after which his name spread across Egypt. Radio officials, having heard of his Upper Egyptian performances, arranged recordings for him when he could not travel to Cairo, and he was officially accepted as a radio reciter in 1953; his broadcasts made him a fixture of Egyptian religious life, with a regular Wednesday-evening slot.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-en}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-ar}

His recorded legacy centres on the complete murattal Qur'an in the Hafs recitation: a khatma recorded in 1965 on eighty-two tapes, approved by a committee of senior scholars and reciters, alongside a mujawwad khatma for Egyptian Radio and a joint recording in the recitation of al-Duri with Kamil al-Bahtimi and Fu'ad al-'Arusi. The Arabic article reports that in June 2026 Egyptian Qur'an Radio began broadcasting a second murattal khatma of his, recorded in the 1960s and held in the archive since its approval in 1967: after hearing the 1965 recording he had asked to re-record thirty-two tapes he felt could be finer, offering to bear the review committee's costs himself.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-ar}

He recited in the great mosques of the Muslim world — the Sacred Mosque in Mecca, the Prophet's Mosque in Medina and al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem — and travelled to Iraq, Indonesia, Syria, Kuwait, Libya, Palestine and Saudi Arabia, receiving honours from Indonesia, Syria, Lebanon and Pakistan. Muhammad al-Sha'rawi is quoted in the Arabic article ranking him with his four companions — al-Hussary, Kamil al-Bahtimi, Abdul Basit and Mahmud Ali al-Banna — as sailors on a ship in the sea of the Qur'an that will not stop sailing.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-ar}

## Personal life and death

He married twice and had fifteen children. He was afflicted with oesophageal varices from 1966 but continued reciting until his death, on Friday 5 Rabi' al-Thani 1389 AH, 20 June 1969, aged forty-nine; the English infobox and Wikidata place his death at al-Munsha'ah, his home town.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-ar}{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-siddiq-al-minshawi-en}
