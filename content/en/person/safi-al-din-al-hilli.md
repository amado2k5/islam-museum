## Overview
Abd al-Aziz ibn Saraya ibn Ali ibn Abi al-Qasim al-Sinbisi al-Ta'i, Safi al-Din al-Hilli, was the poet of his age. Born and raised at al-Hilla between Kufa and Baghdad, he worked in trade, travelling to Syria, Egypt, Mardin and elsewhere and returning to Iraq; he then attached himself to the lords of Mardin, drawing near the Artuqid kings and praising them for ample gifts, and went to Cairo in 726 AH where he praised Sultan al-Malik al-Nasir. He died at Baghdad in 750 AH (1349 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.17}

He left a diwan; al-'Atil al-Hali, an epistle on zajal and mawali; al-Ighlati, a lexicon of popular mispronunciations; Durar al-Nuhur, the panegyrics known as the Artuqiyyat; Safwat al-Shu'ara' wa-Khulasat al-Bulagha'; and al-Khidma al-Jalila on hunting with the musket. The shaykh Ali al-Hazin (d. 1181 AH) wrote a book on him, Akhbar Safi al-Din al-Hilli wa-Nawadir Ash'arih. {cite:al-aalam v.4 p.17}
