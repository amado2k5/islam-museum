## Summary
Aws ibn Khawli was one of the literate men of the Ansar in early times. Al-Baladhuri counts him among the "kumala" — those who combined writing, archery and swimming — and lists him among the men of Aws and Khazraj who could write when Islam came. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 664}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri reports from al-Waqidi that Arabic writing was rare among the Aws and Khazraj: some Jews had taught the Arabic script and the boys of [[place/medina|Medina]] learned it in the early period, so that when Islam came a number of them could write — among them Sa'd ibn 'Ubada, al-Mundhir ibn 'Amr, Ubayy ibn Ka'b, Zayd ibn Thabit who wrote both Arabic and Hebrew, Rafi' ibn Malik, Usayd ibn Hudayr, Ma'n ibn 'Adi al-Balawi the confederate of the Ansar, Bashir ibn Sa'd, Sa'd ibn al-Rabi', Aws ibn Khawli and 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy al-Munafiq {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 663}. He adds: the "kumala" among them — the kamil being one who joins writing to archery and swimming — were Rafi' ibn Malik, Sa'd ibn 'Ubada, Usayd ibn Hudayr, 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy and Aws ibn Khawli {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 664}. Of the people of Yathrib who had combined these skills in the Jahiliyya he names Suwayd ibn al-Samit and Hudayr al-Kata'ib {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 664}.
