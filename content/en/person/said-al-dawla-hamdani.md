## Overview
Sa'id ibn Sharif ibn Ali al-Hamdani, Abu al-Fada'il, is described by al-Zirikli as one of the kings of the Hamdanid state in Aleppo; his title was Sa'id al-Dawla. He took power after the death of his father, Sa'd al-Dawla, in 381 AH, and, like his father, was in obedience to the Abbasids {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 94}.

## The Egyptian army and the siege of Aleppo
Al-Aziz billah, ruler of Egypt, sent against him an army led by the Turk Banjutakin, al-Aziz's governor of Damascus, who took Homs and Hama on the way and besieged Aleppo for a time {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 94}. Sa'id al-Dawla offered him large sums and to enter al-Aziz's obedience, but Banjutakin insisted on entering Aleppo as its conqueror, and the people of the city fought him for thirty-three days until they weakened {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 94}. Al-Zirikli says Sa'id al-Dawla then turned to the worst and most grievous of plans and called on the Rum for help; they came, Banjutakin fought them, and the battles multiplied {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 94}.

## His death
Sa'id al-Dawla died poisoned in Aleppo, he and his wife, in 392 AH, which is 1002 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 94}. Al-Zirikli gives no year of birth.
