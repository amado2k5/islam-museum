## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Muhammad ibn al-Mu'ayyad Abi Bakr ibn Hamwayh al-Juwayni, Sadr al-Din Abu al-Majami', was the shaykh of Khurasan in his time, from Juwayn there. He travelled in search of hadith and heard in Iraq, Syria, the Hijaz, Tabriz, Amul, Jerusalem, Karbala and Qazwin, and died in Iraq in 722 AH (1322 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.62}

Ibn Hajar in al-Durar described him as the Shafi'i Sufi who compiled tasa'iyyat for himself, while al-Amin al-'Amili counted him among the notables of the Shi'a, calling him al-Hamw'i after his grandfather Hamwayh, and listed his Fara'id al-Simtayn on the merits of al-Murtada, al-Batul and al-Sibtayn. Al-Dhahabi noted that he was a night woodcutter of hadith transmission and that Ghazan embraced Islam at his hand. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.62}
