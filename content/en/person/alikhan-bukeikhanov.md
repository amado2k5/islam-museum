# Alikhan Bukeikhanov

## Summary
Alikhan Bukeikhanov (5 March 1866 – 27 September 1937) was a Kazakh statesman, journalist and scholar who led the Alash party and chaired the Alash Orda government, the Kazakh autonomy of 1917–1920. A descendant of Barak Sultan and thus of Genghis Khan, educated at Omsk and St Petersburg, he sat in the first State Duma, co-founded the Kazakh national movement, and after two decades of scholarly work under Soviet suspicion was arrested in the Great Terror and shot in Moscow's Butyrka prison; he was rehabilitated in 1989. {cite:wikipedia-bukeikhanov-en} {cite:wikipedia-bukeikhanov-ru}

## Origins and education
He was born in Tokraunskaya volost, Karkaraly uyezd, Semipalatinsk Oblast, into a Kazakh Muslim family, the son of Nurmuhammed Bukeikhanov. He graduated from the Russian-Kazakh school at Karkaraly and from Omsk Technical School in 1890, spent fourteen years at Omsk — teaching mathematics in a school for Kazakh children and joining the Shcherbina expedition that surveyed the steppe region — and in 1894 took a degree in economics at the St Petersburg Forestry Institute. {cite:wikipedia-bukeikhanov-en}

## Politics and Alash Orda
He joined the Constitutional Democratic Party (Kadets) in 1905, was arrested after trying to found a Kazakh democratic party at Uralsk, and in exile was elected to the State Duma in 1906, signing the Vyborg Manifesto. In April and July 1917 he and his colleagues — among them Akhmet Baitursynov and Mirjaqip Dulatuli — convened the All-Kazakh congresses at Orenburg, which demanded the return of confiscated lands and then proclaimed territorial autonomy and founded the Alash party; in December 1917 the second congress established Alash Orda, with Bukeikhanov as its chairman, a post he held until the autonomy was abolished in 1920. His major political writing, "Kirgizy" ("The Kazakhs", 1910), set out the Kazakh case inside the Kadets' volume on the empire's nationalities, and he helped found the Kazakh newspaper Qazaq. {cite:wikipedia-bukeikhanov-en} {cite:wikipedia-bukeikhanov-ru}

## Soviet years and death
In 1920 he joined the Bolshevik party and returned to scholarly life in Moscow, but his Alash past kept him under suspicion: he was arrested in 1926 and again in 1928 and held in Butyrka, though the 1926 case collapsed for lack of evidence. Arrested a final time in the Great Terror, he was shot in Butyrka prison on 27 September 1937. The Soviet authorities rehabilitated him only in 1989. {cite:wikipedia-bukeikhanov-en} {cite:wikipedia-bukeikhanov-ru}
