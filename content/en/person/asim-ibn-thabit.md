Asim ibn Thabit ibn Abi al-Aqlah al-Ansari al-Awsi, a notable Companion and Badri horseman who witnessed Badr and Uhud, one of the Ansar's famous archers. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Prophet gave him command of the al-Raji' detachment in Safar of year four of the hijra; when Hudhayl betrayed them at al-Raji' water and offered them safety, he said: "as for me, I will not come down into a disbeliever's protection — O God, tell Your Prophet of us," and fought until he was martyred. He had covenanted with his Lord that no polytheist would touch him nor he touch a polytheist in his life; when Quraysh wanted to take his head to sell it to Sulafa bint Sa'd, God protected him with a dense swarm of hornets between them and him, then sent a flood down the valley that carried him away — so he is known as "the one the hornets shielded" (hami al-dabr). {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## In al-Basha's Suwar min Hayat al-Sahaba
[[person/abd-al-rahman-rafat-al-basha|Abd al-Rahman Ra'fat al-Basha]] heads his portrait with a saying attributed to the Prophet: "Whoever fights, let him fight as Asim ibn Thabit fights." {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.377} He relates that Sulafa bint Sa'd came out with Quraysh at Uhud with her husband Talha and her three sons Musafi', al-Julas and Kilab; after the battle she found two sons dead and the third dying, who told her that Asim ibn Thabit had felled him and his brother. She vowed to drink wine from Asim's skull and offered a reward for his head. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.377} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.378} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.379} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.380} He says that after Uhud the Muslims spoke of Asim's prowess, and one reminded them how, before Badr, he had described to the Prophet how they would fight: arrows at a hundred cubits, then spears until they broke, then swords. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.380} He then tells the story of [[event/incident-of-al-raji|al-Raji']]: when Hudhayl surrounded Asim and his companions and offered them safe-conduct, Asim said, "As for me, I will not come down under the protection of a polytheist", prayed God to protect his flesh and bones, and fought until he was killed. When Hudhayl came for his head to sell to Quraysh, swarms of bees and hornets kept them off; they waited for night, and a flood carried his body away, so that no trace of it was found. {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.381} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.382} {cite:suwar-min-hayat-al-sahaba-nafais-1992 p.383}
