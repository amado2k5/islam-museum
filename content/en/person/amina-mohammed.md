## Summary
Amina J. Mohammed is a Nigerian diplomat who has served since March 2017 as Deputy Secretary-General of the United Nations — the highest UN office reached by an African woman — after a career building the global sustainable-development agenda and leading Nigeria's environment ministry. She is among the most influential African diplomats in the contemporary UN system. {cite:wikipedia-amina-mohammed}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1961 in Gombe, northern Nigeria, to a Nigerian veterinary doctor father and a British nurse mother, studied in Nigeria and Scotland where she trained in hospital administration, and worked first in the health sector before founding a consultancy in social development and governance. {cite:wikipedia-amina-mohammed}

## National and UN career
She served in Nigeria as senior adviser to successive presidents on the Millennium Development Goals, and was the architect of Nigeria's file in preparing the Sustainable Development Goals (2012–2015) as Special Adviser to Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon on post-2015 planning. Appointed Minister of Environment in President Buhari's government (2015–2017), she led Nigeria's climate delegations and helped advance implementation of the Paris Agreement. In March 2017 Secretary-General António Guterres appointed her his deputy, and she was re-appointed for a second term in 2022. {cite:wikipedia-amina-mohammed} {cite:muslim500-amina-mohammed}

## In office
Inside the Secretariat she coordinates the sustainable-development file, the UN Development System, and the reform of the resident-coordinator system; she has led high-level delegations on displacement and climate crises and launched, with the European Union, the Spotlight Initiative against violence toward women and girls. {cite:wikipedia-amina-mohammed}

## Recognition and debate
She holds Nigerian national honours and global leadership awards and appears in rankings of the most influential women. In her national career she faced press questions about older government contracts which she answered with reports clearing her; the Nigerian press documents these files and the museum reports them attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-amina-mohammed}

## Legacy
Her career reads as the highest ascent of an African Muslim woman in the UN ladder: from development consultant to architect of a global agenda to the number two position in the largest international organisation, with the saying attributed to her in her speeches that development is not charity but a right — a signature of her public policy. {cite:wikipedia-amina-mohammed}
