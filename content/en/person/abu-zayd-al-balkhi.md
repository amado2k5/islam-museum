## Overview
Abu Zayd Ahmad ibn Sahl al-Balkhi, a sage of many sciences. Yaqut relates, and Ahmad Amin repeats, that in his youth he felt a desire to travel from Balkh to Iraq to sit among the scholars. {cite:duha-islam p.434-435}

## His journey and learning
He went to Iraq on foot with the pilgrims and stayed there eight years. He travelled the neighbouring lands and met their great men and notables. {cite:duha-islam p.434-435}

He was a pupil of Abu Yusuf Ya'qub ibn Ishaq [[person/al-kindi|al-Kindi]]. He went deep into philosophy, took up the secrets of astrology and astronomy, excelled in medicine and natural science, and examined the principles of religion. {cite:duha-islam p.434-435}

The years of his birth and death are not in the museum's data and need an additional source.

## In al-Muqaddasi
In the preface of the [[work/ahsan-al-taqasim|Ahsan al-taqasim]], [[person/al-muqaddasi|al-Muqaddasi]] says that Abu Zayd al-Balkhi aimed in his book at maps and the image of the earth, which he divided into twenty parts, then explained each map briefly, without giving the useful causes or clarifying matters of detail and arrangement, and left out many of the chief cities. {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.14} He says Abu Zayd "had not travelled the lands nor set foot in the provinces", and tells that the ruler of Khurasan summoned him to court for his counsel, but when he reached the Oxus he wrote: "If you summoned me for my sound judgement, my judgement forbids me to cross this river", so the ruler ordered him back to Balkh. {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.14} In al-Muqaddasi's account Abu Zayd likened the southern sea to a bird with its beak at al-Qulzum, its neck at Iraq and its tail between Abyssinia and China. {cite:muqaddasi-ahsan-taqasim-bga-1877 p.20}
