## Overview
[[person/al-harith-ibn-abd-al-uzza|Al-Harith ibn Abd al-Uzza]] al-Sa'di of Hawazin, husband of [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima]], the wet-nurse of the Prophet. Ibn Sa'd says he was called Abu Dhu'ayb. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.261} Among his children was [[person/al-shayma-bint-al-harith|al-Shayma]]. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.261}

## His conversion
Ibn Ishaq relates from men of the Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr that al-Harith came to Mecca, and the Quraysh said to him: "Do you not hear what your son says? People will be raised after death." He asked the Prophet about that, and he said: "Yes", and told him that if it were that day he would take his hand and make him know his account that day. Al-Harith then embraced Islam and his Islam was good. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.261}

## A note
Several views on this matter are given in the Isaba. They are set out in order in the list of views at the top of the page.

## In "Al-Rahiq al-Makhtum" (p. 55)
Al-Rahiq al-Makhtum says that [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] had the Prophet nursed by Halima daughter of Abu Dhu'ayb of the [[group/banu-sad-ibn-bakr|Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr]], and that her husband al-Harith ibn Abd al-Uzza was from the same tribe, whom the book calls Abu Kabsha. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55} {cite:zad-al-maad 1/19} It counts among the Prophet's foster-siblings the children of al-Harith: Abd Allah ibn al-Harith, Unaysa daughter of al-Harith, and Hudhafa or Judhama daughter of al-Harith, who is [[person/al-shayma-bint-al-harith|al-Shayma]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55}

## Notes for researchers
- On the kunya: the Isaba calls him Abu Dhu'ayb, which is the kunya of Halima's father in the book, while al-Rahiq calls him Abu Kabsha. The museum records both views and does not prefer one.
