## Overview
Ka'b al-Ahbar, one of the Jewish scholars (ahbar) of Yemen, who became Muslim in the caliphate of Abu Bakr or Umar. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}

## With Umar
It is related that Umar asked him what they find in the Torah. He said: "I find you in the Torah being killed as a martyr." He also said to him: "We find you in the Book of God at one of the gates of Hell, preventing people from falling into it." {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}

## Iram of the Pillars
Ibn Khaldun relays from Mu'awiya that he asked him about Iram of the Pillars after the story of Abdallah ibn Qilaba. He described a short, red-haired man going out in search of his camels, then turned and saw Ibn Qilaba, and said: "By God, this is that man." {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p.26}

Ibn Khaldun counts this among the entries of error for the commentators. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p.26}

## In Hilyat al-Awliya
Ka'b al-Ahbar's notice in *Hilyat al-Awliya* (volume 5, no. 325) opens: "Among them the sage, master of books and scrolls, awakener of the hidden mysteries and pointer to the signs, Abu Ishaq Ka'b ibn Matau' al-Ahbar," and transmits his reports and sayings through many chains. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/364}

Among his sayings on asceticism: "The ascetic believer and the righteous slave are safe from reckoning"; "When God loves His believing servant He sets aside the world for him to raise him in degrees in Paradise, and when He hates His unbelieving servant He spreads the world for him until He lowers him in degrees in the Fire"; "No servant is honoured before God except that tribulation increases upon him, no man's fortune is given him in passing but it diminishes from his wealth, no dirham is stored but it increases, and no thief steals but it is reckoned from his provision." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/364-365}

He reports the story of Umar asking him about death: "Commander of the faithful, tell us about death." Umar said: a thorny branch enters a man's belly and every thorn draws a vein pulled by a strong puller — it takes what it takes and leaves what it leaves. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/365}

Of his words on silence: "Little speech is wisdom; keep to silence, for it is a good pasture, little burden and lightness from sins." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/367}

The notice transmits reports on angels and the hereafter attributed to him from the Books of the People of the Book, including the report of angels standing, bowing and prostrating since their creation, the report of a ruby pillar in Paradise inscribed on its people's foreheads "these are the friends of God", his exchange with a Jew about "the community of Ahmad" in what he relates from the Torah, what he relates from Moses in the Torah of a people's description, and what he said to Solomon in the hoopoe story. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/368} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/380} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/383-387} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 5/391}
