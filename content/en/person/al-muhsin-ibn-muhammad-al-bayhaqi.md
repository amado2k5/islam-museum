## Overview
al-Muhsin ibn Muhammad ibn Karama al-Jushami al-Bayhaqi was an exegete and a scholar of legal theory and theology. Al-Zirikli describes him as Hanafi, then Mu'tazili, then Zaydi, that is, he ended in the Zaydi school, a Shi'i school, and not in the Sunni school {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 283}. He was a teacher of al-Zamakhshari. He studied at Nishapur and elsewhere and became known in Sanaa in Yemen {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 283}. Al-Zirikli writes that he died a martyr, killed at Mecca, and says that it was said to be because of a treatise he had written called 'Risalat al-Shaykh Iblis ila Ikhwanihi al-Manahis' {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 283}. The entry gives his years as 413 to 494 AH, 1022 to 1101 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 283}.

## His works
Al-Zirikli says he wrote 42 books, among them 'al-Tahdhib', in manuscript, on Qur'anic exegesis, in eight volumes, of which al-Zirikli saw the fourth, the sixth and the eighth, the last, written in 565 AH, in the Vatican library; 'Sharh Uyun al-Masa'il', in manuscript, on theology; 'al-Ta'thir wa-l-Mu'aththir', in manuscript, also on theology; 'al-Muntakhab', on Zaydi jurisprudence; 'al-Safina', in manuscript, a history down to his own time in four large volumes; 'Tahkim al-Uqul', on legal theory; 'al-Imama', according to the Zaydi school; 'al-Risala al-Tamma fi Nasihat al-Amma', in manuscript; 'Jala' al-Absar', on hadith, with chains of transmission; and two exegeses in Persian, one extended and one brief {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 283}.
