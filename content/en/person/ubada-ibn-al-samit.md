Abu al-Walid Ubada ibn al-Samit ibn Qays al-Ansari al-Khazraji, one of the chiefs and great jurists of Banu Awf, among the first of Yathrib's people to pledge to the Prophet. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He witnessed the first pledge of Aqaba among twelve men, and is the narrator of its text in the two Sahihs; then he witnessed the second Aqaba as the naqib of his people Banu Awf ibn al-Khazraj. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

He witnessed Badr, Uhud and all the campaigns with the Messenger of God, was one of those who collected the Qur'an in the Prophet's life, then Umar ibn al-Khattab sent him to Syria as teacher and judge; he died at al-Ramla or Bayt al-Maqdis in Mu'awiya's caliphate at about seventy-two. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## For Sprouts
Ubada ibn al-Samit was one of the first people of Medina to believe in the Prophet, and one of the leaders and scholars of his clan Banu Awf. He witnessed both pledges of Aqaba and the battles of Badr and Uhud, and was among those who gathered the Qur'an in the Prophet's lifetime. Umar ibn al-Khattab sent him to Syria as a teacher and judge; he died there at about seventy-two.

## For Scholars
Abu al-Walid Ubada ibn al-Samit ibn Qays al-Ansari al-Khazraji was one of the chiefs and great jurists of Banu Awf and among the first of Yathrib to pledge to the Prophet. He attended the first Aqaba pledge with twelve men — he narrates its wording in the two Sahihs — then the second as naqib of Banu Awf; he witnessed Badr, Uhud and every scene with the Prophet, and was among those who gathered the Qur'an in his lifetime. Under Umar he was sent to Syria as teacher and judge and settled there; he died at Ramla or Jerusalem under Mu'awiya, at about seventy-two. Historians present him as the field jurist: early pledge, Qur'an gathering, and judgeship in the provinces.
