## Overview
Ma'iz ibn Malik al-Aslami, of [[group/aslam|Aslam]], is counted among the people of Medina. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 686}

## The document for his people
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr records that the Prophet wrote him a document confirming that his people had accepted Islam. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 686}

## His confession
He is the man who confessed to adultery against himself, repentant and turning back to God; being married, he was stoned. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 686} For his story the editor refers to al-Bukhari's *Sahih* (no. 5271) and Muslim's *Sahih* (no. 1691). {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 686} On the penalty and the jurists' discussions of it, see [[concept/al-rajm|stoning (rajm)]].

## Transmission
His son 'Abd Allah ibn Ma'iz narrated a single hadith from him. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 686} Ibn 'Abd al-Barr then lists another man named Ma'iz who asked the Prophet which deeds are best, saying: "I have found no lineage for him." {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 686}
