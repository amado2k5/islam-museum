## Overview

Abu Bakr Shu'ba ibn 'Ayyash ibn Salim al-Khayyat al-Asadi of Kufa was one of the two transmitters of the reading of [[person/asim-ibn-abi-al-najud|Asim ibn Abi al-Najud]], the other being [[person/hafs-abu-umar-ibn-sulayman|Hafs]]. He was born in 95 AH and died in Jumada I of 193 AH. Ibn al-Jazari calls him an imam, a scholar who acted on his knowledge, an authority and one of the great leaders of the Sunna. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 166}

## Reading and chain

Abu Bakr read on Asim, the imam and reader of Kufa. In Ibn al-Jazari's account Asim's own chain runs through Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami, Zirr ibn Hubaysh and Abu Amr al-Shaybani to Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, and through 'Uthman, 'Ali, Ubayy and Zayd back to the Prophet. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 165} Al-Kisa'i narrated letters (individual variants) of the reading from him, and Khalaf ibn Hisham narrated the letters from Yahya ibn Adam on Abu Bakr's authority. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 183} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 201}

## Those who took from him

Ibn al-Jazari names among his companions Yahya ibn Adam (d. 203), al-'Ulaymi (born 105, died 243) and Abu Hamdun. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 167} He also records a small disagreement: many teachers of the readings say that al-'Ulaymi and Yahya ibn Adam read the whole Qur'an to him, while some say they only heard the letters from him; his own view is that Yahya ibn Adam heard the letters from him. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 162}

## An account of his death

Ibn al-Jazari reports that when death approached, his sister wept, and he told her to look at a corner of the room, saying he had completed eighteen thousand recitations of the whole Qur'an in it. The report is given as the book words it and its number is not independently confirmed. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 166}
