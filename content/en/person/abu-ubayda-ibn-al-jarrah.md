## Summary
Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah was an early Meccan emigrant among the first converts. The Prophet named him "the trustworthy one of this community," and he was the commander who held together the conquest of Syria until plague took him.

## Life
A Qurayshi of a modest clan, he embraced Islam at the hand of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] among the earliest Muslims {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He took part in [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]]. At [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] tradition remembers him pulling the chain-mail rings out of the Prophet's wounded face with his teeth {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

After the Prophet's death he was sent to Syria with the armies. In the time of Umar he took over the supreme command: he took Damascus, then directed the victory at [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmuk]] and the surrender of Jerusalem {cite:tabari-donner-1993}. In 639 CE the plague of Amwas struck his army in Palestine. The sources describe him preferring to stay among his men rather than seek his own survival, and he died of the plague in his fifties {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Of the ten whom the sunna gives glad tidings of paradise, his reputation belongs to the quiet kind. It is not a brilliance recounted, but trust: he was the commander whom the caliphs trusted with any army.

## For young visitors
The Prophet called Abu Ubayda "the trustworthy one." When plague struck his army, he stayed among his soldiers instead of saving himself.

## "Do not disagree"
Ibn Ishaq relates that the Prophet sent him as reinforcement to Amr ibn al-As on the [[event/expedition-of-dhat-al-salasil|Dhat al-Salasil expedition]], among the early emigrants, including Abu Bakr and Umar, and said to them: "Do not disagree." When Amr disputed the command with him, Abu Ubayda said: "If you disobey me, you will obey me." Then he submitted: "Then take it, it is yours." Ibn Ishaq describes him as a gentle, easy-going man, for whom the affairs of this world were of little weight {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/272}.

## In the secret call (The Sealed Nectar, p.76)
The book counts him among those who followed the first vanguard of the earliest Muslims in the [[event/al-dawa-al-sirriyya|secret call]]. Ibn Hisham counted more than forty such people from all the clans of Quraysh {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. The book names the "trustworthy one of this community" as Abu Ubayda Amir ibn al-Jarrah of Banu al-Harith ibn Fihr, and refers the title to al-Bukhari's chapter on virtues {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/530}.

## The detachment of Sayf al-Bahr
The Prophet appointed him commander of a detachment to Sayf al-Bahr and gave them a bag of dates. He rationed it to them until each man was given a single date a day. Then God brought out for them a creature from the sea, on which they lived for twenty nights. See [[event/sariyya-of-abu-ubayda-to-sif-al-bahr|the detachment]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/281}.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), signed by H. A. R. Gibb, gives his name as Abu 'Ubayda 'Amir b. 'Abd Allah b. al-Jarrah, of the family of Balharith of the Quraysh tribe of Fihr. He was one of the early Meccan converts and one of the ten promised Paradise. He took part in the emigration to Abyssinia, and was called "amin" (trustworthy) by Muhammad for his courage and unselfishness. He was 41 at Badr, fought at Uhud, and commanded several expeditions. He was sent to Najran to instruct the Yemeni converts, but returned to Medina before the Prophet's death, and with Umar b. al-Khattab helped secure the election of Abu Bakr as khalifa. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 177}

After Umar's accession in 13/634 Abu 'Ubayda was sent to Syria to join the campaigns against the Byzantines. Probably in 15/636 he was given the supreme command there. After the victory on the Yarmuk that year he completed the conquest of northern Syria: Hims, Aleppo and Antioch. In 17/638 Umar visited the Syrian army at Dabiya to regulate its administration and to support Abu 'Ubayda's authority; tradition says Umar meant to name him his successor. When plague broke out in Syria in 18/639, Umar summoned him to Medina, but he refused to leave and died of the plague at the age of 58, leaving no descendants. The article says his personality impressed his contemporaries, but later tradition presents him in a rather colourless way. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 177}

## For Sprouts
Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah was among the first to believe the Prophet; he was a calm, trustworthy man whom the Prophet called 'the trustworthy one of this community'. The state sent him to lead armies in Syria, and he stayed trustworthy until plague took him in the land of Syria.

## For Explorers
Abu Ubayda Amir ibn Abdullah ibn al-Jarrah was among the earliest to accept Islam, at Abu Bakr's hand in the first days, and he witnessed Badr, Uhud and every event. The Prophet named him 'the trustworthy one of this community'. Abu Bakr appointed him among the chief commanders of the Syrian armies in the conquests, alongside Khalid ibn al-Walid, and then Umar made him commander of all the armies of Syria. He was known for asceticism and humility — Umar is said to have found his house almost bare. He led the conquests in Syria until the plague of Amwas in 18 AH; the plague struck him and he died in the Jordan region and was buried there.

## For Scholars
Abu Ubayda Amir ibn Abdullah ibn al-Jarrah al-Fihri of Quraysh was among the very first to accept Islam; the biographical works record that he took the faith at Abu Bakr's hand in the early days. He witnessed Badr, Uhud and every event, and it is related of him that he pulled the two chain-mail rings from the Prophet's wounded face at Uhud with his teeth until two of his own teeth fell. The Prophet named him 'the trustworthy one of this community' when a delegation of Najran asked for a trustworthy man. Abu Bakr sent him in 13 AH among the chief commanders of the Syrian armies alongside Khalid ibn al-Walid, then Umar gathered all command under him until his death. The sources preserve scenes of his asceticism: Umar entered his house and found almost nothing in it, and spoke famous words. He led the conquests until the plague of Amwas in 18 AH; Umar called him to safety but he declined, unwilling to flee God's decree; the plague struck him and he died in the Jordan valley at Baysan and was buried there, succeeded by Mu'adh ibn Jabal. Historians read him, with Khalid, as the pairing of military genius with administrative trustworthiness in the conquering state.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the twenty-seventh episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] introduces Abu Ubayda Amir ibn al-Jarrah as a man who became Muslim through [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] when the Muslims could be counted on the fingers of one hand, the first to be called "the trustee of the community", and one of the Ten; his conversion through Abu Bakr is also given in this record from Ibn Sa'd. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:01:30} {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley} He says Abu Ubayda fought in every campaign with the Prophet and took part in the conquests, calls him the conqueror of [[place/al-sham|Syria]] and the chastiser of the Byzantines, and says that Abu Bakr intended to make him leader of the Muslims right after the Prophet's death, and that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] said on his deathbed that had Abu Ubayda been alive he would have appointed him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:02:00} He counts him among six of the Ten who became Muslim through Abu Bakr before a week of Abu Bakr's own Islam had passed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:03:00} He says Abu Ubayda emigrated to [[place/abyssinia|Abyssinia]]; then a rumour spread that the people of Mecca had accepted Islam, some emigrants came back to find the opposite and were persecuted, and he then made the second emigration, to [[place/medina|Medina]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:03:30}

He says Abu Ubayda fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] in the year 2 and fought hard. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:04:30} He relates that a polytheist horseman kept seeking him out while Abu Ubayda avoided him; when the man persisted Abu Ubayda killed him, and the horseman was his own father, who had come out as an unbeliever intent on killing the Prophet, and the verse was revealed about him that denies that believers befriend those who oppose God and His Messenger, even if they are their fathers. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:05:00} At [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], he says, Abu Ubayda defended the Prophet fiercely, and he describes the archers' error as one of interpretation, not intent. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:06:00} He relates that after the battle Abu Ubayda saw two rings of the helmet embedded in the Prophet's cheeks; Abu Bakr moved to pull them out, but Abu Ubayda swore that no one but he would do it, and drew them out with his teeth, losing his two front teeth, and his smile was the handsomer for their loss; his drawing out the helmet rings with his teeth is also told in this record from Ibn Sa'd. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:06:30} {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}

He relates that after [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] became Muslim the Prophet sent him in command of the [[event/expedition-of-dhat-al-salasil|Dhat al-Salasil]] expedition; camped at a water in the land of Judham, Amr asked for reinforcements, and the Prophet sent them under Abu Ubayda, with Abu Bakr and Umar among them. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:08:00} He says Abu Ubayda handed the command to Amr because the Prophet had told him that the two should not quarrel, telling Amr to lead the people in prayer and never invoking his own precedence; the instruction "do not quarrel" and his yielding to Amr are reported by Ibn Ishaq as given in this record. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:09:00} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/272} He relates, through [[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]] and [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], that the Prophet sent a force to the seacoast under Abu Ubayda, known as the Khabat expedition, with a single bag of dates as provision, which Abu Ubayda shared out until each man had one date a day to suck. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:09:30} He tells that the Prophet had told them that if the dates ran out God would provide for them; they ate dry leaves soaked in seawater, then the sea cast up a creature called the ambergris whale, and they ate from it and stayed by it for twenty nights until they grew fat. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:10:30} He says Abu Ubayda set up one of its ribs and had the tallest man ride the largest camel beneath it without his head touching it, and that when they returned the Prophet said it was provision God had brought forth for them and asked whether they had any of its meat with them; the one date a day and the twenty nights also appear in Ibn Hisham's account cited in this record (see [[event/sariyya-of-abu-ubayda-to-sif-al-bahr|the expedition]]). {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:11:30} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/281}

He relates from [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] that a Christian delegation from [[place/najran|Najran]] asked the Prophet about Christ; he recited the Qur'an to them and they refused it, so he invited them to the mutual imprecation, the mubahala, and came out with [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|al-Hasan]] and [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn]], and [[person/fatima|Fatima]] behind him; seeing his resolve they conferred, al-Aqib advised them not to go through with it, and they made peace with him on payment of the jizya and asked for a trustworthy man. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:12:30} The Prophet said he would send with them a man truly trustworthy; the Companions each hoped to be the one, and he raised Abu Ubayda and said that he was the trustee of this community (see [[event/delegation-of-najran|the delegation of Najran]]). {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:12:30}

He relates that Abu Bakr sent armies against the Byzantines, saying he would make them forget the whisperings of Satan with [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]], and that he died while the Muslims under Khalid were at [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|al-Yarmuk]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:14:30} He tells that Umar wrote to Abu Ubayda dismissing Khalid and appointing him, and that Abu Ubayda kept the letter hidden until the Muslims had won under Khalid; when Khalid reproached him he said that they were brothers, that worldly power passes away, and that he would not break Khalid's campaign; and he quotes Umar as saying that no one is to be made amir over Abu Ubayda. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:15:00} He describes him as forbearing, gentle and trustworthy, sees in this the reason most of Syria entered Islam by treaty, and says he consulted his men at every step and was strictly obedient to the Commander of the Faithful. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:17:00} He gives from Abu Ubayda's exhortation to his troops that many who whiten their clothes soil their religion, that one who honours himself today may be humbled tomorrow, and that they should repel old misdeeds with new good deeds. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:17:30} He relates that when the siege of [[place/ladhiqiyya|Latakia]] dragged on, Abu Ubayda had his soldiers dig pits deep enough to hide a horseman and his horse, then made a show of withdrawing, and when the townspeople opened the fortifications the Muslims came out of the pits and entered with them, and the city was taken. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:18:00}

He relates that when Umar came to receive the key of [[place/al-aqsa-mosque|al-Aqsa]] after the conquest of Palestine, he waded through a muddy ford and soiled his clothes; Abu Ubayda objected, and Umar said that had anyone else said it he would have punished him, for they had been lowly and God had honoured them through Islam, and whenever they sought honour in anything else God would humble them. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:18:30} He relates that Umar asked to visit Abu Ubayda's house and found in it only a felt mat, a bowl and a waterskin, and that Abu Ubayda fed him crusts of bread from a basket; Umar wept and said that the world had changed all of them except Abu Ubayda. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:19:30} He says the [[event/plague-of-amwas|plague of Amwas]] struck Syria in the year 18 of the Hijra and, by his count, killed more than twenty thousand Companions and Followers, among them [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] and Abu Ubayda. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:21:00} He relates that Umar wrote summoning him on the pretext of a need, and that Abu Ubayda understood that Umar wanted to save him and declined, refusing to put himself above his troops, and Umar wept; his choice to stay among his men is also told in this record from Ibn Sa'd. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:21:30} {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley} He relates from [[person/al-tabarani|al-Tabarani]] and [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]], through al-Harith ibn Umayra, that he went in with Mu'adh to Abu Ubayda, who showed them the plague sore on his palm and said he would not wish to have the finest red camels in exchange for it, since whoever dies of plague patiently, hoping for God's reward, is a martyr. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:22:00} He relates from al-Hakim in [[work/al-mustadrak-ala-al-sahihayn|al-Mustadrak]] that Umar asked his companions to name their wishes: one wished for a house full of gold to spend in God's way, another for horses to campaign with, and Umar wished for a house full of men like Abu Ubayda, Mu'adh ibn Jabal, [[person/hudhayfa-ibn-al-yaman|Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman]] and [[person/salim-mawla-abi-hudhayfa|Salim the client of Abu Hudhayfa]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:23:00} The lesson he draws is that the community needs men, and that gold and civilisation are of no use when religion and character are lost. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:24:00} He closes by saying that Abu Ubayda was buried in Jordan. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep27 00:24:30}
