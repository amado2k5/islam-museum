## Brief
Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah was an early Meccan convert the Prophet called "the trustworthy man of this community" — and the commander who held the Syrian conquest together until plague took him.

## Life
A Qurayshi of modest clan standing, Abu Ubayda embraced Islam through [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] among the earliest converts {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. He fought at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], and at [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] tradition remembers him pulling the chain-mail rings out of the Prophet's wounded face with his teeth {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

After the Prophet's death he was sent to Syria with the armies; under Umar he held the high command, taking Damascus and then directing the victories of the [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmuk]] and the surrender of Jerusalem {cite:tabari-donner-1993}. In 639 CE the plague of Amwas struck his army in Palestine — the sources describe his decision to stay with his men rather than flee — and he died of it, still in his fifties {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Among the ten Companions tradition promises paradise, his reputation is the quiet kind: not brilliance, but trust — the commander caliphs could send anywhere.

## For young visitors
The Prophet called Abu Ubayda "the trustworthy one." When plague hit his army, he stayed with his soldiers instead of saving himself.
