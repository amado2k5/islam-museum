## Overview
Qaydar, the second of the twelve sons of [[person/ismail|Ismail]] that al-Rahiq al-Makhtum counts, born to [[person/bint-mudad-al-jurhumi|the daughter of Mudad]] al-Jurhumi. Of the two whose descendants' accounts have been preserved, they are [[person/nabit|Nabit]] and Qaydar {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.230}.

## Ancestor of the Adnanite Arabs
The lineage of Qaydar's sons continued to multiply in Mecca, until [[person/adnan|Adnan]] and his son [[person/maadd-ibn-adnan|Ma'add]] came from him. The [[group/adnan|Adnanite Arabs]] preserved their genealogies from Adnan {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20}. The author notes that Adnan is the twenty-first ancestor in the Prophet's genealogy, and that when the Prophet traced his lineage and reached Adnan, he stopped. Several scholars allowed raising the genealogy above Adnan, and said that there are forty fathers between Adnan and Abraham {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.20} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari 2/191-194} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 2/7-17}.

## Governance of Mecca
After Ismail, two of his sons ruled Mecca, Nabit and then Qaydar, or the reverse is said. Then their grandfather [[person/mudad-ibn-amr|Mudad ibn Amr al-Jurhumi]] took over {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.230-237}.

## For young visitors
Qaydar was a son of the Prophet Ismail. His descendants stayed in Mecca for many generations, and from them came Adnan, the forefather of many tribes, among them Quraysh.
