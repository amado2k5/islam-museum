## Overview
Taqi al-Din Abu Muhammad al-Hafiz Abd al-Ghani ibn Abd al-Wahid ibn Ali ibn Surur al-Maqdisi al-Jamma'ili al-Hanbali was born in 541 AH; he emigrated young to Damascus after the 550s, hearing Abu al-Makarram Ibn Hilal there and Abu al-Fath Ibn al-Batti and others at Baghdad, al-Salafi at Alexandria; he journeyed to Isfahan and transmitted widely there in the 570s, and composed many large famous works, continuing to hear and write until he died in 600 AH (1204 CE). {cite:shadharat-dhahab v.6 p.560}

In him the memorisation of hadith reached its peak, in text, chain and command of its disciplines, together with piety. {cite:shadharat-dhahab v.6 p.560}

## In Ibn Rajab's al-Dhayl ala Tabaqat al-Hanabila
Ibn Rajab opens the deaths of the seventh century with his entry (no. 214) and styles him "the hafiz and traditionist of his time". {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 5}

### Birth
He was born at Jamma'il, in the district of Nablus, in 541 AH. The hafiz al-Diya' said: I think it was in Rabi' al-Akhir, because his mother told him that he was four months older than his brother al-Muwaffaq, who was born in Sha'ban of that year. Al-Mundhiri said that his companions reported things from him indicating a birth in 544, and Ibn al-Najjar records that he asked him and he answered: either 543 or 544, and 544 is the likelier. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 5}

### Travel and study
He came to Damascus young, after 550, and heard Abu al-Makarim ibn Hilal and others. In 561 he travelled to Baghdad with Shaykh al-Muwaffaq ([[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|Ibn Qudama]]); they lodged with Shaykh [[person/abd-al-qadir-al-jilani|Abd al-Qadir]] for about forty days before he died, reading two lessons of fiqh with him every day, then studied fiqh and disputation under Ibn al-Manni, staying in Baghdad four years. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 5} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 6} In 566 he went to Egypt and Alexandria, and returned to Alexandria in 570, where he heard so much from the hafiz [[person/al-silafi|al-Silafi]] that it was said he may have written a thousand parts from him. After 570 he travelled to Isfahan and heard [[person/abu-musa-al-madini|Abu Musa al-Madini]], heard also at Hamadan and Mosul, and returned to Damascus to copy, compile and teach. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 6}

Ibn Rajab quotes al-Taj al-Kindi: after [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] there was none like the hafiz. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 8}

### Works
Ibn Rajab lists his works. With chains of transmission: al-Misbah fi Uyun al-Ahadith al-Sihah in forty-eight parts; Nihayat al-Murad on the Sunan, about two hundred parts, not all fair-copied; Mihnat al-Imam Ahmad in three parts; books of merits and of forty hadith; and I'tiqad al-Imam al-Shafi'i. Without chains: al-Ahkam ala Abwab al-Fiqh in six parts; [[work/umdat-al-ahkam|al-Umda fi al-Ahkam]], on what al-Bukhari and Muslim agree upon, in two parts; Tabyin al-Isaba li-Awham Hasalat fi Ma'rifat al-Sahaba, on Abu Nu'aym's book; and [[work/al-kamal-fi-asma-al-rijal|al-Kamal fi Ma'rifat al-Rijal]], on the men of the Six Books, in ten volumes. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 18} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 19}

### Isfahan and Mosul
Al-Diya' relates that in Isfahan [[person/abu-musa-al-madini|Abu Musa al-Madini]] urged him to correct [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]]'s Ma'rifat al-Sahaba, and he noted about two hundred and ninety places. The notable Abd al-Latif ibn al-Khujandi then sought him, and he hid and left Isfahan wrapped in a waist-cloth. The narrator explained that the Khujandi house were Ash'aris partial to Abu Nu'aym and the city's leaders; Ibn Rajab comments that the matter had nothing to do with differences of school or doctrine. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 19} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 20} He also reports that at Mosul he was imprisoned while hearing [[person/abu-jafar-al-uqayli|al-Uqayli]]'s book on criticism, because of what it says about Abu Hanifa, and released after his fellow student Ibn al-Barni hid the quire containing it. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 20}

### The trial at Damascus
Ibn Rajab quotes al-Diya': the hafiz's sessions in the Damascus mosque drew crowds; his opponents arranged a rival session; then he was summoned alone to the citadel, debated, and asked to sign a creed they had written, which he refused. They removed his pulpit and cupboard from the mosque, broke his pulpit and barred him from teaching, and he went to Baalbek and then to Egypt. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 20} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 21} Ibn Rajab then gives the account of those he calls "some of the opponents" as they told it, and comments on it, as does al-Dhahabi:

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Diya' al-Maqdisi's account, as Ibn Rajab quotes it**: the trial came from his opponents' envy of his sessions; he refused to sign what contradicted his creed, and so his pulpit was removed and he was barred from teaching. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 20} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 21}
- **Sunni: the account of his Shafi'i, Hanafi and Maliki opponents, as Ibn Rajab quotes it**: they met before [[person/al-malik-al-muazzam|al-Mu'azzam Isa]] and al-Sarim Barghash, governor of the citadel; Abd al-Ghani held to his creed on direction (jiha), the rising over the Throne (istiwa') and the letter (harf); the jurists gave a fatwa that he was an innovator who should not be allowed to stay among them, and he was given three days to leave. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 22}
- **Sunni: Ibn Rajab**: he rejects the claim of consensus: how can there be consensus when the man most learned in the Sunna in his time dissents, with al-Muwaffaq and many jurists and traditionists beside him? He sees what befell the hafiz as hostility and partisanship. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 23} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 24}
- **Sunni: al-Dhahabi, as Ibn Rajab read it in his hand**: they did not agree; some Ash'ari imams gave that fatwa, and the man said no more than many Hanbalis and traditionists say, that the established attributes are taken literally, not figuratively. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 24}
:::

### Egypt
Al-Diya' relates that his opponents wrote against him to the ruler of Egypt, al-Aziz Uthman, who threatened to expel him on returning from an outing to Alexandria, and died before he returned. Al-Afdal ibn Salah al-Din then honoured him, and [[person/al-malik-al-adil-ayyub|al-Malik al-Adil]] received him warmly, telling him there was nothing to fault in him in religion or worldly matters. Later [[person/al-malik-al-kamil|al-Malik al-Kamil]] resolved to expel him and held him in a house for seven nights, until the emir Shuja' ibn Abi Dhikra interceded and the Shaykh al-Shuyukh Ibn Hamawayh and Izz al-Din al-Zanjani spoke for him, and al-Kamil wrote that he was not to be harmed. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 25} {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 26} In another account that al-Diya' heard from his companions, he was told to write out his creed and set down each point with its proof from the Qur'an and the Sunna, and al-Kamil let him go. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 26} The opponents' account says instead that the jurists of Egypt declared his blood lawful and that al-Adil's vizier al-Safi ibn Shukr wrote to the governor of Egypt to banish him to the Maghrib, but he died before the letter arrived. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 22}

### Death
He died on Monday 23 Rabi' al-Awwal 600, spent Tuesday night in the mosque, and was buried on Tuesday in [[place/al-qarafa|al-Qarafa]], facing the grave of Shaykh Abu Amr ibn Marzuq. On his deathbed he urged his son to fear God and asked his visitors to remember God. Ibn Rajab notes that Ibn al-Hanbali was mistaken in dating his death to 595. {cite:dhayl-tabaqat-hanabila-faqi-v2 p. 29}
