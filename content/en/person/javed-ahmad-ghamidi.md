## Summary
Javed Ahmad Ghamidi is a Pakistani scholar and philosopher, born on 18 April 1951 in Jiwan Shah village, Pakpattan district, Punjab. He is the founding president of the Al-Mawrid institute of Islamic research and education in Lahore and the Principal Research Fellow and Chief Patron of the Ghamidi Center of Islamic Learning in Dallas, United States. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ar} The centre's YouTube channel (@GhamidiCIL) had 366,000 subscribers in the founder's channel harvest of October 2026. {cite:youtube-ghamidi-cil}

## Early life and education
Ghamidi was born Muhammad Shafique into a Kakazai family; his father, Muhammad Tufayl Junaydi, was a follower of Sufism, the family's ancestral village was Dawud in Sialkot district, and he took the name Ghamidi from his grandfather Nur Ilahi's reputation as a muslih (reformer) in the village. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ur} He was educated in and around Pakpattan, matriculated at Islamia High School, Pakpattan, moved to Lahore in 1967, and graduated from Government College Lahore in 1972 with a B.A. (Hons) in English literature and philosophy; his early Arabic and Persian came from Maulvi Nur Ahmad in the village of Nang Pal, Sahiwal district. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ur}

From 1973 Ghamidi studied under Amin Ahsan Islahi, the exegete of the Farahi school of Qur'anic interpretation; earlier he had been associated with Abul Ala Maududi's Jamaat-e-Islami, from which he was expelled in 1977. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ar}

## Career and projects
Ghamidi taught Islamic studies at the Civil Services Academy in Lahore from 1979 to 1991. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ar} In 1983 he founded Al-Mawrid, a foundation for Islamic research and education in Lahore, and its sister organisation Danish Sara, and he founded and edited the Urdu monthly *Ishraq* (from 1985) and the English monthly *Renaissance*. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ur} He was appointed to Pakistan's Council of Islamic Ideology on 28 January 2006 and resigned in September 2006 amid the controversy over amending the Hudood Ordinances. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ar} In 2009 he received the Sitara-i-Imtiaz civil award. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en}

He appeared regularly on television — *Alif* and *Ghamidi* on Geo TV, *Live with Ghamidi* on Aaj TV, and *Ilm-o-Hikmat, Ghamidi Key Saath* on Dunya TV — and Al-Mawrid keeps its own recording studio, while his lessons also appear on YouTube channels including the Ghamidi Center Of Islamic Learning. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en}

Ghamidi left Pakistan in 2010 because of opposition to his work and threats to his life and to people close to him, after close associates including Muhammad Farooq Khan and Dr Habib-ur-Rehman had been killed; in a 2015 interview with Voice of America he said he had left to safeguard the lives of people near him. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} He lived in Malaysia before moving to the United States in July 2019 to support the establishment of the Ghamidi Center of Islamic Learning, an initiative of Al-Mawrid US, where he is Principal Research Fellow and Chief Patron. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en}

## Works
His books include *Al-Bayan* (five volumes), an annotated translation and exegesis of the Qur'an; *Mizan*, his systematic comprehensive statement of the religion; *Burhan*; *Maqamat*; *Al-Islam*; and *Khayal-o-Khamah*. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-ur} Shehzad Saleem has translated his works into English: *Mizan* appeared as *Islam: A Comprehensive Introduction*, alongside *Islam: A Concise Introduction*, *Selected Essays of Javed Ahmed Ghamidi* and *The Qur'an Translated*. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en}

## Perspectives and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**His positions, as the sources present them.** Ghamidi's method, heir to the Farahi–Islahi school, treats the Qur'an as the mizan (scale) and criterion to which everything presented as religion is referred, separates fiqh (human understanding) from the Sharia, re-reads hadith within the Qur'an's framework, and holds that armed jihad belongs only to an established state. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} The article lists among his notable ideas the separation of fiqh from Sharia, a complete framework for the study of Islam, and a counter-narrative to the mystical interpretation of Islam. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en}

**Criticism directed at him.** Whole books have been written against him, including *Fitna-e-Ghamdiyat* by Hafiz Salahuddin Yusuf, *Fitna-e-Ghamdiyat ka Ilmi Muhasbah* by Muhammad Rafiq, and a critical study by Dr Muhammad Mushtaq, former director general of the Shariah Academy at the International Islamic University Islamabad. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} The article records that opposition to his work extended to threats against his life and the killing of close associates, which led him to leave Pakistan in 2010. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en} It also records his stated principle that disagreement should remain within the bounds of respect, and that he does not pay attention to criticism of himself. {cite:wikipedia-javed-ahmad-ghamidi-en}
