## Summary
Moroccan hadith scholar and historian (1884–1962) of the learned Kattani house of Fez, the most eminent Moroccan musnid of his age. His works include Fihris al-Faharis, a vast biographical dictionary of isnad chains, Al-Taratib al-Idariyya on Moroccan administrative history, and Nizam al-Hukuma al-Nabawiyya. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-hayy-al-kattani}

## Life and formation

Abd al-Hayy ibn Abd al-Kabir al-Kattani was born in Fez in 1884 into a house uniting scholarship and Sufi leadership; he studied at al-Qarawiyyin, took authorisations from the shaykhs of Morocco and travelled east to meet the hadith masters of the Hijaz, Egypt and Syria. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-hayy-al-kattani}

He gathered in his life a great library and numerous ijazas, becoming one of the foremost transmitters (musnids) of the Muslim world, and ranged across hadith, history and political thought. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-hayy-al-kattani}

## Works

His most famous work is Fihris al-Faharis wal-Athbat in two large volumes — a biographical dictionary of the holders of isnads and registers across the Muslim world, now a reference for every student of the science of transmission. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-hayy-al-kattani}

Other works include Al-Taratib al-Idariyya on the history of Moroccan administration and offices, Nizam al-Hukuma al-Nabawiyya, Salwat al-Anfas, and treatises on ijaza, audition and history. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-hayy-al-kattani}

## Legacy

Some Moroccan sources style him 'Commander of the Faithful in hadith'; with his brother Muhammad ibn Ja'far he stands as one of the two pillars of the Kattani house in renewing hadith scholarship in Morocco, his personality uniting transmission and critical investigation. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-hayy-al-kattani}

He died in Fez in 1962; the Fihris al-Faharis remains the standard among the registers of shaykh-chains, and his library and writings are part of Morocco's scholarly memory. {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-hayy-al-kattani}
