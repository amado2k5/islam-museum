## Overview
Abu Sa'id al-Hasan ibn Ahmad ibn Yazid ibn Isa ibn al-Fadl al-Istakhri, a Shafi'i jurist. Ibn Khallikan says he was a peer of Abu l-Abbas ibn Surayj and a contemporary of Abu Ali ibn Abi Hurayra {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 74}. Ibn al-Imad calls him the great scholar and the shaykh of the Shafi'is in Iraq {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 146}.

## Learning and writings
Ibn Khallikan says he left good works in jurisprudence, among them the Book of Judgements (Kitab al-Aqdiya) {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 74}. Ibn al-Imad says he transmitted from Sa'dan ibn Nasr and his generation, and that he had a recognised view (wajh) in the school {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 146}. Among his many books is Adab al-Qada', which the imams approved {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 pp. 146-147}.

## His standing among the Shafi'is
Ibn al-Imad quotes al-Isnawi that he and Ibn Surayj were the two shaykhs of the Shafi'is in Baghdad {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 146}.

## Character and frugality
Ibn Khallikan describes him as scrupulous and frugal {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 75}. Ibn al-Imad describes him as known for asceticism and contentment {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 146}, and adds that he lived with few worldly goods and that there was a sharpness in his manner {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 147}.

## His posts
Ibn Khallikan says he was qadi of Qumm {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 74}, that he took charge of the hisba of Baghdad, and that al-Muqtadir appointed him qadi over Sijistan; he went there, examined the marriages of the people, found most of them contracted without regard to the guardian (wali), disapproved of them and annulled them all {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 75}. Ibn al-Imad says that al-Muqtadir billah appointed him over Sijistan and then over the hisba of Baghdad {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 p. 147}.

## His nisba
Ibn Khallikan says the nisba is to Istakhr, in the land of Fars, from which a number of scholars came {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 75}.

## Birth and death
Ibn Khallikan gives his birth as 244 AH and his death as Friday the twelfth of Jumada II (some say the fourteenth, and some say he died in Sha'ban) in 328 AH {cite:wafayat-ayan vol. 2 p. 75}. Ibn al-Imad says he lived a little over eighty years and died in Baghdad in 328 AH, and adds from Ibn Khallikan that he died on Friday the twelfth of Jumada II (some say the fourteenth) and was buried at Bab Harb {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 4 pp. 146-147}.
