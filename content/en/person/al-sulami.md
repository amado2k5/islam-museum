## Overview
Abu 'Abd al-Rahman Muhammad b. al-Husayn al-Sulami al-Nisaburi, shaykh of the Sufis — author of Tabaqat al-sufiyya, Haqa'iq al-tafsir and others. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.348}

## His death
Ibn al-Jawzi records him among the deaths of Sha'ban 412. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.348}

## His life in the introduction to Tabaqat al-Sufiyya
Nur al-Din Shurayba, in his introduction to [[work/tabaqat-al-sufiyya-sulami|Tabaqat al-Sufiyya]], says Abu 'Abd al-Rahman's father was an ascetic shaykh who kept company with Ibn Manazil and Abu 'Ali al-Thaqafi, shaykhs of the Malamatiyya in Khurasan, and that Abu 'Abd al-Rahman became known by the name of his mother's tribe, the Banu Sulaym, because they were prominent in Nishapur and because his maternal grandfather Abu 'Amr Isma'il ibn Nujayd raised him after his father's death. {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 16} {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 17} He quotes his pupil Abu Sa'id Muhammad ibn 'Ali al-Khashshab, who wrote a book on his life that al-Dhahabi summarised in Siyar A'lam al-Nubala', for his birth on Tuesday 10 Jumada al-Akhira 325 AH; 'Abd al-Ghafir al-Farisi and those who followed him gave 330 AH, and the editor prefers al-Khashshab because al-Sulami wrote in his own hand from Abu Bakr al-Sibghi in 333 AH. {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 17} He says al-Sulami inherited great wealth from his grandfather and devoted himself to writing; his works sold at the highest prices and were transmitted from him in his lifetime; late in life he built a small lodge for the Sufis in Nishapur, which al-Khatib al-Baghdadi visited, and he was buried in it on Sunday 3 Sha'ban 412 AH. {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 30}

## His works and his school
The editor lists more than twenty-nine of al-Sulami's books, among them Adab al-Suhba, al-Arba'in fi al-Hadith, Ta'rikh al-Sufiyya, [[work/haqaiq-al-tafsir|Haqa'iq al-Tafsir]], Risalat al-Malamatiyya, 'Uyub al-Nafs wa-Mudawatuha, al-Futuwwa and Tabaqat al-Sufiyya, with the locations of their manuscripts. {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 34} {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 41} He sees in Nishapur a Sufi school which, like al-Junayd's school in Baghdad, called for measuring everything against the Qur'an and Sunna; its leading figures were [[person/abu-nasr-al-sarraj|Abu Nasr al-Sarraj]], his pupil al-Sulami and al-Sulami's pupil [[person/al-qushayri-abu-l-qasim|al-Qushayri]], and what marked it was that the author rarely gives his own view and is content to set out the shaykhs' sayings. {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 47} {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 48}

## The scholars' views of him
Hadith critics differed over him: al-Qattan accused him of fabrication, al-Khatib rejected the charge, and al-Dhahabi said he was not strong in hadith; see [[question/reliability-of-al-sulami|the question of his standing in hadith]]. {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 44} {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 46} He was also criticised over Haqa'iq al-Tafsir. {cite:tabaqat-sufiyya-sulami-khanji-1986 p. 43}
