## Brief
Al-Abbas was an uncle of the Prophet, a rich merchant of Mecca captured fighting on the Meccan side at Badr, later a Muslim — and the ancestor of the Abbasid caliphs.

## Life
Born about 568 CE, al-Abbas traded and held interests in the pilgrims' water supply at Mecca {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}. When the Meccan army marched to Badr in 624, he marched with it and was captured; the tradition reports he paid his own ransom, and some reports say he had secretly become Muslim already {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He openly embraced Islam around the [[event/conquest-of-mecca]] in 630, fought on the Muslim side at [[event/battle-of-hunayn]], and lived on in Medina until about 653 CE, honored as the last surviving uncle {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
From al-Abbas descended the line that, a century later, overthrew the Umayyads and founded the Abbasid caliphate — the dynasty of Baghdad for five centuries. Where claims about the Prophet's family were disputed, the Abbasids' case rested on him.

## For young visitors
Al-Abbas was captured at the biggest battle of his time — on the losing side. He later joined the winning side, and his great-grandchildren founded an empire.
