## Overview
Mulla 'Ala' al-Din 'Ali ibn Mahmud al-Shahrudi al-Bustami al-Harawi the Hanafi, nicknamed Musannifak ("little author") for composing books while still young; born in 803 AH, he travelled with his brother to Herat and studied under Jalal al-Din Yusuf al-Awbahi (pupil of al-Taftazani), Qutb al-Din al-Harawi, and in both Shafi'i and Hanafi fiqh, then went to the lands of Rum and taught at Sabiquniyya until deafness struck; he moved to Constantinople where Sultan Mehmed assigned him eighty dirhams a day. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.319-321}

## His works
An imam and polymath with a Sufi licence from the line of Zayn al-Din al-Khwafi, he wrote a full quire every day; his works include sharhs of the Irshad, Misbah (grammar), Adab al-bahth, Lubab, Mutawwal, Taftazani's Sharh al-Miftah, a gloss on the Talwih, Bazdawi, Wiqaya and Hidaya, Masabih al-Baghawi and the Kashshaf, plus the Persian Anwar al-ahdaq, Tuhfat al-salatin and a Persian tafsir commissioned by Sultan Muhammad Khan. He died at Constantinople in 875 and was buried near Abu Ayyub al-Ansari's tomb. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.319-321}
