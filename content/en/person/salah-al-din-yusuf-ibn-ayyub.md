## Overview
Salah al-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub, known by the title al-Malik al-Nasir, nephew of Asad al-Din Shirkuh. Al-Maqrizi says he took the kingdom of Egypt after the Fatimid state ended, when the Ghuzz came in from Syria {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.290}.

## According to al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that Salah al-Din wanted a wall to enclose Cairo, Misr and the Citadel together, and that Qaraqush extended Cairo's wall from Bab al-Qantara to Bab al-Sha'riyya and to Bab al-Bahr {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1000}.

He says that after him Cairo was lived in by his son al-Aziz Uthman and then by his grandson al-Mansur Muhammad, then by al-Adil and al-Kamil, and that al-Kamil moved from Cairo to the Citadel {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.1001}.

## According to al-Suyuti and Ibn al-Imad
Al-Suyuti says that he took Alexandria in 562 AH, became vizier of Egypt after his uncle in 564 AH, ended the Fatimid sermon in 567 AH, and ordered the building of the wall of Cairo, the Citadel and al-Shafi'i's tomb {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.679-685}.

In the year 583 AH he took many of the Syrian lands, the greatest of them Jerusalem. He died in the year 589 AH, leaving no more money than one dinar and thirty-six dirhams {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.690-693}.

Shadharat al-Dhahab says that he died in the year 589 AH, before reaching sixty years of age. He had sixteen sons and one daughter. It is said that he conquered sixty fortresses {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.779-780}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy names Salah al-Din the Kurd among the non-Arab figures who advanced Islam {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:08:30}. In a passage on those whom history remembers with honour for dying for what is true, he names Salah al-Din with al-Qa'qa' ibn Amr; this window is partly unclear {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:35:30}.

He says al-Aqsa stayed under Crusader occupation for ninety-two years (needs_source), and reflects on the nation that nursed Salah al-Din {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:09:30}.

He reads a sermon by a judge on Salah al-Din's conquest, which he describes as combining steadfastness, remembrance and gratitude to God, and which prays for "the just king Salah al-Din" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:13:00}. Among its prayers: that God grant victory to Salah al-Din, sultan of Islam and the Muslims, who cleansed al-Aqsa of the polytheists {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:43:30}; that the land be opened by his hand as al-Aqsa was opened, and his command obeyed in East and West {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:44:00}; and that he be given an everlasting kingdom in the abode of the righteous {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:44:30}.
