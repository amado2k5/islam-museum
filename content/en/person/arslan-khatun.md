## Overview
Arslan Khatun, daughter of the caliph al-Qa'im bi-Amr Allah — one of the most famous political marriages of the age. In 454 Sultan Tughril Beg sought her hand; the matter was long drawn out until she reached the caliphal palace on 8 Jumada al-Ula with 'Amid al-Mulk Abu Nasr and the qadi of Rayy, the dower and trousseau in their train; the caliph delayed the surrender until 'Amid al-Mulk pressed it to the contract witnessed by himself and the qadi. She was escorted to the Sultan on Monday night 15 Safar 455 with a pavilion pitched from the Tigris to the palace; the Sultan entered, kissed the ground and left without intimacy — she neither rose for him nor lifted her veil nor saw him — and he sent her two splendid necklaces and a red ruby; he was seventy when he married her. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.560-562; shadharat-dhahab p.302-303}

## Before the marriage
In 451, when al-Basasiri held the caliph's harem captive, she sent with 'Amid al-Mulk to her exiled father twelve large pearls, asking that he take them as prayer beads. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.549}
