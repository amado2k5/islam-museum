## Overview
Abd al-Malik ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Qushayri al-Nasawi al-Daqiqi al-Tammar, the ascetic, known as Abu Nasr al-Tammar. Al-Safadi, in al-Wafi bi-l-Wafayat, gives his death as 228 AH {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 121}. Ibn al-Imad lists him in Shadharat al-Dhahab among those who died in 228 AH, calling him the ascetic in Baghdad and placing his death at the beginning of the year {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 3 p. 130}.

## Transmission of hadith
Al-Safadi says that Muslim transmitted from him, and that al-Nasa'i transmitted from him through one man and through a group {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 121}. Ibn al-Imad says that he transmitted from Hammad ibn Salama and his generation {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 3 p. 130}.

## What was said of him
Ibn al-Imad describes him as reliable, firm, learned, devout, obedient and scrupulous, and says he was counted among the abdal {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 3 p. 130}. Al-Safadi reports that Ahmad ibn Hanbal did not consider it right to write down from him, nor from anyone who had been put to the test and had complied {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 121}.

## A dream about his standing
Al-Safadi relates from Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Abi l-Ward, the muezzin of Bishr al-Hafi, that he saw Bishr in a dream and asked what God had done with him; Bishr answered that he had been forgiven. Asked about Abu Nasr al-Tammar, Bishr replied that his place was far higher, among the highest ranks, because of his poverty and his patience over his building {cite:wafi-bi-l-wafayat-safadi-dar-ihya-2000 vol. 19 p. 121}. This is a dream report that al-Safadi transmits; the museum passes no judgement on it.
