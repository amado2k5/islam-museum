## Overview
Abd al-Razzaq ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Sabuni, known as Ibn al-Fuwati, of Marwazi origin, a Shaybani of Baghdad, Abu al-Fadl Kamal al-Din, was a historian counted among the philosophers, descended from Ma'n ibn Za'ida al-Shaybani; he died at Baghdad in 723 AH (1323 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.349}

Born at Baghdad and taken captive in its catastrophe with the Tatars, he was freed by Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, under whom he read philosophy and letters, and ran the observatory library at Maragha for about ten years; returning to Baghdad in 679 AH he became keeper of the Mustansiriyya library for a time, then stayed long at Tabriz with the vizier Rashid al-Din al-Hamadhani until the latter's execution in 718 AH, when his own and Ibn al-Fuwati's books were burned; he returned to Baghdad and stayed until his death. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.349}

His works include Majma' al-Adab, a biographical lexicon of names and titles in four parts said to run to fifty volumes; Durar al-Asdaf fi Ghurar al-Awsaf; Talqih al-Afham, a history from the creation to the sack of Baghdad; Nazm al-Durar al-Nasi'a on the poets of the seventh century in several volumes; and al-Hawadith al-Jami'a wa-l-Tajarib al-Nafi'a on the same century. {cite:al-aalam v.3 p.349}
