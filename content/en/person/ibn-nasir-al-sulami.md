## Overview
The trustworthy hafiz Abu'l-Fadl Ibn Nasir Muhammad ibn Nasir al-Salami of Baghdad, muhaddith of Iraq; born in 467, he took to hadith after mastering fiqh, and switched from the Shafi'i to the Hanbali school because of a dream in which the Prophet told him "keep to the school of Shaykh Abu Mansur al-Khayyat"; people used to say Ibn Nasir would come out Baghdad's philologist and Ibn al-Jawaliqi its muhaddith — it turned out the reverse. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## Testimonies
Ibn al-Jawzi said: "he is the one who gave me my hadith auditions and from him I took all I took of the science — I read to him thirty years and profited from no one as I did from him"; Abu Musa al-Madini called him the leading hadith scholar of Baghdad in his time; he endowed his books as waqf, left worn clothes and three dinars, and died childless. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## In the chain of Fada'il al-Sahaba
Ibn Nasir is the hafiz with whom the transmission of Ahmad ibn Hanbal's [[work/fadail-al-sahaba-ahmad|Fada'il al-Sahaba]] begins in its unique manuscript: he read it to [[person/abu-al-husayn-al-tayuri|Abu al-Husayn al-Mubarak ibn Abd al-Jabbar al-Sayrafi]] in Rajab 500 AH {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.55}. The editor vocalises his *nisba* as "al-Salami", after Madinat al-Salam (Baghdad), and records that he was of Persian origin and born in 467 {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.37-38}. Ibn al-Najjar quotes him as saying that he began hearing Ahmad's books and *Masa'il* and studying law in his school in Ramadan 493 {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.38}.

### What scholars said of him
Al-Silafi said that Ibn Nasir heard much with him, that he had been a Shafi'i and an Ash'ari and then moved to Ahmad's school in principles and branches and died in it, and that he was excellent, retentive and precise — a firm imam {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.38}. [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] said: he was a hafiz, exact and precise, trustworthy, a man of the Sunna with no fault in him, much given to remembrance of God and quick to tears {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.38}. Ibn al-Najjar said: his transmission was good and his accuracy sound, he had a wide command of grammar and language, his originals were perfectly correct, and he endowed his books to the scholars of hadith {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.38}. He died in 550 and left no children {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.38}.

### Readings of the book before him
At the end of the manuscript a reading-note records that a group heard the whole book from him, read aloud by Diya' al-Din Abu Ahmad Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ali al-Amin — among them Fatima, daughter of Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Abi Ghalib known as Ibn al-Shaykh al-Bazzaz, and Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Qurashi, the copyist — in sessions ending on Tuesday 6 Shawwal 544; Ibn Nasir wrote in his own hand: "This is correct as he has stated" {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1258}. The editor counts the fact that the manuscript was read to him, a man known for the accuracy of his originals, among the reasons to trust it {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.44}.
