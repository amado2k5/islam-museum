## Summary

Abbas Mirza (26 August 1789 – 25 October 1833) was the Qajar crown prince of Iran during the reign of his father Fath-Ali Shah {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en} {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-ar}. As governor of Azerbaijan he commanded in the two wars against Russia and the war of 1821–1823 against the Ottoman Empire, and he is recognized for leading Iran's first reform and modernization attempts {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en}.

## Crown prince and governor

He was born on 26 August 1789 in the village of Nava in Mazandaran, the fourth son of Fath-Ali Shah and of Asiya Khanom Devellu, a marriage arranged by his grand-uncle Agha Mohammad Khan to unite the Qovanlu and Davalu branches of the Qajars {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en}. On 20 March 1799 he was made crown prince with the title Nayeb al-Saltaneh and appointed governor of Azerbaijan, with Tabriz as his regional capital and Mirza Bozorg Qa'em-Maqam as his tutor and minister {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en}.

## Wars with Russia

Designated commander against Russia after the fall of Ganja in 1804, when he was fifteen, Abbas Mirza fought through the first war to the defeats at Aslanduz and Lankaran and the Treaty of Gulistan of October 1813 {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en}. In the second war, from 1826, he first won back most of the lost territory, then suffered the string of defeats that ended in the Treaty of Turkmenchay of February 1828 and the loss of the last Caucasian holdings; the losses broke his health and his enthusiasm for reform {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en} {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-ar}.

## War with the Ottomans and reforms

In 1821–1823 he campaigned against the Ottomans alongside his brother Dowlatshah, taking Bayazit and Toprak Qala and marching on Erzurum before cholera forced peace at Erzurum in 1823 {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en} {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-ar}. His answer to Russian superiority was the Nezam-e Jadid, a new-model army: Russian prisoners and deserters first, then French and mainly British instructors, uniforms, the title sarbaz for the soldier, student missions to Britain in 1811 and 1815, a printing press at Tabriz in 1812 for military manuals, a powder factory and arsenal {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en} {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-ar}. The traditionalist clergy at first opposed the European-style uniforms as un-Islamic clothing, until the state persuaded them the reforms were required for the defence of Muslim lands {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en}.

## Death

In the summer and fall of 1832 he campaigned east and northeast of Mashhad, taking Khabushan, Sarakhs and Torbat-e Heydarieh to bolster his claim and make up his losses {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en}. Under continuous medical care by Western and Iranian physicians, he died of illness at Mashhad on 25 October 1833; his father died the following year and the throne passed to Abbas Mirza's son Mohammad Shah {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-en} {cite:wikipedia-abbas-mirza-ar}.
