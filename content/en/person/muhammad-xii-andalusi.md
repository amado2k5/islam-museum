## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ali, known as Abu Abdallah, son of Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Sa'd ibn Ali ibn Yusuf ibn Muhammad al-Ghani bi-llah, of the Nasrid house of Banu al-Ahmar, an Ansari of the Khazraj, whom the Spaniards call Boabdil, is al-Zirikli's last king of al-Andalus. He quotes al-Maqqari that Granada was taken in this sultan's time and that the realm of Islam in al-Andalus came to an end with his state. Al-Zirikli gives his death as 940 AH, 1533 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 290}.

## Life
He was born in Granada and grew up under his father Abu al-Hasan al-Ghani bi-llah, whom the Spaniards call Muley Hacen. He was present at some of the battles, and the Spaniards captured him in 888 AH. His father went blind and grew too weak to manage the realm, and put forward a brother, Muhammad ibn Sa'd, known as al-Zaghal, giving up power to him before 890 AH. The Spaniards saw strength in al-Zaghal, so they agreed with his nephew Abu Abdallah, still in their captivity, to release him, with a truce and peace between them and those under his rule {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 290}.

He went to Balish, whose people obeyed him in 891 AH, then advanced on the quarter of al-Bayyazin near Granada, where its people backed him, and fighting broke out with his uncle al-Zaghal. Abu Abdallah asked the Spaniards for help while at peace with them and they supplied it. Al-Zaghal had to leave Granada to turn Spanish raiders away from nearby districts, and Abu Abdallah entered it and was given the oath of allegiance by its people in 892 AH. Al-Zaghal ended by making peace with the Spaniards and serving them, then sailed to Oran and settled in Tlemcen, where, al-Maqqari says, his descendants are known as the sons of the sultan of al-Andalus {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 290}.

## The fall of Granada and exile
The Spaniards asked to station a force in the Alhambra in Granada; he refused them entry, they turned on him and the peace broke down, and he fought them from 895 AH in an indecisive war lasting about two years. Granada was besieged and its people went hungry, and their leaders advised him to make peace and let the enemy have the Alhambra. He concluded a treaty of 67 articles, most of which, al-Zirikli says, are set out in volume 2 of Nafh al-Tib, p. 1268. The enemy took the Alhambra and mastered all of Granada, then told him to leave for the village of Andarsh in the Albujarras, and he moved there with his household, servants and goods in 897 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 290–291}.

Al-Zirikli reports that King Ferdinand made it appear that Abu Abdallah had asked to cross to the Maghrib shore, and wrote to the governor of Almeria to see him off wherever he wished. He sailed from Adra, landed at Melilla and settled in Fez. Al-Zirikli quotes the author of Laqt al-Fara'id that the enemy entered Granada on 2 Rabi' al-Awwal 897 AH and that its sultan left and settled in Fez {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 pp. 290–291}.

## What was said of his end
Al-Zirikli quotes al-Maqqari, who died in 1041 AH, that he reached Fez with his family and children and built some palaces there in the Andalusi manner, which al-Maqqari saw and entered, and that his descendants in Fez as of 1037 AH drew on the endowments for the poor and needy. He also quotes Shakib Arslan that Muslim rule in al-Andalus had lasted 778 years, from the defeat of Roderic on the banks of the Guadalete to the surrender of Granada {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 291}.
