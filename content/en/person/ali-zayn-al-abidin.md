## Overview
Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib, known as Zayn al-Abidin, "the ornament of the worshippers", and as al-Sajjad, was the son of [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn]] who survived [[event/battle-of-karbala|Karbala]]. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.68} The Encyclopaedia of Islam calls him the fourth Imam of the Twelver [[group/imamiyya|Shi'a]], and its article on him is the basis of the sections that follow on his name, birth, mother, death and writings. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 500} Positions about him differ according to who is speaking, and this page labels them as the article does.

## Name and birth
The article says that his kunya is given in different ways, among them Abu Abd Allah and Abu Bakr. Many sources place his birth in [[place/medina|Medina]] in 38/658-9, though other years are also given, namely 33, 36 and 37. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 500} Accounts that he had not yet reached puberty at the time of the massacre at Karbala in 61/680 would push his birth into the 660s; the article notes that [[person/al-waqidi|al-Waqidi]] and other authorities reject those accounts. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 500} The article gives his full name as Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib, which traces his descent from [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] through al-Husayn. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 500}

## His mother
Sources give his mother's name variously as Barra, Ghazala or Jayda, and some say she was an umm walad, a concubine who bore her master a child, from [[place/sind|Sind]] or Sijistan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 500} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 501} The Shi'i tradition, by contrast, holds that she was a daughter of the last Sasanian emperor Yazdagird III, and gives her Persian name as Jihanshah, Shahrbanu or Shahzanan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 501} Reports also differ on her fate: some say she threw herself into the Euphrates after the battle, while others say she was among those who survived Karbala. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 501}

## The title "son of the two elect"
The article records that Shi'is call him ibn al-khiyaratayn, "the son of the two elect", on the strength of a tradition attributed to the Prophet about the [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]] and the non-Arabs. The OCR of the rest of this passage is cut off, so the full reasoning is not reproduced here. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 501}

## Death, burial and family
He is said to have died in 94/712 or 95/713; other dates mentioned are 92, 93, 99 and 100. He was buried in the cemetery of [[place/al-baqi|al-Baqi]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502} Shi'i authors maintain that he was poisoned on the orders of the reigning caliph [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid]] or of his brother [[person/hisham-ibn-abd-al-malik|Hisham]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502} He is said to have had between eight and fifteen children, of whom four sons were by his wife Umm Abd Allah, daughter of al-Hasan ibn Ali, and the rest by concubines. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502}

## Writings attributed to him
Several short texts are ascribed to him, including an al-Sahifa fi l-zuhd, found in al-Kulayni's al-Kafi. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502} He is also credited with a Risalat al-Huquq, preserved in two versions in two works of the 4th/10th century: Ibn Babawayh's Kitab al-Khisal and Ibn Shuba's Tuhaf al-uqul. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502} His collection of prayers, known as al-Sahifa al-kamila or al-Sahifa al-sajjadiyya, became very widely popular. The article reports numerous redactions and over twenty commentaries, a Persian translation made in the Safawid period, fifteen further "whispered prayers" added to several modern editions, and a complete English translation by W. C. Chittick published in 1988. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502}

## Positions about him
Sunni position: the article as read here does not set out a separate Sunni statement about him. The museum's sections below draw on other sources, where he appears in a Sunni historical and devotional setting, and no verdict is given between the positions.

Twelver Shi'i position: he is the fourth Imam, the article's own framing, and the Shi'i accounts above give him a Sasanian royal mother, the title ibn al-khiyaratayn and a death by poison ordered by an Umayyad caliph. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 500} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 501} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502}

Other views: the article records disagreement among sources, not between named schools, over his birth year, his mother's origin and the year of his death. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 500} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 501} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 11 p. 502}

## The day of al-Harra
Al-Mas'udi relates that on the day of al-Harra the army saw him clinging to the grave and praying, and he had sworn allegiance on the condition that they were slaves to Yazid. He was brought to Musrif in a rage. When Musrif saw him, he trembled and stood up for him, seated him beside himself and said: "Ask me your needs." He did not ask for anyone who had been brought forward to the sword without Musrif granting him intercession. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.68}

Musrif is quoted as saying: "That was not my opinion; my heart was filled with terror of it." {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.68}

## Narration from his grandfather Ali
Al-Sallabi counts him among those of Ali's household who narrated from Ali, being the son of al-Husayn. He says that his mother was Sulafa, daughter of Yazdajird, and that he died in 94 AH, aged fifty-eight. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 71}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 3)

The Sheikh relates that during the pilgrimage season Ibn Abd al-Malik tried to reach the Black Stone but could not because of the crowd, then a man with a radiant face came, the people made room for him and waited in two lines until he kissed the Stone, and Ibn Abd al-Malik asked who this was {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:52:30}. He says that the man was Ali Zayn al-Abidin ibn al-Husayn of the Family of the Prophet, whom a poet praised in a remarkable poem, and the Sheikh comments on the people's reverence for him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:53:00}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy says that people used to make way for Ali Zayn al-Abidin at the circumambulation out of reverence for him, and that al-Farazdaq praised him in his famous poem when asked who he was (see [[person/farazdaq|al-Farazdaq]]). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:43:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh relates that Ali Zayn al-Abidin ibn al-Husayn was in Kufa for forty years, and the poor opened their doors at dawn to find food, drink, clothing and money without knowing who left them, until he died and it stopped, and they knew it had been he; he tells it as an example of secret charity (needs_source: the place and duration as the Sheikh gives them). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep22 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep22 00:08:30}
