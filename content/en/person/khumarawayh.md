## Overview
Abu al-Jaysh Khumarawayh ibn Ahmad ibn Tulun. He remained over his father's provinces after his father's death in 270 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 568}

## With Abu al-Abbas ibn al-Muwaffaq
Al-Suyuti says that al-Muwaffaq appointed his son Abu al-Abbas over his affairs, and sent him to Egypt with the troops of Iraq. A great battle took place between them, in which the land was soaked with blood, and the victory went to the Egyptians. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 568} In al-Mas'udi, Abu al-Abbas defeated him on the day of the Mills in 271 AH; he escaped to Fustat, and appointed Ali ibn Ahmad al-Madarani as his vizier. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 579-580}

## His killing
In al-Mas'udi he was slaughtered in Damascus in Dhu al-Qa'da 282 AH, and one of his servants took charge of the slaughter. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 610-611} In Ibn al-Jawzi, some of his servants slaughtered him on his bed; after him his son Jaysh succeeded, and then his brother Harun. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 155}

## In Yeomans: wars and settlement
Khumarawayh succeeded his father at twenty, but Samarra did not recognise him and he had to fight to keep Egypt independent: the governors of Mosul, Anbar and Damascus allied against him, and he met them near Ramla in Palestine with seventy thousand men; he fled in the heat of the battle, but his reserve held its ground and won, and he took Damascus. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.40} A year later his former general Sa'd al-A'sar rebelled in Damascus; he marched on him, defeated him, then invaded Iraq and took Mosul. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.40} In 886 al-Muwaffaq concluded an agreement guaranteeing him and his descendants Egypt, Syria, Cilicia and parts of northern Iraq for thirty years; it was reaffirmed in 896 when the caliph al-Mu'tadid married his daughter, a wedding Yeomans puts at a million dinars to Khumarawayh. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.40}

## His palace and garden
Yeomans quotes al-Maqrizi: Khumarawayh enlarged the palace and turned the Maydan into a garden of rare trees and roses, sheathing the trunks in gilt copper with lead pipes carrying water between sheet and trunk; he added a pigeon tower full of birds, adorned his "Golden House" with gold and ultramarine and set up carved wooden statues of himself and his wives, and before the palace laid out a lake of quicksilver fifty cubits square, on his physician's advice against insomnia, where he slept on an air-bed guarded by his blue-eyed lion Zurayq. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.40} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.41} Yeomans thinks such descriptions built the mythology of the Muslim court, fed the imagination of the Arabian Nights and shaped the expectations of later palace architecture. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.41} Khumarawayh was murdered by his slaves on a visit to Damascus in 896 after a harem intrigue, Yeomans writes. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.41}
