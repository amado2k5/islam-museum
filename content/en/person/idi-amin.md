# Idi Amin

Idi Amin Dada was a Ugandan military officer who became the third president of Uganda in the coup of 25 January 1971 and ruled until his overthrow on 11 April 1979. His regime was characterised by rampant human-rights abuses, economic mismanagement and corruption, and international observers and human-rights groups estimate that between 100,000 and 500,000 people were killed under it. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en}

## Early life and family

Accounts of his birth disagree, because no birth records were kept for native Ugandans at the time: British governmental records give the year 1925, while family tradition and the Saudi authorities in Jeddah give 10 Dhu al-Hijja 1346 AH, corresponding to 30 May 1928, at Kampala, and Amin himself gave ages in interviews implying different years. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} {cite:wikidata-idi-amin} He was born to a Kakwa father who had been christened a Roman Catholic and converted to Islam in 1910, changing his name to Amin Dada, and to a Lugbara mother. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-ar} He was given the name Idi after his birth on the Muslim holiday of Eid al-Adha, and as a boy he moved to the home of a sheikh in the town of Semuto and memorised the Quran by recitation until he was twelve; he continued at the madrasa in Bombo until 1944 and reportedly won honours in recitation. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en}

## Military career and rise

Amin joined the King's African Rifles, part of the British colonial army, in 1946 as a cook, rose to the rank of lieutenant, and took part in British actions against Somali rebels and then in the Mau Mau Uprising in Kenya. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} After Uganda's independence in 1962 he remained in the army, becoming deputy army commander in 1964 and commander two years later. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} On 25 January 1971 he led the coup that overthrew President Milton Obote and took the presidency. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en}

## Rule

Amin ruled from 1971 to 1979. In 1972 he expelled Uganda's Asian residents. His regime favoured his own groups in senior posts: by 1977 Muslims formed the large majority of the top generals and of the cabinet, although they were only about five per cent of the population, according to the English article. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} His rule ended with the Uganda–Tanzania War, and he was overthrown on 11 April 1979. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en}

## Exile and death

Amin first escaped to Libya, where he stayed until 1980, and ultimately settled in Saudi Arabia, where the Saudi royal family allowed him sanctuary and paid him a subsidy in return for staying out of politics; in interviews in exile he never expressed remorse for the brutal nature of his regime. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} He died at Jeddah on 16 August 2003 and was buried at Ruwais Cemetery there. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} {cite:wikidata-idi-amin}

## Note on the record

Amin was raised in a Muslim family, memorised the Quran as a boy and carried the title Al-Hajji — upbringing, formation and practice, none of which names a school. None of the sources consulted — the English, Arabic and Urdu articles and Wikidata — names a religious school for him, and Wikidata sets his religion value explicitly to no value. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en} {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-ar} {cite:wikidata-idi-amin} His faith is therefore recorded as unknown.

Neither his predecessor Milton Obote nor his successor Yusuf Lule has a record in the museum, so no succession relation is written; the relation written is his death at Jeddah. {cite:wikipedia-idi-amin-en}
