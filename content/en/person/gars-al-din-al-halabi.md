## Summary
Gars al-Din ibn Ahmad ibn Khalil al-Halabi was a Syrian astronomer who died in 971 AH (1563) according to Suter, as Heinen reports. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 31}

## Tanbih al-naqqad
He wrote [[work/tanbih-al-naqqad|Tanbih al-naqqad ala ma fi al-hay'a al-mashhura min al-fasad]], dated by its colophon to 958 AH (1551), a title showing his dissatisfaction with the prevailing astronomy. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 31} In a chapter on the accidental motion of the celestial bodies and spheres he defended the older view against a newer one, using [[concept/earth-rotation-ship-analogy|the ship analogy]]. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 31} {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 32}

## His critique of Ptolemaic astronomy
In the book he rejects basic Ptolemaic notions such as the equant and the eccentric deferent, citing Ibn al-Shatir and Mu'ayyad al-Din al-Urdi while finding difficulties in their theories too. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 32} He notes that the observed diameters of Venus and Mars do not fit the accepted theory, and in geography draws on navigators' experience that the inhabited north extends to 72 degrees. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 32}
