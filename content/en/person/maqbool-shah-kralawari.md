Peer Maqbool Shah Kralawari (1820–1877) was a nineteenth-century Kashmiri poet educated in Persian literature and counted among the finest poets of his century in Kashmir.{cite:wikipedia-maqbool-shah-kralawari-en} The queue's death year of 1875 is corrected by the articles, which give 1877; Wikidata instead gives 1876.{cite:wikipedia-maqbool-shah-kralawari-en}{cite:wikidata-maqbool-shah-kralawari} He wrote in both Persian and Kashmiri, turning his hand to devotional poems, satire and the ghazal.{cite:wikipedia-maqbool-shah-kralawari-en}

His reputation rests on Gulrez ("Scattered Flowers"), his great mathnavi, a free rendering of a Persian romance by Zia-ud-Din Nakhshabi and often judged the finest descriptive poetry of nineteenth-century Kashmiri literature.{cite:wikipedia-maqbool-shah-kralawari-en} Abdul Ahad Azad praised the range that moved between registers: when Maqbool writes tasawwuf, Azad said, he seems a Sufi of the highest rank, and when he delivers a message of hope he recalls Iqbal's fervour.{cite:wikipedia-maqbool-shah-kralawari-en} Mohammad Yousuf Taing collected most of his surviving poetry and published it in two volumes (1972, 1978).{cite:wikipedia-maqbool-shah-kralawari-en}{cite:wikipedia-maqbool-shah-kralawari-ks}
