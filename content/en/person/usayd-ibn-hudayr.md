Abu Yahya Usayd ibn Hudayr ibn Simak al-Ashhali al-Awsi, chief, knight and noble of Banu Abd al-Ashhal at Yathrib before Islam. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He came up spear in hand, angry at [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]] and [[person/asad-ibn-zurara|As'ad ibn Zurara]] when it reached him they had lodged in Banu Zafar's garden calling to Islam, and he said: what brings you to us, making fools of our weak? Mus'ab said: "will you sit and listen? If you approve a matter, take it; if you dislike it, it is kept from you" — he planted his spear, sat, heard the Qur'an, and his face lit with joy as he became Muslim. He said: "behind me is a man — if he follows you, none of his people will hold back: Sa'd ibn Mu'adh" — and his Islam was the key to the Islam of the Aws and the whole tribe of Banu Abd al-Ashhal. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He witnessed the second pledge of Aqaba as one of the twelve naqibs, witnessed Badr and Uhud and stood firm with the Prophet; he was among the greatest of the Messenger of God's companions and one of those for whose Qur'an recitation the angels descended. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy relates that Usayd ibn Hudayr was reciting the Qur'an at night when his horse started, and he saw above him something like a canopy with what looked like lamps, so he stopped reciting; the Prophet told him, 'Recite, Usayd', and explained that these were angels who had come down for his recitation, and that had he kept reciting the people of Medina would have seen them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:35:30} He concludes that when a righteous man recites with a present heart, mercy descends upon his recitation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:35:30}
