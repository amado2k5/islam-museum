## Summary
Thoraya Ahmed Obaid (born 1945) is a Saudi international civil servant and public figure who served as Executive Director of the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) from 2001 to 2010 — the first Saudi national to head a United Nations agency — and was appointed in 2013 among the first women members of Saudi Arabia's Shura Council. Her career is frequently cited as a landmark for Saudi and Arab women in international public service. {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-en} {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-ar} {cite:themuslim500-thoraya-obaid}

## Early life and education
Obaid was born in Baghdad in 1945 and grew up in Saudi Arabia in an era when formal schooling for girls was only beginning in the kingdom; she was among the early Saudi women sent to school, later completing higher education in the United States. She earned a doctorate in English literature and cultural anthropology from Wayne State University in Michigan, having earlier studied at Mills College in California — one of the first Saudi women to pursue postgraduate study abroad. {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-en}

## United Nations career
She joined the United Nations in the 1970s, working with the Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia (ESCWA) and later with the UNFPA, where she became director of the division for Arab states and Europe. In 2000 the UN Secretary-General appointed her Executive Director of UNFPA, the agency charged with population, reproductive health and gender equality programmes worldwide; she took office in January 2001 and was reappointed for a second term, serving until 2010. During her tenure she argued publicly that reproductive health, women's education and family planning were compatible with religious values, frequently addressing religious scholars and framing the agency's goals in the language of dignity and family welfare. {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-en} {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-ar}

## Shura Council and later work
In January 2013 King Abdullah appointed her among the first thirty women to sit on Saudi Arabia's Shura Council, the consultative assembly — a step she had herself helped prepare by decades of advocacy for women's participation. In council and public life she has been associated with modest, institution-centred reform: education, health and the family rather than confrontation. She has also served on boards and commissions concerned with development and women's advancement, including work with the UN system after her UNFPA tenure. {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-en} {cite:themuslim500-thoraya-obaid}

## Standing and notes
The Muslim 500 lists her under Social Issues, presenting her as the first Saudi to head a UN agency and a member of the Shura Council. Her public image inside and outside the kingdom is that of a pioneer who pressed for women's advancement through institutions rather than through protest — a model admired by supporters of incremental change and seen by critics of gradualism as too cautious; the museum reports these views attributed, without verdict. No source read names a religious school for her, and this record records none. {cite:themuslim500-thoraya-obaid} {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-en}

## Recognition
Obaid's decade at UNFPA coincided with intense debate inside and outside the Muslim world over population policy, and she used the platform to convene religious leaders and to publicise statements of support from Muslim scholars for the agency's goals. She also chaired the UN System Chief Executives Board's coordination work on issues touching women, and after leaving the fund she remained a public advocate for girls' education and health, lending her name to campaigns across the Arab region. Her dual status — insider of the Saudi establishment and senior UN official — made her a unique interlocutor between the kingdom's institutions and international organisations during a period in which both were changing. {cite:wikipedia-thoraya-obaid-en} {cite:themuslim500-thoraya-obaid}
