## Overview
Ibn al-Munayyir, qadi of Alexandria and its famous scholar, a Maliki (620–683); brilliant in fiqh, usul, dialectic, Arabic and rhetoric, with authored works; he died at the start of Rabi' I {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 652}.

## In al-Intisaf
Al-Intisaf is printed in the footnotes of the Dar al-Ma'rifa edition of [[work/al-kashshaf|al-Kashshaf]], each of Ibn al-Munayyir's replies opening with 'Ahmad said'. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.42} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384} On the sealing of hearts he counts six faults against [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]], the first being that it goes against the proof of God's oneness, which requires that nothing comes to be except by His power {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.42}; on God's speaking to Moses he sees in al-Zamakhshari's words an open statement that God's speech is created, and holds that Moses heard the eternal speech without intermediary {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384}; on the request to see God he holds it rationally possible but, by revelation, not to occur in this world {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.385}. See [[question/who-seals-the-hearts-in-al-baqara-7|Who seals the hearts?]] and [[question/does-lan-tarani-deny-that-god-can-be-seen|Does 'You will not see Me' deny the vision?]].
