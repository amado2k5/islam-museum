## Summary

Nasr Hamid Abu Zayd (1943–2010) was an Egyptian researcher in Islamic studies and hermeneutics, author of a project reading the Quranic text as a historically produced cultural product, and the central figure of Egypt's famous 1995 apostasy case.{cite:wikipedia-nasr-hamid-abu-zayd}

## Life

Born in the village of Quhafa, Gharbiyya, he studied Arabic literature at Cairo University, completed a doctorate in tafsir and Quranic sciences, and taught at Cairo's Faculty of Arts. After the 1995 ruling he moved to teach at Leiden University in the Netherlands.{cite:wikipedia-nasr-hamid-abu-zayd}

## Intellectual project

In Mafhum al-Nass (The Concept of the Text, 1990) and Naqd al-Khitab al-Dini (Critique of Religious Discourse) he presented the Quranic text as a linguistic-cultural system read through modern hermeneutics, distinguishing the text from its historical meaning. The religious mainstream rejected the method and opponents challenged his faith in court.{cite:wikipedia-nasr-hamid-abu-zayd}

## The case and the debate

Opponents filed a hisba suit demanding his separation from his wife, and the appeals court ruled him an apostate in 1995 — a ruling that did not imprison him but annulled his marriage and drove the couple abroad. Admirers call him a martyr for free thought, opponents call him a deviant from Muslim belief — the museum records both without a verdict.{cite:wikipedia-nasr-hamid-abu-zayd}
