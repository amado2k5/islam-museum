## Overview
Al-Malik al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun, a Mamluk sultan who held the sultanate three times. The amirs gave him the allegiance after his brother al-Ashraf, when he was nine years old, in 693 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 730-731}

## Between deposition and return
He was deposed in 694 AH, and Kitbugha al-Mansuri took the sultanate, then Lajin in 696 AH. When Lajin was killed in 698 AH, al-Nasir was brought back from his exile at al-Karak. The caliph invested him, and sent al-Adil to Hama as deputy. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 730-731}

## Karak and his third return
Al-Suyuti says that in 708 AH he set out for the pilgrimage, but the bridge broke under him at al-Karak, and he escaped, while four others died. He stayed at al-Karak and wrote to depose himself from the kingdom, so Baybars al-Jashnakir was given the allegiance. Then he returned in Rajab 709 AH and entered Egypt on the day of the Feast of Breaking the Fast. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 734}

## According to al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that in 702 AH the sultan in Egypt was al-Malik al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun, the affairs of state being managed by the amir Rukn al-Din Baybars al-Jashnakir, with Salar as deputy of the sultanate. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.240}

He also says that al-Nasir built a bridge across the Nile to break the force of the current from the bank. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.241}

He says that al-Nasir built the new mosque known as al-Jami' al-Jadid al-Nasiri outside Misr. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.831}

## Notes for researchers
- The years fall in the eighth century AH; al-Maqrizi's mention of 702 AH confirms the century.
- The summary is based on al-Suyuti's "History of the Caliphs"; al-Maqrizi is added in the section above.

## In Ibn Hajar's al-Durar al-Kamina
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Muhammad ibn Qalawun, al-Malik al-Nasir: born in Safar, or mid-Muharram, 684, he reigned three times — first after his brother al-Ashraf was killed in mid-Muharram 693, second after Lajin was killed in 698, and third from the feast of Eid al-Fitr 709 after his withdrawal to al-Karak. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/144} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/145} Ibn Hajar describes him as an obeyed and feared king who honoured scholars, and says he made the pilgrimage three times. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/146} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/147} He died in the Citadel on 19 Dhu al-Hijja 741. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 4/148}

## His three reigns and his building in Yeomans
Al-Nasir Muhammad came to the throne at eight after his brother al-Ashraf Khalil; after a year Kitbugha deposed him, Lajin deposed Kitbugha, and when Lajin was murdered al-Nasir was restored at fourteen, a puppet in his second reign (1299-1309) while the amirs Salar and Baybars al-Jashankir held power, until he abdicated and withdrew to Karak and Baybars became sultan. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.144} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.145} He returned at twenty-five by popular acclamation and began his third reign (1310-1340) by purging the court: Baybars was strangled and Salar starved to death in prison. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.145} He defeated the Mongols at Marj al-Suffar in 1303, made a final peace with the Ilkhanids in 1323 and married the Mongol princess Tulbiya, opening trade with Iran and China that marked Islamic art. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.138} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.140}

Yeomans counts him one of the great patrons of art and architecture: he completed [[building/madrasa-of-al-nasir-muhammad|his madrasa and mausoleum]] on al-Mu'izz street, built [[building/al-nasir-muhammad-mosque|his mosque]] on the Citadel (1318-1335) and its palace complex including the [[building/ablaq-palace-cairo|Ablaq Palace]] (1313-1315), and set up the mashrabiyya screen in his father Qalawun's mausoleum, where he chose to be buried. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.137} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.140} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.143} Under him the city spread between the Citadel and Bab Zuwayla, and his sons-in-law al-Maridani and Aqsunqur built two new congregational mosques. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.151} He died in 1340 aged fifty-eight and was followed in quick succession by eight sons, two grandsons and two great-grandsons. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.154}
