## Summary
Abu Abd Allah Sa'id ibn Yazid al-Saji is one of the ascetics treated by [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] in the ninth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (notice 450). The rhymed opening describes him as a man who cried out from his own self to his Lord and longed for Him. Most of his sayings are related by [[person/ahmad-ibn-abi-al-hawari|Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari]] and by Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Bakr al-Qurashi. The notice gives no date of birth or death. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/310-311}

## Five things
He said that a believer should know five things: knowledge of God, knowledge of what is right, sincerity of action for God, acting by the Sunna, and eating what is lawful. He ordered them so that none is of use to someone who lacks the next, and made lawful food the condition for benefiting from all of them. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/310}

## On contentment
For him the root of worship is three things: to reject none of God's decrees, to hold nothing back from Him, and to ask no one else for a need. He said: if you want to be among the abdal (the hidden saints), love what God wills, for whoever loves that will love every decree that befalls him. He urged his companions to reflect long on contentment with God and to question one another about it. He quoted a man who said that, given one answered prayer, he would not ask for Paradise but for contentment, because it is Paradise brought forward. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/312-315}

## Other sayings
He said that we should rely more on the prayers of our brothers than on our own deeds. He said that whoever lets this world cross his mind for any purpose other than carrying out God's command is veiled from God. Ibn Abi al-Ward relates from him that heedlessness of God is worse than entering the Fire, and that when anger enters the mind, scruple leaves. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/312-313} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/317}

## The prayer at Tarsus
Ibn Abi al-Ward tells that he once led the people of [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]] in prayer when the call to arms was raised, and he did not shorten the prayer. When they reproached him, he said that prayer is so named because it is a joining with God, and he had not thought anyone at prayer could hear anything other than what God was saying to him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/317}
