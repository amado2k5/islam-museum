## Summary
Fartuun Abdisalaan Adan is a Somali social activist, executive director of the Elman Peace and Human Rights Centre in Mogadishu and co-founder of Sister Somalia, the country's first rape crisis and support programme for survivors of gender-based violence. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} Her honours include the International Women of Courage Award in 2013, the Aurora Prize in 2020 and, with her daughter Ilwad Elman, the Right Livelihood Award in 2022. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} No source used here names a religion or school for her, so her data record leaves her faith as unknown. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} {cite:right-livelihood-adan-elman}

## Early life and family
She was born on 2 February 1969; the Right Livelihood Award's laureate page gives her birthplace as Mogadishu, Somalia. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} {cite:right-livelihood-adan-elman} She grew up in Somalia and married Elman Ali Ahmed, a local entrepreneur and peace activist widely known as the "Somali father of peace", who popularised the slogan "Drop the gun, pick up the pen" and created programmes helping war-affected youth, including former child soldiers and orphans, towards schooling and livelihoods; the couple had four daughters. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} In 1996, during the Somali Civil War, her husband was killed near the family's home in southern Mogadishu, and soon afterwards Adan left Somalia with her daughters. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en}

## Canada and return
Adan emigrated to Canada in 1999, settling in Ottawa, and in interviews spoke of the work she and her husband had started in Mogadishu as unfinished. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} In 2007 she returned to Somalia to advocate for peace and human rights and to revive the organisation that became the Elman Peace and Human Rights Centre. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} On 20 November 2019 her daughter Almaas Elman, who had returned to Somalia as an aid worker, was shot and killed near Mogadishu airport; Adan and her surviving daughters chose to continue their work in Somalia. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en}

## Elman Peace and Sister Somalia
Adan is executive director of the Elman Peace and Human Rights Centre, established in honour of her late husband, while her daughter Ilwad works alongside her in a senior leadership role. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} The Right Livelihood Foundation describes the centre's community-based disarmament, rehabilitation and reintegration of former combatants, including child soldiers, combining counselling, basic education, vocational training and job placement, and its growth from a local Mogadishu centre into a network working across Somalia and advising partners in other African countries. {cite:right-livelihood-adan-elman} {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} Through the centre she co-founded Sister Somalia, the country's first programme for victims of sexual violence, which grew into a network of safe houses and crisis centres offering medical care, psychosocial support, emergency shelter, legal accompaniment and livelihood training. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en}

## Honours
In 2013 she received the International Women of Courage Award from the United States Department of State for her work as a champion of human rights and the elimination of sexual and gender-based violence in Somalia, and in 2014 an award from the government of Germany. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} In 2015 she and Ilwad Elman received Harvard Kennedy School's Gleitsman International Activist Award, and in 2020 the Aurora Humanitarian Initiative named them Aurora Prize Laureates. {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} In 2022 the two received the Right Livelihood Award "for promoting peace, demilitarization and human rights in Somalia in the face of terrorism and gender-based violence". {cite:wikipedia-fartuun-adan-en} {cite:right-livelihood-adan-elman}
