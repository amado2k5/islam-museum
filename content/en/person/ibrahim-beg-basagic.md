Ibrahim-beg Bašagić (died 8 November 1902) was a Bosnian official and politician from Nevesinje, of the Redžepagić family whose Sarajevo branch took its name from his father Lutfullah-beg, called Bašaga.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-beg-basagic-cs} Educated in the Islamic sciences at the Travnik madrasa, he entered Ottoman service as a junior official and rose to district governor (kaymakam), serving at Travnik, Nevesinje, Piva and Foča, and briefly sat in the Ottoman parliament at Istanbul, where he received the Order of Osmanieh.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-beg-basagic-cs} His birth year is disputed even within his one biography: its infobox and Wikidata give 25 May 1830, while its family paragraph dates him 1841–1902.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-beg-basagic-cs}{cite:wikidata-ibrahim-beg-basagic}

After the Austro-Hungarian occupation of 1878 he declared loyalty to the new regime and served as district governor at Stolac and Konjic, settling permanently in Sarajevo in 1882.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-beg-basagic-cs} From 1883 he was an accounting inspector of the provincial waqf commission, which supervised Bosnia's many Islamic foundations, and in 1893 he became its president; the empire decorated him with the Order of the Iron Crown (1890) and the Order of Franz Joseph (1898).{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-beg-basagic-cs} He also wrote Turkish poetry under the name Edhem, and by his wife Almasa, a granddaughter of Smail-aga Čengić, he was the father of Safvet-beg Bašagić, the historian of the Bosnian Renaissance.{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-hr}{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-beg-basagic-cs} He died at Sarajevo and was buried in the Bakije cemetery after a funeral prayer at the Gazi Husrev-beg Mosque.{cite:wikipedia-ibrahim-beg-basagic-cs}
