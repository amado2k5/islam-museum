## Overview
Jalal al-Din Muhammad al-Balkhi al-Rumi, known by the honorific Mawlana, the greatest Sufi mystic and poet in Persian; born at Balkh about 30 September 604/1207 and died at Konya on 17 December 672/1273. He is famous for his lyrics and for his didactic epic Mathnavi-yi Ma'navi, which influenced mystical thought and literature throughout the Muslim world; after his death his disciples formed the Mawlawiyya order. {cite:britannica-rumi}

## The road from Balkh to Konya
His father Baha al-Din Walad was a noted mystical theologian, author and teacher. The family left Balkh about 615/1218 — over a quarrel with the ruler or ahead of the Mongols — passed through Nishapur where Farid al-Din Attar blessed them in the report, made the pilgrimage to Mecca and journeyed through Syria and Iraq, until they were called to Konya in 625/1228 where Baha al-Din taught. When he died in 628/1231 Jalal al-Din succeeded him, and Burhan al-Din Muhaqqiq completed his spiritual formation until about 638/1240. {cite:britannica-rumi}

## Shams al-Din and the poetry
On 30 November 642/1244 he met Shams al-Din of Tabriz at Konya, and his state was transformed. After Shams's final disappearance in 645/1247 — now established as a murder — love, longing and loss became poetry in the ghazals of the Divan-e Shams and the quatrains; he then found in the goldsmith Salah al-Din Zarkub and his disciple Husam al-Din Chalabi new manifestations of Shams, and under Husam al-Din's inspiration composed nearly 26,000 couplets of the Mathnavi on the model of Attar and Sana'i, dictating them even in the bath or on the road while Husam al-Din wrote them down. {cite:britannica-rumi}
