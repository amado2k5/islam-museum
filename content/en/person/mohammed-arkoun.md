## Summary

Mohammed Arkoun (1928–2010) was a philosopher and historian of Islamic thought, Algerian-French, professor at the Sorbonne, author of a project applying modern critical method to classical Islamic studies.{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-arkoun}

## Life

Born in Taourirt Mimoun in Algeria's Kabylie region, he studied Arabic literature in Algiers and at the Sorbonne, becoming professor of the history of Islamic thought at the Sorbonne Nouvelle (Paris 3), and worked with international scholarly institutions.{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-arkoun}

## Project

He called for a 'critique of Islamic reason': subjecting Islamic studies to the methods of the modern human sciences, deconstructing the heritage's 'social imaginary', and exploring what he called the 'unthought' in official orthodoxy. He wrote in French; his works were widely translated.{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-arkoun}

## Controversy and standing

The Western academy and some renewalists celebrated him, while Muslim scholars objected that his method imported a secularisation of religious studies; his project figures in every contemporary debate on Islam and modernity. The museum records both descriptions.{cite:wikipedia-mohammed-arkoun}
