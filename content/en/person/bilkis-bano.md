## Summary
Bilkis Bano (born around 1938), popularly known as Bilkis Dadi — "Grandmother Bilkis" — is an Indian activist from Hapur in Uttar Pradesh who became one of the best-known faces of the Shaheen Bagh sit-in of 2019–2020, a roughly hundred-day protest in Delhi led largely by Muslim women against India's Citizenship Amendment Act and the proposed National Register of Citizens. In 2020 Time magazine included her in its list of the hundred most influential people in the world. {cite:wikipedia-bilkis-bano-en} {cite:themuslim500-bilkis-bano}

## Background
Bilkis was born in a village in the Hapur district of Uttar Pradesh into a farming family, and received little formal schooling. She married young, raised eleven children and lived the unrecorded life of a rural homemaker — until, in her early eighties, she began travelling daily from her village to a highway blockade in south-east Delhi. {cite:wikipedia-bilkis-bano-en}

## Shaheen Bagh
The Citizenship Amendment Act, passed in December 2019, offered a fast track to Indian citizenship for non-Muslim refugees from Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan; its critics — including the protesters of Shaheen Bagh — argued that combined with a national register of citizens it placed Muslim Indians' citizenship at risk, while the government maintained the Act granted refuge and took away no one's status. From December 2019 until March 2020, when it dispersed under COVID-19 restrictions, a continuous sit-in on a blocked stretch of road in the Shaheen Bagh neighbourhood of Delhi drew thousands, and its emblem was the participation of women — homemakers, students and grandmothers — sitting through Delhi's coldest winter in decades. {cite:wikipedia-bilkis-bano-en}

## The grandmother at the sit-in
Bilkis Dadi became the sit-in's most recognisable figure: an elderly woman in a shawl photographed daily at the protest site, joined at times by two other elderly women from her family, together remembered as the "Dadis of Shaheen Bagh". She attended despite her age and the winter cold, and interviews with her — plain-spoken statements that she was there for the future of her children and grandchildren — circulated widely in Indian and international media. When police cleared the site in March 2020 under epidemic rules, she returned to her village in Hapur. {cite:wikipedia-bilkis-bano-en}

## Recognition
In September 2020 Time magazine named her in the Time 100, its annual list of influential figures, with a tribute essay describing her as the face of the Shaheen Bagh resistance. The BBC listed her among its 100 Women of 2020. In India she remains a contested symbol: to supporters of the protest movement she is a model of peaceful civic courage; to critics of the protests she represents a movement they regard as misdirected. The museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. No source read names a religious school for her, and this record records none. {cite:wikipedia-bilkis-bano-en} {cite:themuslim500-bilkis-bano}

Her inclusion in the Time 100 drew divided reactions inside India: supporters of the protest movement welcomed it as international recognition of the women of Shaheen Bagh, while opponents of the protests criticised it as politicising the list — a dispute continuous with the contested-symbol status the 'Dadi' had acquired in contemporary Indian politics. {cite:wikipedia-bilkis-bano-en}
