## Summary
Il-Arslan ibn Atsiz, styled Abu al-Fath and Abu al-Muzaffar on his coins, was an Anushtegin Khwarazmshah who reigned 551–567 AH (1157–1172) according to the table Shohruxmirzo Ismoilov takes from Bosworth. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 99} In 547/1152 Atsiz attacked and took Jand and made his son Il-Arslan its governor. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 99}

## His coins
- At Jand: his first coin, struck there, names after the profession of God's oneness the overlord "Abu al-Harith Sanjar", and on the reverse "Malik Arslan son of Ala al-Dawla wa al-Din Khwarazmshah Atsiz"; it weighs 5.495 g. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 99}
- Independent issues: types 1a and 1b carry the names of the caliphs al-Muqtafi and then al-Mustanjid, and on the reverse "the imam, the exalted king, Taj al-Dunya wa al-Din Il-Arslan ibn Atsiz", with a sword pointing upward. The author reads the phrase "Atsiz mu'in" in two ways, "appointed by Atsiz" or "son of the late Atsiz", and thinks it was meant to show him as his father's lawful heir. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 100}
- A gold dinar of 563 AH with the title "the exalted king, Taj al-Dunya wa al-Din". {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 100}
- With the title sultan: after he subdued the rulers of Nasa, Dihistan and Nishapur the title "the greatest sultan" appears on his coins, among them a dinar struck at Nishapur after 1170 in the name of the caliph al-Mustadi. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 100} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 101}

The author counts 8 types for him, and says the title "Khwarazmshah" appears on no ruler's coins after him. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 117} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 118}
