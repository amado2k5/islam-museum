## Overview
Qadi Taqi al-Din Abu Bakr Ibn Qadi Shuhba al-Asadi al-Dimashqi al-Shafi'i (d. 851/1448), the jurist, historian and scholar of Damascus in his day; he was called Ibn Qadi Shuhba because his great-grandfather had held the judgeship of Shahba, a town of Hawran, for forty years. {cite:al-aalam p. 163} {cite:nujum-zahira 15/523}

## His judgeship
He held the chief judgeship of Damascus repeatedly, together with the preachership of the Umayyad Mosque and its muftiship: installed in Jumada I 842 after al-Burhan ibn al-Ba'uni refused the office, dismissed in Dhu al-Qa'da of the same year to the chancery secretary Baha' al-Din Ibn Hajji, restored in Ramadan 843 in place of Shams al-Din al-Wana'i, stripped of the mosque's preachership on 10 Dhu al-Qa'da 843, and finally replaced by Siraj al-Din Umar al-Himsi on 12 Muharram 844 — after which he devoted himself to fatwa, teaching and writing until his death. {cite:suluk-maqrizi p. 1100-1202} {cite:nujum-zahira 15/523}

## His sermon in the Jakami revolt
When Amir Inal al-Hakimi, governor of Damascus, rebelled against Sultan al-Zahir Jaqmaq and restored the name of the deposed sultan al-Aziz Yusuf ibn Barsbay, Ibn Qadi Shuhba, as chief judge, invoked al-Aziz from the minbar of the Umayyad Mosque on a Friday, while the citadel's khutba remained for Jaqmaq; Jaqmaq held that sermon against him, and it was a cause of his later dismissal. {cite:suluk-maqrizi p. 1112} {cite:nujum-zahira 15/523}

## His works
His works include Tabaqat al-Shafi'iyya, Sharh al-Minhaj, Lubab al-Tahdhib, the Dhayl on Ibn Kathir's history, and the Muntaga selections from the History of Alexandria by al-Nuwayri, from the Ansab of Ibn al-Sam'ani, from Nukhbat al-Dahr and from the History of Ibn Asakir. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 269} He also wrote a great history beginning in 200 AH and ending in 792, and a continuation (Dhayl) to the histories of al-Dhahabi and al-Birzali covering 741 to 821 in eight volumes, which he abridged in two, and chronicles of the events of his own time to the day of his death; and Tabaqat al-Nahwiyyin. {cite:al-aalam p. 163} Among them is al-I'lam bi-Tarikh Ahl al-Islam. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 479}

## Death
He died suddenly in Damascus while sitting writing and talking with his son, on Thursday the eleventh of Dhu al-Qa'da 851, after the leadership of his school's jurisprudence had passed to him and most of Damascus's students had profited from him. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 269} {cite:nujum-zahira 15/523} {cite:al-aalam p. 163}
