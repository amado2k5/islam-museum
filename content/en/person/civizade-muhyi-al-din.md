## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ilyas al-Hanafi al-Rumi, Muhyi al-Din, known as Civizade (printed Juwi-zade), was an Ottoman Hanafi judge whom al-Zirikli describes as Turkish in origin and upbringing and Arabic in his writings. He died in 954 AH, 1547 CE, and al-Zirikli gives no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 40}.

## Career
He served as judge in Egypt, then as judge of the Anatolian armies, and was then appointed mufti at Constantinople {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 40}. He objected to some statements of Shaykh Muhyi al-Din ibn al-Arabi, and the sultan removed him from the iftā' office, after which he turned to teaching {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 40}. He was later restored to the judgeship of the Rumelian armies and died in that post {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 40}.

## Learning and the judgement of scholars
Al-Zirikli quotes Ibn al-Imad as saying that he was abundant in knowledge of jurisprudence, Qur'anic exegesis and legal theory, shared in all the other sciences, and was a cutting sword among the swords of truth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 40}.

## Works
He left glosses (ta'liqat) that did not become well known, a collection of fatwas known as Fatawa Civizade (manuscript), and Mizan al-mudda'iyyin fi iqamat al-bayyinatayn (manuscript), a treatise in jurisprudence on the verification of a claim of ownership {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 6 p. 40}.
