## Overview
The hafiz Abu Muhammad 'Abd al-Qadir al-Ruhawi the Hanbali, muhaddith of the Jazira; a slave of a Mosul family who freed him and endeared to him the science of hadith so he heard abundantly — born in Jumada al-Akhira 536 at al-Ruha, then taken captive when Zangi conquered it in 539, bought and freed by the Banu Fahm of Harran; he heard at Isfahan, Hamadan, Herat, Marw, Nishapur, Sijistan, Baghdad, Damascus and Egypt. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}

## His book and death
He composed and compiled — among them "the Forty of differing isnads and lands," a thing no one had preceded him in, nor can a muhaddith hope for after him given the ruin of the lands; Abu Shama called him righteous, awe-inspiring, ascetic, coarse-living and devout, and Ibn Khalil "a hafiz, exact, a prolific writer — with him hadith was sealed"; he died on Saturday 2 Jumada al-Ula 612 at Harran. {cite:shadharat-dhahab}
