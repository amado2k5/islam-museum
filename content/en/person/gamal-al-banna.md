## Summary

Gamal al-Banna (1920–2013) was an Egyptian reformist Islamic thinker, brother of Hassan al-Banna, who stood at the opposite pole from his brother's project: he called for a new jurisprudence built on Quran and reason with a critique of much of the Sunna, and for freedom and social justice.{cite:wikipedia-gamal-al-banna}

## Life

Born in al-Mahmudiyya, Beheira, he worked in writing and technical translation, joined the labour and trade-union movement in the 1940s and was imprisoned with it, then devoted decades to Islamic writing.{cite:wikipedia-gamal-al-banna}

## Ideas and books

His core thesis is that the Quran alone is Islam's binding document, and that much inherited jurisprudence is historical and open to ijtihad; his books include Nahwa Fiqh Jadid (Towards a New Fiqh), The Quran: Fiqh and Reading, and Freedom of Thought and Belief in Islam.{cite:wikipedia-gamal-al-banna}

## Controversy and standing

Renewalists welcomed him while religious currents rejected him for breaking with consensus on the Sunna; in labour circles he is remembered as a pioneer of union culture. He died in 2013 — the museum records both descriptions.{cite:wikipedia-gamal-al-banna}
