## Summary
[[person/umm-zufar|Umm Zufar]], the tall black Abyssinian; her mention is established in Sahih al-Bukhari in Ibn Jurayj's hadith: Ata' told me he saw Umm Zufar, a tall black woman, on the [[place/mecca|Ka'ba]]'s stair {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1999}.

## The hadith
Ibn Abbas said to Ata': "shall I show you a woman of the people of Paradise?" I said: yes. He said: "this black woman came to the Prophet and said: I suffer seizures, and I become uncovered — pray God for me. He said: if you wish, be patient and Paradise is yours; if you wish, I will pray God to heal you. She said: I will be patient — but I become uncovered, so pray God I am not uncovered" — and he prayed for her {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1999}.

## Another account
Abd al-Razzaq narrates from Tawus that the mad were brought to the Prophet and he would strike one's chest and he was healed; a madwoman called Umm Zufar was brought, he struck her chest and she was not healed and her demon did not come out — he said: "it afflicts her in this world, and hers in the next is good" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1999}. From al-Hasan: the woman used to suffer fits in the mosque; her brothers came to the Prophet complaining of it and he said: "if you wish I will pray God and she is healed; if you wish she stays as she is with no reckoning upon her in the hereafter" — her brothers gave her the choice and she said: "leave me as I am" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1999}. Ibn Hajar describes this account as "the trustworthy narrators' account from Ata'" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1999}.

## A misprint in some accounts
Umar ibn Qays related it from Ata' and misprinted her as "Umm Quray'"; Ibn Hajar says the chain to Umar ibn Qays is weak, and that making the hadith Ata' relating from her is anomalous — Ata' related it from Ibn Abbas {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2000}. He mentions her name was Sa'ira — or Shaqiqa {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 2000}.

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the sixth episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] introduces Umm Zufar as one of the women Companions who are among the people of Paradise, proverbial for patience, modesty and chastity {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:01:30}. He gives her name as Su'ayra, or by another account Sa'ida, al-Asadiyya, the hairdresser of [[person/khadija|Khadija]], an Abyssinian Black woman, and thinks it likely she was a slave {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:02:00}; Su'ayra is also the name Ibn Hajar gives in al-Isaba {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.2000}.

He relates from the two Sahihs, calling it agreed upon, through [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata' ibn Abi Rabah]], that [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] took him by the hand and showed him a Black woman of the people of Paradise; she had come to the Prophet complaining that she suffered seizures in which she became uncovered, and asked him to pray for her; he gave her the choice between patience, with Paradise as her reward, and his praying for her healing; she chose patience, and asked him to pray that she would not become uncovered, and he prayed for her {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:02:30}. He says that when a seizure came on her she was seen clinging to the coverings of the Ka'ba {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:23:30}, and that she chose patience because she knew the reward of the patient, citing two verses on the glad tidings given to the patient and on their reward being paid in full without measure {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:23:00}. He reflects on the happiness of one promised Paradise while still alive, who knows that nothing stands between him and it but the departure of his soul {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:04:30}.

He draws lessons from her story. He divides the people of Paradise into two kinds, those testified to by their qualities and those testified to by name because a text has come about them, such as the Ten promised Paradise {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:05:00}. He divides the people of the Fire in the same way: those known by their qualities, such as disbelievers, polytheists, hypocrites, wrongdoers, those who wrong their parents and those who devour the wealth of orphans, and those a text names individually, among whom he counts [[person/firawn|Pharaoh]], [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]], [[person/abu-lahab|Abu Lahab]] and his wife [[person/umm-jamil|Umm Jamil]], Umayya ibn Khalaf, Shayba and [[person/utba-ibn-rabia|Utba]], the sons of Rabi'a, al-Walid ibn Utba, [[person/uqba-ibn-abi-muayt|Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt]], [[person/haman|Haman]], [[person/qarun|Qarun]] and Nimrod {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:06:00}. He holds that no one is declared bound for Paradise or the Fire without a sound and explicit text, so that for whoever dies a Muslim, however righteous, one hopes without being certain {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:07:00}, and that whoever dies a Muslim with unrepented sins, even sins like mountains, is subject to God's will, to be punished by His justice or pardoned by His grace as He wills, citing the verse that God does not forgive that partners be associated with Him but forgives what is less than that to whom He wills {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:09:30}; he uses himself as an example: people see his beard and his learning but cannot know what he does when he is alone {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:07:30}. He relates from al-Tirmidhi, graded hasan sahih as he says, that whoever is wounded in God's cause, and God knows best who is truly wounded in His cause, will come on the Day of Resurrection with his wound flowing, its colour the colour of blood and its scent the scent of musk, and concludes that of one who is killed one says "we reckon him a martyr", without certainty {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:08:30}.

He suggests that the woman was a slave, or an elderly woman who used the concession allowed to older women regarding the outer covering, or that Ibn Abbas knew her as a boy when she was already old, and says that all of these are possible {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:11:00}. He states that Ibn Abbas's describing her as Black was not backbiting, defining backbiting as mentioning a Muslim in his absence with something true that he dislikes, and slander if it is untrue {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:11:30}. He then says the religious law permits mentioning an absent person in six cases. The first is identification, as Ibn Abbas did, and he cites the verse of Surat Abasa about the blind man and the bynames of scholars such as al-A'raj, al-A'mash, al-Ahwal and al-Asamm, as with Hatim al-Asamm {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:13:00}. The second is seeking a ruling, as when [[person/hind-bint-utba|Hind]], the wife of [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]], complained of her husband's stinginess and the Prophet allowed her to take from his wealth what was reasonable {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:14:30}. The third is testimony and counsel, as in the account of [[person/fatima-bint-qays|Fatima bint Qays]], whom the Prophet advised to marry Usama ibn Zayd after describing the circumstances of the men who had proposed to her {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:15:30}. The fourth is complaint against a wrongdoer: a man complained of a neighbour who harmed him, and the Prophet told him to put his belongings out in the road, so that passers-by began to pray against the neighbour until he came repenting {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:17:00}. The fifth is the person who sins openly {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:18:00}. The sixth is seeking the ruler's help to change a wrong: when Umar heard that one of his governors was drinking wine, he wrote him verses from the opening of one of the "Ha Mim" suras on forgiving sin, accepting repentance and the severity of punishment, and the governor understood and repented {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:18:30}.

He divides complaint into two kinds: complaint to the Creator, which for him is part of perfect patience, citing the words of [[person/yaqub|Jacob]] in laying his grief before God; and complaint to people, of three sorts: to explain one's condition, to ask for prayer while content with God's decree, which is what this woman did, and in resentment of fate, which is the blameworthy kind {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:20:00}. He divides epilepsy into two kinds: seizures from a disorder of the nerves, treated by doctors, and seizures from the power of devils, which have their own prevention and remedy {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:21:30}; among the remedies for the second he lists patience, prayer, reciting Surat al-Baqara every three days, lawful ruqya, repentance, since in his words affliction mostly comes down because of sins, diligence in worship such as night prayer and fasting on alternate days, and the remembrance of God {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:22:00}; and he relates from Sahih al-Bukhari that whoever eats seven 'ajwa dates in the morning is harmed that day by neither poison nor magic {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:22:30}. He closes the episode with a hadith that among the words of the earliest prophecy that people have kept is that whoever has lost all shame will do whatever he likes {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:25:00}.
