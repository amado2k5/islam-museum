## Overview

Naim Frashëri (25 May 1846 – 20 October 1900) was an Albanian historian, journalist, poet and translator, regarded as the national poet of Albania, a pioneer of modern Albanian literature and one of the most influential cultural figures of the nineteenth century.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-ur} He was the fourth of the eight children of Halit Bey Frashëri (1797–1859) and Emine (1814–1861), born at Frashër into a wealthy Albanian family affiliated with the Bektashi tariqa.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en} With his elder brother Abdyl and his younger brother Sami he stood among the leading figures of the Albanian National Awakening.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-ur}

## Career

He began his education at the Bektashi tekke of Frashër, learning Arabic, Ottoman Turkish and Persian there.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en} After his parents' deaths the family moved to Ioannina in 1865, where he attended the Zosimaia school — the Albanian article dates his attendance from 1869 — and graduated in 1870, having also taken private lessons in Persian, Turkish and Arabic from two local Bektashis.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq} He spent some eight months at a press office in Istanbul in 1870–71 before his tuberculosis forced him back to Ioannina, then worked as a clerk at Berat and as customs director at Saranda in 1874–77, leaving to seek a cure for his rheumatism at Baden.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq} In 1879 he co-founded, with Sami and some twenty-five others, the Society for the Publication of Albanian Writings in Istanbul.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en} From 1882 he settled in Istanbul, rising to chairman of the Inspection and Control (censorship) Commission at the Ministry of Education — a post he used to ease the ban on Albanian books and education, signing his own Albanian publications only with the initials N.H., N.H.F. and N.F.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq}{cite:source/elsie-naim-frasheri} He edited the magazine Drita (1883–84) behind the scenes, and with Sami wrote the textbooks for the Albanian school at Korçë, whose permit the brothers obtained in 1887.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq} He died in the small hours of 20 October 1900 at his home in Kızıl Toprak, Kadıköy, and was buried the next day at the Merdivenköy Bektashi tekke beside his brother Abdyl's grave.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq} The Albanian article records that his remains were brought to Albania on 2 June 1937 and placed in the Bektashi tekke in Tirana, while the English article dates the Turkish permission for the transfer only to the 1950s — the two accounts are recorded side by side rather than reconciled.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}

## Works

He wrote twenty-two works — fifteen in Albanian, four in Turkish, two in Greek and one in Persian — beginning with a Persian grammar, Kavâid-i farisiyye dar zebân-ı farisi (1871), signed Mehmet Naim.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq} His pastoral Bagëti e Bujqësi (1886) was smuggled into Albania in caravans, and a line from his poem O malet e Shqipërisë — "You Albania, give me honour, give me the name Albanian" — became the country's national motto.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en} His Bektashi Notebook, Fletore e Bektashinjet, is dated 1895 in the English article's works list and 1896 by Robert Elsie; the Albanian article calls it and Qerbelaja the first works of Albanian Bektashism.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}{cite:source/elsie-naim-frasheri}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq} Qerbelaja (1898; the Arabic and Urdu articles' lists give 1894), a Bektashi epic in twenty-five cantos on the Karbala theme, and the verse Istori e Skënderbeut (1898), some 11,500 verses on Albania's national hero, are his longest poems.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq}{cite:source/elsie-naim-frasheri}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-ar} His textbooks carried the first selective translations of Qur'an passages into Albanian.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq}

## Positions and criticism

This section reports what the sources attribute to Frashëri and how they assess him; it is not the museum's own judgement.

The English article states of him directly: "Himself a Bektashi, he desired purity of the Albanian language and had attempted in his lifetime to Albanianise hierarchical terms of the order in his work Fletore e Bektashinjët", and calls him "the most representative writer of Sufi poetry in Albanian".{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en} Robert Elsie writes of "his affinity for his pantheistic Bektashi religion" and records that he hoped the liberal Bektashi beliefs "to which he had been attached since his childhood in Frashër" would one day become the religion of all Albania as a force for national unity.{cite:source/elsie-naim-frasheri} On this record — three independent attestations of the affiliation itself — the museum records his faith as sufi (Bektashi), as his record's faith note sets out.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-sq}{cite:source/elsie-naim-frasheri} His influence on the later Albanian poets Asdreni, Gjergj Fishta and Lasgush Poradeci is noted by the English article, and his portrait appears on the 200 lekë banknote while an Albanian order of merit bears his name.{cite:source/wikipedia-naim-frasheri-en}
