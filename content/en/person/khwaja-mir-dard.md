## Summary

Khwaja Mir Dard (1720 or 1721 – 1785) was a poet and Sufi of Delhi, one of the three pillars of the classical Delhi school of Urdu poetry, and a son of the Persian poet Muhammad Nasir Andalib {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-en} {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-ur}.

## The poet of the Sufi path

Dard wrote in Urdu and Persian, and his verse is so identified with tasawwuf that Urdu tradition calls him the imam of Sufi poetry {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-ur}. His Ilm-ul-Kitab sets out the Sufi path in prose, and the English article quotes from it his farewell to the world — "My friends, we have seen enough of this play. We are going home, you can stay." {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-en}. Where Mir Taqi Mir subordinated thought to feeling, Dard's ghazal is reflective and serene, and he held aloof from courts and patrons, composing only when the verse came of itself. He died at Delhi in January 1785 {cite:wikidata-khwaja-mir-dard}.

## Faith

His faith is recorded as Sufi, documented. The Arabic article calls him in its lead a poet, a Sufi and an Indian Muslim author, and names his work on tasawwuf {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-ar}; the Urdu article calls him the imam of Sufi poetry in Urdu {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-ur}; and his own Ilm-ul-Kitab is a treatise on the Sufi path {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-en}. No source read names a madhhab for him, and none is recorded; his father's Sufi standing is family background and was not counted, and the English article's Sufi poets category is a filing.

## Notes for researchers

Both his dates are debated. His birth is given as 1720 by the English and Urdu articles but 1721 by Wikidata and by the English and Arabic articles' own birth categories {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-en} {cite:wikidata-khwaja-mir-dard}; his death is 7 January 1785 in the Urdu article and 6 January 1785 in Wikidata, the Arabic article filing the year under 1199 AH {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-ur} {cite:wikipedia-khwaja-mir-dard-ar}. Wikidata's birthplace, Malihabad, is not followed against the articles' Delhi. His father Muhammad Nasir Andalib has no museum record yet, so no child_of edge is recorded.
