## Overview
Inan al-Natifiyya was a poet. Al-Zirikli calls her unrestrained in her way of life, and one of the cleverest of women and the most gifted at verse. He gives her death as 226 AH, 841 CE, and no year of birth {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 90}.

## Her origins
She was the slave girl of a man called al-Natifi, of the people of Baghdad. She was one of the muwalladat of al-Yamama, or, it is said, of Medina, and became famous in Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 90}.

## Her reports
Al-Abbas ibn al-Ahnaf was in love with her, and there are reports of her with him, with Abu Nuwas and with others {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 90}. Al-Zirikli quotes Abu Ali al-Qali as saying that Inan the poet of al-Yamama was accomplished in letters and quick of wit, that the foremost poets traded verses with her and she held her own against them, and that her reports are on record {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 90}.

## Her death
Al-Zirikli says she died in Khurasan, and then cites al-Mustatraf min Akhbar al-Nisa', pages 38 to 47, for a second report: that when she was freed she went to Egypt and died there {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 90}.
