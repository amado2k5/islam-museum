## Summary
Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Sahl ibn Ata was a dedicated worker and a pure-hearted man, who kept the Qur'an in his breast; he stood among the ranks of the captives and the stations of the afflicted, and wished for what they had been given. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him a notice in the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], printed number 575, sequence 576, on pages 302 to 305. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/302} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/305}

## On manners and knowledge
Abu al-Husayn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Hubaysh, a companion of al-Junayd, relates that he kept company with Abu al-Abbas ibn Ata for years, learning his manners. Ibn Ata finished the Qur'an every day and three times a day in Ramadan, and for about ten years remained in one reading, drawing out its meanings. His sayings include: whoever binds himself to the courtesies of the Sunna, God fills his heart with the light of knowledge; remembering the reward while remembering God is heedlessness of God. Asked what worship is, he said: leaving one's choice and keeping to need. He said: beware of looking at a creature when you can look at the Truth. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/302} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/304}

## Manners of the righteous, the friends of God and the prophets
Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Muqsim relates from Ibn Ata: whoever is trained in the manners of the righteous is fit for the carpet of honour; whoever is trained in the manners of the friends of God is fit for the carpet of nearness; whoever is trained in the manners of the prophets is fit for the carpet of intimacy and ease. He also said: compassion for the believer never ceased until it brought him to his best state, and heedlessness of the wicked never ceased until it brought him to his worst state. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/305}

## Trials and the heart's longing
Ibn Ata said: when the heart longs for Paradise, Paradise's gifts hasten to it, and these gifts are the things that are disliked, for disliked things are Paradise's gifts to the bodies of the truthful. Whoever flees with himself into the fortress of what is disliked, the desires of greed leave his heart. A sign of truthfulness is the heart's contentment with what is disliked. He said: whoever witnesses the Truth through the Truth is cut off from seeing creation. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/303} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/304}

## Vanity and self-regard
Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Hubaysh heard Ibn Ata say: bring your heart near the company of those who remember God, so that it may wake from its heedlessness, and set yourself in service to the righteous. Ibn Ata said that the nearest thing to God's hatred is seeing oneself and one's acts, and worse than that is demanding a return for one's acts. He said the signs of the friends of God are keeping their secret before God, guarding their limbs, bearing harm from creatures, and dealing gently with people according to the differences of their minds. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/305}

## Verses and wisdom
Ibn Hubaysh recited verses of Ahmad ibn Sahl ibn Ata, and Ibn Ata recited verses on glory and patience, among them that glory is not reached except through patience, and verses on the companionship of remembrance. Some words of the verses are unclear in the scan and are not given. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/304}

## Traditions and remarks
Ibn Ata relates traditions from several transmitters on connected chains, among them a report from Wathila ibn al-Asqa' on the intercession of a man of the community, and a report from Ibn Abbas that eating salt in company is better than eating fine food in division. The chains are given in the text; the wording is not reproduced here. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/305}

## A verse on night-vigil and the bed
Ibn Ata was asked about the verse on those whose sides forsake their beds, and said: the ones who forsake their beds are of degrees: one who lies on his bed, one who lies in his worldly life, and one who lies in himself; he gave each a reward. The verse itself is referred to by sense only. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/304}
