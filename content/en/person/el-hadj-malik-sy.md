## Overview

El Hadj Malik Sy (1855 – 27 June 1922), whose full name was Sheikh al-Saidi al-Hajj Malik ibn Ousmane ibn Demba ibn Chamseddine Sy, was a Senegalese scholar and sheikh of the Tijaniyya Sufi order.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr} The French article describes him as a scholar of the Maliki school of jurisprudence and the Ash'ari school of theology, and a scholar of the Tariqa Tijaniyya "which he contributed greatly to spreading in Senegal and Black Africa".{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr} From his zawiya at Tivaouane, founded in 1902, the Tijani order spread primarily through him across Senegal's Wolof country, and on his death his son succeeded him as Caliph-General of the Tijanis.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} His Tivaouane zawiya became a centre of Islamic education and culture, and its annual Gamou, the Mawlid gathering, still draws many followers each year.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en}

## Life

He was born at Gaya (Gaé), near Dagana in northern Senegal, to a Fulani family; the French article gives the year as about 1855 and cautions that the date remains uncertain, since the oral tradition's 25 February 1855 marks his "entry into the village", from which a birth date was inferred.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr}{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} Wikidata gives 18 February 1855, a different inferred day, and the English article's lead gives 1853; the record follows 1855 and keeps year precision.{cite:source/wikidata-el-hadj-malik-sy}{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} He travelled to Mauritania as a religious student, then to Saint-Louis in 1884, and taught at Louga and Pire Goureye.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} He performed the Hajj, from which his title El Hadj derives.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} At the suggestion of his father-in-law, the scholar Mor Massamba Diery Dieng, and at the invitation of the local notable Djibril Guèye, he established his zawiya at Tivaouane and settled there permanently in 1902; the French article notes that the zawiya is now on Senegal's list of classified historic sites and monuments.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr} Under his leadership Tivaouane became a centre for Islamic education and culture, and in the Wolof country of Kajoor and Jolof especially, the Tijani order was spread primarily by him.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en}

## Death and succession

He died on 27 June 1922 at Tivaouane.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en}{cite:source/wikidata-el-hadj-malik-sy} He was succeeded by his son Seydi Ababacar Sy as khalife général des Tidjanes, Caliph-General of the Tijanis, from 1922 to 1957, and the family line has continued to hold the title since, through El-Hadji Abdou Aziz Sy (1957–1997) to the present khalife, Serigne Mabye Sy Mansour.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} The Gamou of Tivaouane gathers many followers each year, and an Avenue Malik Sy in Dakar bears his name.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en}

## Writings

Malick Sy wrote extensively in Arabic on Islam, the Prophet and Ahmad al-Tijani.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} The English article lists, among others: Ifham al-Munkir al-Jani, "Silencing the Deniers", a defence of the Tijaniyya and of points of law; Faakihatul Tullab, a primer on the Tijaniyya, its teachings and practices; Kifayat ar-Raghibin, "Who are the good believers", a series of jurisprudential and spiritual texts; Khilasu ez-Zahab, a comprehensive rhyming biography of Muhammad, his companions and relatives, regularly recited at religious celebrations in Senegal, especially the Mawlid, with his own commentary upon it; Al-Hidayat al-Wildan, a treatise on Islamic theology based on the Ash'ari school; Zajr al-Qulub, a poem of pious exhortation and asceticism; and collections of poetry and sermons for Friday and the feasts.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} Several of these have been translated into French by Rawane Mbaye, whose Sorbonne thesis on Sy's thought and action, "between the Shari'a and the Tariqa", is listed in the French article's bibliography.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en}{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr}

## Positions and criticism

This section reports what the sources attribute to Malik Sy and how they assess him; it is not the museum's own judgement.

**His confession.** The French article names him a scholar of the Maliki school of jurisprudence and the Ash'ari school of theology as well as of the Tijaniyya, and the English article's lead likewise calls his brotherhood "Tijaniyya Sufi Malikite and Ash'arite".{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr}{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} The English article, however, states in its bibliography that it derives in part from a translation of the French article, so the two are one source family for the school; the museum records his faith as sufi — on the two articles' attestation of his Tijaniyya leadership and the caliphate his son inherited — and states the Maliki-Ash'ari description here without counting it twice.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr}{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-en} Scholarship treats him, in David Robinson's chapter title, as a "Teacher in the New Colonial Order", a formulation the French article's bibliography records.{cite:source/wikipedia-el-hadj-malik-sy-fr}
