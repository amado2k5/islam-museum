## Summary

Chérif Ousmane Madani Haïdara is a Malian Sufi preacher: the French Wikipedia article's lead calls him in prose "un prédicateur Soufi malien" (a Malian Sufi preacher), and an academic policy brief of 2024 names him "the Sufi Chérif Ousmane Madani Haïdara".{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr}{cite:raineri-sahel-brief-2024} He founded the Ançar Dine movement — in 1991 according to the English article and the Faso Actu profile, in 1993 according to the French article — a Sufi-oriented Malian movement that is not the armed group of a similar name in the north of Mali, a distinction the English article itself states expressly.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-en}{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr}{cite:fassoactu-haidara-2026} He has been president of the High Islamic Council of Mali since April 2019.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-en} His reach is institutional, not platform-based: no follower count exists for him on any platform, while the movement's own site, as quoted by the scholar Gilles Holder, claimed more than one hundred thousand members in twenty-four countries.{cite:holder-ansar-dine-2012}

## Early life and education

He was born at Tamani in the Ségou region of Mali; the French article's biography and the Faso Actu profile give his birth as 12 May 1955, while the French article's own infobox gives 11 May 1955 — both readings are preserved here and neither is preferred.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr}{cite:fassoactu-haidara-2026} He studied religious sciences at Saad Touré's establishment and was then entrusted to the marabout Bama Worofana.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr} About 1979–1980 he preached in Côte d'Ivoire before returning to Mali.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr}

## Beginnings and career

In 1986 the government of Moussa Traoré suspended him from preaching, and he was questioned by the police several times; the suspension was officially lifted only in 1998, and since then he has organised annual activities for the Mawlid.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr} In the early 1990s he founded Ançar Dine — 1991 in the English article and Faso Actu, 1993 in the French article — a popular reform movement with a Sufi base, strong in southern Mali, Ivory Coast, Burkina Faso and the Malian diaspora.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-en}{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr}{cite:holder-ansar-dine-2012} Ansar Dine International was formed in January 2004 with national offices in Mali, Ivory Coast and Burkina Faso, and its Mawlid celebrations draw tens of thousands from across Mali and West Africa.{cite:holder-ansar-dine-2012} In 2002 he became vice-president of the High Islamic Council at its creation, and on 21 April 2019 its president, succeeding Imam Mahmoud Dicko.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr}{cite:fassoactu-haidara-2026}

## Positions and criticism

The following positions are related as their sources attribute them; they are not the museum's verdict on their subject: his teaching is radically opposed to violence and to terrorist groups, and he advocates a tolerant Muslim devoted to building his country through work, honesty, uprightness and integrity.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr} In late 2011 the Groupement des leaders spirituels musulmans du Mali (GLSM) was formed, claiming to represent the majority Malikite Islam, with Haïdara as its president; according to Voice of America (2016) the collective was formed to counter foreign-sponsored radical preachers, and Haïdara leads prayers at the main mosque in Ségou and was visiting a new mosque in a different city almost every Friday.{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-fr}{cite:voa-sufi-leaders-2016} The Sant'Anna brief (2024) reports his support for preserving laïcité so that religion does not become a political issue.{cite:raineri-sahel-brief-2024} On 21 June 2025 he organised a day of prayers for the country's stability at the Grand Mosque of Bamako.{cite:fassoactu-haidara-2026}

## Impact and audience

Haïdara's impact is not measured in platform figures — no follower count exists for him on any platform — but in two institutions: the presidency of the High Islamic Council of Mali since 2019, and the Ançar Dine movement, whose own site Holder (2012) quotes as counting "more than one hundred thousand members, in twenty-four countries, thirty sections, 169 subsections and nine hundred committees"; that figure is the movement's report of itself as relayed by Holder, and is related here as such.{cite:holder-ansar-dine-2012}{cite:wikipedia-ousmane-madani-haidara-en}
