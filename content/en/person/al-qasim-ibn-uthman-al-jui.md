## Summary
Al-Qasim ibn Uthman al-Ju'i is one of the ascetics treated by [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] in the ninth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (notice 453). [[person/ahmad-ibn-abi-al-hawari|Ahmad ibn Abi al-Hawari]], who relates his sayings, calls him "al-Qasim al-Ju'i the Elder". The notice gives no town and no date. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/322-323}

## Why he was called al-Ju'i
Al-Qasim says that the friends of God are filled by love in place of food, so they have lost the pleasure of eating and drinking, having tasted a pleasure that cut them off from every other. He then explains his nickname: if he were left without food he would not mind, and he had trained his self until, left a month without eating or drinking, it would not mind. He ends by ascribing this to God's favour to him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/323}

## His principles
He used to say that the root of love is knowledge of God, the root of obedience is belief, the root of fear is watchfulness, the root of sins is long hope, and love of leadership is the root of every calamity. He said that a little action with knowledge of God is better than much action without it. He made contentment with God the head of all works, scruple the pillar of religion, hunger the marrow of worship, and control of the tongue the fortress. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/323}

## With Salm al-Khawwas
He told that he stayed with Salm al-Khawwas, who set before him a melon and half a loaf and mentioned that a brother of his had once offered him the same and said: what is lawful does not allow for excess, and whoever knows where his earnings come from knows how to spend. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/323}

## Transmission
Abu Nu'aym transmits through him hadiths that al-Qasim relates from Abd Allah ibn Nafi' from Malik, from [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Sufyan ibn Uyayna]], and from Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Firyabi. He is also the transmitter of the agreement of a group of ascetics, among them [[person/mada-ibn-isa|Mada' ibn Isa]], that giving up one mouthful is better than standing a night in prayer. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/324-325}
