# Muhammad Jamalul Alam II

## Summary
Muhammad Jamalul Alam II (1889 – September 1924) was the 26th Sultan of Brunei, reigning from 1906 until his death. A son of Hashim Jalilul Alam Aqamaddin, he came to the throne at seventeen under a regency council and was crowned in 1918. The first Bruneian sultan to speak English, he was long regarded as a model ruler through one of Brunei's poorest periods, encouraging agriculture, medicine and education and promoting Islamic learning. He was succeeded by his eldest son Ahmad Tajuddin. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ms} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ar}

## Accession and regency
Born in 1889 at the Istana Kampong Ayer to Sultan Hashim and Pengiran Anak Siti Fatimah, he succeeded on his father's death on 10 May 1906. Because he was seventeen, a regency council governed for him: the Malay article describes a State Council of two nobles formed in 1907 that ruled until 1918, and the Arabic and Urdu articles likewise say a regency council held the sultan's responsibilities until his coronation. He was crowned on 15 May 1918. Early in the reign he met resistance from conservative royal relatives opposed to the British Residency's reforms, established the same year he succeeded. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ms} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ar} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ur} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-en}

## Reign
Brunei had its own national flag from 1906; Malay schools strengthened the education system from 1911; he founded the Brunei police force in 1921; and from 1922 development works — electricity, postal and water services for Bandar Brunei — pressed ahead alongside oil exploration, and he moved from the Istana Kampong Ayer to the Istana Majlis. On 10 May 1922 he received the Prince of Wales, the future Edward VIII, with full royal ceremonial at the Lapau. The English article, citing the standard histories, describes him as admired for his intelligence and diplomatic approach, a loyal British ally who steered Brunei through economic decline and social challenge. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ms} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-en}

## Death and succession
He died at the Istana Majalis in September 1924 and was buried at the Royal Mausoleum in Bandar Seri Begawan. The date is debated: the Malay article and Wikidata give 11 September 1924, the English article 19 September 1924; the record follows the Malay article and Wikidata and keeps both readings in the note. The Arabic article's categories file his death under the year 1343 AH and under malaria deaths. He was succeeded by his eldest son Ahmad Tajuddin, who has no museum record yet, so the succession is prose only; this record carries his own child_of and successor_of edges to his father, built in the same run. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ms} {cite:wikidata-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ar}

## Faith
His faith is recorded as unknown, and the case is flagged. A school reading exists in exactly one infobox: the English article's infobox gives his religion as Sunni Islam. The Malay article's infobox gives Islam in general; the Arabic and Urdu articles name no religion; the English text names no school; and Wikidata gives Islam only, without references. His promotion of Islamic learning is state patronage, not a personal school, and headship of the sultanate is not one either; one infobox family short of the two-source bar, the Sunni reading stands in the record's note, unresolved. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ms} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii-ar} {cite:wikidata-muhammad-jamalul-alam-ii}
