## Overview
Abd Allah ibn Abi Najih, Abu Yasar al-Thaqafi al-Makki, whose father's name was Yasar and who was a client of the Companion al-Akhnas ibn Shuraiq, is called by al-Dhahabi the imam, the reliable, the exegete {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Al-Dhahabi notes that he entered the doctrine of qadar, and the critics describe him as holding i'tizal; the museum names this at the outset so each critic's remark can be placed {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. He was handsome and eloquent and never married {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Ibn Uyayna said he was the mufti of Mecca after Amr ibn Dinar {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Mujahid, Tawus, Ata and their like, and al-Dhahabi says he found nothing from him from any Companion {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Shu'ba, al-Thawri, Abd al-Warith, Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Ibn Ulayya and others narrated from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. It was said he did not hear all of the tafsir from Mujahid, and al-Dhahabi replies that he was among the people closest to Mujahid {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}.

## What the critics said
Yahya ibn Ma'in and others held him reliable, but he entered qadar {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Yahya al-Qattan said he was a Mu'tazili, and Ya'qub al-Sadusi said he was reliable but a Qadari {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Al-Bukhari said he was accused of i'tizal and qadar; Ibn al-Madini said he held i'tizal; Ahmad said they corrupted him late in life and that he sat with Amr ibn Ubayd {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Ali ibn al-Madini reports Yahya ibn Sa'id saying he was among the heads of the callers to it, while Ali himself held him reliable in tafsir, which he knew {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Al-Qattan also relates that Ibn Safwan reported Ibn Abi Najih saying he called him to the view of al-Hasan, meaning qadar {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Al-Bukhari relays from Ibrahim ibn Kaysan that he stayed thirty years without saying a word that would hurt his companion {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. Al-Dhahabi adds that the authors of the sound collections used him as proof, that perhaps he turned back from the innovation, and that a number of reliable men held qadar and erred {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}. The museum gives each judgement to the one who made it and chooses none.

## His death
He died in 131 AH, and about a hundred raised hadith are found from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص2540}.
