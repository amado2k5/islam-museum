## Summary
Ilham Tohti is a Uyghur economist and public intellectual whom the world came to know through his website Uyghur Online (Uyghurbiz), which for two decades sought to build bridges of understanding between Uyghurs and Han Chinese — and then through his trial and life sentence in 2014, a case that became one of the best-known Chinese human-rights files of the century. {cite:wikipedia-ilham-tohti}

## Early life and education
He was born in 1969 in Artux in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, studied at the Central University for Nationalities in Beijing where he took degrees in economics, and returned to teach at the same university, specialising in regional economic disparities between China's east and west and development in minority areas. {cite:wikipedia-ilham-tohti}

## Uyghur Online
In 2006 he founded the Uyghur Online website in Chinese and Uyghur as a dialogue platform for economic and cultural discussion, publishing research and articles on Uyghur unemployment and developmental inequality, becoming the best-known Uyghur voice addressing Chinese readers directly inside China — until the authorities shut it repeatedly. {cite:wikipedia-ilham-tohti} {cite:muslim500-ilham-tohti}

## Arrest and trial
Tohti was detained in January 2014 after years of documented harassment — surveillance, house arrest and travel bans — and tried in September 2014 on charges of "separatism"; the Urumqi court convicted him and imposed life imprisonment with confiscation of property, upheld on appeal. Western governments and rights organisations denounced the trial as a prosecution of opinion, while the Chinese authorities presented the charge as criminal; the museum reports both positions attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-ilham-tohti}

## International awards
In prison he received major rights prizes: the Martin Ennals Award (2016), the Václav Havel Prize (2019) and the European Parliament's Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought (2019), while his daughter Jewher Ilham leads an international campaign for his release. {cite:wikipedia-ilham-tohti}

## Legacy
His case is read in rights literature as the end of the era of internal Uyghur-Chinese dialogue: a moderate academic voice that believed in the linguistic bridge received the severest sentence, making his biography the evidence rights organisations cite on the limits of rational dissent inside the contemporary Chinese system. {cite:wikipedia-ilham-tohti}
