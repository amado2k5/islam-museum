## Summary
Umar ibn Harun al-Balkhi, Abu Hafs, client of Thaqif, narrated from [[person/jafar-al-sadiq|Ja'far ibn Muhammad]] and [[person/ibn-jurayj|Ibn Jurayj]], and [[person/qutayba-ibn-said|Qutayba]], [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]], Nasr ibn Ali and many others narrated from him, as [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] records in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]]. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 228} Ibn Jurayj married his sister, and he lived near him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 228} He died at [[place/balkh|Balkh]] in 194 AH. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 229}

## The critics' verdicts
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: abandoned, even called a liar**: Ibn Mahdi, Ahmad and al-Nasa'i said his hadith is abandoned; Ibn Ma'in called him a wicked liar, and in Abbas's report "nothing"; Salih Jazara said liar; Abu Dawud said not trustworthy; Ibn al-Madini and al-Daraqutni said very weak; Ibn Hibban said he relates from trustworthy narrators reports missing two links. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 228}
- **Sunni: seeking an excuse (Bahz ibn Asad, Qutayba, al-Dhahabi)**: Bahz ibn Asad said: I think Yahya ibn Sa'id envied him; he narrated much from Ibn Jurayj, but whoever attends a man for twelve years will narrate much from him. Qutayba said he was hard on the Murji'a and among the most learned in the Qur'an readings; Ibn Hibban reports that Ibn Mahdi thought well of him; al-Dhahabi concludes that he was a vessel of learning despite his weakness and many objectionable reports, and that he does not think he was one to lie deliberately. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 228} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 229}
:::

## The bulk of his hadith
Abu Ghassan Zunayj reported Umar ibn Harun as saying: I threw away seventy thousand hadiths of mine. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 228} Muhammad ibn Amr al-Suwayqi said he saw him in Baghdad asked about a hadith of Ibn Jurayj which al-Thawri related and in which he had no partner; he narrated it to them, and he saw them tear up their books on him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 229} Abu Talib said he heard Ahmad say: I do not narrate from Umar ibn Harun, though I wrote much from him, but Abd al-Rahman used to say he had no worth in his eyes. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 229}
