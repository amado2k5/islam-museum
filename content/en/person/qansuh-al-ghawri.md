## Overview
Qansuh al-Ghawri al-Circassian, al-Malik al-Ashraf, the last sultan of the Circassian state. "Shadharat al-Dhahab" describes him as extremely greedy, given to injustice and oppression, and seizing people's money. It says that inheritance was abolished in his days, so that the whole estate of a deceased person was taken. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.530-531}

The book also says that he conquered Yemen and took mamluks who spread corruption. One story is told of one of his agents who, to a merchant who would not accept his price, said "the law of God" and struck him with a mace. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.530-531}

## Marj Dabiq
A confrontation took place between him and Sultan Selim, king of the Rum, over Isma'il Shah. They met at Marj Dabiq north of Aleppo on 15 Rajab 922 AH. His army was routed by the trick of Khayr Bey, and al-Ghawri was lost under the hooves of the horses. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.530-531}

He was sultan of Egypt and Syria, and the seat of his realm was [[place/cairo|Cairo]].

## In al-Salabi
Al-Salabi adds that al-Ghawri sided with the Ottoman princes fleeing Selim — foremost Prince [[person/ahmad-ibn-bayazid|Ahmad]], the sultan's brother — that Ottoman intelligence found his secret alliance letter with the Safavids in the Topkapı archive, and that the victors prayed over him and buried him at Aleppo's edge {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 184-187}.
## Al-Ghuri in Yeomans
After Qaytbay's abdication weak sultans followed until al-Ghuri, coming to the throne at sixty in 1501, restored stability by force of character, refilled the bankrupt treasury with heavy taxes, and spent on [[building/madrasa-of-sultan-al-ghuri|his madrasa]], [[building/mausoleum-sabil-kuttab-al-ghouri|khanqah]] and [[building/wakala-of-al-ghuri|wakala]] in the Ghuriyya, and on fortifying Alexandria and Cairo, digging wells, cutting canals and building roads, bridges and aqueducts. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.229} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.231} In Yeomans's view he spent on a lavish court and on buying Mamluks but failed to modernise his army. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.231} He faced the Portuguese, who threatened Egypt's trade; his fleet beat them off Chaul near Bombay in 1512 but lost at Diu in 1513. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.231} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.232} When Selim occupied disputed Mamluk territory al-Ghuri marched to Aleppo; at [[event/marj-dabiq-922|Marj Dabiq]] in 1516 Ottoman firearms overcame the Mamluk cavalry and al-Ghuri, over seventy, died of a seizure in the battle, never to lie in his tomb. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.229} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.232}
