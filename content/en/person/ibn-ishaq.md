## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ishaq ibn Yasar, author of the *Sira* (Biography of the Prophet). {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.345}

## His lineage according to al-Baladhuri
Al-Baladhuri says that his father [[person/yasar-mawla-qays|Yasar]] was among the captives of the church of Ayn al-Tamr in the days of Khalid ibn al-Walid, and was a client of Qays ibn Makhrama ibn al-Muttalib ibn Abd Manaf. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.345}

## His death
According to al-Mas'udi he died in 150 AH. It is also said to be 151 AH, or 152 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.293}

## His lineage and teachers in al-Dhahabi
In [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] gives an entry to Muhammad ibn Ishaq ibn Yasar, Abu Bakr al-Makhrami, their client, of Medina, and calls him "one of the eminent imams". {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 468} He states that his grandfather Yasar was among the captives of Ayn al-Tamr, a client of Qays ibn Makhrama ibn Abd al-Muttalib ibn Abd Manaf. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 468} He saw Anas ibn Malik and [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]], and narrated from Sa'id ibn Abi Hind, al-Maqburi, Ata', al-A'raj, Nafi' and their generation. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 468} From him narrated the two Hammads, Ibrahim ibn Sa'd, Ziyad al-Bakka'i, Salama al-Abrash, [[person/yazid-ibn-harun|Yazid ibn Harun]] and many others, and Ibn Ma'in said that he had heard from Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 468} His entry carries the symbol "[عو، م معاً]": the authors of the four Sunan cite him, and Muslim cites him paired with another transmitter. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 468} Al-Dhahabi closes by noting that Muslim used five of his hadiths as corroboration in his Sahih. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 475}

## Between those who trusted him and those who attacked him
Al-Dhahabi says that several declared him trustworthy and others, such as al-Daraqutni, declared him weak; that he is sound in hadith; and that he has no fault in al-Dhahabi's eyes except the objectionable, broken reports and forged verses he packed into the Sira. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469}

**Those who trusted him.** Al-Dhahabi reports [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]] saying "Ibn Ishaq is the commander of the faithful in hadith", that he is truthful, and that if Shu'ba had authority he would make him commander over the traditionists. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 473} [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said he was fair in hadith; [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]] said he was trustworthy but not a proof; [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ali ibn al-Madini]] said his hadith was sound to him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469} Asked whether he had any doubt about Ibn Ishaq's truthfulness, Ibn Ma'in answered: no, he was truthful. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 472} Al-Dhahabi reports, through [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]], [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]] saying that knowledge would remain in this lava-field as long as "that squint-eyed man" — Ibn Ishaq — was there. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 472} Ahmad ibn Abd Allah al-Ijli said: trustworthy. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 475} He reports [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] saying that Ibn Ishaq would likely have a thousand hadiths found with no one else, and observes that al-Bukhari did not list him in his book of weak transmitters. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 471} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 475} Ibn Adi said he had searched Ibn Ishaq's many hadiths and found nothing on which weakness could be firmly pronounced; he might err or slip as others do, trustworthy men and imams did not hold back from narrating from him, and there was no harm in him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 474}

**Those who attacked him.** Sulayman al-Taymi called him a liar; Wuhayb reported the same from [[person/hisham-ibn-urwa|Hisham ibn Urwa]], and when Wuhayb asked [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] about him, Malik accused him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469} Al-Nasa'i and others said he was not strong, and al-Daraqutni said he was not relied on. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469} Al-Fallas heard [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya al-Qattan]] tell a man on his way to write down the Sira: "You will write much falsehood." {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469} Ibn Idris reported that Malik was told Ibn Ishaq had said "Present Malik's knowledge to me, for I am its veterinarian", and that Malik answered with a harsh word about him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469} Ahmad said he was very much given to *tadlis*, and that he would say "he informed me" and still differ from others. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 470}

**The charge of Qadarism.** Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Numayr said he was charged with the Qadari view and was the farthest of men from it; Abu Dawud called him a Qadari Mu'tazili; and [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Ibn Uyayna]] said he had heard no one speak against him except about his view on qadar. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 469} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 470} Humayd ibn Habib is reported to have seen him flogged over qadar, flogged by the governor Ibrahim ibn Hisham. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 472} The general question is set out in [[question/riwayat-ahl-al-bida|narration from people of innovation]].

## The story of Fatima bint al-Mundhir
The root of Hisham ibn Urwa's charge, as al-Dhahabi tells it, is that Ibn Ishaq narrated from Hisham's wife Fatima bint al-Mundhir, and Hisham said that no man had ever seen her. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 470} {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 471} Al-Dhahabi rejects this: perhaps he heard from her in the mosque, or as a boy, or she spoke to him from behind a screen, and she was by then an old woman. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 470} He adds that Muhammad ibn Suqa also narrated from her, and that the story's claim that she was brought to Hisham as a bride at nine is a plain error, since she was thirteen years older than Hisham; Ibn Ishaq took from her when she was in her fifties or older. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 471} He asks: "Is a man of learning to be called a liar on the strength of something like this? It is rejected." {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 471} He reports Ali ibn al-Madini saying that [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] neither sat with him nor knew him, and that what Hisham said is no proof. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 475}

## The Sira, the verses and the Isra'iliyyat
Al-Dhahabi quotes al-Khatib al-Baghdadi: it was reported that Ibn Ishaq gave the poets of his time accounts of the campaigns and asked them to compose verses on them, so as to attach the verses to the accounts. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 471} He reports Ibn Abi Fudayk saying he saw Ibn Ishaq writing from a man of the People of the Book, and comments that there is nothing to prevent narrating Isra'iliyyat from the People of the Book, the proof lying only in the Book and the Sunna. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 470} He records from Abd Allah ibn Fa'id that whenever Ibn Ishaq took up a branch of learning, the whole session went with him into it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 472}

## Al-Dhahabi's verdict and his death
Al-Dhahabi gives his death as the year 151, or a year after it. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 475} He then says that what appears to him is that Ibn Ishaq is fair in hadith, upright and truthful; that what he alone reports carries something objectionable, because there is something in his memory; and that imams relied on him. {cite:mizan-itidal-bijawi-v3 p. 475}
