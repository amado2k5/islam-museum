## Overview
Quzman is a man Ibn Ishaq mentions in his account of [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], on the authority of [[person/asim-ibn-umr-qatada|'Asim ibn 'Umar ibn Qatada]]: among Banu Zafar there was a stranger, of unknown origin, called Quzman, and whenever he was mentioned to the Prophet he would say, "He is one of the people of the Fire." {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 12}

## The day of Uhud
At Uhud he fought fiercely and killed eight or seven of the enemy on his own, for he was a man of great courage; then his wounds disabled him and he was carried to the quarter of [[group/banu-zafar-al-aws|Banu Zafar]]. Some of the Muslims said to him, "By God, you have done well today, Quzman; rejoice!" He said, "Rejoice in what? By God, I fought only for the honour of my people; but for that I would not have fought." When his wounds grew worse he took an arrow from his quiver and killed himself with it. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 12} The edition's heading for the passage reads: "The death of Quzman as a hypocrite, as the Messenger had said." {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 12}

## His name in al-Suhayli's commentary
Al-Suhayli says the name comes from *qazam*, the worthless part of one's property, and that *quzman* is said of the poorest of anything. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 36} The editor remarks that he found nothing in *al-Lisan* or *al-Qamus* except that it is a name or a place-name. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 36}
