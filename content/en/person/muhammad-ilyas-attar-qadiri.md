## Summary
Muhammad Ilyas Attar Qadiri is a Pakistani Islamic scholar and preacher, founder in 1981 of Dawat-e-Islami, the Barelvi missionary movement headquartered in Karachi and instantly recognisable by its followers' green turbans. The movement describes its mission in the formula of reviving the practice of the Prophet in everyday life. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ilyas-attar-qadiri}

## Life and formation
He was born on 12 July 1950 in Karachi (26 Ramadan 1369 in the movement's biographies). He studied under the Barelvi mufti Waqaruddin Qadri Razavi and took the Qadiri spiritual path, from which the nisba in his name comes. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ilyas-attar-qadiri}

## Founding Dawat-e-Islami
On 2 September 1981 he founded Dawat-e-Islami, modelled in its methods on the itinerant preaching of the Tablighi Jamaat but squarely inside the Barelvi (Ahl-e-Sunnat wal-Jama'at) tradition of devotion to the Prophet and the saints. Its Madani Qafila groups travel from town to town, its weekly ijtema congregations draw large crowds in Karachi, and Madani Channel, launched in 2008, broadcasts its teaching worldwide. His best-known book, Faizan-e-Sunnat, collects practices of daily life arranged for lay readers, and the movement has produced a large body of his sermons and booklets through its Maktaba-tul-Madinah publishing arm. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ilyas-attar-qadiri}

## Standing and readings
The Muslim 500 placed him 44th in its 2027 edition. Admirers credit him with building the Barelvi world's most effective dawah organisation; critics from other Sunni schools dispute particular Barelvi positions the movement teaches, and observers note the centralised, charismatic nature of his leadership. The museum reports the movement's size, methods and the disagreements around it attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-ilyas-attar-qadiri}
