# Asaf Ali

Mohammad Asaf Ali (11 May 1888 – 2 April 1953) was an Indian lawyer, independence activist and politician, the first Indian ambassador to the United States and twice Governor of Odisha {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}. He died in office as ambassador to Switzerland {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}.

## Early life and education

He was born on 11 May 1888 at Seohara, a town in Bijnor district in British India {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}{cite:wikidata-asaf-ali}. He was educated at St. Stephen's College, Delhi, and was called to the Bar from Lincoln's Inn in England {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}.

## The national movement

In 1914, Britain's declaration of war against the Ottoman Empire had a large effect on the Indian Muslim community; Asaf Ali supported the Ottomans, resigned from the Privy Council as an act of non-cooperation, and returned to India in December 1914 to throw himself into the nationalist movement {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}. He was elected to the Central Legislative Assembly in 1935 as a member of the Muslim Nationalist Party, then became significant as a Congress member and deputy leader {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}. The last of his several imprisonments came after the Quit India resolution of August 1942, when he was detained at Ahmednagar Fort with Jawaharlal Nehru and the Congress Working Committee {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}.

## Legal career

He rose to become one of the most respected lawyers in the country {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}. He defended Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt over the 1929 Central Legislative Assembly bomb case, and in 1945 he was convener of the INA defence team established by Congress for the officers of the Indian National Army charged with treason {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}.

## Ambassador and governor

He was in charge of Railways and Transport in the Interim Government headed by Nehru from September 1946, and served as the first Indian ambassador to the United States from February to mid-April 1947 {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}. He was then Governor of Odisha for two terms, from 1948 to 1952, and afterwards ambassador to Switzerland {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}.

## Personal life and death

In 1928 he married Aruna Ganguly, a marriage that raised eyebrows on grounds of religion — the article states that Asaf Ali was a Muslim while Aruna was a Hindu — and of the twenty-year age difference {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}. His wife is widely remembered for hoisting the Congress flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay during the Quit India movement of 1942 {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}.

He died in office in Bern on 2 April 1953, while serving as ambassador to Switzerland {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}; the Arabic article and Wikidata give 1 April instead {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-ar}{cite:wikidata-asaf-ali}. India Post issued a stamp in his honour in 1989 {cite:wikipedia-asaf-ali-en}.
