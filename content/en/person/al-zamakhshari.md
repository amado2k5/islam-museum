## Overview
Abu al-Qasim Mahmud ibn Umar ibn Muhammad al-Zamakhshari al-Khwarazmi. Ibn al-Imad describes him as the great imam in exegesis, hadith, grammar, philology and the science of eloquence. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.598-600}

## His creed
He held a Mu'tazili creed, and he showed and declared it. Ibn al-Imad says that when he asked permission to enter upon a patron he would say: "Tell him that Abu al-Qasim the Mu'tazili is at the door." {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.598-600}

## Mecca and the name
He stayed in Mecca for a time, and he was called "Jar Allah", and this name became his title. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.598-600}

## His leg
His leg fell, so he walked in a wooden shell. He had with him a document bearing the testimony of many people. Ibn al-Imad relates from him that he attributed this to his mother's supplication against him in his youth. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.598-600}

He was born on the seventeenth of Rajab 467 AH and died in 538 AH at the age of seventy-one. The Gregorian dates in the museum's data are derived. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.600}

"Kashf al-Zunun" attributes to him the book [[work/asas-al-balagha|Asas al-Balagha]]. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan}

## His life in the introduction to the Dar al-Ma'rifa edition of al-Kashshaf
In his introduction to al-Kashshaf, Khalil Ma'mun Shiha gives al-Zamakhshari's name as Mahmud ibn Umar ibn Muhammad ibn Umar, his kunya Abu al-Qasim, his title Jar Allah ("God's neighbour") from his long residence in Mecca, and his nisba from Zamakhshar, a village of Khwarazm. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.7} He says he was born in Zamakhshar on Wednesday 27 Rajab 467 AH — *Shadharat al-Dhahab* has the 17th — and travelled as a boy to Bukhara to study, becoming the most learned of the non-Arab scholars of his time in Arabic. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.7} He notes that the biographies name his legal school in only two books, al-Fasi's *al-Iqd al-Thamin* and Muhammad Tahir al-Hindi's *al-Mughni*, both of which call him a Hanafi, which Muhammad Husayn al-Dhahabi confirms. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.8} Among his teachers he names Abu al-Khattab Nasr ibn al-Batir, Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn al-Muzaffar of Nishapur and Abu Mudar Mahmud ibn Jarir al-Dabbi of Isfahan. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.8} He lists forty-nine works, among them *Rabi' al-Abrar*, *al-Fa'iq fi Gharib al-Hadith*, *al-Mufassal* in grammar, *al-Mustaqsa* on proverbs, *Asas al-Balagha* and *al-Kashshaf*, besides a diwan of poetry. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.9} He says he died on the eve of Arafa 538 AH at al-Jurjaniyya in Khwarazm after returning from Mecca. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.10} The editor describes him as zealous for the Mu'tazili school — that is the editor's judgement; see [[work/al-kashshaf|al-Kashshaf]] and [[question/how-did-sunni-scholars-read-al-kashshaf|How did Sunni scholars treat al-Kashshaf?]]. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.8}
