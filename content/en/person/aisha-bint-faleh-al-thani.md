## Summary
Sheikha Aisha bint Faleh Al-Thani is a Qatari educationist and businesswoman who founded and chairs Al Faleh Educational Holding, building it into one of Qatar's largest private education institutions and making it, in April 2021, the first woman-led Qatari company listed on the Qatar Stock Exchange and the first Qatari education institution ever listed. {cite:alfaleh-aisha-al-thani}

## Education
She earned a bachelor's in education and a second bachelor's in English literature (with distinction) from Qatar University, then an MBA from the University of Hull in the United Kingdom, then a PhD from Cass Business School in corporate governance — a formation combining school-level education with institutional management. {cite:alfaleh-aisha-al-thani}

## Public service and institutions
She served as a member of Qatar's Supreme Education Council, appointed in 2006 for two terms until 2014 — the period of Qatar's great education reform that built the "Education for a New Era" system — and sat on the board of Reach Out to Asia, the charitable initiative supporting schooling in Asian countries; she is also a founding member of the board of patrons of the World Congress of Muslim Philanthropists. {cite:alfaleh-aisha-al-thani} {cite:muslim500-aisha-bint-faleh-al-thani}

## Al Faleh Educational Holding
She founded Al Faleh Educational Holding as an umbrella for schools and education bodies including Doha Academy schools (Al Waab and Salwa), Doha International Kindergarten, and AFG College in partnership with the University of Aberdeen — the first British university operating a dedicated campus in Qatar — serving students of more than fifty nationalities. In April 2021 the company listed on the venture market of the Qatar Stock Exchange, registering two firsts: the first woman-led Qatari company to go public, and the first Qatari educational institution listed on the market. {cite:alfaleh-aisha-al-thani}

## Recognition
She featured in a 2023 Time magazine profile on Qatar and investment in education, and is presented in Qatari media as a pioneer of women's education entrepreneurship and a model of moving from public education service to building listed private institutions. {cite:alfaleh-aisha-al-thani}

## Legacy
Her career reads as the completion of private Qatari education: from teacher to policymaker on the Supreme Council to education entrepreneur who opened schools and a British university in the heart of Doha, while enshrining women's leadership of listed companies on the Gulf's exchanges. {cite:alfaleh-aisha-al-thani}
