## Overview
Labid ibn al-Asam is a Jewish man of the Banu Qaynuqa'. Al-Bukhari relates in the Book of Medicine that a man bewitched the Prophet — three people: a man, a woman and his nephew — whereupon the Prophet sought refuge in God from the evil of what He created and recited the two protective suras. He was told that Labid was in a lock of hair; he was brought and questioned and confessed. The Prophet ordered that a branch of the lote-tree with three leaves be brought, burned to ash, water blown into it, and upon the Prophet were recited al-Baqara, Al 'Imran and the two protective suras — he recovered, while Labid's hair fell out and the marks showed on his body. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

This report is the root of the "sorcery worked against the Prophet" tradition in the hadith and sira literature — the most famous account of magic in Islam, and the model for lawful ruqyah by Qur'an.
