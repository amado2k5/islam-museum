## Summary
Khaled Montaser is an Egyptian physician, writer and television presenter, born in Giza on 27 January 1960. A dermatologist who headed the dermatology and venereology department at the Suez Canal Authority, he became one of Egypt's most widely read newspaper columnists and one of its best-known public defenders of scientific method against pseudoscience. His book Wahm al-I'jaz al-'Ilmi (The Illusion of "Scientific Miraculousness", 2005) opened a long public argument about the genre that reads modern science into the Quran, and from September 2015 he presented Kharej Al-Nass (Outside the Text) on ON TV, a long-form interview programme with writers and thinkers. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-ar} {cite:arageek-khaled-montaser} {cite:abjjad-khaled-montaser-bio}

He describes his own project as enlightenment: a defence of reason and science, a campaign against female genital mutilation and miracle-cure fraud, and a call to renew religious thought — a task he insists belongs to the whole society, "the people of culture, thought, science and art", and not to clerics alone. The same positions have made him a target: Islamist and Salafi critics call him an unbeliever, and in 2011 Egypt's Dar Al-Iftaa issued a conditional fatwa about a sentence he had spoken on television. This article reports his statements and his critics' statements as the sources give them; it is not a verdict by the museum. {cite:ahl-masr-montaser-interview-2019} {cite:islamstory-dar-al-iftaa-fatwa-292399}

## Early life and education
He was born in Giza on 27 January 1960. His mother died while he was young, and he was raised partly by relatives in El-Shaara, a village in Damietta, in the Nile Delta. His memoir of 2024 returns to that childhood, and to the drowning of his younger sister Nadia. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-ar} {cite:wikidata-khaled-montaser} {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-arz}

The memoir describes how he entered the Faculty of Medicine at Cairo University (Kasr Al-Ainy) after the secondary-school examinations of 1976–77, by way of a first year in the Faculty of Science. He graduated in medicine in 1983. {cite:amireta-montaser-interview-2018}

## Medical career
He specialised in dermatology and venereology (skin and sexually transmitted diseases). His own official biography, repeated by Wikipedia, Abjjad, Goodreads and publisher pages, states that he headed the dermatology and venereology department at the Suez Canal Authority. One Al-Masry Al-Youm interview instead says "Suez Canal University"; the museum follows his own biography's wording, the Authority, which is the more widely repeated. Whether he still holds the post or has retired is not established by the sources read; his 2024 memoir jokes about joining "the retirees' club". {cite:abjjad-khaled-montaser-bio} {cite:masry-al-youm-montaser-interview}

Alongside the clinic he held public cultural posts. From an interview of 2018: he was a member of Egypt's Supreme Council of Culture until 2015, a member of the Family Library (Maktabat al-Usra) book-selection committee in 2017, and supervisor of the scientific-culture series at the Palaces of Culture (Qusur al-Thaqafa), the state cultural centres. {cite:amireta-montaser-interview-2018}

He also wrote for the screen early on: elCinema credits him with the story, screenplay and dialogue of the television series Sanaye' Sanaye' (2002), and with co-preparing the radio series Al-Kalam Al-Mubah (Permissible Speech). {cite:elcinema-khaled-montaser}

## Newspaper columns
His first article appeared in the magazine Sabah Al-Kheir, as his memoir recounts. He became a long-standing columnist at Al-Masry Al-Youm, one of Egypt's leading independent dailies, which still interviews him as one of its own writers. His earlier and parallel bylines include Sawt Al-Umma, Al-Dostour, Nisf Al-Dunya and Rose Al-Youssef, and he ran the sexual-culture section of the Elaph website. Arageek credits him with more than 1,800 articles. {cite:abjjad-khaled-montaser-bio} {cite:arageek-khaled-montaser} {cite:amireta-montaser-interview-2018}

In 2010 he received the Bahrain prize for the best newspaper columnist in the Arab world. {cite:amireta-montaser-interview-2018}

## Television programmes
His television work began in medicine and widened into the interview format for which he is now known:

- **The medical segment of Al-Ashera Masa'an** (The Tenth at Night), Dream TV — he presented and prepared the programme's medical slot; Arageek dates his start there to 2011. It was on this programme that his 2011 exchange with the Muslim Brotherhood figure Sobhi Saleh, discussed below, took place. {cite:arageek-khaled-montaser} {cite:abjjad-khaled-montaser-bio}
- **Din wa-Dunya** (Religion and Worldly Life), Dream TV — presenter and preparer, listed in his standard biography. {cite:abjjad-khaled-montaser-bio}
- **Hissat Qira'a** (A Reading Session), Dream TV — named in his 2018 Al-Masry Al-Youm interview. {cite:amireta-montaser-interview-2018}
- **Kharej Al-Nass** (Outside the Text), ON TV — from September 2015. Long-form interviews with writers and thinkers; among them a two-part interview with Farag Foda and a recorded interview with the screenwriter Wahid Hamed, clips of which he still posts to his YouTube channel. {cite:arageek-khaled-montaser} {cite:youtube-khaled-montaser}
- **Yatafakkarun** (They Reflect), Al-Ghad channel, 2018 — a programme discussing what he calls supposed axioms, and political-Islam concepts such as the caliphate, jihad and takfir. {cite:arageek-khaled-montaser}

He remains a television guest in 2026: in January 2026 he appeared on Al-Shams channel's Kul al-Kalam with Amr Hafez to discuss his novel, and in 2026 he has appeared on Sada El-Balad's Nazra with Hamdi Rizk, speaking against alternative medicine and about public religiosity, and on Al-Hayah TV's Atyaf with Safaa El-Naggar. {cite:arageek-khaled-montaser}

- **Wahm al-I'jaz al-'Ilmi** (The Illusion of "Scientific Miraculousness"), 2005 — his best-known book. ## His public project, in his own framing
The positions below are his, stated in his interviews and books, and are reported in his own terms.

**Science against superstition.** The theme of his career is the defence of the scientific method against pseudoscience, "alternative medicine" and miracle cures. Of alternative medicine he said in Al-Masry Al-Youm that it is "a term God never sanctioned… a road to obscene wealth through fraud". He campaigns against female genital mutilation and for sex education in schools. {cite:masry-al-youm-montaser-interview} {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-ar}

**Against "scientific i'jaz".** His 2005 book argues that linking religion to science — which changes as knowledge grows — exposes the text to every revision of science. The Quran's miraculousness, in his stated view, lies in "the humanity and nobility of its ideas", not in anticipating physics. {cite:masry-al-youm-montaser-interview}

**Secularism.** He accepts the label openly: "Yes, I am secular in thought, and I do not shy from the description." Following the philosopher Mourad Wahba, he defines secularism as thinking about the relative in relative terms, and argues that it protects all religions by keeping belief in the private sphere and out of state institutions: "it is not hostile to religions… it protects them all." {cite:masry-al-youm-montaser-interview}

**Renewal of religious thought.** He prefers "renewal of religious thought" to "renewal of religious discourse", and holds that it is the task of the whole society, not a clerical monopoly: in 2019 he put it that renewal "needs the people of culture, thought, science and art", and called religious specialisation "a scarecrow the sheikhs invented to keep their authority". {cite:ahl-masr-montaser-interview-2019}

**Al-Azhar and the clerics.** "I am not in conflict with Al-Azhar as an entity," he told Al-Masry Al-Youm, "but with a Salafi-Wahhabi current that controls it." In Ahl Masr in October 2019 he framed his attacks on clerics as a defence of Islam itself: "I do not attack beliefs… it is my right — indeed my duty, and the duty of every Muslim — to criticise practices of religiosity, which are human endeavour, not the core of the creed." {cite:masry-al-youm-montaser-interview} {cite:ahl-masr-montaser-interview-2019}

**Women.** He opposes polygamy, calling it "an insult to women, a male reading of the Quran", opposes clerical doctrines he sees as demeaning women, campaigns against FGM, and has called for a Tunisian-style law restricting polygamy. {cite:masry-al-youm-montaser-interview}

**Political Islam and folk religiosity.** He attacks the Muslim Brotherhood — he has said he received assassination threats during their rule, his own statement as reported in 2026 — and Salafism, and practices such as saint-veneration at mawlid festivals: of the 2025 festival of Al-Sayyid Al-Badawi he wrote on Facebook that Egypt "won't manufacture medicine or defend its borders with superstition — we need science, not saints". {cite:facebook-khaled-montaser}

**His faith, in his own words.** In a press-reported interview of about 2011 he described himself as "an Egyptian Muslim liberal" (Misri Muslim Librali), adding that a Muslim may hold any political orientation. In Al-Masry Al-Youm he calls the Quran "a sacred divine text" and says of its humane miraculousness: "this is my certainty". The museum records his faith classification as unknown, for the reasons set out in the record's faith note: his self-description is attested, but the classification is publicly contested by opponents, and the museum does not resolve a contested classification. {cite:kenanaonline-montaser-interview} {cite:masry-al-youm-montaser-interview} {cite:ahl-masr-montaser-interview-2019}

## Perspectives and criticism
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**Dar Al-Iftaa, 2011.** After his exchange with Sobhi Saleh on Al-Ashera Masa'an, in which he asked "What does God have to do with politics?" (Ih dakhl Allah fi al-siyasa?), Dar Al-Iftaa's fatwa no. 292399, of May–June 2011, called the phrase "bad manners toward God" and ruled conditionally: if he meant by it that divine legislation is unfit to govern between people, "he disbelieves thereby". The fatwa is a conditional ruling on a meaning, not a personal sentence of apostasy, and the museum reports it in exactly those terms. {cite:islamstory-dar-al-iftaa-fatwa-292399}

**Islamist and Salafi critics.** Critics in that current have long attacked him as an unbeliever. After a seminar talk in May 2015 criticising "scientific i'jaz", Copts United reported attacks "calling him an infidel that should be killed". Hostile forums run refutation threads against his articles. {cite:copts-united-montaser-2015} {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-ar}

**Religious-establishment pushback.** Preachers such as Mohamed Abu Bakr Gad Al-Rab have publicly rebutted his criticism of the inheritance rules — he had asked on Facebook "where is the logic" in an uncle inheriting equally with a daughter — and Al-Nada TV aired a segment attacking his 2019 article "Al-Shaarawy caused the death of liver patients". {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-ar}

**Supporters.** His supporters present him as an enlightenment figure; the Bibliotheca Alexandrina hosted the discussion of his memoir, and mainstream papers — Al-Masry Al-Youm, Al-Ahram, Asharq Al-Awsat, Shorouk — cover his books seriously. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-ar}

## Personal life
Arabic Wikipedia records that he is married to the writer Samah Abou Bakr Ezzat, a children's author, daughter of the actor Abu Bakr Ezzat and the writer Kawthar Heikal; he has himself written a book about his father-in-law. The Egyptian-dialect article names two sons, Ali and Omar. The museum publishes no family detail beyond this, which is already in the public sources. {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-ar} {cite:wikipedia-khaled-montaser-arz}

## Reach
His most active public channel is Facebook, where his professional-mode account had 145,670 followers as read on 17 November 2026; Egyptian news sites covered his posts of 14–16 November 2026. His X account, @khaledmontaser — described by Al-Arab newspaper as his official account — had 72,822 followers in a SpeakRJ audit snapshot of 28 August 2026. His YouTube channel, described as "the physician, writer and broadcaster", carries his Kharej Al-Nass interviews and talks, mostly from 2020–2023, with view counts in the low thousands; its subscriber count could not be verified from accessible sources and is not stated. His official website is khaledmontaser.com; no published email address was found on any of his own outlets.
