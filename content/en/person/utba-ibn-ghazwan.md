## Summary
[[person/utba-ibn-ghazwan|Utba ibn Ghazwan]] ibn Jabir al-Mazini, ally of Banu Abd Shams or Banu Nawfal. Of "the first forerunners" — he emigrated to Abyssinia, then returned an emigrant to Medina, companion of al-Miqdad, and witnessed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] and all after it {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 999}.

## The conquests and Basra
Umar employed him in the conquests; he marked out [[place/basra|Basra]] and won conquests. He was tall and handsome. Muslim and the Sunan scholars related for him {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 999}.

## His hadith on hunger
In Muslim, from his hadith: "I saw myself the seventh of seven with the Messenger of God, with no food but the leaves of trees" {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 999}.

## His death
Ibn Sa'd and others said: he came to Umar asking release from the governorship; Umar refused, and he set back and died on the road at Ma'din Banu Sulaym in year 17 AH — or it is said 20, or before that — having lived fifty-seven years {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 999}.

## A weak hadith
Al-Tabarani narrates from him "whoever lies against me deliberately, let him take his seat in the Fire"; Ibn Hajar mentions that in its chain is Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr ibn Nadla, who is abandoned {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 999}.

## In Khalid Muhammad Khalid's Rijal Hawl al-Rasul
[[person/khalid-muhammad-khalid|Khalid Muhammad Khalid]] heads his chapter with Utba's words "Tomorrow you will see the governors after me". He says Utba was the seventh of the first seven to accept Islam, emigrated to Abyssinia and then returned to Mecca out of longing for the Prophet, later emigrated to Medina, and was an outstanding archer. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.450} He relates that Umar sent him with an army to "the farthest land of the Arabs and the nearest land of the Persians"; he took al-Ubulla, laid out [[place/basra|Basra]] in its place and built its mosque, and when he wished to give up the governorship Umar ordered him to stay. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.451} He fought luxury and urged contentment; he told the people in a sermon that he had seen himself with the Messenger of God as the seventh of seven with nothing to eat but tree leaves until their mouths were sore, and that when he was given a cloak he split it in two and gave half to Sa'd ibn Malik. He would say, "I seek refuge with God from being great in your world and small in God's sight", and when people chafed at his strictness he said, "Tomorrow you will see the governors after me." {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.452} On pilgrimage he asked Umar to relieve him, and Umar refused; he prayed that God would not return him to Basra or to office, and death overtook him on the road back. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.452} {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.453}
