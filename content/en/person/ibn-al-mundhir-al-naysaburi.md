## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn al-Mundhir al-Naysaburi, known as Abu Bakr, was a jurist who exercised ijtihad and one of the hadith memorisers; he was the shaykh of the Haram in Mecca. He was born in 242 AH (856 CE) and died in Mecca in 319 AH (931 CE) {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 290}.

## His works and what was said of him
Al-Zirikli reports al-Dhahabi as saying that Ibn al-Mundhir was the author of books the like of which no one had composed {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 290}. He names "al-Mabsut" in jurisprudence; "al-Awsat fi al-Sunan wa-al-Ijma' wa-al-Ikhtilaf", in manuscript; "al-Ishraf 'ala Madhahib Ahl al-'Ilm", in manuscript, of which the third part, on jurisprudence, survives; "Ikhtilaf al-'Ulama'", in manuscript, of which the first part survives; and "Tafsir al-Qur'an", in manuscript, a large volume; and others {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 5 p. 290}.
