## Summary
A scribe of the revelation in Medina who apostatised and fled to Mecca; the Prophet declared his blood lawful on the conquest day, but he hid with his foster-brother [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]], who brought him seeking safety. The Prophet pardoned him, he became Muslim and his Islam was good, and he held the governorship of Egypt under Umar and Uthman {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/51-52}.

## His affair on the conquest day
Ibn Ishaq relates that Abdullah ibn Sa'd had written for the Messenger of God, then apostatised into polytheism and joined Quraysh; on the conquest day his killing was ordered, so he fled to Uthman ibn Affan — his foster-brother — who hid him until he brought him to the Prophet after the people had settled, saying: Messenger of God, accept Abdullah's pledge. The Prophet was long silent, then accepted his pledge {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/51-52}.

Then the Prophet said to his companions: "was there no wise man among you to go to this one when he saw me withhold from his pledge, and kill him?" They said: Messenger of God, we cannot know what is in your mind — why did you not signal us with your eye? He said: "it is not fitting for a prophet to have treacherous eyes" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/52}.

Abdullah returned to Islam and did well, took part in the conquests, and Umar ibn al-Khattab then Uthman ibn Affan appointed him over Egypt {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/52}.

## For young visitors
A man who had left Islam and fled to Mecca; at the conquest his foster-brother Uthman ibn Affan interceded for him, the Prophet accepted his Islam and pardoned him — and afterwards he became a commander and governor.
