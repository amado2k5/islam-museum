## Overview
Ahmad ibn Uways ibn Hasan al-Jalayiri, Ghiyath al-Din, has an entry in al-Zirikli's al-A'lam under the name Sultan Ahmad Bahadur. Al-Zirikli calls him the last sultan of the Jalayirid state at Baghdad, Mongol by origin and Arabised, and gives his death as 813 AH, 1410 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 101}. He belongs to [[polity/jalayirids|the Jalayirids]].

## Upbringing and accession
Al-Zirikli says his forebears were men of Genghis Khan and Hulagu, and that rule over Iraq passed to his grandfather Shaykh Hasan. He grew up in Tabriz, lived for a time in Baghdad, and acted for his brother Sultan Husayn in Basra; then he killed his brother and took the sultanate in 784 AH, and killed a number of army emirs whom he feared would turn on him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 101}.

## Character and literary taste
Al-Zirikli reports that his biographers called him bloodthirsty, a man who joined injustice with learning. He took part in literary life, loved music and painting, and wrote much verse in Arabic and Persian {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 101}.

## Struggle with Timur
Al-Zirikli says that [[person/timur-lang|Timur]] appeared in Turkestan and Bukhara and attacked Khurasan, and Sultan Ahmad, busy fighting him, could not hold him back. In 795 AH he went to Aleppo with about four hundred horsemen, and al-Zahir [[person/barquq|Barquq]] summoned him to Cairo and honoured him {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 101}. He returned to Iraq; Timur withdrew into the Qipchaq steppe, so Ahmad went back to Baghdad and retook it in 797 AH, staying until 802 AH, when he sought out the Ottoman sultan Bayezid; Timur then came again to Baghdad, took it and left {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 101}. Ahmad fled alone to Aleppo in 806 AH, where its authorities, as a courtesy to Timur, arrested him and sent him to Damascus. News came in 807 AH of Timur's death on his way to China, and the Egyptian sultan ordered Ahmad freed; he went to Tabriz, whose people welcomed him, retook Baghdad and stayed about five years {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 101}.

## His death
Al-Zirikli says another Mongol, the emir [[person/qara-yusuf|Qara Yusuf]], rose against him; Ahmad fought him and was defeated, captured and strangled in Baghdad {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 1 p. 101}.
