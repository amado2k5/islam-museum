## Summary
Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Hirzihim was a Maghribi jurist whose story appears in [[work/tarif-al-ahya-bi-fadail-al-ihya|Ta'rif al-Ahya' bi-Fada'il al-Ihya']] by [[person/abd-al-qadir-ibn-shaykh-aydarus|Abd al-Qadir al-Aydarus]], quoting [[person/abdullah-al-yafii|Abd Allah ibn As'ad al-Yafi'i]], who calls him "the great shaykh and imam" and "the famous Maghribi jurist". {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 779} His well-known story is that of his rejection of [[work/ihya-ulum-al-din|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]] and his retraction. {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 779} {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 780}

## The rejection of the Ihya'
Al-Yafi'i relates, as al-Aydarus quotes him, that Ibn Hirzihim denounced the Ihya' vehemently and, being "obeyed and listened to", ordered all the copies he could find gathered and intended to burn them in the congregational mosque on a Friday. {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 779}

## The dream and the retraction
According to the story, on the eve of that Friday he dreamt that he entered the mosque and saw the Prophet there with [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], and [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]] standing before him, complaining of his opponent and handing him the book. The Prophet leafed through it page by page and approved of it, then Abu Bakr, then Umar; he ordered Ibn Hirzihim stripped and given the punishment for false accusation, and after five lashes Abu Bakr interceded, and al-Ghazali was satisfied and accepted the intercession. {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 779} {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 780}

The story goes on: Ibn Hirzihim woke with the marks of the lashes on his back, told his companions, repented of his objection and sought forgiveness, and suffered from the marks for a long time until another dream after which he was healed; he then devoted himself to reading the Ihya', "and God opened it to him", and he became one of the great masters of both inward and outward knowledge. {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 780}

## The chain of the story
Al-Yafi'i says, "We have related this with sound chains," and gives his chain through Shihab al-Din Ahmad ibn al-Mayliq al-Shadhili, from Yaqut al-Shadhili, from [[person/abu-al-abbas-al-mursi|Abu al-Abbas al-Mursi]], from [[person/abu-al-hasan-al-shadhili|Abu al-Hasan al-Shadhili]]. He states that al-Shadhili was Ibn Hirzihim's contemporary and said that Ibn Hirzihim died with the marks of the lashes on his back. {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 780}

:::perspectives
- **Ahl al-Sunna: al-Yafi'i and al-Aydarus.** Both relate the story as evidence of the Ihya's merit, and al-Yafi'i calls its chains sound. {cite:ihya-kitab-al-shab-pts13-16 p. 780}
- **Not given here:** an independent biography of Ibn Hirzihim and his death, and the academic view of the story; not yet documented with a defined source.

## For young visitors
Ibn Hirzihim was a scholar of law in the Maghrib who disliked the book Ihya' and wanted to burn it. The story says he then had a dream that changed his mind, and he began to read the book and learn from it.
