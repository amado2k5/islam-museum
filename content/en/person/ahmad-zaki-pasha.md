## Summary
Ahmad Zaki Pasha edited the first volume of [[person/ibn-fadl-allah-al-umari|Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari]]'s [[work/masalik-al-absar|Masalik al-absar]], published by the Dar al-Kutub al-Misriyya press in 1924 in a series he named "Ihya' al-adab al-arabiyya" (Reviving Arabic Letters); he signed his introduction at Giza in the spring of 1342 AH (1924). {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.3} {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.10}

## His search for the book
In his introduction, "A short word on great encyclopedias", he says that Egyptians had been deprived of this book until he restored it to them after a quarter-century of effort, tracking its copies in the libraries of Europe's capitals and the collections of Constantinople; the first volume was as good as lost, the Aya Sofya copy being poor, until he chanced in the Topkapi palace on a book stuck among loose papers under the title "Mir'at al-ka'inat", which proved to be the missing volume, read by a scholar to the author and bearing corrections and additions in the author's hand. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.9} He says that in 1910 he alerted the keeper of that library, who restored its proper title, and that he photographed the whole work together with the Aya Sofya copy and brought it to Cairo, where it is kept in the Dar al-Kutub. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.10}

He says he travelled to Palestine the previous summer to check on the spot the author's architectural account of al-Aqsa, and that he intended to add to later volumes photographs of the author's and copyists' hands and a dictionary of technical terms that had fallen out of use. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.10}
