## Summary
[[person/muhammad-ibn-maslama|Muhammad ibn Maslama]] ibn Salama ibn Khalid al-Awsi al-Ansari al-Harithi, Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Madani, ally of Banu Abd al-Ashhal. Born twenty-two years before the mission per al-Waqidi; he was among those named Muhammad in the Jahiliyya {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}.

## His Islam and his battles
Ibn Sa'd said he became Muslim early at the hand of [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]], before Sa'd ibn Mu'adh; the Prophet made brothers of him and [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda]]. He witnessed Badr and all that followed except the Tabuk expedition — he stayed behind by the Prophet's leave to remain in Medina {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}. He was among those who went to the [[event/killing-of-kab-ibn-al-ashraf|killing of Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf]] and to Ibn Abi al-Huqayq {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}.

## The fitna
Ibn Abd al-Barr said: he was among the virtuous Companions; the Prophet left him in charge of Medina on some of his expeditions; and he was among those who kept clear of the fitna — he witnessed neither the Camel nor Siffin {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}. Hudhayfa said of him: "I know a man whom the fitna cannot harm", and stated he had heard that from the Prophet — narrated by al-Baghawi and others {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}.

## Under Umar
Ibn al-Kalbi said Umar put him over Juhayna's alms {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}. Another said he was kept by Umar for probing knotty matters — he was his envoy in investigating [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]] when he built the palace at Kufa, and burned the palace's gate {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}.

## His death
He dwelt in Medina then al-Rabadha after Uthman's killing, per Ibn Shahin {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}. Al-Waqidi said he died in Medina in Safar of year forty-six at seventy-seven; al-Mada'ini dated it year forty-three {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}. Ibn Abi Dawud and Ya'qub ibn Sufyan said a man of Syria's people, of Jordan, killed him in his house {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1370}; Ibn Hajar mentions he was tall, well-built and bald {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p. 1371}.
