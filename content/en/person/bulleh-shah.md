## Summary

Bulleh Shah (c. 1680–1757), whose name was Sayyid Abdullah Shah Qadri, was a Punjabi Sufi poet among the greatest of the Punjabi language, who spent most of his life at Kasur and died and was buried there {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ur}.

## Life and education

Abdullah Shah was born at Uch, in the Multan Subah, into a Sayyid family of religious scholars; his father Shah Muhammad Darwaish was versed in Arabic, Persian and the Qur'an {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en}. The family moved in his childhood to Malakwal and then to Pandoke near Kasur, where he was schooled by his father and worked as a herder, before his higher education at Kasur under Hafiz Ghulam Murtaza and then at Lahore under Shah Inayat Qadiri, a Sufi murshid whose disciple he became {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ar}. The Urdu article records that he took bay'a at Shah Inayat's hand and that his affiliation was with the Qadiriyya school of the Sufis, with Shattari thought also shaping him {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ur}.

## Poetry and persecution

Bulleh Shah left over 150 kafis and some 94 other compositions in colloquial Punjabi, on the oneness of God, divine love and the equality of human beings {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en}. They attacked the authority and double standards of the clerics, and in his lifetime some Kasur clerics declared him a kafir and he once took refuge in a Sikh gurdwara from fundamentalist threats; at his death the same party refused his funeral prayer, which Syed Zahid Hamdani led, and he was buried on the outskirts of Kasur {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en}. His kafis have passed into proverbs and are sung to this day, from Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan to rock bands {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en}.

## Faith

His initiation into a Sufi order is attested for the man himself in two independent publications — the English article records his discipleship at Lahore under Shah Inayat Qadiri, a Sufi murshid {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en}, and the Urdu article records his bay'a at Shah Inayat's hand and states that he belonged to the Qadiriyya {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ur}, the affiliation his very name carries and which the Arabic article styles al-Qadiri al-Shattari al-Qasuri {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ar}. He is therefore recorded as sufi, documented, with his edge to the Qadiriyya, the Shattari colouring of his thought noted alongside. The kafir declarations of his contemporaries are an episode his sources record, not a settlement of his faith.

## Notes for researchers

Neither his real name nor his years command consensus — the English article gives about 1680 for the birth with the warning, the Urdu article gives 3 March 1680 paired with 1091 AH, and the Punjabi article gives a lifespan of 1680–1758 {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en} {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ur} {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-pa}. For the death, the Urdu article gives 22 August 1757 at Kasur, aged 77, and Wikidata agrees, while the English infobox gives 30 August, Christopher Shackle gives 1758, and the Urdu article also preserves a Tazkira Auliya-e-Pakistan tradition of 1181 AH {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ur} {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-en} {cite:wikidata-bulleh-shah}. The Arabic article's pairing of 1061 AH with 1675 CE is internally inconsistent and is not followed {cite:wikipedia-bulleh-shah-ar}. Neither Uch nor Kasur has a museum place record yet, so he carries no place edges.
