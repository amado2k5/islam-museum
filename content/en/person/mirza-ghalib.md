# Mirza Ghalib

Mirza Ghalib (1797–1869), born Mirza Asadullah Baig Khan, is regarded as the greatest Urdu poet of the late Mughal age, and wrote divans in both Urdu and Persian {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-en}. His pen names were Ghalib, and before it Asad {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-ar}. He was born on 27 December 1797 at Kala Mahal in Agra, into a family of Turkish military descent, and moved to Delhi as a boy after his father's death {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-en}{cite:wikidata-ghalib}.

Most of his life was spent in Delhi in straitened circumstances, pursuing a long dispute over the family pension that took him as far as Calcutta in 1828 {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-en}. The emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar conferred on him the titles Dabir-ul-Mulk and Najm-ud-Daula in 1850, appointed him his poet tutor in 1854 and later his royal historian {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-en}. He lived through the rebellion of 1857 and its aftermath in Delhi, and died there on 15 February 1869 {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-en}{cite:wikipedia-ghalib-ar}.

On his religious views, the English article states that Ghalib "followed Shia theology and had said many verses in praise of Ali ibn Abi Talib", citing Zahra Sabri's study of Shi'a poets in pre-modern Urdu poetry, while also stressing that he placed the seeking of God above ritual practice and questioned some clerical readings {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-en}. The Urdu article files him in its categories among Indian Sunni Muslims, a filing the museum records in his entry's note without treating it as an attestation {cite:wikipedia-ghalib-ur}.
