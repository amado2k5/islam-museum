## Summary
Abu Bakr Ayyub b. Abi Tamima al-Sakhtiyani was a jurist and hadith scholar of [[place/basra|Basra]] and one of the teachers of [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]]. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 597} Al-Shirazi records that he died in 131 AH. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p.47}

## Name and origin
His father Abu Tamima's name was Kaysan, and he was among the captives taken at [[place/kabul|Kabul]]. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 597} He was a client of 'Anaza — or, it is said, of 'Ammar b. Shaddad, client of al-Mughira — and the family later attached itself to Banu Tuhayya. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 597} He sold leather in Basra, which is why he was called al-Sakhtiyani ("the leather-seller"). {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 597}

## His standing among his contemporaries
[[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Ibn 'Abd al-Barr]] calls him one of the imams of the community in hadith, trustworthiness and uprightness, and one of the devout, memorising and best of the scholars. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 597} He gathers his contemporaries' words:
- [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]]: "I never saw the like of these: Ayyub, Yunus and Ibn 'Awn"; and once, relating a hadith: "the master of the jurists, Ayyub, told it to us". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 597} {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 599}
- [[person/hisham-ibn-urwa|Hisham b. 'Urwa]]: no one from Iraq came to us better than Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani and Mis'ar. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 598}
- [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|Al-Hasan al-Basri]]: Ayyub is the master of the young men of Basra. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 598} {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p.47}
- [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Muhammad b. Sirin]]: when Ayyub told him a hadith he would say, "the truthful one told me". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 598}
- [[person/abu-awn-abd-allah-ibn-awn|Ibn 'Awn]]: after al-Hasan and Muhammad there was no one in Basra like Ayyub; he knew hadith best of us. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 599}
- [[person/nafi-mawla-ibn-umar|Nafi']]: the best Easterner I have seen is Ayyub. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 599}
- [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|'Ali b. al-Madini]]: four men of the great cities in whom the heart finds rest in hadith — Yahya b. Sa'id in Medina, 'Amr b. Dinar in Mecca, Ayyub in Basra and Mansur in Kufa. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 599}

## Malik and Ayyub
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr relates that Malik was asked when he heard from Ayyub; he said Ayyub made two pilgrimages during which Malik watched him without hearing from him, except that whenever the Prophet was mentioned Ayyub wept until Malik felt pity for him; when he saw his reverence for the Prophet, he wrote from him. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 599} Malik also said: "I have not seen among ordinary people anyone better than Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani." {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 599} Abu Usama reports Malik and Shu'ba saying: "We have told you from no one but that Ayyub was better than him." {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 598}

In Yahya's recension of the Muwatta' Malik has two musnad reports from him, the first being the hadith of Dhu al-Yadayn on forgetfulness in prayer, from [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Ibn Sirin]] from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]; the other recensions have two more from him on the pilgrimage. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 600-601}

## Death
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says he died in 131 AH on the road from Mecca back to Basra, in the great plague (al-ta'un al-jarif), adding that he knew of no disagreement about it, and that he was sixty-three. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 600} The Tamhid's editor quotes Ibn Sa'd in a note as saying that all agreed he died in the plague at Basra in 131 AH. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 600} Al-Shirazi gives the same year. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981 p.47}
