# Shamsi Asadullayev

## Summary
Shamsi Asadullayev (1840–1913) was an Azerbaijani oil baron and philanthropist, a farmer's son from Amirjan who rose through the Baku refineries to become one of the great exporters of Baku oil — the first to ship it out by barge — and a benefactor of schools, students and Moscow's Muslim community. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en} {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-az}

## Early life and career
He was born at Amirjan village, Baku, in 1840 — the Russian article notes that other data give 1841 — and helped his father farm until the oil boom bought up the village lands, pushing farmers into refinery work. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en} {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-ru} He started at the refinery of Vasily Kokorev and by 1860 was its deputy director; he then became a subcontractor to oil and salt producers, and in 1875 opened his own kerosene plant. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en} In 1890 he co-founded an oil company, and in 1891 he bought three barges to export oil from Baku to Russia across the Caspian — the first businessman to move Baku oil this way. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en} In the early 1890s he bought oil-rich lands outside Baku, where a gusher in 1895 flowed for fifty-six days; as exports up the Volga grew he opened refineries along the route in Moscow, Poland, Central Asia and Iran, and the Azerbaijani article records that a firm capitalised at 500 manats in 1893 was worth ten million by 1913. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en} {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-az} He also contributed to building up an Azerbaijani navy and funded mansions in central Baku. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en}

## Patronage
Asadullayev paid for the building of the Baku Real School (now the Azerbaijan State University of Economics), endowed two scholarships in his own name at the Alexander Teachers' Institute in Tiflis — the largest in the Caucasus — and paid for the studies of dozens of young Azerbaijanis in Germany, France, Warsaw, Kazan, Kiev, Moscow, Odessa, St Petersburg and Kharkov, among them the architect Ziver bey Ahmedbekov, St Petersburg's first Azerbaijani civil-engineering graduate in 1901. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en} He presented one of his Moscow mansions, in Zamoskvorechye, to the Moscow Muslim community: the Asadullayev House served as a national cultural centre for Moscow's Muslims until 1941 and was returned to the Tatar national-cultural autonomy of Moscow in 2003. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-en} {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-ru}

## Death
He died at Yalta on 21 April 1913; the Azerbaijani article says the cause was sunstroke and his age seventy-two. {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-az} {cite:wikipedia-shamsi-asadullayev-ru} {cite:wikidata-shamsi-asadullayev}
