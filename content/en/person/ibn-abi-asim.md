## Overview
Ahmad ibn 'Amr ibn Abi 'Asim al-Dahhak ibn Mukhallad al-Shaybani, Abu Bakr, called Ibn al-Nabil, was a scholar of hadith, an ascetic and a traveller, of Basra. He held the judgeship of Isfahan in 269-282 AH and died in 287 AH (900 CE). {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.189}

He wrote some three hundred works, among them al-Musnad al-Kabir of about fifty thousand hadiths, al-Ahad wa-l-Mathani of about twenty thousand, Kitab al-Sunna, al-Diyat and al-Awa'il. It is said his books were lost at Basra in the Zanj revolt, so he dictated fifty thousand hadiths again from memory. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.189}

## In Ibn Asakir's Tarikh Dimashq
[[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]] describes Ahmad ibn Amr ibn al-Dahhak ibn Makhlad, Abu Bakr al-Shaybani, as the jurist and judge, a hadith scholar son of a hadith scholar son of a hadith scholar, of Basran origin, who lived in [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]] and became its judge, a prolific author in hadith who travelled from there to [[place/damascus|Damascus]] and elsewhere. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 104} In Damascus he heard Hisham ibn Ammar, Duhaym and others; he narrated from his maternal grandfather Abu Salama al-Tabudhaki, Abu al-Walid al-Tayalisi, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba and others; and [[person/abu-al-shaykh-al-isfahani|Abu al-Shaykh]], the judge Abu Ahmad al-Assal and others narrated from him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 104} Ibn Abi Hatim said: I heard from him, and he was truthful. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 105}

**The ascetic judge.** Ibn Asakir quotes [[person/ibn-khafif|Ibn Khafif]] that Ibn Abi Asim kept company for a time with Abu Turab al-Nakhshabi, and that once he became judge, when asked a question about Sufism he would say: judgeship, worldly life and talk of the Sufis' sciences cannot go together. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 105} He relates that a Daylamite ruler, told that he was a *nasibi*, sent a man to bring him his head; Ibn Abi Asim lay back, put his book over his face and said "do as you wish"; then a second messenger came with contrary orders, and he rose and resumed the hadith he had been reading. The editor glosses *nasibi* as one hostile to the Prophet's family. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 105} He is quoted as saying: I do not want in my session any innovator, slanderer, curser, obscene or foul-mouthed person, or anyone who has turned away from al-Shafi'i or from the people of hadith. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 106}

**School and death.** Abu Nu'aym said he was a jurist of the Zahiri school, served as judge of Isfahan for thirteen years after the death of Salih ibn Ahmad, died in 287 AH, had his funeral prayer led by his son al-Hakam, and was buried in the Dushabadh cemetery; Abu al-Shaykh said he died in Isfahan in Rabi' II 287. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 106} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 107}
