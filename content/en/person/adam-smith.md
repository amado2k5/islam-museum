## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Adam Smith as one of the West's founders of modern management, through his book The Wealth of Nations (1776) and the pin-factory story from which he drew the principle of [[concept/taqsim-al-amal|division of labour]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:20:30}.

## The pin-factory story
Al-Suwaidan recounts that Smith sat in a pin factory watching the workers, and saw each worker performing every stage of production: melting the iron, forming it into wires, cutting them, adding the head and sharpening the point {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:21:30}.
He then reorganised the work so that each worker performed a single step, adding workers wherever delay appeared, and the factory's output rose tenfold with the same workers, machines and materials — only the management had changed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:22:30}.
Al-Suwaidan counts this story as the beginning of the Western industrial revival and of modern management and economics together {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:21:00}.
