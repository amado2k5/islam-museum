## Overview
Dawud ibn Sulayman al-Baghdadi al-Naqshbandi al-Khalidi al-Shafi'i, known as Ibn Jirjis, was a jurist-littérateur of Baghdad, where he was born and died in 1299 AH (1882 CE). He journeyed to the Hijaz and Syria and lived in Mecca for some ten years. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.332}

He composed small books, among them Ashadd al-Jihad fi Ibtal Da'wa al-Ijtihad against the Najdi Hanbalis for the claim of ijtihad attributed to them, which Abd al-Latif ibn Abd al-Rahman answered with Minhaj al-Ta'sis wa-l-Taqdis fi Kashf Shubuhat Dawud Ibn Jirjis. He also wrote Sulh al-Ikhwan min Ahl al-Iman defending Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim; Musalli al-Wajid, an expansion of an elegy on Shaykh Khalid al-Naqshbandi; Rawd al-Safa on the merits of al-Mustafa's father; a versification of the Burda; Dawhat al-Tawhid on kalam; and al-Minha al-Wahbiyya. Al-'Azzawi said he became famous for his rejoinder to Abu al-Thana' al-Alusi and sold well for a time, but his works could not carry the day. {cite:al-aalam v.2 p.332}
