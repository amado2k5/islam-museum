Safvet-beg Bašagić (6 May 1870 – 9 April 1934), who wrote poetry as Mirza Safvet, was a Bosnian historian, poet and politician from Nevesinje, described as the father of the Bosnian Renaissance.{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-en} Educated at Mostar, Konjic and Sarajevo, he took a doctorate at the University of Vienna in Arabic and Persian (dissertation defended 1910) and taught Oriental languages; on 1 May 1900 he launched the journal Behar together with Edhem Mulabdić and Osman Nuri Hadžić, in 1903 he founded the cultural society Gajret, and in 1910 he was elected to the Bosnian Diet and, on Ali-beg Firdus's death, became its president.{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-bs}{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-en} After the First World War he served as curator of the National Museum in Sarajevo from 1919 to 1927.{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-bs}

His scholarship mapped Bosnia's Islamic past for the first time: Kratka uputa u prošlost Bosne i Hercegovine (A Short Guide to the Past of Bosnia and Herzegovina, 1900) long remained the standard study, and Bošnjaci i Hercegovci u islamskoj književnosti (Bosnians and Herzegovinians in Islamic Literature, 1912) began a lifetime's compilation of some 700 biographies.{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-en}{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-bs} He also collected a great private library of Arabic, Persian and Turkish manuscripts and old books — acquired after his death by the University Library in Bratislava and inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register in 1997.{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-en} He died at Sarajevo on 9 April 1934 and was buried in the cemetery of the Gazi Husrev-beg Mosque.{cite:wikipedia-safvet-beg-basagic-bs}{cite:wikidata-safvet-beg-basagic}
