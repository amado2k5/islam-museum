## Summary
Naledi Pandor is a South African politician, a veteran of the African National Congress (ANC) struggle against apartheid, and a multi-portfolio minister across two decades — most prominently Minister of International Relations and Cooperation (2019–2024), through which she became the diplomatic face of South Africa's historic genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice. {cite:wikipedia-naledi-pandor}

## Early life and education
She was born in 1953 in Durban into a family active against apartheid — her grandfather was the Congress leader Z. K. Matthews — and grew up between South Africa and exile in Swaziland, earning degrees in languages, literature, history and politics at the universities of Botswana, Swaziland and South Africa, and working as a teacher and academic before entering parliamentary politics after the first democratic elections of 1994. {cite:wikipedia-naledi-pandor}

## Ministerial career
She entered government as deputy minister then minister in successive portfolios: Education (2004–2009), Science and Technology (2009–2012 and 2014–2018) where she championed the SKA telescope project, Home Affairs (2012–2014), and Higher Education (2018–2019). In 2019 President Cyril Ramaphosa appointed her Minister of International Relations and Cooperation, leading South African diplomacy until 2024. {cite:wikipedia-naledi-pandor} {cite:muslim500-naledi-pandor}

## The ICJ case
In December 2023 South Africa, led by her ministry, filed its case against Israel at the International Court of Justice alleging genocide in Gaza, and obtained provisional-measures orders in January and May 2024 obliging Israel to prevent genocidal acts — a step her ministry described as a legal and humanitarian duty, criticised by Israel, Washington and other allies; the museum reports these positions attributed, without verdict. Pandor thereby became the most prominent Muslim diplomat to carry the Palestinian file from the platform of a major regional state. {cite:wikipedia-naledi-pandor}

## Conversion to Islam
Biographical reporting records that she embraced Islam, influenced during her years of exile and life in Botswana, and she later became one of the highest-ranking Muslim figures in South African politics. {cite:wikipedia-naledi-pandor}

## Legacy
Her career reads as a bridge between the anti-apartheid generation and post-apartheid diplomacy: a literature academic who became the longest-serving woman in South Africa's cabinet, spending the height of her international fame in a Hague courtroom defending her country's position on Palestine. {cite:wikipedia-naledi-pandor}
