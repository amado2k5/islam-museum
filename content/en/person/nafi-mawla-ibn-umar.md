## Overview
Abu Abd Allah Nafi', the freedman (mawla) of [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Abdallah ibn Umar]]. He was of Daylamite origin, taken captive by his master on one of his campaigns, and became one of the greatest pious Successors; he heard Ibn Umar and Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, and al-Zuhri, Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani and Malik ibn Anas transmitted from him. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## His place in hadith
Ibn Khallikan counts him among the most famous hadith transmitters and among the reliable authorities whose reports are transmitted, accepted as proof and acted upon; most of Ibn Umar's hadith runs through him. Malik said: when I heard Nafi's report from Ibn Umar, I did not mind not hearing it from anyone else. Scholars of hadith call the chain al-Shafi'i ← Malik ← Nafi' ← Ibn Umar the "golden chain" for the eminence of each of its transmitters. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## The shepherd's pipe story
Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi relates in al-Muhadhdhab on Nafi's authority that while walking with Ibn Umar he passed a shepherd's pipe; Ibn Umar put his fingers in his ears and turned from the road, asking: Nafi', do you still hear it? until Nafi' said no; then he removed his fingers and returned to the road, saying: this is what I saw the Messenger of God do. Ibn Khallikan notes that the jurists discussed a difficulty in this report and answered that Nafi' was then still a boy. {cite:wafayat-ayan}
