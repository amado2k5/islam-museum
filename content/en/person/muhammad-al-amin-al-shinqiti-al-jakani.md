## Summary
Muhammad al-Amin ibn Muhammad al-Mukhtar ibn Abd al-Qadir al-Jakani al-Shinqiti, later of Medina, was an exegete and legal theorist, author of [[work/adwa-al-bayan|Adwa' al-Bayan]]. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.7} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.19} He is not to be confused with his namesake [[person/muhammad-al-amin-al-shinqiti|Muhammad al-Amin ibn Ahmad al-Shinqiti al-Alawi]].

## Lineage, birth and upbringing
His descent goes back to Jakan al-Abarr, ancestor of the Jakani tribe, which traces itself to Himyar. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.19} He was born in 1325 AH at a water called Tanba in the district of Kiffa in Mauritania and grew up an orphan: his father died while he was a small boy still reading the last part of the Qur'an, and he was raised in his maternal uncles' household, his mother being his father's cousin. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.19} He memorised the Qur'an at ten with his uncle Abd Allah ibn Muhammad al-Mukhtar, then learned the Uthmanic script and the recitation of Nafi' in the transmissions of Warsh and Qalun, studied short Maliki legal texts, and learned literature, grammar, Arab genealogy and the Prophet's life from his uncle's wife. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.20} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.21} He tells how his mother equipped him for study with two camels, one for his mount and books, the other for his provisions. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.21}

## His learning
His biographer Khalid ibn Uthman al-Sabt says he knew by heart thousands of verses of Arabic poetry and grammatical examples, most of the hadith of the two Sahihs, and Ibn Malik's Alfiyya, Maraqi al-Su'ud and al-Iraqi's Alfiyya, and quotes him: "There is no verse in the Qur'an I have not studied on its own." {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.23} He says the shaykh constantly set out the Sunni creed on the names and attributes, with a firm knowledge of the theologians' schools. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.24} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.25}

## At home and in the Hijaz
At home he taught, gave fatwas and judged, and was one of the two members of the 'committee on blood' to which retaliation sentences were referred. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.26} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.27} He left for the hajj on 7 Jumada II 1367 AH and settled in [[place/medina|Medina]], saying: "There is no work greater than explaining the Book of God in the mosque of the Messenger of God." {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.27} He explained the whole Qur'an in the Prophet's Mosque, taught at Dar al-Ulum in Medina, then in the Riyadh institutes and the colleges of Shari'a and Arabic from 1371 AH, then at the Islamic University of Medina from 1381 AH until his death, and in 1385 AH led a university mission to ten African countries. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.28} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.29} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.30}

## His austerity
He is reported to have kept only enough money for a month, giving the rest to poor students and widows among his kin, and not to have sold his books. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.31} His biographer quotes Muhammad al-Uthaymin: seeing him for the first time at the Riyadh institute, the students took him for a Bedouin from his shabby clothes, until his lesson began and they knew they were before a great scholar. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.32}

## Works
They include [[work/adwa-al-bayan|Adwa' al-Bayan]]; Daf' Iham al-Idtirab 'an Ayat al-Kitab, written in fifteen nights in 1373 AH; Man' Jawaz al-Majaz fi al-Munazzal li'l-Ta'abbud wa'l-I'jaz; Mudhakkirat Usul al-Fiqh on Rawdat al-Nazir; Adab al-Bahth wa'l-Munazara; a commentary on Maraqi al-Su'ud dictated to his student Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Amin; Rihlat al-Hajj ila Bayt Allah al-Haram; and lectures, among them Manhaj wa-Dirasat li-Ayat al-Asma' wa'l-Sifat. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.34} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.35} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.36} {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.37} His tafsir lessons were collected as al-Adhb al-Namir. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.28}

## Death
He died on the morning of Thursday 17 Dhu al-Hijja 1393 AH at his home in [[place/mecca|Mecca]]; [[person/ibn-baz-abd-al-aziz|Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz]] led the funeral prayer, and he was buried in the al-Ma'la cemetery. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.39}
