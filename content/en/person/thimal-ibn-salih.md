## Overview
Thimal ibn Salih ibn Mirdas al-Kilabi, Abu Alwan, called Mu'izz al-Dawla, was one of the kings of the Mirdasid dynasty in Aleppo; al-Zirikli describes him as generous, forbearing and brave, and gives his death as 454 AH, 1062 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 99}.

## His life
He took the rule in 434 AH, when the state in Egypt belonged to the Fatimids, who sent three armies against him; Thimal fought them and drove them back {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 99}. He then wrote to the Fatimid al-Mustansir bi-Allah, sent him costly gifts, gave up Aleppo to him and handed it to Makin al-Dawla al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Mulhim, and went to Egypt in 449 AH {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 99}. In 452 AH Mahmud ibn Nasr ibn Mirdas rose against Makin al-Dawla and took Aleppo; the Fatimids returned to Mu'izz al-Dawla to negotiate the recovery of Aleppo from his cousin Mahmud ibn Nasr, and he marched with an army from Egypt and took it a second time in 453 AH, after which his rule there was settled; he then raided the Byzantines and was victorious {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 99}.

## His death
He died in Aleppo in 454 AH, 1062 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 2 p. 99}.
