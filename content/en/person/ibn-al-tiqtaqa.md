## In brief
Safi al-Din Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Tabataba, known as Ibn al-Tiqtaqa, an Iraqi historian of Mosul whose line runs back to al-Hasan ibn Ali through Ibrahim al-Tabataba; he was born, it seems, shortly after the Mongol conquest of Baghdad in 656/1258, which he does not mention witnessing himself {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 988}.

## A house of naqibs
His father Taj al-Din Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ramadan, chief naqib of the Alids, had gained great wealth and influence, but lost his life and property in a game of political intrigue against the brothers Ala' al-Din and Shams al-Din al-Juwayni; Ibn al-Tiqtaqa too was a naqib of the Alids, though probably with more regionally limited authority {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 988}.

## Al-Fakhri
He appears to have travelled widely in Iraq and Adharbaydjan. During a stay in Mosul on the way to Tabriz, between Jumada II and Shawwal 701, he wrote his history al-Fakhri for Fakhr al-Din Isa ibn Ibrahim of Mosul: a brief mirror for princes followed by biographies of the caliphs down to al-Musta'sim, each with the biographies of that caliph's viziers. Its skilfully chosen anecdotes and reflective approach make it enjoyable and instructive to a degree uncommon in medieval scholarly historiography {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 988}.

## Death and afterlife of the book
He died in 709 AH as al-Dhahabi records {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi s.a. 601-610 p. 738}, while al-Zirikli gives only 1302 CE {cite:al-aalam j.3 p. 149}; Hindushah ibn Sanjar translated al-Fakhri into Persian in 723-4/1323-4 under the title Tajarib al-salaf {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 988}.
