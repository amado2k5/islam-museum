## Overview
Abd al-Hamid ibn Muhammad Shakir ibn Ibrahim al-Zahrawi was a leader of the political awakening in Syria and one of the Arab martyrs of the military court at Aley (Diwan Aliyah). He was born at Homs and was a man of religious learning and of politics. The printed text gives his birth as 1272 AH and his death as 1334 AH, that is 1855 and 1916 CE {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 286}.

## His life and works
He opposed the policy of the Hamidian era before the Ottoman constitution and issued a newspaper called 'al-Munir', which he printed on gelatine and distributed in secret. He travelled to Istanbul and helped found the Turkish newspaper 'Ma'lumat'; the Hamidian authorities exiled him to Damascus, where he wrote for the Egyptian newspaper 'al-Muqattam'. The governor of Damascus, Nazim Pasha, learned of it and sent him under guard to Istanbul. Abu al-Huda al-Sayyadi interceded for him and he was returned to Homs. He then fled to Egypt and worked in journalism until the Ottoman constitution was proclaimed in 1327 AH, 1908 CE as the printed text has it, when he returned to Syria. {cite:alam-zirikli-dar-al-ilm-2002 vol. 3 p. 286}.

## In politics
He was elected deputy for Hama and went to Istanbul. He took part in founding the 'Liberty and Moderation' party and the 'Entente' party, both opposed to the Unionists, and issued the weekly 'al-Hadara'. When the reform movement appeared in Syria and the first Arab Congress met in Paris, he was elected its president. The Unionists then won him over and persuaded him of their resolve to reform, and made him a member of the Ottoman Senate.

## His death
The First World War broke out; they arrested him and brought him before the military court at Aley, which sentenced him to death, and the sentence was carried out by hanging at Damascus. The entry names two printed works: the treatise 'al-Fiqh wa al-Tasawwuf' and the book 'Khadija Umm al-Mu'minin'
