## Overview
Al-Mufaddal ibn Umar ibn al-Mufaddal al-Samarqandi al-Abhari, one of the best-known scholars in Islamic thought in logic, astronomy and mathematics. His nisba "al-Abhari" refers to the town of Abhar, and the place of his birth is disputed. {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.187-201}

## Death
He died in 663 AH (1265 CE). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p.187-201}

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.) entry, signed by C. Brockelmann, gives Athir al-Din al-Abhari (d. 663/1264; Barhebraeus gives 1262) as a philosophical writer about whose life little is known. He wrote two works of scholastic philosophy that were widely studied: the Hidayat al-Hikma, in three parts on logic, physics and theology, and al-Isaghuji, an adaptation of Porphyry's Isagoge. The best-known commentary on the Hidayat is by Mir Husayn al-Maybudi. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 117}
