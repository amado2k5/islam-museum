## Summary
Yusuf Muhammad Ahmad Ziedan is an Egyptian writer, scholar and university professor, born on 30 June 1958 at Saqulta in Sohag Governorate in Upper Egypt and raised in Alexandria, where he still lives with his family. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} He specialises in the Arabic manuscript heritage and its sciences, and his research covers Islamic thought, Sufism and the history of Arabic medicine; he is a public lecturer and columnist, and the author of more than fifty books. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

At the Bibliotheca Alexandrina he founded the manuscripts department in 1994 and went on to direct the library's Manuscript Center and Museum, becoming one of the best-known cataloguers of Arabic manuscripts, with more than 18,000 manuscripts catalogued in thematic, analytical catalogues. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} As a novelist he is known above all for «عزازيل» (Azazeel), his second novel, which won the International Prize for Arabic Fiction in 2009 and was translated into many languages; his later novel «فردقان» (Fardeqan) was shortlisted for the same prize in 2020. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-azazeel-en}

## Early life and education
Ziedan was born on 30 June 1958 in the village of al-Awamiya at Saqulta, in Sohag Governorate in Upper Egypt. While he was still a small child he moved with his grandfather to Alexandria, where he was raised and educated, and he has remained in the city since. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

He joined the philosophy department of the Faculty of Arts at the University of Alexandria, taking his bachelor's degree in philosophy in 1980 and graduating summa cum laude. His postgraduate studies were in Islamic philosophy and Sufism: his master's thesis treated the Sufi thought of Abd al-Karim al-Jili, with a study and edition of al-Jili's poem al-Nadarat al-Ayniyya together with al-Nabulsi's commentary on it. In 1989 he took his doctorate with a dissertation on the Qadiri Sufi order — its thought, method and conduct — including a study and edition of the poetical works of Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani. In 1999 he received the full professorship in philosophy and the history of science. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

Sufism and the Qadiriyya are his academic field and the subject of his scholarship, not attestation of personal adherence. No source read for this article names a school or affiliation for Ziedan himself, and Wikidata carries no religion statement for him; the museum therefore records no faith for him. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikidata-youssef-ziedan}

## The Bibliotheca Alexandrina and the manuscript heritage
In 1994 Ziedan founded the manuscripts department of the Bibliotheca Alexandrina and headed it; he went on to direct the library's Manuscript Center and Museum, the post the library itself used when it announced his 2009 fiction prize. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} He worked as a consultant in Arabic heritage preservation and conservation for international institutions including UNESCO, ESCWA and the Arab League, and directed projects aimed at identifying and preserving the Arabic manuscript heritage. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

The Arabic article reports that he was later dismissed from his post at the library after a dispute with its then director, Ismail Serageldin; Ziedan spoke about the dismissal in a 2012 interview with Al-Ahram, and Serageldin answered in Al-Masry Al-Youm. The museum records the dispute as the two sides presented it at the time, without a verdict. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

## Scholarship
### Sufism
Ziedan's work on Sufism stresses not only its mystic, introspective side but its philosophical underpinnings. He has concentrated on Ibn Arabi and on Abd al-Karim al-Jili, two of the central figures of philosophical Sufism; commentators quoted in the English article describe his work on al-Jili as the most authoritative in the field. His books in this area include «المقدمة في التصوف» (A Prologue to Sufism), «عبد الكريم الجيلي فيلسوف الصوفية» (Abd al-Karim al-Jili, Philosopher of Sufism), «شرح مشكلات الفتوحات المكية» (An Explanation of the Difficult Passages of the Meccan Revelations, on Ibn Arabi), «المتواليات: دراسات في التصوف» (Al-Mutawaliyat: Studies in Sufism) and «الطرق الصوفية والقادرية في مصر» (Sufi Orders and al-Qadiriyya in Egypt), together with his editions of the poetry of Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani and of Afif al-Din al-Tilimsani. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

### Islamic philosophy
The most distinctive feature of Ziedan's work on Islamic philosophy, as the English article presents it, is his attempt to uncover a strand of Islamic philosophical thought that developed on its own terms rather than under Hellenistic influence — the "illuminationist" line of the Islamic East. His central exhibit is the parable of Hayy Ibn Yaqzan, which he re-edited complete in «حي بن يقظان: النصوص الأربعة ومبدعوها» (Hayy Ibn Yaqzan: the Four Texts and their Authors), gathering the versions of Avicenna, Ibn Tufayl and al-Suhrawardi and adding a fourth, less famous version, Ibn al-Nafis's Fadil Ibn Natiq. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

His work as a public intellectual appears in «اللاهوت العربي وأصول العنف الديني» (Arabic Theology and the Roots of Religious Violence), which examines the dynamics behind the key ideas that shaped Judaism, Christianity and Islam, and their links to one another and to the geography of the region. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

### History of Arabic medicine
A third dimension of his scholarship is the history of Arabic science, and of medicine in particular. He has studied the Arabic translations of, and commentaries on, Hippocrates and Galen, and is regarded as a leading specialist on Ala al-Din Ibn al-Nafis. His critical edition of Ibn al-Nafis's great medical encyclopedia «الشامل في الصناعة الطبية» (al-Shamil fil Sina'a al-Tibbiyya) ran to thirty volumes, published by the Cultural Foundation in Abu Dhabi; the Arabic article reports that its publication brought him the Kuwait Foundation for the Advancement of Sciences prize for the second time, after an earlier award for his Ibn al-Nafis studies. His other medical-history works include a commentary on the Hippocratic Aphorisms, Ibn al-Nafis's Treatise on the Body Parts and his al-Mujaz fil Tibb, a treatise on gout by al-Razi, and «إعادة اكتشاف ابن النفيس» (Rediscovering Ibn al-Nafis). {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

### Cataloguing the manuscripts
Ziedan treats cataloguing as an ars maior — a major art — that has not received the attention it deserves, and as the key to a panoramic view of a manuscript heritage. The Arabic article credits him with cataloguing more than 18,000 Arabic manuscripts. His catalogues, some twenty in the English article's count, are mostly thematic rather than general: they describe each manuscript in detail, giving its subject, its state and the opening and closing words of its text, instead of short bibliographic records. They cover, among others, the manuscript collections of Alexandria University, the Alexandria Municipality library, the Rifaa al-Tahtawi library, the Abu al-Abbas al-Mursi mosque library, the religious institute of Smouha and the Escorial monastery in Spain. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

## Fiction
Ziedan's first novel, «ظل الأفعى» (Zil al-Af'a, "Shadow of the Serpent", 2006), treats the notion of the sacred female: its first part is set in the present day, and its second part gives letter fragments from a female anthropologist to her daughter, the heroine of the first part, explaining how the role of the female was reshaped and abused through history. The English article calls the novel critically acclaimed, and notes that it has also been criticised for its unusual structure and its intellectualism. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

His second novel made him famous across the Arab world, and it is treated in its own section below. After it came «النبطي» (The Nabatean, 2012), set at the coming of Islam to Egypt; «محال» (Places, 2013) and its sequel «جونتنامو» (Guantanamo, 2013); and «نور» (Nur). «فردقان: اعتقال الشيخ الرئيس» (Fardeqan: the Detention of the Great Sheikh, 2018), a novel about Ibn Sina published by Dar El Shorouk, was shortlisted for the 2020 International Prize for Arabic Fiction. His later novels include «حاكم: جنون ابن الهيثم» (Hakim: the Madness of Ibn al-Haytham, 2021), «الورّاق: أمالي العلاء» (Al-Warraq, 2023) and «الحصيد» (Al-Hasid, 2025). His novels have been translated into English, French, Italian and Russian, among other languages. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

## Azazeel
«عزازيل» (Azazeel) was published by Dar Al-Shorouk in 2008. It is presented as the translation of thirty scrolls written in Aramaic by an Egyptian monk named Hypa in the first half of the fifth century AD and found, in the fiction, in a wooden box in the ruins around the monastery of Saint Simeon Stylites near Aleppo. Hypa, born in Aswan in 391, tells how he witnessed Christians kill his pagan father, studied medicine at Akhmim, and came to Alexandria at about twenty-three to become a monk. There he is caught up in the city of Bishop Cyril: he lodges with the pagan Octavia, attends a lecture by the philosopher Hypatia, and in 415 watches a mob kill Hypatia and Octavia. He crosses Sinai to Jerusalem, befriends the priest Nestorius, and settles in a monastery near Aleppo, where his medical knowledge makes his name. In 430 Nestorius, by then bishop of Constantinople, summons him to Antioch as his dispute with Cyril over the nature of Christ breaks open; Hypa counsels caution, Nestorius is deposed at Ephesus, and back at the monastery Hypa falls in love with Martha, a singer he is set to teach. Throughout, a voice he calls Azazeel — who tells him he is only the name men give their darker impulses — urges him to write his life down; the scrolls are that writing. {cite:wikipedia-azazeel-en}

On 16 March 2009 the novel won the second International Prize for Arabic Fiction, chosen from a shortlist of six among 120 novels; the Bibliotheca Alexandrina announced the win the same week, and the prize carried a guarantee that the book would be translated into English. The Arabic article counts sixteen consecutive printings by Dar El Shorouk by early 2010. Jonathan Wright's English translation appeared with Atlantic Books in 2012; it won the Anobii First Book Award at the Edinburgh International Book Festival in 2012, chosen by readers' vote, and shared the 2013 Saif Ghobash Banipal Prize for Arabic Literary Translation with William M. Hutchins. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-azazeel-en}

## Reception and controversy
This section sets out different positions as reported in the sources. It is not a verdict by the museum.

**The novel's defenders and its prize.** Azazeel's supporters point to its literary standing: the International Prize for Arabic Fiction, the speed of its reprinting, and its translation into many languages, which the Arabic article describes as most of the world's languages. Its English translator's two prizes are part of the same record. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-azazeel-en}

**The Church's objection.** The novel deals with the ancient Christian theological disputes over the nature of Christ and the standing of the Virgin, and with violence by Christians against Egypt's pagans as Christianity became the majority religion. The Coptic Orthodox Church accused the novel of defaming Christianity and inciting sectarian strife between Muslims and Christians in Egypt; the affair has been studied academically, among others by Saba Mahmood in "Azazeel and the Politics of Historical Fiction in Egypt". {cite:wikipedia-azazeel-en}

**Plagiarism accusations.** In 2017 the writer Ala Awda accused Ziedan of taking Azazeel's details, structure and dialogue from Charles Kingsley's novel Hypatia, and of drawing ideas from Umberto Eco's The Name of the Rose; the Tunisian novelist Kamal al-Ayadi made similar claims. The Arabic article reports the accusations; the museum records them as accusations, not as findings. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

**Later public debates.** Ziedan's later media remarks repeatedly drew wide public argument, which the Arabic article collects. In March 2017 he questioned the Mi'raj, saying that in his view the Qur'an fixes only the Isra' and that storytellers of the first centuries spread the ascent narrative; religious scholars answered that the Mi'raj is established in the Qur'an and the Sunna and accused him of shaking settled doctrine. In December 2017 he argued that the al-Aqsa Mosque named in the Qur'an is not the mosque in Jerusalem; the Israeli embassy in Cairo publicly welcomed the remarks, while Egyptian cultural and parliamentary figures, among them the MP Mustafa Bakri and the Al-Azhar scholar Ahmad Karima, attacked them sharply. On Amr Adib's programme Koll Youm he described Saladin as, in his words, "among the most despicable figures of history", and historians and commentators objected that the judgement ignored Saladin's historical context. The Egyptian Writers Union referred him for investigation after he expressed readiness to lecture at Tel Aviv University, which the union's rules forbid. In June 2022 he described the story of the People of the Elephant as Isra'iliyyat; Al-Azhar scholars answered that the event is established in the Qur'an and the Sunna. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

## Public role and the Takween Foundation
Alongside his books Ziedan is a public lecturer and a columnist in Egyptian and Arab newspapers, and his television appearances have made his readings of history and heritage familiar to a wide audience. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

On 4 May 2024 the Takween Foundation for Arab Thought ⁦(مؤسسة تكوين الفكر العربي)⁩ was launched in Cairo, with Ziedan on its board of trustees alongside the journalist Ibrahim Issa, the researcher Islam Behery, the Syrian scholar Firas al-Sawwah, the Tunisian thinker Olfa Youssef and the Lebanese scholar Nadira Abi Nader. The foundation describes itself, on its website, as working to develop the discourse of tolerance, open horizons of dialogue, encourage critical review, raise questions about intellectual givens, and re-examine the gaps that prevented the success of the renaissance project begun two centuries ago. Its first annual conference, held on the launch day, was titled "Fifty years after the death of Taha Hussein: where are we on renewal today?" {cite:sawalif-takween-2024}

The launch met sharp criticism from the first day: preachers and scholars quoted in the coverage accused the foundation of seeking to dismantle religious constants and spread doubt, and voices called for its closure, while others — the novelist Nader El-Sharkawy among them — urged that it be judged by its published output rather than by its founders' names. {cite:sawalif-takween-2024}

Seven weeks later, on 23 June 2024, Ziedan announced on Facebook that he was resigning from the foundation's board of trustees, writing that he would devote all his time to writing, "for it alone endures". Al Jazeera's report noted his earlier public opposition to a proposed debate between Islam Behery and the Azhari preacher Abdullah Rushdy, and press speculation about disputes among the founders; the foundation thanked him and said it respected his decision in light of the pressures and attacks directed at him and at the foundation. {cite:aljazeera-takween-resignation-2024}

## Recognition
- **International Prize for Arabic Fiction (2009).** Azazeel won the prize's second edition, announced on 16 March 2009, from a shortlist of six novels out of 120; the award guaranteed the novel's translation into English. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}
- **Anobii First Book Award (2012).** Jonathan Wright's English translation of Azazeel won the Edinburgh International Book Festival's prize for a first book translated into English, by readers' vote. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}
- **Banipal Prize (2013).** The same translation shared the Saif Ghobash Banipal Prize for Arabic Literary Translation with William M. Hutchins's translation of Wajdi al-Ahdal's A Land Without Jasmine — the first time the judges split the prize between two winners. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}
- **Kuwait Foundation for the Advancement of Sciences.** The Arabic article reports that he won the foundation's prize twice for his work on Ibn al-Nafis, the second time on the publication of his thirty-volume edition of al-Shamil; the English article's honours list names a Medical Knowledge and Heritage Authentication Award from the Islamic Organization of Medicine and the Kuwait Foundation. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}
- **Abdul-Hamid Shuman Prize.** The English article lists the Jordanian Shuman Prize in the field of social sciences among his honours. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}
- **International Prize for Arabic Fiction shortlist (2020).** Fardeqan was shortlisted for the prize eleven years after Azazeel won it. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

## Books
Ziedan has written more than fifty books. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} A selection, by field:

**Sufism and philosophy:** «المقدمة في التصوف» (A Prologue to Sufism); «عبد الكريم الجيلي فيلسوف الصوفية» (Abd al-Karim al-Jili, Philosopher of Sufism); «الفكر الصوفي عند عبد الكريم الجيلي» (The Sufi Thought of Abd al-Karim al-Jili), his master's work; the edition of al-Jili's al-Nadarat al-Ayniyya with al-Nabulsi's commentary; «الطريق الصوفي وفروع القادرية بمصر» (The Sufi Path and the Branches of the Qadiriyya in Egypt); his study and edition of the Diwan of Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani, his doctorate; «شعراء الصوفية المجهولون» (The Unknown Sufi Poets); «شرح مشكلات الفتوحات المكية» (on Ibn Arabi); «المتواليات» (Al-Mutawaliyat); «حي بن يقظان: النصوص الأربعة ومبدعوها» (Hayy Ibn Yaqzan: the Four Texts and their Authors); «اللاهوت العربي وأصول العنف الديني» (Arabic Theology and the Roots of Religious Violence). {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

**History of Arabic medicine:** his edition of «الشامل في الصناعة الطبية» (al-Shamil) by Ibn al-Nafis, in thirty volumes; «شرح فصول أبقراط» (Commentary on the Hippocratic Aphorisms); «رسالة الأعضاء» (Treatise on the Body Parts) and «المختار من الأغذية» (Selections on Foods) and «الموجز في الطب» (al-Mujaz fil Tibb) by Ibn al-Nafis; «مقالة في النقرس» (Treatise on Gout) by al-Razi; «إعادة اكتشاف ابن النفيس» (Rediscovering Ibn al-Nafis). {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

**Manuscript catalogues:** catalogues of the manuscripts of Alexandria University (two parts), the Alexandria Municipality library (several parts, including the Sufism volume), the Rifaa al-Tahtawi library (three parts), the Abu al-Abbas al-Mursi library, the religious institute of Smouha, Rashid and Damanhur, Shibin al-Kom, and the Escorial library in Spain; and «التراث المجهول: إطلالة على عالم المخطوطات» (The Unknown Heritage: a Look into the World of Manuscripts) and «بدائع المخطوطات القرآنية بالإسكندرية» (Masterpieces of Qur'anic Manuscripts in Alexandria). {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en}

**Essays and criticism:** «التقاء البحرين» (Iltiqa' al-Bahrayn: essays in literary criticism); «متاهات الوهم» (Labyrinths of Illusion); «دوامات التدين» (Whirlpools of Religiosity); «فقه الثورة» (The Jurisprudence of Revolution); «فقه الحب» (The Jurisprudence of Love) and its sequels «فقه العشق» and «فقه الهيام»; the three «شجون» collections (Egyptian, Arab and Heritage Sorrows); and the short-story collection «حل وترحال». {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}

**Novels:** «ظل الأفعى» (2006); «عزازيل» (2008); «النبطي» (2012); «محال» (2013); «جونتنامو» (2013); «نور»; «فردقان: اعتقال الشيخ الرئيس» (2018); «حاكم: جنون ابن الهيثم» (2021); «الورّاق: أمالي العلاء» (2023); «الحصيد» (2025); and «سفر العذارى», which the Arabic article lists among his novels. {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-en} {cite:wikipedia-youssef-ziedan-ar}
