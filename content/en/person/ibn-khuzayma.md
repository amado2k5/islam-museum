## Overview
Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Ishaq ibn Khuzayma ibn al-Mughira al-Sulami of Nishapur, whom the source calls "the imam of the imams" and the hadith master of Nishapur, compiled the *Sahih* known by his name. He was born in 223 AH (838 CE) and died in 311 AH (924 CE). {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba}

## His journey to Egypt
Ibn al-Jawzi tells a well-known story in *al-Muntazam*. Ibn Khuzayma was in Egypt with Muhammad ibn Nasr al-Marwazi, Muhammad ibn 'Ulwayh al-Wazzan and Muhammad ibn Harun al-Ruyani, hearing al-Shafi'i's books from al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman. Their provisions ran out and they went three days without food. They drew lots for who would go out and ask for help; the lot fell on Ibn Khuzayma, who asked for time to pray two rak'as and make the prayer of seeking guidance. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 172}

Meanwhile there was a knock at the door: a servant of Ahmad ibn Tulun, the governor of Egypt, brought each of them a purse of fifty dinars, with the governor's message to spend it and expect as much again when it was gone. Asked why, he said that Ibn Tulun, napping at midday, had seen someone in a dream ask him what his excuse would be for four men of learning who had gone three days hungry; he woke in alarm and sent to find them. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 172}

## His stance on al-Tabari
Ibn al-Athir relates that al-Husayn ibn 'Ali al-Razi was asked why he had not written from Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari, at a time when the Hanbalis kept people from visiting him, and Ibn Khuzayma told him: "I wish you had not written from everyone you wrote from, and had heard from Abu Ja'far instead." {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 100}

He is also reported to have said, after reading al-Tabari's commentary: "I know of no one on the face of the earth more learned than Abu Ja'far, and the Hanbalis have wronged him." {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 100}
