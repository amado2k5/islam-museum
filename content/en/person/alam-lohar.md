Muhammad Alam Lohar (1928 – 3 July 1979) was a Pakistani Punjabi folk singer, born at Achh village near Kotla Arab Ali Khan in Gujrat district into a family of Lohars (blacksmiths).{cite:wikipedia-alam-lohar-en}{cite:wikipedia-alam-lohar-ur} As a child he read Sufiana Kalaam, a collection of Punjabi stories and poetry, and began singing young; he recorded his first album at the age of thirteen.{cite:wikipedia-alam-lohar-en}

Lohar created and popularised Jugni, the narrative device that became his signature, and developed a new style of singing the Punjabi Vaar, accompanying himself on the chimta in a high-pitched voice; his renditions of Waris Shah's Heer and of Saif-ul-Malook made him famous across the Punjab, and songs such as Bol Mitti Diya Baawiya, Mirza Sahiban and Wajan Mariyan Bulaya followed.{cite:wikipedia-alam-lohar-en} From the 1950s until his death he dominated folk singing in Pakistan and toured Britain, Canada, Norway, the United States and Germany for South Asian audiences abroad.{cite:wikipedia-alam-lohar-en} He received the Pride of Performance in 1979, the year he died in a road accident near Shamki Bhattian on 3 July; he was buried at Lalamusa.{cite:wikipedia-alam-lohar-en}{cite:wikidata-alam-lohar} His son Arif Lohar continued his tradition.{cite:wikipedia-arif-lohar-en}
