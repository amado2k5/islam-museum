## Summary
Sitt al-Mulk, sister of the Fatimid caliph [[person/al-hakim-bi-amr-allah|al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah]] and daughter of al-ʿAziz Billah. The books of the people of Egypt, as Ibn al-Jawzi transmits them, relate that she arranged to conceal her brother's killing for three days, then brought his son Ali forward to the oath of allegiance under the title [[person/al-zahir-fatimid|al-Zahir li-Iʿzaz Din Allah]]; her hand was the higher one in the transfer between the two caliphs. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.302-305}

## Her part in the killing of al-Hakim
The account says that al-Hakim feared his close companion [[person/ibn-dawwas|Ibn Dawwas]], so Sitt al-Mulk contrived a reconciliation between them. Then the body of her brother, who had been killed on [[place/al-muqattam|Mount al-Muqattam]], was brought to her, and she buried it in her assembly room. She circulated that he had been absent for seven days, and she paid members of the Rikabiyya guard who could give the impression that he was still there. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.303-304}

## Making the new caliph and removing witnesses
She brought out Ali ibn al-Hakim, who was given the oath of allegiance; she installed him and placed on him the crown of al-Muʿizz. She held the mourning ceremony for three days, then had Ibn Dawwas put to death at the hand of Nasim, the man of the curtain and the sword, and had put to death whoever among the servants knew too much. Her standing in the state grew great. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.304-305}

## In Kahhala's A'lam al-Nisa'
Kahhala describes her as a lady of influence, authority, statecraft and judgement, and relates from Ibn al-Athir, Ibn Iyas, Abu al-Fida' and *al-Nujum al-Zahira* that she used to warn her brother al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah — "beware lest the ruin of this house come at your hands" — and that he answered roughly and threatened her, until he accused her of misconduct and wanted to send midwives to examine her. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.170} She then met Sayf al-Dawla ibn Dawwas by night in disguise, told him she feared the Muslims would rise against al-Hakim for openly claiming divinity and that the dynasty would end, and they agreed to kill him and set up his son in his place; she gave two of Ibn Dawwas's slaves money and two knives. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.171} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.172} She concealed his disappearance for seven days while she arranged matters and took oaths from the troops, then dressed Ali son of al-Hakim in the crown of al-Mu'izz, and he received allegiance as al-Zahir li-I'zaz Din Allah. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.172} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.173} He adds that she afterwards had Ibn Dawwas, the two slaves and everyone who knew her secret killed — Ibn al-Sabi' says the vizier al-Khatir as well — and then ran her nephew's realm with great skill until her death in 415 AH. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.174} His note records that Ibn Iyas calls her "Sitt al-Nasr". {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.170}
