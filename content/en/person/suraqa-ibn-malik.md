## Summary
Suraqa ibn Malik ibn Ju'shum al-Mudliji, hero of the pursuit and safe-conduct scene on the hijra road {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 168-169}.

## The pursuit
A man of his people told him he had seen dark figures on the coast he thought were Muhammad and his companions; Suraqa put him off, then went out with his spear seeking the hundred-camel prize. He caught them up — in Zad al-Ma'ad's addition — as they climbed from Qudayd on the third day of their journey {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 168-169} {cite:zad-al-maad}.

His mare stumbled under him, then her forelegs sank into the ground to the knees; he drew lots with the arrows and out came the one he hated — so he called out to them offering safety and they stopped {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 168}. He told them his people had set blood-money on them, and offered them provision and goods — they took nothing and said: conceal us — and he asked for a writ of safety; the Prophet had [[person/amir-ibn-fuhayra|Amir ibn Fuhayra]] write it for him on a scrap of leather {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 169}.

## "Their guardian"
Suraqa went back and found the people in pursuit, and kept saying: I have thoroughly scouted for you — you are covered on this side. He had been the day's early hunter of them, and its late guardian {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 169} {cite:zad-al-maad}. The tradition mentions that his appearance in his own shape was what gave Quraysh their assurance before Badr — as a related report {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 168-169}.
