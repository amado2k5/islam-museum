## Summary
Vivy Yusof (born 11 December 1987) is a Malaysian entrepreneur, fashion figure and social-media personality who co-founded the online fashion platform FashionValet and the headscarf brand dUCk, and starred in the reality television series Love Vivy. Listed by the Muslim 500 under Celebrities and Sports, she became one of Malaysia's most-followed businesswomen — and, after FashionValet's financial troubles became public in 2024, one of its most debated. {cite:wikipedia-vivy-yusof-en} {cite:themuslim500-vivy-yusof}

## Blogging to business
Vivy began as a fashion blogger while studying law in the United Kingdom, where she met her husband and future business partner Fadza Anuar. Returning to Kuala Lumpur, the couple turned her online following into FashionValet in 2010, an e-commerce platform that aggregated hundreds of Southeast Asian fashion labels, and in 2014 they launched dUCk, a premium scarf and accessories brand aimed at the hijab-wearing market that became a Malaysian retail phenomenon with queue-forming product drops. {cite:wikipedia-vivy-yusof-en}

## FashionValet and dUCk
FashionValet grew from a blog-adjacent startup into one of Malaysia's best-known fashion tech companies, raising venture funding and expanding into physical retail, while dUCk opened standalone stores and expanded into cosmetics. Vivy herself — styling herself as the public face of both brands — accumulated millions of followers across Instagram, YouTube and her blog, and fronted the reality series Love Vivy and a stint on the Malaysian version of Dragons' Den. {cite:wikipedia-vivy-yusof-en}

## The FashionValet controversy
In 2024 disclosures about FashionValet's accumulated losses and the write-down of investments by government-linked funds Khazanah Nasional and Permodalan Nasional Berhad became a Malaysian public issue, with parliamentary and public scrutiny of how state investment vehicles had backed the company; Vivy and her husband apologised publicly and relinquished their positions in the company, while denying misuse of funds as investigations proceeded. The museum reports the episode and its competing characterisations attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-vivy-yusof-en}

## Context
Vivy's rise is entangled with two larger Malaysian stories: the growth of the global modest-fashion industry, in which Malaysia became a hub, and the pathway by which social-media influencers became institutional entrepreneurs. In that setting, the 2024 FashionValet disclosures raised public questions not only about one company but about how government-linked investment funds back startups — a debate in which the company's defence, parliamentarians' questions and ongoing investigations ran side by side. {cite:wikipedia-vivy-yusof-en}

Her name has remained linked in Malaysian media to a double story: on one side she is emblematic of a generation of influencers who built companies out of online followings; on the other, the FashionValet affair became a test case for the transparency of government-linked investment in startups — a debate that kept her at the centre of business news well beyond the fashion pages. {cite:wikipedia-vivy-yusof-en}
