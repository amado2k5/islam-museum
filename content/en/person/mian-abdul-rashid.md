Mian Abdul Rashid (29 June 1889 – 6 November 1981) was the first Chief Justice of Pakistan. He was born in Lahore to the Arain Mian family of Baghbanpura, educated at Central Model School and Forman Christian College in Lahore, and took his Tripos and master's degree at Christ's College, Cambridge, in 1912; he was called to the Bar at the Middle Temple in 1913.{cite:wikipedia-mian-abdul-rashid-en}

He practised first at Multan and then at Lahore, sat as an acting judge of the Lahore High Court in 1923, became a judge of that court in 1933, and in 1946 its Chief Justice, being knighted the same year.{cite:wikipedia-mian-abdul-rashid-en} On 15 August 1947, as the most senior Muslim judge in British India, he administered the oath of office to Muhammad Ali Jinnah as first Governor-General of Pakistan, and himself became the new state's first Chief Justice.{cite:wikipedia-mian-abdul-rashid-en} In October 1955 he succeeded Khalifa Shuja-ud-Din, who had died suddenly in office, as president of the Commission on Marriage and Family Laws.{cite:fazle-omer-muslim-family-laws-thesis} He died on 6 November 1981, at Islamabad according to Wikidata,{cite:wikidata-mian-abdul-rashid} and in 2005 the Government of Pakistan renamed Islamabad's Seventh Avenue in his honour.{cite:wikipedia-mian-abdul-rashid-en}
