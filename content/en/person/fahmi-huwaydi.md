## Summary
Fahmi Huwaydi is an Egyptian journalist counted among the holders of an intellectual journalistic project, combining daily politics with documented authorship on Islam, democracy and citizenship. {cite:wikipedia-fahmi-huwaydi}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Egypt in 1937, he entered journalism early, writing for al-Ahram, Kuwait’s al-Arabi and Qatar’s al-Sharq, becoming one of the Arab press’s longest-running weekly columnists. {cite:wikipedia-fahmi-huwaydi}

## Career and works
His books include Iran from Within on the early Iranian revolution, The Quran and the Sultan on religion and power, Citizens Not Dhimmis defending non-Muslim rights, and many essays on the Arab Spring and military rule. {cite:wikipedia-fahmi-huwaydi}

## Method and contribution
His discourse is described as civil moderate Islamism: defending equal citizenship and democracy from a Muslim, non-party position. He is respected across many currents, while various positions over the decades draw criticism; the museum attributes these descriptions to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-fahmi-huwaydi}
