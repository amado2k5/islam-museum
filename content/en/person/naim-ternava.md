## Summary
Naim Ternava is the Grand Mufti of Kosovo and president of its Islamic Community, having led the country's Muslims through war and independence while combining religious administration, education and interfaith work. {cite:wikipedia-naim-ternava}

## Life and upbringing
Born on 7 January 1961 in the village of Lismir in the municipality of Fushe Kosova, he descends from a family expelled from Ternava in the Toplica district of the Sandzak, an origin carried in the family name. He finished primary school at home, secondary school at the Alauddin Madrasa in Pristina, then studied at al-Azhar in Cairo, graduating from the Faculty of Usul al-Din in 1984 and taking a master's degree there in 2003. {cite:wikipedia-naim-ternava}

## Career and works
Appointed imam of his village in 1984, he began teaching creed and Islamic philosophy at the Alauddin Madrasa in 1985, served three terms on the editorial board of the magazine Dituria Islame and two terms on the presidency of the Islamic Community between 1990 and 1998. He directed the Alauddin Madrasa from 1995 to 2003 and has lectured on Islamic philosophy at Pristina's Faculty of Islamic Studies since the 1994/95 academic year. {cite:wikipedia-naim-ternava}

## War and the muftiate
On 9 March 1998, during the Kosovo war, the Islamic Community's presidency formed an Emergency Council — the first of its kind in Kosovo — and placed Ternava at its head. He was elected president of the Community's assembly in 1999, then president and mufti on 15 October 2003, and was re-elected on 18 October 2008. He helped found Kosovo's inter-religious council and is among the Balkans' most visible figures of religious coexistence, with seven authored monographs and dozens of articles. {cite:wikipedia-naim-ternava}

## Legacy
Ternava's tenure is read as the model of a national religious institution in a young state: village imam, teacher and madrasa director, then an elected mufti in a transitional era. He trained generations of imams through the Alauddin Madrasa and the Faculty of Islamic Studies, represented Kosovo's Albanian Muslims in European forums, and kept the country's religious discourse within the Hanafi tradition customary in the Balkans. {cite:wikipedia-naim-ternava}
## Origin and name
His family carries the name of the village of Ternava in the Toplica district of the Sandzak — then attached to the sanjak of Niš — from which his forebears were expelled in 1878 before settling in Lismir; his official biography tells that the family, like many displaced Albanian families, kept the memory of the ancestral homeland so alive that the surname itself became a marker of origin. {cite:wikipedia-naim-ternava}

## Interfaith work
Ternava helped found Kosovo's inter-religious council, and his 2008 election ceremony in Pristina's Sultan Murat Mosque was attended by the chief imams of Macedonia, Montenegro and Bosnia and leading Kosovo Albanian figures, per German documentation — making him a face of religious dialogue in Europe's newest state. {cite:wikipedia-naim-ternava}
