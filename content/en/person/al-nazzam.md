## Overview
Al-Nazzam, Ibrahim ibn Sayyar ibn Hani, kunya Abu Ishaq. A Mu'tazili theologian of the Basran masters, who was a theologian, poet and man of letters. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 526}

## In Murij al-Dhahab
Al-Mas'udi relates that he said in the session of Yahya that love is more subtle than a mirage and finer than wine. The text says that if it is excessive it returns as a deadly madness. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 358-359}

## His relation with Abu Nuwas
Ibn al-Nadim says that he used to rebuke Abu Nuwas, and he has several fragments about him. He says that Abu Nuwas alluded to him in a verse about one who claims philosophy. The reason was that al-Nazzam used to call Abu Nuwas to say the doctrine of threat, and he refused. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 526}

## In al-Jahiz's Kitab al-Hayawan
The editor of [[work/kitab-al-hayawan|Kitab al-Hayawan]], in the biography of [[person/al-jahiz|al-Jahiz]] he takes from Shadharat al-Dhahab, says al-Jahiz studied with Abu Ishaq al-Nazzam. {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v1 p. 4} Al-Nazzam is the theologian most often cited in the book; al-Jahiz usually calls him "Abu Ishaq", and in one place "Abu Ishaq Ibrahim ibn Sayyar al-Nazzam". {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 3} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 104}

- **On fire and latency**: al-Jahiz opens the fifth part with al-Nazzam's statement that fire is a name for heat and light, then sets out his doctrine of [[concept/kumun|latency]], his replies to [[person/dirar-ibn-amr|Dirar ibn 'Amr]] and to the "people of accidents", and his view that burning a thing is drawing out its own fires. {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 3} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 5} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 10}
- **With the Manichaeans**: al-Jahiz reports that Abu Ishaq used to put to the Manichaeans a short, decisive question, "and claimed it was not his own": if for them each sense is a separate kind that cannot aid another, how does the tongue answer "yes" to a question heard by the ear about what the eye has seen? {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v2 p. 478}
- **On ghouls**: al-Jahiz reports his view that the origin of what the Bedouins tell of the jinn's humming and the ghouls' shape-shifting is that, settling in wild lands, they were worked on by loneliness, so that small things appeared to them as large, and they then turned what they imagined into poems and stories they handed down. See [[concept/ghul|the ghul]]. {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 445} {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 446}
- **On secrets**: al-Jahiz relates that he was the least able of men to keep a secret, and that when Qasim al-Tammar reproached him for spreading his secret the same day, he answered: ask him whether I betrayed him once, twice, three or four times — so whose is the fault now? {cite:hayawan-jahiz-ilmiyya-2003-v3 p. 104}

## In 'Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi
In al-Farq bayn al-Firaq, [[person/al-baghdadi-abd-al-qahir-ibn-tahir|'Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi]] counts among al-Nazzam's scandals his claim that God created people, animals, plants and minerals at one time, priority and lateness being only in their emerging from their places, and his doctrine of manifestation and latency, which he judges worse than the view of the Dahriyya; he also counts among them his claim that the composition and fine arrangement of the Qur'an are not miraculous, its miraculous aspect lying in its reports of the unseen. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-tawfiqiyya p. 109} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-tawfiqiyya p. 110}
