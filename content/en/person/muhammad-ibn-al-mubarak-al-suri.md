## Summary
Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn al-Mubarak al-Suri is one of the ascetics treated by [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] in the ninth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]] (notice 451). Abu Nu'aym describes him as a man of full judgment, clear piety and eloquence, and his sayings are taken from Muhammad ibn Asbagh ibn al-Faraj, who heard him, while the long dialogue on faith comes from a written record kept by Abu Nu'aym's grandfather, Muhammad ibn Yusuf, who met some of his companions. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/298-303}

## His sayings on sincerity and the works of the heart
He said that the works of the truthful for God are done in the heart, while those of the showy are done with the limbs before people; whoever is truthful should stand in works done for God, because God knows what the hearts contain. He said that people who suffice themselves with trade renounce it only for themselves, and make the surrender of their souls to something else seem acceptable. He said that whoever does not believe in God when he hopes in a creature for what God has guaranteed has not complete faith. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/298-299}

## A dialogue on faith and knowledge
The notice tells of a young man who asked him about the road to the afterlife and the knowledge of the blights that befall the soul, and who received answers in a tongue wise and patient in repetition. He answered that the road to God is faith in Him, stretched out for believers from this world to the next, and that whoever wears this road away with his desire goes astray. He divided faith into an outward and an inward part. The outward is the tongue's confession of God's oneness and the limbs' keeping of the obligations, by which blood and property are protected. The inward is the faith of the heart, which is threefold: assent to what God has promised and threatened, good opinion of God, and clearing God of charges against Him by those who have tied their trust to Him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/299-302}

## On assent and good opinion
Assent to God, he said, comes from the sight of knowledge of God: once knowledge of God is sound, doubt falls away, and once the heart knows that God is the Originator, who brought creation from nothing, what is hidden from a person in the report becomes more wonderful to him than what he sees, and the heart rests in assent to the promise. Good opinion of God, in his teaching, is to know that God has done good to you by grace and not by desert. Ignorance of God is what weakens assent and good opinion, and the heart's charges against God come from weak knowledge and little assent to God's might, and from a heart gathered to love of this world rather than the next. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/301-302}

## The hadiths on pages 304–309
The following pages give chains that Muhammad ibn al-Mubarak transmits from his shaykhs, among them a hadith of Abu al-Darda' about Abu Bakr coming to the Prophet after a dispute with a companion, a hadith from Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan that a group of this community will remain upon the truth until God's command comes, and a hadith from Aisha that the Prophet would tilt the vessel for her so she drank, and then made ablution with what remained. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 9/304-309}
