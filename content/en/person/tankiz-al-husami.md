## Overview
[[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] gives an entry for Tankiz, viceroy of Syria: brought to Egypt as a child, he was bought by al-Ashraf, passed to Lajin and then to [[person/al-nasir-muhammad-ibn-qalawun|al-Nasir Muhammad]], served al-Nasir at al-Karak, was made an amir of a drum-band in late Shawwal 709, and became viceroy of Damascus in Rabi' al-Akhir 712. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/520} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/521} Malatya was taken under him in 715. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/521}

## His governorship
Ibn Hajar quotes al-Safadi's praise of his justice: the roads were safe, prices fell, he appointed no one for a bribe, and the governors of Syria were ordered to correspond through him. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/521} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/522} He heard hadith and made the pilgrimage in 721. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/522} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/523}

## His end
He was arrested on 23 Dhu al-Hijja 740 after al-Nasir turned against him, taken to Alexandria, and died in prison there early in 741. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/525} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/526} Ibn Hajar gives al-Dhahabi's harsh portrait of him and al-Ala'i's reply that al-Dhahabi had gone too far and overlooked his justice, without deciding between them. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/526} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 1/527}
