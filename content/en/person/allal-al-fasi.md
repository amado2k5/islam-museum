## Summary
Moroccan jurist and political leader (1910–1974), graduate of al-Qarawiyyin who led the Istiqlal (Independence) Party in the struggle against French colonialism. He combined Maliki fiqh with the objectives (maqasid) of sharia in works such as Maqasid al-Shari'a al-Islamiyya wa-Makarimuha, Self-Criticism and Defense of the Sharia. {cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fasi}

## Life and formation

Allal al-Fasi was born in 1910 in Fez into an old learned family, educated first in the kuttab then at al-Qarawiyyin University where he later taught, his formation joining Maliki jurisprudence, Arabic letters and national politics. {cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fasi}

He entered the Moroccan national movement early, founding with companions a politico-cultural circle in Fez, before becoming chief leader of the Istiqlal Party founded in 1943–1944, which presented the independence petition. {cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fasi}

## Struggle and writings

France exiled him to Gabon from 1937 to 1946 for his nationalist activity; he returned to lead Istiqlal against the Protectorate until Morocco's independence in 1956, then held ministerial posts and continued in opposition and writing. {cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fasi}

His works include The Independence Movements in the Arab Maghreb, Maqasid al-Shari'a al-Islamiyya wa-Makarimuha, Self-Criticism, Defense of the Sharia and Hadith al-Maghrib wal-Maghrib; he sought to renew jurisprudence on the objectives of the law rather than mere textual literalism. {cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fasi}

## Legacy

Al-Fasi is remembered as a model of the scholar-politician in modern Morocco: a Qarawiyyin jurist who waged the national struggle and wrote on renewal together, enriching Morocco's debate on the place of sharia in the modern state. {cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fasi}

He died in 1974 in Bucharest during an official visit and was brought home for burial in Fez; the Istiqlal Party and his books remain witness to his role. {cite:wikipedia-allal-al-fasi}
