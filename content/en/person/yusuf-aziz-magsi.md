Nawab Mir Yusuf Aziz Magsi (1908 – 31 May 1935) was a Baloch nationalist writer and activist from Jhal Magsi, chief of the Magsi tribe, and with Abdul Aziz Kurd a co-founder of the Anjuman-e-Ittehad-e-Balochan-wa-Balochistan, the first open political party of the Baloch. I. A. Rehman's history of the awakening calls him "a poet, short-story writer, agitator and a charismatic character in the history of Balochistan". {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-aziz-magsi-en} {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-aziz-magsi-ur} {cite:wikidata-yusuf-aziz-magsi} {cite:dawn-balochistans-undying-dream}

He entered politics as a writer. In 1929 his article "Faryaad-e-Balochistan" — Balochistan's Lament — in the Lahore newspaper *Musawat* attacked the British and Kalat's prime minister, Sir Shams Shah, and brought him a heavy fine and a year in prison; it was while he was in Quetta jail that the leaders of the Young Baloch movement met him and the Anjuman was announced. Released, he became the party's president when it came into the open; at its first open meeting in 1931 it adopted the abolition of the sardari system, the unification of Balochistan under a constitutional and democratic government, more schools, and the establishment of an Islamic society on non-exploitative ideals. {cite:pakistan-monthly-review-baloch-nationalism-ii} {cite:dawn-balochistans-undying-dream}

His organising built the All-India Baloch Conference, held at Jacobabad on 28–30 December 1932; after it, he became sardar of the Magsi tribe and introduced in his own area some of the reforms the conference had demanded. The second conference met at Hyderabad on 26–28 December 1933, which he attended in defiance of the Kalat political agent's order. The authorities then crushed the party: Abdul Aziz Kurd was jailed for five years, Abdus Samad Achakzai confined in Machh jail, and Magsi — whom the Khan of Kalat had sent to argue Balochistan's sovereignty in Britain, without success — was sent into forced exile in England. He came back to die at home: he perished in the Quetta earthquake of 31 May 1935, aged twenty-seven. {cite:dawn-balochistans-undying-dream} {cite:wikipedia-yusuf-aziz-magsi-ur} {cite:wikidata-yusuf-aziz-magsi}
