## Summary
Amina Rasul-Bernardo is a Filipina Muslim civic leader, president of the Philippine Center for Islam and Democracy (PCID), and a former member of the Philippine cabinet. Her work joins three threads — democracy-building among the Muslims of the southern Philippines, literacy for Bangsamoro communities, and the public voice of Muslim women. {cite:muslim500-amina-rasul-bernardo}

## Family and formation
She is the daughter of Santanina Tillah Rasul, the first Muslim woman elected to the Senate of the Philippines, and grew up inside the public life of the country's Muslim minority. Trained in business and economics, she moved between government service and civil society, carrying into both the question of how the Philippines' Muslims — concentrated in Mindanao and the Sulu archipelago — can be represented in a majority-Christian republic. {cite:wikipedia-amina-rasul-bernardo}

## The Philippine Center for Islam and Democracy
PCID, which she leads, is a think-tank and advocacy body founded in the early 2000s to study and strengthen democratic participation in Muslim Filipino communities. Under her direction it has published on Islamic perspectives on democracy and conflict, run programmes for local leaders and women, and convened dialogues between Muslim leaders, government and civil society during and after the peace processes that produced the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region. {cite:wikipedia-amina-rasul-bernardo}

## Literacy and women's work
With her mother she co-founded the Magbassa Kita Foundation — 'let us read' — which promotes literacy in Bangsamoro communities as the basis for development and political participation. Her writing and columns argue that Muslim Filipina women are among the country's most under-served citizens, and that their education is both a development question and a peace-building one. {cite:muslim500-amina-rasul-bernardo}

## Standing
The Muslim 500 profiles her as a bridge figure: a Muslim advocate who works inside Philippine democratic institutions, and a democrat trusted inside Muslim communities — a combination few public figures in the country have sustained across the decades of the Mindanao conflict and its settlement. {cite:muslim500-amina-rasul-bernardo}
