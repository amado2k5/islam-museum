# Mahmudkhodja Behbudiy

Mahmudkhodja Behbudiy (1875–1919) was an Uzbek writer, publisher and Jadid leader in Russian Turkestan, founder of the magazine Ayina and the newspaper Samarkand, and the author of the first modern play in Central Asia {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-en}.

## Life and office

He was born on 20 January 1875 in the village of Bakhshitepa on the outskirts of Samarkand, into a family of Islamic scholars; the Russian article's infobox gives 30 January against its own lead and every other edition {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-en} {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-ru} {cite:wikidata-behbudiy}. He studied at a madrasa in Samarkand, worked as a scribe and secretary to the qadi courts, and himself became a qadi and, in the Russian and Uzbek accounts, mufti of Samarkand {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-en} {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-ru} {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-uz}.

## Publishing and theatre

In 1903–1904 he opened one of the first new-method schools at Samarkand, published textbooks such as Mukhtasar-i jugrofiya-yi Rusi, and in 1911 staged his play Padarkush (The Murderer of the Father), the first modern drama in Central Asia {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-en}. In 1913 he launched the newspaper Samarkand and the magazine Ayina, which carried the debates of the Jadid movement until the wartime closures {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-en} {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-uz}.

## Travels and the hajj

He travelled widely: a first journey in 1899 took him to Istanbul, Cairo, Mecca and Medina on the hajj, and he returned via the Caucasus and Iran; a second journey in 1914 to Istanbul and Edirne fed reports in Ayina, and in Syunik in Armenia he founded a school {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-en} {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-ru}.

## Death

After the February Revolution he sat on the Samarkand city council and in April 1917 led the election of the city's Muslim executive committee; in the civil-war years he was seized on a journey south, held and tortured at Qarshi, and executed there on 25 March 1919 on the orders of the last Emir of Bukhara {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-en} {cite:wikipedia-behbudiy-ru}.
