## Summary

Muhammed Fethullah Gülen ⁦(27 April 1941 – 20 October 2024)⁩ was a Turkish preacher, writer and educator, the leading figure of the 'Hizmet' ⁦(Service)⁩ movement — a loose transnational community inspired by the writings of Said Nursi that at its height ran schools, universities, charities, media and dialogue institutions in more than 160 countries — and, in his last quarter-century, one of the most contested Muslim figures in the world: saintly teacher to his followers, architect of a secretive parallel structure to the Turkish state that pursued him. {cite:wikipedia-fethullah-gulen} {cite:muslim500-2026 p. 232}

## Life and formation

Born in the village of Pasinler near Erzurum in eastern Anatolia, he was formed in the classical madrasa pattern: Qur'an memorisation, Arabic, fiqh and the study of Nursi's Risale-i Nur. He served as an imam in Edirne from 1959 and in Izmir's Kestanepazarı Qur'an school from 1966, where his preaching circles and student residences seeded what became the movement. {cite:wikipedia-fethullah-gulen}

## The movement

Through the 1980s and 1990s Gülen's circles institutionalised: preparatory schools and university halls, then schools abroad — beginning in Central Asia after the Soviet collapse — alongside media holdings, the daily Zaman, the journal Sızıntı, and interfaith dialogue platforms in which he met Pope John Paul II and other religious leaders. By the 2000s the network's schools operated in over 160 countries. In 1999 he moved to the United States, where he lived in Pennsylvania until his death. {cite:wikipedia-fethullah-gulen}

## The rupture with Ankara

A long alliance between the movement and Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's AKP broke openly in 2013; in 2016 Ankara accused Gülen of directing that July's coup attempt from Pennsylvania and designated the movement a terrorist organisation — 'FETÖ' — demanding his extradition, which the United States declined on the evidence provided. Gülen denied involvement and condemned the coup. The accusation is contested: the Turkish state treats the movement as a criminal network purged from its institutions in tens of thousands of cases, while governments and analysts elsewhere have questioned whether the coup was centrally directed by him at all; the museum records the positions attributed and states no verdict. {cite:wikipedia-fethullah-gulen}

## Thought and writings

Gülen authored dozens of books and his sermons were widely circulated. His public teaching dwelt on education as worship, dialogue between faiths, 'service' to humanity as the believer's vocation, and an Anatolian-Turkish piety articulated in a Sufi register; admirers describe him as a renewer of Nursi's legacy, critics as the builder of a disciplined hierarchy opaque to outsiders — both characterisations are attested and neither is adopted here. {cite:wikipedia-fethullah-gulen}

## Death

He died on 20 October 2024 in Pennsylvania, aged 83, and was buried at the movement's compound in Saylorsburg. {cite:wikipedia-fethullah-gulen}
