## Summary
Ali Muhi al-Din al-Qaradaghi is an Iraqi Kurdish jurist resident in Qatar, among the foremost contemporary authorities on the jurisprudence of financial transactions and Islamic economics, and secretary-general of the International Union of Muslim Scholars. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-qaradaghi} {cite:muslim500-ali-al-qaradaghi}

## Early life and education
Born in 1949 in the Qara Dagh district of Sulaymaniyah governorate in Iraqi Kurdistan, into a family known for producing religious scholars, he took his first religious education in Qara Dagh, then moved to Sulaymaniyah and Baghdad, taking a bachelor's in sharia at the Great Imam College in Baghdad in 1975, an MA in comparative jurisprudence at Al-Azhar's Faculty of Sharia and Law in 1980, and a doctorate in sharia and law at Al-Azhar in 1985 with a dissertation on contracts and financial transactions. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-qaradaghi}

## Academic career
He joined the Faculty of Sharia at Qatar University as assistant professor in 1985 and was promoted to full professor in 1995; he holds Qatari nationality, and sources describe him as the grand mufti of Qatar and the ideological successor of Yusuf al-Qaradawi. In 1988 he founded the Islamic Kurdish League, and he presides over the board of trustees of the University of Human Development in Sulaymaniyah. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-qaradaghi}

## Juristic offices
He was elected secretary-general of the International Union of Muslim Scholars — a position he took in 2022 after the death of its founder al-Qaradawi — after years on its board of trustees and executive office and chairing specialised committees; he is a member of the International Islamic Fiqh Academy in Jeddah and of the European Council for Fatwa and Research. In finance he chairs the sharia board of Qatar First Bank and sits on the sharia supervisory boards of institutions including Qatar National Bank, Dubai Islamic Bank, Qatar Insurance and Gulf Investment House. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-qaradaghi} {cite:muslim500-ali-al-qaradaghi}

## Works and legacy
He has published more than thirty books and over a hundred research papers, most centred on the jurisprudence of financial and banking transactions, Islamic economics and comparative law; among the titles is his 2016 "Formulating Ethical Principles in the Light of Higher Sharia Goals", which expanded the five necessities of sharia to seven by adding the protection of humanity and dignity. The Muslim 500 lists him among the influential figures of the Muslim world, and his line is widely read as continuing the maqasid school of jurisprudence elaborated by al-Qaradawi. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-qaradaghi} {cite:muslim500-ali-al-qaradaghi}

## Standing in contemporary jurisprudence
Al-Qaradaghi's standing combines three positions rarely united in one jurist: a university chair of comparative jurisprudence in Qatar, the secretary-generalship of the largest cross-border union of Muslim scholars, and membership of the sharia boards that certify the products of the Islamic financial market. Researchers associate his name with the continuation of the wasatiyya and maqasid school elaborated by al-Qaradawi, to whose union he moved — after the founder's death in 2022 — from participant to leader, and his membership of the International Islamic Fiqh Academy and the European Council for Fatwa and Research is read as continuing the line of collective institutional ijtihad on newly arising questions. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-qaradaghi} {cite:muslim500-ali-al-qaradaghi}
## Notes on the record
Al-Qaradaghi's record, as the sources document it, joins two dimensions many contemporary jurists keep apart: academic research in comparative jurisprudence and the history of transactions on one side, and normative decision-making inside collective fatwa bodies and the market's sharia boards on the other. Given the sensitivity of his leadership position at the scholars' union amid regional disputes, the museum's record confines itself to the attributed offices and scholarly output and does not engage political positions beyond what the sources themselves document. {cite:wikipedia-ali-al-qaradaghi}
