## Overview
'Isa al-Hajari, the soldier of the ajnad families of Irbil, called al-Hajari for Hajar al-Bayda which he praised often in verse though he was not of it; his diwan — tenderness prevails — holds fine meanings, spanning verse, dubayt and mawaliya, excelling in all three though few excel across the set. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 156-158}

## His killing
He was held in Khafatid citadel then Irbil's; released, he attached himself to Muzaffar al-Din, lord of Irbil, advanced at his court and dressed as a sufi. When Muzaffar al-Din died and Irbil fell to al-Mustansir's realm, a man fell upon him before noon and stabbed him with a knife — while struggling with death he wrote to its deputy Batkin complaining — and died that day. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 156-158}
