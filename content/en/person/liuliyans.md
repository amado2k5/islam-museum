## Overview
Ibn al-Nadim mentions Liuliyanus as a king who turned away from Christianity to the doctrine of the philosophers after Galen, and Themistius was his secretary, and he had his letter. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.355}

## Lilian in the account of al-Andalus
In *Nafh al-Tib* of al-Maqqari there is a man of this name, said to have held to obedience to the Goths and their faith, and to have been king of the Berbers in the mountains of Ghumara. Al-Maqqari says that he incited Musa ibn Nusayr to invade al-Andalus because of what had happened between him and Roderic, and that he joined Tariq and uncovered for the Arabs the weakness of the Goths. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.267-268}

The file brings together under one entry two persons of the same name: the king whom Ibn al-Nadim mentions, and the figure in the account of al-Andalus in al-Maqqari. Telling them apart needs an additional source.
