Bismillah Khan (21 March 1916 – 21 August 2006) was an Indian shehnai maestro, born Qamaruddin Khan at Dumraon in Bihar, the second son of Paigambar Bux Khan, a court musician of the Dumraon Raj.{cite:wikipedia-bismillah-khan-en}{cite:wikidata-bismillah-khan} Apprenticed at Varanasi to his uncle Ali Bux Khan, a shehnai player attached to the Kashi Vishwanath Temple, he brought the shehnai — until then chiefly a ceremonial and folk instrument — to the concert stage, first winning wide notice at the All India Music Conference in Calcutta in 1937.{cite:wikipedia-bismillah-khan-en}{cite:guardian-bismillah-khan-obituary-2006}

He played at Delhi's Red Fort as the Indian flag was raised at independence on 15 August 1947, and in 2001 received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour, only the third classical musician so honoured.{cite:wikipedia-bismillah-khan-en}{cite:bbc-bismillah-khan-2006} A devout Muslim who performed at both Hindu and Muslim ceremonies, he was celebrated as a symbol of religious harmony.{cite:bbc-bismillah-khan-2006}{cite:guardian-bismillah-khan-obituary-2006} He died at Varanasi on 21 August 2006, and India observed a day of mourning.{cite:wikipedia-bismillah-khan-en}
