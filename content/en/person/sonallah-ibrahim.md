## Summary
Sonallah Ibrahim was an Egyptian novelist and short-story writer (1937–2025), a leading figure of the "Sixties Generation" in Arabic literature. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} Imprisoned from 1959 to 1964 for his membership in the Marxist Democratic Movement for National Liberation, he drew on his imprisonment in his first book, That Smell; his novels include The Committee, Zaat, Honor and Warda. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-ar} In 2003 he refused a major state literary prize in protest at the Egyptian government's policies. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en}

## Life
Ibrahim was born in Cairo in 1937; the English article and Wikidata give 3 August 1937, while the Arabic and Urdu articles give 24 February 1937, pairing it with 13 Dhu al-Hijja 1355, so the museum records both dates and carries the first under debated status. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-ar} His father was an upper-middle-class civil servant; he entered Cairo University to study law in 1952 and there joined the Democratic Movement for National Liberation. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} Arrested in 1959 and sentenced to seven years, he was released in 1964, and wrote about the prison experience in That Smell (1966) and his memoir Diaries of Oasis Prison. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} He was among the Egyptian intellectuals who contributed to the avant-garde magazine Galerie 68. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} In 2003 he refused to accept the Cairo novel prize, worth £E100,000, in protest at government policy. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-ar} He died in Cairo on 13 August 2025, aged 88, after suffering from pneumonia. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-ar} {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} The sources describe his leftist views and political affiliation and name no religion for him; the museum records none. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} {cite:wikidata-sonallah-ibrahim}

## Work
His novels are typically told in the first person in a cold, objective tone resembling press reportage, and his later works incorporate excerpts from newspapers and magazines. {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} His novels include Star of August (1974), The Committee (1981), a satire of the Open Door policy, Beirut, Beirut (1984), on the Lebanese civil war, Zaat (1992), Honor (1997), Warda (2000), on leftist activism in Yemen and Oman in the 1960s and 1970s, Amricanly (2003), Stealth (2007), Ice (2011) and 1970 (2020). {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en} He received the Al Owais Award for Stories, Novels and Drama (1992–1993) and the Ibn Rushd Prize for Freedom of Thought (2004). {cite:wikipedia-sonallah-ibrahim-en}
