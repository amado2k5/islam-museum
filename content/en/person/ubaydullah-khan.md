## Summary
Ubaydullah Khan (1487–1539), also known as Ubaid Khan, was the fourth Shaybanid khan of Bukhara, reigning formally from 1534 to 1539. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en} He was the son of Mahmud Sultan and the nephew of Muhammad Shaybani, the dynasty's founder. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-ubaydullah-khan}

## Bukhara and the wars with Babur and Iran
When Muhammad Shaybani was killed in 1510, the Uzbek lands were divided among the family and Bukhara fell to Ubaydullah, who had to defend it against Babur and the Safavids in 1511–1512; Babur's defeat at Ghajdivan in 1512 secured the Uzbek hold on the land between the Amu Darya and the Syr Darya. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en} Against Safavid Iran he mounted five offensives into Khorasan without permanent success, suffering a major defeat at Turbet-i-Sheikh Jam in 1528. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en}

## Builder and patron
Ubaydullah was also a scholar, poet and builder: his years saw the Kalan Mosque raised in Bukhara in 1514 and the Mir-i-Arab Madrasa built in 1535–1536. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en}

## Last campaign and death
In 1538, now khan himself, he moved against Khwarazm and killed its khan Avanish, but was soon driven out by Avanish's son Din Muhammad. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en} He died shortly afterwards, in 1539, and was buried in the Mir-i-Arab Madrasa in Bukhara; power struggles among the Uzbeks followed until 1556. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en}

## Religion
The English article names no religion or school for him personally, and Wikidata carries no religion statement; his documented career is that of a Sunni Shaybanid ruler at war with Safavid Iran, and his record keeps his faith unattributed beyond that context. {cite:wikipedia-ubaydullah-khan-en} {cite:wikidata-ubaydullah-khan}
