## Overview
Farouk I (11 February 1920 – 18 March 1965) was the tenth ruler of Egypt from the Muhammad Ali dynasty and King of Egypt and the Sudan from 1936 until his overthrow in a military coup in 1952. He entered Egyptians' homes by voice and image: he was the first Egyptian sovereign to address the nation by radio, and news of his palaces, parties and travels filled the press and the airwaves until his name became fixed in public memory with the monarchy's splendour and corruption alike. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en} {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-ar}

He began his reign widely popular — "the beloved king" — but his reputation eroded with the corruption and incompetence of his governments and the defeat of the 1948 Palestine war, until the Free Officers forced him to abdicate in favour of his infant son in 1952. He died in exile in Italy in 1965. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

## Early life and education
Farouk was born at Abdeen Palace in Cairo, the eldest child of Sultan Fuad I — later King Fuad I — and his second wife Queen Nazli Sabri, and grew up with his sisters inside the royal palaces under a tightly controlled upbringing that restricted his contact with outsiders. Languages were the strongest part of his education: he spoke Egyptian Arabic, Turkish and French, later learned English and Italian, and became fluent in classical Arabic, which he used in public speeches, while tutors criticised his performance in other subjects. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

After failing the entrance examinations for Eton College, he attended the Royal Military Academy, Woolwich, in England as an extramural student, moving to Surrey in 1935 at the age of fifteen; in January 1936 he represented Egypt at the funeral of King George V in London. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

## Accession and coronation
King Fuad I died on 28 April 1936, and the sixteen-year-old Farouk returned to Egypt as king, greeted in Alexandria on 6 May 1936 by large crowds; with the throne he inherited a fortune the article estimates at about US$100 million, including vast Nile-valley land, five palaces, 200 automobiles and two yachts. He addressed the nation by radio — the first Egyptian sovereign to speak directly to his people — and his coronation was held in Cairo on 29 July 1937 with extensive celebrations. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

## Career: war and the palace incident
On 4 February 1942 British forces surrounded Abdeen Palace and the British ambassador presented Farouk with an ultimatum that forced him to appoint Mustafa el-Nahas as prime minister, an incident that permanently damaged the king's prestige before Egyptians. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

Under Farouk, Egypt entered the 1948 Palestine war and was defeated; the king's name became linked to the defective-weapons scandals and the country's deterioration, while short-lived governments followed one another amid accusations of corruption and favouritism. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en} {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-ar}

On 26 January 1952 the Cairo fire known as "Black Saturday" broke out: 26 people were killed and more than 400 cinemas, cabarets, bars, restaurants and shops in downtown Cairo were burned. Farouk dismissed Nahas the next day, but the deterioration did not stop. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

## Overthrow, exile and death
On 23 July 1952 the Free Officers carried out their coup, and Farouk was forced to abdicate in favour of his six-month-old son Ahmed Fuad, who became Fuad II, the last King of Egypt and the Sudan until the monarchy was abolished in June 1953. Farouk sailed to Italy, arriving in Naples on 29 July 1952, settled first in Naples and later in Rome, and moved between Italy, Monaco, Switzerland and France until he died in Rome on 18 March 1965. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en} {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-ar}

In exile as on the throne he was known for an extravagant lifestyle the article describes as that of a playboy fond of nightlife, gambling and collecting, and his doings were constant material for the European and Egyptian press alike. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

## Positions and criticism
This section presents differing views as reported in the sources; it is not the museum's verdict.

**His supporters** point to the wide popularity of his early reign — he was called "the beloved king" — and to the young king who presented himself as a friend of the peasants, declaring in his coronation speech that "the poor are not responsible for their poverty but rather the wealthy". {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

**His critics** held him responsible for the corruption and incompetence of successive governments, the humiliation of the 1942 Abdeen Palace incident, the 1948 defeat and its scandals, and the palace's extravagance in a country whose cultivated land was largely held by a small landowning elite. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

## Personal life
Farouk married Safinaz Zulficar — renamed "Farida" — in an elaborate public celebration in Cairo on 20 January 1938. The museum publishes no family detail beyond what understanding his public career needs. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en}

## Impact and audience
Farouk was the last king of Egypt to actually rule, and with his fall the Muhammad Ali dynasty ended after a century and a half. His presence in the media and in popular memory remained strong — from his radio speeches and coronation festivities to the news of his exile, and the many books and series that later treated his life. On his death his body was returned to Egypt and buried there. {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-en} {cite:wikipedia-farouk-of-egypt-ar}
