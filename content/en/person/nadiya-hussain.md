## Summary
Nadiya Hussain is a British-Bangladeshi baker, television presenter and author born in Luton in 1984, winner of The Great British Bake Off in 2015 and one of Britain's most-loved food personalities; The Muslim 500 lists her among influential figures. {cite:muslim500-nadiya-hussain} {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## Bake Off
A stay-at-home mother of three, she won the sixth series of The Great British Bake Off in 2015 — the programme's most-watched final — with a speech about self-belief that became a cultural moment; she went on to bake Queen Elizabeth II's 90th birthday cake. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## Television and books
Her BBC series — including Nadiya's British Food Adventure, Nadiya Bakes, Time to Eat and travel and documentary programmes — pair recipes with warmth and her signature hijab; her bestselling cookbooks, children's books and a novel made her one of Britain's most-published food writers. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## Public role
She speaks openly about anxiety, racism and faith, and is regularly cited as a figure who changed the face of British television and public imagination of British Muslim women. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## Assessment
She is credited with a uniquely warm, accessible presenting voice and with normalising a visibly Muslim woman at the centre of British popular culture; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain} {cite:muslim500-nadiya-hussain}


## Television career
Her presenting credits run from The Chronicles of Nadiya and Nadiya's British Food Adventure to Nadiya Bakes, Nadiya's Family Favourites and travel series — a decade of BBC food broadcasting that made her one of Britain's most-watched food presenters. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## Books
She has published more than a dozen cookbooks — plus novels, memoir Finding My Voice and children's titles — several of them bestsellers, and writes a long-running magazine column. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## Public voice
Her openness about anxiety and panic disorder — including a BBC documentary on her treatment — and her public discussion of British-Muslim identity have made her a figure beyond food media. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain} {cite:muslim500-nadiya-hussain}

## Origins
Born Nadiya Begum in Luton in 1984 to a Bangladeshi family, she won The Great British Bake Off in 2015 — a victory that press coverage made a national moment for British-Muslim visibility in mainstream food media. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## The Bake Off moment and beyond
Nadiya Hussain's 2015 Great British Bake Off victory was covered as a national moment for British-Muslim visibility in mainstream food media: a hijabi baker from a Bangladeshi family winning the country's most-watched competition programme. Her subsequent career — a decade of BBC food broadcasting, more than a dozen cookbooks, novels, a memoir Finding My Voice and a BBC documentary on her anxiety and panic disorder — made her a figure beyond food media and a public voice on British-Muslim identity. {cite:wikipedia-nadiya-hussain}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists her among the most influential Muslims in arts and media, citing her Bake Off victory as a landmark for British-Muslim representation and her subsequent career as one of Britain's most-watched food presenters. {cite:muslim500-nadiya-hussain}
