## Overview
Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr, son of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]]. He fought on the side of the polytheists at Badr, then accepted Islam and his Islam was sound. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 597}

## His entry in al-Isaba
He is Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq ibn Abi Quhafa; al-Bukhari listed him in his book, while Muslim listed him among the Successors, so the scholars hold two readings on whether he counted as a Companion or a Successor {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.783}. Ibn Hajar notes that the rest of his entry comes elsewhere, in the entry of Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Uthman {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.783}.

## His descendants
Al-Mas'udi says that he had many descendants, nomads and settled, in the region of the Hijaz on the side of the road toward Iraq, in the place known as al-Safiniyyat and al-Masih. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 597}

## For younger visitors
Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr was the son of the caliph Abu Bakr al-Siddiq; he accepted Islam after Badr and his Islam was sound, and the scholars mention him among the narrators of the Companion generation {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.783}.

## Notes for researchers
- al-Isaba fi Tamyiz al-Sahaba, entry 4654 (al-Maktaba al-Asriyya scan, p. 783); al-Bukhari listed him, Muslim placed him among the Successors — the disagreement on his Companionship is recorded.
