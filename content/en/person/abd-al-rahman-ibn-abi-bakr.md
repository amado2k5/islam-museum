## Overview
Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr, son of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]]. He fought on the side of the polytheists at Badr, then accepted Islam and his Islam was sound. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 597}

## His entry in al-Isaba
He is Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq ibn Abi Quhafa; al-Bukhari listed him in his book, while Muslim listed him among the Successors, so the scholars hold two readings on whether he counted as a Companion or a Successor {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.783}. Ibn Hajar notes that the rest of his entry comes elsewhere, in the entry of Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Uthman {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.783}.

## His descendants
Al-Mas'udi says that he had many descendants, nomads and settled, in the region of the Hijaz on the side of the road toward Iraq, in the place known as al-Safiniyyat and al-Masih. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 597}

## For younger visitors
Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr was the son of the caliph Abu Bakr al-Siddiq; he accepted Islam after Badr and his Islam was sound, and the scholars mention him among the narrators of the Companion generation {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.783}.

## In Khalid Muhammad Khalid's Rijal Hawl al-Rasul
[[person/khalid-muhammad-khalid|Khalid Muhammad Khalid]] titles his chapter "A hero to the end". He says Abd al-Rahman long held to his people's religion: he fought with the polytheists at Badr and at Uhud led Quraysh's archers, and when he called for single combat his father [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] rose to face him, but the Prophet held him back. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.530} He later embraced Islam, came to the Prophet and pledged allegiance, and thereafter missed no campaign; at al-Yamama he fought with distinction, and the author says it was he who killed al-Muhakkam ibn al-Tufayl, Musaylima's chief strategist, opening a breach in the fort through which the Muslims poured. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.531} He relates that when Marwan ibn al-Hakam read out in the mosque Mu'awiya's letter calling for allegiance to Yazid, Abd al-Rahman rose and said: "By God, it is not choice you want for Muhammad's community — you want to make it Heraclian: whenever a Heraclius dies, a Heraclius rises", and a group supported him, among them al-Husayn ibn Ali, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and Abd Allah ibn Umar. The comment that he saw in this a turning of consultation into hereditary kingship is the author's own. {cite:rijal-hawl-al-rasul-muqattam-1994 p.532}
