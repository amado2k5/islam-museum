## Summary
Egyptian scholar and jurist (1893–1963), Grand Imam of al-Azhar from 1958 to 1963. A leading figure of twentieth-century Azharite renewal, he issued his famous 1959 fatwa recognizing the Twelver Ja'fari school as a valid school of worship; his books include Fiqh al-Qur'an wal-Sunna, Al-Islam 'Aqida wa-Shari'a and Al-Qur'an wal-Qital. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-shaltut}

## Life and offices

Mahmoud Shaltut was born in 1893 in the village of Minya Bani Mansur in Beheira, Egypt; he memorised the Qur'an young, entered al-Azhar, graduated and taught there, holding posts including deputy rector and head of its higher division. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-shaltut}

Appointed Grand Imam of al-Azhar in 1958 under Gamal Abdel Nasser, he oversaw the 1961 al-Azhar development law that introduced modern sciences, medicine and engineering into the institution and opened colleges for men and women. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-shaltut}

## Reforms and fatwa

He is famed for his 1959 fatwa declaring the Twelver Shia (Ja'fari) school a valid Islamic school of worship alongside the Sunni schools and calling for rapprochement between the madhhabs — a position documented by Cairo's Dar al-Taqrib and taught widely afterwards. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-shaltut}

His books include Fiqh al-Qur'an wal-Sunna, Al-Islam 'Aqida wa-Shari'a, Al-Qur'an wal-Qital, a tafsir of the first ten parts of the Qur'an, and a collected volume of fatwas. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-shaltut}

## Legacy

Shaltut is a landmark figure in modern al-Azhar history: he opened the institution to modern sciences and took the widest official step toward Sunni–Shia rapprochement in the twentieth century, though scholars differ in assessing the motives and circumstances of the reform. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-shaltut}

He died in 1963 and was succeeded in the office; his fatwa is still cited in every debate on inter-madhab rapprochement, and his books are set texts in Islamic-studies departments. {cite:wikipedia-mahmoud-shaltut}
