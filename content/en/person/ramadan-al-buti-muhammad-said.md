## Summary
Muhammad Sa'id Ramadan al-Buti (1929–2013) was a Syrian Sunni scholar of Kurdish origin, among the most widely read Muslim authors of his age. Dean of the Faculty of Sharia at Damascus University and preacher of the Umayyad Mosque, he wrote more than sixty books in defence of the Shafi'i school and the Ash'ari creed, and was assassinated while teaching at a Damascus mosque in March 2013. {cite:wikipedia-ramadan-al-bouti-en}

## Early life and education
Al-Buti was born in the village of Jilka near Cizre, in the Botan district of Turkey, in 1929; the Arabic biography pairs the year with 1347 AH. His father, Mulla Ramadan, was himself a scholar who had taken part in Shaykh Said's revolt, and in 1933 he fled with his family to Damascus, where the boy grew up. Muhammad Sa'id studied at the Manjak Mosque in the al-Midan quarter and under Shaykh Hasan Habannaka, finished at the Institute of Islamic Orientation in 1953, and travelled to Cairo, where he took his degree at al-Azhar's Faculty of Sharia and a diploma in education. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-buti-ar} {cite:wikipedia-ramadan-al-bouti-en}

## Damascus career
He taught at Homs from 1958 to 1961, then joined Damascus University as an instructor in 1960, gaining his doctorate in the principles of Sharia in 1965. He rose through the Faculty of Sharia — vice-dean in 1975, dean in 1977 — and headed its department of creeds and religions; from 2008 he preached at the Umayyad Mosque, and he presided over the Union of Levant Scholars. {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-buti-ar} {cite:wikipedia-ramadan-al-bouti-en}

## Thought and writings
A Shafi'i in law and an Ash'ari in creed, al-Buti belonged to the neo-traditionalist current that upheld the four Sunni schools and Sufi-inflected piety against both secularism and Salafism. The English biography quotes his specialisation "according to the Shafi'i school of law", and the Arabic biography describes him in its opening as "Shafi'i in madhhab, and on the creed of Ahl al-Sunna according to the Ash'ari method". His more than sixty books include Fiqh al-Sira, a thematic reading of the Prophet's biography; al-La Madhhabiyya, arguing that abandoning the schools is a dangerous innovation; Kubra al-Yaqiniyyat al-Kawniyya, on creed; and studies of women, human rights and interreligious dialogue. In 2004 the Dubai International Holy Quran Award named him personality of the Muslim world. {cite:wikipedia-ramadan-al-bouti-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-buti-ar}

## The Syrian crisis and death
Through the 1979–1982 conflict he had opposed the Muslim Brotherhood's uprising and supported the state, while still criticising state measures he saw as anti-religious; during the crisis that began in 2011 he again supported the government and called the protests a fitna, yet also publicly criticised the dismissal of hijab-wearing teachers, after which President Assad reversed the order. On Thursday 21 March 2013 — 9 Jumada al-awwal 1434 in the Arabic dating — he was killed while giving a tafsir lesson at the al-Iman Mosque in Damascus's Mazraa district, in an explosion that killed at least 42 people, including his grandson Ahmad. Responsibility was disputed: the state blamed Jabhat al-Nusra, while opposition figures accused the regime itself. He was buried beside the shrine of Saladin, next to the Umayyad Mosque. {cite:wikipedia-ramadan-al-bouti-en} {cite:wikipedia-muhammad-said-ramadan-al-buti-ar}
