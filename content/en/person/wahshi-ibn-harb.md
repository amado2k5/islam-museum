## Brief
Wahshi ibn Harb was an Abyssinian slave famed for his javelin. At Uhud he killed [[person/hamza|Hamza]] to win his freedom; after the conquest he became a Muslim — and the tradition says his javelin later killed the false prophet Musaylima.

## Life
His owner Jubayr ibn Mut'im promised him freedom if he killed the Prophet's uncle. At [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] Wahshi threw the javelin that killed Hamza {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

After the conquest he kept out of the Prophet's sight — the tradition remembers him saying he feared he could not be forgiven. He converted, and the Prophet is reported to have accepted him while asking him to keep from his presence {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

At [[event/battle-of-yamama|Yamama]] in 632 his javelin found [[person/musaylima|Musaylima]]. The tradition preserves his own summation: "I killed the best of people and the worst of people" {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.

## Legacy
Wahshi's two javelins frame the era: the man whose weapon killed the faith's first great martyr and then its first great pretender.

## For young visitors
Wahshi's spear killed a hero of Islam — and then, after he joined the Muslims, killed the man who falsely claimed to be a prophet. He said so himself.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
In the reflection on perspicacity, [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] narrates a report that he says al-Bukhari gives in the Book of Campaigns, and that he would not have believed had it not been in the Sahih: Ja'far ibn Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri and Ubayd Allah ibn Adi ibn al-Khiyar went to Hims to ask Wahshi how he killed Hamza, and found him very dark, like a black waterskin, sitting in the shade of his house. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:39:00} Ubayd Allah was wrapped so that only his eyes and feet showed, and Wahshi told him that Adi ibn al-Khiyar had married Umm Qital bint Abi al-Is, that he had carried her baby boy to a wet-nurse in Mecca, and that his feet were like that child's feet. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:41:00}

He says Wahshi killed Hamza to win his freedom; that when he became Muslim and met the Prophet, the Prophet asked: are you Wahshi? did you kill Hamza? and asked him to keep his face out of his sight. Al-Heweny sees in this that Hamza's killing still pained the Prophet after the conquest, though Islam wipes out what came before. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:42:00} Wahshi then went out against Musaylima, hoping to make up for Hamza; in Abd Allah ibn Umar's report a slave girl cried that the black slave had killed him, and Wahshi used to say he had killed the best of people and the worst of people. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:44:00}
