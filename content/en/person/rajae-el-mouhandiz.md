## Summary
Rajae El Mouhandiz is a Dutch-Moroccan-Algerian interdisciplinary artist born in Larache, Morocco, in 1979 and raised in the Netherlands; she works across music, film, performance and curation, and The Muslim 500 lists her among influential figures in arts and culture. {cite:muslim500-rajae-el-mouhandiz} {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Career
A conservatory-trained vocalist and poet, she released albums mixing soul, jazz and Maghrebi sounds, composed for theatre, and developed multimedia performance pieces that have toured European festivals and museums. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Themes
Her projects — among them the short film Home! ⁦(2015)⁩ and the performance work Tracing Traces — examine migration, memory, the female body and the place of Muslim identity in European cultural institutions. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Curation and policy
She founded the arts platform Dialna, curates exhibitions on diaspora art, and advises Dutch and European cultural bodies on inclusion policy, arguing for space for diaspora voices inside mainstream institutions. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Assessment
She is regarded as a leading voice of the Dutch-Moroccan creative generation and a case study in art-world debates on representation; the museum reports assessments attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz} {cite:muslim500-rajae-el-mouhandiz}


## Albums and projects
Her recorded work runs from the early EP My Fear through Incarnation, Hand of Fatima and Summer of Alariat to the album Home ⁦(2018)⁩ and the theatre piece Ode to the Sahara, moving between R&B, gnawa and orchestral folk settings. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Film and theatre
She wrote and starred in the short film HOPE ⁦(2016)⁩, founded the theatre company Ailaq, and tours spoken-word and music-theatre productions that place North-African women's voices on European stages. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Recognition
Her advocacy sits inside her art: performances and talks — including a TEDx — address migration, belonging and women's autonomy, and she is regularly profiled as a leading Moroccan-Dutch cultural voice. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz} {cite:muslim500-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## From Morocco to the Netherlands
Born in Morocco in 1979 and raised in Amsterdam, she studied classical and jazz vocals and began recording in the early 2000s — one of the first artists to build a catalogue explicitly spanning North-African and Dutch idioms. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Independent path
She founded her own imprint rather than signing to a major, releasing through it for two decades — a career profiled in Dutch and Moroccan press as a model of independent diaspora art-making. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## Identity in the work
Her songs, films and theatre pieces return to questions of migration, gender and North-African heritage — work that critics place within a wave of artists reclaiming 'Moroccan-Dutch' as a creative identity rather than a policy category. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz} {cite:muslim500-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## The Moroccan-Dutch position
Rajae El Mouhandiz's work is defined by the Moroccan-Dutch position she occupies: an artist whose catalogue moves between gnawa, R&B, orchestral folk and spoken-word theatre, and whose films, books and performances address migration, gender and North-African heritage from inside that dual location. Her independent path — founding her own imprint rather than signing to a major — is profiled as a model of diaspora art-making, and her theatre work places North-African women's voices on European stages in ways critics describe as unprecedented. {cite:wikipedia-rajae-el-mouhandiz}

## The Muslim 500 listing
The Muslim 500 lists her among the most influential Muslims in arts, citing her position as a leading Moroccan-Dutch cultural voice and her work across music, film and theatre on questions of migration and belonging. {cite:muslim500-rajae-el-mouhandiz}
