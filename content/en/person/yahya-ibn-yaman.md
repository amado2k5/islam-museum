## Overview
Yahya ibn Yaman al-Ijli al-Kufi, Abu Zakariyya, is described by al-Dhahabi as the imam, the memoriser, the truthful, the devout and the Qur'an reciter {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4216}. The sign printed with his entry shows that Muslim and the four Sunan recorded his hadith {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4216}. He kept company with Sufyan al-Thawri and narrated much from him, and was counted among the scholars who acted on what they knew {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4216}.

## Teachers and students
He narrated from Hisham ibn Urwa, al-Minhal ibn Khalifa, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid and a group of others, and recited the Qur'an to Hamza al-Zayyat {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4216}. His son Dawud the memoriser, Bishr ibn al-Harith, Abu Kurayb, Sufyan ibn Waki', Ali ibn Harb, al-Hasan ibn Arafa and a great many others narrated from him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4217}.

## What the critics said
Ali ibn al-Madini said he was truthful but was struck by paralysis and his memory changed {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4217}. Al-Dhahabi reports Waki' as saying none of their companions memorised hadith better than Yahya ibn Yaman, who could memorise five hundred hadith in one sitting and then forget them {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4217}. Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Numayr said he was quick to memorise and quick to forget, and Ahmad ibn Hanbal said he was not a proof {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4217}. Ya'qub ibn Shayba said he is counted with al-Ashja'i for the volume he narrated from Sufyan, and that they objected to his many errors {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4217}. Al-Dhahabi adds that Muslim approved of him {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4217}.

## His death
Al-Dhahabi says he died in 189 AH {cite:siyar-alam-nubala-ideas-house ص4217}.
