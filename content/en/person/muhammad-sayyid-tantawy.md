## Summary

Muhammad Sayyid Tantawy (1928–2010) was an Egyptian Sunni scholar who held the muftiship (1986–1996) then the sheikhdom of al-Azhar (1996–2010), occupying Egypt's highest official religious post for nearly a quarter-century.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-sayyid-tantawy}

## Life

Born in Salim, Sohag, he graduated from al-Azhar's Faculty of Usul al-Din, taught at al-Azhar and universities in Libya and Saudi Arabia, then was appointed Egypt's mufti in 1986 and sheikh of al-Azhar in 1996.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-sayyid-tantawy}

## Works and office

His best-known work is Al-Tafsir al-Wasit li-l-Qur'an al-Karim (15 volumes), plus Banu Isra'il fi al-Qur'an wal-Sunna and Mu'amalat al-Bunuk wa Ahkamuha al-Shar'iyya. In office he issued public fatwas including permitting bank interest and on the niqab in French schools.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-sayyid-tantawy}

## Controversy

His fatwas divided opinion: supporters saw institutional ijtihad, critics saw politicisation of office and closeness to authority; his meetings with Israeli figures and quarrels with other scholars are also discussed — the museum records both without a verdict.{cite:wikipedia-muhammad-sayyid-tantawy}
