## Summary

Shaista Khan (22 November 1600 – c. 1694), born Mirza Abu Talib, was a Mughal general and the subahdar of Bengal, and the maternal uncle of Emperor Aurangzeb {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-en} {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan}. The son of Asaf Khan and grandson of Mirza Ghias Beg Itimaduddaula, he rose through the Mughal court from childhood rank to the title Amir-ul-Umara, chief of the nobles {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan} {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-ar}. His Bengal tenure, from 1664 to 1688 with a short break, was the longest of any subahdar of the province {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan}.

## The Deccan and the attack at Pune

Shaista Khan made his name as a general under Shah Jahan and worked closely with his nephew Aurangzeb in the Deccan {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan}. As viceroy of the Deccan from 1660 he took Pune, the centre of Shivaji's realm, and occupied the Lal Mahal there {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-ur}. On the night of 5 April 1663 Shivaji's men entered Pune in the guise of a wedding procession and attacked the palace; Shivaji cut off three of Shaista Khan's fingers as he escaped through a window, and one of his sons was killed {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-ur}. Aurangzeb, angered, transferred him to Bengal without the customary audience {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-en}.

## Subahdar of Bengal

In Bengal Shaista Khan restored a disordered administration, overawed corrupt officers and recalcitrant zamindars, and abolished illegal taxes {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan}. His fame rests chiefly on the conquest of Chittagong: he rebuilt the Bengal fleet to some three hundred vessels within a year, won over the Portuguese of Chittagong and Dutch support, took Sandwip in November 1665, and captured Chittagong itself on 26 January 1666, freeing thousands of kidnapped Bengali peasants {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan} {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-en}. He disciplined the European companies' trade, and in his second term the English were expelled from Bengal after making war on the Mughal government {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan}. A great builder in Dhaka, he raised the Chhota Katra, added to Lalbagh Fort and built there the tomb of his daughter Bibi Pari, and is linked to the Sat Gambuj and Chowk Bazaar mosques {cite:banglapedia-shaista-khan} {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-en}. One scholarly survey of Islam in Bengal names him among the Shia subahdars of the Mughal period; a single naming falls short of the museum's two-text standard, and his faith is recorded as unknown {cite:banglapedia-islam-bengal}. In his last years he left Dhaka for Delhi, where he died in about 1694 {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-en} {cite:wikipedia-shaista-khan-ar}.
