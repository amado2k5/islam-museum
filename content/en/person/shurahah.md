## Overview
A woman named Shurahah confessed to zina before Ali ibn Abi Talib while pregnant; her husband was away in Syria. Ali ordered her flogged and then stoned {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.303}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.304}.

## The case
Al-Sallabi reports, on the authority of al-Sha'bi, that Shurahah's husband was away in Syria, that she became pregnant, and that her master brought her to Ali {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.303}. Ali ordered her flogged with one hundred lashes on the Thursday and stoned on the Friday, and said that stoning is a sunna set by the Prophet {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.304}. The book notes that combining flogging with stoning is a judicial ruling disputed among jurists, and that the majority hold that the two are not combined {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.304}.

## Pregnancy
On the same page the book gives a rule attributed to Ali: a pregnant woman found guilty of zina is not punished until she has given birth {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.304}. The book does not reconcile this rule with the case of Shurahah.
