## Overview
A king of the Sasanian Persians. al-Tabari mentions him under the heading "The Reign of Shapur Dhu al-Aktaf" {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.2 p.55}.

## Al-Hadr and al-Nudayra
al-Mas'udi reports that he besieged the fortress of al-Hadr, held by al-Dayzan, king of Tanukh, for a month, and found no way to take it. Then al-Nudayra, daughter of al-Dayzan, showed him how to scatter straw on the Tharthar river and follow it wherever it entered. He took the fortress, killed al-Dayzan and razed it {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.552-554}.

He also reports that when he discovered the gentleness of her upbringing, and that she had betrayed her father and her people, he ordered her tied by her braids to two unruly horses, which tore her apart {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.552-554}.

The years of his reign and his affairs need an additional source.

## In al-Dinawari
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports that the crown was placed on the belly of Shapur's mother while she was pregnant, the nobles agreeing to await his birth, and that in his childhood Arabs from al-Bahrayn and Kazima raided Abarshahr. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 47} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 48} As a youth he drove them out and besieged al-Dayzan on the Euphrates; “they claim” his daughter Mulayka showed him the way into the city, and he dislocated the shoulders of his captives, hence the name Dhu al-Aktaf. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 48} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 49} He built [[place/al-anbar|al-Anbar]] and named it Fayruz-Sabur. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 49} He fought the Roman emperor who besieged Ctesiphon and was killed by a stray arrow, and the emperor's successor made peace with him over Nisibis. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 49} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 50} He reigned seventy-two years. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 50}
