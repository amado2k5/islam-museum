## Summary
Halit Ziya Uşaklıgil (1866 – 27 March 1945) was a Turkish novelist and writer, born at Istanbul and died there. {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en} {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-tr} Wikidata carries three birth values for him (12 February 1865, 1 January 1867, and the year 1866); this entry follows the three articles, which agree on the year 1866. {cite:wikidata-halit-ziya-usakligil} He was the leading novelist of the Edebiyat-ı Cedide (New Literature) movement and its journal Servet-i Fünun; his best-known works are Mai ve Siyah (1897), Aşk-ı Memnu (1900) and Kırık Hayatlar (1923). {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en}

## Life and career
Halit Ziya was born at Istanbul in 1866 to a merchant family originally from Uşak; when his father's business failed, the family moved to İzmir in 1879, where he was educated and learned French at an Armenian Catholic school. {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en} {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-ar} He published early pieces in Hazine-i Evrak under the pen name Mehmet Halid and founded the newspaper Hizmet at İzmir in 1886. {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-tr} {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en} From 1896 he wrote for Servet-i Fünun at the centre of the renewal movement; his Kırık Hayatlar was censored under Abdülhamid II in 1901 and published in full only in 1923, and he was an outspoken critic of that reign. {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en} He spent his last years at San Stefano — whose new name, Yeşilköy, he himself proposed in 1926 — and died at Istanbul on 27 March 1945. {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en}

## Works
His novels include Nemide (1889), Bir Ölünün Defteri (1889), Ferdi ve Şürekâsı (1894), Mai ve Siyah (1897), Aşk-ı Memnu (1900) and Kırık Hayatlar (1923). {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en} Aşk-ı Memnu was adapted in our time into a widely exported television series that carried its author's name far beyond Turkey. {cite:wikipedia-halit-ziya-usakligil-en}
