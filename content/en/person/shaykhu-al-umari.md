## Overview
Sayf al-Din Shaykhu al-Umari al-Nasiri, also written Shaykhun, was a Turkish mamluk who became atabek of the armies of Egypt and the most influential amir of the early reign of [[person/al-nasir-hasan|al-Nasir Hasan]]. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/324-325} The Encyclopaedia of Islam names him among the three veterans of al-Nasir Muhammad's household who held real power in the regency of 748/1347. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1047}

## Origin
Ibn Taghribirdi says he was Turkish by origin; the merchant Khwaja Umar brought him from his homeland and sold him to [[person/al-nasir-muhammad-ibn-qalawun|al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun]]. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/324} He rose after the sultan's death and was a little over fifty when he died. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/324-325}

## Rise to the top
Ibn Hajar says he came forward in the reign of al-Muzaffar Hajji and, at the start of Hasan's reign, was among the heads of the council: petitions were read to him and the reins of the state were in his hand. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/196} Ibn Taghribirdi says that on the accession of Hasan he became ra's nawba kabir, the senior commander of the guard, and shared in managing the kingdom. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/188} In Shawwal 751, Ibn Hajar relates, a decree made him governor of Tripoli while he was out hunting; he was taken to Damascus, ordered to stay, and then arrested and held in Alexandria until al-Salih came to the throne and freed him in Rajab 752. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/196} After the fall of Hasan he formed a triumvirate with [[person/sarghitmish|Sarghitmish]] and [[person/taz-al-nasiri|Taz]], keeping the sultan's privy treasury in his own hands. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 1058} Ibn Taghribirdi describes him as a man who disliked strife and sought to calm the amirs, restraining his colleagues when they wished to move against one another. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/286}

With Sarghitmish he deposed al-Salih and restored Hasan in Shawwal 755. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/302} He interceded for Taz, who was merely sent to Aleppo rather than condemned. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/302} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 259} Ibn Taghribirdi says he was the first atabek to be called al-Amir al-Kabir and to receive the post with a robe of investiture; before him any senior amir could bear the title, and after him the atabekate became a regular office. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/303} The Encyclopaedia of Islam agrees that in the days of Shaykhun al-Umari, which it dates 752/1352, the title became reserved for the commander-in-chief. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 466}

## The mosque and khanqah
Shaykhu built a mosque and a Sufi khanqah in the Salibah quarter of Cairo; Ibn Taghribirdi records the khanqah as completed in 756, with two baths and a rental block beside it, and the Hanafi scholar Akmal al-Din al-Babarti appointed as its shaykh. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/303-304} Ibn Hajar says he built the mosque and khanqah in al-Salibah. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/197} The Nujum's editor notes that both buildings survive and are still used. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/303} See [[building/shaykhu-complex-cairo|Mosque and Khanqah of Shaykhu, Cairo]].

## The attack and death
On Monday in Shaban 758, while the sultan sat in the Dar al-Adl, a royal mamluk, Qutlunaja the silahdar, struck Shaykhu three times with a sword on the face, head and arm. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/305} His mamluks carried him home and surgeons treated him. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/305} The sultan visited him the next day and swore he had known nothing of it; the attacker said he had asked Shaykhu for an iqta that was refused. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/305} The attacker was executed. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/305} Shaykhu stayed in bed until he died. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/305}

The date is given differently. Ibn Taghribirdi says the seventh of Dhu al-Hijja, adding that some put it early in Dhu al-Qada, and his editor notes that the author's own al-Manhal al-Safi says 16 Dhu al-Qada; Ibn Hajar says 26 Dhu al-Qada. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/324} {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 2/197} The Encyclopaedia of Islam says only late in 758/1357. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1047} He was buried in his khanqah. {cite:nujum-zahira 10/325}
