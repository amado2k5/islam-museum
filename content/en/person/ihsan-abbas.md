## Summary

Ihsan Abbas (1920–2003) was a Palestinian literary historian and manuscript editor, among the foremost scholars of Arabic literature in the twentieth century and professor at the American University of Beirut for decades.{cite:wikipedia-ihsan-abbas}

## Life

Born in Ayn Ghazal near Haifa, he graduated from the American University of Beirut, taught in Sudan and Beirut, and was professor of Arabic literature in Beirut until retirement, then in Amman.{cite:wikipedia-ihsan-abbas}

## Works

His editions include Ibn Khallikan's Wafayat al-A'yan (8 volumes), Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani's Kitab al-Aghani and Akhbar Abi Tammam; his authored works include A History of Literary Criticism Among the Arabs, Greek Traces in Arabic Literature and Creative Figures; he translated Moby-Dick and novels into Arabic.{cite:wikipedia-ihsan-abbas}

## Method and standing

He joined textual editing from manuscripts to comparative literary study; researchers rank him among the last greats of the modern Arabic editing and translation school.{cite:wikipedia-ihsan-abbas}
