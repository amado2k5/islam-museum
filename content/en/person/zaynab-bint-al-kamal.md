## Summary
Zaynab bint Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahim ibn Abd al-Wahid al-Maqdisiyya, known as Bint al-Kamal, was an eminent hadith transmitter of [[place/damascus|Damascus]], born in 646 AH and died in 740 AH aged over ninety. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.50} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.53}

## Her teachers
She heard from Muhammad ibn Abd al-Hadi, Ibrahim ibn Khalil, Abu al-Fahm al-Yaldani and Ahmad ibn Abd al-Da'im, and held licences (*ijazat*) from a large number, among them Ibrahim ibn Mahmud ibn al-Khayr, Abu Nasr ibn al-Ulayq, Ajiba al-Baqadariyya, Ibn al-Sayyidi, Yusuf ibn Khalil, the grandson of al-Silafi, al-Zaki al-Mundhiri, Abu Ali al-Bakri and others. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.50} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.51}

## Those who heard from her
Muhammad al-Wani heard part of Sahih Muslim from her, the traveller [[person/ibn-battuta-muhammad|Ibn Battuta]] heard from her in the Umayyad Mosque of Damascus, and a group transmitted from her, among them Muhammad ibn al-Muwaffaq al-Mardawi, Ibrahim al-Kattani, Abd al-Rahman ibn Ahmad al-Dhahabi and Shams al-Muluk bint al-Nasir. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.51}

## What was read to her
Kahhala lists a long series of books and parts read to her, including the introduction to Ibn Abd al-Barr's *al-Istidhkar*, al-Mahamili's *Kitab al-Du'a'*, al-Ajurri's work forbidding backgammon and chess (through her licence from Abu al-Barakat ibn Taymiyya), the *Musnad* of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, Ibn Abi Asim's *Kitab al-Awa'il*, *Fada'il al-Qur'an* by one Ja'far (read "al-Qarmani" in the OCR), the dictations of Abu Musa al-Madini and others, by hearing or by licence. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.51} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.52} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.53}

## Death
She died on 19 Jumada I 740 AH, aged over ninety. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.53}

## For young visitors
Zaynab bint al-Kamal was a woman of Damascus who lived more than ninety years; students came from everywhere to hear the Prophet's hadith from her — even the famous traveller Ibn Battuta.
