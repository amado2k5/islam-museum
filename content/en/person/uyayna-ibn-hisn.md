## Summary
Chief of the Banu Fazara and of Ghatafan in Najd. He led Ghatafan with the confederates at the Battle of the Trench, then embraced Islam in the year of the Conquest. The Prophet gave him one hundred camels at [[place/al-jirana|al-Ji'rana]], from the share of [[concept/al-muallafa-qulubuhum|those whose hearts are to be reconciled]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/136}.

## His stances
Uyayna was brave and obeyed among his people. He was nicknamed "the foolish one whom his people obey", for his large following despite his hastiness. The Prophet gave him a large gift at al-Ji'rana to win over his people, Fazara {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/136}. He apostatised with Tulayha ibn Khuwaylid in the Ridda wars, was then captured and brought to Abu Bakr in Medina. He repented, embraced Islam a second time, his Islam was good, and he took part in the conquests {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/136}.

## For young visitors
A strong tribal chief from Najd who was followed by thousands of horsemen. The Prophet gave him many camels to win his heart towards peace. After the Ridda he repented and became a Muslim.

## With the delegation of Banu Tamim
Ibn Ishaq gives his lineage as Uyayna ibn Hisn ibn Hudhayfa ibn Badr al-Fazari. He mentions that he was with [[event/delegation-of-banu-tamim|the delegation of Banu Tamim]] when they came in the year 9 AH, and that he and [[person/al-aqra-ibn-habis|al-Aqra ibn Habis]] had both witnessed with the Prophet the Conquest of Mecca, Hunayn and al-Ta'if. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/207}

## The raid on Banu al-Anbar
Ibn Ishaq counts among the expeditions "the raid of Uyayna ibn Hisn ibn Hudhayfa ibn Badr against Banu al-Anbar of Banu Tamim". The Prophet sent him; he attacked them, took booty and captives. Then a delegation from Tamim came, and the Prophet freed some of the captives and ransomed others. See [[event/sariyya-of-uyayna-to-banu-tamim|the sariyya]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/269}

## The dispute over the blood of Amir ibn al-Adbat
At Hunayn he disputed before the Prophet with al-Aqra ibn Habis over the blood of Amir ibn al-Adbat al-Ashja'i. Muhallam ibn Jaththama had killed Amir in [[event/raid-of-ibn-abi-hadrad-on-batn-idam|the sariyya of Ibn Abi Hadrad]]. Uyayna was then chief of Ghatafan. He swore that he would not let the matter drop until the women of the killer's side knew the same burning grief that the killing had brought upon Amir's women. The Prophet offered blood-money of one hundred camels, and they accepted it in the end. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/276}

## For Explorers
Uyayna ibn Hisn, chief of Banu Fazara and Ghatafan in Najd; he led Ghatafan at the Trench battle with the Confederates, then entered Islam in the year of the conquest and the Prophet gave him a hundred camels at al-Ji'rana in the reconciled-hearts share. Brave and obeyed among his people, he was called 'the obeyed fool of his people' for his many followers despite haste; the Prophet's large gift aimed to win his tribe Fazara. He turned with Tulayha ibn Khuwaylid in the Ridda wars, was taken and brought to Abu Bakr in Medina, repented and entered Islam again well, and shared in the openings. Ibn Ishaq gives his line as Uyayna ibn Hisn ibn Hudhayfa ibn Badr al-Fazari, and says he was with Banu Tamim's delegation in 9 AH, having witnessed the conquest of Mecca, Hunayn and Taif with the Prophet alongside al-Aqra' ibn Habis.
