## Summary
Yamin al-Dawla Bahram Shah was a sultan of [[polity/ghaznavid-empire|the Ghaznavid state]] at Ghazna, one of three sons of Mas'ud III who ruled in turn after him: Shirzad, Arslan Shah and Bahram Shah {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.155, scan159}. Ibn al-Athir counts his reign as thirty-six years {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.9 p.391}.
## Sanjar and the Ghurids
Hasan Ibrahim records that in Dhu al-Qa'da 529 AH the Seljuk sultan [[person/sanjar|Sanjar]] doubted Bahram Shah's loyalty and his keeping of promises and heard that he wronged the people, so he marched on Ghazna. Bahram Shah begged his pardon; Sanjar promised it if he came and returned to obedience, but when Bahram Shah drew near the camp fear took hold of him and he fled. Sanjar entered Ghazna, collected its taxes and then, after letters of excuse, gave the city back and reached Balkh in Shawwal 530 {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.156, scan160}.

Twelve years later, the same account says, Bahram Shah plotted the killing of his son-in-law Qutb al-Din Muhammad al-Ghuri. In 543 AH (1148 CE) the dead man's brothers Sayf al-Din Suri and 'Ala' al-Din Husayn drove him from Ghazna to India; he came back when the roads filled with snow and he was invited, seized Suri and had him paraded and then hanged or crucified {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.156, scan160}. 'Ala' al-Din Husayn then punished the people of Ghazna in 550 AH (1155 CE), three years after Bahram Shah's death, and sacked the city for three days {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.158, scan162}.
## Death and dates
Ibn al-Athir records his death in Rajab in his annals of 548 AH, and describes him as just, of good conduct and a friend and patron of scholars, who gathered books and had them read before him and understood them; his son Khusrawshah succeeded him {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir OpenITI text v.9 p.391}. Hasan Ibrahim ends his reign at 547 AH (1152 CE) {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.155, scan159}. The museum's date field carries 1157, taken from the Encyclopaedia of Islam {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2}. The three do not agree, and none is preferred here.
