## Overview
[[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]], Sakhr ibn Harb ibn Umayya al-Qurashi al-Umawi, "Commander of the Faithful", as Ibn Hajar calls him. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1431} He was born five years before the mission, it is also said seven years before, and it is also said thirteen; "the first is more famous". {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1431} He was the son of [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] and [[person/hind-bint-utba|Hind bint Utba]], and his sister was [[person/umm-habiba-bint-abi-sufyan|Umm Habiba]], mother of the believers. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432}

## His appearance and companionship
Abu Nu'aym said that he was among the eloquent scribes, forbearing and dignified. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1431} Khalid ibn Ma'dan said that he was tall, fair and bald-headed. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1431} "He accompanied the Prophet and wrote for him." {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1431} Al-Mada'ini said that Zayd ibn Thabit wrote the revelation, and that Mu'awiya wrote for the Prophet in matters between him and the Arabs. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432} In the Musnad of Ahmad, and in its origin in Muslim, Ibn Abbas relates that the Prophet said: "Call Mu'awiya to me", and he was his scribe. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432}

## From command to caliphate
Ibn Hajar summarises: [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] appointed him over Syria after his brother Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan, and Uthman confirmed him. "Then he persisted and did not give the oath to Ali; then he fought him and became independent in Syria; then he added Egypt to it; then he called himself caliph after the two arbitrators." Then he became sole ruler when [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|al-Hasan]] made peace with him and the people united around him, "so that year was called the year of unity." {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1431} {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432} For the details see [[event/battle-of-siffin|Siffin]] and [[event/arbitration-of-siffin|the arbitration]]. Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan said: "Ibn Hind lived twenty years as an amir and twenty years as a caliph"; Ibn Hajar comments that this is a loose statement, since he did not complete twenty years as caliph. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432}

## What was said about him
In the Sahih of al-Bukhari, from Ikrima: I said to Ibn Abbas that Mu'awiya prayed a single rak'a of witr, and he said: "He is a jurist"; in another version: "He accompanied the Messenger of God." {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432} From Ibn Abbas: "I have never seen anyone sweeter to kingship than Mu'awiya", and when Umar looked at him he would say: "This is the Kisra of the Arabs." {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432} In the version of Abu Ya'la, the Prophet said to him: "O Mu'awiya, if you are given command, fear God and be just"; its chain includes Suwayd, of whom there is criticism, and al-Bayhaqi relates it through another channel. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432}

## Those who transmitted from him
He transmitted from the Prophet, from Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and his sister Umm Habiba. From the Companions, Ibn Abbas, Jarir al-Bajali, Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj, al-Sa'ib ibn Yazid, Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr, al-Nu'man ibn Bashir and others transmitted from him; and from the great Successors, Marwan ibn al-Hakam, Abdallah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal, Qays ibn Abi Hazim, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib and Abu Idris al-Khawlani. {cite:ibn-hajar-isaba p.1432}

## For researchers

Other Sunni sources have not yet been added, nor the views of non-Sunnis on Mu'awiya and his wars with Ali. ## In al-Tabari
His name appears in the history of the Muslims in Syria: he was governor of Caesarea in the events of the nineteenth year, according to the accounts of Abu Ma'shar and al-Waqidi. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.4 p.102} In the index of the fourth volume, there is mention of Ali sending Jarir ibn Abdallah to him calling him to obedience, and of Siffin and the fighting over the water. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.4 p.584} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl}
