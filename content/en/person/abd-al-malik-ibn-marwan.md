## Overview
Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, an Umayyad caliph whose governor was al-Hajjaj, and who died in 86 AH. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.6 p.338} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.6 p.418}

## Accession and death
According to al-Mas'udi, he was given the oath of allegiance on the night of Sunday, the first of Ramadan 65 AH, and he died in Damascus on Saturday, fourteen days into Shawwal 86 AH, aged sixty-six. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.86-87} Al-Salabi says that al-Walid led the funeral prayer and that he was buried between the Jabiya Gate and the Small Gate. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.115-128}

## His character as al-Mas'udi describes it
Al-Mas'udi says that he loved poetry, boasting and panegyric, that stinginess predominated in him, and that he was bold in shedding blood. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.86-87}

## Al-Hajjaj and Ibn al-Ash'ath
Al-Tabari reports that the followers of Ibn al-Ash'ath deposed him, saying that deposing al-Hajjaj meant deposing Abd al-Malik. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.6 p.338} Al-Mas'udi reports that he wrote to al-Hajjaj asking him to describe the rebellion to him, and that when news of Ibn al-Ash'ath's deposition reached him, he went up the pulpit and prayed that God would give the sword of the people of al-Sham power over the people of Iraq. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.111}

## The killing of Amr ibn Sa'id al-Ashdaq
Al-Mas'udi reports that Amr ibn Sa'id al-Ashdaq was killed in 70 AH after correspondence between them, each seeking power, and that the reports differ on how he was killed. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.95-97}

## His sermon in Medina
Al-Mas'udi reports that when he went on pilgrimage he ordered the distribution of gifts, and a purse was brought out marked as alms. The people of Medina refused to accept it, because their allowances came from the fay' (property taken without battle). He then addressed them, reminding them of the governorship of Umar and Uthman and of the day of al-Harra. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.113}

## In his administration
Al-Salabi says that he was the first to set aside a day for hearing complaints of injustice, that he fixed the dowry at four hundred dinars as an upper limit, and that he upheld the judgements of Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr and did not overturn them. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.27-28} He would instruct his commanders to beware of night raids and to send scouts ahead of every army, and he consulted people before sending an army to the land of the Romans. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.115-128}

## For Sprouts
Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan was an Umayyad caliph who ruled about twenty years in the seventh century. He reunited the caliphate after years of civil war, built the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, and made the coinage and records Arabic. He died in 705 CE.

## For Explorers
Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, fifth Umayyad caliph (65–86 AH / 685–705 CE), inherited a broken caliphate: he retook Iraq and the Hijaz after Ibn al-Zubayr's defeat by al-Hajjaj in 73 AH and reunited the empire a second time. His reign built the state: he Arabized the government registers from Persian and Greek, struck the first imageless Islamic coins and then the famous gold dinar around 77 AH, built the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem in 72 AH, and created the postal and road systems. His strongest governor was al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf in Iraq and the east, who founded Wasit in his day. Abd al-Malik died in 86 AH (705 CE) and was succeeded by his son al-Walid.

## For Scholars
Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan ibn al-Hakam (65–86 AH / 685–705 CE), fifth Umayyad caliph and founder of the dynasty's second state. He came to power after Marwan ibn al-Hakam's death with the caliphate torn: Ibn al-Zubayr in Mecca, Kharijites and Shia in Iraq, the Romans on the front. He retook Iraq with Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr killed in 71 AH, then al-Hajjaj took Mecca in 73 AH and the caliphate was reunited; al-Hajjaj was made governor of Iraq and the east, founding Wasit and crushing revolts by force. His policies made the model state: Arabization of the registers — records switched from Persian and Greek to Arabic — striking the imageless Islamic dinar and dirham around 77 AH, building the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem 69–72 AH which historians read as both a rival sanctuary to Ibn al-Zubayr's and a proclamation of Islam, and organizing the post, caravans and the administration that led to his son al-Walid's era of peak conquests. He died in 86 AH; historians treat his reign as the foundation of the Marwanid state.
