## Summary
Murad Wilfried Hofmann (6 July 1931 – January 2020) was a German diplomat, jurist and author. Born into a Catholic family at Aschaffenburg, he served in the German Foreign Service from 1961 to 1994, embraced Islam in 1980 while an active diplomat, and became a widely read writer on Islam in the West. {cite:aboutislam-murad-hofmann-obituary-2020 notice} {cite:radiance-murad-hofmann-1931-2020 biography}

## Background
He came from an intellectual family said to be related to the Dada co-founder Hugo Ball. He began university studies in 1950 at Union College in Schenectady, New York, took an LL.M. at Harvard Law School in 1960, and received a doctorate in jurisprudence from the University of Munich in 1975 with a thesis on contempt of court by publication in American and German law. {cite:radiance-murad-hofmann-1931-2020 biography} {cite:wikipedia-murad-hofmann-en Early life}

## The path to Islam
Three texts give 1980 as the year he embraced Islam; none gives a date within the year or a place. The English article, citing an IslamOnline biography, lists three reasons: his experience of the Algerian War of Independence, his interest in Islamic art and his view that Pauline Christian doctrine contains contradictions; it adds that the conversion drew some controversy because of his standing in the German government. The reasons are the biography's, not the museum's. Radiance Weekly says he "reverted" and gives no details of the step. {cite:aboutislam-murad-hofmann-obituary-2020 notice} {cite:radiance-murad-hofmann-1931-2020 biography} {cite:wikipedia-murad-hofmann-en Conversion}

## After embracing Islam
He served in the German Foreign Service for 33 years, from 1961 to 1994: first in Algeria as a specialist on nuclear defence, then as Director of Information for NATO in Brussels (1983–1987), ambassador to Algeria (1987–1990) and ambassador to Morocco (1990–1994). He became an honorary member and adviser of the Central Council of Muslims in Germany and signed the open letter *A Common Word Between Us and You*. His books include *Tagebuch eines deutschen Muslims* (1985), *Der Islam als Alternative* (1992, published in English as *Islam: The Alternative*), *Reise nach Mekka* (1996, *Journey to Makkah*) and a revision of Max Henning's German Qur'an translation (1998). In 2008 he published a book in Arabic with Abd al-Majid al-Sharafi on the future of Islam in the West and the East. {cite:aboutislam-murad-hofmann-obituary-2020 notice} {cite:radiance-murad-hofmann-1931-2020 biography} {cite:wikipedia-murad-hofmann-en Publications}

## Difficulties and reactions
The sources read do not report how his family reacted. The one reaction they record at the time of his conversion is public: according to the English article, the step drew some controversy because of his prominence in the German government. On his death the chairman of the Central Council of Muslims in Germany, Aiman Mazyek, called it a great loss to Muslims and non-Muslims and called him a role model for many people. {cite:wikipedia-murad-hofmann-en Conversion} {cite:radiance-murad-hofmann-1931-2020 biography}

## Criticism and debate
No criticism of him or his writing appears in the sources read. A master's dissertation on his views of the Western worldview on religion was submitted at the International Islamic University Malaysia in January 2016, which shows that his work has been studied. {cite:radiance-murad-hofmann-1931-2020 biography}
