## Summary
Georges Marçais wrote *Les poteries et faïences de Bougie* (Constantine 1916), part III of his series *Contribution à l'étude de la céramique musulmane*. {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 1} In it he refers to his earlier study of the pottery and faience of the Qal'a of the Banu Hammad and to his book *Les Arabes en Berbérie*. {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 6} {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 10} {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 30}

## Method
- He contrasts Bougie with the Qal'a of the Banu Hammad: the Qal'a's pottery is securely dated because the town was born, grew and lost its importance in less than a century and never rose again, whereas Bougie was a port held by many masters and always in contact with the Mediterranean, so its fragments come from varied origins. {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 9} {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 10}
- He therefore does not apply his Qal'a method but studies each group separately by form, technique and decoration, compares it with foreign ceramics, and presents his hypotheses as hypotheses. {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 10}
- He calls for methodical excavations in the town. {cite:marcais-poteries-faiences-bougie-1916 p. 9}
