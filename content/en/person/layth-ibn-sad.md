## Overview
Al-Layth ibn Sa'd al-Fahmi al-Misri, jurist and hadith scholar of Egypt in the second century AH. The sources say that he studied under [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]] and wrote much of his learning. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981}

## His death
Al-Shirazi relays that he died in mid-Sha'ban 175 AH. A variant reading in the edition's margin gives "ninety-five", so the date needs an additional source. {cite:tabaqat-fuqaha-abbas-1981}

## The fatwa on Cyprus
Al-Baladhuri relates that Abd al-Malik ibn Salih, governor of the frontiers, wrote to him and to the jurists of his age, asking about the people of Cyprus after they had done something new. His fatwa was that they were accused of cheating the Muslims and advising the Romans. Their covenant should be annulled for them, and they should be given a year in which to deliberate. Whoever wished to join the lands of the Muslims under protection was accepted; whoever wished to go to the lands of the Romans might go; and whoever stayed in the war remained in his state. He cited a verse on annulling a covenant when treachery is feared. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} See [[event/fuqaha-consulta-on-cyprus|the consultation of the jurists on Cyprus]].

## His books and school
Ibn al-Nadim says that he was one of the companions of Malik and followed his school, and then chose for himself. He used to correspond with Malik and ask him questions. He wrote a *History* and a book of *Questions on Jurisprudence*. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.281}
