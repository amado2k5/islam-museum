## Summary
Ubayd Allah ibn Umar al-Qawariri was a jurist from Baghdad, mentioned in the account of the [[event/mihna-of-the-quran|Mihna]] as one of those who were tested {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 490,492-493}.

## In the Mihna
Al-Suyuti relates that he refused during the test and was shackled, then answered on the third occasion. In al-Ma'mun's letter to his governor, al-Ma'mun criticised him for accepting bribes and favours. This judgement is attributed to al-Ma'mun's letter, as al-Suyuti transmits it {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 490,492-493}.

## Teachers and students

He was Abu Sa'id al-Jashmi, their freedman, a Basran who settled in Baghdad. He transmitted from Hammad ibn Zayd, Abu Awana, Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Hushaym, Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan, Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Ghundar and Khalid ibn al-Harith. Among those who transmitted from him were Abu Dawud al-Sijistani, Abu Zur'a and Abu Hatim al-Razi, Ibrahim al-Harbi, Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Abu al-Qasim al-Baghawi, among others. He said that Ahmad ibn Hanbal wrote two hadiths from him while he was in prison. Yahya ibn Ma'in said: "al-Qawariri is trustworthy." Ahmad ibn Sayyar said: "I have not seen the like of Musaddad in Basra, or of al-Qawariri in Baghdad, or of Sadaqa in Marw" {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib j.9 p.320-321}.

## The merit of congregation in a dream

He recounted that he once missed the night prayer in congregation because of a guest. He went round the tribes of Basra and found that people had already prayed, so he prayed it twenty-seven times, seeking the merit of congregation. Then he saw riders in a dream, and the last of them said to him: "Do not tire your horse; you will not catch up with us, for we prayed the night prayer in congregation" {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib j.9 p.321}.

The data do not record his birth or death year.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] mentions Ubayd Allah ibn Umar al-Qawariri, whom he calls a teacher of al-Bukhari and Muslim, and relates that al-Qawariri said he never missed the congregational prayer except one night when a guest came and kept him from the night prayer. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep06 00:09:30} He went round the mosques of Basra and found that people had prayed; remembering the reports of twenty-five and twenty-seven degrees for the congregation, he took the highest, prayed the night prayer twenty-seven times and slept, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep06 00:10:30} and dreamt he was racing riders on horseback, and one of them turned and told him he would not catch them, because he had not prayed the night prayer in congregation. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep06 00:11:00} In the same episode al-Heweny mentions a report of twenty-one degrees and says its authenticity is doubtful. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep06 00:10:00} The same report, with the twenty-seven prayers and the riders, is in «Tarikh Baghdad», as given above on this page. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib j.9 p.321}
