## Summary
Al-Khirniq bint Haffan was a pre-Islamic Arab poet; [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']] says she rose to fame a little after the War of al-Basus, and that most of her poetry mourns her brother [[person/tarafa-ibn-al-abd|Tarafa ibn al-Abd]], one of the poets of the Mu'allaqat, and then her husband Amr ibn Marthad. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.352} In a note he cites her diwan, in Abu Amr ibn al-Ala's transmission, as calling her al-Khirniq bint Badr ibn Haffan, Tarafa's half-sister through their mother. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.352}

## The Day of Qulab
He relates that her husband Amr ibn Marthad, chief of Banu Marthad, raided Banu Amir ibn Sa'sa'a jointly with Amr ibn Abd Allah al-Ashall, won and filled his hands with booty, then insisted on passing Mount Qulab in the territory of Banu Asad despite his partner's warning, so that al-Ashall turned off with his men toward al-Yamama. Amr fell upon Banu Asad, who fled at first, then turned back on him at their soothsayer's urging, killed him and routed his companions and sons. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.352} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.353}

## Her elegies
She mourned her husband, her son Alqama and his two brothers in the poem that opens "May my people not be far away", calling them poison to their foes and ruin to the slaughter-camels, men who stood in every battle and wore their waist-wraps with honour; she also lamented Bishr ibn Amr, and mourned her husband. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.353} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.354} Kahhala gathers her verse from Cheikho's Shawa'ir al-Jahiliyya, al-Qali's Amali, her diwan, Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan and Taj al-Arus. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.354}

## For young visitors
Al-Khirniq was an Arab poet of long ago, sister of the poet Tarafa; she lost her brother, her husband and her son in wars, and wrote sad poems praising them.
