## Summary
Askia Ishaq II (c. 1550 - 1592) was the last effective ruler of the Songhai Empire, reigning from 1588 to 1591. A son of Askia Dawud, he commanded the Songhai army at the Battle of Tondibi, where Moroccan firearms destroyed the empire's power; deposed in exile, he was killed at Bilanga in April 1592. {cite:wikipedia-askia-ishaq-ii-en}

## Rise to power
Ishaq was the son of Askia Dawud and a Fula Zughrani concubine from the middle Niger — hence his byname Zughrani — and, in Hunwick's account, the first son born to Dawud after Dawud's accession in 1549, which is the basis of the conventional c. 1550 birth year the article gives, at Gao. He came to power on the death of his brother Askia Muhammad Bani in 1588, forestalling a court conspiracy and defeating the rebel Balma'a, the military governor of Kabara, whose army was marching on Gao; he was proclaimed Askia on 10 April 1588. {cite:wikipedia-askia-ishaq-ii-en}

## Reign
He purged dozens of rebel leaders from the western provinces, campaigned twice into the Gurma region south of the Niger, and was reputed kind, honest, generous and popular in Gao. But the empire he inherited was fractured. A royal slave who escaped from the Taghaza salt mines told Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur of Morocco how weakened Songhai was; al-Mansur's letter of December 1589 demanded the Taghaza revenues, and Ishaq's defiant answer failed to deter him. {cite:wikipedia-askia-ishaq-ii-en}

## Tondibi and the fall of Songhai
Al-Mansur sent an expedition under Judar Pasha — some 4,000 men in the Arabic article's count — across the Sahara in October 1590; it reached the Niger on 28 February 1591. Ishaq assembled more than 40,000 soldiers, but at the decisive Battle of Tondibi in March 1591 his army broke and fled before the Moroccans' gunpowder weapons. Gao was evacuated and then seized and looted, as were Timbuktu and Djenne. Ishaq sued for peace; al-Mansur instead sent reinforcements. Defeated again at Zanzan on 14 October 1591, Ishaq fled to Dendi, was deposed and sent into exile in Gurma. {cite:wikipedia-askia-ishaq-ii-en} {cite:wikipedia-askia-ishaq-ii-ar}

## Death
Seeking refuge at Bilanga, one of the towns he had campaigned against in his reign, he was killed there by a vengeful mob in April 1592 — the English article's infobox brackets his death as March/April 1592, citing Gomez and the Tarikh al-Fattash. The death year of 1595 sometimes listed for him is supported by none of the sources read for this record. {cite:wikipedia-askia-ishaq-ii-en}

## Religion
No source read for this record names a school for him. He ruled the deeply Islamised empire his great-grandfather Askia Muhammad had shaped, and his defiance of al-Mansur was conducted in the idiom of rival Muslim sovereigns.
