## Overview
Ibrahim ibn Ali ibn Yusuf al-Fayruzabadi al-Shirazi, Abu Ishaq, was the famous debating scholar. Born at Fayruzabad in Fars, he moved to Shiraz and studied under its scholars, then went on to Basra and to Baghdad in 415 AH, where he completed his study and research. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.50}

His brilliance in the sciences of the shari'a made him the reference of students and the mufti of the community in his age, renowned for the force of his argument in disputation; the vizier Nizam al-Mulk built him the Nizamiyya school on the bank of the Tigris, where he taught and which he directed. He lived poor and patient, good company, cheerful, eloquent and a composer of verse. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.50}

Among his many works are al-Tanbih and al-Muhadhdhab in fiqh, al-Tabsira on Shafi'i principles, Tabaqat al-Fuqaha', al-Luma' in usul al-fiqh and his commentary on it, and al-Mulakhkhas and al-Ma'una in disputation. He died at Baghdad in 476 AH (1083 CE) and the Abbasid al-Muqtadi prayed over him. {cite:al-aalam v.1 p.50}
