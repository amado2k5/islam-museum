# Ahmed Ben Bella

Ahmed Ben Bella (1916–2012) was an Algerian politician and revolutionary, and the first president of the Republic of Algeria. He was born at Maghnia in western Algeria to a family that had come from Marrakech, began his schooling in the Qur'anic zawiya where his father was muqaddam, then passed to the French school; he played professional football at Marseille and served in the French army during the Second World War. {cite:source/wikipedia-ahmed-ben-bella-ar} {cite:source/wikipedia-ahmed-ben-bella-en}

## A leader of the war of independence

Ben Bella joined the Movement for the Triumph of Democratic Liberties and then its paramilitary Special Organisation; the French arrested him in 1950, he escaped from prison in 1952 and took refuge in Cairo, where he became one of the FLN's external leaders and a principal channel of its arms. When the revolution broke out in 1954 he was among its leading figures; in 1956 France diverted the aircraft carrying him and held him until the ceasefire of 1962. {cite:source/wikipedia-ahmed-ben-bella-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-ahmed-ben-bella-ar}

## Presidency, fall and death

Ben Bella became head of government of independent Algeria on 27 September 1962, and on 15 September 1963 its first president, elected with 99.6 per cent of the vote; he steered the country towards Arab socialism and fought the Sand War with Morocco in 1963. On 19 June 1965 his defence minister Houari Boumédiène overthrew him in a bloodless coup, and he spent long years under house arrest and then in exile before returning to Algeria. He died at his family home in Algiers on 11 April 2012; eight days of national mourning were declared, and he was buried in the Martyrs' Square of El Alia Cemetery. {cite:source/wikipedia-ahmed-ben-bella-en} {cite:source/wikipedia-ahmed-ben-bella-ar}
