## Summary
Umama bint al-Harith, in the entry of [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Kahhala]] in [[work/alam-al-nisa-kahhala|A'lam al-Nisa']], was a pre-Islamic woman of eloquence, judgement and intelligence; when al-Harith ibn Amr, king of Kinda, sought the hand of her daughter Umm Iyas bint Awf ibn Muhallim al-Shaybani and the girl's father married her to him, her mother counselled her on her wedding night. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.78} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.79}

## The counsel
She begins by saying that if counsel could be left to one already well-bred she would leave it, but it is a reminder for the heedless and a help for the wise; she tells her daughter she has left the nest in which she grew for a lair she does not know and a companion she is not used to, and then lists "ten qualities" that will be a treasure and a memorial for her: companionship with contentment, good listening and obedience, care for what his eye falls on and his nose perceives, attention to his mealtimes and quiet when he sleeps, keeping his house and wealth and caring for his household and dependants, never betraying his secret nor disobeying his order, and avoiding gaiety when he is troubled and gloom when he is glad. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.79} She ends: you will not reach what you love until you put his pleasure before your own and his wishes before yours, in what you like and dislike. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.79} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.80}

## The outcome
Kahhala says the daughter was brought to her husband, rose high in his regard, and bore him the seven kings who ruled Yemen after him; he takes the story from Fara'id al-La'al and Ibn Sallam's al-Dhakha'ir. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.80}

## For young visitors
Umama was an Arab mother of long ago who gave her daughter much advice on her wedding day — contentment, keeping secrets, kindness — and her advice became famous among the Arabs.
