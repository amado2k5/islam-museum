## Summary
Mihyar ibn Marzuya, the Persian katib of Daylam, famous poet; a Zoroastrian who converted at the hands of al-Sharif al-Radi Abu'l-Hasan al-Musawi — his shaykh, on whose hands he fashioned his verse; a poet of solid utterance, foremost among his contemporaries, with a large diwan filling four volumes; he used to attend the Mansur mosque at Baghdad on Fridays where his diwan was read out; al-Khatib al-Baghdadi and Ibn Bassam of the Dhakhira praised him. {cite:wafayat-ayan}

## From his reports
The Muntazam says he converted in 394 and became an extreme Rafidi — with charm in his verse, though he speaks of the Companions unfittingly; Abu'l-Qasim Ibn Burhan told him: 'Mihyar, your Islam moved you in the Fire from one corner to another'; his house was at Darb Riyah in the Karkh — he had hidden a thousand dinars in his garment's belt, his servant betrayed him to Jalal al-Dawla, who arrested then released him; he died in Jumada II 428. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.436-437}
