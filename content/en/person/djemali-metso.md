## Summary

Djemali Metso ⁦(Greek: Μέτσο Τζεμαλή; 10 March 1937 – 20 December 2019)⁩ was the Mufti of Komotini — the state-recognised religious leader of the Muslim Turkish minority in Greece's Rhodope prefecture, Western Thrace — for roughly three decades, until a 2018 law retired him compulsorily. The Muslim 500 lists him among its preachers and spiritual guides for a community of some 150,000 Muslims who continued to regard him as their spiritual head after his removal. {cite:muslim500-djemali-metso} {cite:elwiki-djemali-metso}

## Early life and formation

He was born on 10 March 1937 in the village of Echinos, in the prefecture of Xanthi, the son of a village religious teacher, Hafuz Idris Metso, who taught him his first letters. After primary school he memorised the Qur'an and earned the title of hafiz, then continued his religious studies under the teacher Hafiz Ali Resat in Komotini, and later at Arriana in the Rhodope prefecture where he settled permanently. He graduated from the medrese religious school at Echinos in 1957. {cite:elwiki-djemali-metso}

In 1966, on the invitation of King Faisal of Saudi Arabia, he went to Medina to study at the Islamic University there, taking the higher theological sciences; he graduated in 1976 and returned to Greece in 1978 after twelve years abroad. {cite:elwiki-djemali-metso}

## Preacher, then mufti

Back in Western Thrace he travelled the villages of Rhodope prefecture preaching, until in 1985 he was appointed acting mufti ⁦(topotiritis)⁩ of Komotini, and five years later confirmed as mufti by presidential decree. The office is a state position: the mufti supervised the mosques and appointed or dismissed their religious officials, oversaw the waqf properties of the Rhodope region, and held judicial competence in family and inheritance matters among Muslims under Islamic law — a jurisdiction surviving in Greece from the treaty framework that protects the Western Thrace minority. {cite:elwiki-djemali-metso}

## Compulsory retirement and death

In 2018 a Greek law retired him and other senior muftis of Western Thrace compulsorily — part of a long-running dispute between the Greek state and the minority over whether muftis should be state-appointed or community-elected. Metso appealed the decision to Greece's Council of State. He died of cardiac causes in Alexandroupoli on 20 December 2019, aged 82, and was buried at Arriana in a funeral attended by a crowd of the minority's faithful together with Greek state and church representatives. {cite:elwiki-djemali-metso} {cite:muslim500-djemali-metso}

## Assessment

Greek press remembered him as a mufti of low tones and excellent relations with the wider local society, whose long Medina formation gave him theological weight across the minority. His Muslim 500 entry captures the attachment of a community that 'looks upon him as not only a mufti but as a spiritual leader' — a judgement borne out by the mixed official and popular presence at his burial. {cite:muslim500-djemali-metso} {cite:elwiki-djemali-metso}
