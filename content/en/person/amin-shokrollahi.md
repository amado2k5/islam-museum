## Summary
Mohammad Amin Shokrollahi is an Iranian-German mathematician whose work in coding theory shapes how digital information moves across unreliable networks. The Muslim 500 places him in Science & Technology as the inventor of tornado codes and co-developer of raptor and chordal codes; Wikipedia records his professorship at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne ⁦(EPFL)⁩ and his IEEE Information Theory Paper Award of 2002. {cite:muslim500-amin-shokrollahi} {cite:wikipedia-amin-shokrollahi}

## Formation
Shokrollahi was born in Tehran in 1964. He studied at the University of Karlsruhe in Germany and took his doctorate at the University of Bonn in 1991 under Joachim von zur Gathen, working on algebraic complexity theory — the study of the smallest number of operations needed to compute polynomial functions. He held research posts in the United States, including at Bell Labs, before joining EPFL in Lausanne as a professor of mathematics and computer science. {cite:wikipedia-amin-shokrollahi}

## Codes that carry the internet
His best-known contribution belongs to the family of erasure codes: ways of encoding data so that a receiver can rebuild a message even when parts are lost in transit. With Michael Luby and collaborators he developed tornado codes, and later raptor codes — the first practical class of fountain codes, in which a sender produces an effectively endless stream of encoded packets and the receiver needs only to collect slightly more packets than the original data contained. These ideas now sit inside standards that carry video streaming, satellite broadcast and mobile data. {cite:wikipedia-amin-shokrollahi} {cite:muslim500-amin-shokrollahi}

## Enterprise
The mathematics moved into industry: Shokrollahi co-founded and served as chief technology officer of Digital Fountain, the company that commercialised fountain codes, which was later acquired by Qualcomm. He also founded Kandou Bus, a Lausanne company developing high-speed chip-to-chip communication based on his work in coding and signalling. {cite:wikipedia-amin-shokrollahi}

## Recognition
His honours include the IEEE Information Theory Society Paper Award ⁦(2002)⁩, the IEEE Eric E. Sumner Award for contributions to communication technology, and fellowship of the IEEE. The Muslim 500 counts him among the influential Muslims of the Science & Technology section — a recognition of ideas that live, invisibly, inside everyday data. {cite:muslim500-amin-shokrollahi} {cite:wikipedia-amin-shokrollahi}

## From Tehran to Lausanne
Shokrollahi's biography joins two scientific cultures: the Iranian mathematical tradition that produced his generation's stars, and the European university that gave his ideas an industrial life. His dual nationality — Iranian and German — and his long professorship in francophone Lausanne are recorded in the sources as facts of a career rather than as confessional markers; the museum notes them as context for a figure whom The Muslim 500 counts among the year's influential Muslims in science. {cite:wikipedia-amin-shokrollahi} {cite:muslim500-amin-shokrollahi}

## The invisible mathematics
The deeper significance of his work is its invisibility: the codes he helped invent are not an app users open but the reason a stream survives a bad connection, a satellite broadcast survives weather, a file survives a network's failures. When a standard embeds a code, the mathematics becomes infrastructure — and the mathematician's name disappears into the specification. That is the shape of Shokrollahi's fame: enormous inside coding theory, silent everywhere else. {cite:wikipedia-amin-shokrollahi}

## The mathematician as entrepreneur
Shokrollahi's record includes a chapter unusual for a pure mathematician: the founding of Digital Fountain, the company that commercialised his coding inventions before the standards bodies took them up. The episode matters for the museum's record not as business history but as a pattern — the mathematician who understands that a code is only useful when it is inside a product, and who builds the company that puts it there. The Muslim 500 lists him in science and technology for that combined career: the professor whose theorems became the plumbing of digital life. {cite:muslim500-amin-shokrollahi}
