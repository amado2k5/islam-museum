## Summary
In his [[work/tuzuk-i-jahangiri|memoirs]] for 1618, [[person/jahangir-mughal|Jahangir]] wrote that Abu'l Hasan the painter, titled Nadir al-Zaman, had drawn his accession as the frontispiece of the Jahangir-nama; that he had no rival or equal in his day; that there was no comparison between him and his father [[person/aqa-riza-jahangiri|Aqa Riza]]; and that the emperor had reared him until his art reached this rank. No other painter received such homage. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 84}

## What we know
A drawing of St John after an engraving by Albrecht Dürer, made by Abu'l Hasan in 1009 AH/1600–1601 "in my thirteenth year", dates his birth to 997 AH/1588–89. A portrait of him by [[person/daulat-painter|Daulat]] in the margins of the [[object/muraqqa-e-gulshan|Gulshan Album]] shows that he was left-handed. This is more than we know of any other major Mughal painter; the rest comes from his paintings. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 84}

## Style
His earliest fully mature work is a page in the British Library Anwar-i-Suhaili finished in 1610, signed humbly "dust of the threshold of Riza", beside pages by his father; already his lines follow the texture and fall of cloth rather than serve surface pattern, and his faces are individual and expressive. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 84} He used European prints for shading, and his later works are notable for mass and dark shadowed backgrounds. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 84} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 85} An Infant Prince (Shah Shuja) follows an English portrait type brought by Sir Thomas Roe in 1615. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 85} Milo Cleveland Beach counts his portraits among the most penetrating in Mughal art and finds no evidence that he painted beyond the first years of [[person/shah-jahan-mughal|Shah Jahan]]'s reign. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 85}
