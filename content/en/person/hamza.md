## Brief
Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the uncle of the Prophet and one of the fiercest horsemen of Quraysh. His Islam strengthened the first community, and his death at Uhud in 625 CE made him, in the eyes of Muslims, the Master of the Martyrs.

## Life
Close to the Prophet in age {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}, he was a hunter and one of the strong men of Mecca. The narrative tells that he embraced Islam around 616 CE out of anger at what he had found done to his nephew. His Islam was public, so that the opponents of the community feared it afterwards {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He migrated to Medina and fought with distinction at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]]. At [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], in March 625 CE, he was killed by [[person/wahshi-ibn-harb|Wahshi ibn Harb]], a slave who was freed on account of it {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. After the battle the dead were mutilated, and Hamza's body was among those so treated. The sources record this in a dry tone, and it stayed in memory {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## Legacy
The title that stayed with him in Muslim memory is "Master of the Martyrs" (sayyid al-shuhada), an uncle whose strength stood between his nephew and those who wished him harm.

## For young visitors
Hamza was the Prophet's uncle and his strongest protector. When he died in the battle, the whole community wept for him.


## The Prophet's foster-brother twice over (al-Raheeq al-Makhtum p.55)
[[person/thuwayba|Thuwayba]], freedwoman of Abu Lahab, nursed Hamza before she nursed the Prophet. Hamza was also nursed in [[group/banu-sad-ibn-bakr|Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr]], where his mother there nursed the Prophet one day while the Prophet was with [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima]]. So Hamza was the Prophet's foster-brother in two ways: through Thuwayba and through the woman of Sa'd {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.55} {cite:talqih-fuhum-ahl-al-athr p.4} {cite:zad-al-maad 1/19}. The book counts him the fifth of the ten sons of [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.52}.


## The expedition of Sif al-Bahr (al-Raheeq al-Makhtum p.197)
The Prophet put him in command of thirty men of the emigrants who were to intercept a caravan of Quraysh carrying [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]]. When the two groups met, Majdi ibn Amr al-Juhani came between them, and they did not fight. Hamza's banner was the first banner the Prophet tied ([[event/expedition-of-sif-al-bahr|the expedition of Sif al-Bahr]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.197}.
