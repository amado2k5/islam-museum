## Brief
Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib was an uncle of the Prophet and one of the most feared warriors of Quraysh. His conversion strengthened the early community; his death at Uhud in 625 CE made him, in tradition, the Master of the Martyrs.

## Life
Close in age to the Prophet {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}, Hamza was a hunter and one of Mecca's strong men. Tradition tells that he embraced Islam about 616 CE, in anger at the treatment of his nephew — his conversion openly announced, and the community's opponents thereafter more cautious {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}.

He migrated to Medina, fought with distinction at [[event/battle-of-badr]], and at [[event/battle-of-uhud]] in March 625 was killed by [[person/wahshi-ibn-harb]], a slave promised his freedom for the deed {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}. After the battle the victors mutilated the dead, and Hamza's body was among those dishonored — a wound the sources record soberly and the tradition never forgot {cite:watt-1956-medina}.

## Legacy
The title attached to him in Islamic memory is *Sayyid al-Shuhada*, "Master of the Martyrs" — an uncle whose strength stood between his nephew and those who wished him harm.

## For young visitors
Hamza was the Prophet's uncle and its strongest defender. When he died in battle, the whole community mourned him.
