## Summary
Muhammad ibn Ali al-Nasawi was known as Muhammad ibn Ulayyan. [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] gives him notice 649 near the end of the tenth volume of [[work/hilyat-al-awliya|Hilyat al-Awliya]], among the gnostics whose days he says he reached, and describes him as a man of high aspiration and manifest marvels. The notice is short. It names no teachers and gives no date, and everything in it is related by [[person/al-sulami|al-Sulami]] through one intermediary. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/376}

## Sayings
He said that renouncing this world is the key to desiring the next. He said that the signs and marvels of God's friends are their contentment with what ordinary people resent in the course of destiny. He defined manly virtue as guarding one's religion, protecting one's self, respecting what is inviolable for the believers, and being generous with what one has. He used to say: how can you not love One whose kindness never leaves you for the blink of an eye, and how can you claim to love One with whom you do not agree for the blink of an eye? {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 10/376}
