## Summary
Indian hadith scholar (1857–1911), a luminary of the Ahl-i Hadith in the subcontinent. He is famed for 'Awn al-Ma'bud 'ala Sunan Abi Dawud, now the most-used commentary on Abu Dawud, and for Ghayat al-Maqsud, Al-Ta'liq al-Mughni on the Muwatta of Muhammad al-Shaybani, and other editorial services. {cite:wikipedia-shams-al-haqq-azimabadi}

## Life and formation

Shams al-Haqq al-Azimabadi was born in 1857 in Azimabad (Patna), India, into a learned family, and studied under the Ahl-i Hadith scholars of India, among them Sayyid Nazir Husayn al-Dihlawi. {cite:wikipedia-shams-al-haqq-azimabadi}

He excelled in the sciences of hadith, narrator biography and accreditation, corresponded with the scholars of the two sanctuaries and Yemen, and became an authority on elevated chains and registers. {cite:wikipedia-shams-al-haqq-azimabadi}

## Works

His most famous work is 'Awn al-Ma'bud 'ala Sunan Abi Dawud, a commentary joining hadith benefits, narrators and fiqh with use of al-Khattabi's commentary and others; it has been printed repeatedly and become the most-used commentary on the Sunan. {cite:wikipedia-shams-al-haqq-azimabadi}

His other works include Ghayat al-Maqsud on the mursal reports of Abu Dawud, Al-Ta'liq al-Mughni on the Muwatta of Muhammad al-Shaybani, and biographical treatises on chains and ijazas. {cite:wikipedia-shams-al-haqq-azimabadi}

## Legacy

Azimabadi stands with Siddiq Hasan Khan and Nazir Husayn among the pillars of the Indian Ahl-i Hadith school that led the hadith-publishing renaissance of the nineteenth-century subcontinent. {cite:wikipedia-shams-al-haqq-azimabadi}

He died in 1911; 'Awn al-Ma'bud — printed in some editions with the supercommentary of his student Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyya of India — remains the standard teaching text on Sunan Abi Dawud in the madrasas of India, Pakistan and the Arab world. {cite:wikipedia-shams-al-haqq-azimabadi}
