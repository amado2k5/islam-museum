## Overview
Al-Ta'i' billah Abu Bakr Abd al-Karim ibn al-Muti', an Abbasid caliph from 363 to 381 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.624}

## The allegiance
He was given the allegiance when his father was stricken with paralysis and his tongue became heavy, on the advice of Subuktakin, the chamberlain of Izz al-Dawla. The matter was settled for him on Wednesday, the thirteenth of Dhu al-Qa'da 363 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.624}

Ibn al-Athir says that al-Muti' deposed himself, handed the affair to his son, and had the deposition witnessed on that day. He says the length of his caliphate was twenty-nine years and five months. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.468-469}

## In his time
Al-Dhahabi, as al-Suyuti quotes him, said that al-Muti' and his son were weak under the Buyids. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.624} Al-Suyuti says that the caliphate was never weaker in anyone's time than in his, and that no ruler's power was ever stronger than that of Adud al-Dawla. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.626-630}

The sources differ on the name of his mother and on whether he was the son of al-Muti' or of al-Mustakfi. This needs an additional source.
