## Summary
Abd Manaf ibn [[person/qusayy-ibn-kilab|Qusayy]] was honoured and led during his father's lifetime. In the division of offices between his sons and those of his brother Abd al-Dar, the watering (siqaya) and the feeding (rifada) went to him. From Abd Manaf came four branches: Abd Shams, Nawfal, al-Muttalib and Hashim {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.21} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31}.

## In Qusayy's lifetime
Abd Manaf had been honoured and had led during his father's lifetime. Abd al-Dar was Qusayy's firstborn, and Qusayy said to him: "I will certainly bring you into line with the people, even if they outrank you." He bequeathed to Abd al-Dar the duties he held for Quraysh: the Dar al-Nadwa, the custody of the House (hijaba), the banner (liwa'), the watering and the feeding {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/129-132}.

## After his death
When Qusayy died, his sons kept his affairs without dispute. But when Abd Manaf died, his sons competed with their cousins, the sons of Abd al-Dar, for these offices. Quraysh split into two camps, and fighting nearly broke out between them. They then agreed to a settlement and divided the offices: the watering and the feeding to Banu Abd Manaf, and the Dar al-Nadwa, the banner and the custody to Banu Abd al-Dar {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/129-132}. Then the lot fell to [[person/hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31}. Its details are in [[event/partition-of-meccan-offices|the division of the offices]].

## For young visitors
Abd Manaf was the son of Qusayy. From his house came Hashim and Abd al-Muttalib, who served the pilgrims house by house.
