## Summary
Suad Salih is an Azharite professor of jurisprudence and one of the few women to issue public fatwas in Egyptian media. Her name is attached to the campaign for a woman mufti and to fatwas that sparked wide debate. {cite:wikipedia-suad-salih}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Egypt in 1945, she was educated at al-Azhar to a doctorate in sharia, then taught comparative jurisprudence at al-Azhar University, serving as dean of the Faculty of Islamic and Arabic Studies. {cite:wikipedia-suad-salih}

## Career and works
She presented television fatwa programmes for years and called for women to hold the official state mufti office, arguing that issuing fatwas is scholarly work, not public authority. Her works include writings on women’s and family jurisprudence and university lectures in comparative fiqh. {cite:wikipedia-suad-salih}

## Method and contribution
Her project is a juristic reading within the tradition that widens women’s places in issuing fatwas and adjudication. Some of her fatwas (including one on suckling relations) drew controversy and official responses, which she defended as ijtihad; the museum reports the disagreement attributed, without verdict. {cite:wikipedia-suad-salih}
