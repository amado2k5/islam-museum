## Summary
Seyyed Hossein Nasr is an Iranian philosopher based in Washington, among the most influential Islamic voices in the Western academy, presenting Sufism and transcendent wisdom through the Traditionalist school. {cite:wikipedia-seyyed-hossein-nasr}

## Life and upbringing
Born in Tehran in 1933 to a scholarly family, he studied physics at MIT then history of science and philosophy at Harvard, before returning to Iran to teach and lead philosophical projects, settling in the United States after the revolution. {cite:wikipedia-seyyed-hossein-nasr}

## Career and works
He has authored dozens of books on traditional metaphysics, Islamic science and religious ecology, including Science and Civilization in Islam, Knowledge and the Sacred and The Heart of Islam. He founded journals and intellectual circles, presenting the West with an image of Islam grounded in perennial wisdom. {cite:wikipedia-seyyed-hossein-nasr}

## Method and contribution
His perennialist method sees unity of metaphysical essence behind religious diversity while affirming the Islamic character of his project. He is celebrated in comparative-wisdom studies and criticised by historians who see Traditionalism as reading into the texts and by conservatives over interreligious conciliation; the museum attributes these descriptions to their holders. {cite:wikipedia-seyyed-hossein-nasr}
