## Summary
Kitab al-Tashil li-Ulum al-Tanzil ("Making Easy the Sciences of Revelation") is the commentary of [[person/ibn-juzayy-al-kalbi-mufassir|Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Juzayy al-Kalbi]], whom the cover of its first printing calls "the shaykh, the imam, the learned hafiz and exegete, servant of the mighty Qur'an". {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-j2 p.4} It was first printed in Cairo in 1355 AH after collation with several manuscripts in the Royal Library. {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-j2 p.4} {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-ghafir p.4}

## Method
The opening of al-An'am shows Ibn Juzayy explaining words in short sentences, sometimes citing reports — such as [[person/kab-al-ahbar|Ka'b]]'s saying that the opening of al-An'am is the opening of the Torah — and setting out meanings: 'made' here means 'created', the darkness is night and the light is day, and the verse answers the Magians. {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-j2 p.5} He lists views with 'it is said' and then weighs them: of the two appointed terms, the first is death and the second the Day of Resurrection, or, it is said, the first is sleep and the second death; and of 'in the heavens and on earth', the first view is "preferable and more eloquent" while the second "gains support" from the context — see [[question/how-is-he-is-god-in-the-heavens-understood|How is 'He is God in the heavens and on earth' in Surat al-An'am understood?]]. {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-j2 p.5} He attends to grammar, saying that 'then' marks surprise in one place and in another orders the statements rather than the events. {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-j2 p.5}

## The 'notes'
Ibn Juzayy adds 'notes' (tanbih) on particular questions, among them one in al-A'raf on the differences of wording between that passage and al-Baqara in the story of the Children of Israel — 'gushed' and 'burst', 'enter' and 'dwell', 'eat' joined with 'and' or with 'so'; he quotes [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] that differing expressions do no harm where there is no contradiction, and notes that his teacher [[person/abu-jafar-ibn-al-zubayr|the master Abu Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr]], in Milak al-Ta'wil, and the author of al-Durra explained them with reasons, some strong and some weak. {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-j2 p.55}

## The edition used
The museum used the first printing (1355 AH) from the press of Mustafa Muhammad, owner of al-Maktaba al-Tijariyya al-Kubra in Egypt, with the Qur'an at the top of each page and the commentary below; part 2 (from al-An'am) and the part beginning with Surat Ghafir were read. {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-j2 p.4} {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-ghafir p.4} {cite:tashil-maktaba-tijariyya-1355-ghafir p.5}
