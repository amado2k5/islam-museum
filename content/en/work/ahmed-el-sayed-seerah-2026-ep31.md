## Summary

The lecturer places the years between the seventh and tenth of the mission against how Ibn Ishaq and later authors filled them with events, then reads Ibn Ishaq's list of Companions who came back from Abyssinia to Mecca. He argues that the rest stayed until after Hudaybiyya by the Prophet's decision or approval, tells the stories of the neighbour-protection (jiwar) of Uthman ibn Maz'un, Abu Salama and Abu Bakr, and defends Ibn Ishaq's standing as a hadith narrator against al-Waqidi's. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Ibn Ishaq's treatment of the years 7 to 10 and later attempts to date Meccan events

- He says the lessons now sit between the seventh and tenth years of the mission, after the siege of the valley, and that many biographers pass from the siege straight to year ten. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds that Ibn Ishaq did not leave these years empty but filled them with disputes and events, and he says Ibn Kathir noticed this and kept Ibn Ishaq's material in the same place. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that a Hanafi scholar from Sindh, Muhammad Hashim al-Sindi, tried in his book Badhl al-Quwwa to assign Meccan events to individual years, and he repeats that dating Meccan events precisely is hard. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates from that book that Dar al-Arqam was still standing in the author's time near the Safa hill, known as Dar al-Khayzuran after the mother of Harun al-Rashid who rebuilt it as a mosque. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that the same book places the news of Persian victory over the Romans, the wager of Ubayy ibn Khalaf with Abu Bakr, the war of Bu'ath and the splitting of the moon around the eighth year, and records almost nothing for the seventh and ninth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He concludes that the Meccan period should not be read only through its large dated events, and that an event can be placed in an approximate period without forcing a year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Open questions on the return of the Abyssinia emigrants

- He says that Ibn Ishaq places the return from Abyssinia in the years of the siege, while a common view puts the return right after the first emigration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds that two readings are possible: an early return after the first emigration, or, as Ibn Ishaq's arrangement suggests, the main return during the siege years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says that some individuals returned between the main return to Mecca and the final return to Medina, and that identifying them needs separate research. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Ibn Ishaq's list of those who returned from Abyssinia, by clan

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says the emigrants heard a report that Mecca had accepted Islam, came back, learned the report was false, and entered only under a protector's guarantee or in secret. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He explains that the list gives those who stayed in Mecca until the Hijra and fought at Badr, those held back, and those who died in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that the list runs by clan from the closest to the Prophet to the farthest, and that Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib are absent because none of them had gone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates the names from Banu Abd Shams: Uthman ibn Affan with Ruqayya, Abu Hudhayfa ibn Utba with Sahla bint Suhayl, and, as an ally, Abdullah ibn Jahsh; from Banu Nawfal, Utba ibn Ghazwan; from Banu Asad, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam; from Banu Abd al-Dar, Mus'ab ibn Umayr and Suwaybit ibn Sa'd; and from Banu Abd, Tulayb ibn Umayr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates from Banu Zuhra: Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, and as allies al-Miqdad and Abdullah ibn Mas'ud; from Banu Makhzum: Abu Salama with Umm Salama, Shammas ibn Uthman, Salama ibn Hisham, held in Mecca until after Badr, Uhud and the Trench, and Ayyash ibn Abi Rabia, whom his half-brothers took back to Mecca and held. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says Ammar ibn Yasir is listed as an ally with doubt over whether he went to Abyssinia, and that he judges it more likely he did not. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates from Banu Jumah: Uthman ibn Maz'un with his son al-Sa'ib and the brothers Qudama and Abdullah; from Banu Sahm: Khunays ibn Hudhafa and Hisham ibn al-As, held in Mecca until after the three battles; from Banu Adi: Amir ibn Rabia, an ally, with his wife Layla bint Abi Hathma. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates from Banu Amir ibn Luayy: Abdullah ibn Makhrama, Abdullah ibn Suhayl, who was held back and later slipped to the Muslim army at Badr, Abu Sabra ibn Abi Ruhm, Sawda bint Zam'a with her husband al-Sakran ibn Amr, who died in Mecca before the Hijra, and the ally Sa'd ibn Khawla. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates from Banu al-Harith ibn Fihr, the clan farthest from the Prophet: Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, Amr ibn al-Harith and Suhayl ibn Bayda, with Amr ibn Abi Sarh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says the total who came to Mecca was thirty-three men, out of about eighty-two, with six women named, which leaves about fifty men and about ten women, roughly sixty people, still in Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Why the rest stayed in Abyssinia until after Hudaybiyya

- He says the pressing question is why the others did not return to Medina after the Prophet's Hijra, given that they were staying in a land of unbelief while an abode of Islam existed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds, with caution, that they stayed by the Prophet's direct order or at least his approval, and that they returned in the seventh year, after Hudaybiyya, shortly before Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He argues that until Hudaybiyya the survival of the Muslims in Medina was not secure, and that the Trench was in his view more dangerous than Uhud because the allies could have ended the Muslim presence. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates the prayer of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh at the Trench, and reads it as the sign that the major rounds of fighting with Quraysh were ending. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He suggests that those in Abyssinia may have been kept as a reserve of the faith's bearers away from danger, and that after the treaty they could safely join the main body. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says that after Hudaybiyya the letters to rulers began, followed by Khaybar, Mu'ta and Tabuk, so the strategic situation changed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates the scene at Hudaybiyya where the Prophet had his title and the opening formula changed in the document, and says this shows the Prophet put the benefit of the treaty above formalities. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He notes that some scholars point to the phrase erased from the treaty being present in the chapter named al-Fath, and he says this cannot be asserted firmly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds that the decision both to return and to stay came from the Prophet, since those in Abyssinia could hardly have judged the moment themselves, having once acted on a false report. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Lessons from the return, and an estimate of the number of Muslims

- He holds that both the early returners and the late ones carry lessons, including risk-taking for a benefit and attention to the stage of the struggle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says Ja'far ibn Abi Talib's presence in Abyssinia was like a spark of the Prophet's own presence, and that his staying was a great matter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He estimates that about forty returned and about sixty stayed, about a hundred together, and with the forty in Mecca whom he links to Umar's acceptance of Islam, about a hundred and forty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He concludes that by the tenth year the Muslims numbered about a hundred and fifty, counting those in outlying places such as Abu Dharr, and says this is an approximation that counted mostly men. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says that those called the early forerunners can be understood in more than one way, and recalls the differing views he gave earlier. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Uthman ibn Maz'un returns the protection of al-Walid ibn al-Mughira

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Uthman ibn Maz'un entered Mecca under the protection of al-Walid ibn al-Mughira and Abu Salama under that of Abu Talib, his maternal uncle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says the chain for the Uthman story has an unnamed link and so is weak, and he treats it as supporting narrative rather than a pillar of the account. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that Uthman, seeing his companions suffer while he went about safely, returned the protection publicly in the mosque, and that al-Walid called him faithful in his guarantee. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds that it is not wrong to describe an unbeliever, even a hostile one, by a real good quality when the occasion calls for it, and he criticises online reactions that read description as praise or attack. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says that Quraysh respected the principle of protection even toward people they opposed, and ties it to the tradition about the Prophet being sent to perfect noble character. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that later the poet Labid ibn Rabia recited among Quraysh, Uthman disputed one line with him, a man struck Uthman's eye, and al-Walid said he had been safe under his guarantee; Uthman replied that his other eye wished to share the suffering and that he preferred God's protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Abu Talib protects Abu Salama and Abu Lahab sides with him

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that men of Banu Makhzum complained to Abu Talib about protecting a Makhzumi man, and he answered that Abu Salama was his sister's son. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that Abu Lahab then warned Quraysh to stop pressing his brother or he would stand with him, and that Quraysh backed off since they valued Abu Lahab's alliance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says Abu Talib hoped Abu Lahab might change, and wrote verse urging him to support the Prophet, but Abu Lahab never accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says Abu Lahab had earlier left Banu Hashim during the siege, and he calls that conduct a shameful betrayal of kin separate from his disbelief. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He notes that the chain given for this story is broken twice, since Abu Salama died long before the narrator's time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Ibn Ishaq's chains and the test against al-Bukhari

- He says that for the Abu Bakr story Ibn Ishaq gives the chain of al-Zuhri, Urwa, Aisha, and that he found in al-Bukhari the same chain through Uqayl. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds that such agreement raises trust in Ibn Ishaq, and that Ibn Ishaq tends to give weak or broken chains openly for stories he alone transmits, which he reads as honesty rather than a defect. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says that Ahmad ibn Hanbal hesitated over a purification ruling resting on Ibn Ishaq alone, and reads that hesitation as a sign of his stature. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds that after thirty-one lessons of detailed reading, the experience is itself a test of Ibn Ishaq's narrations, and that it confirms his worth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Ibn Ishaq against al-Waqidi in hadith criticism

- He holds that hadith scholars tested narrators objectively, and that Ibn Ishaq passed this test even against the sharp criticism of Malik ibn Anas, which most scholars did not accept. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says al-Dhahabi counted ten scholars who approved of al-Waqidi, yet most critics rated him from weak to abandoned, and that approval did not outweigh the test of his reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says that in practice he finds al-Waqidi alone in many striking stories about individuals, while stories with the light of the sound collections come through other chains, including in Ibn Sa'd. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He holds that this weakness does not mean rejecting everything al-Waqidi transmits, and that his specialist statements on dates and on who fought where are consulted, as with his listing of an emigrant to Abyssinia whom Ibn Ishaq omits. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He sums up that he admires Ibn Ishaq and is somewhat dissatisfied with al-Waqidi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Abu Bakr's emigration attempt and the protection of Ibn al-Dughunna

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq, through Aisha, that Abu Bakr asked the Prophet's leave to emigrate and set out, and a day or two from Mecca met Ibn al-Dughunna, chief of the Ahabish. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says the Ahabish were a group allied to Quraysh, and that the name comes from the alliance rather than from Abyssinians, though the origin of the name is disputed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He notes that the spelling of Ibn al-Dughunna's name differs between linguists and hadith transmitters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that Ibn al-Dughunna praised Abu Bakr's character, took him back under his protection and announced it in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that Abu Bakr prayed in a mosque at his door among Banu Jumah, wept when reciting, and drew onlookers, so Quraysh asked Ibn al-Dughunna to make him pray indoors. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that Abu Bakr then returned the protection and accepted God's, and Ibn al-Dughunna told Quraysh to deal with him themselves. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### Al-Bukhari's version and the departure toward Medina

- He relates that al-Bukhari's version adds details, including Aisha's account that the Prophet visited Abu Bakr's house twice every day, and that Abu Bakr reached Barak al-Ghimad before Ibn al-Dughunna met him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says that Barak al-Ghimad is placed south of Mecca on the coast, five nights away according to early authors, and that a place of similar name exists there today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that the report then moves to the Prophet telling the Muslims of the abode of emigration with palms between two lava fields, and that most of those in Abyssinia returned to Medina, a phrase he says needs further study. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet asked Abu Bakr to wait and that Abu Bakr kept two mounts ready for four months, and he leaves the full story for the lessons on the Hijra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}

### How al-Bukhari divides the Abu Bakr hadith into chapters, and the road to the pledges of al-Aqaba

- He says that al-Bukhari cites this hadith in several books, under chapters on Abu Bakr's protection in the Prophet's time, a sale, hiring a non-Muslim guide when necessary, covering the head, and visiting a friend daily. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says this shows al-Bukhari draws legal and behavioural points from every long narrative. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
- He says the series is now close to the first meetings with people of Yathrib, then the first and second pledges of al-Aqaba and then the Hijra, which he expects to take several lessons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep31}
