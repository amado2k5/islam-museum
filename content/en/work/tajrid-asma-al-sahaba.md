## Overview
*Tajrid Asma' al-Sahaba* ("The Extracted Names of the Companions") is by the hadith master [[person/al-dhahabi|Shams al-Din Abu 'Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn 'Uthman al-Dhahabi]] (d. 748 AH). He opens by saying that this is an extraction of the names of the Companions from the work of 'Izz al-Din [[person/ibn-al-athir-izz-al-din|Ibn al-Athir al-Jazari]], a fine and exhaustive book of the Companions named in the four works written on them — those of [[person/ibn-manda|Ibn Manda]], [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym]] and [[person/abu-musa-al-madini|Abu Musa]] of Isfahan (a supplement to Ibn Manda's), and that of [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Abu 'Umar Ibn 'Abd al-Barr]] — together with Ibn al-Athir's own additions. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 64-65}

## Sigla
Al-Dhahabi explains that Ibn al-Athir marked each Companion by source: (د) for Ibn Manda, (ع) for Abu Nu'aym, (ب) for Ibn 'Abd al-Barr and (س) for Abu Musa al-Madini. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 65} He added marks of his own: a name written in red is a Successor whose report is mursal; a name crossed with a red mark is an error; (ع) means the six books narrate from him, and the sign of any one of the six that its author includes him; another sign marks those in Ahmad's *Musnad*; a sign at the start marks those with one or two hadiths in the *Musnad* of Baqi ibn Makhlad; and a name in green is one of the Companion-poets recorded by [[person/ibn-sayyid-al-nas|Abu al-Fath Ibn Sayyid al-Nas]]. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 65-66} The editor observes that al-Dhahabi followed this scheme throughout, though he lapsed from it in many places. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 7-8}

## His additions to Usd al-Ghaba
Al-Dhahabi added a large group from the "history of the Companions who settled in Hims", many from [[work/tarikh-dimashq|the *History of Damascus*]], a number from Ahmad's *Musnad*, a group of the Companions in [[person/baqi-ibn-makhlad|Baqi ibn Makhlad's]] *Musnad*, several from glosses on [[work/al-istiab|*al-Isti'ab*]], several more — women especially — from [[work/al-tabaqat-al-kubra-ibn-sad|Ibn Sa'd's *Tabaqat*]], and some of the Companion-poets. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 65} A marginal note in one manuscript, which the editor gives as the author's, says that Ibn al-Athir, like others, neglected the *Tabaqat* and so missed many men and women. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 66}

## The number of Companions in his preface
Al-Dhahabi quotes and comments on estimates of the number of Companions, and reckons that those named in his book come to some eight thousand persons, most of them little known. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67} See [[question/adad-al-sahaba|How many Companions were there?]]

He then records Abu Musa al-Madini's account of Ibn Manda's book and of Yahya ibn Manda's supplement, judging that Abu Musa's work came out as a large volume containing fabrications and oddities and missing people found in *al-Isti'ab*, which Abu Musa had not seen; the same passage mentions a supplement to *al-Isti'ab* by a scholar of the Maghrib that was arranged together with the additions. He then summarises Ibn al-Athir's chains to the books he draws on: al-Tha'labi's *Tafsir*, al-Wahidi's *al-Wasit*, the two *Sahih*s, the *Muwatta'*, the *Musnad*, the *Sunan*, Abu Ya'la's *Musnad*, Ibn Ishaq's *Maghazi* in Yunus ibn Bukayr's transmission, Ibn Abi 'Asim's *al-Ahad wa-al-Mathani* and al-Azdi's *History of Mosul*. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67-68}

## Arrangement
The book opens with the letter hamza, its first entry being Abi al-Lahm al-Ghifari, and each entry gives, in a line or two, the name, what distinguishes the person, who narrated from him, and the sigla of the sources. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 69} It has a section on "those known only as 'a Companion of the Messenger of God'", where al-Dhahabi says: "Whenever I say of a man 'only' and add nothing, the original says he was a Companion." {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 8}

## Attribution
The book was first printed at Hyderabad in 1315 AH; some of the manuscripts used attributed it to Ibn al-Athir, and the edition's corrector, Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Hanafi, added a note showing — on the strength of Ibn Hajar's statement in the introduction to *al-Isaba* that al-Dhahabi extracted the names in Ibn al-Athir's book with additions — that the book is al-Dhahabi's and not Ibn al-Athir's. {cite:tajrid-asma-sahaba-dhahabi} {cite:tajrid-marifa-reprint-v1 p. 2} One manuscript is in the hand of al-Bishtaki (d. 828 AH), copied from Ibn al-Wani's copy that was read to the author; its first page reads: "The book of the extraction of the Companions whom Ibn al-Athir recorded, abridged by the hadith master al-Dhahabi, who added many names." {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 37-38} The Egyptian National Library copy (793 AH) bears the title *al-Isaba fi Tajrid al-Sahaba*, which the editor found no scholar using. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 29}

## Sibt Ibn al-'Ajami's glosses
The editor of the Dar al-Birr edition took as his base the Murad Molla manuscript in Turkey (no. 748), copied by Muhammad ibn Abi al-Walid Ibn al-Shihna al-Hanafi in 836 AH, which carries valuable glosses transferred from the copy of his teacher Sibt Ibn al-'Ajami — who had himself copied onto it the glosses of Abu al-Fath Ibn Sayyid al-Nas and of Ibn al-Amin of Cordoba on *al-Isti'ab*. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 15}
