## Summary

The lecturer continues the account of Hudaybiyya in the sixth year of the Hijra, following the long narration in Sahih al-Bukhari: the halted camel, the water, the Quraysh envoys, the negotiation of the treaty with Suhayl ibn Amr, the case of Abu Jandal, Umar's protest, the reluctance to slaughter and shave, and the later story of Abu Basir. He closes with the Qur'anic chapter of al-Fath, the pledge under the tree, and lessons he draws for reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### Recap: the 1,400 and the lesson on numbers

- He recalls that the previous session opened the Hudaybiyya episode in the sixth year of the Hijra, with a Muslim force of 1,400 whom he describes as the distilled core of the believers of the earth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says that after about nineteen years of calling people, the Prophet had only this number of committed men, yet within two or three years after Hudaybiyya, and especially in the months after the conquest of Mecca, people entered Islam in crowds. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that those who entered quickly also left quickly, while those trained in the Prophet's school, who migrated, fought and learned beside him, were the ones who stood firm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He draws the lesson for reformers that numbers of followers and wide audiences do not truly change reality; those who really carry the religion do. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### The march, the halted camel and the water

- He says he reads the narration of al-Miswar and Marwan in Sahih al-Bukhari because it is given there in full and in detail. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He explains that Hudaybiyya is an area with a well close to Mecca, on the edge of the sacred precinct. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Prophet learned Khalid ibn al-Walid, then not yet Muslim and a young cavalry leader of Quraysh, was scouting ahead, so he turned the army onto another path and Khalid only saw the dust before riding back to warn Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that at the mountain pass the Prophet's camel knelt and would not rise, that the people thought it stubborn, and that the Prophet answered that it was held back by the One who held back the elephant. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He describes the mood of the Muslims, eager for the umra and ready to face fighting or death, but unwilling even to imagine being turned away from the House. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Prophet said he would grant any plan in which Quraysh honoured the sanctities of God, and that the camel then rose. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the army camped at a small, nearly exhausted pool, that thirst was reported, and that the Prophet had an arrow from his quiver placed in it, after which water kept welling up until all 1,400 had drunk and left. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### Intelligence-gathering and Budayl of Khuza'a

- He relates that Budayl ibn Warqa of Khuza'a came with some of his people, whom he describes as trusted advisers of the Prophet from Tihama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says that Khuza'a was then still polytheist but had entered the Prophet's alliance when the agreement with Quraysh was made. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says it was the Prophet's habit to keep watch on the enemy's movements and numbers, and he recalls the Badr story of the questions about how many camels were slaughtered daily to estimate the size of Quraysh's army. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He draws the lesson for reformers of vigilance and alertness, and mentions the Qur'anic passage of fear prayer in the chapter of al-Nisa as a related teaching about taking precautions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Budayl reported that Quraysh had come out with everything, including nursing camels, and meant to fight and bar the Prophet from the House. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### What Quraysh feared and the Prophet's offer

- He holds that Quraysh's real concern, once they knew the Prophet came only for umra, was that the Arabs not hear that he had entered Mecca by force; they were ready to accept a return the following year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that the same idol of reputation and ancestral pride drove Abu Jahl at Badr and kept Abu Talib from entering Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Prophet told Budayl he had not come to fight, that war had exhausted Quraysh, and that they could agree a truce and leave him to deal with the other people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Prophet added that if they refused, he would fight them for this cause even alone, until his neck was severed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that this statement shows the Prophet was first in work, sacrifice and giving, and he compares it with the Prophet's steadiness at the Trench, Uhud and Hunayn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Budayl went to Quraysh with the message; the hotheads wanted to hear nothing, while the men of judgement asked for it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Urwa ibn Mas'ud's mission

- He relates that Urwa ibn Mas'ud urged Quraysh to accept the offer as a sound plan and went to speak with the Prophet, who gave him the same answer he gave Budayl. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Urwa asked whether any Arab had ever uprooted his own people, and said that if the other outcome came, the mixed men around the Prophet would flee. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He criticises this as the logic of the people of falsehood, who ignore their own past deeds, and recalls that the Prophet had been patient with his people for thirteen years in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says that Abu Bakr answered Urwa in very harsh words, and that Urwa replied he would have answered back were it not for a past favour. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Urwa reached for the Prophet's beard and al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, standing by with a sword, struck his hand with the sword's scabbard, which led to a remark about al-Mughira's old betrayal and the Prophet's saying about accepting his Islam but not his seized wealth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Urwa watched the Companions' devotion, how they caught his spit, raced to his wishes and lowered their voices, and told Quraysh he had visited kings and seen none so honoured by his followers; he returned advising them to accept. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### More envoys and the Prophet's reading of people

- He notes how the Prophet waited with the army and the sacrificial animals while one Qurayshi envoy after another travelled between Mecca and Hudaybiyya, and he points to this as patience. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that a man of Banu Kinana came, and that the Prophet recognised him as belonging to a people who honour sacrificial animals and ordered them to be sent out for him to see. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Companions raised the talbiya in front of the man, who returned to his people saying the animals were garlanded and marked and that these pilgrims should not be barred. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that this shows the Prophet's care for people's characters and levers of influence, a point he calls important for reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Mikraz ibn Hafs then came, whom the Prophet described as a dissolute man, and that Suhayl ibn Amr arrived while Mikraz was speaking, prompting the Prophet to remark that the matter had become easier. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### Writing the treaty: the basmala and the Prophet's title

- He says that the Prophet's life is comprehensive, offering a model in war and siege as well as in treaty and political negotiation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that the obstacles raised against the treaty were of two kinds, formal and substantive, and that the Prophet passed over both for the sake of a great benefit that the Qur'an later described as a clear opening. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that when the Prophet dictated the opening formula naming God, the Merciful and the Compassionate, Suhayl objected that he did not know the Merciful and asked for the older formula. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He compares this with the Meccan-period Qur'anic reaction of the same kind, and says the Muslims refused to write anything but the Prophet's wording, until the Prophet agreed to the older formula. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Suhayl also rejected the title messenger of God in the text, saying they would not have fought him had they accepted it, and that the Prophet had Muhammad son of Abdullah written instead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates a variant in which Ali was writing and refused to erase the title, and the Prophet himself had it removed; he reads this as the Prophet seeing the facts behind changes of wording and treating hostile attacks as empty, as with his words about being called by a name he does not bear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Suhayl also insisted the Prophet could not enter that year but only the next, which the Prophet accepted in the text. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### Abu Jandal and the return clause

- He relates that Suhayl then demanded that any man from Quraysh who came to the Prophet, even a Muslim, be returned, and that the Muslims were shocked at the idea of returning a Muslim to the polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that while the treaty was still being written Abu Jandal, Suhayl's own son, who had been chained and tortured for his Islam, broke free and threw himself among the Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Suhayl said this was the first thing he demanded, and that the Prophet replied the document was not yet concluded. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Suhayl threatened to make no peace at all, that the Prophet asked him repeatedly to allow Abu Jandal as a personal favour, and that Suhayl refused, though Mikraz offered to allow it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that the Prophet repeated the request because of the value of freeing the weak among the believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Abu Jandal then cried out to the Muslims about what he had suffered, which he describes as the last strain on their endurance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### Umar's protest and Abu Bakr's steadiness

- He relates that Umar ibn al-Khattab went to the Prophet and questioned why the Muslims should accept humiliation in their religion, and that the Prophet answered that he is God's messenger, does not disobey Him, and will be helped. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Umar reminded the Prophet of the vision of circling the House, and that the Prophet replied he had not said it would be that year, adding that Umar would come to it and circle it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Umar then put the same questions to Abu Bakr, who answered in nearly the same terms and told him to hold fast. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that this shows why Abu Bakr is called al-Siddiq and the best of the community after its Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says that even someone of Umar's rank may be moved to anger under pressure, and relates that Umar afterwards gave alms, prayed and freed slaves in repentance for that day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates a report in Sahih Muslim about Abu Bakr's good deeds in a single ordinary day as further evidence of his standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### The reluctance to slaughter and shave, and Umm Salama's counsel

- He relates that when the document was done the Prophet told the Companions to rise, slaughter and shave, and that none moved even after three times, which he calls the first such case. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Prophet went to Umm Salama, who advised him to speak to no one, slaughter his own animal and call his barber. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that when the Prophet did so, the Companions rose, slaughtered and shaved one another, so grieved that they nearly harmed each other. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He calls this one of the hardest scenes in the whole biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### Believing women exempted and Abu Basir

- He relates that believing women then came to the Prophet, and that a Qur'anic revelation exempted them from the return clause, telling the Muslims to test them and not send them back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that this shows how grave it is when Muslim women are oppressed or held captive, and that their cause should come even before the freeing of male captives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that on that occasion Umar divorced two of his wives who remained polytheists, one of whom married Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan and the other Safwan ibn Umayya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Abu Basir, a Muslim of Quraysh, came to Medina and was handed over to two men sent after him, but at Dhu al-Hulayfa he took one man's sword and killed him, and the other fled to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Abu Basir told the Prophet God had fulfilled his pledge by returning him, and that the Prophet's remark about him was that he would kindle war if he had men with him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Abu Basir then went to the seashore, where Abu Jandal and other Muslims escaping Quraysh joined him until a band formed that attacked Quraysh caravans bound for Syria. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Quraysh then appealed to the Prophet in the name of God and kinship to take them in, and that he sent for them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that the Prophet had foretold that God would make a way out for those who came from Quraysh, and that he saw their independence as no breach of the treaty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that Qur'anic verses about restraining hands in the valley of Mecca came down in this connection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### The pledge under the tree

- He says an important part of the episode was missed earlier: the Prophet sent Uthman to negotiate with Quraysh after Umar suggested someone with stronger protection in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that news came that Uthman had been killed because Quraysh had held him, and that the Prophet took a pledge from the Companions under a tree, a pledge not to flee, or according to some reports to the death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says it is called the pledge of ridwan because it brought God's approval upon those present, and that those present were held to have special merit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates a report in the sound collections that no one who pledged under the tree would enter the fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that the Companions' hearts raced their hands, so that God said His hand was above theirs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}

### The chapter of al-Fath and what it affirms

- He says that on the return journey, while grief still gripped the Companions, revelation came on the day's events, and he treats this as a reflection on the divine character of the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He notes that the chapter opens by announcing an opening already achieved, not one still to come, and then lists rewards, tranquillity sent into believers' hearts and promises to believing men and women. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Prophet recited these verses to Umar, who asked in surprise whether it was indeed a victory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says the chapter affirms the Prophet's vision, that the believers will enter the Sacred Mosque in safety, and that a near victory comes first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He says the only Qur'anic place with the phrase Muhammad is the messenger of God occurs in this chapter, which he links to the title erased in the treaty, noting that some commentators remark on this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He holds that the chapter consoles and praises the Companions, as God knew their reactions came from zeal for Him and not from refusing the Prophet's order. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He relates that the Prophet said this chapter was dearer to him than all the sun shines on, and counts the word tranquillity three times in it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
- He closes by saying the story holds many lessons, including submission, the worth of Abu Bakr and the pledgers under the tree, sacrifice, and weighing the greater interest even at the cost of lesser ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep26}
