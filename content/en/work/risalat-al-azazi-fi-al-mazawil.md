## Summary
The Bibliotheca Alexandrina book calls this one of the late works in the history of astronomy, written as an accessible guide to the lines of *fadl al-da'ir* (hour-angle) and the afternoon-prayer arcs; it has an introduction, two chapters and a conclusion, the first section explaining how to derive the azimuths of *fadl al-da'ir* with the almucantar quadrant. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 18}

## Contents
- It gives a practical method for making the instrument called *basita* with a needle housing (compass), used according to the book to find the qibla direction of towns. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 18} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 19}
- The author says he made a practical test of the line in 1282 AH; the treatise includes tables, among them the circle drawn around an instrument to give correct mihrab directions. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 18}
- The Azhar copy (no. 497/71517) was copied by Ali Mayyas al-Shafi'i al-Bayyumi al-Ahmadi in 1302 AH, 19 folios. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 18}
