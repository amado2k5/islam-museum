## Summary
A daily lesson series by Omar Abdelkafy in the month of Ramadan on the attributes of "the servants of the Most Merciful" in Surat al-Furqan. The first episode opens with supplication for the community at the month's start, announces the daily program — the "servants of the Most Merciful" with their nine attributes — and grounds the theme that this world is an abode of trial, that humility, kindness to parents and charity are the practical path of servanthood in this month. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:30}

## Episode 1: introducing the "servants of the Most Merciful" series in Ramadan
- He opens with supplication at the start of the blessed month: praying for the community's advancement, honour and unity, mending rifts among Muslims, and softening rulers' hearts toward their subjects. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:01:30}
- He announces a daily program in this time slot throughout the blessed month, expounding the "servants of the Most Merciful" — a distinguished, noble group — through the attributes drawn from [[passage/quran-25|Surat al-Furqan]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:30}
- He explains that "servants of the Most Merciful" is an intensive form for the abundance of their worship: the believer never tires of remembering his Lord for a moment, remembers Him in ease so as not to forget Him, remembers Him in hardship, and remembers Him in wealth through spending and in poverty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:03:30}
- He holds that this world is an abode of trial, not of honour: what does the rich man do with his wealth, and the poor man with his poverty — citing Qarun, the landowner who said "I give life and I take it" and for whom God sank him and his abode into the earth, and the man of the two gardens who said "I do not think this will ever perish, nor that the Hour will be established". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:05:30}
- He calls for renewing intention in Ramadan: that blessings — health, wealth, children, home, security and people's love — are God's gifts deserving gratitude, and that the servant is created from dust and does not deserve the blessing by himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:11:30}
- He calls for humility: that man is dust and a breath, so he should not be proud before anyone — not before his brother for his standing, nor before his uncle for his status, nor before his neighbours, being worthier of mercy than them all. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:08:30}
- He notes that in Ramadan homes hold many mushafs and fasters flock to [[practice/taraweeh|the taraweeh prayer]], and asks about those absent in the eleven preceding months — answering that the resolve came with the month. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:10:00}
- He sets a practical program for the servants of the Most Merciful in Ramadan: attending prayer and taraweeh, reciting the Qur'an at home, [[concept/ukhuwwa|reconciliation]] and [[practice/sawm-ramadan|fasting]], noting that taraweeh is a sunna that should not divide Muslims or cause them to miss the obligation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:17:30}
- He discusses accumulated Ramadan fasting: one who did not fast for many years — missed days are made up, repentance is offered, and negligence is regretted; rights owed to God lie between servant and Lord, while rights owed to people are returned to their owners. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:21:30}
- He closes with an introduction to the daily Ramadan program and the recitation of "and the servants of the Most Merciful who walk the earth gently, and when the ignorant address them, they say peace". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:22:30}

## Episode 2: humility — the first attribute of the servants of the Most Merciful
- He completes the introduction: the nine attributes in Surat al-Furqan are a general frame under which many subsidiary attributes fall, beginning with the first — humility — in God's words "who walk the earth gently". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:15:00}
- He states that [[concept/tawadu|humility]] is the only blessing that is not envied: wealth is envied, knowledge is envied, children are envied, and standing is envied — but no one is envied for humility. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:02:30}
- He details the faces of pride and envy: a story of one who envied his son for memorizing the Qur'an and feared he would forget — and pride was the ruin of Iblis, which its bearer should never let flourish. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:15:30}
- He calls for kindness to parents: daughters who sleep until dawn until their mothers wake them to eat suhur with their fathers and brothers — and at the start of Ramadan children must please their fathers and mothers. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:05:30}
- He states "you reap what you sow": seeds are requited — whoever presented evil finds it, and whoever sows thorns cannot expect grapes. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:06:30}
- He discusses clean money: that people deal in lawful and unlawful — an employee on a modest salary looking at one who cannot even ride a train — and that wealth built on unlawful money is built on sand, with a call to account for one's sustenance. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:10:00}
- He prescribes humility toward kin and scholars: that our elders should not let us down and our juniors should not be despised, and that we know our scholars their due — "blessed is he who is humble without diminishing himself". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:10:30}
- He mentions the Prophet's humility in serving his guests: "it is I who slaughtered the sheep, skinned it and cooked it, and it is I who gathered the firewood" — and he reserved no seat for himself among the Companions. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:16:30}
- He digresses into stories of humility among caliphs and scholars — tales of sharing shade and walking with subjects — order held cautiously. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:20:00}
- He calls for humility toward the weak: visiting mosques, prisoners and the sick, and being humble with the poor and those who lost their provider — for humility is a trait planted in children, not imposed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:12:30}

## Episode 3: affection and friendliness — the completion of humility
- He recalls that the previous lesson dealt with humility and states that what completes it is friendliness: whoever is humble draws near to people. He explains that the words wudd and mawadda are used for love, pointing to the Qur'anic mention of affection and mercy between spouses and to the promise that the Most Merciful will grant the believers affection. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:01:30}
- He relates that [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]] saw a man by the Kaaba and placed his own turban on the man's head; asked why he favoured him so, he answered that the man belonged to those his father Umar had been fond of, and he wished to keep up his father's friendship. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:02:00}
- He holds that affection does not come out of an envious or rancorous heart: rancour shows on the face as anger and haughtiness, and such a person knows no affection with wife, child, relative or neighbour, so that his household thanks God when he falls asleep or travels. His reason is that the [[concept/hasad|envier]] is absorbed by the blessing in another's hand and wishes it gone, and the more the blessing grows the more his rage and grief grow. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:04:00}
- He defines friendliness as looking first at people's good deeds. In his view the essential difference between his contemporaries and the righteous early generations is that the latter were busy with their own faults rather than other people's, whereas today a person throws his own faults behind his back and spreads other people's out before him, and so falls into [[concept/kibr|pride]]: so-and-so does not pray, so-and-so's daughter does not wear the headscarf. He urges whoever has been spared something to thank God for it instead of occupying himself with others. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:05:30}
- He calls for treating the one who falls short as a patient: one prays for his healing, not against him. He divides people into the afflicted and the spared, urges praise of God for well-being and mercy toward the afflicted, and counts affliction in religion as its hardest kind. Advice and enjoining what is right, he says, must themselves be done in a right way, that is, with affection and mercy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:07:00}
- He distinguishes three terms: mutual mercy, which rests on the strength of faith; mutual affection, which rests on love; and mutual sympathy, which is people helping one another. He says of the Companions that one of them would put a date in his brother's mouth and find its sweetness in his own. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:08:30}
- He holds that affection does not come from a hard heart, and that [[concept/qaswat-al-qalb-wa-amradih|hardness of heart]] shows in the features of the face. He recalls the Qur'anic description of hearts that hardened until they were like stones or harder, although water bursts from some stones. The hard-hearted man, as he pictures him, never carried a child, never stroked his daughter's head, and never showed mercy to an orphan, a poor person or a widow. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:09:30}
- To the obstacles to affection he adds [[concept/al-bukhl|miserliness]]: the miser, as he describes him, is stingy not only with money but with a kind word and kind treatment, for fear of being asked for something, and his motto is that each should keep to himself. Living for others as well as for oneself, in his view, is generosity and not failure, and the generous person stays affectionate and sympathetic. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:11:00}
- He calls the people of affection "brothers of purity" and quotes a sage's judgement that they are the best gain of this world: an adornment in ease, a provision in hardship and a help against enemies. They attend one's celebrations and congratulations, stand beside one in a crisis, and defend one's name when one is spoken ill of in one's absence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:13:30}
- He cites a hadith, which he attributes to al-Bukhari's narration, to the effect that among those of the community most intense in love for the Prophet are people who come after him, one of whom would wish to have seen him at the price of his family and wealth. He comments that love for him makes one long to see him. (needs_source: the attribution to al-Bukhari is the lecturer's and has not been verified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:14:30}
- He ascribes to the Irish writer Bernard Shaw a saying to the effect that if Muhammad ibn Abd Allah were alive today he would solve all the problems of the age over a cup of coffee. His explanation is that the Prophet turned the rigid hearts of Mecca into soft hearts that held affection, and he gives as examples what Islam made of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] and [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]]. (needs_source: the saying ascribed to Bernard Shaw is reported from the lecturer without a source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:15:30}
- He passes on a maxim from the teachers of his childhood: kinship needs affection, but affection does not need kinship, so one may hold dear an American, a South African or a Scandinavian Muslim with no tie of blood. He attributes to [[person/jafar-al-sadiq|Ja'far al-Sadiq]] the saying that a day's affection is a bond, a month's affection is kinship, and a year's affection is a tie of the womb for whose severing God holds one to account. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:17:00}
- He lists the fruits of affection: it increases familiarity, wipes grudges out of society and makes people feel safe in their dealings. He calls overlooking each other's faults between spouses "the practice of overlooking", and urges seeking excuses for one's brother up to seventy; if none is found one says that he may have an excuse one does not know of, such as money trouble or an illness he keeps to himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:18:30}
- He concludes that affection is a noble attribute of the servants of the Most Merciful, and that planting it requires uprooting what spoils the soil of the heart: envy, rancour, pride and looking down on people. Among those who have no affection he counts the bribe-taker, the embezzling employee and the overbearing manager who rates the one who bows before him as excellent and the one who keeps his self-respect as weak. He quotes the righteous as praying for mercy on whoever made them a gift of their faults, in the sense of welcoming [[concept/nasiha|sincere advice]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:20:00}

## Episode 4: "they say: peace" — forbearance with the ignorant
- He moves to the second attribute in [[passage/quran-25-63|verse 63 of Surat al-Furqan]]: the reply of the servants of the Most Merciful when the ignorant address them. He asks who the ignorant one is, and answers that such a person may hold the highest degrees from Harvard, Oxford and the Sorbonne while being ignorant of religion, of the standing of scholars and of values; but the one meant in the verse is the person who behaves rudely and aggressively toward people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:02:00}
- He reports from the exegetes that the peace in the verse is a peace of parting and letting be, not a greeting. His example is the answer of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]] to his father when the father's heart hardened and he threatened him: Ibrahim wished him peace and promised to ask forgiveness for him, and then each went his own way. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:03:00}
- He asks why people cannot bear with one another, and criticises the husband who listens to every relative yet treats his wife's advice as idle talk and dismisses her as deficient and her opinion as worthless. He puts a question to him in the holy month: would you be content to marry your daughter to a man of your own character? He calls on him to stand before "the mirror of the self" and ask himself about his temper, his pride and his coarseness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:04:30}
- He holds that Ibrahim set a rule of hospitable manners when he brought his guests the calf and placed it close to them, sparing them even the reach of a hand. He suggests that the husband cook an iftar or suhur meal for his household once to give his wife a rest, and recalls that the Prophet used to serve his family, mending his sandal and patching his garment, and that his rank did not keep him from humility. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:06:30}
- He tells of a scholar who said among his disciples that whoever saw the Prophet will not enter the Fire; one of them objected that Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl had seen him. The scholar replied that they did not look at him as the Messenger of God but as the orphan of Abu Talib. He ties this to the haughtiness of the notables of Quraysh at revelation coming to him rather than to their great men. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:08:00}
- He divides those who wrong a person into three: one who does not know his worth, one who knows it and wants to belittle him, and one who has heard nothing of him but ill. In all three cases, he holds, the offence is good deeds that God has driven his way and a raising of his rank. He reports of a great man that he was not angered by someone who spoke of an evil not in him, because others spoke of a good not in him, so the one balanced the other. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:10:00}
- He states that what counts is God's good pleasure alone: whoever pleases God at the cost of people's displeasure, God is pleased with him and makes people pleased with him, and whoever displeases God to please people, God is displeased with him and makes people displeased with him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:10:00}
- He explains "peace" as meaning that nothing but peace will come to the ignorant from the servants of the Most Merciful: a person is safe from them while with them and after leaving them. He then digresses to spreading the greeting of peace to those one knows and those one does not, and counts restricting the greeting to acquaintances among the signs of the Hour. His example is two neighbours in a lift who exchange the greeting time after time until affection, and perhaps a marriage tie, grows between them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:11:30}
- He asks what helps one bear with the ignorant, and answers that one excuses him for his ignorance of good conduct, and that [[concept/hilm|forbearance]] comes by practising forbearance, patience by practising patience and knowledge by learning. He commends a deliberate feigning of not noticing, and advises parents not to punish a child the moment he errs but to remind him of it in a calm moment rather than a heated one. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:12:30}
- He recalls that the Prophet was called a sorcerer, a soothsayer and a madman, and states that he never mounted the pulpit to deny it of himself but left the matter to God to defend him. He concludes that a person should leave it to God to ward off ill from him, and that those who wrong him expiate his sins and raise his rank. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:13:30}
- After the break he states that this attribute has described the prophets and the righteous in every nation: the arrogant notables made light of prophethood and despised the bearers of the messages while knowing their standing, and the messengers bore with them until forbearance became second nature to them and they became models of it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:16:00}
- He says that the word halim, "forbearing", occurs in the Qur'an twelve times by his count: eight as an attribute and name of God, twice describing Ibrahim, once describing [[person/ismail|Isma'il]], and once in the words of the people of [[person/shuayb|Shu'ayb]] to him. He defines the forbearing person as one who says "peace" with his tongue and his limbs together. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:17:00}
- He defines God's forbearance as not hastening punishment on the sinner, and contrasts it with what human beings would do if punishment were in their hands. He says that close to three billion people out of six deny God or ascribe to Him a child or a consort, and yet He has not withheld from them air, rain or provision. He cites Ibrahim's prayer for provision for those who believed and the divine answer that included those who disbelieved as well. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:19:00}
- He states that God gives the gift before the asking: a person sleeps, digests his food, his heart beats and he breathes without asking his Lord for any of it, and likewise he is granted hearing, sight and understanding as he sits before the screen. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:20:00}
- He guards against his words being taken as a call to passive reliance, and criticises those who plead that this world is the believer's prison while doing no work. He remarks that the watch by which the programme is timed is Swiss and the camera is not of Arab make, and that if everything returned to the country that made it people would be left without clothes. He calls for "marrying this world for the sake of the next": putting the world to work, and a community that produces rather than only consumes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:21:00}
- He closes by saying that production comes only from a heart joined to God and free of grudges, and asks how long the courts will be crowded with cases of divorce, inheritance and injustice. He swears that injustice will not be lifted from the community until it lifts the injustice among its own members, husbands and wives, children and relatives, and that [[concept/rahma|mercy]] is obtained by showing mercy to one another: the elder to the younger, spouses to each other, parents and children. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:22:30}

## Episode 5: the night prayer — those who spend the night prostrating and standing for their Lord
- He announces the third attribute of the servants of the Most Merciful, the one in [[passage/quran-25-64|verse 64 of Surat al-Furqan]]. He explains that the Arabic verb bata is said of anyone overtaken by night, whether he sleeps or not. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:01:30}
- He describes [[practice/qiyam-al-layl|the night prayer]] as an honour that God confers like a medal on whoever has served His religion and its call by day. He likens it to the student who shakes the hand of the head of state or the king on the platform only if he is first in his class; in the same way God wakes His servant as a mark of honour, to stand before Him prostrating, bowing and turning back to Him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:03:00}
- He relates that [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata ibn Abi Rabah]], his mother and his wife used to divide the night into thirds and take turns standing in prayer. When his mother died he shared it in halves with his wife, and when his wife died years later he came to stand the whole night so that remembrance would not cease in his house. He says that the dwellers of heaven see a house in which the night is kept as shining, the way the people of the earth see the stars. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:04:30}
- He anticipates his listeners' objection that this was the age of the Followers, with no television and no football matches broadcast at three in the morning. He answers that the matter is one of servanthood and nearness, and gives the example of the employee who, on learning that his minister exercises by the sea at five in the morning, would buy sports clothes and be there before him, and who, given an appointment at ten, would stand at his door from seven. He asks: what then of the Lord of humankind? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:06:30}
- He says he does not ask people for the state of Ata's household, nor that of [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]], of whom he mentions that he prayed the dawn prayer with the ablution of the night prayer for forty years, but for what suits today's weak faith, and through the whole year, not in Ramadan alone. The first degree of night prayer, for him, is keeping the night and dawn prayers in congregation, and then two units of prayer at any time of the night, even a quarter of an hour before dawn. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:07:30}
- He states that the servant has a daily, a weekly, a monthly and a yearly scale and a scale for the whole of life, and that the scale of the day is the dawn prayer: whoever prays it on time begins the morning with an open heart and an appetite for life, and whoever rises after sunrise begins it sluggish and dispirited. He reports from the righteous that one who delays the morning prayer until after sunrise is still provided for, but blessing is withdrawn from his provision; and he counts among provision the obedience of children and the soundness of a spouse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:09:30}
- He quotes "the scholars of hearts" as saying: whoever tastes, knows. The sweetness of worship, for him, is felt and not described, like the scent of a perfume or the beauty of a flower, and he cites the hadith on tasting the sweetness of faith for one content with God as Lord. He distinguishes the pleasure of the senses, which passes when its cause passes, from the pleasure of the heart, which remains, and calls on a person to take up the night prayer out of conviction and not as an experiment. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:11:30}
- He calls the last third of the night the moment of "the divine descent", and cites the hadith on the descent to the lowest heaven and the call: is there anyone seeking forgiveness, anyone with a need, anyone repenting. He adds that the supplication of the wronged at that hour is answered, and that supplications before dawn are "the arrows of destiny". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:12:30}
- He proposes a graded programme for the whole year: waking half an hour before dawn, half of it for reciting the Qur'an and half for two or four units of prayer, kept up for three to six months. It is then raised to forty-five minutes: Qur'an, seeking forgiveness and prayer. After a year or a year and a half it becomes an hour divided into quarters: remembrance, blessings on the Prophet, Qur'an and two units of prayer. He says that one who perseveres then wakes without an alarm and it becomes a devotional habit for him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:14:30}
- He dwells on the words "for their Lord": the night prayer, for him, is the act of worship furthest from [[concept/riya|showing off]], because it is between the servant and his Lord. He mentions that a Companion or a righteous man would slip from his bed as a mother slips away from her nursing child, so that his wife would not notice, and that one of the righteous, when his disciples saw his eyes red from weeping, would blame a cold in order to hide his deed. He pairs this with concealing charity so that the left hand does not know what the right has spent, in the sense of [[concept/ikhlas|sincerity]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:18:00}
- He lists the effects of keeping up the night prayer: less sleep together with more bodily and spiritual energy, and a clarity of mind that helps in pondering the Qur'an. He reports that [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]] was asked about the radiance seen on his face and answered that they were people who had been alone with God in the darkness of the night, so He clothed them in His light. He passes on a traditional saying: whoever wants a companion, God suffices him; whoever wants an intimate, the Qur'an suffices him; whoever wants wealth, contentment suffices him; and whoever wants a preacher, death suffices him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:19:30}
- He likens the servant who has no secret deed between himself and God to a cheque with no funds behind it. He describes the believer's heart, softened by the night prayer, as "the central bank" that distributes to the other banks, meaning the limbs, which then spend in obedience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:22:30}
- He pictures hardness of heart through the man who leans his back against a pillar of the mosque on Friday to doze while the preacher reminds people of the Garden and the Fire. To those who think the night prayer too heavy for a student with examinations he replies that time is one of God's soldiers: it is with whoever fears God and against whoever disobeys. He states that the heart is the sultan of the body: if it fills with light, the eye sees, the ear hears and the hand reaches only toward good, and if it fills with darkness and rancour, the hand turns aggressive and the writer writes what tears homes down. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:22:00}
