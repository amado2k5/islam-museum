## Summary
A daily lesson series by Omar Abdelkafy in the month of Ramadan on the attributes of "the servants of the Most Merciful" in Surat al-Furqan. The first episode opens with supplication for the community at the month's start, announces the daily program — the "servants of the Most Merciful" with their nine attributes — and grounds the theme that this world is an abode of trial, that humility, kindness to parents and charity are the practical path of servanthood in this month. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:30}

## Episode 1: introducing the "servants of the Most Merciful" series in Ramadan
- He opens with supplication at the start of the blessed month: praying for the community's advancement, honour and unity, mending rifts among Muslims, and softening rulers' hearts toward their subjects. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:01:30}
- He announces a daily program in this time slot throughout the blessed month, expounding the "servants of the Most Merciful" — a distinguished, noble group — through the attributes drawn from [[passage/quran-25|Surat al-Furqan]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:30}
- He explains that "servants of the Most Merciful" is an intensive form for the abundance of their worship: the believer never tires of remembering his Lord for a moment, remembers Him in ease so as not to forget Him, remembers Him in hardship, and remembers Him in wealth through spending and in poverty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:03:30}
- He holds that this world is an abode of trial, not of honour: what does the rich man do with his wealth, and the poor man with his poverty — citing Qarun, the landowner who said "I give life and I take it" and for whom God sank him and his abode into the earth, and the man of the two gardens who said "I do not think this will ever perish, nor that the Hour will be established". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:05:30}
- He calls for renewing intention in Ramadan: that blessings — health, wealth, children, home, security and people's love — are God's gifts deserving gratitude, and that the servant is created from dust and does not deserve the blessing by himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:11:30}
- He calls for humility: that man is dust and a breath, so he should not be proud before anyone — not before his brother for his standing, nor before his uncle for his status, nor before his neighbours, being worthier of mercy than them all. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:08:30}
- He notes that in Ramadan homes hold many mushafs and fasters flock to [[practice/taraweeh|the taraweeh prayer]], and asks about those absent in the eleven preceding months — answering that the resolve came with the month. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:10:00}
- He sets a practical program for the servants of the Most Merciful in Ramadan: attending prayer and taraweeh, reciting the Qur'an at home, [[concept/ukhuwwa|reconciliation]] and [[practice/sawm-ramadan|fasting]], noting that taraweeh is a sunna that should not divide Muslims or cause them to miss the obligation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:17:30}
- He discusses accumulated Ramadan fasting: one who did not fast for many years — missed days are made up, repentance is offered, and negligence is regretted; rights owed to God lie between servant and Lord, while rights owed to people are returned to their owners. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:21:30}
- He closes with an introduction to the daily Ramadan program and the recitation of "and the servants of the Most Merciful who walk the earth gently, and when the ignorant address them, they say peace". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep01 00:22:30}

## Episode 2: humility — the first attribute of the servants of the Most Merciful
- He completes the introduction: the nine attributes in Surat al-Furqan are a general frame under which many subsidiary attributes fall, beginning with the first — humility — in God's words "who walk the earth gently". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:15:00}
- He states that [[concept/tawadu|humility]] is the only blessing that is not envied: wealth is envied, knowledge is envied, children are envied, and standing is envied — but no one is envied for humility. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:02:30}
- He details the faces of pride and envy: a story of one who envied his son for memorizing the Qur'an and feared he would forget — and pride was the ruin of Iblis, which its bearer should never let flourish. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:15:30}
- He calls for kindness to parents: daughters who sleep until dawn until their mothers wake them to eat suhur with their fathers and brothers — and at the start of Ramadan children must please their fathers and mothers. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:05:30}
- He states "you reap what you sow": seeds are requited — whoever presented evil finds it, and whoever sows thorns cannot expect grapes. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:06:30}
- He discusses clean money: that people deal in lawful and unlawful — an employee on a modest salary looking at one who cannot even ride a train — and that wealth built on unlawful money is built on sand, with a call to account for one's sustenance. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:10:00}
- He prescribes humility toward kin and scholars: that our elders should not let us down and our juniors should not be despised, and that we know our scholars their due — "blessed is he who is humble without diminishing himself". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:10:30}
- He mentions the Prophet's humility in serving his guests: "it is I who slaughtered the sheep, skinned it and cooked it, and it is I who gathered the firewood" — and he reserved no seat for himself among the Companions. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:16:30}
- He digresses into stories of humility among caliphs and scholars — tales of sharing shade and walking with subjects — order held cautiously. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:20:00}
- He calls for humility toward the weak: visiting mosques, prisoners and the sick, and being humble with the poor and those who lost their provider — for humility is a trait planted in children, not imposed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep02 00:12:30}

## Episode 3: affection and friendliness — the completion of humility
- He recalls that the previous lesson dealt with humility and states that what completes it is friendliness: whoever is humble draws near to people. He explains that the words wudd and mawadda are used for love, pointing to the Qur'anic mention of affection and mercy between spouses and to the promise that the Most Merciful will grant the believers affection. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:01:30}
- He relates that [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]] saw a man by the Kaaba and placed his own turban on the man's head; asked why he favoured him so, he answered that the man belonged to those his father Umar had been fond of, and he wished to keep up his father's friendship. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:02:00}
- He holds that affection does not come out of an envious or rancorous heart: rancour shows on the face as anger and haughtiness, and such a person knows no affection with wife, child, relative or neighbour, so that his household thanks God when he falls asleep or travels. His reason is that the [[concept/hasad|envier]] is absorbed by the blessing in another's hand and wishes it gone, and the more the blessing grows the more his rage and grief grow. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:04:00}
- He defines friendliness as looking first at people's good deeds. In his view the essential difference between his contemporaries and the righteous early generations is that the latter were busy with their own faults rather than other people's, whereas today a person throws his own faults behind his back and spreads other people's out before him, and so falls into [[concept/kibr|pride]]: so-and-so does not pray, so-and-so's daughter does not wear the headscarf. He urges whoever has been spared something to thank God for it instead of occupying himself with others. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:05:30}
- He calls for treating the one who falls short as a patient: one prays for his healing, not against him. He divides people into the afflicted and the spared, urges praise of God for well-being and mercy toward the afflicted, and counts affliction in religion as its hardest kind. Advice and enjoining what is right, he says, must themselves be done in a right way, that is, with affection and mercy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:07:00}
- He distinguishes three terms: mutual mercy, which rests on the strength of faith; mutual affection, which rests on love; and mutual sympathy, which is people helping one another. He says of the Companions that one of them would put a date in his brother's mouth and find its sweetness in his own. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:08:30}
- He holds that affection does not come from a hard heart, and that [[concept/qaswat-al-qalb-wa-amradih|hardness of heart]] shows in the features of the face. He recalls the Qur'anic description of hearts that hardened until they were like stones or harder, although water bursts from some stones. The hard-hearted man, as he pictures him, never carried a child, never stroked his daughter's head, and never showed mercy to an orphan, a poor person or a widow. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:09:30}
- To the obstacles to affection he adds [[concept/al-bukhl|miserliness]]: the miser, as he describes him, is stingy not only with money but with a kind word and kind treatment, for fear of being asked for something, and his motto is that each should keep to himself. Living for others as well as for oneself, in his view, is generosity and not failure, and the generous person stays affectionate and sympathetic. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:11:00}
- He calls the people of affection "brothers of purity" and quotes a sage's judgement that they are the best gain of this world: an adornment in ease, a provision in hardship and a help against enemies. They attend one's celebrations and congratulations, stand beside one in a crisis, and defend one's name when one is spoken ill of in one's absence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:13:30}
- He cites a hadith, which he attributes to al-Bukhari's narration, to the effect that among those of the community most intense in love for the Prophet are people who come after him, one of whom would wish to have seen him at the price of his family and wealth. He comments that love for him makes one long to see him. (needs_source: the attribution to al-Bukhari is the lecturer's and has not been verified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:14:30}
- He ascribes to the Irish writer Bernard Shaw a saying to the effect that if Muhammad ibn Abd Allah were alive today he would solve all the problems of the age over a cup of coffee. His explanation is that the Prophet turned the rigid hearts of Mecca into soft hearts that held affection, and he gives as examples what Islam made of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] and [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]]. (needs_source: the saying ascribed to Bernard Shaw is reported from the lecturer without a source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:15:30}
- He passes on a maxim from the teachers of his childhood: kinship needs affection, but affection does not need kinship, so one may hold dear an American, a South African or a Scandinavian Muslim with no tie of blood. He attributes to [[person/jafar-al-sadiq|Ja'far al-Sadiq]] the saying that a day's affection is a bond, a month's affection is kinship, and a year's affection is a tie of the womb for whose severing God holds one to account. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:17:00}
- He lists the fruits of affection: it increases familiarity, wipes grudges out of society and makes people feel safe in their dealings. He calls overlooking each other's faults between spouses "the practice of overlooking", and urges seeking excuses for one's brother up to seventy; if none is found one says that he may have an excuse one does not know of, such as money trouble or an illness he keeps to himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:18:30}
- He concludes that affection is a noble attribute of the servants of the Most Merciful, and that planting it requires uprooting what spoils the soil of the heart: envy, rancour, pride and looking down on people. Among those who have no affection he counts the bribe-taker, the embezzling employee and the overbearing manager who rates the one who bows before him as excellent and the one who keeps his self-respect as weak. He quotes the righteous as praying for mercy on whoever made them a gift of their faults, in the sense of welcoming [[concept/nasiha|sincere advice]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep03 00:20:00}

## Episode 4: "they say: peace" — forbearance with the ignorant
- He moves to the second attribute in [[passage/quran-25-63|verse 63 of Surat al-Furqan]]: the reply of the servants of the Most Merciful when the ignorant address them. He asks who the ignorant one is, and answers that such a person may hold the highest degrees from Harvard, Oxford and the Sorbonne while being ignorant of religion, of the standing of scholars and of values; but the one meant in the verse is the person who behaves rudely and aggressively toward people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:02:00}
- He reports from the exegetes that the peace in the verse is a peace of parting and letting be, not a greeting. His example is the answer of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]] to his father when the father's heart hardened and he threatened him: Ibrahim wished him peace and promised to ask forgiveness for him, and then each went his own way. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:03:00}
- He asks why people cannot bear with one another, and criticises the husband who listens to every relative yet treats his wife's advice as idle talk and dismisses her as deficient and her opinion as worthless. He puts a question to him in the holy month: would you be content to marry your daughter to a man of your own character? He calls on him to stand before "the mirror of the self" and ask himself about his temper, his pride and his coarseness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:04:30}
- He holds that Ibrahim set a rule of hospitable manners when he brought his guests the calf and placed it close to them, sparing them even the reach of a hand. He suggests that the husband cook an iftar or suhur meal for his household once to give his wife a rest, and recalls that the Prophet used to serve his family, mending his sandal and patching his garment, and that his rank did not keep him from humility. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:06:30}
- He tells of a scholar who said among his disciples that whoever saw the Prophet will not enter the Fire; one of them objected that Abu Lahab and Abu Jahl had seen him. The scholar replied that they did not look at him as the Messenger of God but as the orphan of Abu Talib. He ties this to the haughtiness of the notables of Quraysh at revelation coming to him rather than to their great men. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:08:00}
- He divides those who wrong a person into three: one who does not know his worth, one who knows it and wants to belittle him, and one who has heard nothing of him but ill. In all three cases, he holds, the offence is good deeds that God has driven his way and a raising of his rank. He reports of a great man that he was not angered by someone who spoke of an evil not in him, because others spoke of a good not in him, so the one balanced the other. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:10:00}
- He states that what counts is God's good pleasure alone: whoever pleases God at the cost of people's displeasure, God is pleased with him and makes people pleased with him, and whoever displeases God to please people, God is displeased with him and makes people displeased with him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:10:00}
- He explains "peace" as meaning that nothing but peace will come to the ignorant from the servants of the Most Merciful: a person is safe from them while with them and after leaving them. He then digresses to spreading the greeting of peace to those one knows and those one does not, and counts restricting the greeting to acquaintances among the signs of the Hour. His example is two neighbours in a lift who exchange the greeting time after time until affection, and perhaps a marriage tie, grows between them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:11:30}
- He asks what helps one bear with the ignorant, and answers that one excuses him for his ignorance of good conduct, and that [[concept/hilm|forbearance]] comes by practising forbearance, patience by practising patience and knowledge by learning. He commends a deliberate feigning of not noticing, and advises parents not to punish a child the moment he errs but to remind him of it in a calm moment rather than a heated one. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:12:30}
- He recalls that the Prophet was called a sorcerer, a soothsayer and a madman, and states that he never mounted the pulpit to deny it of himself but left the matter to God to defend him. He concludes that a person should leave it to God to ward off ill from him, and that those who wrong him expiate his sins and raise his rank. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:13:30}
- After the break he states that this attribute has described the prophets and the righteous in every nation: the arrogant notables made light of prophethood and despised the bearers of the messages while knowing their standing, and the messengers bore with them until forbearance became second nature to them and they became models of it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:16:00}
- He says that the word halim, "forbearing", occurs in the Qur'an twelve times by his count: eight as an attribute and name of God, twice describing Ibrahim, once describing [[person/ismail|Isma'il]], and once in the words of the people of [[person/shuayb|Shu'ayb]] to him. He defines the forbearing person as one who says "peace" with his tongue and his limbs together. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:17:00}
- He defines God's forbearance as not hastening punishment on the sinner, and contrasts it with what human beings would do if punishment were in their hands. He says that close to three billion people out of six deny God or ascribe to Him a child or a consort, and yet He has not withheld from them air, rain or provision. He cites Ibrahim's prayer for provision for those who believed and the divine answer that included those who disbelieved as well. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:19:00}
- He states that God gives the gift before the asking: a person sleeps, digests his food, his heart beats and he breathes without asking his Lord for any of it, and likewise he is granted hearing, sight and understanding as he sits before the screen. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:20:00}
- He guards against his words being taken as a call to passive reliance, and criticises those who plead that this world is the believer's prison while doing no work. He remarks that the watch by which the programme is timed is Swiss and the camera is not of Arab make, and that if everything returned to the country that made it people would be left without clothes. He calls for "marrying this world for the sake of the next": putting the world to work, and a community that produces rather than only consumes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:21:00}
- He closes by saying that production comes only from a heart joined to God and free of grudges, and asks how long the courts will be crowded with cases of divorce, inheritance and injustice. He swears that injustice will not be lifted from the community until it lifts the injustice among its own members, husbands and wives, children and relatives, and that [[concept/rahma|mercy]] is obtained by showing mercy to one another: the elder to the younger, spouses to each other, parents and children. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep04 00:22:30}

## Episode 5: the night prayer — those who spend the night prostrating and standing for their Lord
- He announces the third attribute of the servants of the Most Merciful, the one in [[passage/quran-25-64|verse 64 of Surat al-Furqan]]. He explains that the Arabic verb bata is said of anyone overtaken by night, whether he sleeps or not. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:01:30}
- He describes [[practice/qiyam-al-layl|the night prayer]] as an honour that God confers like a medal on whoever has served His religion and its call by day. He likens it to the student who shakes the hand of the head of state or the king on the platform only if he is first in his class; in the same way God wakes His servant as a mark of honour, to stand before Him prostrating, bowing and turning back to Him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:03:00}
- He relates that [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata ibn Abi Rabah]], his mother and his wife used to divide the night into thirds and take turns standing in prayer. When his mother died he shared it in halves with his wife, and when his wife died years later he came to stand the whole night so that remembrance would not cease in his house. He says that the dwellers of heaven see a house in which the night is kept as shining, the way the people of the earth see the stars. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:04:30}
- He anticipates his listeners' objection that this was the age of the Followers, with no television and no football matches broadcast at three in the morning. He answers that the matter is one of servanthood and nearness, and gives the example of the employee who, on learning that his minister exercises by the sea at five in the morning, would buy sports clothes and be there before him, and who, given an appointment at ten, would stand at his door from seven. He asks: what then of the Lord of humankind? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:06:30}
- He says he does not ask people for the state of Ata's household, nor that of [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]], of whom he mentions that he prayed the dawn prayer with the ablution of the night prayer for forty years, but for what suits today's weak faith, and through the whole year, not in Ramadan alone. The first degree of night prayer, for him, is keeping the night and dawn prayers in congregation, and then two units of prayer at any time of the night, even a quarter of an hour before dawn. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:07:30}
- He states that the servant has a daily, a weekly, a monthly and a yearly scale and a scale for the whole of life, and that the scale of the day is the dawn prayer: whoever prays it on time begins the morning with an open heart and an appetite for life, and whoever rises after sunrise begins it sluggish and dispirited. He reports from the righteous that one who delays the morning prayer until after sunrise is still provided for, but blessing is withdrawn from his provision; and he counts among provision the obedience of children and the soundness of a spouse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:09:30}
- He quotes "the scholars of hearts" as saying: whoever tastes, knows. The sweetness of worship, for him, is felt and not described, like the scent of a perfume or the beauty of a flower, and he cites the hadith on tasting the sweetness of faith for one content with God as Lord. He distinguishes the pleasure of the senses, which passes when its cause passes, from the pleasure of the heart, which remains, and calls on a person to take up the night prayer out of conviction and not as an experiment. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:11:30}
- He calls the last third of the night the moment of "the divine descent", and cites the hadith on the descent to the lowest heaven and the call: is there anyone seeking forgiveness, anyone with a need, anyone repenting. He adds that the supplication of the wronged at that hour is answered, and that supplications before dawn are "the arrows of destiny". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:12:30}
- He proposes a graded programme for the whole year: waking half an hour before dawn, half of it for reciting the Qur'an and half for two or four units of prayer, kept up for three to six months. It is then raised to forty-five minutes: Qur'an, seeking forgiveness and prayer. After a year or a year and a half it becomes an hour divided into quarters: remembrance, blessings on the Prophet, Qur'an and two units of prayer. He says that one who perseveres then wakes without an alarm and it becomes a devotional habit for him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:14:30}
- He dwells on the words "for their Lord": the night prayer, for him, is the act of worship furthest from [[concept/riya|showing off]], because it is between the servant and his Lord. He mentions that a Companion or a righteous man would slip from his bed as a mother slips away from her nursing child, so that his wife would not notice, and that one of the righteous, when his disciples saw his eyes red from weeping, would blame a cold in order to hide his deed. He pairs this with concealing charity so that the left hand does not know what the right has spent, in the sense of [[concept/ikhlas|sincerity]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:18:00}
- He lists the effects of keeping up the night prayer: less sleep together with more bodily and spiritual energy, and a clarity of mind that helps in pondering the Qur'an. He reports that [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]] was asked about the radiance seen on his face and answered that they were people who had been alone with God in the darkness of the night, so He clothed them in His light. He passes on a traditional saying: whoever wants a companion, God suffices him; whoever wants an intimate, the Qur'an suffices him; whoever wants wealth, contentment suffices him; and whoever wants a preacher, death suffices him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:19:30}
- He likens the servant who has no secret deed between himself and God to a cheque with no funds behind it. He describes the believer's heart, softened by the night prayer, as "the central bank" that distributes to the other banks, meaning the limbs, which then spend in obedience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:22:30}
- He pictures hardness of heart through the man who leans his back against a pillar of the mosque on Friday to doze while the preacher reminds people of the Garden and the Fire. To those who think the night prayer too heavy for a student with examinations he replies that time is one of God's soldiers: it is with whoever fears God and against whoever disobeys. He states that the heart is the sultan of the body: if it fills with light, the eye sees, the ear hears and the hand reaches only toward good, and if it fills with darkness and rancour, the hand turns aggressive and the writer writes what tears homes down. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep05 00:22:00}

## Episode 6: seeking refuge from the torment of Hell, and supplication
- He reviews the attributes covered so far: humility, forbearance, affection and standing in prayer at night, even a little; then he moves to what is in [[passage/quran-25-65|verse 65 of Surat al-Furqan]]: the prayer of the servants of the Most Merciful that the torment of [[concept/jahannam|Hell]] be turned away from them. He marvels that such people seek refuge from the Fire, while the negligent person who does not pray, asked about his fate, answers that he is bound for the Garden because God is merciful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:02:00}
- He states that no one holds the keys of the Garden and the Fire or may pass judgement on a particular person now that revelation has ended. He mentions that the Prophet was told by revelation the names of seventeen hypocrites and confided them to [[person/hudhayfa-ibn-al-yaman|Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman]], the keeper of his secret, so that when one of them died the caliph would not pray over him. (needs_source: the number seventeen is the lecturer's and has not been verified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:05:00}
- He states that faith and disbelief are the servant's own choice and that "the key is with you": the message reached Abu Bakr, he stood at the door of faith and was given its key; the same message reached Abu Jahl and Abu Lahab, and they stood at the door of disbelief and obstinacy. He names the two roads "the track of safety" and "the track of regret". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:04:00}
- He relates that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]], who had been promised the Garden, went to Hudhayfa as soon as he took office as Commander of the Faithful and adjured him: had the Prophet named him on that list? Hudhayfa wept at the sight of his fear, and Umar, supposing he was hiding it from him, wept too. He quotes [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]] as saying that Umar's Islam was a conquest, his emigration a victory and his rule a mercy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:06:00}
- He observes that those given the tidings of the Garden grew in [[concept/khawf|fear]] of God after the tidings, contrary to what one would expect. His example is a father whose son studies in a school owned by the boy's uncle and staffed by relatives who have assured him the boy will pass: the natural thing is for him to be at ease, and if his worry increased it would be thought a disorder. He explains the Companions' state by saying that those who fear God most are those nearest to Him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:08:30}
- He explains the verse's description of the torment as gharam in the sense of something that clings and does not let go, as one says a person is mughram with another, meaning inseparable from him. He recalls the Qur'anic image of the Fire as a refuge that clasps its inmate as a mother clasps her child. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:09:30}
- He reminds his listeners that people cannot bear a very hot day without air conditioners, so what of the Fire. To those who object to frightening people he replies with a saying he passes on: that I frighten you so that you find safety on the Day of Resurrection is better than that I reassure you and you find fear. He says that the Qur'an mentions the Fire fifty-seven times or thereabouts and the Garden the same, and mercy two hundred and thirteen times and punishment the same, because the Qur'an comes in pairs. (needs_source: the counts are the lecturer's and have not been verified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:11:00}
- He distinguishes the two terms in [[passage/quran-25-66|verse 66]]: the "resting place", for him, is for the sinning believer who stays in the Fire for a time until what he owes is settled and then leaves, and the "abode" is for those who remain in it for ever without death. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:12:00}
- He recommends asking God's protection from the Fire seven times after the dawn prayer and after the sunset prayer, and asking for the Garden after prayer. He mentions that when the verses of punishment were recited to the Companions it was as if they felt the scorch of the Fire. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:13:00}
- After the break he turns to [[practice/dua|supplication]]: one supplicates in ease so as to be relieved in hardship, and in wealth and health for the days of want and illness. He criticises the person who despairs after a month or two of supplication and thinks himself unaccepted because of his many sins, and reminds him that God answers the distressed one whose means have all been cut off. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:16:00}
- His example of the distressed is the surgeon who has prepared the operating theatre and his team and then asks God for success. He attributes to [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]], in his last days, the remark that they sent for the physician of people while he sought the Physician of heaven: the one gives the medicine and the Other places the cure in it. He says that ten people take the same medicine and three benefit, because it is God who puts its secret into the medicine. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:17:30}
- He adds the example of water: God placed in it the secret of quenching thirst, and its bitterness or sweetness depends on the state of the one who drinks; the sick or the troubled drinks it and finds it bitter, and the healthy and light-hearted drinks it and finds it sweet. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:18:30}
- He warns against weariness and haste in supplication: the supplicant is either answered, or has a calamity lifted from him, or has the reward stored up for him on the Day of Resurrection, so no supplication is lost. He states that God needs no time in order to answer, and that His command is before the letters kaf and nun of "Be", not between them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep06 00:20:00}
- He tells of a righteous man aboard a ship when the sea rose against it; the passengers asked him to pray, and he said: Lord, You have shown us Your power, so show us Your mercy, and the sea grew calm. ## Episode 7: the middle course in spending — neither waste nor stinginess
- He opens by addressing the oppressor, the sinner and the person in authority: how long will [[concept/zulm|injustice]] and the violation of rights go on? He asks what [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj]] gained from serving the Umayyads, and whether he threw away his hereafter for the sake of pleasing them. He warns against wronging one who has no helper but God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:02:00}
- He announces the day's attribute from [[passage/quran-25-67|verse 67 of Surat al-Furqan]]: the middle course in spending, between waste and stinginess. He observes that people and their children have grown so used to blessings that a father can no longer surprise his child with a present, because the child's room is full of toys and the latest devices. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:03:00}
- He criticises a community that consumes what it does not make, such as the mobile telephone, and cites the sense of two hadiths: the strong believer is better than the weak, and the upper hand is better than the lower. He says that cutting the internet off from the Arabs would bring their life to a halt, because their necks are in other people's hands. He quotes his teacher: whoever's loaf does not come from his own axe, his opinion will not come from his own head. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:04:00}
- He draws for spending a middle and two extremes: if generosity goes past its limit it becomes [[concept/al-israf|waste]]; if it falls a little short it becomes tight-fistedness, and if that grows it becomes miserliness. He states that going to extremes is rejected in Islam in everything, even in worship. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:05:00}
- As evidence against excess in worship he relates that the Prophet was on a campaign in Ramadan and raised a cup of water and drank in the middle of the day, so that people would know it was permitted to break the fast on a long journey. Some insisted on fasting until a few of them collapsed from the heat, and he described them as the disobedient. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:05:30}
- He distinguishes "the servants of the Most Merciful" from "the servants of Ramadan": the latter fill the mosques at dawn in Ramadan and then vanish, so their worship, for him, is a temporary permit that lapses when its cause is removed, like a device that stops when its cable is pulled from the power source. What he asks for is a worshipper of God all year round. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:06:30}
- To those who ask about the limit of waste in the case of a billionaire who buys a car for millions, he answers that this is an exceptional case, and that what is asked of such people is to pay the zakat on their wealth, give charity and not use money to wrong those who work for them. He states that religion addresses rich and poor alike, so that the gratitude of the rich and the patience of the poor may be seen. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:07:30}
- He defines waste in relation to means: someone whose income is ten and who spends twelve or fifteen is wasteful, while someone whose income is a hundred thousand and who spends ninety is not, because he stays within his means. Most debtors, in his view, borrowed in order to live beyond their means, so interest piles up, the car and the house are seized and they are barred from travel. He calls this "false show", counts the love of appearances a sickness, and explains "the strain of affliction" as a person loading himself with more than he can bear. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:09:00}
- He explains the Qur'an's description of squanderers as brothers of the devils by saying that Satan is a fool, and part of his folly was refusing to prostrate out of pride. Among kinds of folly he counts folly in spending, in foul speech and in harming society. He lays down the rule: wealth is to be earned lawfully and spent lawfully. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:10:00}
- After the break he returns to the other extreme: the [[concept/al-bukhl|miser]], for him, is a misery in the home, and he gives "a father's advice" not to keep company or marry into the family of such a person, because his money is his life, and some people have taken their dirham and dollar as their passion. He mentions that wealth is called mal because it makes people incline away from the truth, and that a friend turns into an enemy when he thinks he has been wronged over a single pound. He says that miserliness was blamed among the Arabs before Islam, and that the miser is stingy with every good that would reach people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:13:30}
- He draws a satirical picture of the miser: when a dinar reaches him he tells it that it has arrived at its resting place and will not see the light again. If Ya'qub came to him with Yusuf's shirt, with the prophets as guarantors, to borrow a needle from him, he would not lend it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:14:00}
- He counts as the most miserly of people the one who is stingy with the greeting of peace and answers it with a curt word out of pride. He reports from Ibn Mas'ud, in effect, that when a person greets people who do not answer, One better than they answers him. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:14:30}
- He widens the verse to emotions and conduct: lavishing too much on a child makes him spoiled or undutiful; a husband sides with "the wife's party" and turns undutiful to his parents once he is grown and well off, and he counts obeying the wife while being undutiful to the mother among the signs of the Hour. He advises a policy of "the door a little open" in dealing with wife, children, relatives and neighbours: the door is neither shut nor thrown wide, so that excessive praise does not turn into a lawsuit. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:16:30}
- He concludes that the community is a [[concept/ummat-wasat|middle community]] and that the servant is to keep to the middle in everything, even in the pledge to God "as far as I am able" and in preparing according to one's ability rather than with the latest weapons. Among the causes of strength he counts strength of faith, sincere supplication, good upbringing, and giving up waste, competition in tall buildings and boasting of tribe. He prays that his listeners be among the moderate, neither on the far right nor on the far left. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep07 00:18:30}

## Episode 8: pure monotheism — those who call on no other god beside God
- At the start of the lesson he greets the viewers of the Sharjah satellite channel and says that he is their guest every day at this hour of the month, which shows that the programme was broadcast on that channel in [[place/sharjah|Sharjah]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:01:00}
- He moves to the sixth of the nine attributes, in [[passage/quran-25-68|verse 68 of Surat al-Furqan]]: that they call on no other god beside God. He raises a question: why does the matter of [[concept/tawhid|monotheism]] come at this late rank, after humility, forbearance, the night prayer, supplication and the middle course in spending? He answers that all those deeds came from such a person only because he believes in God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:03:00}
- He states that Islam stands on two things: "the word of unity and the unity of the word". He recalls the Qur'anic likeness of the good word to a good tree whose root is firm and whose branches are in the sky, reading the firmness as faith and the height as loftiness of aspiration, and contrasts it with Pharaoh, who exalted himself on the earth and not toward the sky. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:03:30}
- He digresses to an earthquake of about nine on the Richter scale in Japan, saying that it turned the country from a donor state into a state in need, as evidence that no one has true height on the earth. He declares his sympathy for its people and disclaims any gloating, and praises their seriousness and love of work: courses are held there to persuade employees to take their leave, whereas the Arab employee, in his opinion, needs a course to persuade him to take less of it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:05:00}
- He explains "the unity of the word" by saying that Muslims, for all their different schools and opinions, are joined by one Lord, one qibla and one preserved Book that has not changed. He says that the Qur'an has one hundred and fourteen suras and six thousand two hundred and thirty-six verses, and that its Meccan suras are eighty-five and its Medinan suras twenty-nine by his count. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:09:00}
- He mentions that the Prophet is the seal of the prophets, and that the prophets were gathered for him on the night of [[event/isra-and-miraj|the Night Journey]] at [[building/al-aqsa-mosque|al-Aqsa Mosque]] and prayed behind him. He recites verses from the praise-poetry of [[person/ahmed-shawqi|Shawqi]], cites the Qur'an's commendation of his great character, and says that God swore by no one's life as He swore by his. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:08:00}

He notes that it begins with the basmala, which is the creature's relation to the Creator, and ends with the word "people", which is the creature's relation to the creature, and that what lies between its covers orders both relations, so that "religion is how one deals with others". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:09:00}

He then illustrates a flaw in it with someone who prays and fasts and yet says: had the doctor not reached us in time, the boy would have died. He replies that death comes by God's decree, not by the doctor's treatment or the lack of it, and that a person's part is only to take the means. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:11:00}

He concludes that this universe has a Lord who distributes tasks among His creatures and puts them at one another's service. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:15:00}
- He returns to the example of the doctor: if the treating doctor were away or had died, would the child die? He answers that the child dies at the time God wills, not at the time people suppose, and that a person struck by a car would have died at that moment even had he not crossed the road. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:16:00}
- He attributes to al-Shafi'i that, asked for the proof of God's existence, he answered with the mulberry leaf: a beast eats it and brings it out as dung, the silkworm as silk, the bee as honey and the gazelle as musk. Who made the origin one and the outcomes many, if not God? He contrasts this with the machine, which turns out only one kind of product. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:17:00}

He warns against leaning on the creature, like the man who carries a minister's card of recommendation and counts his son's appointment as assured, and calls for [[concept/tawakkul|reliance on the Creator]] with the means kept in their proper place. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:18:00}

His advice: do not say, Lord, I have a great worry; say, worry, I have a great Lord. He defines the summit of monotheism as the servant seeing no doer in the universe but God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep08 00:21:30}

## Episode 9: the sanctity of life and "moral killing"
- He announces an attribute he counts among the most important for the security of society, the one in [[passage/quran-25-68|verse 68 of Surat al-Furqan]]: refraining from killing the soul that God has made inviolable, except by right. He says plainly that he will not deal with the rulings on retaliation, blood money and bodily injury, because their place is the programmes and recognised books of jurisprudence, not this programme. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:02:30}
- He puts forward a notion he calls "moral killing". His example is a man of keen mind married to a woman who discourages him whenever he speaks to her of his work and his success: she changes the subject, or makes light of his standing, or is moved to it by envy. In his view this kills the genius in him, and envy between spouses can come from either side. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:04:30}
- He passes on a piece of advice he attributes to the scholars for anyone who wants to marry: not to marry "the pining woman", "the woman who reminds of her favours" or "the moaning woman", and he adds, as a joke, "the nagging woman". The first, for him, pines for a former husband; the second holds her money and her support over her husband; the third complains of her aches so much that her husband starts his day depressed; and the fourth repeats a request twenty times until his chest tightens. He counts all of this as kinds of moral killing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:09:30}
- He says of himself that he has been reading faces for forty years, and that some faces cannot be read: the frozen face that does not change whether he speaks to it of mercy or punishment, of the Garden or the Fire. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:06:30}
- He describes the constant complainer at work: he sees himself as the only genius among hundreds of millions of Arabs, and explains his lack of advancement by the envy and hypocrisy of his manager and colleagues. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep09 00:08:30}
- He digresses with an opinion of his own: that osteoporosis and infertility now afflict the young, which he puts down to hormones and pesticides in imported food, accusing its exporters of not wanting the community to have offspring. He criticises stopping at two children and using contraceptives to that end. ## Episode 10: "nor do they commit fornication" — closing the ways to indecency
- He sums up the previous lesson on literal and moral killing, adding to the moral kind the appointment of someone who neither fears God nor shows mercy to those under him. He then moves to the rest of the verse: keeping away from [[concept/zina|fornication]]. He states that when societies leave the Book and the Sunna, disorder spreads in them and social ills appear, and that the duty is "to close Satan's entrances". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:02:00}
- His example is the delaying of [[practice/nikah|marriage]]: a young man and a young woman of thirty not yet married, and a father who insists on demands the suitor cannot meet. He recalls that the Companions made the lawful easy: the suitor was asked for something, even an iron ring, and if he had none he was married on what he knew of the Qur'an as a [[practice/mahr|dowry]]. He asks his listeners: if a suitor came to you who owned nothing but his memorisation of the Qur'an, would you marry your daughter to him? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep10 00:03:30}

## Episode 11: the outcome for whoever does these things, and the rights of a Muslim
- He joins what went before to the end of [[passage/quran-25-68|verse 68]] and to [[passage/quran-25-69|verse 69]]: the outcome for whoever takes another god beside God, kills a soul or commits indecency. On the word atham he reports two readings: one, from some exegetes, that it is the name of a valley, and another that it is the requital of sin. He states that whoever loses God's side has gained nothing, and whoever gains it has lost nothing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:02:30}
- He counts it the great disaster that people receive the commands and prohibitions as if they were meant for others: a Muslim sits describing Muslims as negligent and lazy and does not ask himself what he has contributed. He proposes a daily question: what have I offered Islam today? Prayer and fasting, in his view, are for the one who performs them, and their fruit is visiting the sick, following funerals, opening doors of good and bringing joy to people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:04:00}
- He draws attention to setting one's intention right when visiting the sick: it should not be the return of a courtesy to someone who attended one's own occasions, but intended for God, as the discharge of a Muslim's right. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:05:00}
- He lists six rights that a Muslim has over his brother, as he gives them: to greet him on meeting him, to visit him when he is ill, to congratulate him when good comes to him, to console him when a calamity strikes him, to respond to him when he sneezes and praises God, and to follow his funeral when he dies. He reports that the righteous among the early generations were offered condolences for three days if one of them missed the opening takbir or the congregational prayer. (needs_source: the recording is badly garbled here) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep11 00:07:30}

## Episode 12: "except those who repent" — repentance, and emptying before adorning
- He states that God opens the door of [[concept/tawba|repentance]] to whoever wants it, and explains the exception in [[passage/quran-25-70|verse 70 of Surat al-Furqan]] by its three elements: he repented, believed and did righteous work. He sets out repentance: to desist, to feel remorse, to resolve not to return, and then to give people back their rights. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:01:30}
- He distinguishes material rights, which are to be returned, from moral ones: he does not think the penitent should disclose to the person wronged what he said of him if the disclosure would cause greater harm; it is enough to pardon one another and to pray for him in his absence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:02:00}
- On why faith is mentioned after repentance he answers that repentance is the first step on the road of true faith. He defines righteous work as that by which God's face is sought, and attributes to Umar that he asked his Lord for the most sincere and the most correct of work, explaining the most sincere as what is purely for God and the most correct as what accords with the Book and the Sunna. (needs_source: the attribution of the saying to Umar is the lecturer's and has not been verified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:02:30}
- He widens the notion of righteous work to every deed that brings a lawful benefit in this world or the next, and everything that makes others happy and builds society. In his opinion it includes the work of the censor who cuts bad scenes from serials and films, and he would like a committee to consider what is fit for society before it is broadcast. (The lecturer's view.) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:03:30}
- He gives his opinion on atheism: he accepts philosophical thought, logic and sociology, but does not count atheism as thought; he calls it a sickness whose holder needs treatment. (The lecturer's view.) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:04:00}
- He explains the changing of evil deeds into good ones as meaning that someone who was recorded in the column of disbelief and disobedience comes to be recorded in the column of faith and obedience, and that repentance is put in the place of the evil deed and good deeds are then built on it, as a tower is built on a sound foundation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:05:00}
- He passes on from the scholars the rule of "emptying before adorning". His example is a merchant in debt to the bank: every profit that comes to him goes first to paying his debt, so his profit is not secure until his capital is. In the same way the penitent first rids himself of sins and disobedience and then adorns himself with remembrance, reliance on God, truthfulness and reciting the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:06:30}
- He gives the example of a man who seized land his sisters had inherited from their father and sold it to go on the hajj and the umra, and states that this is not accepted so long as the money is unlawful, and that the pilgrimage does not cancel wrongs done to people. He likens it to someone who wants to hang a picture before he has built the house and raised its walls. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep12 00:07:30}

## Episode 13: "they do not bear false witness" — an incomplete recording
- He announces a new attribute from [[passage/quran-25-72|verse 72 of Surat al-Furqan]]: that they do not bear false witness. He states that false testimony tears societies down and loses people their rights, giving to one who has no right and denying the one who has, and that through it spreads [[concept/zulm|injustice]], which he defines as putting a thing where it does not belong. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:01:30}
- He recalls that God has forbidden injustice to Himself and made it forbidden among His servants, and that the worst of wrongdoers is the one who wrongs those who have no helper but God: the weak, the orphan, the widow, the aged and the poor. He describes false testimony as the last nail in the coffin of any civilisation or state. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:02:30}
- He cites the hadith on the gravest of the major sins: associating partners with God and undutifulness to parents, and then that the Prophet, who had been reclining, sat up and repeated the warning against false testimony until those present wished he would stop. From his sitting up he infers that what followed was the most serious. In the course of this he goes so far as to say that good works do not avail one who is undutiful to his parents and that sins do not harm one who is dutiful to them, a statement he makes without a source. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:03:30}
- He joins cheating to false testimony, and relates that the Prophet passed a seller of food in the market, put his hand into the heap and found it damp; he asked him, and the man blamed the rain; the Prophet told him to put that part on top so that people could see it, and said that whoever cheats is not one of them. His example is a seller who puts the good fruit at the top of the box and the spoiled at the bottom. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:04:30}
- He criticises the presence of hired false witnesses at the doors of some courts in Arab and Muslim countries, one of whom will testify for a few coins so that a person's right is cut away. He then begins to speak of false testimony in vouching for one's children at the time of marriage, and the recording in the archive breaks off at this point, after about five and a half minutes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep13 00:05:00}

## Episode 14: evil deeds turned into good, and the wide door of repentance
- He returns to [[passage/quran-25-70|verse 70]] and the changing of evil deeds into good, and gives as an example a non-Muslim man who committed grave sins for fifty years and was then guided by God to Islam: it is as if he had just been born with a blank page; the "emptying" is complete for him, and he begins the "adorning". {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:02:30}
- He states that when God wills repentance for a servant He opens its road to him, and relates that [[person/al-fudayl-ibn-iyad|al-Fudayl ibn Iyad]] was a highway robber who climbed a house to rob it and heard its owner reciting a verse that asks whether the time has not come for the believers' hearts to be humbled; he said: it has come, Lord, and began a new page. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:04:00}
- He cites a traditional maxim: the door of action may be opened to a servant while the door of acceptance is closed to him, and a sin may be decreed for him that becomes the cause of his arriving. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:04:00}
- He relates the conversion of Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib: he came back from hunting and was told that Abu Jahl had hurt his nephew, so he went to him, split his head and said: do you strike him when I follow his religion? He struck his brother-in-law and slapped his sister until she bled; then his heart softened and he asked for the sheet; she told him to purify himself first; he read from it, asked where the Prophet was, went to [[building/dar-al-arqam|the house of al-Arqam]] and embraced Islam. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:07:30}

- He lists the witnesses to an act of disobedience, using the example of a bribe: the hand that took it, the tongue that agreed it, the eye that saw, the ear that heard and the foot that walked; then the ground on which it happened, the time, by night or by day, the two recording angels, and God, the best of witnesses. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:10:30}

- He states that there is no doorkeeper at God's door: someone who wants to bring a grievance to a minister or an official is turned back by guards and office hours, but whoever knocks at God's door at three in the morning is not turned away. He cites the sense of the hadith that God extends His hand by night so that the sinner of the day may repent and by day so that the sinner of the night may repent, and the hadith that God rejoices at His servant's repentance more than a man who lost his camel, with his food and drink upon it, and then found it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:18:30}

When the man saw that none of these was possible, he repented sincerely. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep14 00:23:00}

## Episode 15: "they pass by with dignity" — turning away from idle talk and guarding the tongue
- He announces the next attribute from [[passage/quran-25-72|verse 72 of Surat al-Furqan]]: that when they pass by idle talk they pass by with dignity. He defines laghw, in the language, as everything worthless and of no value, whether word or deed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:01:30}
- He first takes idle talk to mean people's mockery of the good, citing what is in [[passage/quran-83|Surat al-Mutaffifin]] about the wrongdoers laughing and winking at the believers, and then the reversal of the situation on the Day of Resurrection. His example is someone who greets a bearded man or a woman in a face veil with jokes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:02:30}
- He recalls that mockery of the people of truth is old: Pharaoh said of Musa that he feared he would change his people's religion or bring corruption to the land, and the notables of Mecca said of the Prophet that he was mad, a sorcerer and a soothsayer. His explanation is that the mocker feels a deficiency in himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:04:00}
- He takes the headscarf in workplaces as an example: he explains a man's irritation at a veiled colleague by a desire in himself, finds it strange that a woman should be irritated by another woman's headscarf, and asks the one who mocks people who pray: have they harmed you by their prayer? (The lecturer's view.) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:05:30}
- He mentions a second meaning of idle talk: all acts of disobedience, so that passing them with dignity means turning away from them, disapproving of them and not sitting with their people until they move on to other talk. He asks the man who went into a gathering of water-pipes and songs and then said he had not been happy about it: and what brought you to that place? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:06:00}
- He attributes to [[person/ibn-ata-allah-al-iskandari|Ibn Ata']] a measure for knowing a servant's standing with his Lord: to look at what He has set him to do. Someone whose occupation brings him near to God, such as overseeing Islamic books or school curricula, giving the call to prayer, teaching people, or practising medicine with knowledge and skill, has in that, for him, a sign of being loved. He contrasts him with someone who has spent twenty years beating a drum behind a band, and calls on him to review his account. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:07:30}
- He observes that people speak thousands of words a day, and cites the traditional saying: whoever talks much slips much, whoever slips much sins much, and whoever sins much has long regret. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:08:30}
- He likens the person who tears down his good deeds by backbiting, [[concept/namimah|tale-bearing]] and attacking people's honour to someone who put up a fine building that people admired and then called in a company to demolish it. Holding back from people's honour, in his view, is better than many voluntary acts of worship, in the sense of [[concept/hifz-al-lisan|guarding the tongue]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:09:30}
- He reports of [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]] that he addressed the [[building/kaaba|Kaaba]], magnifying its sanctity, and then said that the sanctity of the believer with God is greater than its own. He asks: if pulling a stone out of the Kaaba is a grave thing, what of breaking a believer and hurting him? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:10:30}
- He ties this to what was said earlier on repentance and restoring rights: whoever has spoken ill of people behind their backs should, whenever he remembers it, pray for their good, so that his prayer becomes credit for him when they claim their due on the Day of Resurrection. He states that repentance is a duty, but that leaving the sin is a greater duty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:11:30}

He concludes that faulting people for being fat, thin or plain is faulting what the Creator created. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:12:30}
- After the break he observes that many gatherings, under the name of chat and small talk, attack people's honour and make fun of their looks. He recalls that whoever defends his brother's honour, God will keep the Fire from him on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:15:00}
- He criticises spouses who insult each other's families: the husband who blames his son's poor schoolwork on the boy's maternal uncle, and the wife who blames her daughter's mistake on the girl's paternal aunt. He asks: why are the absent used to hurt those present? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:16:30}
- He cites the Prophet's manners when some of the Jews of Medina greeted him with a word meaning death: he would answer only "and upon you". When Aisha answered them harshly he checked her and said that the believer is not foul-mouthed or coarse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:17:30}
- He relates that Umar, making his rounds at night to look after his subjects, passed people who had lit a fire in front of their tent and greeted them as "people of the light", avoiding the words "people of the fire". He distinguishes looking into people's condition for their good from spying. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:18:00}
- He likens the tongue to a ladle and the heart to a pot: the ladle brings out only what is in the pot. He rejects the excuse made for a sharp-tongued person that "his heart is white", and states that a person is known for what he really is in the hour of anger and dispute. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:19:30}
- He states that someone sitting among his superiors who hears something wrong should reject it in his heart if he can do no more, and that someone who has moral courage should make the point with courtesy, citing the sense of the hadith on a word of truth before an unjust ruler. He relates that [[person/izz-al-din-ibn-abd-al-salam|al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam]] admonished the Ayyubid sultan and raised his voice to him, and when his students asked whether he had not been afraid, he said that he had called to mind the majesty of his Lord and saw the sultan before him like a small cat. (needs_source: the sultan's name is garbled in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:20:30}
- He ends by describing the believer among people as like a fragrant flower and like a bee: when it eats it eats what is good, when it feeds it feeds what is good, and when it settles on a twig it does not break it. He mentions the employee who cannot leave a place where people's honour is being attacked, and so rejects it in his heart, and cites the saying that whoever is present at a sin and rejects it in his heart is like one who was absent from it, and whoever is absent from it and approves of it is like one who was present. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep15 00:22:30}

## Episode 16: pondering God's signs — not falling on them deaf and blind
- He announces the day's attribute from [[passage/quran-25-73|verse 73 of Surat al-Furqan]]: that when they are reminded of the signs of their Lord they do not fall upon them deaf and blind. He reports from the exegetes that one of its meanings is being reminded of the hereafter and the return. He explains the verb kharra as falling without order, as a roof collapses, and takes the point to be a denial that they stay in their former state as though they had not heard. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:02:30}
- He asks what made the early generations so moved by the signs, and mentions that under Umar's eyes there were two dark lines from much weeping. He addresses these words to everyone who is responsible for people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:04:30}

- He recalls the hadith of the woman who shut up a cat, neither feeding it nor letting it eat of the creatures of the earth, and compares to it someone who shuts up a whole people and then waits for mercy to descend. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:05:30}
- He passes on from Ibn Mas'ud the counsel to read the Qur'an as if it had come down to the reader alone, and criticises those who turn the verses of threat toward other people, in the sense of [[concept/tadabbur-al-quran|pondering the Qur'an]]. He relates that the Prophet passed by a woman at night who was reciting the opening of Surat al-Ghashiya and answered: it has reached me. He recalls that what counts is the generality of the wording, not the particular occasion. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:07:00}
- He likens people before the hereafter to two pupils: a bright one who knows the examination is coming, prepares for it and does not mind when it falls, and a lazy one who busies himself asking about the form and language of the examination. He compares to the latter someone who asks in what language he will be questioned in his grave. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:08:00}
- He recalls that on the Day of Resurrection every person is told to read his own book himself, whether he could read in this world or not, that he reckons himself by what he sent ahead, and that the book leaves out nothing small or great. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:09:00}
- He calls on the listener to [[concept/muhasabat-al-nafs|take himself to account]] at such verses: has he wronged his family, taken what was not his, or hurt a humble employee by docking his pay so that the man prayed against him at night? He advises a person to go to sleep with the thought that he may not wake. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:09:30}
- He distinguishes the one who hears God's verses and acts on them from the one who hears them and they become evidence against him, and states that the Qur'an is either a proof for the servant or a proof against him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:10:30}
- His example of going against the verses is a man with daughters whose wife presses him to sign over to them everything he owns by way of sale, so that their paternal uncles do not inherit. He counts this as going against God's Book and depriving an heir of his inheritance, and calls for [[concept/mirath|inheritance]] to follow the prescribed division: two thirds to the daughters, the wife her share, and the remainder to the paternal uncle. (The lecturer's view.) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:12:00}
- After the break he calls for taking lessons from the events of life, and works through the example of a child's illness: the doctor whose family spent on him until he graduated, the rented clinic and its guards, the nurse, the laboratory with its equipment and the factory that made it, the pharmacist, and the transport companies that carried the medicine. He says that all of these earned their living from the child's temperature rising two degrees, and that this is one of the doors of provision that God opens. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:16:30}
- He adds that when the patient does not recover with the first doctor he goes to a second, because the second has a provision written for him with this patient since he was in his mother's womb. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:17:00}
- His example of pondering is a father who asks why his son does not wake for the dawn prayer or stumbles in his studies, and so reviews his money: perhaps there is an unlawful coin in the boy's pillow, his blanket or his school fees. He says that when the unlawful comes in upon the lawful it scatters it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:19:30}
- He warns against taunting a relative for bringing up his children badly, since the one who taunts may be tried with the same. He cites the sense of the well-known line: people find fault with their age, and the fault is in themselves. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:20:30}
- He distinguishes sight from insight with an example: someone with a hundred in his pocket gives ten in charity; by sight he has ninety left, and by insight he has a hundred and ninety, because a good deed counts tenfold. He criticises the materialist outlook that sees only the loss. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:21:30}
- He ends with the lesson of [[person/yusuf|Yusuf]]: his brothers threw him into the well, and later he was set over the storehouses of Egypt in the years of famine, and they came to him asking him to give them full measure and be charitable to them. The hands that had hurt him became the lower hand through their disobedience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep16 00:22:30}

## Episode 17: "comfort of our eyes" — spouses, offspring and leadership in piety
- He announces the last attribute, in [[passage/quran-25-74|verse 74 of Surat al-Furqan]]: the prayer of the servants of the Most Merciful, "Our Lord, grant us from our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes, and make us leaders for the godfearing". He explains that the word zawj in the language applies to husband and wife alike, so the prayer comes from wife and husband together: a wife asks for a righteous husband, and a husband asks that his wife be a comfort to his eyes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:01:30}
- He notes that the wife of Pharaoh was a believer in an unbelieving house, while the wives of Noah and of Lot were unbelievers under two prophets. Each person answers for himself, he says, and a husband's goodness does not save his wife from her own deeds. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:03:30}
- He counts disclosing the secrets of the home to relatives and friends as betrayal, and cites [[passage/quran-66-10|Surat al-Tahrim]] and the sense of a saying that God withdraws His hand from one of two partners when either betrays the other. (needs_source: the attribution of the ruling to the Prophet is the lecturer's and has not been verified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:04:00}
- He reads [[passage/quran-24-26|the verse of bad women for bad men and good women for good men]] as more than a matching of pairs: a bad person inclines to a bad partner, a good one to a good partner, so marriage is not a bargain between a man and a woman without regard to piety. (needs_source: his reading of the verse is his own and has not been checked against a source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:05:00}
- He states that a righteous man may be tried with a bad wife so that God expiates his sins through her, and that a righteous wife may have a husband of bad character, which is a test for her. (needs_source: the story of the wife and the husband's illness is the lecturer's and has no source given) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:05:30}
- He mentions the prayer of guidance (istikhara) when a daughter is to be married, and says that the acceptable husband is one who fears God: if he loves her he honours her, and if he dislikes her he does not wrong her. His reason is that one who fears God is afraid to do wrong. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:06:30}
- He describes the believing woman in terms of what gladdens a husband: when he looks at her it pleases him, when he asks her she obeys, and when he is away she guards herself and his property, in the sense of a well-known saying. He says a smile on her face opens what closes in a house, and that beauty for him is more than form. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:08:30}
- He distinguishes a good life from mere subsistence: subsistence, for him, is a kind of life like that of beasts, whereas a good life needs peace. He says that a home where complaint and quarrel repeat becomes a grave, and that constant bitterness shortens life, in the sense of [[passage/quran-16-97|the verse in Surat al-Nahl]] on the good life. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:09:30}
- He holds that partial happiness, such as health or food alone, is not enough, and that full happiness needs someone who understands you, so that one is with a person who shares one's taste. He cites what is told of Sulayman and the hoopoe in [[passage/quran-27-20|Surat al-Naml]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:11:30}
- He says souls were created acquainted, and so they attract, and unacquainted, and so they repel; some people recognise one another at first meeting, while others live forty years under one roof as strangers. (needs_source: the hadith on gathered souls is ascribed by the lecturer without a named source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:13:30}
- He explains "make us leaders for the godfearing" as meaning that a person should call others to good. He says that whoever starts a good practice has its reward and the reward of all who act by it until the Day of Resurrection, and gives the example of [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]], who opened Egypt, saying that reward attaches to deeds. (The lecturer's view.) (needs_source: the hadith on a good practice and the reward flowing on is the lecturer's, with no specific transmission named) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:16:30}
- He says that the family of the dead are urged not to announce the death to someone who does not keep up kinship, nor bring him to the funeral, and he counts kinship as a bond that death does not cancel. (needs_source: the instructions he describes are his own account, with no documented bequest named) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:18:30}
- He states that provision and term are written before birth, and that no soul dies until it has taken its full provision and term, so a person seeks provision by fair pursuit and prays for good rather than for abundance alone. (needs_source: the sense he gives of the hadith on provision and term is his own, with no source named) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:19:30}
- He recalls the verse at the close of the sura, "Say: my Lord would not care for you were it not for your supplication", and counts supplication the greatest means of meeting what has been decreed; he holds that a supplication is answered in one of three ways, one of which is that it is stored up for the servant in the hereafter. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep17 00:21:00}

## Episode 18: closing the attributes — the chamber for what they endured, and supplication that lifts what was decreed
- He recalls that the verses listed many attributes of the servants of the Most Merciful, from verse 63 to verse 74, and calls on the listener to count them and ask himself whether he has them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:01:30}
- He says a person may have some of the attributes but not others; if he manages most of them in Ramadan he should aim to complete the rest through the year. He holds that false witness, backbiting or pride must be uprooted, not postponed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:03:30}
- He warns that the Qur'an becomes proof against the reader rather than for him when a person reads it and does not bring his conduct into line with it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:04:30}
- He comments on the verse at the close, "those will be rewarded with the chamber for what they endured": he likens the chamber to the cleanest and finest place in the house, and says that in it there is greeting and peace. He tells the story of a weightlifter who finds the weight of life is not in the iron but in the bearing of it, and that burdens grow light when borne with patience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:05:30}
- He widens on patience with people's harm and in obedience, saying that patience in obedience needs certainty and faith. He describes the believer as the bee, which when it eats eats what is good and when it settles on a twig does not break it, and cites the modesty of [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:08:00}
- He reads the close of the sura, "Say: my Lord would not care for you were it not for your supplication", and explains that supplication has two sides: that God may grant the answer quickly, or turn away an affliction in proportion, or store up the reward. He holds that only supplication turns back what has been decreed: the decree descends and the supplication rises. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:11:00}
- He states that kindness to parents adds to one's years, and that prayer turns nothing away from one who is sincere. He counts undutifulness to a father as lack of manners, lack of religion and loss of honour. (needs_source: the saying on prayer reversing the decree is the lecturer's and carries no transmission) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:12:00}
- He mentions people who want to choose the sex of a child, and says that the Creator makes what He wills and chooses. He objects to wanting to know a child's sex before birth, and counts it a shortfall in submission. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:18:00}
- He says the angels enter upon the people of the Garden with greeting and peace, that there is no sleep or fatigue in it, and that God rewards the godfearing for their patience. He asks the listener to reorder his priorities so as to be in the company of the servants of the Most Merciful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:19:30}
- He concludes that every day and every month a person should add to himself one attribute of the servants of the Most Merciful, and notes that knowledge is easy to reach today through computers, so that what is needed is practice rather than more knowledge. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:24:00}
- He sets two brothers side by side, each of whom spent ten times the earth's worth, and states that the world passes, leaving only the face of God, and that wealth and standing do not last. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-ebadu-al-rahman/ep18 00:23:00}
