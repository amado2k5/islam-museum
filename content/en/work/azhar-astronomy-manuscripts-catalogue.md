## Summary
This book is the second publication in the series "Contributions of Arab and Islamic Civilisation to the Sciences" prepared by the Bibliotheca Alexandrina's Center for Documentation of Cultural and Natural Heritage (CULTNAT) in partnership with UNESCO's regional office in Cairo, as a product of the digital documentation programme for al-Azhar Library's astronomy manuscripts. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 5} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 6} It was printed in 2006 in 1,500 copies. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 4}

## Contents
- Al-Azhar Library holds about 434 manuscripts and treatises on astronomy and timekeeping by 136 authors, commentators and compilers, all in Arabic except two in Turkish and one in Persian. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 7}
- The book opens with a scientific introduction, "Astronomy and its applications in Arab-Islamic civilisation", by Ahmad Fuad Basha, professor of physics at Cairo University, then presents 31 selected manuscripts with images, arranged by subject: astronomical instruments; calendar and times; moon, sun, zodiac and planets; trigonometric operations; astronomical tables; and the sphericity of the earth. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 7} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 8} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 14} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 28} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 38} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 62} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 68} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 76}
- A glossary of astronomical terms with foreign equivalents follows, with appendices on *abjad* numerals, the astrolabe, the zodiac and the lunar mansions, and the complete bibliographical list of al-Azhar's astronomy and timekeeping manuscripts. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 7} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 78} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 88} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 92} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 98} {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 104}

## From the introduction
Ahmad Fuad Basha writes that the Arabs called astronomy by several names, among them *ilm al-hay'a*, *ilm al-anwa'* and *ilm ahkam al-nujum*; that they first absorbed the learning of the Greeks, Egyptians, Persians and Indians; that the first astronomical book translated was the *Key of the Stars* attributed to Hermes in Umayyad times, followed by the *Almagest* and the *Sindhind* under al-Mansur; and that a phase of innovation ran from the third to the mid-ninth century AH. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 8} He notes that Ibn Yunus compiled the *Great Hakimi Zij*, which Laplace used for the obliquity of the ecliptic, and that al-Biruni discussed in *al-Qanun al-Mas'udi* seven methods of finding the cardinal directions. {cite:bibalex-azhar-astronomy-manuscripts-2006 p. 10}
