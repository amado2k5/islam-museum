## Summary

A series of thirty-two lectures by Ahmed Elsayed on the biography of the Prophet Muhammad, addressed to reformers and published as audio in his podcast between March and October 2023. It runs from an introduction to a last session on the expeditions of Hunayn and Tabuk and the farewell pilgrimage. As he describes it in the first episode of his 2026 series, it was his second series on the sira and stressed the lessons for reform. These records give what he narrates and explains in each episode, attributed to him, and cite the whole episode with no time. It is a modern secondary source, not a primary source and not independent corroboration of the events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

## The episodes

- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep01|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 1: Introduction]]: The opening lecture of the series on the Prophet's biography for reformers. The speaker explains why the study of the Prophet's life is an act of gratitude and a duty, why his guidance must be read as a whole drawing on both the Sunna and the biographical books, and which three fruits he wants from the series: guidance, understanding of the religion, and firmer faith in the Prophet's mission. He also describes how he will read the biography through the school the Prophet ran and the Companions who were its students.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep02|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 2: Signs Preceding Prophethood]]: The second lecture of the series on the Prophet's biography for reformers, on what happened before the sending. The speaker sorts the events into signs that drew people's attention and signs and preparation aimed at the Prophet himself: the Year of the Elephant, his mother's vision, the opening of his chest, the earlier tidings in the scriptures of the People of the Book, his truthfulness, his shepherding and his retreats. He draws from each the lesson that God prepares those He sends for great tasks.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep03|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 3: The Importance of the Pre-Prophetic Period]]: The third session continues the argument that the period before the prophetic mission matters for more than proving the Prophet's greatness. He gives examples where knowing pre-Islamic practice explains Qur'anic verses and reports, shows how Islam corrected earlier customs, and argues that the Qur'an's content could not have come from the Arabs' own setting or from the People of the Book.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep04|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 4: The Coming of Revelation]]: The fourth session opens the account of the coming of revelation to the Prophet. He stresses the awe and weight of the event, then reads Khadija's words of reassurance, the role of Waraqa ibn Nawfal, and the pause in revelation, drawing lessons for reformers about the gravity of their task and the certainty of trials.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep05|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 5: The Secret Call]]: The fifth session turns from the first revelation to the secret stage of the call. He reflects on the opening verses of revelation as a call to knowledge of God, then describes who first embraced Islam: the young, the freed and enslaved, women and a few men of standing. He draws four lessons for reformers and ends with a passage of Ibn Ishaq on Abu Bakr's role in bringing early converts.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep06|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 6: The earliest converts to Islam]]: The sixth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers examines the earliest Muslims as the core of the Prophet's school. The speaker argues that the Companions' role in Mecca was endurance and preserving the faith, while in Medina they carried many leadership and teaching tasks, and that the early converts held a large share of them. He then discusses the Prophet's concern about the opening of worldly wealth and closes with the foundation that, in his view, explains the rapid conquests.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep07|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 7: The public call]]: The seventh session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers treats the move from the secret to the public call. The speaker recounts the proclamation on al-Safa, Abu Lahab's reaction, the early torture of vulnerable converts, the story of Dhimad, the Qurayshi council on how to label the Prophet and their pressure on Abu Talib. He draws two lessons: the duty of conveying the religion and the centrality of patience.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep08|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 8: The harm done by the polytheists and the verses of steadying]]: The eighth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers continues the early Meccan years. The speaker treats Abu Bakr's buying and freeing of tortured slaves, Khabbab's complaint to the Prophet, the physical harm done to the Prophet at the Kaaba, the themes of early revelation, and the pressure that led toward the migration to Abyssinia, including Abu Bakr's protection under Ibn al-Dughna. He closes with the centrality of the Qur'an for the reformer.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep09|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 9: The migration to Abyssinia]]: The ninth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers closes the first half of the Meccan period. The speaker treats Surat al-Furqan as a case of how the Qur'an steadied the believers and answered the polytheists' objections, the plain way the Prophet conveyed the message, and the migration to Abyssinia: its numbers, the feelings of the emigrants, their contact with Christianity and the Asma bint Umays report. He ends with the conversion of Umar and the protection he still needed.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep10|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 10: The Muslims in Abyssinia]]: The tenth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers stays in Abyssinia and is built around one long report from Umm Salama: the Quraysh embassy with gifts to the Negus, Jafar ibn Abi Talib's account of Islam and his answer about Jesus, the Negus's refusal to hand the emigrants over, and the clash with a rival to his throne. The speaker then draws lessons from it for reformers: clarity about the faith, renewed trials, a firm foundation of knowledge, unity, not bending the truth, and the power of clear expression.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep11|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 11: The Death of Abu Talib]]: The eleventh session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers returns to Mecca: Quraysh's plot to kill the Prophet, Abu Talib's withdrawal with Banu Hashim to the quarter, the three-year social and economic siege and its cancellation, then the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadija in the Year of Sorrow. The speaker explains the Khaybar share for kin, recounts Abu Talib's deathbed and draws lessons on God's right, intercession and bad company, then dwells on Khadija's standing.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep12|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 12: The journey to al-Ta'if, the Night Journey and the Ascension]]: The twelfth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers covers what followed the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadija: the Prophet's journey to al-Ta'if and its rejection, why he counted that day the hardest of his life, the angel of the mountains and his hope for the descendants of his enemies, the jinn who listened to the Qur'an, and his return to Mecca under the protection of Mut'im ibn Adi. The speaker draws lessons on wisdom in dealing with non-Muslims, then gives a short account of the Night Journey and the Ascension, Quraysh's reaction and the place of prayer.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep13|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 13: The pledge of al-Aqaba]]: The thirteenth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers treats the last Meccan events before the migration: the Prophet's presenting himself to the Arab tribes at the markets and the pilgrimage season, the six men of the Khazraj, the twelve of the following year and the seventy-odd of the second pledge of al-Aqaba, drawing on the accounts of Jabir and Ka'b ibn Malik. The speaker discusses how the pledge gave the Ansar their name, their later loyalty at Badr and Uhud, and the lessons he draws for those who work for reform today.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep14|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 14: The Merit of the Ansar]]: The fourteenth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers pauses before the migration to speak about the Ansar as a group and through individual figures: the Prophet's last sermon on them, their identity in Medina, their steadfastness at Uhud, and the lives of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, Usayd ibn Hudayr, Thabit ibn Qays, Abu Talha, Ubada ibn al-Samit, Umm Haram, Haram ibn Milhan and Mu'adh ibn Jabal. He closes with the lesson that the meaning behind their merit, supporting the religion and its messenger, remains open to later believers.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep15|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 15: The Hijra to Medina]]: At the midpoint of the series he turns from Mecca to Medina and tells the story of the Hijra from two Sahih al-Bukhari narrations, one by Aisha and one by Abu Bakr through al-Bara ibn Azib. He draws lessons on prayer before reform work, on taking practical precautions while trusting God, and on trials that come to reformers, and he closes with Anas ibn Malik on the welcome in Medina and the city's new meaning.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep16|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 16: Brotherhood, the Building of the Mosque and the Medinan Society]]: The lecturer follows the Prophet's arrival in Medina: the waiting of the Medinans, the stay at Quba, the purchase of the site and the building of the Prophet's Mosque, the beginning of the call to prayer, the fever of Medina, and the brotherhood between the Emigrants and the Helpers. He closes with the make-up of Medinan society, the Prophet's treaty with the Jews, and the change of the prayer direction as it is treated in Surat al-Baqara.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep17|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 17: The Expeditions and the Preparation for the Battle of Badr]]: The lecturer covers events of the first two years in Medina: the death of As'ad ibn Zurara, the Prophet's marriage to Aisha and anecdotes of her place in his life, and the first expeditions sent against Quraysh, including the Nakhla patrol of Abdullah ibn Jahsh and the revelation that followed it. He then lists the first raids led by the Prophet himself, outlines the departure for Badr and the Prophet's consultation of the Helpers, and ends with the story of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Umayya ibn Khalaf.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep18|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 18: The Battle of Badr]]: The second session on the Battle of Badr. The speaker follows the march from Medina, the discovery that Quraysh had come out in force, the consultation with the Ansar, the night before the battle, the duels, the fighting and the fate of the Quraysh leaders, and closes with the standing of those who fought at Badr.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep19|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 19: The consequences of the Battle of Badr]]: He closes his treatment of the Battle of Badr by adding scenes he had left out and then turns to its consequences: the council on the captives, the quarrel over the spoils, the shock in Medina and Mecca, the open hypocrisy, the thirst for revenge that led to the incident of al-Raji and to Uhud, and the status of the Badr veterans. He then covers events of the second year, the story of Ali's two she-camels and Hamza, the gradual prohibition of wine, and the two kinds of criterion between truth and falsehood.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep20|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 20: The Battle of Uhud]]: The speaker follows the months between Badr and Uhud: the expedition to Banu Sulaym, the raid of al-Sawiq, the confrontation with Banu Qaynuqa, and the Medinan routine of the Prophet. He then narrates the battle of Uhud in the third year of the Hijra from Quraysh's preparations to Abu Sufyan's departure, with the terrain, the archers, the reversal, the defence of the Prophet and the burials.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep21|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 21: The Effects of Uhud]]: Ahmed Elsayed resumes the series after the Ramadan sessions and draws out the lessons of the Battle of Uhud, relying mostly on the chapter Ibn al-Qayyim devotes to its wisdoms in Zad al-Ma'ad. He then follows the aftermath, the steadfastness at Hamra al-Asad, and the sacrifices of the Companions who protected the Prophet.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep22|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 22: Bi'r Ma'una and Banu al-Nadir]]: Ahmed Elsayed turns to the events between Uhud and the Trench. He treats the tragedy of Bi'r Ma'una, the Prophet's grief and the prayer of supplication that followed, and then the expulsion of Banu al-Nadir, with the lessons he draws for reformers, ending on the hypocrites who abandoned their allies.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep23|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 23: The Battle of the Trench]]: The lecturer opens the account of the Battle of the Trench, the last time the polytheists marched on Medina. He covers the causes, the digging of the trench and the hardships of the siege, the breach of the covenant by Banu Qurayza, the inner trials of believers and hypocrites, the delayed prayer, and the wind that broke the allied camp.
- [[work/ahmed-el-sayed-seerah-lilmuslihin-ep24|The Prophetic Biography for Reformers, episode 24: The Expedition of Banu Qurayza]]: The lecturer continues from the Battle of the Trench to the siege of Banu Qurayza: the angel Jibril's summons, the terror cast into the besieged, their surrender and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh's judgment, and Sa'd's prayer and death. He then treats the marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh as the practical end of adoption, and closes with the dispute over praying the afternoon prayer on the road, which he reads as a model of accepted disagreement.
