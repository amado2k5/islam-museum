## Summary

Ahmed Elsayed covers the expedition of Dhat al-Salasil, the Bakr and Quraysh breach of the Hudaybiyya pact, the letter of Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a, the march of ten thousand in Ramadan of year 8, the night meeting with Abu Sufyan, the entry into Mecca and the general amnesty. He draws lessons for reformers on verification, weighing a person's past service, and the Prophet's care for morale and humility. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### Expedition of Dhat al-Salasil

- He relates that about a month after Mu'ta the Prophet sent a force of three hundred north under Amr ibn al-As, a recent convert who had earlier shown hostility to the Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He says that, according to the biographers, the Prophet heard that the tribes of Lakhm and Judham had gathered against him, and that Amr, judging their numbers too large, asked for reinforcements. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet sent two hundred more, including Abu Bakr and Umar, under Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, with an instruction that the two commanders must not disagree. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Amr told Abu Ubayda he was only reinforcement and that he himself was the commander, and that the gentle Abu Ubayda yielded, so Amr led the prayer and the force. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that on this expedition Amr forbade fires at night and forbade pursuing the routed enemy, and that the Prophet approved his reasons: fires would reveal how few the Muslims were, and pursuit risked a counterattack. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Amr then asked the Prophet who was dearest to him, and was told first Aisha, then among men her father, then Umar, and so on, which he reads as a check on Amr's sense of his own standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He draws from this period the point that the Prophet's life had no idle gaps, with raids, teaching, night prayer and revelation all running side by side. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### Bakr's attack on Khuza'a and the appeal to the Prophet

- He relates that within about two months Banu Bakr, allies of Quraysh under the Hudaybiyya pact, attacked Khuza'a, who were allied to the Prophet, over an old blood feud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Quraysh supplied Bakr with weapons during the attack and that a number of Khuza'a were killed, at the watering place of al-Watir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Amr ibn Salim al-Khuza'i came to the Prophet and appealed to him in verse to honour the alliance, and that the Prophet promised him victory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He says that a chain going back to Muhammad ibn Abbad ibn Ja'far reports that the Prophet sent to Quraysh asking them to pay blood money or announce war, and that they did neither, so he resolved to march on Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He notes that after Hudaybiyya the Muslims had grown from fourteen hundred and that notable Meccans such as Khalid ibn al-Walid, Amr ibn al-As and Uthman ibn Talha had accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### Secrecy and the letter of Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a

- He relates that the Prophet gathered the army with even greater secrecy than usual and prayed that news of it be hidden from Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a wrote to Quraysh warning of the coming army and sent the letter with a woman, and that the Prophet was informed by revelation of a woman at Rawdat Khakh carrying it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He observes that revelation rarely settled tactical matters, citing the trench and the decision at Uhud, which were decided by consultation, and takes the revelation here as a sign of how grave the matter was. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He holds that the conquest of Mecca had more than one exceptional feature, including a legal one, namely that fighting in Mecca was permitted to the Prophet for a single hour of a day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet sent Ali, al-Zubayr and al-Miqdad, whom he counts among the best horsemen, and that they made the woman produce the letter from her hair. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He draws the point for reform work that a leader should know his people's abilities from having tested them and should choose the right person for each task. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### The inquiry into Hatib and Umar's request

- He stresses that Hatib was a veteran of Badr and Uhud with no record of doubt or hypocrisy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He holds that the Prophet's method was to ask and hear the accused before any penalty, since the man's record contradicted the act. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Hatib answered that he was an outsider attached to Quraysh without kin there, and sought a favour with them to protect his family, not out of leaving his religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that a report from Jabir in the Musnad of Ahmad, with a sound chain by his account, adds that Hatib said he knew God would help His Messenger, and he reads this as showing the act did not aim to harm the Muslims but only lost the element of surprise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet said Hatib had told the truth, and that Umar then asked to strike off the neck of this hypocrite. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet answered that Hatib had been at Badr and that God may have forgiven the people of Badr, and he notes the Prophet did not say the act deserved no punishment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He draws the principle that a person's earlier service for the faith, especially in hard times, should be weighed and may offset faults, without calling the fault anything but a fault. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that a Qur'anic passage was revealed about the incident, and takes from it how serious allying with the enemies of the believers is, even when the act itself did not break the Muslims' strength. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The march of ten thousand

- He relates that the Prophet set out for Mecca in Ramadan of the same year as Mu'ta and Dhat al-Salasil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He cites a report in the Musnad of Ahmad from Ibn Abbas that Sulaym came with seven hundred and Muzayna with a thousand, and he stresses that a thousand from outside Medina was unprecedented. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He counts the army at ten thousand, growing from fourteen hundred in about two years, and sees this as the fruit of the clear victory of Hudaybiyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that near Mecca the Prophet told the men they were close to the enemy and that breaking the fast would give them strength, and that he then broke his fast and stayed so to the end of the month. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, long in Mecca, met the army on the road as an emigrant with his family and turned back with it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### Quraysh without news and Abu Sufyan's night encounter

- He says Quraysh lived in anxiety with no news, since Hatib's letter had been seized, and that their former intelligence was now plainly weakened along with their other means of power. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He reads a long report in which al-Abbas, fearing Quraysh would be destroyed if the army entered by force, rode the Prophet's white mule to find someone to warn them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that al-Abbas heard Abu Sufyan and Budayl ibn Warqa' talking, that Abu Sufyan had come out to gather news and saw the Muslims' fires, and that Budayl guessed they were Khuza'a's. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that al-Abbas took Abu Sufyan up behind him on the mule to seek protection, and that as they passed the fires Umar recognised him and ran to the Prophet, asking to kill him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that al-Abbas said he had granted protection, and that when Umar pressed, he said Umar would not speak so if the man were of Umar's own clan, to which Umar replied that al-Abbas's conversion was dearer to him than his own father's would have been. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He notes that Umar had once urged at Badr that prisoners be executed, so he was the last person one could accuse of tribal partiality. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### Abu Sufyan before the Prophet

- He relates that the Prophet told al-Abbas to take Abu Sufyan to his lodging and bring him in the morning. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He contrasts the man who had been leader at Uhud with his present position, riding behind a mule and facing either belief or execution. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet asked Abu Sufyan whether the time had not come to know there is no god but God, that Abu Sufyan said that had there been another god he would have helped, and that on the Prophet's status he still hesitated. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that al-Abbas urged him to testify at once, and that Abu Sufyan did so. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that al-Abbas asked the Prophet to give Abu Sufyan something to take pride in, and that the Prophet declared those who entered Abu Sufyan's house or closed their doors would be safe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He says the saying about Abu Sufyan's house may belong to a later moment, joined here in the narration, and that God knows best. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### The army passes before Abu Sufyan

- He relates that the Prophet told al-Abbas to hold Abu Sufyan in a narrow place of the valley so God's troops would pass by. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He holds that the Prophet always cared about the psychological side of conflict, giving the examples of the sacrificial animals at Hudaybiyya and the quick pace in the circuits at the Umrat al-Qada. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He sees two benefits in leaving the tribes in separate units under their own banners: rivalry that spurs a lagging tribe, and kin loyalty that makes a group fight harder. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan asked about each banner as it passed, and finally saw the green battalion of the Emigrants and Ansar clad in iron, and said no one could face them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan remarked to al-Abbas that his nephew's kingdom had become great, and that al-Abbas answered that it was prophethood, not kingship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He cites a version in Sahih al-Bukhari from Urwa in which the Ansar pass with Sa'd ibn Ubada carrying their banner. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### Dividing the army and the persons to be killed

- He relates that the Prophet divided the army into several columns to enter Mecca from different directions, each leader planting his banner at an assigned place, so as to scatter any defenders. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He names al-Zubayr, Khalid ibn al-Walid and Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah among the commanders. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He observes that Khalid, who had commanded the Meccan cavalry against the Prophet at Hudaybiyya under two years earlier, now led a column, and attributes the change to Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet ordered a few named persons killed even if found clinging to the Kaaba's covering, among them Abdullah ibn Khatal, who had gone far in harming the Muslims and the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He holds that the Prophet treated verbal abuse and satire more severely than physical harm, citing his forgiveness of the bedouin who drew a sword on him and of the woman who poisoned the sheep, against the sentences on Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, two satirising women and Ibn Khatal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He cites a report in Sahih Muslim from Abu Hurayra that Quraysh had gathered a mixed crowd, and that the Prophet had the Ansar ordered to move against them and meet him at al-Safa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### Entry into Mecca with little fighting

- He relates that Abu Sufyan told the Prophet Quraysh were being wiped out, and that the Prophet repeated the promise of safety for those who closed their doors or entered Abu Sufyan's house. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He says people shut their doors and in practice there was no battle, only slight resistance, as the columns entered. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He stresses that God had made Mecca lawful to the Prophet for a short while that day, so the fate of Quraysh lay in a single word from him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### The Prophet at the Kaaba and the general amnesty

- He describes the Prophet standing at the Kaaba among the places of Meccan memory: the torture of Bilal, the afterbirth thrown on him, Abu Jahl's threats, Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt's abuse, the beating of Abu Dharr, the killing of Sumayya and Yasir and the torture of Ammar, and the insults of Abu Lahab and his wife. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet asked the Quraysh what they thought he would do, that they answered that he was a noble brother and nephew, and that he replied he would say what Joseph said, that there was no reproach on them that day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He says this is why they were called the Tulaqa, the released. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that there were three hundred and sixty idols around the Kaaba, which the Prophet and his Companions struck down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He notes that the Mecca-period verse about the vanishing of falsehood was revealed in Surat al-Isra' near a prayer he reads as preparing the Prophet for the Hijra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### The Prophet's humility and the cleansing of the sanctuary

- He relates that the biographers say the Prophet entered Mecca with his head bowed in humility to God, and he sets this against the arrogance of those who left their homes boastfully. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet cleansed the Kaaba of idols and sent commanders to destroy those outside Mecca: al-Lat at Ta'if, al-Uzza at Wadi Nakhla, and Manat towards Qudayd, with Khalid ibn al-Walid sent to al-Uzza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet prayed two rak'as inside the Kaaba, that the call to prayer was raised, and that he led the people in prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that inside he found a painting of Ibrahim and Ismail holding divining arrows, removed it, and said that he had never used such arrows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}

### The sermon on the sanctity of Mecca and no hijra after the conquest

- He relates that the Prophet gave a sermon, reported by al-Bukhari, saying that God made Mecca sacred, that bloodshed and cutting trees there are forbidden, and that fighting there was allowed him only for an hour. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that the Prophet also said, according to al-Bukhari, that there is no hijra after the conquest but only striving and intention. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates from Ibn Kathir that most commentators take the conquest mentioned in the verse distinguishing earlier spenders from later ones to be the conquest of Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
- He relates that it is agreed that the conquest in Surat al-Nasr is the conquest of Mecca, and calls it the greatest direct victory over enemies in the Prophet's biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep31}
