## Summary
'Hamasat' ('Whispers') is the name under which the Assabile archive keeps fourteen short episodes by [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]], each twelve to sixteen minutes long. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar} He himself names the programme 'The Prophet's Counsels to Women' («وصايا الرسول للنساء») in its first episode and welcomes listeners back to it under that name in later ones; the word 'Hamasat' is never spoken in the episodes. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep12 00:01:00} Each episode rests on one hadith, whose collection and narrator he names, often with al-Albani's grading, which he supports with verses he describes, then with stories of the Companions, the Successors and present-day life, then with direct advice and a closing prayer. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:01:00} He addresses Muslim women, at times turning to husbands, fathers and young men, and presents the twelfth episode as a counsel for women and men alike. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep12 00:01:00} In the first episode he greets his listeners for the month and prays that they leave Ramadan freed from the Fire, and in the fifth he prays for their fasting and night prayer to be accepted, so both were recorded in Ramadan by his own words; no year is given. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:01:30}

What this page holds is his explanation and advice; hadiths and their gradings are as he attributes them, and numbers are his statements. Contested points of fiqh and remarks about particular groups in these episodes are not written here; they are recorded in the lane's log.

## The episodes
**Episode 1: The maker of men.** [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] opens the programme during Ramadan, announcing it as the first episode of a new programme he has titled «وصايا الرسول للنساء» ('The Prophet's counsels to women'); the episode is called «صانعة الرجال» ('The maker of men'), and he asks why the Prophet singled out women for so many counsels. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:02:00} His answer is that woman is half of society and bears the other half, so that she is "a whole nation" and the soundness of the umma depends on hers; he cites the Arab saying that behind every great man stands a mother. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:02:30} He recounts that [[person/safiyya-bint-abd-al-muttalib|Safiyya]], widowed when al-Awwam died, spent her life raising her son [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]], making arrow-shaping his play and beating him hard when he erred; when one of his paternal uncles told her she beat him like one who hated him, she answered in verse that she beat him so he would grow shrewd and defeat armies, and al-Zubayr became one of the ten promised Paradise. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:03:00} He says [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] grew up an orphan raised by his mother on love of knowledge and the Sunna, and that the mother of [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]] told him to seek knowledge while she provided for him with her spindle. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:04:00} He holds that because the man leaves at dawn after his livelihood, the true educator is the woman, and that a man who chose his wife well would set a whole generation right. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:05:30} He lists women's firsts: the first to dwell at the Sanctuary was a woman, [[person/hajar|Hagar]]; the first martyr of Islam was a woman, [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya bint Khayyat]]; and the mother is put before the father three times. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:06:00} He says the Qur'an has a sura named al-Nisa' ('Women') and none named for men, a sura named after a woman, Maryam, and one revealed over a woman's complaint, al-Mujadila. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:06:30} He states that Islam made men and women equal in duties, reward and punishment except for the arduous duties, such as jihad, the Friday prayer and congregational prayers. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:07:00} He says Islam counts a daughter as God's gift to her father, and that the Prophet commended women in the Farewell Sermon before more than a hundred thousand pilgrims, by his count; he mentions the hadith declaring inviolable the right of the two weak ones, the woman and the orphan, and the hadith on whoever raises two girls to maturity. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:09:30} He says Islam made one killed defending his family's honour a martyr, and made woman an heir after she had been inherited. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:11:00} He holds that Hagar's running between Safa and Marwa became a pillar without which Hajj and Umra are not valid, and that God set two women as examples for believers, [[person/asiya|Pharaoh's wife]] and [[person/maryam|Mary daughter of Imran]]. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep01 00:12:30} He also discusses a saying he attributes to opponents of Islam and a story set in the gold market of Medina, which are not written here.

**Episode 2: The counsel of chastity.** He cites a hadith he attributes to [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]] from [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], graded sound, he says, by al-Albani in Sahih al-Jami': a woman who performs the five prayers, fasts Ramadan, guards her chastity and obeys her husband enters Paradise by whichever gate she wishes; he says it holds four counsels and then dwells on one, chastity. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:02:00} He holds that no religion commanded chastity as Islam did, citing the verses on the traits of the successful believers and the verse commanding restraint for those who cannot marry, and tells young people unable to marry that their path is restraint. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:04:00} He cites the hadith in which the Prophet tells young men to marry if they can and otherwise to fast, attributing it to al-Bukhari from Ibn Mas'ud. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:04:30} He takes [[person/maryam|Mary]], and [[person/yusuf|Joseph]] with al-Aziz's wife, as two models of chastity, saying in his account that al-Aziz's wife was the king's niece and of surpassing beauty. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:07:00} He cites the hadith of the seven whom God shades, among them a man who refused a woman of rank and beauty out of fear of God; the hadith promising Paradise to one who guards his tongue and his private parts; and a hadith he attributes to al-Tabarani meaning that men's chastity brings their women's chastity. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:09:30} He tells the story of the date-seller and the water-carrier, which ends with the wife's words "a blow for a blow". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:10:30} He addresses the Muslim woman as a guarded pearl, protected by Islam through the lowering of the gaze and the ban on touch, and as a treasure kept until its owner, her husband, comes. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:12:30} (See [[concept/iffa|Chastity]].)

**Episode 3: Stories of chaste women.** He says the episode continues the talk on chastity and repeats the Ibn Awf hadith. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:01:00} He then narrates the hadith of the three men in the cave, the first of whom left his cousin when she reminded him of God, so that the rock moved, and concludes that chastity guards one from evil ends. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:03:30} From [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]]'s [[work/kitab-dhamm-al-hawa|Dhamm al-Hawa]] he relates the story of the woman who said that one door remained unshut, the door between them and God. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:08:00} He tells of the Bedouin woman who asked her pursuer where the Maker of the stars was, and of the neighbour who reminded the man chasing her that God was more worthy of fear than people. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:10:00} He says Joseph heads the chaste among men. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:10:30} He tells the story of al-Rabi', the Kufan ascetic, over whom libertines wagered a singing-girl a thousand dinars for a kiss; he admonished her until she repented and was called "the worshipper of Kufa". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:10:30} From Dr al-Affani's Tartib al-Afwah he relates the story of Abu Bakr al-Miski, who escaped a woman who had lured him by smearing himself with filth, then dreamed that God had made his scent sweet, and for years gave off musk without perfume. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:14:00} He closes by urging women to guard their honour and their husbands in their absence. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:15:00} He also tells two stories about veiling the face, which are not written here. (See [[concept/iffa|Chastity]].)

**Episode 4: Asking for divorce without cause.** He welcomes listeners back to "your programme, The Prophet's counsels to women", and cites a hadith he attributes to the authors of the Sunan, graded sound, he says, by al-Albani, warning that a woman who asks her husband for divorce when no harm has befallen her is denied the scent of Paradise; he glosses the absence of harm as the absence of a sharia reason or a strong, accepted excuse. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:01:30} He says God described marriage in the Qur'an as a solemn covenant, so Islam works to keep homes from breaking up and children from being lost. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:02:30} He cites the hadith forbidding a believer to hate his wife, since if he dislikes one trait he will like another, says no man or woman is without faults, and cites the verse on living with wives in kindness. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:04:00} He holds that if the husband is upright, good-mannered and kind, the wife's request for divorce brings her under the warning, and cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari that most of the people of the Fire are women because of ingratitude to their husbands. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:06:00} He warns against the wife who demands divorce at every quarrel, is divorced, and regrets it while her children are lost, and says women have come to him after pressing for divorce, looking for someone to reconcile them. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:07:30} He cites a hadith he attributes to Muslim in which Iblis holds dearest the one of his troops who separated a man from his wife. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:09:00} He says that, according to statistics he cites, divorce in "our countries" now outnumbers marriage, mostly among the newly married, and holds that the children of broken homes are raised by the street and become "Iblis's strategic reserve". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep04 00:11:00} He also discusses the grounds on which he holds a wife may seek divorce, and the wisdom he sees in placing divorce in the husband's hand, which are not written here. (See [[concept/talaq|Divorce]].)

**Episode 5: A woman in Paradise and a woman in the Fire.** He opens by praying that the fasting and night prayers be accepted and that listeners not leave Ramadan unforgiven, so the episode was recorded in Ramadan. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:01:30} He cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari in [[work/al-adab-al-mufrad|al-Adab al-Mufrad]] and to Ahmad, Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim, from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]: a woman known for much prayer, fasting and charity who harms her neighbours with her tongue is in the Fire, and one with few extra devotions who gives pieces of dried curd and does not harm her neighbours is in Paradise; he glosses the dried curd as pieces of cheese. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:02:30} He holds that Islam is not only hijab, Qur'an memorisation, tarawih and umra but creed, worship, law, manners and morals, citing the hadith that the Prophet was sent to perfect noble character and the hadith of the "bankrupt", which he attributes to Muslim. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:04:30} He says lands in Southeast Asia, such as Indonesia, Malaysia and Bangladesh, entered Islam through the conduct of honest Muslim traders. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:05:30} He states that a neighbour has rights even if not a Muslim, and cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari that [[person/jibril|Gabriel]] kept enjoining the neighbour until the Prophet thought he would make him an heir. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:07:00} He says that [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]], whenever a sheep was slaughtered in his house, asked whether some had been sent to his Jewish neighbour, and narrates that a Jewish neighbour of [[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak]] priced his house at two thousand dinars and asked as much again for having Ibn al-Mubarak as a neighbour; when Ibn al-Mubarak heard, he gave him the four thousand and asked him to stay. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:08:00} He relates that a Canadian youth at an exhibition on Islam said Muslims were the first to break its rules of honesty, cleanliness and order, and that a French Muslim praised God for knowing Islam before knowing Muslims, and concedes that "we are a distorted image of Islam". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:11:00} He ends by counselling women not to harm their women neighbours or belittle any kindness they do them. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep05 00:12:30} (See [[concept/jiwar|Neighbourliness]] and [[concept/khuluq|Good character]].)

**Episode 6: A woman the Prophet prayed for.** He holds that a woman for whom the Prophet prayed will most likely be happy in both worlds, and cites a hadith he attributes to Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, Ahmad and al-Hakim from Abu Hurayra, praying for mercy on a man who rises at night and wakes his wife, and on a woman who rises and wakes her husband. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:02:30} He describes homes built on obedience, remembrance and the Qur'an as homes on which tranquillity descends. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:03:30} He relays from [[person/aisha|Aisha]] that the Prophet stayed up through [[concept/ashr-awakhir-ramadan|the last ten nights of Ramadan]] and woke his family, says that outside Ramadan he would call his aunt [[person/safiyya-bint-abd-al-muttalib|Safiyya]] and his daughter [[person/fatima|Fatima]] to night prayer, and that he knocked at night at the door of Fatima and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] asking about their prayer. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:05:30} He says [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] divided the night among his household so that his house was never without someone praying. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:05:30} He narrates that [[person/al-hasan-ibn-salih-ibn-hayy|al-Hasan ibn Salih]] sold a slave-girl who asked to be returned to him because her new household rose only for the dawn prayer. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:06:30} He narrates that [[person/sila-ibn-ashyam|Sila ibn Ashyam]] married [[person/muadha-al-adawiyya|Mu'adha al-Adawiyya]] and the two prayed on their wedding night until the dawn call, and that Sila said the hot bathhouse reminded him of Hell and the perfumed room of Paradise; he states it is Sunna for a groom to pray two rak'as with his bride on the wedding night. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:09:30} He observes that most people know night prayer only in Ramadan, citing the hadith on the merit of night prayer after the obligatory prayers, which he attributes to Muslim, and the Prophet's first words in Medina, which he attributes to al-Tirmidhi. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:11:30} He relays [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]]'s answer to a man who could not rise at night, that his sins had held him back, and counsels wives to encourage their husbands in Qur'an, night prayer and remembrance. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep06 00:13:00} He also criticises people who stay up before screens, which is not written here. (See [[concept/qiyam-al-layl-fiqh|Night prayer]].)

**Episode 7: A woman the Prophet disowned.** He cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari and Muslim from Abu Hurayra, disowning the woman who strikes her cheeks, tears her clothes and cries out as in the Jahiliyya at a calamity; he explains the striking as a woman beating her face in discontent at a death, and counts lamentations of anguish as forbidden wailing. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:02:00} He cites a hadith he attributes to Muslim on the punishment of a wailing woman who dies unrepentant, and the hadith that the dead are punished by their family's weeping, answering the objection that the Prophet wept over [[person/hamza|Hamza]] and [[person/ibrahim-ibn-muhammad|Ibrahim]] by saying that wailing is meant. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:03:30} He mentions the hadith disowning the woman who shrieks, shaves her head or tears her clothes in calamity, and states that tearing clothes is a major sin but not unbelief, so a woman needs repentance, not "restoring her religion". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:05:00} He sets out what a Muslim woman does at a bereavement: submission and acceptance of the decree, then the istirja' and the prayer for reward and a better replacement. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:06:00} He urges solace in the life of the Prophet, all of whose children died in his lifetime except Fatima, who died six months after him, as he states, and recounts his weeping over his son Ibrahim and his answer to Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf that it was mercy. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:08:00} He narrates the story of [[person/umm-sulaym|Umm Sulaym]], who hid her son's death until [[person/abu-talha-al-ansari|Abu Talha]] returned and then told him through the parable of a loan reclaimed, and says the Prophet blessed them and a son was born to them from whose line came nine, all memorisers of the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:10:00} From Dr Muhammad Ismail al-Muqaddim's Awdat al-Hijab he relates the story of [[person/al-asmai|al-Asma'i]] and Umm Aqil, who said the istirja' on hearing of her son's death and ordered her guests honoured. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:10:30} He mentions the patience of [[person/muhammad-al-baqir|al-Baqir]] when his son died, and of [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] when he lost his son Abd al-Malik, his brother Sahl and his servant Muzahim on three successive days. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep07 00:13:00} (See [[concept/niyaha-fiqh|Wailing]] and [[concept/sabr|Patience]].)

**Episode 8: The husband's right over his wife.** He presents it as a new counsel for the women of the umma, and cites a hadith he attributes to Ibn Majah and al-Hakim that a woman does not fulfil her Lord's right until she fulfils her husband's, the hadith on the best of women, and the hadith of prostration, which he attributes to al-Tirmidhi from Mu'adh ibn Jabal. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:03:00} He then lists the rights: obedience in what is right but not in sin, not refusing him intimacy without excuse, and adorning herself for him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:07:30} He explains the Farewell Sermon's words on husbands making their wives lawful by God's word: the contract, with dower, two witnesses and public announcement, makes the forbidden lawful. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:06:30} He advises the wife to prepare an hour before her husband returns, pokes fun at the one who after the honeymoon turns from "a chirping sparrow" into "a roaring lion", and holds that adorning herself for him before the mirror is worship. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:10:30} He states that after marriage the person with the greatest right over a woman is her husband, and over a man his mother, and advises her to give what she owes before asking for what she is owed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:12:30} He also discusses the verses on men's guardianship and the "degree", and the husband's permission for voluntary fasting, for going out and for travel, which are not written here. (See [[concept/huquq-al-zawjayn|Rights of the spouses]].)

**Episode 9: Immodest display.** He cites a hadith he attributes to Muslim from Abu Hurayra on two kinds of the people of the Fire whom the Prophet had not seen, one of them immodest women wearing clothes that do not cover them, who are denied Paradise and its scent, and holds that the hadith enjoins covering and chastity and forbids display. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:02:30} He counsels women to go out only in what he calls "the garment of honour", which makes them feel their body's worth, rejects the idea that display is civilisation, and cites the verse forbidding the display of the people of the Jahiliyya. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:03:30} He calls the follower of fashion "a slave of fashion", citing the hadith on the wretchedness of the slave of clothes. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:05:30} He reminds the immodest woman of the grave and of God's questioning, and asks her where her modesty is. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:09:00} He says [[person/aisha|Aisha]] entered her chamber, where the Prophet and then her father were buried, in house clothes, but once [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] was buried there she entered only fully veiled, out of modesty before him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:09:30} He glosses the hadith's description of their heads as wigs and hair piled up under the scarf, and qualifies the curse it mentions as general, not specific, so that no particular immodest woman may be cursed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep09 00:10:30} He cites the verse warning those who love indecency to spread, and holds that **Episode 10: Charity, cursing and ingratitude to husbands.** He cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari and Muslim from [[person/abu-said-al-khudri|Abu Sa'id al-Khudri]]: the Prophet went out to the prayer-ground on an Eid and told the women to give charity, for he had seen that they were most of the people of the Fire because they cursed much and were ungrateful to their husbands;
