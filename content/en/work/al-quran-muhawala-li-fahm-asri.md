## Summary
Al-Qur'an: Muhawala li-Fahm 'Asri ("The Qur'an: an attempt at a contemporary understanding") is a book by [[person/mostafa-mahmoud|Mostafa Mahmoud]] in which he reads the Qur'an in what he calls a "contemporary" way. {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 1} The edition read here opens with a preface announcing "the new edition" of the book in the Kitab Mayu series, followed by the chapter "The architecture of the Qur'an". {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 3} {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 4}

## The preface: the Qur'an and the sciences of the age (the author's view)
:::perspectives
- **Mostafa Mahmoud's view (opinion).** He holds that the Qur'an "still challenges minds" as if revealed to speak of today's sciences of astronomy, physics, geology and medicine, and gives examples he regards as Qur'anic anticipations: Joseph's dream in [[passage/quran-12-4|Yusuf 4]], which he links to the number of planets observed around the sun; the singling out of the fingertips in [[passage/quran-75-4|al-Qiyama 4]], which he links to fingerprints; mountains as pegs in [[passage/quran-78-7|al-Naba' 7]], which he links to mountain roots and the balance of the earth's rotation; and the sending down of iron in [[passage/quran-57-25|al-Hadid 25]], which he links to exploding stars. {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 1} {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 2} He links the covenant of witness in [[passage/quran-7-172|al-A'raf 172]] to President Clinton's announcement of the decoding of the human genome, and holds that the witnessing "in this new sense is wider and more comprehensive than what is in the old books of exegesis". {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 2} {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 3}
- **The dispute over scientific exegesis:** these matters belong to [[concept/al-ijaz-al-ilmi|the "scientific miracle" question]], on which scholars and exegetes differ; the arguments of supporters and opponents are not yet documented with a defined source in this entry.
:::

## "The architecture of the Qur'an": the first meeting
The author relates that his first meeting with the Qur'an was at the age of four in the Qur'an school of Shaykh Mahmud, repeating after him the opening of Surat al-Duha without understanding a letter; that he received the Qur'anic words "with a completely blank mind" because his family forced neither religion nor study on him; and that the phrases would come back to him of their own accord "as if a living, independent creature". {cite:mm-quran-muhawala-digital p. 4}

## For young visitors
In this book Mostafa Mahmoud tells of his love for the Qur'an since childhood, and tries to link its verses with what modern science has found; this is his view, which some scholars share and others reject.
