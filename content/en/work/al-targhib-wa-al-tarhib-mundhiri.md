## Summary
*Al-Targhib wa-l-Tarhib min al-Hadith al-Sharif* is [[person/abd-al-azim-al-mundhiri|Zaki al-Din 'Abd al-'Azim b. 'Abd al-Qawi al-Mundhiri]]'s (d. 656 AH) collection of hadiths encouraging good deeds and warning against their opposites. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 1} In his preface he says that once he had finished dictating his *Mukhtasar* of Abu Dawud and *al-Khilafiyyat wa-Madhahib al-Salaf*, a student asked him to dictate a comprehensive book on encouragement and warning, free of the length that chains and long discussion of defects bring; so he dictated a small book, limited to reports that explicitly encourage or warn, leaving aside the Prophet's bare actions except rarely. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 2-3}

## Citation and grading
He gives the hadith, then names the compilers of the well-known books who transmit it — sometimes only some of them, for brevity — and indicates whether its chain is sound, good or weak unless the compiler is one who undertook to include only sound reports; this, he says, makes giving the chain unnecessary. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 3} He leaves aside the finer hidden defects, which only the great critics can hope to judge. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 3}

He has a convention for the opening word. Reports whose chain is sound, good or close to them — and also mursal, broken or interrupted ones, or those with an unnamed or disputed narrator — open with "from" ('an), and he then notes their defect; reports whose chain includes someone called a liar, forger, suspect, universally abandoned or very weak open with "it is related" (ruwiya), so that a weak chain is signalled twice: by "it is related" and by the absence of comment at the end. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 4} He gave the disputed narrators a chapter of their own at the end of the book, in alphabetical order, briefly stating the criticism and praise of each. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 4} {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.4 p. 280}

He holds that every hadith he cites to [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Abu Dawud]] on which Abu Dawud is silent is as Abu Dawud said and does not fall below good, and he points out cases where Abu Dawud was lenient in keeping silent, [[work/jami-al-tirmidhi|al-Tirmidhi]] in declaring good, or [[work/sahih-ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]] and [[work/al-mustadrak-ala-al-sahihayn|al-Hakim]] in declaring sound — not as criticism of them but as a measure for the discerning reader. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 8}

## Sources
He says he included all material of this kind from Malik's Muwatta', Ahmad's Musnad, [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]], [[work/sahih-muslim|Sahih Muslim]], Abu Dawud's Sunan and Marasil, al-Tirmidhi's Jami', al-Nasa'i's *al-Sunan al-Kubra* and *al-Yawm wa-l-Layla*, Ibn Majah's Sunan, al-Tabarani's three Mu'jams, the Musnads of Abu Ya'la and al-Bazzar, Ibn Hibban's Sahih and al-Hakim's Mustadrak, except what escaped him or he forgot while dictating. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 4-7} He added hadiths from Ibn Khuzayma's Sahih, the books of Ibn Abi al-Dunya, al-Bayhaqi's *Shu'ab al-Iman* and *al-Zuhd al-Kabir*, and [[work/al-targhib-wa-al-tarhib-asbahani|Abu al-Qasim al-Asbahani's al-Targhib wa-l-Tarhib]], taking everything from the last that was not in the other books and omitting what in it was shown to be fabricated. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 7-8} When a hadith is in the seven core books he rarely cites it to any other. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 8}

## Arrangement
After finishing he placed a table of its books and chapters at the front; the books open with Knowledge, then Purity, Prayer and Supererogatory Prayer, each chapter headed "Encouragement to ..." or "Warning against ...". {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 8-10} The fourth volume ends with sections on the description of Paradise and then the chapter on disputed narrators. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.4 p. 280}

## Weak hadith in encouragement
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Mundhiri**: notes that earlier scholars were lenient in kinds of encouragement and warning, many even citing fabricated reports without saying so; he therefore indicates each hadith's grade, marks the seriously weak with "it is related", and leaves out what is shown to be fabricated. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 3-4} {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 8}
- **Sunni: the annotator of the al-Maktaba al-Qayyima edition**: reports that some scholars allowed acting on weak hadith in the merits of deeds, qualifies this — a report weak because of its narrator's honesty or integrity is not used — and then says he inclines not to use weak hadith as proof at all, either in rulings or in the merits of deeds. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 3}
:::

## The edition in the library
Al-Maktaba al-Qayyima, Cairo, in four volumes, collated with several copies and including additions missing from the earlier printing, together with *al-Targhib wa-l-Tarhib min al-Qur'an al-Hakim* by Muhammad Munir al-Dimashqi; its footnotes gloss rare words and give notices on the compilers. {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 1} {cite:targhib-tarhib-qayyima j.1 p. 4-7}
