## Summary

The lecturer closes his treatment of the Hudaybiyya settlement, then tells the raid on the Prophet's camels near Medina and the pursuit that became the expedition of Dhu Qarad, following Salama ibn al-Akwa's account in Sahih Muslim. He draws lessons on honouring effort and on a Prophetic life without idle time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Closing lessons of Hudaybiyya: why the pledgers were honoured

- He recalls that a whole chapter of the Qur'an was sent down about Hudaybiyya and that it names the event a clear conquest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates from Sahih Muslim reports in which the Prophet praises those who attended and pledged under the tree and promises that none of them will enter the fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that God keeps the record of great stands taken for the faith and forgives such people their other shortcomings, and he compares this to a person who forgives a friend who once stood by him in a crisis. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He gives as the main parallel the report about the people of Badr, whom he describes as those who stood firm while few in number on the day that separated truth from falsehood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that Hudaybiyya is close to Badr in this respect, since the pledgers gave their word to the Prophet even unto death while poorly equipped, and God recorded this in a verse of the Qur'an that he explains as bringing tranquillity and a near conquest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Scholars' benefits drawn from Hudaybiyya and why it is called a conquest

- He says that Ibn al-Qayyim wrote two chapters in Zad al-Ma'ad, one of legal benefits and one of wisdoms from the story, and that Ibn Hajar likewise gathered benefits in Fath al-Bari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that naming Hudaybiyya a conquest corrects the way people judge, because the Muslims got none of what they set out for, neither the lesser pilgrimage nor a victory in fighting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that God names things by their outcome and not only by their immediate condition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Outcomes of the settlement: growth in numbers, da'wa and the road to Mecca

- He holds that the conquest of Mecca would not have happened without the settlement, since it came after Quraysh broke the terms. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He counts about fourteen hundred men at Hudaybiyya and about ten thousand at the conquest of Mecca two years later, and he says the growth shows another kind of conquest, people entering Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that once war was laid down people mixed safely, heard the Qur'an and the call to Islam was reopened, in contrast to the Medinan years before it, which he describes as nearly all fighting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that Jubayr ibn Mut'im came to Medina, heard the Prophet recite a sura, and said that was when Islam first settled in his heart. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that the exchange between Heraclius and Abu Sufyan took place in this period and that Abu Sufyan could only insert one remark against the Prophet, about the truce. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that the conquest of Khaybar was a divine reward for those who attended Hudaybiyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### The mission against Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq

- He places this mission between the Trench and Hudaybiyya and says the Prophet sent a small Khazraj party against Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq, a Jewish leader at Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He says Sallam had been one of those who incited the polytheists to besiege Medina at the Trench. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that the party was led by a man named Abd Allah ibn Rafi', and that the name is uncertain in the recording. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates a story from Sahih al-Bukhari in which the leader entered the fortress alone by a ruse, locked each door behind him in the dark, struck Sallam, checked that he was dead, and broke his leg on the stair while leaving. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that the man waited until the morning announcement confirmed the death, then returned and the Prophet passed a hand over his leg and it was healed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### The tribes around Medina and the dangers after Hudaybiyya

- He holds that the tribes round Medina and in Arabia were still mostly unbelieving and a source of danger at this time, though many of their young people had accepted Islam, especially in Aslam and Ghifar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He says early converts such as Abu Dharr led later ones from their tribes to Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He says the biographers count the emigrants and helpers in each campaign, that Badr had few emigrants, and that at Hudaybiyya there were hundreds, many from the surrounding tribes, so emigration was not limited to Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He mentions the account of al-Nawwas ibn Sam'an staying a year in Medina, and a later saying that there is no emigration after the conquest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He recalls the incidents of al-Raji' and Bi'r Ma'una, in which many Companions were killed by tribes, as proof of that danger. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### The raid on the Prophet's camels at al-Ghaba

- He says the episode falls between Hudaybiyya and Khaybar and is told by Salama ibn al-Akwa, recorded in Sahih Muslim, and he describes Salama as one of the best known swift runners of the Arabs and brave. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that after returning to Medina, Salama went out with the Prophet's servant Rabah and Talha's horse to graze the Prophet's camels. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that Abd al-Rahman al-Fazari raided the herd at al-Ghaba, north of Medina near Uhud, killed the herdsman and drove the camels off. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that Salama sent Rabah with the horse to tell Talha and the Prophet, then climbed a hillock and gave the alarm cry of war three times. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Salama's solitary pursuit of the raiders

- He relates that Salama followed the raiders on foot, shooting arrows and chanting, and wounded one in the shoulder. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He explains that a lone man on foot can be overcome by a horseman, so Salama sheltered under a tree, shot the horses, then climbed the mountain and rolled stones down on them in the narrow pass. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that Salama kept at it until he had freed every camel and the raiders threw down more than thirty cloaks and spears to run faster, and Salama piled stones on each to mark them for the Prophet's party. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that reinforcements joined the raiders, who complained of Salama's harassment, and that four of them climbed up to him; he told them who he was and that none of them could catch him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that Salama was trying to delay the raiders until the Prophet's army could arrive. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Arrival of the horsemen and the clash at Dhu Qarad

- He relates that the Prophet's horsemen arrived first, led by al-Akhram al-Asadi, then Abu Qatada al-Ansari and al-Miqdad, and that Salama warned al-Akhram not to be cut off before the Prophet arrived. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that al-Akhram asked Salama not to stand between him and martyrdom, that al-Akhram engaged Abd al-Rahman and was killed, and that Abu Qatada then killed Abd al-Rahman. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that Salama kept running after the raiders until they reached a spring called Dhu Qarad, and that he drove them off so that they drank none of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that he struck another man there, who recognised him as the same Ibn al-Akwa from the morning, and that he then led two captured horses back to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that he drank water and milk brought by Amir, and found the Prophet at the spring with the recovered camels, spears and cloaks, and Bilal roasting the liver and hump of a slaughtered camel. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Salama asks for a hundred men and the Prophet's reply

- He relates that Salama asked to take a hundred men and pursue the raiders so that none was left to report back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that the Prophet laughed until his back teeth showed in the firelight and said the raiders were already being hosted in the land of Ghatafan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that a man from Ghatafan later reported that the tribe slaughtered a camel for them but saw dust and fled in fear, though no one had followed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that in the morning the Prophet named Abu Qatada the best horseman and Salama the best foot soldier of the day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Honouring effort: the reward, the lesson and the return to Medina

- He relates that the Prophet gave Salama two shares from the spoils, a horseman's and a foot soldier's, and seated him behind him on his camel for the return. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He draws the lesson that the Prophet honoured Salama in three ways, by praise, by a double share and by the ride, and that valuing people's efforts matters even when they give gladly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that on the road an Ansari known as the fastest runner challenged anyone to race to Medina, that Salama asked the Prophet's leave, and that Salama won the race after pacing himself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### Departure for Khaybar and Amir's verses

- He relates that Salama says only three nights passed before they set out for Khaybar, which he takes to show how closely the events followed one another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He relates that Salama's uncle Amir chanted verses on the march, that the Prophet asked who he was and asked forgiveness for him, and that Umar then asked why the Prophet had not let them enjoy Amir longer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He says he leaves the rest of the Khaybar story for the next session, and that the narrator notes the man the Prophet asked forgiveness for in this way was martyred. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}

### A Prophetic life without gaps

- He holds that the Prophet's life was full of work with no idle gaps, whether seen as campaigns, teaching the Qur'an, or debating the polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He says some scholars even named the biography maghazi, because of the number of campaigns and detachments before and after each major battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
- He holds that the years from Uhud to Hudaybiyya, full of fighting, were also when the Prophet married most of his wives, and that the life was blessed and full without letting small details outgrow their size. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep27}
