## Summary
The Assabile archive holds thirty audio episodes titled "al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya" ("The Prophetic Civilisation") by [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]], most of them ten to thirteen minutes long, in which he speaks alone; each opens with the tag "the Islamway site presents". {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:00:00} The subject of the series, as he presents it, is the [[concept/al-ijaz-al-ilmi|scientific miracle]] in the Sunna: that the Prophet pointed, in his words, deeds, approvals and life, to a great mass of scientific facts that no one could have grasped in the age of revelation or for centuries after it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:01:00} The series opens with the rules and reasons for this field, then goes hadith by hadith: from the earth, the sea, the moon, eclipses and the new moons, to the fly, the heart, food and sleep, then rain and the water cycle, then suckling, heredity, medicine and the tooth-stick, and finally the tailbone, embryos, joints, dates and the signs of the Hour. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep18 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:02:00}

The machine transcripts read clearly for the most part; only what read clearly is written here, and figures that came out contradictory are left out. The figures, dates and scientific readings are the speaker's statements as recorded, not the museum's judgements, and the hadith he cites are described here in the museum's words, not quoted. Two remarks of his place the recording in time: he says the Prophet was sent 1,431 years ago or a little more, and he mentions Iceland's "latest" volcanic eruption. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep17 00:09:00} Episode 19 is a word-for-word repeat of the recording of episode 18, and episodes 22 and 30 break off before their end in the archive. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep19 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:06:00}

## Episode 1: Rules for the scientific miracle in the Sunna
He defines the scientific miracle in the Sunna as the Prophet's having pointed ahead of time to facts that came to light only a few decades ago, and says students of the Sunna did not take these up because the facts had not yet appeared, and because they studied the Sunna as an "explanatory note" to the Qur'an, which rests on creed, worship, ethics and dealings. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:01:30} He says the hadith in this field turn on the creation, annihilation and resurrection of the universe, life and humankind, and that he avoids the absolute unseen and keeps to what can be judged in this world. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:02:30}

He then lists his rules: know the grade of the hadith as the scholars of [[concept/mustalah-al-hadith|hadith science]] set it, sound, good, weak or forged; gather the narrations and the hadith on one subject; understand the text through the meanings and usages of Arabic, through its context, the occasion it was said on and its aims; and read it in the light of the Qur'an, whose general statements the Sunna details. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:04:30} He says no Muslim may reject a sound hadith: whoever understands it acts on it, and whoever does not, pauses at it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:05:00} He holds that a hadith pointing to a conclusive scientific fact is not to be rejected because one weak narrator is in its chain, and that a settled scientific fact can be "one of the means of judging a hadith's soundness"; this view is set out under the perspectives below. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:06:00}

Further rules: distinguish the literal from the figurative and move to the figurative only with sufficient evidence; do not strain the words; do not go into equations and fine detail; and never enter the absolute unseen, such as the divine essence, the spirit, the barzakh, the scale, the bridge, paradise and hell. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:08:00} He limits the miracle to conclusive facts, except in matters of creation, annihilation and resurrection, which in his view lie beyond the reach of science. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:09:00} The investigator must be a specialist known for his learning and piety; what he reaches is not the last word, since meanings widen as knowledge widens; and scientific interpretation, a human effort that earns two rewards if right and one if wrong, differs from the scientific miracle, which for him is a challenge that may rest only on a conclusive fact. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:10:30} He closes by warning against belittling earlier scholars and by allowing that a hint in a hadith may lead to a new discovery. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep01 00:11:00}

## Episode 2: Why the scientific miracle in the Sunna today
He says Muslim scholars gathered, sifted, arranged and explained the Sunna in dozens of volumes but paid no attention to its scientific hints, which are not meant for their own sake but came as witness to the Prophet's prophethood. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:01:00} In an age of science, he says, people ask for scientific proof of prophethood, and the Sunna is under heavy attack. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:02:00} He sets out the arguments of those he calls the orientalists: that the Qur'an is mass-transmitted and the Sunna is not, that the Qur'an was written down in the Prophet's lifetime while the Sunna was compiled in full only after his death, and that forgers slipped material into it; he answers that the study of the Sunna sorted the sound and good from the weak and forged, and that Muslim scholars "taught the whole world what documentation means" through it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:03:00}

He says the Sunna is revelation in its meaning, its wording being the Prophet's, who was given concise comprehensive speech, and that the Qur'an and the Sunna are the two basic sources of Islam, a subject the museum treats under [[concept/hujjiyyat-al-sunna|the authority of the Sunna]]. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:04:00} Most of these facts, he says, became known only in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, most of them late in the twentieth, which makes them in his view the fittest address to the people of this age. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:07:30} He cites the verses on obeying the Messenger and the hadith on holding to the Sunna and on lying about the Prophet. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:09:30} He mentions the hadith forbidding the writing down of anything from the Prophet other than the Qur'an, and says it belonged to the beginning of revelation, out of care for the Qur'an, after which the Prophet allowed writing. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:11:00} He cites the hadith of the man reclining on his couch who is content with the Qur'an alone, counts it among the proofs of prophethood, and says the call of those who style themselves [[concept/quranism|Qur'anists]] is that very call, and that "whoever demolishes the Sunna demolishes the Qur'an". {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep02 00:12:00}

## Episode 3: The kindled sea, and the hadith of fire beneath the sea
He says that only in the late 1960s and early 1970s did scientists establish that the oceans rest on deep rifts that cut through the rocky shell down to the "zone of weakness", where the rock is plastic and partly molten. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:09:30} He describes magma pushing up through these rifts to build the mid-ocean ridges, and peaks rising above the water as volcanic islands like Hawaii, Japan, the Philippines and Indonesia. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:02:00} He says that in Arabic "sajara" is to stoke a fire under something until it glows, that the seas and oceans cover about three quarters of the earth's surface at an average depth of four kilometres, and that their water, most of the earth's water, which he puts at 1,400 million cubic kilometres, neither puts out these fires nor is boiled away by magma hotter than a thousand degrees. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:03:30}

He links this to the oath at the opening of sura al-Tur ([[passage/quran-52-6|al-Tur 6]]) and to the hadith that restricts sea travel to the pilgrim, the one performing umra and the one going out in God's cause and speaks of fire beneath the sea, and marvels that the Prophet never travelled by sea. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:05:00} The hadith, he says, does not forbid sea travel but warns that it is a risk to be taken only for a great purpose, and "God's cause" for him covers jihad, preaching, the pursuit of knowledge, trade and diplomacy. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:07:30} He reads the second half as meaning that all the earth's water was brought out from the zone of weakness, which holds more of it, so he disagrees with those who say the coming wars will be water wars, and recalls that water is provision and that the Prophet instituted [[practice/salat-al-istisqa|the rain prayer]]. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:08:30} He says the Arabs referred the kindled sea to the hereafter on the strength of the verse of al-Takwir ([[passage/quran-81-6|al-Takwir 6]]), yet everything sworn by at the opening of al-Tur exists in our time: Mount Tur in Sinai, the inscribed Book, the much-frequented House in the seventh heaven and the raised roof; so for him the kindled sea must exist now too. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep03 00:10:30}

## Episode 4: The seven earths
He calls the whereabouts of the seven earths one of the hardest questions before scholars, and says the Qur'an names them in a single verse at the end of sura al-Talaq ([[passage/quran-65-12|al-Talaq 12]]), where the likeness is, for him, in number and in form. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:03:30} He holds that God first created one earth and then differentiated it into seven, and one heaven and then differentiated it into seven. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:01:00} He cites the hadith against wrongfully taking a span of land, whose taker will be encircled by seven earths, says al-Bukhari, Ahmad and others narrated it, and lists six narrations of it that for him show the seven earths all lie within our own earth, shell upon shell around one centre. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:03:00}

He counts them from outside in: the crust, the rest of the rocky shell below it, three mantles, the liquid core and the solid core, and says natural and man-made earthquake waves mark the boundaries between them as they bend from one medium to the next. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:10:00} The solid core, he says, is a ball of iron, 90 per cent, with 9 per cent nickel and 1 per cent light elements. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:09:30} From the phrase "the heavens and the earth and what is between them", which he says comes in twenty verses, and from the verse in sura al-Rahman on passing beyond the regions of the heavens and the earth ([[passage/quran-55-33|al-Rahman 33]]), he argues that the earth is at the centre of the universe, while also saying that the earth today lies thirty thousand light years from the centre of the galaxy. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:09:00} He sees the atmosphere as a mixture of the matter of earth and heaven prepared by God for life. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:08:30} He ends by saying the hadith came to forbid injustice, yet is scientifically exact without qualification. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep04 00:11:30}

## Episode 5: "God was, and there was nothing other than Him"
He begins with the hadith of [[person/imran-ibn-husayn|Imran ibn Husayn]] in the Sahih of [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] on God's existence before all things, the Throne upon the water, the writing in the Record and the creation of the heavens and the earth, and calls it a scientific miracle before it is a doctrinal one. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:00:30} The Creator, he explains, is not bounded by place, which He created, nor by time, which He brought into being, nor shaped by matter and energy. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:01:00} He contrasts Western civilisation, which in his view began by breaking with religion and the Church, with "our Islamic civilisation", which he calls the only one he knows of to join this world and the hereafter in a single equation. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:01:30} Books of astronomy and earth science, he says, held for centuries that the world was eternal, until science in its overall findings confirmed that the universe had a beginning, since stars are born and die and the universe changes every moment. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:03:00}

He says the observable part of the nearest heaven holds between one hundred thousand million and three hundred thousand million galaxies, our own galaxy more than one hundred thousand million stars, that the universe is about 13.7 billion years old, and that the earth's rocks are close to five thousand million years old. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:04:30} Some non-Muslim astronomers, he says, accept that the universe has a "reference point" outside it that must precede it and differ from it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:06:30} He then speaks of God's transcendence, of the bar on reflecting on God's essence, and of associating partners with God as a confusion of the Creator's attributes with the creature's; he relates that ten generations between Adam and Noah held to pure monotheism, that idol worship began with images of righteous men made so that people would remember them, and that [[person/nuh|Noah]] and then Hud were sent. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep05 00:09:00}

## Episode 6: The splitting of the moon
These hints, he says, came to argue for God's divinity and the possibility of resurrection, and as an address suited to our age. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:01:00} He then tells of the [[event/splitting-of-the-moon|splitting of the moon]]: the unbelievers of Quraysh asked for a sign other than the Qur'an and, to defeat him, asked him to split the moon; he prayed and pointed at the moon, which split into two parts that stayed apart for some hours and then joined; some said they had been bewitched, while the wiser among them said to wait for travellers, and the caravans told them they had seen it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:03:00} He names the narrators Anas ibn Malik, Ibn Umar and Ibn Abbas, Anas's report that they saw Hira between the two halves, and Ibn Mas'ud's that it split at Mina and the Prophet told them to bear witness, reports kept in the Sahih of al-Bukhari. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3868} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3869} He links it to the opening of sura al-Qamar ([[passage/quran-54-1|al-Qamar 1]]), mentions that some exegetes, early and modern, held that the splitting has not yet happened and is a sign of the Hour, and answers them with the hadith that the Prophet's mission and the Hour are like two fingers. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:04:00}

## Episode 7: The hadith of the fly
He repeats the rule against impugning a sound hadith, then takes up the hadith of the fly that falls into a drink, which he says "stirred long debate" among Muslims and others. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:01:30} He reports an experiment that he says scientists at the Faculty of Science of Cairo University and of King Abdulaziz University in Jeddah carried out: two rows of vessels with water, milk, honey and juices, flies landing on the first row without being dipped and dipped in the second; under the microscope and in cultures, what the flies had been dipped in held few pathogens. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:03:00} (needs_source) He explains this by saying one wing carries bacteria and the other what counters them, "bacteriophage", and that without it the fly could not live on carrion and refuse. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:07:00}

The hadith, he says, is advice, not an obligation, for necessity, as for someone stranded in the desert with no other drink; whoever finds it repugnant and has an alternative may pour it away. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:08:00} He says about 87,000 species of fly are known, that flies help break down waste and carrion, and sees in the pairing of disease and cure a witness to "pairing in creation". {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep07 00:09:30}

## Episode 8: The Ka'ba, the first land
He takes up a hadith saying that the Ka'ba was a small rise on the water from which the earth was spread out, says hadith scholars wondered at it, and that "dahw" in Arabic is stretching, spreading and throwing, for him a fitting description of a volcano throwing out lava and widening its cone. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:01:30} Earth scientists, he says, have shown that the early earth was covered by water with no dry land, until volcanoes erupted on the floor of that ocean and built a ridge in its middle; the first peak to rise from it became a volcanic island, in his view the first dry land, on which the angels built the Ka'ba. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:03:00} He cites the verse on the first House for people ([[passage/quran-3-96|Al Imran 96]]), saying its verb is passive so that for him it was the angels who placed it, and the hadith on the first place of worship set up on earth: the Sacred Mosque, then al-Aqsa, forty years apart. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:04:00}

The land, he says, grew from that island into a single continent scientists call "Pangaea", which a network of faults broke into today's continents, still moving; Mecca lay at the centre of that single landmass and still lies at the middle of the land, so that a circle centred on Mecca would enclose the land. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:06:00} He cites the hadith that the Sanctuary lies opposite the seven heavens and seven earths, and the hadith of the much-frequented House in the seventh heaven directly above the Ka'ba, entered each day by seventy thousand angels who never return. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:07:30} Some missionaries, he says, objected that the Prophet could not have been sent to all people when he was told to warn the Mother of Cities and those around it, and he offers the centrality of Mecca as the answer. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:10:00} Bringing out the standing of Mecca, al-Aqsa and the Prophet's city by scientific method is, for him, "a fine card for preaching". {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep08 00:11:00}

## Episode 9: The lump of flesh that sets the body right
He takes up the hadith on the clear lawful, the clear unlawful and the doubtful matters between them, and stops at its end, on the lump of flesh that is the heart. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:02:30} He describes the heart as a muscular pump sending blood to the body's cells and back to the lungs, says that a few seconds without blood to the brain is fatal, and reads the hadith as likening the heart in the body to the religious restraint in the soul. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:05:30} Muslims, he says, grasped the hadith's worth only when [[person/ibn-al-nafis|Ibn al-Nafis]] discovered the lesser (pulmonary) circulation, which in his view is "wrongly attributed" to William Harvey. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:05:00}

He relates that he attended a cardiology conference in Saudi Arabia three years earlier at which the doctors said the heart is merely a pump; he reminded them of verses that ascribe understanding to the heart; a year later he was invited to a second conference, attended by three scientists from a research centre in California, who said the heart sends the brain more commands than it receives and is linked to it by blood, hormones and electromagnetic waves, and called the conference "The King of Organs". {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:08:30} He says heart-transplant surgeons' observations that recipients' desires changed set this research going, tells of a woman who saw in her dreams the murder of the heart's donor (needs_source), and names the researcher Paul Pearsall and his two books on "the remembering heart" and "the heart's code", and the HeartMath institute in California. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep09 00:10:30}

## Episode 10: Every fanged beast and every taloned bird
He takes up the hadith of [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] that the Prophet forbade eating any beast with fangs and any bird with talons, giving as examples of the first the lion, wolf, tiger, hyena, dog and cat, and of the second the falcon, hawk, saker, eagle and vulture. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:03:00} These animals, he says, eat carrion full of bacteria, fungi and viruses, God gave them immunity that protects them, and a person who eats their meat is struck by those agents. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:05:00} He adds the Prophet's ban on the jallala, permitted livestock and fowl that feed on carrion and refuse, forbidding their meat and milk until they are penned long enough to become clean. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:06:30} He cites the English saying "you are what you eat" to argue that people take on something of the nature of what they eat, and ends by saying a Muslim keeps the command whether or not he knows its wisdom. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep10 00:08:30}

## Episode 11: The sun and the moon are two signs
He relates that the sun was eclipsed on the day [[person/ibrahim-ibn-muhammad|Ibrahim, the Prophet's son]], died, and people said it was eclipsed for his death; the Prophet led them in two long rak'as and then told them that the sun and the moon are two signs that are not eclipsed for anyone's death or life, and told them at such times to remember God, magnify Him, pray and give charity. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:02:00} The world then, he says, was "drowned" in superstition, explaining cosmic events by the death of a great man or a birth in a royal house, while the Prophet declared it a recurring natural event. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:09:00}

He explains total, partial and annular solar eclipses, that a total eclipse is seen along a narrow strip of the earth, and that a lunar eclipse happens when the earth is between the sun and a full moon. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:08:30} Solar eclipses, he says, happen two to five times a year and a total one may last seven and a half minutes, while a total lunar eclipse may last about a hundred minutes. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:09:00} He passes on scientists' advice not to look at the sun during an eclipse, and holds that both events disturb natural conditions on earth, so that much remembrance, prayer and charity are, for him, a way to ward off what may come with them. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:08:00} He mentions the Sunna of calling "the prayer is gathering" and praying the long [[practice/salat-al-kusuf|eclipse prayer]] with much supplication. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep11 00:09:30}

## Episode 12: "Fast when you see it"
He takes up al-Bukhari's hadith on fasting when the new moon is seen, breaking the fast when it is seen and completing thirty days of Sha'ban if it is hidden, its narrations on estimating for it and on the month of twenty-nine nights, and the hadith of the unlettered community that neither writes nor reckons, whose month is sometimes twenty-nine and sometimes thirty. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:02:00} The Muslim day, he says, begins at sunset astronomically and Islamically, and every act of worship has an astronomical sign: maghrib the disappearance of the sun's disc, isha the disappearance of the red twilight, red being the longest wavelength of light, fajr its appearance, zuhr the sun's passing the meridian, asr the length of the shadow. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:04:00} He explains the dark of the moon, conjunction and the birth of the crescent, and says astronomical tables compute the crescent's birth precisely but count the month from whatever moment it is born, while for him the month begins only when the crescent is seen just after sunset. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:05:30}

He puts Muslims' disagreement over the start of Ramadan, Shawwal and Dhu al-Hijja down to the community's division into more than 62 states and the lack of a single authority, and holds that a sighting anywhere begins the month for the whole earth, allowing for time differences that for him never exceed twelve hours; the question of differing horizons is set out under [[concept/ruyat-hilal-fiqh|sighting the crescent]]. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:08:00} The lunar month, he says, lasts between 29 days 5 hours and 29 days 19 hours, so never more than thirty days, and two months of 29 or of 30 may follow each other. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:10:00} He ends by saying the Muslim year is lunar-solar and that the eleven-day difference between the two years makes fasting and the Hajj move through all the seasons, as in the [[concept/hijri-calendar|Hijri calendar]]. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:12:00}

## Episode 13: Talbina, and senna and sannut
He reports a study that he says researchers from Egypt's Ministry of Agriculture, the Faculty of Agriculture of Cairo University and a professor at the University of Alberta in Canada carried out on [[concept/barley-shair|barley]]: they found compounds that lower cholesterol, such as beta-glucan, vitamins and minerals such as potassium, magnesium, calcium, iron and zinc, found that a lack of them makes people irritable and prone to depression, and found that they act well on the connections between nerve cells. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:02:30} He links this to the hadith that talbina comforts the sick person's heart and takes away some grief, and defines talbina as a thin soup of barley flour with its bran, named for looking like milk (laban) in whiteness and thinness, regretting that many think barley is animal fodder. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep13 00:07:00}

## Episode 14: Disliking sleep before isha and talk after it

and that a man whose religion and character are approved should be given in marriage. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:04:00} The counsel is for women as for men, he says: a woman should ask about the man who proposes and about his family, because the laws of heredity play a weighty part. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:03:30} He quotes [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|Abu Hamid al-Ghazali]] on the manners of a woman when a man proposes: she should have someone she trusts among her family ask about his religion and creed, his manliness and honesty, his companions, his keeping to congregational prayer and his fair dealing in trade, and put his religion before his wealth and looks, a matter linked to [[concept/kafaa|equality in marriage]]. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:05:00} The recording breaks off there.

## Episode 23: The black seed
He takes up the hadith that the black seed holds a cure for every disease except sam, which is death, in several narrations, and describes it as a dicotyledonous herb of the Mediterranean basin, scientifically "Nigella sativa", known in Egypt as habbat al-baraka and used as a spice, on bread and in pickles. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:02:30} He relates that an Egyptian doctor who emigrated to the United States, Ahmad al-Qadi, who died a few years earlier, understood from the hadith that the seed had to do with the immune system, and showed at the Doctors' Syndicate in Cairo pictures of an immunodeficient patient given the seed in successive doses whose defensive cells rose to normal levels within a month. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:04:30} (needs_source) He says the doctor found in it about 33 per cent fixed oils and 1.5 per cent volatile oils and a compound called "nigellone", and mixed it with honey and garlic into a capsule produced in the United States. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:07:30} He calls on Muslim scholars to study every prophetic indication on remedies by laboratory method, holds that preaching with argument and scientific language is what the community has left, and calls on the rulers of Muslim states to support it; the subject connects with [[concept/al-tibb-al-nabawi-wal-badil|prophetic medicine]]. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep23 00:10:30}

## Episode 24: "No year has more rain than another"
He takes up a hadith he says al-Bayhaqi and al-Hakim narrated, that no year has more rain than another but God distributes it, and mentions the Companions' wonder at it given runs of drought years and floods. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:02:00} He repeats that God brought all the earth's water out of its interior, then says 97.5 per cent of it is in the seas and oceans, about 2 per cent is ice on mountain tops and the poles, about four kilometres thick at the South Pole and about three at the North, and less than half a per cent is in rivers, rocks, the air and the soil. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:04:00} Evaporated water, he says, comes back drop by drop but newly distributed; without the ice the water would drown the land; a little more rain would be a disaster and a little less would bring desertification. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep24 00:09:30}

## Episode 25: The tooth-stick at every prayer
He takes up the hadith that, were it not a hardship for his community, the Prophet would have ordered the tooth-stick at every prayer, with its narrations "for the people" and "for the believers", and says this came before Europe and America knew what cleaning the teeth meant. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep25 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar among them tannic acid and mustard oil, aromatic, resinous and mineral matter, and sodium carbonate, which he calls the basic ingredient of twentieth-century toothpaste. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep25 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep25 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar ## Episode 26: The tailbone
He calls creation, annihilation and resurrection among the most closed questions before scientists, then takes up the hadith that the earth consumes all of a human being except the tailbone, from which he was created and on which he will be reassembled, citing its narrations by Abu Dawud from Abu Hurayra, Ibn Hibban from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, and al-Bukhari from Abu Hurayra, with the forty between the two blasts and the water from which people will sprout. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:03:00} Doctors used to say all bones decay, he says, so the hadith was disputed for centuries. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:04:30}

He then credits the German embryologist Hans Spemann and his team, studying amphibian embryos, with noticing a fine line that appears on the embryo on the fifteenth day, where its layers specialise and the body's systems form, and which then withdraws to the end of the spine; they called it the "primitive streak" or "primary organizer"; he says they removed it and grafted it into another embryo, where it grew, and crushed, boiled and burned it and grafted it again, and it grew (needs_source), and that Spemann received the Nobel Prize for this. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:08:00} He cites the verse in sura Qaf on God's knowing what the earth takes from the dead ([[passage/quran-50-4|Qaf 4]]), and faults Muslim scholars for leaving the hadith without laboratory study. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep26 00:09:30}

## Episode 27: "Not from all the fluid does the child come"
He takes up the Prophet's answer on coitus interruptus, that every soul has a book in God's knowledge, and the hadith that a child does not come from all the fluid and that when God wills to create something nothing prevents it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:01:00} A single ejaculation, he says, carries between one hundred and three hundred million sperm, most of which die; no more than two hundred reach the egg and only one fertilises it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:01:30} He describes the embryo's stages from the morula to the clinging form attached to the wall of the womb, and says every species has its number of chromosomes, 46 in humans, with half carried by the sex cells. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:03:30} The choice of the sperm that fertilises a given egg, he holds, is made only by God's will, and every human has a genetic fingerprint of their own. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:05:30} Sperm carry the sign of maleness or femaleness while the egg carries only femaleness, he says, so the woman is not to be blamed for the sex of the child. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:10:00} All of this he refers to [[concept/qadar|divine decree]] and God's prior knowledge of every soul. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:06:30}

## Episode 28: Three hundred and sixty joints
He says the Prophet's sayings covered creed, worship, ethics, dealings, the universe, food, prevention, death and resurrection "without a single error". {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:02:00} He then takes up Muslim's hadith that every human was created with three hundred and sixty joints, and that whoever magnifies, praises, declares God's oneness, glorifies, asks forgiveness, removes a stone, thorn or bone from people's path, and enjoins good and forbids evil to that number has moved himself away from the Fire; al-Bukhari's narration that each joint owes a charity every day; and the narration of [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr]] in Muslim that two rak'as of the forenoon suffice for all of it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:05:00} He urges the [[practice/salat-al-duha|forenoon prayer]], which many neglect, and gives its time as from when the sun has risen a spear's length until it passes the meridian. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:05:30}

He says he asked many orthopaedic doctors and none knew the number of joints, that the Encyclopaedia Britannica groups the skeleton without counting them, and that the doctor Hamid Ahmad Hamid counted them in his book "Rihlat al-Iman fi Jism al-Insan" ("The Journey of Faith in the Human Body") and reached 360. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:09:30} What is meant, he says, are the movable synovial joints, not the fixed seams between the skull bones, and he gives as examples the ball-and-socket hip joint, from which engineers learned to make "ball and socket" joints, the gliding wrist joints and the joint between the two top vertebrae. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:11:00}

## Episode 29: A house without dates
He takes up Muslim's hadith in which the Prophet told [[person/aisha|A'isha]] that a household with dates does not go hungry and that a house without dates has a hungry household, repeating it two or three times. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:01:00} He distinguishes the unripe busr, the fresh rutab and the dried tamr, which keeps for a year or more, which is why, he says, the Prophet chose it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:06:00} The palm, he says, is mentioned in twenty places in the Qur'an; the date palm species has more than a thousand varieties, about four hundred in Arabia and about six hundred in Iraq; the palm bears drought and salty water, and its trees are male and female. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:03:30} He describes its spear-shaped leaves, no more than thirty or forty at the crown, and water rising in its trunk up to thirty metres by capillary action. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:05:00}

He gives the make-up of dates as he states it: 67 to 71 per cent carbohydrates, mostly sugars, about 2.5 per cent protein, about 1.5 per cent minerals and about 10 per cent fibre, with vitamins and antioxidants, and says dates keep their qualities for a year or more until the new harvest. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:09:00} He says they are a natural laxative and strengthen the muscles of the womb, helping childbirth, links this to the story of Mary and the palm trunk, cites the hadith on whoever starts the day with seven 'ajwa dates, and calls for laboratory study of such hadith to make them "a card for preaching". {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep29 00:12:00}

## Episode 30: The sun rising from the west
Some of what the Prophet foretold, he says, concerns the future and is hard to prove scientifically, yet some facts may give a glimpse of it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:02:00} He takes up the hadith that the Hour will not come until the sun rises from the west and people believe when belief no longer helps those who had not believed before, and the hadith that the first of the signs to appear is the sun's rising from the west or the beast coming out in the forenoon, and lists the ten major signs as he relates them: the beast, the Antichrist, the smoke, Gog and Magog, the descent of Jesus son of Mary, the sun's rising from the west, three sinkings of the earth in the east, the west and Arabia, and last a fire from Yemen driving people on. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:03:30} He says the sun's rising from the west was the deniers' argument through history, then begins to set out evidence he finds in ancient climates recorded in rocks and air bubbles in the Antarctic ice, and in annual growth rings, in which, he says, scientists found daily lines under the microscope and noticed that the number of days in a ring grows the older it is. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep30 00:06:00} The recording breaks off before the argument is finished, so nothing further is written.
