## Summary

The speaker argues that knowing Arab and Qurayshi lineages is needed to read the hadith collections and the early biographies, drawing on Ibn Hazm, al-Tabari and many chapters of al-Bukhari, and traces the Prophet's line to Adnan. He then turns to reports of the Prophet before his mission among scripture people, soothsayers and the jinn, closing with the story of Salman al-Farisi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Plan of the pre-prophethood phase and the place of lineage

- He says this is the second session of the series, which shares its title with Ibn Hisham's biography, and that the first session covered introductions on Ibn Ishaq's method and the aims of studying the Prophet's life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He says the reading of Ibn Hisham will begin from the first revelation, while one to three sessions on the earlier period will draw on several references rather than a reading of the book. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He holds that much written about the pre-prophethood period mixes sound and weak material, and that Arab tales and genealogies are partly well founded and partly not. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He holds that knowledge of lineages, of Quraysh and of the Prophet's descent belongs to the core of learning, and that many hadiths in the two Sahih collections and the early biographies cannot be fully understood without it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Ibn Hisham's method in presenting the lineage

- He relates a passage in which Ibn Hisham announces that he will begin with Ishmael and trace the line down to the Prophet, and will leave out some of Ibn Ishaq's material for brevity, such as verses no poetry expert knew and reports he found distasteful. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He says this passage is among the clearest statements of how Ibn Hisham treated Ibn Ishaq's text. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He says scholars agree conclusively on the named ancestors from the Prophet up to Adnan, and that the link from Adnan to Ishmael is not doubted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He holds that the names between Adnan and Ishmael rest on nothing firm, that reports differ on their number from four to twenty or thirty, and that the detailed stories about them lack a reliable chain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Ibn Hazm and al-Tabari on the purpose of lineage knowledge

- He relates that Ibn Hazm opens his genealogical compendium by arguing that knowing lineages is a stated purpose of how peoples and tribes were made, and that it is part of religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He says the verse in question is often used today for interfaith closeness, but that al-Tabari and Mujahid explain the word for getting to know one another as knowing one another's descent, not as a basis for ranking. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates al-Tabari's account of peoples and tribes, in which a people is a distant descent group such as Mudar or Rabia, a tribe is nearer such as Tamim, and a clan is nearer still, and reports Ibn Abbas, Said ibn Jubayr, Mujahid and Qatada giving close variants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates that Ibn Hazm divides lineage knowledge into an obligatory part and a part of merit, the obligatory part including knowing that Muhammad was of Quraysh and Banu Hashim, who may hold the caliphate, who is a forbidden marriage partner, who inherits, who may receive a share of the fifth, and who is barred from alms. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates that Ibn Hazm names Fihr ibn Malik as the ancestor of Quraysh, while Ibn Kathir names al-Nadr ibn Kinana, and he lists the early reckoning of the stipend registers by tribe as evidence that the caliphs relied on lineage knowledge. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Al-Bukhari's chapter on virtues and tribal reports

- He says al-Bukhari opens his book of virtues with the verse on peoples and tribes, the verse on kinship ties and a chapter on what is forbidden of the call of pre-Islamic days, and he holds this shows how rooted the subject is. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates that al-Bukhari reports, through Abu Hurayra, the Prophet's answer that the most honoured person is the most God-fearing, and a report from Zaynab bint Abi Salama that the Prophet descended from Mudar and al-Nadr ibn Kinana. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates reports on the Prophet's kin among Quraysh clans, on the east as the source of strife among Rabia and Mudar, on Yemen and wisdom, and on the saying that rule rests with Quraysh as long as two remain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates the account of Muawiya rebuking a report that a king would arise from Qahtan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He says al-Bukhari also has chapters on the descent of Yemen from Ishmael, on Aslam, Ghifar, Muzayna, Juhayna and Ashja, and on Qahtan, and that scholars of lineage commonly hold Yemen to be Qahtanid rather than Ishmaelite. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib: the share of the kin

- He relates a report that Jubayr ibn Mutim and Uthman ibn Affan asked the Prophet why he gave the Banu al-Muttalib a share from the Khaybar spoil and not them, and that the Prophet answered that Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib were one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

- He relates Ibn Abbas's explanation of the verse on love of kin as addressed to Quraysh, each clan of which was related to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Tribal virtue, the Aslam and Ghifar report, and context of preference

- He relates the story of Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and Aisha, in which men of Banu Zuhra, the Prophet's maternal kin, were used to win her over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates the report in which al-Aqra ibn Habis of Tamim disparaged Aslam, Ghifar, Muzayna and Juhayna as pilgrim thieves, and the Prophet's reply that they are better than Tamim, Amir, Asad and Ghatafan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Lineage as life and death, and the shift to deeds

- He holds that for the Arabs at the time of the mission lineage and standing were their central material, since they were not a dominant civilization like Persia and Rome. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He says one reason for Abu Jahl's refusal was rivalry between Makhzum and Hashim, and relates that followers of Musaylima from Rabia preferred a liar of Rabia to a truthful man of Mudar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He holds that within a few years the main labels in Medina became Emigrants and Helpers, names of deeds and not of descent, and that this was a great reversal of standards. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates the report of a quarrel between an Emigrant and a Helper on a campaign, the Prophet's rebuke of the pre-Islamic call, and his refusal to have Abdullah ibn Ubayy killed so that people would not say he killed his companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Further lineage hadiths, Hudaybiyya and early maternal ties

- He relates the report of al-Ash'ath ibn Qays, which he says Ibn Kathir calls strong in chain, that the Prophet said his people were the sons of al-Nadr ibn Kinana, and the report in Muslim that God chose Kinana, then Quraysh, then Hashim, then the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Announcements before the mission among scripture people

- He says the next topic, announcements of the Prophet before his birth, follows the order Ibn Kathir uses in his history, and that the mission is the greatest event in earth's history because it seals prophecy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir that Jewish rabbis, Christian monks and Arab soothsayers spoke of the Prophet before his mission, the first from their books and the last from jinn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Soothsayers, jinn and the guarding of the heavens

- He holds that soothsayers had some true reports because jinn could overhear the heavens before the mission, and that this ended when the shooting stars were set against them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

- He says most reports on soothsayers and voices of the jinn in the long biographies are not sound, but that the idea explains why some news of the Prophet was known to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}

### Salman al-Farisi's search

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's account of Salman al-Farisi, as told to Ibn Abbas, of a Persian from Isfahan, the son of a village chief, who served a fire temple and was drawn to Christian prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates that Salman fled with traders to Syria, served a bishop who hoarded charity, then a series of upright men at Mosul, Nisibis and Amorium, the last of whom told him of a prophet coming with Abraham's religion to a land between two lava fields with date palms. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates that Salman was carried toward Arabia, sold in Wadi al-Qura to a Jew and then to a man of Banu Qurayza, and reached Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
- He relates the three signs Salman tested: the Prophet declined charity, accepted a gift, and showed the seal of prophecy between his shoulders. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep2}
