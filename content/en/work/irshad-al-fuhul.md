## Summary

Irshad al-Fuhul ila Tahqiq al-Haqq min Ilm al-Usul ("Guiding the Masters to the Ascertainment of Truth in the Science of Principles") is a work on the principles of jurisprudence by [[person/al-shawkani|Muhammad ibn Ali al-Shawkani]], the scholar of Sana'a. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.3} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.5} Its closing lines record that the author finished it on Wednesday 4 Muharram 1231 AH (December 1815). {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.289} The museum uses the Dar al-Fikr printing, which carries in its margin Ahmad ibn Qasim al-Abbadi al-Shafi'i's commentary on Jalal al-Din al-Mahalli's commentary on al-Waraqat by Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.3}

## Why it was written

In his preface al-Shawkani says that usul al-fiqh is the science scholars fall back on when they settle questions, and that its rules had come to be taken for granted: when someone quoted a phrase of the usul scholars his opponents gave way, and so many scholars slipped into personal opinion while believing they were following transmitted proof. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.4} He says he wrote it at the request of a group of scholars, to show the stronger from the weaker and the sound from the unsound in this field, so that a scholar might see clearly. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.4} He keeps only those preliminary topics that are truly useful, and says that because of this science many independent reasoners had drifted back into imitation without realising it. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.4} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.5}

## Structure

He arranged it in an introduction, seven aims (maqasid) and a conclusion. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.5} The edition's index gives its divisions with printed pages: {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.291} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.292} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.293} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.294}

- **Introduction**, in four chapters: the definition, subject, use and sources of usul al-fiqh; legal rulings (the ruling, the one who rules, the act ruled on and the person addressed); linguistic foundations; the division of words, with derivation, synonymy, homonymy and literal and figurative usage (printed pp. 3–28).
- **Aim 1: the Book**: its definition, readings transmitted by single reports, the clear and the ambiguous, and foreign words in the Qur'an (pp. 29–32).
- **Aim 2: the Sunna**: its independent authority in law, the protection of prophets from sin, the Prophet's acts, tacit approval, intentions and gestures, then reports and their division into mass-transmitted and single, the terms of transmission and the grades of receiving, sound and broken chains, criticism and validation of narrators, and the probity of the Companions (pp. 33–70).
- **Aim 3: [[concept/ijma|consensus]]**, in twenty sections, including its possibility and authority, the consensus of the people of Medina, the passing of the generation, tacit consensus and the introduction of a third opinion (pp. 71–89).
- **Aim 4: commands, prohibitions, the general and the particular**, the unrestricted and the qualified, the ambiguous and the clarified, the apparent and the interpreted, explicit and implied meaning, and abrogation (pp. 90–197).
- **Aim 5: [[concept/qiyas|analogy]]** and related inference, in seven chapters: its definition, its authority, its pillars, the ways of establishing the cause, where analogy does not apply, objections, and inference (pp. 198–248).
- **Aim 6: [[concept/ijtihad|independent reasoning]] and [[concept/taqlid|imitation]]**, with the rules for the mufti and the questioner (pp. 249–272).
- **Aim 7: equivalence and [[concept/tarjih-usul|preference]]** between conflicting proofs (pp. 273–284).
- **Conclusion**, on two questions: whether the default for what no proof addresses is permissibility or prohibition, and whether thanking the Benefactor is obligatory by reason (pp. 284–287).

## Method

For each question al-Shawkani sets out the definitions and positions of the usul scholars, quoting al-Baqillani, al-Juwayni, al-Ghazali, al-Razi's al-Mahsul, al-Amidi, al-Zarkashi's al-Bahr al-Muhit, Ibn al-Sam'ani and others, and then gives his own view. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.200} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.201} On analogy, for example, he goes through the Qur'anic proofs offered for it one by one; he holds that the verse "take heed" ([[passage/quran-59-2|al-Hashr 2]]) does not point to legal analogy in any way, and he discusses al-Shafi'i's use in the Risala of the verse on compensation for game ([[passage/quran-5-95|al-Ma'ida 95]]). {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.202} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.203} He concludes that the analogy to be followed is the one whose cause is stated in a text, the one in which any difference is ruled out with certainty, and the a fortiori kind, and he regards the dispute with the deniers of analogy over this kind as merely verbal. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.205} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.206}

## Notable positions

- **Independent reasoning is easier for later scholars.** He answers those who said the age was empty of independent reasoners, arguing that because Qur'an commentaries, the Sunna and the imams' work of authenticating and weighing had all been written down, ijtihad had become easier for later scholars than for earlier ones; he names a chain of Shafi'i scholars, each the student of the one before: Ibn Abd al-Salam, Ibn Daqiq al-Id, Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, Zayn al-Din al-Iraqi, Ibn Hajar and al-Suyuti. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.256} See [[question/can-an-age-be-without-a-mujtahid|Can an age be without an independent reasoner?]].
- **Rejection of imitation.** He defines taqlid as acting on another's word without proof, holds that forbidding it is the view of the majority if not a consensus, and places between ijtihad and taqlid a middle way: the unlearned asking the learned about God's ruling, not about his opinion. For a full treatment he refers to his treatise [[work/al-qawl-al-mufid-fi-adillat-al-ijtihad-wa-al-taqlid|al-Qawl al-Mufid fi Hukm al-Taqlid]] and his book Adab al-Talab wa-Muntaha al-Arab. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.267} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.269} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.270}
- **The default ruling of things.** In the conclusion he sets out the positions on the default for matters no specific proof addresses, gives the proofs of those who hold permissibility and answers those who hold prohibition. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.286} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.287} See [[question/is-the-default-ruling-of-things-permissibility|Is the default ruling of things permissibility?]].

## The edition

The Dar al-Fikr printing is one volume of 289 printed pages with the index; its scanned pages give no year of printing, and the scan comes from the Bibliotheca Alexandrina. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.1} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.291} Al-Abbadi's commentary on al-Waraqat, printed in its margin, is a separate book from Irshad al-Fuhul. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.3} Pages cited here are pages of the scanned file; the printed page number is two lower.
