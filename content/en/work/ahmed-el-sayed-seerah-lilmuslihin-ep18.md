## Summary

The second session on the Battle of Badr. The speaker follows the march from Medina, the discovery that Quraysh had come out in force, the consultation with the Ansar, the night before the battle, the duels, the fighting and the fate of the Quraysh leaders, and closes with the standing of those who fought at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Badr as the Day of Criterion

- He says that this day carries a second title given in the Qur'an, the Day of Criterion, in addition to its famous name. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He holds that a thing having many names and titles points to its greatness, and he compares this with the many names of God and of the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He explains the title as the day on which truth was separated from falsehood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Intelligence and the scout sent after the caravan

- He describes an information war between the two camps, in which each side followed the other's movements. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that the Prophet never set out on a campaign without appearing to head elsewhere, to hide his plan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from Sahih Muslim that the Prophet sent Busaysa, also given as Basbas ibn Amr, as a scout to follow the caravan of Abu Sufyan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He recalls that the earlier attempt at the expedition of al-Ushayra, near Yanbu, had failed because the caravan passed before the Prophet arrived, and that the Prophet then returned to Medina for a few days. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He mentions the raid of Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri on the Prophet's camels as the reason for the first Badr outing, and says the Prophet followed him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says that once the scout returned, the Prophet announced that he had a target and told only those whose mounts were at hand to ride with him, refusing delays to fetch animals from the upper part of Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Those left behind and the deputies in Medina

- He says that Uthman ibn Affan stayed behind because his wife Ruqayya, the Prophet's daughter, was ill, and that the Prophet learned of her death on returning from Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates that the Prophet put Abu Lubaba in charge of Medina and Abdallah ibn Umm Maktum in charge of leading the prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He uses this to recall that several roles were assigned at once in a single event. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the force numbered a little over three hundred men. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Sending back the young and the march with few mounts

- He says that the Prophet reviewed the army near al-Anbariyya in Medina and sent back boys under fifteen. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that al-Bara ibn Azib and Abdallah ibn Umar were among those judged too young that day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He remarks that upbringing, surroundings and companions decide what young people can achieve, while fighting also needs physical strength. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the army stopped at stations on the road, among them al-Rawha, about seventy-five kilometres from Medina on the way to Yanbu, and notes a report that Jesus son of Mary will pass through it on pilgrimage near the end of time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the force had only about seventy camels and a single horse, belonging to al-Miqdad, so the rest walked and took turns riding, roughly three men to a camel, the Prophet included. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from the Musnad that the Prophet answered his two companions, when they offered to walk for him, that they were no stronger than he and he no less in need of reward. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Abu Sufyan's response and the captured water carriers

- He says the aim of the march was to intercept the large Quraysh caravan and take it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates that news of the march reached Abu Sufyan, who sent Damdam ibn Amr to warn Quraysh and also altered the caravan's route to evade pursuit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the Prophet in turn sent scouts ahead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from Sahih Muslim, on the authority of Anas, that Companions seized Quraysh water carriers at Badr, including a slave of the Banu al-Hajjaj, who first denied knowing about the caravan and then said Quraysh had come out with Abu Jahl, Utba, Shayba and Umayya ibn Khalaf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He adds that the Companions beat the man when he said this and released him when he fell silent, and that the Prophet, finishing his prayer, remarked that they beat him when truthful and let him go when he lied. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from the Musnad, on the authority of Ali, that a freedman of Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt was taken and would say only that the enemy were many and strong, until the Prophet worked out their number as about a thousand from the ten camels they slaughtered each day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He concludes from the two reports that the Companions had marched for a caravan and were not psychologically ready for a full battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Divine planning and the stop at Dhafiran

- He holds that the encounter came about by God's decree, as the Qur'an says the two sides had not arranged to meet, and that the believers had hoped for the unarmed party. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He draws the lesson that a believer should give what he can and be sincere with God, and God then arranges matters, even beyond what believers had planned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the Prophet halted at Dhafiran, close to Badr, where the news that Quraysh had marched was confirmed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Consultation and the pledge of the Ansar

- He holds that the Prophet wished to be sure of his men: the Muhajirun were with him in any case, but he feared some Ansar read their pledge at al-Aqaba as covering only the defence of Medina itself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the Prophet consulted several times, Abu Bakr and Umar spoke, and the Ansar understood that they were the ones being asked. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates that Sad ibn Mu'adh answered for the Ansar, affirming their faith and their pledges of obedience, saying they would follow even into the sea, that not one would stay behind, and that they were steady in war. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that al-Miqdad also spoke, saying they would not answer as the Children of Israel answered Moses but would fight on every side of the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the Prophet was gladdened by these words. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Choosing the camp at the wells of Badr

- He says the Muslims reached Badr, which he explains is named after a well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the Prophet camped at the main well and ordered the other wells spoiled so the Quraysh could not drink, a strength given that the Muslims were few and the enemy many. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He adds that Quraysh had sent water carriers for its army, as shown by the captured men. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says some Companions were reluctant at the start, as the Qur'an describes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### The night before the battle and the plea for help

- He holds that the Prophet carried an unmatched burden that night, not fear of death but concern that truth prevail and its people not perish. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from Ali, a report he notes was given the day before, that the others slept while the Prophet prayed and wept under a tree. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says rain fell that night as a mercy to the believers, and links it to a Qur'anic passage in Surat al-Anfal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the Prophet had a shelter built from which he watched the battle, set the lines and gave tactical directions, including the use of arrows at a distance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He describes the Prophet stretching out his hands toward the direction of prayer in a plea for help, so that his cloak fell from his shoulder, and repeating a request that God fulfil his promise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates that Abu Bakr replaced the cloak and told him that his appeal was enough, since God would fulfil what He promised. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says that afterwards the Prophet turned from deep worry to certainty and joy at the coming victory, and reads this as a sign of prophethood alongside his human condition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from the sound reports that the Prophet pointed out the places where leading Quraysh figures would fall. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### The duels, the fighting and the angels

- He asks listeners to read the arrival of Quraysh against thirteen years of torture, mockery and hostility in Mecca, and calls Badr the first military meeting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says three leading men of Quraysh, Utba, Shayba and al-Walid ibn Utba, came out to challenge to single combat, and the Prophet sent Ali, Hamza and Ubayda ibn al-Harith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says Ali and Hamza quickly overcame their opponents, while Ubayda and his opponent wounded each other, after which Hamza and Ali killed the opponent and carried Ubayda back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says Ubayda died of his wound on the way back to Medina and that his grave is known at Dhafiran. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the lines then clashed in fierce fighting and God sent angels, citing the Qur'an, and that some Companions saw the marks of their blows on the enemy, such as a report of a Muslim chasing a man who fell with a green weal on his nose. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He reads this as a physical, and not only moral, share of the angels in the battle, and as an example of their lasting link with believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates that the Prophet took a handful of pebbles and threw them at the enemy, and holds that this was a miracle which struck the face of every polytheist, with the Qur'an saying it was God who threw. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Casualties, prisoners and the mother of Haritha

- He says the polytheists broke and fled, and the Muslims killed seventy and captured seventy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He links this to the Qur'anic reminder after Uhud that the believers had inflicted twice the loss they suffered there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says fourteen Muslims were killed, among them a brother of Sad ibn Abi Waqqas aged sixteen and Haritha, who was struck by a stray arrow nobody could trace. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that Haritha's mother asked the Prophet about her son, saying she would be patient if he was in Paradise and would weep if not, and that the Prophet told her Paradise has many gardens and her son had reached the highest Firdaws. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### The well of the dead and Hassan's verses

- He says the Prophet had twenty-four leading Quraysh dead thrown into a foul well at Badr and stayed three days at the site, as was his habit after a victory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from al-Bukhari, through Anas and Abu Talha, and from Muslim, that on the third day the Prophet stood at the well's edge and addressed the dead by name, asking whether they had found their Lord's promise true. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says Umar asked how bodies without souls could hear, and that the Prophet replied that they heard as well as the living but could not answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says Hassan ibn Thabit recorded the scene in a poem that opens in the traditional love-prelude style and then turns to Badr, praising the Ansar, young and old, and telling of Abu Jahl, Utba and Shayba left dead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### The killing of Abu Jahl and the death of Umayya

- He relates from Sahih Muslim, on the account of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, that two young Ansar boys asked him to point out Abu Jahl, saying they had heard he insulted the Prophet and meant to kill him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says that when Abd al-Rahman pointed him out, the two struck him with their swords, and that the Prophet, asked who killed him, saw both swords and said both had, giving the spoils to Mu'adh ibn Amr ibn al-Jamuh, the other being Mu'adh ibn Afra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says Abu Jahl was still breathing and Abdallah ibn Mas'ud finished him, standing on his chest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says Umayya ibn Khalaf, who had tortured Bilal in Mecca, was captured by Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, who tried to protect him as a prisoner. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates that Bilal saw him and rallied a group of Ansar, and that Umayya was killed despite Abd al-Rahman's efforts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}

### Badr as a sign, and the standing of its fighters

- He relates that the Prophet sent Zayd ibn Haritha to Medina with the news of victory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He holds, with a lesson for reformers, that a constant triumph of unbelievers over weak believers is a trial for the unbelievers too, since it suggests they are right, and that Badr removed this impression and gave clear proof to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He mentions an interpretation of a Qur'anic supplication, held by some commentators, as meaning do not give unbelievers power over us so that we become a trial for them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says the lessons to draw include that God wills truth and falsehood to meet in the field so truth prevails, and that the believer gives and is sincere while God arranges his affairs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He says those who took part in Badr had their names recorded for Paradise, and that God knew none of them would abandon faith, even though some made mistakes, as Hatib did later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from Muslim and al-Bukhari that the Prophet rejected a slave's claim that Hatib would enter the Fire, saying a man who had been at Badr and al-Hudaybiya would not, and recalls the report that God looked on the people of Badr and forgave them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that Gabriel asked how the Prophet counted the Badr fighters and was told they were among the best of the believers, and that Gabriel said the same of the angels who attended Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He concludes that God preserves the stand of those who support truth in times of weakness, and that this may be a ground of forgiveness for later faults. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
- He announces that the next session will cover the return from Badr and the spoils. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep18}
