## Summary
A series by Omar Abdelkafy in which he reads hadiths from Sahih al-Bukhari, explains them and draws lessons. This set holds episodes from the Assabile archive, and everything in it is the sheikh's speech as it stands in the machine transcript; what is not traced to a chain or is garbled in the transcript is marked as needing a source.

## Episode 01: introduction to the series and deeds are by intentions
- He opens by saying the series explains the hadiths of Sahih al-Bukhari, taking three or four hadiths in a session, and that these hadiths cover the whole religion, from revelation to prayer, worship, dealings and every side of life, all drawn from the Prophet's Sunna. - He says Ibn Hajar spent twenty-five years explaining al-Bukhari's hadiths in Fath al-Bari, that al-Bukhari himself spent years gathering them and examining their narrators for soundness and trustworthiness, and that the community received the book with acceptance as the soundest book after the Book of God. See [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani]], [[work/fath-al-bari|Fath al-Bari]] and [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]]. This is his view. - He says that whoever questions a hadith must be a specialist in hadith, as one expects of a specialist in any other field. - He says he will speak of the place of the Sunna in legislation and mentions people who say they follow the Quran alone. He holds that whoever rejects a sound hadith has committed disbelief, since lying against the Prophet is the same as denying his hadith, and mentions by sense that whoever lies deliberately against the Prophet should take his seat in the Fire. - He says the Sunna explains the Quran, as an explanatory memorandum accompanies a law: the Quran commands prayer and the Sunna shows the five prayers, their times and how they are done, and the Quran commands zakat while the Sunna sets its threshold and its kinds, such as farmland, horses and livestock. - He says whoever denies the Sunna denies the Quran, and likens it to one who says wine is not forbidden or who rejects prayer, whom he calls a disbeliever. - He turns to the first hadith of Sahih al-Bukhari, which he says everyone knows by heart, and reads it as saying that deeds are by intentions and each person gets what he intended, so that one whose emigration was for God and His Messenger has it for them, and one whose emigration was for something worldly or a woman to marry has it for that.
