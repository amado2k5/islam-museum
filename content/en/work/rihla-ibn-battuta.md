## Summary
*Tuhfat al-nuzzar fi ghara'ib al-amsar wa-'aja'ib al-asfar* ("A Gift to Onlookers on the Curiosities of Cities and the Wonders of Journeys"), known as the Rihla of Ibn Battuta, is what the traveller [[person/ibn-battuta-muhammad|Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Lawati al-Tanji]] dictated of what he had seen, written up by [[person/ibn-juzayy-muhammad|Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Juzayy al-Kalbi]] at the order of the Marinid sultan [[person/abu-inan-faris|Abu Inan]]. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.14} The writing-up was finished on 3 Dhu al-Hijja 756 AH. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.566}

## How it was written
Ibn Juzayy says in his preface that Ibn Battuta, having travelled the earth and crossed its climes, settled at the court of Fez, where the sultan ordered him to dictate the cities he had seen, the rare reports he remembered and the kings, scholars and saints he had met, and ordered Ibn Juzayy to gather the dictation into a book. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.14} Ibn Juzayy describes his work: he conveyed the meaning of the shaykh's words in his own wording, sometimes keeping his exact words; he included every story and report he told, "and I did not undertake to investigate or test their truth"; he vocalized difficult names of places and people, and explained foreign names where he could. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.14} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.15} Passages beginning "Ibn Juzayy says" add reports, verse or comments. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.15} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.21}

## The journey in two parts
The text opens with Ibn Battuta saying he left Tangier, his birthplace, on Thursday 2 Rajab 725 AH to make the pilgrimage and visit the Prophet's tomb, alone, without a companion, at the age of twenty-two. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.15}

**Part one** passes through Tunis, Alexandria, Egypt, Syria, Medina and Mecca, then Iraq and Persia, Yemen and the Red Sea and East African coasts from Sawakin to Mogadishu and Kilwa, Oman, Hormuz and the Gulf, then Anatolia and its sultans, the lands of Sultan Muhammad Uzbek and Constantinople, Khwarazm, Transoxiana and Khurasan, and ends at "Panj Ab", the waters of the Indus, as the new moon of Muharram 734 AH appeared. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.7} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.9} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.10} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.312}

**Part two** opens with the voyage on the Indus and the lands of India; it describes Delhi, its walls and congregational mosque and the history of its kings, then dwells on Sultan [[person/muhammad-ibn-tughluq|Muhammad Shah ibn Tughluq]], who made him judge of Delhi, then the Maldives, Ceylon and China, the return to the Maghrib, al-Andalus, and the journey to the lands of the Sudan and Mali, ending with his arrival at Fez. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.568} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.572} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.15} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.354} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.412} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.566}

## How the material is set out
The book divides its material under headings beginning "Account of": the sultan of the town, its judges, its scholars, its mosques and shrines, its people's customs. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.7} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.8} Ibn Battuta describes what he saw, sometimes with the date of his visit, as when he says he first saw the [[place/al-manara-iskandariyya|Lighthouse of Alexandria]] with one side ruined and found it, on his return in 750 AH, so ruined that it could not be entered. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.20} He separates what he saw from what he was told: what he says of the kings of India he learned, wholly or mostly, from the chief judge Kamal al-Din ibn al-Burhan al-Ghaznawi, whereas most of what he reports of Muhammad Shah he witnessed, and he swears by God that what he relates of the sultan's generosity is "certain truth". {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.354} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.355}

## Samples of its content
- **Alexandria**: the Lighthouse and the Pillar of the Columns, and the trick of an archer who climbed the pillar with an arrow and a rope, as Ibn Juzayy heard it from one of his teachers. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.20} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.21}
- **Delhi**: the sultan's palace and its three gates, Muhammad Shah's generosity and severity together, and his removal of Delhi's people to Dawlatabad. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.355} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.378} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.385}
- **The lands of the Sudan**: [[place/iwalatan|Iwalatan]], first province of the Sudan; the court of [[person/mansa-sulayman|Mansa Sulayman]], sultan of Mali; and [[place/takedda|Takedda]] and its copper mine. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.547} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.551} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.564}

## Ibn Juzayy's conclusion and the book's reception
Ibn Juzayy closes by saying that what precedes is his summary of the shaykh's account, that Ibn Battuta is "the traveller of the age", and that whoever called him "the traveller of this community" would not be wrong, and that he chose Fez as his home after twenty-five years of travel. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.566} In an appendix to part one the edition's corrector quotes Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddima* on Ibn Battuta's tales of India and the whispers at court that he was lying; see [[question/credibility-of-ibn-battuta|the question of believing Ibn Battuta's reports]]. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.313} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.314}

## The edition read
The first edition at al-Matba'a al-Khayriyya in Cairo, owned by Umar Husayn al-Khashshab, was read: two parts whose title pages bear the year 1322 AH, printing finished in Safar 1323 AH, corrected by Abd al-Jawad Khalaf. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.11} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.315} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.567}
