## Summary
The Maqamat of Badi' al-Zaman al-Hamadhani — the celebrated work with which he founded the genre, later followed by al-Hariri {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 159}.

## Details
Ibn al-'Imad describes Badi' al-Zaman — Abu al-Fadl Ahmad ibn al-Husayn ibn Yahya ibn Sa'id, the hafiz — as "author of the famous maqamat and elegant epistles," an eloquent master of prose and a difficult poet, who transmitted from Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Faris, author of al-Mujmal, among others {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 158}. In the maqamat he created a form blending rhymed prose, fictional narrative and philological display, so that the maqama became a literary genre in its own right, and the chronicler notes expressly that "al-Hariri followed him in his maqamat" {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 159}. He died on Friday, 11 Jumada al-Akhira, per al-Hakim Abu Sa'id 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Dust, compiler of his epistles, who reports that he died of apoplexy and was buried in haste {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 159}.
