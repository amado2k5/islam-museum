## Overview
*Al-Rawd al-Unuf fi Sharh al-Sira al-Nabawiyya* ("The Untrodden Meadow: a Commentary on the Prophetic Biography") is the commentary of [[person/al-suhayli|Abu al-Qasim 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Abd Allah al-Suhayli]] (d. 581 AH) on [[work/sira-ibn-ishaq-ibn-hisham|Ibn Hisham's Sira]]. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 5} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.751} Ibn al-'Imad lists among its author's other works his book on the unnamed persons of the Qur'an and *Nata'ij al-Nazar*. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.751}

## Its layout in al-Wakil's edition
In 'Abd al-Rahman al-Wakil's edition the Sira text is printed section by section, each followed by al-Suhayli's commentary on it: volume 6 gives Ibn Ishaq's and Ibn Hisham's text from the killing of Ubayy ibn Khalaf at Uhud onward, then the Rawd chapters on that same stretch, and so on to Khaybar. The index marks Sira headings (س), grammar and language (ن. ل) and poetry (ش), leaving the Rawd headings unmarked. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 5, 595}

## Al-Suhayli's method
His chapters range over:
- **Language and rare words:** for instance his gloss of *sha'ra'* in the story of Ubayy ibn Khalaf as a kind of fly, with al-Qutabi's reading "as the hairs scatter", and his explanation of "owls of today or tomorrow" and "a donkey's interval between drinks" in the story of Husayl ibn Jabir and Thabit ibn Waqsh. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 32, 35-36}
- **Grammar:** such as his discussion of the dropping of the n of *min* before a following unvowelled letter in a verse of Hind bint Uthatha — allowed, he says, only with *min* because of its frequency, citing Sibawayh — and of the use of *laka'i* outside the vocative. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 37}
- **Genealogy and names:** for example his explanation of the nickname [[person/husayl-ibn-jabir-al-yaman|al-Yaman]], the derivation of the name [[person/quzman|Quzman]], and the vocalisation of Waqsh. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 34, 36} [[person/ahmad-muhammad-shakir|Ahmad Muhammad Shakir]] quotes from the Rawd its rejection of Ibn al-Anbari's reading of the name "al-Yas ibn Mudar". {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban-shakir-maarif-v1 p. 52}
- **Additions to Ibn Ishaq:** such as the story of [[person/qatada-ibn-al-numan|Qatada ibn al-Nu'man]]'s restored eye, al-Daraqutni's report that both his eyes were restored, the endowment of [[person/mukhayriq|Mukhayriq]]'s estates as the first *hubus* in Islam — which he calls "a completion" of Ibn Ishaq's account "and an added benefit" — and 'Amr ibn al-Jamuh's prayer not to be brought back. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 32-36, 47}
- **Sources:** he cites the *Muwatta'*, 'Abd ibn Humayd's *Tafsir*, Ibn Wahb's *Jami'*, Ibn 'Abbas's *Tafsir*, al-Daraqutni, the book of Abu Hanifa, and others. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 32-35}

## The editor's notes
Al-Wakil comments on the commentary in his notes and sometimes disagrees with it: he calls the addition, from outside Ibn Ishaq, about 'Amr ibn al-Jamuh's camel refusing to go to Medina "a legend", and on Qatada's eyes he cites al-Nawawi's remark that Abu Nu'aym said "his eyes ran" and was faulted for it, and notes that 'Ammar ibn Nasr has no corroborator in his report from Malik. {cite:rawd-unuf-wakil-v6 p. 34, 36} These are the editor's views, not al-Suhayli's.
