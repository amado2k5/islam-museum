## Overview
Nawadir Juha al-Kubra is a modern collection of the anecdotes of Juha, prepared and revised by Darwish al-Juwaydi and published by al-Dar al-Namudhajiya in Sidon and Beirut {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 3}. The scan the museum read has 198 pages: an introduction on Juha's name and biography, a biography of Nasreddin Hoca, the numbered anecdotes, and an index of titles.

## The introduction
The introduction gathers what is said of the Arab Juha, Abu al-Ghusn: his Fazari descent and Kufan upbringing, his encounter with Abu Muslim al-Khurasani, al-Jahiz's mention of him, the book Nawadir Juha in Ibn al-Nadim's Fihrist, and the dispute over his name between al-Jawhari, the author of the Qamus and Ibn Hajar {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 4-5}. It then gives a life of Shaykh Nasreddin: his birth in Sivrihisar and death in Akşehir, his tomb and the customs of his town {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 7-8}. The editor holds that the Juha literature is cumulative, the work of no single person or age {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 4}.

## The anecdotes
The anecdotes begin on the ninth page of the scan, with the preacher who asks the congregation whether they know what he is about to say {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 9}. They are short, each with a title, and set in the house, the market, the mosque, the court, the bathhouse and the road: Juha and his donkey, Juha and his neighbour, Juha and the judge, Juha and his wife, Juha and Timur. Some belong to the legacy of the Anatolian Nasreddin, visible in the names Akşehir, Sivrihisar and Konya, and some to the older Arabic anecdotes.

## In the museum
The museum machine-read the whole book and made a record for each anecdote: its printed title, a retelling in the museum's own words in five languages, its page and number, and an excerpt of at most forty words. The text has not been checked against the page image, the translations are machine translations no human has read, and the edition's copyright status is not known, so the museum carries its facts and a short retelling, not its text. The Juha and Islamic Folklore Browser shows them in the section on Juha's anecdotes.
