## Overview
[[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] gathered in this book forty-two comprehensive hadiths on the roots of the religion and its manners. He took the core from the session of "Al-Ahadith al-Kulliyya" by Ibn al-Salah, which had twenty-six hadiths, then added to it until the number was complete, and named the book "Al-Arba'un" (The Forty). The book became well known and was widely memorised, and commentators wrote a great deal on it. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 26}

## Arrangement
Al-Nawawi arranges the hadiths by their meaning rather than by the chapters of jurisprudence. He opens with the hadith that actions are judged by intentions, then the levels of religion and the pillars of Islam, and closes with hope in God's wide forgiveness. Each hadith has a short heading that names its theme. In the Dar al-Salam edition used here, each hadith is introduced by the name of the Companion who transmitted it, and its sources are given in the footnotes. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.3} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.32}

### Themes
- **Intention, faith and the levels of religion** (hadiths 1 to 4): the book opens with actions judged by intentions, then the hadith of Gabriel that defines Islam, faith and excellence (ihsan), then the hadith that Islam is built on five pillars, and then the hadith on the creation of a person in the mother's womb and the writing of their provision, term and deeds. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.3} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.4} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.5} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.6}
- **Innovation, scrupulousness, the lawful and the unlawful** (hadiths 5 to 12): rejecting innovations in the affair of religion; the clear lawful, the clear unlawful and the doubtful between them; sincere counsel; the command to fight people until they bear witness; leaving what is forbidden; good provision; leaving what causes doubt for what does not; and leaving what does not concern a person. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.7} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.8} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.9} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.10} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.11}
- **Protection of life, property and honour, and good character** (hadiths 13 to 20): loving for one's brother what one loves for oneself; the three cases in which a Muslim's blood may be spilt; honouring neighbours and guests, and speaking well or keeping silent; the prohibition of anger; excellence in killing and slaughter; the counsel to fear God and follow a bad deed with a good one; the boy's counsel to guard God's commands; and modesty as part of faith. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.11} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.12} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.13} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.14} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.15}
- **Steadfastness, worship and reward** (hadiths 21 to 28): steadfastness after faith; a question about the path to Paradise; purity as half of faith; God forbidding injustice to Himself and among His servants; dhikr, charity and good deeds all counted as charity; holding to the Sunna and avoiding innovations; and righteousness as good character. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.16} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.17} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.18} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.19} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.21}
- **The foundations of the law and its limits** (hadiths 29 to 34): the head of the matter is Islam, its pillar is prayer and its highest point is struggle in the path of God; restraint of the tongue; the limits and prohibitions, and the matters left unsaid out of mercy; asceticism toward the world; no harm and no reciprocal harm; the burden of proof on the claimant; and changing wrong with the hand, then the tongue, then the heart. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.22} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.23} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.24} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.25}
- **Brotherhood, mutual help and hope** (hadiths 35 to 42): the prohibition of envy, rivalry and aversion; the rights of a Muslim over a Muslim; relief of another believer's distress, covering a Muslim's faults and seeking knowledge; the lifting of hardship, error, forgetting and compulsion from the community; living in the world as a stranger; desires following what was brought; and God's wide forgiveness for whoever calls on Him and hopes in Him. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.26} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.27} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.29} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.30} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.31}

## Takhrij and grading
For each hadith the editor names the compilers who recorded it, distinguishing the two Sahihs from the other collections. Where he gives a grade in a grader's own words he attributes it to that grader, for example Tirmidhi's "hasan sahih" on the hadith of doubt, and Ibn Majah and al-Daraqutni's "hasan" on the hadith of no harm, where he also notes that Malik transmitted it as a mursal report. {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.11} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.24} {cite:dar-al-salam-arbaeen-bakri p.25}

## Other editions
