## Summary

The tenth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers stays in Abyssinia and is built around one long report from Umm Salama: the Quraysh embassy with gifts to the Negus, Jafar ibn Abi Talib's account of Islam and his answer about Jesus, the Negus's refusal to hand the emigrants over, and the clash with a rival to his throne. The speaker then draws lessons from it for reformers: clarity about the faith, renewed trials, a firm foundation of knowledge, unity, not bending the truth, and the power of clear expression. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### Place in the series and plan of the session

- He says that this is the tenth session, that the series is still in the Meccan period and has reached about its midpoint or a little past it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that in terms of place the story is still in Abyssinia, since the account of the emigrants there is not finished. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that the session will be a long pause over a single report, and that perhaps nothing else will fit in it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### The report of Umm Salama and who she was

- He relates that the report is recorded in the Musnad of Imam Ahmad and comes from Umm Salama, the wife of the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that Umm Salama was among the women who emigrated to Abyssinia with her first husband, who died later in Medina in the third or fourth year after the Hijra, after which the Prophet married her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that she begins by describing the emigrants' life under the Negus as safe: they worshipped God without being harmed and without hearing anything they disliked, and he reads this as a contrast with their earlier condition in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### The Quraysh embassy and its gifts

- He relates that when Quraysh learned of the emigrants' security they decided to send two strong men to the Negus with gifts of the best goods of Mecca, above all tanned leather. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that they also prepared a gift for every one of the Negus's patriarchs, and sent the gifts with Abdullah ibn Abi Rabia of Banu Makhzum and Amr ibn al-As of Banu Sahm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the envoys were told to give each patriarch his gift first, then present the gifts to the Negus, and ask him to hand the emigrants over before he heard anything from them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that the plan was to win the patriarchs over so that they would support the demand, and that the aim was to have the emigrants returned without the king ever hearing what the Muslims had to say. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### The envoys' accusation and the Negus's refusal

- He relates that the envoys told the patriarchs that foolish youths of their people had come to the king's land, left their people's religion without entering the king's, and brought a new religion unknown to both sides, and that the noblemen of Quraysh had come to take them back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the patriarchs backed this and advised the Negus to hand the emigrants over, but that the Negus grew angry and refused, saying he would not give up people who had chosen his protection until he had summoned and questioned them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that this showed the Negus to be a wise and just king, and that Umm Salama says the envoys most feared that the king would hear the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He calls this the great crisis of the emigrants, because on it hung either their continued safe worship in Abyssinia or a return to Mecca that would undo everything. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### Jafar ibn Abi Talib's account of Islam

- He relates that the Companions met and agreed to say before the king what they had been taught by the Prophet, whatever might follow, and that Jafar ibn Abi Talib was the one who spoke when the Negus questioned them in front of his bishops with their scrolls open. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that Jafar described their former life of idol worship, eating carrion, indecency, cutting kinship ties, bad neighbourliness and the strong devouring the weak, then the coming of a messenger from among them whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness and chastity they knew. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that Jafar listed what the messenger called them to: worshipping God alone, abandoning stones and idols, honest speech, keeping trusts, kinship, good neighbourliness, avoiding bloodshed and forbidden things, and prayer, almsgiving and fasting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that prayer, almsgiving and fasting are meant here in the general sense, not the fixed forms of the obligatory alms with their thresholds or the fasting of one set month. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that Jafar ended by describing how their people tortured them to return them to idols, and how they left for Abyssinia, choosing the king and hoping not to be wronged under him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### The recitation and the Negus's verdict

- He relates that the Negus asked whether Jafar had anything of what the Prophet brought from God, and that Jafar recited to him the opening of Surat Maryam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the Negus wept until his beard was wet and his bishops wept until their scrolls were wet, and that he said this message and the one brought by Musa come from a single source of light. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He compares this saying to what Waraqa ibn Nawfal said earlier about the angel who came to Musa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the Negus swore he would never hand the emigrants over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### The second day: the question about Jesus

- He relates that Amr ibn al-As swore to expose the emigrants' belief about Jesus the next day, that Abdullah ibn Abi Rabia, whom the report calls the more God-fearing of the two, urged him not to because they were kin, and that Amr insisted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that Amr told the Negus the emigrants say something grave about Jesus, so the Negus sent for them again, and he says Umm Salama calls this a worse crisis than the first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the Companions again agreed to say what God had said and what the Prophet brought, and that Jafar answered that Jesus is a servant of God, His messenger, His spirit and His word cast to the virgin Mary. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the Negus took a twig from the ground and said that Jesus went no further than what Jafar had said, as much as that twig, and that the patriarchs snorted in protest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the Negus declared the emigrants safe in his land, said anyone who insulted them would be fined, and would not harm one of them for a mountain of gold, a mountain being what the word means in the Abyssinian tongue according to the report. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### The envoys sent back and the rival to the throne

- He relates that the Negus ordered the gifts returned to the two envoys, said God took no bribe from him when He restored his kingdom, and that the envoys left defeated. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that later a man came to dispute the Negus's rule, that the emigrants felt a grief greater than any before, fearing a ruler who would not know their right, and that the Negus marched out with the Nile lying between the two armies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam volunteered to go and bring news of the battle, that he was among the youngest of the group, and says that Al-Zubayr was about sixteen when he accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that Al-Zubayr inflated a waterskin, put it under his chest and swam across the Nile, while the Muslims prayed that the Negus would overcome his enemy, and that the Negus did and secured his kingdom. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the emigrants stayed in Abyssinia in good conditions until they came back to the Prophet, who was in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### First pause: the language of the report

- He says that the report has been written down in the books of Islam for about twelve hundred years and was narrated from the time of Umm Salama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that it reads smoothly and clearly even now, so that the whole story is understood without effort, and that this should make us reconsider how well Arabic has kept its standing over the centuries. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He recalls that in his earlier lessons on Sahih al-Bukhari he usually found little need to explain the wording of the reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### Second lesson: the clarity of the message

- He holds that one of the greatest lessons for reformers is that the message of Islam was entirely clear to the believers, as Jafar's description to the Negus shows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that Muslims today often lack this clarity about their religion, and that many notice the weight of its central principles only when someone from outside asks them what Islam is. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that the fundamentals must be honoured not only in speech but in the whole setting of a believer's life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that this clarity was shared by the Companions generally and not only Jafar, and he gives two parallel cases. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari the account of al-Mughira ibn Shuba, who was asked by a Persian commander who the Arabs were, with an army of forty thousand facing a force led by al-Numan ibn Muqarrin sent under Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that al-Mughira described the Arabs as having lived in hardship and idol worship until a prophet from among them was sent, whom they knew by parentage, and that in that setting of war he stated what the Prophet had commanded them in relation to fighting, to payment of the jizya and to the fate of those killed and those who survive. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that the first part of al-Mughira's reply resembles Jafar's, though the occasion differed: Jafar was seeking a king's protection, al-Mughira was speaking in a campaign. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan, still a polytheist, when asked by Heraclius what the Prophet commanded, answered plainly with worship of God alone, rejecting the ancestors' ways, prayer, truthfulness, chastity and keeping kin, and holds that this shows the message was clear even to opponents. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### Lessons on trials, foundations and unity

- He holds that a Muslim reformer on the path of the prophets is never free of trial at some stage, and notes that the Companions faced trials in Mecca and then a new one in Abyssinia once they were safe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that the Companions handled the crisis alone, with no direct guidance from the Prophet, because he had raised them in the little time before the emigration on a clear base of knowledge and method. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that a reformer needs such a base at the start of his road so that he can meet crises, and that a sign of a successful educator is training students to draw out principles for situations he never described to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He gives as an example students raised in Qur'an circles who later fell away or abandoned belief when they met crises alone that they had not been trained for. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that the Companions' being together was among the things that steadied them, since they met at once, consulted, agreed on one answer and put forward one speaker, and he says disunity today is among the greatest problems. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

### Lesson: not bending the truth, and Surat al-Kafirun

- He holds that it is among the great principles of the religion that its truths must not be blurred with others, and that those who hold the truth must not trim it to suit a crisis or a situation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He recalls that he spoke the previous day of the Prophet conveying the truth openly without flattery, citing the report of Amr ibn Abasa, and he points to verses of Surat al-Qalam and Surat al-Isra on the polytheists' wish for compromise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that one reported occasion for Surat al-Kafirun was the polytheists' offer that the Prophet worship their god for a year and they his for a year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds, against those who use the last verse of that chapter to bring unbelief and Islam closer, that the chapter is a declaration of disassociation from unbelief and its people, and says it is reported that the Prophet described it as a disavowal of polytheism. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that justice and fair dealing toward non-Muslims are right, but that telling someone that all religions lead to God and they will meet in paradise is, in his view, a betrayal and deception of that person, since God alone judges who enters the Fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He relates that the Prophet visited a sick Jewish boy and invited him to Islam, and that when the boy accepted, the Prophet thanked God for saving him from the Fire, which he holds shows the Prophet's concern for people to enter Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that the Companions in Abyssinia stated the truth about Jesus as it is, even in crisis, and that how to put it well is a separate matter of expression. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Lesson: the power of clear expression, and closing

- He holds that expression is vital for a reformer, since the truth is sometimes harmed by the inability of its bearer to state it, for instance when someone unfit debates an unbeliever or a doubter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that Jafar's reply to the Negus is a text to study as a model of expression, and says that real eloquence is not memorising rare phrases or elaborate similes but conveying the intended meaning in fitting words. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that Jafar's speech has no figures of speech, and its strength lay in laying out the argument and putting the points in order rather than pleading for pity. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He holds that the report shows how the Prophet's school raised its students, and that its result was a clear stand, one voice, the ability to defend themselves and keeping the religion's truths against the Quraysh plot. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He adds briefly that the report also shows the need to tell apart the kinds of opponents, citing the second envoy whom it calls the more God-fearing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
- He says that the next session will finish with Abyssinia and its migration and move to the last third of the Meccan period, the final four or five years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep10}
