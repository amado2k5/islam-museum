## Summary
Al-Bahir in algebra is [[person/al-samawal-al-maghribi|al-Samawal al-Maghribi]]'s work, described by biographies as composed at nineteen with possible later revisions; no exact composition year is calculated from that age and approximate biographical dates {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography} {cite:samawal-dsb algebra}. It preserves information about lost work of [[person/al-karaji|Karaji]] while presenting broader algebraic operations and calculations {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography}.

## Four parts
The first treats premises, multiplication, division and root extraction; the second unknown quantities, equations and summations; the third irrational magnitudes; and the fourth problem classification {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography}. The biography describes negative quantities and polynomial operations alongside geometrical demonstrations for quadratic equations, so arithmetic development does not mean the abolition of all geometry {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography}. The irrational-magnitudes section follows Euclid's tenth book, while problem classification distinguishes solvable cases, cases not known to be solvable, and cases whose assumed solution leads to contradiction {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography}.

## Coefficient witness
The second part preserves [[invention/karaji-binomial-coefficient-recurrence|binomial-coefficient calculation]] attributed to Karaji; the biographies describe it as the surviving account of a lost original computation {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography} {cite:samawal-dsb algebra}. The work's relation to that record is consequently “describes,” not “first describes” or “Karaji's autograph” {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography}. MacTutor's reproduced translation is a modern intermediary, not an independent Arabic reading undertaken for this page {cite:karaji-mactutor Biography}.

## Chronology and identity
The biography places youthful composition before al-Samawal's1163 conversion to Islam, while Anbouba allows subsequent revisions; neither fixes every surviving passage's date {cite:samawal-mactutor Biography} {cite:samawal-dsb algebra}. No blanket claim that the entire book was composed during his Islamic period, or an unsupported personal religious school, is added.
