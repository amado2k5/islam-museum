## Summary
The [[work/farewell-sermon|Farewell Sermon]] the Prophet delivered at Arafat on 9 Dhu al-Hijja 10 AH, repeating its sentences on the Day of Sacrifice at Mina; it is the fullest text attributed to him in the field of rights and rites {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 459-462}.

## The sermon
The Prophet opened it: "O people, hear my words, for I do not know — perhaps I shall not meet you after this year of mine at this station ever again" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 459; ibn-hisham-sira}. In it came: "Your blood and your wealth are inviolable to you as the inviolability of this day of yours in this month of yours in this town of yours" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 459; sahih-muslim}. In it he laid down every matter of the Jahiliyya — its blood-wrongs and its usury — beginning with the blood of Ibn Rabi'a ibn al-Harith and the usury of al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 459; sahih-muslim}. He commended women: "Fear God regarding women, for you have taken them by God's trust" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 460; sahih-muslim}. He announced he had left among them that which, if they held to it — God's Book — they would never stray; and that there is no prophet after him and no umma after them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 460}. He closed with the people's testimony to the delivery, raising his forefinger to heaven: "O God, bear witness" — thrice {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 460}. He repeated on the Day of Sacrifice at Mina, in a second sermon on a grey mule: "Time has turned full circle to its form the day God created the heavens and the earth"; the inviolability of blood, wealth and honour; and that a wrongdoer wrongs only himself {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 461-462; sahih-al-bukhari}.

## For young visitors
A sermon the Prophet gave before more than a hundred thousand on the hajj, gathering Islam's most important teachings on people's rights and their rites.
