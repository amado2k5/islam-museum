## Summary
al-Mu'jam al-Saghir by Abu al-Qasim Sulayman ibn Ahmad ibn Ayyub al-Lakhmi al-Tabarani (d. 360 AH) is the third of his three mu'jams. It is a collection of hadiths with their unbroken isnads, gathered from the shaykhs he met on his long travels between Syria, the Hijaz, Egypt, Iraq, Persia and Isfahan. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.3}

## Structure and method
The author arranged the book as a mu'jam: the hadiths of each shaykh come in a row under his name, and the chapters follow the alphabet of the shaykhs' names. So there is a chapter for the letter alif, then one for those named Ibrahim, then Isma'il, Ishaq, and so on. He held that this order makes it possible to gather everything transmitted from each shaykh, to fix the routes by which each hadith was transmitted, and to describe each shaykh's time, place and social circumstances. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.3}

In the first volume of the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya edition (Beirut, 1403 AH / 1983 CE), the chapters begin with the letter alif on scan page 7 and run to the letter kaf. The opening pages contain the publisher's preface. It says that reprinting the book had long been a wish of Shaykh Muhammad Abd al-Muhsin al-Kutubi, and that the book is transmitted through Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Zayd. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.4}

## The author
Al-Tabarani came from the Yemeni tribe of Lakhm. He grew up between Palestine and Syria, memorising the Qur'an and learning the basics of knowledge. He then spent his life travelling in search of hadith: from Syria to the Hijaz, from Yemen to Egypt, Iraq, Persia, the Jazira and Isfahan. He died in Dhu al-Qa'da 360 AH, at the end of a life that the preface says reached a hundred years. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.3}

He wrote on many subjects, including tafsir, the proofs of prophethood and rites of pilgrimage. Among his works are the al-Mu'jam al-Kabir (on the Companions' names), the al-Mu'jam al-Awsat (on the rare reports of his shaykhs), and books on the hadith of the Syrians, the Women's Companionship, the Firsts, the Sunna and the Rare Reports. Among those who transmitted from him were Abu Ahmad al-Assal, qadi of Isfahan, Ibrahim ibn Muhammad ibn Hamza, and Abu al-Shaykh. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.3}

## What the first volume contains
The volume opens with the publisher's preface. The text then begins with the chapter on alif (those named Ahmad and similar names), followed by the chapters of the names that come after, down to kaf (Kawshadh and Kunayz). Each report is given with its isnad and matn. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.7}

The cover says the volume is followed by Ghunyat al-Alma'i by Shams al-Haqq al-Azimabadi. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.1}

## The edition used
Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, Beirut, first edition 1403 AH (1983 CE). Volume 1 runs from the preface through the chapters from alif to kaf. {cite:mujam-saghir-dar-kutub-v1 p.1}

## For young visitors
The al-Mu'jam al-Saghir is a book by the hadith master al-Tabarani. He arranged its hadiths by the names of the shaykhs he heard them from, so each shaykh has his own chapter. In the first volume you find his shaykhs from alif to kaf.

## Research notes
- The order is by the shaykhs' names, not by subject, so a single report appears under the shaykh who transmitted it.
- This article describes volume 1 only. Later volumes, from lam onward, will be described when they are harvested.
- Judgements on the hadiths belong to the editors and commentators, not to the author.
- For comparison, read al-Tabarani's al-Mu'jam al-Kabir and al-Mu'jam al-Awsat, which the preface lists among his works.
