# Archnet

## Summary

Archnet is an open digital project on Islamic architecture and the built environment of Muslim societies. It was conceived in 1998 by [[person/aga-khan-iv|Aga Khan IV]], MIT's president Charles Vest and William Mitchell, dean of its School of Architecture and Planning, and was officially launched on 27 September 2002. {cite:wikipedia-archnet-en}

## Aim

The article traces the idea to a 1983 speech by the Aga Khan in Istanbul on the need of architects and students in the Muslim world for resources they could reach, and says it was meant as a global community joining architects, planners, historians and students. {cite:wikipedia-archnet-en}

## Contents

The site holds records of buildings and sites, images, drawings and texts, and the archives of architects including Michel Ecochard, Hassan Fathy and Rifat Chadirji; from March 2007 it published the International Journal of Architectural Research (IJAR). {cite:wikipedia-archnet-en}

## Stewardship

Since 2011 it has been run by the [[group/aga-khan-trust-for-culture|Aga Khan Trust for Culture]] with the Aga Khan Documentation Center at the MIT Libraries, and in January 2014 it was relaunched open, without logins. {cite:wikipedia-archnet-en}
