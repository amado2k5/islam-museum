## Summary
The Seh Nasr is [[person/zuhuri|Zuhuri]]'s major work at Bijapur: a trilogy of the Nauras, a Persian preface to the sultan's [[work/kitab-i-nauras|Urdu book]] of the same name; the Gulzar-i Ibrahim (The Rose Garden of Abraham); and the Khwan-i Khalil (The Table of the Friend of God). The first two praise the sultan's character and accomplishments; the third gives valuable information on his courtiers. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 67}

## Content
In the Nauras Zuhuri presents [[person/ibrahim-adil-shah-ii|Ibrahim]] as a skilled musician, painter and calligrapher and a tireless patron who sent messengers across the world for skilled men and built at Nauraspur, a suburb of Bijapur, a residence for musicians. The Gulzar-i Ibrahim lists his nine virtues, borrowing the story of Abraham for whom the fire became a garden; the Khwan-i Khalil likens the court to the Friend's table and names six courtiers: the chief minister Shah Nawaz Khan, a mathematician; the poet Malik Qumi; the calligrapher Hazrat Shah Khalilullah; the painter [[person/farrukh-husain|Farrukh Husain]]; the wit Mullah Haidar Zehri; and Zuhuri himself. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 68}

## Value for art history
Mark Zebrowski notes that Deccani literature, like its painting, is idealised and gives little factual data, the Khwan-i Khalil praising in metaphor rather than recording. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 155} Yet from Zuhuri's lines on Farrukh Husain he infers that the painter led the royal workshop and that some painters in Islamic India held very high standing. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 69}
