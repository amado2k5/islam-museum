## Summary

The lecturer opens the account of the Battle of the Trench, the last time the polytheists marched on Medina. He covers the causes, the digging of the trench and the hardships of the siege, the breach of the covenant by Banu Qurayza, the inner trials of believers and hypocrites, the delayed prayer, and the wind that broke the allied camp. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### Why the Trench is a major event

- He says that the great events of the biography are marked by Qur'anic revelation, and he lists Badr, Uhud, the Trench, the Conquest of Mecca, Hudaybiyya, Tabuk, Hunayn and Banu al-Nadir as examples. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He holds that the Trench stands out because a whole sura, al-Ahzab, was revealed with a large part of it concerning this event. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that the Prophet said at its end that the Muslims would now march against the polytheists and no longer be marched against. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He holds that this was the last campaign in which the polytheists came to Medina, after which the balance turned and the Muslims went to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### Causes of the battle and the gathering of tribes

- He relates that biographers count the Jews' efforts among the main reasons the polytheists came to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He explains that after the setback at Uhud the Jews hoped for a decisive final blow, so they urged that not only Quraysh but the Arab tribes be gathered in groups. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the battle is called al-Ahzab, the confederates, because the attackers were many tribes and not Quraysh alone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He names Ghatafan as one of the largest tribes taking part, along with Banu Sulaym and Banu Asad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Lesson: vigilance and intelligence gathering

- He says that the Prophet was always on guard and kept scouts to learn the enemy's movements, so no campaign took him by surprise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that before Badr the Prophet sent a scout to follow the Quraysh caravan, and acted at once when the report came. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He holds that the Prophet trained his companions in awareness, and that reformers must not be naive about how enemies approach. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says, citing al-Bukhari, that the Prophet usually concealed a campaign's real destination by appearing to head elsewhere. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### The idea of the trench and the layout of Medina

- He relates that when news came that the confederates were marching, the Prophet consulted his companions, and the idea of digging a trench round Medina, unknown to the Arabs before, was adopted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He holds that the plan shows how large the enemy army was and served as a means of surprising and demoralising it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He describes Mount Sal as lying just west of the Prophet's Mosque, with the Muslims camped behind it, and the trench running from there west, then north, then back slightly east to form a protective angle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the other sides of Medina were guarded by lava fields and dense palm groves that an army of ten thousand or five thousand could hardly cross. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the polytheists first camped in the north-west, close to where they had camped at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### Digging in winter, hunger and the Prophet's labour

- He says the trench was long, wide enough that horses could not leap it easily, and deep, though in a few narrow places some leading horsemen managed to cross. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that the Prophet worked first among them, carried earth and stones, and was called on when a rock would not break. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the work took place in cold winter and in hunger, citing a report in al-Bukhari that hunger afflicted the people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that Jabir ibn Abdullah saw the Prophet with a stone bound to his belly after three days without food, a scene Jabir could not bear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says that Banu Haram, Jabir's clan, lived close to the trench just beyond Mount Sal, unlike the distant Banu Haritha, which explains Jabir's story. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that Jabir had his wife prepare a small amount of barley and a young kid, and invited the Prophet with one or two companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### The multiplied food at Jabir's house

- He relates that the Prophet called all the people of the trench to Jabir's food, and that Muhajirun and Ansar followed him there hungry. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that the Prophet took charge of the pot himself, serving from it and covering it each time, until everyone had eaten and some was left over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He counts this among the Prophet's miracles, and says the Prophet had leftovers shared out because people were suffering hunger. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### Lesson: leaders work in front; the rock and the promise of empires

- He holds that leaders of reform should work at the front and not remain theorists, because the gap between word and deed undermines them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that the Prophet encouraged the workers with verses of poetry, raising his voice in them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that a stubborn rock stopped the diggers, and the Prophet, strong in body, struck it several times until it broke, with a flash at each of the first three blows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the Prophet announced with those flashes the future treasures of the Persian emperor and the Byzantine emperor, to men who had gone three days without food. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says that the same promise was repeated later to Adi ibn Hatim, who asked, according to al-Bukhari, whether it meant Khosrow son of Hurmuz, and was told yes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the promise was fulfilled in the time of Umar, only a few years later, and that the hypocrites judged by the visible world and called the promise a deception. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### The siege and constant guard

- He says that the trench surprised the confederates and weakened their morale, but that it was only a first barrier and the real defence was the Muslims' own guard. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the constant guarding, arrow exchanges and watching of weak points absorbed all the Muslims' effort. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that Sa'd ibn Mu'adh was among the Companions struck by an arrow there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### Banu Qurayza break the covenant

- He relates that word spread that Banu Qurayza, in the inner part of Medina and bound to the Prophet by a covenant, had broken it and would enter the city from behind. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the danger was that the women and homes of the Muslims had no barrier, and the polytheists could cross the trench while the army was pinned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that the Prophet sent al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam to verify, and al-Zubayr returned reporting that the Qurayza had indeed betrayed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says Banu Qurayza betrayed at the hardest moment for the Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### Inner trial of believers and hypocrites

- He says that fear rose among the hypocrites, who began to think of withdrawing as they had at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He holds that even believers can be touched by doubts and fear under long or severe hardship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that sura al-Ahzab records what stirred inside hearts, and he links its description of the trial to an earlier verse of al-Baqara about testing by fear and hunger. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the hypocrites and the weak-hearted spread discouragement, claimed Medina could not be held, and sought leave by pleading that their houses were exposed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He holds that reformers must be brave and steady of heart, and he describes the Qur'anic portrait of the cowards' terror. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the believers, seeing the confederates, responded with greater faith and submission, so the same crisis split people into those who grew in faith and those who weakened. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### The delayed afternoon prayer

- He relates that during the siege Umar ibn al-Khattab came to the Prophet cursing the polytheists, saying he had not prayed the afternoon prayer until the sun was nearly setting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that the Prophet answered with a prayer against them for keeping the Muslims from the middle prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the cause of Umar's anger was not the length of the siege, hunger or cold but the missed prayer, and that this report is sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says this happened before the legislation of the fear prayer, and that some scholars use this to place the expedition of Najd after al-Ahzab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He explains that the fear prayer needs a rotation of lines, which is impossible in close combat, where jurists allow praying on foot or mounted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that some jurists raised the case where even that is impossible, and that they cite Companions at the siege of Tustar who prayed the dawn prayer only after sunrise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The wind and the night mission of Hudhayfa

- He holds that after the trial the Companions' sincerity showed, and God's help came through a wind as one of His soldiers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the wind overturned pots, put out fires and tore up tents in the polytheists' camp, and was also harsh, though milder, in the Muslim camp. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates, citing Sahih Muslim, that the Prophet asked who would bring news of the enemy, and that no one rose until he named Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says the task was infiltrating the enemy camp, harder than fighting, and that Hudhayfa rose because he was named. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He relates that Hudhayfa felt warm as he walked and found Abu Sufyan warming his back at a fire, and that he did not shoot because the Prophet had told him not to alarm them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says Hudhayfa returned and reported, and that the wind kept the enemy from settling, so Abu Sufyan, the army's leader, withdrew with the confederates. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says that, unlike at Badr, where Abu Jahl insisted on fighting, everyone now wanted to go home to warmth and safety. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}

### Outcome and the next meeting

- He says that the confederates thought a second round might follow, but the siege ended with the wind and the sending of two soldiers of God against them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
- He says that the next session will discuss the consequences, Banu Qurayza and the lessons to be drawn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep23}
