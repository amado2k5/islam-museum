## Summary

*Takwin al-'Aql al-'Arabi* (The Formation of Arab Reason) is a book by the Moroccan thinker Mohammed Abed al-Jabri, the first part of his *Critique of Arab Reason* series. In the copy read, the fourth edition from al-Markaz al-Thaqafi al-'Arabi in Beirut and Casablanca, September 1991, al-Jabri opens by arguing that the critique of reason is a basic, first part of any project of renaissance, and that the modern Arab renaissance did not take that path, which may be one of the main reasons it faltered. {cite:archive-org-takwin-al-aql-al-arabi-1991}

In his introduction al-Jabri says that in the book's second part he searched the components of Arab culture for the systems of knowledge that found it and clash within it. The second chapter starts from the French philosopher André Lalande's distinction between 'constituting reason' and 'constituted reason', then turns to Arab cultural time and the problem of progress. {cite:archive-org-takwin-al-aql-al-arabi-1991}
