## Summary

A question on forex trading where some companies say leverage is not a loan and charge no swap fees, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that leverage is a loan that brings a benefit to the lender, so any transaction involving it is not allowed. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3871}

## The question

The questioner said some companies told him leverage is not a loan but a tool that magnifies capital, and that they charge no swap fees; he asked about trading forex. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3871}

## The answer

The Department answered that leverage is, in fiqh terms, a loan from the broker to the trader; with interest it is clearly riba, and without interest it is still riba, because the broker gains the agreed commission on every transaction and because it combines a loan with a sale. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3871} It said leverage has a real effect: it multiplies the trader's capital, profits and risk, and earns the broker more commission than if the trader used only his own capital, so it is not a notional sum. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3871} It concluded that no transaction involving leverage is allowed, since it is an interest-bearing loan, and that this alone suffices to rule the transaction asked about forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3871}
