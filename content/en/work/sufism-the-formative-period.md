## Summary

*Sufism: The Formative Period* is an English book by Ahmet T. Karamustafa, published in 2007 by University of California Press in 202 pages and by Edinburgh University Press in its New Edinburgh Islamic Surveys. In his preface the author says he writes in a historicist mode, and that the earliest phase of Sufi history, from the third/ninth to the sixth/twelfth century, had not yet received a book-length monograph. {cite:google-books-sufism-formative-period} {cite:archive-org-sufism-formative-period-karamustafa-text}

## Publication and editions

The book has six chapters. The first treats the Sufis of Baghdad and their emergence in the second half of the third/ninth century, with portraits of al-Kharraz, al-Nuri and al-Junayd. The second surveys mystical trends outside Baghdad: al-Tustari in lower Iraq, the Malamatis in north-eastern Iran and al-Tirmidhi in Central Asia. The third traces the spread of Baghdad Sufism to other regions, Iberia among them. The fourth covers the rise of Sufi literature in Arabic and then Persian. The fifth covers the formation of communities around training masters in Sufi lodges, together with the emergence of saint cults. The sixth covers the spread of Sufism through all levels of society in the fifth/eleventh and sixth/twelfth centuries. {cite:archive-org-sufism-formative-period-karamustafa-text}
