## Summary
*Al-Tuhfa al-'Iraqiyya fi al-A'mal al-Qalbiyya* ("The Iraqi Gift, on the Works of the Heart") is a treatise by [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] on the inner works of the heart. It opens volume 10 of [[work/majmu-al-fatawa|Majmu' al-Fatawa]], titled "The Science of the Path", and fills its pages 5–90. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 3} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 770} At its start the author calls it "brief words on the works of the heart that are sometimes called the stations and the states", says they belong to the roots of faith and the foundations of religion, and that one of the people of faith asked for it to be written, so he wrote it in haste; the compiler notes in the margin that it is called *al-Tuhfa al-'Iraqiyya fi al-A'mal al-Qalbiyya*. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 5} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 6}

## Its founding principle
Ibn Taymiyya lists among these works love of God and His Messenger, reliance on God, making religion sincerely His, gratitude, patience with His judgement, fear of Him and hope in Him. He holds that all of them are obligatory on everyone under the command, by agreement of the imams of religion, and that people stand in them at three degrees, as in the works of the body: the one who wrongs himself by leaving a command or doing a forbidden thing; the one who keeps the middle way, doing the obligations and leaving the forbidden; and the one who races ahead in good deeds, drawing near with what he can of the obligatory and the recommended and leaving the forbidden and the disliked. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 6} He counts those of the middle way and those who race ahead among the friends of God, the first being their common rank and the second their elite, and cites the hadith "Whoever shows enmity to a friend of Mine", narrated by al-Bukhari. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 6} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 7}

From this principle he holds that the believer who wrongs himself has friendship with God in the measure of his faith and piety, and that one person may combine good and evil deeds so that he is both rewarded and punished; he attributes this to the Companions and the imams of Ahl al-Sunna, who say that no one with an atom's weight of faith in his heart remains in the Fire forever, and sets it against the view of the [[group/khawarij|Kharijites]] and the [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazila]] that such people remain forever and that there is no intercession for those who commit major sins. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 7} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 8} He draws on the hadith of the man repeatedly flogged for drinking, whom the Prophet forbade anyone to curse because he loved God and His Messenger, to show that a sinner may be a lover of God, while a devout ascetic may incur God's displeasure for the innovation in his heart. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 8}

## Reliance
Ibn Taymiyya answers those who made the stations belong to the common people rather than the elite; his example is the saying that reliance is "struggling for oneself in seeking one's sustenance", which the elite do not do. He replies that reliance is wider than reliance for worldly interests: the one who relies on God relies on Him for the soundness of his heart and his religion and the guarding of his tongue and his will; God joins worship and reliance in several places of the Qur'an, and these two gather the whole religion. He reports from some of the early generations that the knowledge of the revealed books was gathered in the Fatiha and then in [[passage/quran-1-5|al-Fatiha 5]]. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 18} He defines worship as a name gathering the utmost love for God and the utmost humility before Him: love without humility, or humility without love, is not worship. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 19}

## Patience, contentment and praise
He makes patience the condition of right conduct as knowledge is the condition of guidance, and quotes Ali's saying that patience is to faith as the head is to the body. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 40} He then reports the dispute among the scholars and shaykhs of Ahmad's school and others over [[question/is-contentment-with-the-decree-obligatory|whether contentment with the decree is obligatory or recommended]], and distinguishes contentment with calamities, contentment with what God commands, and contentment with forbidden things. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 40} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 41} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 42} He holds that the perfection of contentment is praise, and names two contemplations that call for praise in hardship, the first being the servant's knowledge that God deserves praise in Himself, for He made beautiful everything He created. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 43}

## The rest of the treatise
According to the volume's table of contents the treatise goes on to: innovation being dearer to Iblis than sin; truthfulness and sincerity, with hypocrisy as the opposite of sincerity; the division of God's words, command, will, permission and decree into the cosmic and the religious; the three kinds of people who have marvels, by what they use them for; the four kinds of people in worship and seeking help; then a long section on love of God and His Messenger as the root of every work, on the two Suras of sincerity and the two kinds of tawhid, and on worship as containing the utmost love and humility; then a section on fear and hope and how they return to love; then the [[group/jahmiyya|Jahmiyya]]'s denial of love on both sides; then the difference between lawful and innovated [[concept/sama|audition (sama')]]. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 770} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 771} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 772} The treatise also discusses annihilation in love, the two kinds of union and of indwelling, and the Malamatiyya. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 772}

## Its place
The treatise covers the subjects of the Sufi books on "stations and states", but builds each station on the texts of the Qur'an and Sunna and on the rule that the works of the heart are obligatory on the common people and the elite alike; it reads them from a Sunni Hanbali position that criticises what it sees as error in the words of some shaykhs while keeping the standing of their upright masters. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 6} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 18}
