## Summary
*Kitab al-I'tibar fi bayan al-nasikh wal-mansukh min al-athar* is a book by Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Musa al-Hazimi al-Hamadhani (d. 584 AH) ([[person/al-hazimi-muhammad|his biography]]). It deals with which hadiths and reports were abrogated and which remain in force. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 5}

## Introduction
The author opens by praising the subject. He calls it a deep field in which minds turn, and says that anyone who thinks it is easy has not looked closely at how the Companions differed on rulings transmitted from the Prophet. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 5}

He then gathers statements that treat this knowledge as a condition of legal reasoning. He cites a report that al-Zuhri said jurists could not identify which hadith was abrogated, and that al-Shafi'i was the first to take it up in full and arrange it into chapters. He also reports that Ahmad ibn Hanbal's circle did not distinguish the general from the specific or the abrogating from the abrogated until they sat with al-Shafi'i. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 6}

Further reports follow, all carried by chains the author gives. Hudhayfa is cited as saying that only someone who knows the abrogating and the abrogated may issue a fatwa. Ali is cited as telling a storyteller that he was ruined and would ruin others if he did not know the difference. Ibn Abbas is cited as rebuking a preacher on the same point. The introduction closes with the hadith of al-Miqdam ibn Ma'dikarib on the Book and its like with it, which frames the discussion that follows. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 7}

## What volume one covers
Before the main subject, volume one sets out what abrogation is, with its consequences and related matters. The author holds that knowledge of transmitted reports is a pillar of independent legal reasoning. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 7}

## A note on the source
The chains and statements in this record are those the author gives and reports from others. They have not been re-checked against other books. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 8}
