## Summary
*Kitab al-I'tibar fi bayan al-nasikh wal-mansukh min al-athar* is a book by Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Musa al-Hazimi al-Hamadhani (d. 584 AH) ([[person/al-hazimi-muhammad|his biography]]). It gathers the hadiths and reports that the author holds were abrogated by others, and arranges them under the headings of fiqh. Its introduction sets out the concept of [[concept/naskh|abrogation]] before the chapters begin. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 5}

## Introduction
The author opens by praising the subject. He calls it a deep field in which minds turn, and says that anyone who thinks it is easy has not looked closely at how the Companions differed on rulings transmitted from the Prophet. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 5}

He then gathers statements that treat this knowledge as a condition of legal reasoning. He cites a report that al-Zuhri said jurists could not identify which hadith was abrogated, and that al-Shafi'i was the first to take it up in full and arrange it into chapters. He also reports that Ahmad ibn Hanbal's circle did not distinguish the general from the specific, or the abrogating from the abrogated, until they sat with al-Shafi'i. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 6}

Further reports follow, all carried by chains the author gives. Hudhayfa is cited as saying that only someone who knows the abrogating and the abrogated may issue a fatwa. Ali is cited as telling a storyteller that he was ruined and would ruin others if he did not know the difference. Ibn Abbas is cited as rebuking a storyteller on the same point. The introduction closes with the hadith of al-Miqdam ibn Ma'dikarib about the Book and its like with it. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 7}

## Structure of the book
After the introduction the book runs through the headings of fiqh in order. Its chapter titles on the scan show this sequence:

- **Purification** (about scan pp. 26–64): water, wudu, touching the penis, touching what fire has touched, renewing wudu for each prayer, and hides of carrion. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 26}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 49}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 55}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 57}
- **Prayer** (about scan pp. 65–133): facing the qibla, turning the head in prayer, repeating the iqama, speaking in prayer, passing in front of a worshipper, pictures, hand placement, the basmala aloud or silently, bowing, qunut, the dawn prayer, the late-comer, following the imam, and prostration of forgetfulness. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 65}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 111}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 116}
- **Funerals** (about scan pp. 122–134): standing for a bier, the number of takbirs, prayer over hypocrites and over a debtor, visiting graves, and asking forgiveness for dead polytheists. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 122}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 133}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 134}
- **Fasting** (about scan pp. 136–148): the Ashura fast, the one who is janaba in Ramadan, cupping while fasting, fasting and breaking the fast on a journey, and suhur after dawn. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 136}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 145}
- **Pilgrimage, sacrifice and food** (about scan pp. 149–166): perfume for the pilgrim, conditions in the pilgrimage, sacrifice after three days, the fara' and atira, the flesh of domestic donkeys and horses, and usury. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 156}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 162}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 166}
- **Earnings, marriage, divorce and conduct** (about scan pp. 177–184): a cupper's earnings, temporary marriage, beating wives, and triple divorce with return. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 179}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 182}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 184}
- **Penalties and retribution** (about scan pp. 194–209): retaliation before the wound heals, mutilation, killing a drunkard, flogging a married person before stoning, fornication, and the duty of emigration. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 194}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 202}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 209}
- **Struggle and conduct in war** (about scan pp. 212–228): calling to Islam before fighting, killing women and children, fighting in the sacred months, seeking help from polytheists, spoils, and the pledge of women. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 212}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 218}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 227}
- **Dress, ornament and miscellaneous conduct** (about scan pp. 233–258): silk brocade and gold rings, hanging pictured curtains, killing dogs and snakes, charms, letting the hair fall loose, entering the bath, combining two dates, and saying "what God wills and you will". {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 233}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 241}, {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 258}

## Method
Under each heading the author gives the report with its full chain, then passes a judgement on the chain and the text, for example that a hadith is sound and naming its compiler, or noting that a report is strange. In the chapter on visiting graves he gives both the prohibition and the permission, grades the report of Buraydah as sound and attributes it to Muslim, and states that visiting graves is permitted for men by the agreement of scholars, while the report on women is handled separately. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 133}

In the opening chapter, on abrogation in the Sunna as it happened, he gives reports that the Prophet's hadiths abrogate one another as the Qur'an does, and notes that one of the chains comes through a narrator known for unusual reports. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 26}

## Editions
The book was printed in Aleppo in 1346 AH (1927 CE) by Muhammad Raghib al-Tabbakh al-Halabi, who corrected it and paid for the printing at his own press. The publisher's note says that before this printing only two books on abrogation had been published, one of them a very short work by Ibn al-Jawzi. {cite:itibar-hazimi-aleppo-1346 p. 2}

## A note on the source
The chains and statements in this record are those the author gives and reports from others. They have not been re-checked against other books. Page numbers are scan pages, because the printed folios are not legible throughout the OCR. {cite:itibar-hazimi-1359 p. 258}
