## Summary
The *Amali* ("Dictations") were dictated by [[person/ibn-al-shajari|al-Sharif Abu al-Sa'adat Hibat Allah ibn 'Ali Ibn al-Shajari]], the Baghdad grammarian (450–542 AH), in eighty-four sessions. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 2} {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 189} Hajji Khalifa describes the work as covering five branches of literature in eight volumes and dates its author's death to 572 AH. That date differs from the biographical works his editor follows, which give 542 AH. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 597} {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 16}

## Contents
- It is chiefly on syntax and morphology but also takes up lexicon, literature, rhetoric, prosody, history, reports and geography. For this reason 'Abd al-Qadir al-Baghdadi counted it among his sources on grammar. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 9} {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 189}
- His editor Mahmud al-Tanahi counts it among books of Qur'anic studies, for its attention to the exegesis, syntax, ellipses and difficulties of the Qur'an. He notes that Ibn al-Shajari supports every rule with one or more verses, cites the seven readings and explains some irregular ones. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 7} {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 9}
- Its poetic witnesses exceed 1,100 lines, not counting repeats. He extended citation to the "modern" poets such as Di'bil, Abu Tammam, al-Buhturi and Ibn al-Mu'tazz, and gave special attention to [[person/al-mutanabbi|al-Mutanabbi]]: he names him in eighty-five places and devotes the last session to his merits. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 7} {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 10}
- It preserves quotations from lost books, such as al-Akhfash's al-Awsat, Ibn al-Anbari's al-Wasit and some works of Abu 'Ali al-Farisi. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 7}
- The editor judges Ibn al-Shajari a pure Basran in grammar, and holds that many of the Basran arguments in Abu al-Barakat al-Anbari's al-Insaf are taken from him. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 8}

## Dated sessions
This Amali, says its editor, is unique among works of its kind in dating its sessions, though not consistently. Session 8 is dated Saturday, the first of Jumada al-Ula 524 AH, and sessions followed on Saturdays. Session 22 falls on a Tuesday in Jumada al-Ula 526 AH, after a pause of about two years. Dictation then stopped for some ten years between session 31 (Tuesday 23 Shawwal 526 AH), which ends the first part in the manuscripts, and session 32 (Saturday 8 Rabi' al-Awwal 536 AH). The editor asks whether dictation really stopped, or whether the students who took it down made errors in the dates. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 189}

## This edition
Edited by Mahmud Muhammad al-Tanahi. His work began as a doctoral thesis at the Faculty of Dar al-'Ulum, Cairo University, defended on 18 Shawwal 1398 AH/20 September 1978 under 'Abd Allah Darwish, covering 49 sessions with a study on "Ibn al-Shajari and his grammatical views". He then completed it, and Maktabat al-Khanji in Cairo published it in three volumes, first edition 1413 AH/1992 CE. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 2} The base manuscript is a fine naskh copy made in 581 AH and collated with its exemplar. It ends with hearing notes of scholars of the sixth and seventh centuries, and its margins carry notes, some by the author's student Abu al-Yumn al-Kindi. {cite:amali-ibn-al-shajari-tanahi-khanji-1992 p. 9}
