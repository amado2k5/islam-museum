## Overview
Futuh Misr wa-l-Maghrib is the book of the Egyptian historian [[person/ibn-abd-al-hakam|Ibn Abd al-Hakam]] (d. 257 AH). Its editor, Abd al-Mun'im Amir, counts it among the most important early Arabic sources for the conquest of Egypt. It goes beyond Egypt, following the conquests of North Africa under [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] and the governors and commanders after him. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ج}

## Contents
The author opens with reports of Egypt before the conquest as religious storytelling imagined it and as the storytellers told it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ج} He then gives the Prophet's letter to [[person/al-muqawqis|al-Muqawqis]], the conquest and siege of Egypt and the fall of Alexandria, Amr's move to [[place/al-fustat|al-Fustat]] and its plots, Amr's dealings with Umar, and the conquests of the Maghrib and al-Andalus with the reports of the governors of Ifriqiya. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 52} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 133} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 262}

## Method
Ibn Abd al-Hakam gives full chains of transmission in the manner of the hadith scholars, without discussing his sources. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ك} Most of his transmitters are Egyptian, among them Uthman ibn Salih, Ibn Lahi'a, [[person/layth-ibn-sad|al-Layth ibn Sa'd]] and [[person/yazid-ibn-abi-habib|Yazid ibn Abi Habib]]. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ك} The chapter on the plots seldom gives chains, because he drew it from what was common knowledge in al-Fustat and from what he had seen. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ل}

## Editions
The editor relied on the Paris, British Museum and Leiden manuscripts and on the edition of the orientalist Torrey, which was based on the British Museum manuscript. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 intro p. ص}
