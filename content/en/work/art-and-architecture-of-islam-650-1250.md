## Summary

*The Art and Architecture of Islam, 650–1250* is a book by Richard Ettinghausen and Oleg Grabar in The Pelican History of Art, published by Penguin Books in 1987. {cite:cca-art-and-architecture-of-islam-650-1250} The book has three parts: the Caliphate, the breakdown of the Caliphate, and the eleventh to thirteenth centuries. {cite:cca-art-and-architecture-of-islam-650-1250}

Part I opens with the rise of Islam and the artistic climate of the period, then Umayyad art (650–750): the Dome of the Rock, the early congregational mosques, the mosques of al-Walid, secular buildings and the decorative arts; then the Abbasid tradition (750–950): architecture and its decoration, the decorative arts, the art of the book and painting. {cite:cca-art-and-architecture-of-islam-650-1250} Part II covers the Muslim West (750–1260), the Fatimids in North Africa and Egypt (910–1171), and Iran and Central Asia (800–1025); Part III returns to architecture in Iran, Central Asia and other regions. {cite:cca-art-and-architecture-of-islam-650-1250}

## Publication and editions

Penguin Books published it in Harmondsworth and New York in 1987, in 448 illustrated pages, with a bibliography (pages 387–428) and an index. {cite:cca-art-and-architecture-of-islam-650-1250} Open Library gives its ISBN as 0140560599. {cite:openlibrary-art-and-architecture-of-islam-650-1250} It is continued in the same series by Sheila S. Blair and Jonathan M. Bloom's *The Art and Architecture of Islam 1250–1800* (Yale University Press, 1994). {cite:cca-art-and-architecture-of-islam-650-1250}
