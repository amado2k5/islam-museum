## Summary

A question on importing a medical book already sold through an agent, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that selling it is permissible if there is no exclusive agency, but working to break an existing exclusive agency is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3837}

## The question

The questioner said a medical book is sold on the market through an agent, but the publisher told him the book has no exclusive agent; he asked whether he may sell it, and whether that counts as selling over another's sale or as fair competition. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3837}

## The answer

The Department answered that sale rests on mutual consent, and the question has two cases. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3837} In the first, the supplier and the first importer have no exclusive-agency agreement, so there is no objection to contracting with the same supplier to import and sell its goods, even at a different price from the first importer. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3837} In the second, the first importer holds an exclusive agency and the second persuades the supplier to cancel that contract and sign with him; this is forbidden because it sows enmity among Muslims, and it cited the hadith forbidding people to sell over one another's sales. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3837} It concluded that since there is no exclusive-agency agreement, as in the question, the second importer may bring in the book and sell it at the price he sees fit. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3837}
