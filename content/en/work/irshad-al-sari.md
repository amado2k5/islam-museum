## Summary
*Irshad al-Sari li-Sharh Sahih al-Bukhari* ("Guiding the Night-Traveller: a Commentary on Sahih al-Bukhari") is the commentary of [[person/al-qastallani-shihab-al-din|Shihab al-Din Ahmad b. Muhammad al-Qastallani]] on [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]]. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 5} It was printed several times at the Amiriyya press in Bulaq, among them the 6th edition of 1304 AH and the 7th of 1323 AH in ten volumes. {cite:irshad-sari-bulaq-1304 p. 1} {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 3}

## Why and how he wrote it
Al-Qastallani opens with praise of the science of the Sunna, holding that al-Bukhari's book was preferred to all others after the Book of God. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 5} He says he had long thought of writing a commentary "blended into it", marking the original off from the commentary in red ink and the variant transmissions in other colours, so the reader would grasp the meaning quickly — clarifying its difficulties, establishing its transmissions and explaining its rare words. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 5} He then says he held back for a long time out of awe for the book, until his youth had passed, and then began. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 5-6}

He describes the work as a distillation of the great scholars of the field, built on study of the books written on it, consultation of teachers and discussion with experts; he did not avoid repetition where clarity needed it, aiming to benefit specialists and ordinary readers. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 6} He acknowledges that the light of his commentary flowed "from Fath al-Bari" — [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar's]] [[work/fath-al-bari|Fath al-Bari]] — and calls himself one who borrows from the gleams of his predecessors. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 6}

## The introduction and its chapters
He prefaced the commentary with an introduction in five chapters. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 6} {cite:irshad-sari-bulaq-1304 p. 2}
- **First, on the merit of the hadith scholars:** he gives the hadith "May God brighten the face of one who heard my words…" with its routes, and "This knowledge will be carried in every generation by its upright ones", noting that Ibn 'Adi cited the latter by many routes, all weak, as al-Daraqutni, Abu Nu'aym and Ibn 'Abd al-Barr stated, and that al-'Ala'i held it strengthened to the level of hasan by its multiple routes. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 6-7}
- **Second, on the first to compile hadith:** he holds that the first to order hadith written down was [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|'Umar b. 'Abd al-'Aziz]], that the first compilers were al-Rabi' b. Subayh and Sa'id b. Abi 'Aruba, followed by Malik, Ibn Jurayj, al-Awza'i, al-Thawri and Hammad b. Salama, then the authors of musnads and of books by chapter; see [[event/beginning-of-tadwin|the beginning of codification]]. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 9-10}
- **Third, on hadith terminology:** he names the first authors on it — al-Ramahurmuzi in *al-Muhaddith al-Fasil*, then al-Hakim, Abu Nu'aym, al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, Qadi 'Iyad, and then [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]], whose book everyone took up — and then lists and defines the categories of hadith, such as the mass-transmitted, citing al-Nawawi that "whoever lies about me deliberately" is reported from two hundred Companions. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 10}
- **Fourth, on al-Bukhari and his Sahih:** on [[concept/shart-al-shaykhayn|his conditions]], the preference for his book over Muslim's and the answers to its critics — a chapter he says he summarised from the introduction to Fath al-Bari. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 21}
- **Fifth, on al-Bukhari's lineage, birth and upbringing.** {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 32}

## Defending the two Sahihs
In the fourth chapter al-Qastallani relays from Ibn Hajar that it is no fault of the two Shaykhs to have transmitted from narrators who were criticised, since a Sahih author's use of a narrator implies that narrator's uprightness in his view, and criticism is accepted only when its grounds are explained; he quotes Abu al-Hasan al-Maqdisi saying of any narrator used in the Sahih, "he has crossed the bridge". {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22-23} He divides the hadiths criticised in them into six kinds, the first being reports whose chain varies by adding or dropping a narrator, with the example of the hadith of the two graves criticised by [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] and Ibn Hajar's answer that however the chain turns, it turns on a trustworthy man. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 23}
