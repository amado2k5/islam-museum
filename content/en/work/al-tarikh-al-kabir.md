## Summary
*Al-Tarikh al-Kabir* ("The Great History") is [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]]'s book on hadith transmitters; its editor al-Dabbasi says that in it al-Bukhari tried to cover the transmitters from the Companions down to the generation of his own teachers. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 8} {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 9} He counts it among the greatest and richest works of hadith criticism, though demanding and at times hard to grasp even for specialists. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 13}

## Its composition
The editor quotes from *Tarikh Baghdad* al-Bukhari's own words: when I reached eighteen I wrote the book of the rulings of the Companions and Successors, then I wrote the *Tarikh* in Medina by the Prophet's grave, writing it on moonlit nights; hardly a name in it lacks a story I know, but I disliked making the book long. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 10} Musabbih ibn Sa'id reported him saying: if some of my teachers were raised from the dead, they would not understand how I composed the *Tarikh*; and that he composed it three times. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 16} The editor explains that each time al-Bukhari added, removed and corrected, so the recensions of his students differ. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 16}

## Method
- It is alphabetical, with transmitters ordered by their fathers' names, except that those named Muhammad come before all others for the honour of the name. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 13}
- It opens with the Prophet's biography and lineage, after an introduction on the merit of Quraysh, some of his qualities and the length of his mission, notes how the Hijri calendar began under Umar, then the Prophet's death; under each letter it gives the Companions, then the Successors, then those after them. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 13}
- Its entries range from a single line to more than ten pages, as for Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Ka'b ibn Malik. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 14}
- It usually gives the transmitter's name, father, grandfather, kunya and nisba, some teachers and students, and a sample of his narrations. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 14}
- Al-Bukhari avoids harsh terms of criticism: most often he says "there is a doubt about him" (*fihi nazar*) or "they kept silent about him", and at his harshest "denounced in hadith". {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 14} The editor quotes al-Dhahabi reporting al-Bukhari's words "I hope to meet God without His holding me to account for backbiting anyone", and al-Dhahabi's note that al-Bukhari said: when I say "there is a doubt about his hadith", he is accused and flimsy. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 14} {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 15}
- The editor estimates that fewer than two thousand transmitters receive a verdict of criticism or vindication while more than twelve thousand are passed over in silence, that more than four thousand hadiths are critically discussed, and that his terms of criticism and vindication exceed a hundred. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 15}

Mughultay, as the editor quotes him, said that al-Bukhari's habit is to mention something, raise an objection, then mention something else, so that whoever does not reflect on it can hardly understand it. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 13}

## Its standing among scholars
- When [[person/ishaq-ibn-rahwayh|Ishaq ibn Rahawayh]] first saw the *Tarikh*, he took it in to the governor [[person/abd-allah-ibn-tahir|Abd Allah ibn Tahir]] and said: O governor, shall I not show you magic? {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 9}
- Ibn Uqda said: a man who had written thirty thousand hadiths could still not do without Muhammad ibn Isma'il's *Tarikh*. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 9}
- Abu Ahmad al-Hakim said: a book without precedent, which no later author of history or names could do without; and that [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]]'s *al-Asma' wa-l-Kuna* was copied from it as closely as one arrow-feather matches another. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 9} {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 10}
- [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|Al-Hakim al-Naysaburi]] said, in the chapter on knowing traditionists' names: al-Bukhari spared us this field. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 9}

**Its relation to Ibn Abi Hatim's al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil.** The editor quotes al-Khatib: Abu Ahmad al-Hakim saw the people of Rayy reading *al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil* to [[person/ibn-abi-hatim-al-razi|Ibn Abi Hatim]] and said: I see you reading al-Bukhari's *Tarikh* to your shaykh word for word, having attributed it to Abu Zur'a and Abu Hatim. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 11} The editor suggests Abu Ahmad perhaps heard only some short entries, and quotes al-Mu'allimi that, though Ibn Abi Hatim mostly followed al-Bukhari's order and many of his entries, he stands out for stating the critics' verdicts explicitly, adding entries and points and correcting al-Bukhari's errors. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 11} {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 12}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Abu Ahmad al-Hakim**: Ibn Abi Hatim's *al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil* is for the most part al-Bukhari's book attributed to Abu Zur'a and Abu Hatim. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 11}
- **Sunni: al-Mu'allimi as quoted by the editor**: Ibn Abi Hatim followed al-Bukhari, but added the critics' explicit verdicts, further entries and points, and corrected errors. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 12}
:::

## Its recensions
The editor lists among those who transmitted the book from al-Bukhari: al-Fadl ibn al-Abbas al-Sa'igh; Abu Ahmad Muhammad ibn Sulayman ibn Faris al-Dallal of Nishapur, who read it to him as far as the "chapter of Fudayl" and received the rest by licence, and from whom Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Mustamli transmitted it in Baghdad, [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]] writing it all down from him; Muhammad ibn Sahl al-Basri, who heard it in Basra in 246; Musabbih ibn Sa'id; Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Fadl al-Fasawi; and Abd al-Rahman ibn Isma'il al-Farisi. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 17} {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 18} {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 19} He quotes al-Mu'allimi's view that Ibn Abi Hatim's objections concern al-Bukhari's first version, that al-Khatib's copy goes back to Ibn Faris's recension, and that the earlier printed text goes back to Ibn Sahl's, the latest. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 21} {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 22} Among those who supplemented or corrected it he names al-Daraqutni, al-Khatib in *al-Mudih*, and Ibn Abi Hatim in *Bayan Khata' al-Bukhari fi Tarikhih*. {cite:tarikh-kabir-dabbasi-v1 p. 19}
