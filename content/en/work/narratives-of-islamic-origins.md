## Summary

*Narratives of Islamic Origins: The Beginnings of Islamic Historical Writing* is a book by Fred M. Donner, published by the Darwin Press in Princeton in 1998 in 358 pages as number 14 of the series Studies in Late Antiquity and Early Islam; a second printing followed in 1999. {cite:openlibrary-narratives-of-islamic-origins-1998} {cite:archive-org-narratives-of-islamic-origins-scan} Donner opens his introduction by noting that contemporary documentary evidence for Islamic origins, such as inscriptions, coins, archaeology and papyri, is almost non-existent, so the received picture rests on written accounts from the Islamic tradition itself, whose documentary value historians sharply dispute. {cite:archive-org-narratives-of-islamic-origins-scan}

## Publication and editions

The introduction surveys historians' approaches to these sources and critiques the sceptical approach. Part I treats the intellectual context of early historical writing, beginning with the date of the Qur'anic text; Part II treats the emergence of that writing through themes of prophecy, community, hegemony and leadership, then chronological schemes and formal features such as the hadith format. {cite:archive-org-narratives-of-islamic-origins-scan}
