# The Message of the Qur'an

## Summary
*The Message of the Qur'an* is [[person/muhammad-asad|Muhammad Asad]]'s English rendering of the meanings of the [[work/quran|Qur'an]] with his commentary in the notes, published complete in Gibraltar in 1980 by Dar al-Andalus. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-2020} {cite:wikipedia-message-of-the-quran-en} It is one of the most influential modern Qur'an translations and has itself been translated into other languages. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition}

## The translator
Asad was born Leopold Weiss in 1900 in Austria-Hungary, became Muslim in 1926, lived as a journalist, diplomat and scholar, worked on his translation in Switzerland and then Morocco, and died in 1992; he is buried in Granada. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-2020} He stayed at the court of King Abd al-Aziz between 1927 and 1932 and kept the support of the Saudi authorities, King Faisal above all, after he moved back to the West in 1959. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition}

## From the first edition to the complete one
Asad began his first draft around 1961 in Geneva. A partial first edition of the first nine suras appeared in 1964 from the Islamic Center of Geneva under the auspices of the [[group/muslim-world-league|Muslim World League]], printed in The Hague, as the first of a planned three or four volumes. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition} Its structure differs little from the complete version, but much of the wording moved from a more grammatical rendering to a more explanatory one, and some notes were changed. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition} The complete translation followed in 1980, in about 1,200 pages. {cite:wikipedia-message-of-the-quran-en}

## What it contains
In his introduction Asad says he set out to make the Qur'an's meaning comprehensible further than earlier translators had, and declares his debt to the Egyptian reformer [[person/muhammad-abduh|Muhammad Abduh]]. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-2020} His footnotes show his use of classical tafsir; in them he explains his translation choices and clarifies passages of the text. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-2020} In places he offers a rational explanation of verses the tradition read as miracles, a line with parallels in contemporary Egyptian commentaries influenced by Abduh. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition}

## Reception
:::perspectives
- **Sunni (the Saudi Permanent Committee for Scholarly Research and Ifta)**: in fatwas issued in 1979 and confirmed after the complete translation appeared, the Committee found grave errors of creed in it, among them its wording on the raising of Jesus, and stated that the League's Consulting Board in Mecca had prohibited its printing and distribution. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition}
- **Academic reading**: Sohaib Saeed called Asad's notes a model of transparency few have followed, noted that many Muslims see the translation as rationalist and modernist, and said that separating the notes from the text in digital formats takes much of its value away; Mykhaylo Yakubovych wrote that it remains one of the most influential translations worldwide and is viewed by many as a kind of tafsir. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-2020} {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition}
:::

Yakubovych reports unconfirmed accounts that copies of the first edition were destroyed after objections in Saudi Arabia, and states that the translation has never been printed there. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition}

## Today
*The Message of the Qur'an* has been translated into German, Spanish, Bosnian, Turkish and Swedish, among others, and has Wikipedia articles in Arabic, English, Hindi and Turkish. {cite:gloqur-asad-message-1964-edition} {cite:wikipedia-message-of-the-quran-en}
