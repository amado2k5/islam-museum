## Summary
*'Umdat al-Qari fi Sharh al-Bukhari* is the commentary of [[person/badr-al-din-al-ayni|Badr al-Din Abu Muhammad Mahmud b. Ahmad al-'Ayni]] on [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]]; he gives it this title in his preface. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 22}

## Why he wrote it
Al-'Ayni says he long held back from commenting on al-Bukhari out of awe; travelling to the northern lands before 800 AH with the book, he gathered from some of his teachers rare points for drawing out its treasures; back in Egypt he commented on al-Tahawi's *Ma'ani al-Athar*, then began a commentary on Abu Dawud's Sunan that obstacles kept him from finishing; finally, under the Mu'ayyadi state, three things moved him to comment on al-Bukhari: to show that hidden things lie in corners, to make known in gratitude the knowledge God had given him, and the repeated urging of some companions. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 20-21} He asks the reader to judge it fairly and mend any flaw he finds. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 22}

## His chains
Al-'Ayni transmits the book by two routes, the first from [[person/al-iraqi-zayn-al-din|Zayn al-Din 'Abd al-Rahim al-'Iraqi]], with whom he heard it in the Citadel mosque, read aloud by Shihab al-Din Ahmad b. Muhammad al-Ashmuni the Hanafi. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 22-23}

## Method
The biography printed with this edition describes it as twenty-one volumes by the author's division, and says that it:
- completes the wording of a hadith where al-Bukhari abridged it, and locates its other occurrences in the book;
- notes differences among the book's transmitters, and treats narrators and the spelling of names and lineages fully;
- explains language, grammar, rhetoric, and the finer points of chains, high and low;
- derives rulings at length, documents the hadiths on disputed questions according to the schools of the great cities, and lists "questions and answers";
- puts all this under separate headings, and seldom sends the reader elsewhere. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 15-16}

## Date and relation to Fath al-Bari
The biography says al-'Ayni began his commentary in 821 and finished it in 847 AH, five years after [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] finished [[work/fath-al-bari|Fath al-Bari]]; that he read Ibn Hajar's commentary part by part through Burhan al-Din b. Khidr, one of Ibn Hajar's companions, and criticised it in places; and that where the two agree in their quotations it is because they used the same sources. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 16} It adds that after seeing the 'Umda, Ibn Hajar began [[work/intiqad-al-itirad|Intiqad al-I'tirad]], writing out the objections with blank space beneath them, and died without answering most of them. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 16}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: the biography printed with the edition**: holds that, to the fair-minded, al-'Ayni has the larger share in discharging "the debt of commenting on al-Bukhari" that Ibn Khaldun reported from his teachers, the difference between the two being that between the full moon (badr) and the shooting star (shihab). {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 16}
- **Sunni: al-Sakhawi, as the biography reports**: inclines to the view that his teacher Ibn Hajar is the one who discharged it. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 16}
- **Sunni: the author of Kashf al-Zunun, as the biography reports**: judged that the two commentaries discharged it equally. {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 16}
:::

## The edition in the library
Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya, Beirut, 1st ed. 1421 AH/2001 CE, edited by 'Abd Allah Mahmud Muhammad 'Umar, with books, chapters and hadiths numbered after the Concordance; vol. 1 runs from the Book of the Beginning of Revelation to the Book of Faith (hadiths 1–58). {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p. 1-2}
