## Summary

A question from a car dealer about selling a car to someone who borrowed its price from an interest-based bank, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that there is no objection if the loan was taken before the sale, but it is forbidden if the loan is granted only on papers the seller provides to complete the sale. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3923}

## The question

A car-showroom owner asked whether he may sell a car to a person who took its price as a loan from an interest-based bank. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3923}

## The answer

The Department answered that a seller is not required to ask the buyer where his money comes from, because the sin of the forbidden attaches to the one who commits it, not to the money itself, unless the money itself is stolen. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3923} It said there is no objection to selling goods to someone who has already obtained an interest-based loan, because the loan was concluded with no link to the sale contract and did not depend on it, so the sale gives no help to usury. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3923} It said it is forbidden to sell to someone who wants to buy with an interest-based loan that is granted only once the seller supplies the papers needed to complete the sale, because that helps him do what is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3923} It concluded that if the buyer has already obtained the loan and brings the money, there is no objection to selling to him and the sin of usury rests on whoever dealt with the bank; if he has not yet obtained it and asks for papers to help him get it, helping him is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3923}
