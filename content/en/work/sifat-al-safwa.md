## Summary
*Sifat al-Safwa* ("The Description of the Elite") is a book by Abu al-Faraj [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] (510–597 AH) on those who acted on their knowledge and renounced the world, written as a corrected replacement for the *Hilyat al-Awliya'* of [[person/abu-nuaym-al-asbahani-ahmad|Abu Nu'aym al-Isfahani]] {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.25} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.28}. Ibn al-Jawzi says that his book covers more than a thousand people — men well over eight hundred and women well over two hundred — whereas the men of the *Hilya* whose conditions are described do not reach six hundred, and he doubts it mentions even twenty women {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.30}.

## His critique of the Hilya
Ibn al-Jawzi relates that a student complained to him of the *Hilya*'s length — its chains of hadith and its many words of little benefit — and asked him to abridge it; he answered that the book holds much that is good but "was muddied by things and missed things" {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.25}. The ten faults he lists are: naming people without reporting anything of their conduct, as in the entry on Hisham ibn Hassan filled with what he narrates from al-Hasan; including material unsuited to the book, such as parts of the Qur'an commentary of Mujahid and Ikrima, or a piece of the Torah in the entry on Ka'b al-Ahbar; repeating reports; lengthy Prophetic hadiths; fabricated hadiths left unmarked; cold rhymed prose in defining Sufism; ascribing Sufism to the great early figures; idle sayings; Sufi practices that the Shari'a forbids; and a confused order {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.25-28} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.23-24}. What it missed are three things: it did not mention the Prophet, "the master of the ascetics"; it omitted many people known for devotion; and it named few devout women, although mentioning them spurs on negligent men — Sufyan al-Thawri, he notes, benefited from Rabi'a and learned manners from her words {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.28}.

## Method and arrangement
Ibn al-Jawzi says he included those famed for renunciation and devotion rather than those famed for learning alone, opened with the Prophet as "the elite of creation", and left out the earlier prophets because what is transmitted of them is isolated reports unsuited to the book's purpose of healing hearts {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.28-29}. Then come the Companions by rank of merit, then the women Companions, then the Successors and later generations by city {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.29}. He took Baghdad as the centre of his circle, but began with Medina as the abode of the Hijra, then Mecca, al-Ta'if and Yemen, returned to Baghdad, went down to al-Mada'in, Wasit, Basra and on through Persia, Khurasan and Transoxiana to Nakhshab, then turned west through Ukbara, Mosul, Syria, Egypt, Alexandria, the Maghrib, the mountains, the coasts and the deserts {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.29}. He ended with those met on the road, at Arafa and in the circumambulation, then those whose names and places are unknown, then little girls who spoke like grown devout women, then the devout among the jinn {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.30}. He states that he selects the best of what is reported, and that he allowed himself to include some Sufis from whom both objectionable and fine sayings are reported, choosing the fine {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.30}.

## Tartusi's edition
Khalid Mustafa Tartusi edited the book for Dar al-Kitab al-Arabi, Beirut, in 1433/2012: he traced the verses and hadiths, explained rare words, gave the sources of each biography, numbered the entries, used the *Hilya* as the basis for correcting the text, and compiled indexes of places, persons, sources and subjects {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.3} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.7-8}. He says that entries for which he found no biography outside this book are barely five per cent {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.7}. He prefaced it with an essay on role models and with biographies of Ibn al-Jawzi and of Abu Nu'aym and his book {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.8} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.23}. The edition has 1,031 numbered entries {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.1007}.
