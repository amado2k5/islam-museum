## Summary
*Kitab al-Sunna* by Abu Abd al-Rahman [[person/abdullah-ibn-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Abd Allah, son of Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal]], al-Shaybani (213–290 AH) is a book of creed that gathers, with chains, the sayings of his father [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] and other hadith imams and reports from the Companions and Followers. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 5} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 11} Its editor Adil Al Hamdan says that Abd Allah wrote it in answer to the [[group/jahmiyya|Jahmiyya]] when sects such as the Jahmiyya, the Kharijites, the Murji'a, the Mu'tazila and the Rafida had spread in his time, and counts it among the finest books on the Sunna and creed. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 7} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 10}

## The author
The editor's biography gives his name as Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal ibn Hilal ibn Asad al-Shaybani, his kunya Abu Abd al-Rahman, born in 213 and died in 290 AH aged seventy-seven, and says its aim is to show his standing in knowledge and the Sunna, not as a mere transmitter. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 11} Among its anecdotes is al-Khallal's report from Harb al-Kirmani that Ahmad came out to read to him, when Abd Allah, then a boy, came to remind his father of his promise to read to him and wept; Ahmad went in, read to his son, and came out again. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 11}

## Title and attribution
Both manuscripts are headed "Kitab al-Sunna", the name used by Ma'mar ibn Ahmad al-Isbahani, by [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] in the *Dar'*, *Minhaj al-Sunna* and *al-Hamawiyya*, and by al-Dhahabi in *al-'Arsh*; al-Sijzi and al-Dhahabi in the *Siyar* call it *al-Radd 'ala al-Jahmiyya*. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 15} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 16} The editor holds that its attribution to Abd Allah is established by mass transmission, since scholars quote it with their chains to him, and that the chain of the Zahiriyya copy is given by Abu Isma'il al-Harawi in *Dhamm al-Kalam*. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 16}

## Its chapters
The book opens with what the scholars said about the Jahmiyya, declaring them unbelievers and praying behind them, beginning with Ahmad's saying that whoever says the Qur'an is created is in our view an unbeliever, since the Qur'an is part of God's knowledge. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 25} Then come chapters on the Qur'an, on those who say "my utterance of the Qur'an is created", on what he memorised about Jahm and Bishr al-Marisi, and on Ahmad's view of [[group/al-waqifa|the Waqifa]]. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 92} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 98} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 117} Then the chapter "What I memorised from my father and other shaykhs about Abu Hanifa". {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 120} Then chapters on the attributes, on faith and the answer to the [[group/murjia|Murji'a]], on what the scholars said about Amr ibn Ubayd and destiny, on the imams, the caliphate and the ranking among them, and on the punishment and trial of the grave. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 303} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 453} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 637} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 665} The editor's indexes include one of the sects and schools mentioned, such as the Kharijites and the Qadariyya, by report number. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 806} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 807}

### The chapter on Abu Hanifa
The editor notes that the Umm al-Qura copy "has had the chapter on Abu Hanifa removed", which he counts a defect. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 15} He prefaces the chapter with a note gathering reports holding that exposing an innovator or a weak transmitter is counsel, not backbiting – among them Qatada's words to Asim al-Ahwal that when a man introduces an innovation it should be mentioned so that people beware of it, and Ahmad's reply to Abu Turab al-Nakhshabi, "This is counsel, not backbiting." {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 120} The chapter is criticism of Abu Hanifa by hadith scholars as Abd Allah gathered it; this page reports it without a verdict, and the Hanafi answers to it have not yet been added.

## Manuscripts and editions
The editor used two manuscripts: the Zahiriyya Library copy in Damascus, no. 1074, copied by al-Anjab ibn Makki al-Tibi in 644 AH in 112 folios with audition notes, which he took as the base as the oldest and most complete; and the Umm al-Qura University copy, no. 1497, copied by Abd Allah ibn Muhammad al-Hanbali al-Nabulusi at the Hanbali college in Nablus in 783 AH. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 14} To fix the text he drew on books of the Sunna that give the book's reports through the author, such as al-Khallal's *Sunna*, Ibn Batta's *Ibana* and al-Ajurri's *Shari'a*. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 15} He faults earlier editions for omitting several reports and for grading many reports of the early generations weak or objectionable. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 17} The present copy is the fourth edition, Dar al-Lu'lu'a, Beirut, 1441 AH / 2020, the first book of the editor's series on the books of the Sunna and creed. {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 4} {cite:sunna-abdullah-ahmad-hamdan-1441 p. 7}
