## Summary

A question from a food trader about merchants who withhold goods because they know prices will soon rise, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that hoarding is forbidden, and more gravely so for staple foods. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3856}

## The question

The questioner said he trades in food and that some merchants withhold goods because they know prices will rise soon; he asked for the ruling on hoarding. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3856}

## The answer

The Department answered that the sharia forbids hoarding because it harms people, defining it as holding what was bought at a time of high prices to sell it dearer when need is acute. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3856} It said hoarding covers all goods people need if withholding them harms people, the Maliki view and that of [[person/abu-yusuf-al-qadi|Abu Yusuf]] among the Hanafis, quoting [[person/ibn-rushd-al-jadd|Ibn Rushd al-Jadd]]'s al-Bayan wa-l-Tahsil and [[person/ala-al-din-al-kasani|al-Kasani]]'s Bada'i al-Sana'i. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3856} It said hoarding staple foods is more gravely forbidden, quoting [[person/zakariyya-al-ansari|Zakariyya al-Ansari]]'s Asna al-Matalib that it is forbidden because it straitens people, [[person/ibn-hajar-al-haytami|Ibn Hajar al-Haytami]]'s al-Zawajir counting it among the major sins, and his report that [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]] extended staples to what supports them, such as meat and fruit. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3856} It concluded that merchants may not withhold basic foodstuffs or other goods bought at a time of high prices in order to sell them dearer when need for them is acute. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3856}
