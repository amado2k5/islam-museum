## Summary

Al-Umda fi al-Ahkam fi Ma'alim al-Halal wa-l-Haram an Khayr al-Anam mimma Ittafaqa alayhi al-Shaykhan al-Bukhari wa-Muslim, known as Umdat al-Ahkam ("The Mainstay of Legal Rulings"), is a book by al-Hafiz [[person/abd-al-ghani-al-maqdisi|Abd al-Ghani ibn Abd al-Wahid al-Maqdisi]] (d. 600 AH) gathering legal hadiths from [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]] and [[work/sahih-muslim|Sahih Muslim]]. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.1} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.7} In his preface he says that a fellow scholar asked him to abridge a set of legal hadiths on which the two imams al-Bukhari and Muslim agree, and that he answered, hoping it would be of use. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.39} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.40}

## Its name and place among the author's works

In his list of Abd al-Ghani's works, al-Zuhayri gives "al-Ahkam al-Sughra" (the Smaller Rulings), saying it is this book, followed by "al-Ahkam al-Kubra" (the Larger Rulings). {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-zuhayri-1419 p.17} In the notes of al-Faryabi's edition, the commentators are quoted comparing its wording with the author's "larger Umda". {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.244}

## Arrangement and content

It is arranged by the chapters of law, from the Book of Purity to the Book of Manumission; the last hadith in al-Faryabi's edition, number 423, is that of a man who promised freedom to his slave at his death. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.41} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.244} Shaykh Abd Allah al-Sa'd says in his foreword that its hadiths are of the highest degree of soundness, since all are from the two Sahihs or one of them, that the author chose them from the legal hadiths to cover all chapters of law, and that scholars therefore gave it much attention in memorising, studying, teaching and commenting; he suggests memorising it after al-Nawawi's Forty. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.5} The editor says he records the objections of al-Zarkashi, Ibn al-Mulaqqin and Ibn Hajar to hadiths for which the author was criticised, and compares the Umda's wording with that of the two Sahihs and of al-Humaydi's al-Jam' bayna al-Sahihayn, which he considers one of the author's main sources. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.8}

## Its transmission

The editor finds no disagreement over its attribution and lists several lines of transmission from the author, among them that of al-Fakhr Ibn al-Bukhari (d. 690 AH) and those of Ahmad ibn Abd al-Da'im al-Maqdisi (d. 668 AH) and Abd al-Hadi ibn Abd al-Karim al-Qaysi (d. 671 AH), both of whom heard it from the author; he quotes Ibn Hajar criticising those who transmitted the Umda through al-Fakhr by licence when the higher, heard transmission of Ibn Abd al-Da'im and al-Qaysi was available. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.13} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.14}

## Its manuscripts

Al-Faryabi edited the book from seven manuscripts. His base is Princeton (Garrett Yahuda) no. 637, copied by Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Shafi'i in 733 AH and collated with a copy corrected against the grammarian Ibn Malik in difficult places, bearing a reading certificate from the slopes of Mount Qasiyun in 657 AH. Others include Paris (Bibliothèque nationale) no. 726, copied by Muhammad ibn Ilyas ibn Uthman in 742 AH from an original bearing a certificate of hearing from the author himself in four sessions ending on Friday 22 Shawwal 591 AH, corrected in his hand; Paris no. 727, bearing the hearing and handwriting of [[person/murtada-al-zabidi|Muhammad Murtada al-Zabidi]], author of Taj al-Arus, dated 1189 AH, with his connected chain to the author; and the oldest, Princeton no. 1377, copied in 713 AH. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.15} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.16} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.17} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.18} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.19} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.20}

## Its commentaries

The editor lists thirty-four works serving it, among them Ihkam al-Ahkam by [[person/ibn-daqiq-al-id|Ibn Daqiq al-Id]] (d. 702 AH), al-Udda by Ibn al-Attar (d. 724 AH), Riyad al-Afham by al-Fakihani (d. 734 AH), al-Nukat ala al-Umda by [[person/badr-al-din-al-zarkashi|al-Zarkashi]] (d. 794 AH), [[work/al-ilam-fawaid-umdat-al-ahkam|al-I'lam bi-Fawa'id Umdat al-Ahkam]] by [[person/ibn-al-mulaqqin|Ibn al-Mulaqqin]] (d. 804 AH), Kashf al-Litham by [[person/al-saffarini|al-Saffarini]] (d. 1188 AH), al-San'ani's gloss al-Udda on Ihkam al-Ahkam, and among modern works Tanbih al-Afham by [[person/muhammad-ibn-salih-al-uthaymin|Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymin]] (d. 1421 AH) and Taysir al-Allam by Abd Allah Al Bassam; among works on its narrators is Ibn al-Mulaqqin's al-Udda fi Ma'rifat Rijal al-Umda. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.21} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.22} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.23}

## Editions in the museum

The museum's archive holds four printings: al-Faryabi's (Dar Tayba, Riyadh, 1423/2002) from seven manuscripts, used here; {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.1} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.3} Samir al-Zuhayri's (Maktabat al-Ma'arif, Riyadh, 1419 AH); {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-zuhayri-1419 p.1} {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-zuhayri-1419 p.3} and two other printings not used here. Al-Faryabi considers that the book's printings are not free of errors and distortions. {cite:umdat-al-ahkam-faryabi-2002 p.8}
