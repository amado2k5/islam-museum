## The book and its question

Cengiz Aydin describes [[person/abu-kamil-shuja-ibn-aslam|Abu Kamil]]'s Rare Things in Calculation as among the earliest surviving Arabic witnesses in its subject and notes its absence from the Fihrist. This qualified description is not global priority for every numerical problem. MacTutor places the work in indeterminate analysis and reports Sesiano's arguments about procedures absent from the known books of the Arithmetica {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work2} {cite:abu-kamil-mactutor rare-things}.

The distinctive concern is [[invention/abu-kamil-integer-enumeration|multiple integer answers]], rather than stopping at one successful example. Aydin contrasts a search for integers with Diophantus's attention to rational solutions. That comparison does not erase the diversity of ancient mathematics or make every Diophantine result a copy of these problems {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work2}.

## Shape of the problems

Aydin reduces the problems to two linear equations with three to five unknowns: a total quantity and total cost, using countable things. Birds, swords, lances, men, women, and children can supply substitutable quantities; the constraints provide the common structure. Those words alone do not establish historical prices or an actual purchase {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work2}.

He describes verbal unknown names such as thing, dinar, fals, and ring. Numbers were mainly written in words, with exceptions for tables and some figures. Modern museum symbols are therefore not attributed to the original pages. A monetary word can function as an unknown name; not every variable necessarily denotes literal money {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work2-notation}.

## Precedents and disagreement

Aydin records Indian precedents associated with Aryabhata and similar problems, but distinguishes knowledge of a question from knowledge of a procedure. He mentions a general scholarly opinion favoring independent solutions and an anonymous commentator's report. We retain the attributed disagreement: resemblance does not prove transmission, and different presentation does not disprove every contact {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work2}.

This work is not silently merged with the [[work/abu-kamil-al-jabr|algebra book]] or surveying book. Aydin also distinguishes a Book of Birds listed in the Fihrist with unknown contents. Birds in an example do not establish identical works, even when a modern note or abstract uses another title {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work1-work2-and-conclusion}.

## Witnesses and translations

The entry speaks of one surviving manuscript in one passage but later lists Leiden and Paris witnesses. We preserve that discrepancy in its wording without deciding a new manuscript census; the originals were not collated here. It reports a Hebrew translation associated with Finzi, including Sacerdote's proposed Spanish intermediary and Suter's support, and an attributed Latin witness {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work2-witnesses-translations}.

These are reception reports, not proof that exactly the same table and totals traveled in every witness. Approximate lifetime dates also do not establish an exact date of composition. No invented day, Hijri conversion, or room of composition is supplied {cite:abu-kamil-mactutor biography} {cite:tdv-abu-kamil work2}.
