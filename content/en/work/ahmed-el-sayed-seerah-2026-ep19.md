## Summary

The lecture opens with a short biography of Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib taken from a reference work on the Badr veterans, then begins the emigration to Abyssinia: Ibn Ishaq's account, the lessons the speaker draws, his own reading of why the emigrants stayed away until Khaybar, the names of the first group and of the later group, and tafsir reports that link verses to the emigration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### Biography of Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib

- He relates Hamza's profile from a reference book on the Badr participants: his byname, his lineage, his mother from the Zuhra clan, and his birth two or four years before the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Hamza was the Prophet's uncle, cousin and foster brother through the nurse Thuwayba, and that this kinship decided the later case of Hamza's daughter, who was not lawful for the Prophet to marry. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He notes two views on Hamza's conversion: the second year of the mission, which he calls early and weakly supported, and the fifth or sixth year, given by Ibn Sa'd, when Islam was strengthened by Hamza and Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He holds that the later date fits the context better, since people took pride in Hamza's conversion once the call was public, and he stresses that the secret phase was never absolutely secret. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Hamza emigrated to Medina and led an early raiding party of thirty riders near the coast against a Qurayshi caravan with Abu Jahl, without fighting; he notes the dispute over whether the first banner went to Hamza or to Ubayda ibn al-Harith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that at Badr Hamza wore an ostrich-feather mark and took part in the duels in which Utba, Shayba and others were killed, with Ali and Ubayda also taking part; Ubayda was wounded and died on the way back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that at Uhud Hamza killed Siba' ibn Abd al-Uzza in single combat, and that Wahshi later came as a Muslim; he says the Prophet neither refused his conversion nor punished him but asked him to keep out of sight, which shows how deeply Hamza's death affected him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates, citing Ibn Hajar's al-Isaba, that Hamza was titled Lion of God and chief of the martyrs and is said to have killed over thirty men at Uhud, and that the Ansar women mourned him first before their own dead after Sa'd ibn Mu'adh brought them to the Prophet's door; he says this report needs further checking. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He holds that the abundance of names such as Abd al-Uzza among Quraysh, replaced after the Hijra by names like Muhammad and Abd al-Rahman, is historical evidence of how much the mission changed Arab society. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Ibn Ishaq's report of the Prophet advising emigration

- He recalls that the previous session covered torture, the story of Khabbab, and tafsir of passages from Surat al-Kahf cited by Ibn Ishaq, and he praises Ibn Ishaq's formulation of narrative context. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's account that, seeing his companions afflicted and unable to protect them, the Prophet advised them to go to Abyssinia, whose king wrongs no one, and that they left to protect their faith in what was the first emigration in Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He notes that Ibn Ishaq gives this report without a chain, that the editor traces it to al-Bayhaqi's Dala'il, and that al-Iraqi called its chain good; he calls the Dala'il the most important chained source for the biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### Lessons drawn from the emigration

- He draws five lessons: God's earth is wide, so believers oppressed in one land can find another; the prophetic path carries costs such as opposing one's people, torture and leaving home; leaders must find solutions for the weak among their followers; unbelievers differ, and a just one deserves fair treatment; and the leader bears responsibility for those who sacrificed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He holds, as a reflection he labels uncertain, that if the whole earth ever closed to believers a change in God's ordering would follow. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that the Negus was not yet Muslim when the Companions came, and that it is said he converted later through Ja'far, noting that the Prophet had not yet addressed kings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He contrasts Abu Talib, who heard the message and the Qur'an, protected the call without accepting it, and was treated with closeness and esteem by the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He argues that the Prophet's sending companions to a just non-Muslim king shows that fairness and political justice were weighed beside doctrine, and he criticizes present-day rigid people who object to this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### Why the emigrants returned only at Khaybar

- He holds that the emigrants fled for their faith but also to preserve Islam in a safe place, and he presents this as his own tentative judgement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He argues that they came back only around Khaybar, in the seventh year, after the Hudaybiyya truce made Medina secure, since Badr, Uhud and the Trench had not removed the threat of total destruction. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He supports the reading by noting that many emigrants were noble Qurayshis rather than tortured slaves, citing Uthman and Abu Hudhayfa, and that Ja'far went although his father Abu Talib protected the family. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He concludes that the emigration had three aims: escape from torture, preservation of the faith elsewhere, and a freer place of worship even for those not tortured. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### The first group of emigrants

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's list of the first ten men and four women, with each man's clan: Uthman ibn Affan with his wife Ruqayya, Abu Hudhayfa ibn Utba with Sahla bint Suhayl, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Mus'ab ibn Umayr, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Abu Salama with Umm Salama, Uthman ibn Maz'un, Amir ibn Rabi'a with Layla, Abu Sabra ibn Abi Ruhm, and Suhayl ibn Bayda. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham says Uthman ibn Maz'un led them, and notes a report that Uthman and Ruqayya were the first emigrants since Lot. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He says the first and second emigrations were separated by a few months and that the first took place in the fifth year of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Sahla bore Muhammad ibn Abi Hudhayfa in Abyssinia, and notes that her father Suhayl ibn Amr was a harsh opponent who converted late. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### Size of the emigration and what it meant

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's total of eighty-three men who reached Abyssinia, eighty-two if Ammar ibn Yasir is not counted, apart from children, and he is unsure whether women are included. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He holds that the figure shows most early Muslims emigrated, and notes a view that this explains the report of about forty men with Umar at Dar al-Arqam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He holds that the emigration eased the pressure on the companions and was also a trial for the Prophet, who remained with very few. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### The seven sons of al-Harith ibn Qays

- He presents seven brothers of the Sahm clan, sons of al-Harith, as little-known early Muslims who emigrated to Abyssinia in the second emigration, citing Ibn Sa'd. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Abu Qays and Abd Allah, a poet, died as martyrs at Yamama; al-Sa'ib was wounded at Ta'if and killed at Fihl in the year 13; al-Hajjaj and Sa'id died at Yarmuk. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Tamim or Bishr is a disputed name, that Ibn Ishaq said Bishr and Ibn Sa'd preferred Tamim, and that nothing is told of Ma'bad beyond his name. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He presents them as an example of forgotten early emigrants who ended their lives as martyrs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### The second group: Hashim, Umayya and allies

- He relates that Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, with his wife Asma' bint Umays, was the only man of Banu Hashim among the emigrants, and that he will treat Ja'far at length later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates the Umayyad names in Ibn Ishaq's list: Uthman with Ruqayya, Amr ibn Sa'id ibn al-As with his wife Fatima, Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As with his wife, and their allies Abd Allah ibn Jahsh and Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh with Umm Habiba, Qays ibn Abd Allah, and Mu'ayqib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Khalid's daughter Umm Khalid later married al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, who had children by her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### Khalid ibn Sa'id and the family of Sa'id ibn al-As

- He relates that Khalid ibn Sa'id is said to have converted before those who converted through Abu Bakr, right after Khadija, Ali and Abu Bakr, and that al-Iraqi's versified biography mentions this view. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that the Prophet appointed Khalid over parts of Yemen, that Abu Bakr later gave him a banner for Syria and took it back, that he served beside Shurahbil ibn Hasana, and that he was killed at Marj al-Suffar south of Damascus. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that their father Sa'id ibn al-As, called Abu Uhayha, was a leading and harsh opponent of the Muslims who withdrew to Ta'if. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that a descendant Sa'id ibn al-As ibn Sa'id, born around the first year of the Hijra, was made governor of Kufa by Uthman, took part in conquests in the east, was governor of Medina under Mu'awiya, and was praised in a line of the poet al-Farazdaq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He says the Prophet gave command to several members of this family and that he will treat them later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}

### Verses linked to the emigration in tafsir

- He relates a report that a verse of Surat al-Nisa' about the one who leaves home as an emigrant and dies on the way was revealed about a man who died en route to Abyssinia, whom he names uncertainly as a relative of al-Zubayr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir doubted this because the sura is Medinan, and that the speaker suggests the verse may simply cover this case in its general sense. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He relates that al-Tabari, through Qatada, explains a verse of Surat al-Nahl on those who emigrated after being wronged as referring to Companions who reached Abyssinia and were later settled in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
- He says this is not exhaustive, since scholars may include the emigrants in the general sense of other verses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep19}
