## Summary
Ma'alim al-Sunan is the commentary of [[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|Abu Sulayman al-Khattabi]] of Bust (319–388 AH) on the [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan of Abu Dawud]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 1} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13} Dar Ibn Hazm printed it in 1997 in the footnotes of the Sunan: the editor 'Izzat 'Ubayd al-Da''as distributed its remarks under the reports they explain and closed each excerpt with the word "(Khattabi)". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 7-8} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 17}

## What the commentary does
Volume 1 shows al-Khattabi combining four tasks:
- **Explaining rare words**: he glosses and vocalises unusual terms — for example, that *baraz* with *a* is a name for open ground used as a euphemism for relieving oneself, and that reading it with *i* is a mistake, since *biraz* is the verbal noun of single combat; and that *damath* is soft ground into which urine sinks. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 17-18}
- **Drawing out law and manners**: he derives rulings and etiquette from a report, such as moving away from people and screening oneself when relieving oneself, and that the prohibition on cleaning oneself with the right hand is, for most scholars, a prohibition of good manners and propriety. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 17} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 19}
- **Relating the schools**: he gives the views of the Companions and the jurists and who held each, as on [[question/wudu-after-touching-the-private-part|ablution after touching the private part]], where he names the Companions who required it and those who did not, and the views of [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]], [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]], [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] and [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 95}
- **Reconciling and weighing reports**: on [[question/facing-the-qibla-when-relieving-oneself|facing the qibla when relieving oneself]] he sets out the view of Abu Ayyub and Sufyan and that of Ibn 'Umar, Malik and al-Shafi'i, then says that Ibn 'Umar's view is "preferable, because it reconciles the differing reports". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 21} On [[place/bir-budaa|the well of Buda'a]] he explains that its report does not contradict that of [[concept/qullatayn|the two qullas]], since its water reached two qullas, and "the particular rules over the general and explains it, and does not abrogate it". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 45-46}

## Al-Khattabi's own chains
Al-Khattabi does not keep to what is in the Sunan; at times he cites with his own chains. He relates the report of [[person/safwan-ibn-assal|Safwan ibn 'Assal]] on the time limit for wiping from his teachers [[person/ibn-al-arabi-ahmad|Ibn al-A'rabi]] and Isma'il ibn Muhammad al-Saffar, from Sa'dan ibn Nasr, from Ibn 'Uyayna, and then explains its wording. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 84-85} He relates the addition "by the qullas of Hajar" through 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn Jurayj. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 44} On the prostrations of forgetfulness he gives Abu Sa'id al-Khudri's report through Sulayman ibn Bilal from Zayd ibn Aslam, to answer those who weakened it because Malik transmitted it without the Companion. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 438}

## Notable positions in volume 1
- **Witr and the festival prayer**: from the report of the man from Najd ("five prayers") he argues that witr is not obligatory, that the obligation of night prayer was abrogated, and that the festival prayer is supererogatory for most scholars; he relates from Abu Sa'id al-Istakhri that it is a communal obligation. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 197-198}
- **Swearing by fathers**: he takes the words "he has succeeded, by his father" as a turn of Arab speech used for emphasis without intent to swear, or as said before the prohibition, and cites Arabic verse. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 197-198}
- **Wiping over leather socks**: he rejects a report that "a group of the Shi'a" ascribe to 'Ali, that wiping was abrogated by the verse of al-Ma'ida, saying it is not authentically from him. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 82}
- **Speaking in prayer by mistake**: he rejects the claim that the report of Dhu al-Yadayn is abrogated, because speech in prayer was forbidden in Mecca while this happened in Medina, its narrator Abu Hurayra being a late convert. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 428}
- **Prostrations of forgetfulness**: he sets out the positions of the Hanafis ("people of opinion"), Malik, Ahmad and al-Shafi'i and the reports each built on. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 437}

## His school in the commentary
His biographer notes that al-Khattabi learned law from [[person/al-qaffal-al-shashi|al-Qaffal al-Shashi]], a Shafi'i master. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 13} This shows in the commentary: he often prefers what agrees with al-Shafi'i — the limit of two qullas for water, or that fewer than three stones do not suffice even if the place is clean — while relating the other views. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 43}
