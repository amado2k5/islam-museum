## Summary

A question from a young man whose family stop him from praying in congregation at the mosque, in which Jordan's General Iftaa Department answered that he must obey them if they forbid it for a clear benefit, but need not if they act from obstinacy or imagined fears. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3824}

## The question

The questioner said he loves praying in congregation at the mosque, but his family stop him, citing illnesses and other reasons, and asked whether he may disobey them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3824}

## The answer

The Department explained that congregational prayer is established, needs at least two people, and its merit can be had at home or at work; it is a communal obligation on resident men for the obligatory prayers and is better for men in the mosque, and it listed the general excuses for missing it, such as heavy rain or severe cold, and personal ones such as illness or fear for one's life or property. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3824} It said that if the parents forbid it for a clear benefit, to protect their son from a harm they think likely, such as illness during an epidemic or kidnapping or assault in times of unrest, or for their own benefit, such as old age, illness or wanting his company, he must obey them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3824} If they act from obstinacy or imagined fears, obedience is not required of a sane, mature son, since the obedience owed is in what clearly benefits them; it cited a reply of Ibn Hajar al-Haytami in al-Fatawa al-Fiqhiyya al-Kubra about a father who kept his mature son from seeking knowledge for fear of bad company. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3824} It closed by saying that the son should be gentle with his parents in every case. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3824}
