## Summary
Asbab nuzul al-Qur'an ("The Occasions of the Revelation of the Qur'an"), known as Asbab al-nuzul, is the book of Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ahmad [[person/al-wahidi|al-Wahidi]] of Nishapur, the Qur'an commentator who died in 468 AH. Hajji Khalifa calls it the most famous work on the occasions of revelation, and reports that the first to write on the subject was Ali ibn al-Madini, and that Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Hajar and al-Suyuti also wrote on it. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 381-382}

## Why he wrote it, in his preface
Al-Wahidi opens by saying that God sent down the Qur'an "on occasions of differing causes", "in separate instalments", and relates from al-Hasan that between its first and last parts were eighteen years, eight in Mecca and ten in Medina, and from al-Sha'bi: twenty years or about twenty. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.9} He then says the sciences of the Qur'an are abundant, that he had already compiled collections covering most of them, but that interest in them had turned away, "so we were brought to teaching beginners in the sciences of the Book what occasions there were for what was revealed in it", because a verse's interpretation and purpose cannot be known "without knowing its story and the account of its revelation". {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.10}

He lays down that "it is not lawful to speak of the occasions of the Book's revelation except by transmission and hearing from those who witnessed the revelation and knew the occasions", and recalls the caution of the early generations, as when Abida al-Salmani told a man asking about a verse: "fear God and speak rightly; those who knew on what the Qur'an was revealed are gone"; whereas today, he says, everyone "invents something and fabricates falsehood", which is what moved him to dictate this book gathering the occasions. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.10} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.11} He prefaces it with an account of the beginnings of revelation and how the Qur'an first came down, then gives the occasion of every verse for which one is reported. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.11}

## Title and transmission
Its editor Mahir Yasin Fahl holds that the title is Asbab nuzul al-Qur'an, "Asbab al-nuzul" being a loose usage, since the author names it so in his preface and conclusion and so it appears on the title leaf of the base manuscript. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.7} He says earlier editions were "patched together from several transmissions", so he used the Baghdad Awqaf Library manuscript (2369) in the transmission of Badr al-Islam Abu Nasr Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Arghiyani, which he considers the best because it is a complete hearing from al-Wahidi, and which Ibn Hajar used in al-Ujab fi bayan al-asbab. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.6} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.7}

## Method
The editor explains that al-Wahidi arranged the occasions by the order of the suras and then of the verses, with the sura's name as a heading and each verse introduced by "His saying, Mighty and Exalted: ..."; under it he lists what is reported of its occasion, sometimes giving the differing reports or most of them, sometimes noting "they differed on the occasion of its revelation", without committing himself to the soundest or preferred report. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.52} He draws most on the Companions, especially Ibn Abbas, naming the transmitter of each when two reports from him conflict; then on Successors such as Mujahid, Ikrima, al-Dahhak, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ata', al-Zuhri and Qatada; on later collectors such as al-Kalbi and the two Suddis; and on Ibn Ishaq's Maghazi, sometimes with his chain and sometimes without. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.53} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.54} What he ascribes to "the commentators" in general, or leaves unattributed, comes mostly from his teacher [[person/abu-ishaq-al-thalabi|al-Tha'labi]], and the editor quotes Ibn Hajar's criticism in al-Ujab of this way of merging reports into "a single narrative". {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.54}

The editor says that al-Wahidi "was not a specialist in hadith", so weak and very weak chains entered the book, and he examined and graded them all. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.7} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.8} Kamal Basyuni Zaghlul likewise notes weak chains in his annotations, such as that of the hadith "beware of narrating from me except what you know", which includes Abd al-A'la ibn Amir al-Tha'labi. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.11}

## Its place in the field
Fahl calls it the most comprehensive book in the field, covering the occasions of more than 500 verses, and quotes al-Suyuti: "among the best known books on the occasions is al-Wahidi's, for all its shortcomings". {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.49} He lists earlier attempts, including a "Detailing of the causes of revelation" from Maymun ibn Mihran (d. 117), Ali ibn al-Madini's Asbab al-nuzul (d. 234) and Abd al-Rahman ibn Futays's al-Qisas wa-l-asbab (d. 402), and later works including two abridgements of al-Wahidi's book, Asbab nuzul al-ay by al-Malik al-Salih al-Artuqi (d. 619) and Aja'ib al-nuqul by al-Ja'bari (d. 732), then Ibn Hajar's al-Ujab and al-Suyuti's Lubab al-nuqul. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.49} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.50} {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.51}

## Editions
Kamal Basyuni Zaghlul based his edition (Beirut 1991) on al-Sayyid Ahmad Saqr's, noting that it contains additions, in square brackets, to the edition printed in Cairo in 1316 AH. {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-ilmiyya-1991 p.3} Mahir Yasin Fahl published al-Arghiyani's transmission (Riyadh 2005). {cite:asbab-nuzul-wahidi-maiman-2005 p.2}
