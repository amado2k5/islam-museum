## Summary

A question about a school canteen manager taking supplier bonuses, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that a school canteen is run as a partnership and profit-sharing arrangement (mudaraba) and its manager is an agent holding it in trust, so he may not take supplier bonuses or gifts for himself and must return anything he has taken to the canteen. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3688}

## The question

The questioner asked about exploiting a school canteen for personal gain. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3688}

## The answer

The Department answered that school canteens are, in legal terms, a partnership and profit-sharing (mudaraba) contract: the contributors pool the capital and then appoint someone to run the canteen and its affairs, with the profits set as known shares in the contract. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3688} It said the managing partner is like an agent whom the owners of the capital have chosen to trade on their behalf, so he holds the property in trust and must safeguard the interests of those who appointed him, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] whose meaning is that each of you is a shepherd responsible for his flock, and quoting al-Shihab [[person/shihab-al-din-al-ramli|al-Ramli]]'s gloss on [[work/asna-al-matalib|Asna al-Matalib]] (2/257) that a partner is like an agent and must act in the interest of the others. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3688} It said the canteen manager may not take suppliers' extra allowances (bonuses) or accept gifts for his own benefit; otherwise he betrays his trust and consumes what is unlawful, and his sin is greater if he raised prices for the pupils, citing [[passage/quran-4-29|Surat al-Nisa' 4:29]]. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3688} It concluded that whoever has taken any of this must return it to the canteen, and the manager must be trustworthy and act in good faith toward those he works for so that he is free of liability. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3688}
