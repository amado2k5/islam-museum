## Summary

The lecturer opens a new recurring segment on the Badr veterans, starting with Abu Sabra ibn Abi Ruhm, then continues Ibn Ishaq's account of the Quraysh delegations to Abu Talib, the offer to exchange Umara ibn al-Walid for the Prophet, and Abu Talib's rallying of Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib. He adds commentary on the Quraysh leaders, on why only Abu Lahab is named in the Qur'an, and on al-Suhayli's linguistic notes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### New segments: Badr veterans and Sahih al-Bukhari

- He announces that each session will now open with one biography of a Badr veteran, taken in order from a reference work on the Badr participants by Sulayman al-Awda, which he praises for documenting each entry scholarly rather than narratively. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says he is still undecided about also reading the virtues and expeditions chapters of Sahih al-Bukhari alongside the Ibn Hisham lessons, and will decide by the next session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### Biography of Abu Sabra ibn Abi Ruhm

- He relates that Abu Sabra ibn Abi Ruhm was a son of Barra bint Abd al-Muttalib, an aunt of the Prophet, and a maternal brother of Abu Salama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says Abu Sabra was among the earliest Muslims and took part in both migrations to Abyssinia, with his wife Umm Kulthum bint Suhayl joining the second, and that Ibn Hajar mentions him only for the second. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates that Abu Sabra settled in Medina with a host from the Ansar and was paired in brotherhood with Salama ibn Salama ibn Waqsh, citing Ibn Sa'd. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He reports that his presence at Badr is undisputed, noting that Ibn Abd al-Barr, Ibn Uqba, Ibn Ishaq, al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar, Ibn Sa'd, al-Dhahabi and Ibn Mandah list him, and that he fought in the later battles too. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates that, according to al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar and Ibn Sa'd, Abu Sabra went back to Mecca after the Prophet's death, which the Muslims disliked, while his sons denied it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He adds that Abu Sabra took part in the expedition of Abu Salama to Qatan, narrated one report with a weak chain, and died in the caliphate of Uthman. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### The first blood shed in Islam (Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas)

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's story that Muslims used to pray in the mountain glens of Mecca out of sight, and that a group of polytheists came upon Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and others there, mocked them and fought, and Sa'd wounded one of them with a camel's jawbone, the first blood shed in Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He holds that Ibn Ishaq's placement of the story is uncertain: it may belong to the secret phase or early in the open phase, its date is not given, and Ibn Ishaq reports it without a chain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says the open phase itself had degrees of concealment, that the conversion of Umar marked a step toward greater openness, and that earlier attempts at openness, such as Abdullah ibn Mas'ud's public recitation for which he was beaten, preceded it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### Quraysh's reaction and Abu Talib's protection; Ibn Ishaq as the base of the sira

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's statement that Quraysh did not reject the Prophet's open call until he spoke against their gods, after which they turned against him, while Abu Talib shielded him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says that, having reviewed many old and modern sira books, he found nearly all rely on Ibn Ishaq for the stories of Quraysh and Abu Talib, so that Ibn Ishaq's work remains the principal foundation of the sira whatever criticism it receives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He announces that he will postpone al-Suhayli's linguistic discussion of a word in this passage until the narrative is finished. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### The Quraysh nobles who approached Abu Talib

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq names the men who went to Abu Talib, among them Utba and Shayba sons of Rabi'a, Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, Abu al-Bakhtari, al-Aswad ibn al-Muttalib, Abu Jahl, al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, Nubayh and Munabbih sons of al-Hajjaj, and al-As ibn Wa'il. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says these names deserve to be known because many Qur'anic passages about mockers and opponents concern them, and because the Prophet named some of them in supplication against them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He notes that some stayed opposed through to the conquest of Mecca, such as Abu Sufyan, who led the war after Badr until he accepted Islam, and Suhayl ibn Amr, the Quraysh envoy at al-Hudaybiyya, while most of the others were killed at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He explains the kinship of Abu Jahl and the family of al-Mughira: Abu Jahl was a son of Hisham ibn al-Mughira, so Khalid ibn al-Walid was his cousin, and Khalid's mother was a sister of the Prophet's wife Maymuna. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### The first approach and tribal diplomacy

- He relates that the nobles told Abu Talib that his nephew had insulted their gods and religion and called their forefathers astray, and asked him either to restrain him or to leave them free to deal with him; Abu Talib answered gently and they left. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says Ibn Ishaq counts three visits of Quraysh to Abu Talib and that the sequence is the natural conduct of a tribal society, where the elders first address the chief of the offender's clan, as Abu Talib was then chief of Banu Hashim after Abd al-Muttalib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### Was Quraysh really not hostile at first? Abu Lahab and the Qur'an

- He holds that Ibn Ishaq's statement that Quraysh did not oppose the open call until the gods were criticised needs qualification, because Abu Lahab confronted the Prophet publicly after the call on al-Safa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He observes that Abu Jahl, Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, al-Walid, Utba and Shayba were harsher in some respects, yet Abu Lahab is the only opponent named in the Qur'an, and offers as a possibility that he was the first and closest relative to deny the Prophet in public, which made his act especially grave. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says al-Walid ibn al-Mughira was a leading chief whom Abu Jahl feared would convert, and that most commentators apply a passage of Surat al-Muddaththir to him; he notes that some also apply a passage of Surat Maryam to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates that Quraysh brought Umara ibn al-Walid, described as the finest young man in Mecca, to Abu Talib and offered to exchange him for Muhammad, and remarks how Khalid, al-Walid's son, later became a sword of God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### Umm Jamil and the Prophet's concealment

- He relates, from al-Bayhaqi's Dala'il citing a chain through Asma bint Abi Bakr, that after the revelation about Abu Lahab his wife Umm Jamil came to the Kaaba carrying a stone and chanting a rhyme against the Prophet using a reversed form of his name. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says the report tells that Abu Bakr feared she would see the Prophet, and the Prophet said she would not; she saw only Abu Bakr and left claiming she was the daughter of Quraysh's chief. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates the Prophet's remark in another report that God turns Quraysh's insults away from him, since they revile a name that is not his own, and calls this a remarkable lesson. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He adds that, if the report is sound, it shows Surat al-Isra was revealed in parts, as does another report he mentioned last time, and he singles out al-Bayhaqi's Dala'il as the most comprehensive musnad collection of reports related to the sira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### Why the open call began after a secret phase

- He holds that the open phase did not end all concealment: the message became public, but the number, places and movements of Muslims stayed partly hidden. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He offers as an explanation that the secret phase built a solid base inside the community over about three years, so the open call would find people who supported and protected it, and the Qur'an strengthened the Prophet and believers meanwhile. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says the severity of persecution afterwards led a large group, including Uthman and Ruqayya the Prophet's daughter, to migrate to Abyssinia within about two years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### God's providence: noble lineage and Abu Talib's protection

- He holds that the Prophet was sent from the heart of Quraysh's noble lineage as part of divine providence, citing the exchange between Heraclius and Abu Sufyan on prophets being sent among their people's nobility. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He contrasts the treatment of less protected believers such as Bilal, Sumayya, Ammar, Yasir and al-Miqdad, who were tortured harder, with that of those backed by lineage, and notes that Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a later feared for his family because he was not of Quraysh's core. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says that God also made Abu Talib, the noblest man of Banu Hashim and heir of Abd al-Muttalib, a protector, and relays the remark of some scholars that his remaining on his people's religion made Quraysh regard his protection more weightily. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The second visit and Abu Talib's appeal to the Prophet

- He relates that on the second visit Quraysh raised their tone, telling Abu Talib they would no longer tolerate insults to their forefathers and gods and would fight until one side perished. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates, through Ibn Ishaq and a transmitter Ya'qub ibn Utba, that Abu Talib asked his nephew to spare him a burden beyond his strength, and the Prophet, thinking his uncle might abandon him, replied that he would not abandon his mission even if given the sun and moon, then wept; Abu Talib called him back and pledged never to give him up. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says the commonly preferred wording of the Prophet's reply is about the sun and a flame being lit from it, notes that Ibn Hajar judged this report's chain good, and holds that the chain is not strong but the core event is accepted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### The offer to exchange Umara and Abu Talib's reply

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's account that Quraysh, once they knew Abu Talib would not abandon the Prophet, brought Umara ibn al-Walid, offered him as an adopted son with his intelligence and support, and asked Abu Talib to hand over the Prophet to be killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says Abu Talib rejected the offer as a bad bargain, asking whether they expected him to raise their son while giving up his own. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates that al-Mut'im ibn Adi told Abu Talib that his people had been fair, and Abu Talib answered that Mut'im had resolved to forsake him; he notes Mut'im was a polytheist who died before Badr and who later gave the Prophet protection, and that Abu Talib says 'forsake' because Mut'im was a near relative. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### Abu Talib's poem against the clans that abandoned him

- He relates that after the dispute grew into open hostility Abu Talib composed verses addressing those of Banu Abd Manaf and other clans who had forsaken him, among them al-Mut'im, singling out Abd Shams and Nawfal, and says Ibn Hisham omitted two verses as too harsh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He says Ibn Ishaq reports that each clan of Quraysh then set upon its own Muslims to torture them from their religion, while Abu Talib gathered Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, believer and unbeliever, to protect the Prophet, and all agreed except Abu Lahab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates the verses Abu Talib then recited praising his clan and the Prophet as the chosen of Quraysh, and says he regards Abu Talib as a poet, mentioning that his longer ode is the other poem covered in the lesson. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}

### Al-Suhayli's linguistic notes

- He relates al-Suhayli's explanation that the verb for sheltering with tenderness originally meant a bending of the back, and that the verb for blessing similarly derives from bending in compassion, and holds that al-Suhayli distinguishes it from supplication by the preposition used. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He relates al-Suhayli's glosses of other rare words in the passage, such as the one for the matter becoming difficult, tied to a camel's girth, and the one for evil spreading, and notes some verse readings in al-Suhayli differ from those in Ibn Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
- He ends by stressing the value of combining sources, here Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Hisham and al-Suhayli, and says the next session will continue from this point. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep13}
