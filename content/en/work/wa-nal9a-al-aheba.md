## Summary
"Wa Nalqa al-Ahibba" (And We Meet the Beloved) is a lecture series by Amr Khaled, recorded on video and held in the Assabile archive, with 79 lessons by the series page's own count. It opens with the Rightly Guided Caliphs and with Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, setting out his lineage, his names and titles, his conduct before and after Islam, and the presenter's view of his character. What follows is the speaker's own speech as the machine transcript of the recording renders it. Anything not traced to a source, or read from a line that is unclear in the transcript, is marked as needing a source.

:::perspectives
- **The speaker's view (opinion):** he holds that the key to Abu Bakr's personality is his love of the truth and his attachment to it, that truthfulness, mercy, wisdom and humility were among his most marked qualities, and that what set him ahead of others was not chiefly the number of his prayers and fasts but something that settled in his heart. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:31:30}
- **The sources he names:** he names books on Abu Bakr, among them a work by Ali al-Tantawi (the name is read from a garbled line) and al-Aqqad's "Abqariyyat al-Siddiq", together with "Usd al-Ghaba" and "al-Isaba fi tamyiz al-sahaba" as older standard references. These are the speaker's recommendations; the books were not checked for this entry. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:07:30}
:::

## Episode 1: the series and Abu Bakr al-Siddiq

- The episode announces that the series begins with the Rightly Guided Caliphs, the first of whom is Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, and the speaker calls it one of the hardest episodes he has given. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:05:30}
- The speaker asks what the miracle of Islam is. His answer is the Qur'an, not only for its eloquence but because whoever follows it and acts on it becomes a great figure in history. He gives [[person/sad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] as examples and presents Abu Bakr as another instance of this miracle. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:04:30}
- His name before Islam is given as Abdullah ibn Abi Quhafa, and his kunya is Abu Bakr. The speaker says his father's original name was Uthman and that his mother was Umm al-Khayr, who embraced Islam early in the house of al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:12:00} (needs_source: the mother's name is not given in the transcript, and the name Umm al-Khayr is read from a line that is only partly clear)
- He belonged to the Banu Taym of the Quraysh. The speaker says his lineage meets the Prophet's at the sixth forefather, and that the Banu Taym were responsible in the Jahiliyya for paying blood-money and for the sums due in such cases. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:13:00}
- His title "Atiq". The speaker says the title is reported as coming from the Prophet, in the sense of being freed from the Fire, and that some say he had the title before Islam for the beauty of his face or the goodness of his character. He explains that "atiq" means the noble, the fine and the old. The report he gives is not traced to a chain in the episode, and its wording is not reproduced here. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:11:00} (needs_source: the report is cited without a chain)
- Ages the speaker gives: born two years after the Year of the Elephant, or two and a half years according to a variant; began Islam at thirty-eight; migrated at fifty-one; held the caliphate for about two years and some months; died at sixty-three, on a Monday, the day the Prophet also died. These are the speaker's statements and arithmetic, not checked against the sources. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:15:30}
- Wives and children as the speaker lists them: Qutayla and Umm Rumman before Islam, Asma bint Umays after it, and Habiba bint Zayd. Among his children he names Abdullah, Asma, Abd al-Rahman, Aisha, Muhammad and Umm Kulthum, and gives Abdullah as his eldest. The names are read from overlapping lines of the transcript and need a source. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:19:00} (needs_source)
- Before Islam, as the speaker tells it, he never prostrated to an idol: his father brought him to his idols to prostrate, and he broke one with a stone. He never drank wine, and he explains this by saying that he guards his honour and keeps himself. He was the most knowledgeable of the Quraysh in genealogy, and the speaker gives an anecdote in which he introduces the Prophet to the tribes of Arabia by name. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:25:30}
- He was a friend of the Prophet before the mission. The speaker says he travelled with the Prophet and the Prophet's uncle Abu Talib on a journey to Syria, in which he places the meeting with the monk Bahira. This is a tradition the speaker gives without a source in the episode. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:27:30} (needs_source: the journey and the meeting are given without a source)
- The speaker opens with reports in which the Prophet describes Abu Bakr as the most truthful and the firmest in faith, and says that Abu Bakr surpassed others not by many prayers and fasts but by something that settled in his heart. He does not name the sources of these reports in the episode, and their wording is not reproduced here. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:28:00} (needs_source: the reports are cited without named sources)
- He describes Abu Bakr's appearance as thin, fair of face, with a dyed beard, a prominent forehead and a fine nose, and says that appearance reflects character. This is the speaker's own description, given without a cited report in the episode. {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-amr-khaled wa-nal9a-al-aheba/ep01 00:21:30} (needs_source: no report is named for the description)
