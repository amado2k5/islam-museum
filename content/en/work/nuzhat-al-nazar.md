## Summary
Nuzhat al-Nazar fi Tawdih Nukhbat al-Fikar fi Mustalah Ahl al-Athar is [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani]]'s (773–852 AH) commentary on his own [[work/nukhbat-al-fikr|Nukhbat al-Fikar]]; the title appears in this form on the manuscript chosen by its editor, [[person/nur-al-din-itr|Nur al-Din 'Itr]]. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 18} {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 22} The editor describes it as holding "the place of the foundation" in the methodology of hadith, for the brevity of its wording, the wealth of its points and the precision of its divisions. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 5}

## Why and when it was written
Ibn Hajar opens by naming those who wrote on hadith terminology before him: [[person/al-ramahurmuzi|al-Ramahurmuzi]] in al-Muhaddith al-Fasil, who was not comprehensive; [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]], who neither refined nor arranged; Abu Nu'aym al-Isfahani, who wrote a supplement to al-Hakim; then [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib al-Baghdadi]] in [[work/al-kifaya-fi-ilm-al-riwaya|al-Kifaya]] and al-Jami'; then [[person/al-qadi-iyyad|Qadi 'Iyad]] in al-Ilma' and al-Mayanshi in Ma la Yasa'u al-Muhaddith Jahluh; until [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] compiled [[work/muqaddimat-ibn-al-salah|his famous book]] when he taught hadith at the Ashrafiyya school, on which people then set to work in verse, abridgement, supplement and rebuttal. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 33-35} He then says that a friend asked him to sum up what mattered, so he wrote it "in a few slender leaves" called Nukhbat al-Fikar; the friend then asked for a commentary "to untie its knots and open its treasures", and he agreed, judging it better to give it "in expanded form", merging base text and commentary. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 36} The editor quotes a marginal note in a sound manuscript that the author finished it at the start of Dhu al-Hijja 818 AH. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 18}

## Method
The editor sums up its method in four points: {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 19-20}
- A new formulation of the hadith sciences by "probing and division", a method known to legal theorists, beginning with a division of reports: those with unlimited chains, those with a limited number of chains above two, those with two, and those with one. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 19}
- The inclusion of questions from legal theory, such as the widely spread report (mustafid) and the community's acceptance of a report. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 19}
- Brevity, avoiding digressions. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 20}
- The "expanded" style, so that if the brackets were removed the base text could not be told apart from the commentary. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 20}

## Structure
- **Reports by their chains**: [[concept/al-hadith-al-mutawatir|the mass-transmitted]], then the kinds of solitary report — [[concept/al-hadith-al-mashhur|the well-known]], [[concept/al-hadith-al-aziz|the rare]] and [[concept/al-hadith-al-gharib|the singular]] — and the standing of the solitary report and of the report supported by indications. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 36-52}
- **The accepted report**: [[concept/al-hadith-al-sahih|the sound]] in itself and through another, its ranks, the soundest chains and the comparison of the two Sahihs; [[concept/al-hadith-al-hasan|the good]] in itself and through another; additions by reliable narrators; the preserved and the anomalous, the known and the denounced; the corroborating chain, the supporting report and their search; then the unopposed report and reports that differ. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 52-72}
- Then the rejected report, through a gap in the chain or a defect in a narrator, and kinds concerning chains and narrators, down to the terms of criticism and praise at the end. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 133}

## Its influence
The editor holds that the terms Ibn Hajar chose in this book became the practice of the hadith scholars after him, such as his definitions of the anomalous and the denounced and his distinction of the accepted report as sound in itself, sound through another, good in itself and good through another. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 21}

## Manuscripts and this edition
'Itr took as his base the Zahiriyya manuscript in Damascus, no. 4895 (31 folios), copied by the jurist and hadith scholar Shihab al-Din Ibn al-Akhsasi of Damascus, a Shafi'i (818–889 AH), who finished it in the middle ten days of Ramadan 851 AH and read it to the author for close study; it bears Ibn Hajar's note "Its owner has completed reading it to me. Written by Ibn Hajar", and his hand appears on it in some twenty-five places. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 22-23} The editor added headings in square brackets, defined terms the author left undefined, traced the reports and identified the persons named. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 24-25} The Maktabat al-Bushra printing in Karachi (1432 AH/2011 CE) follows his edition with changes of layout: the base text is set in a distinct colour and is not repeated separately at the end. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 2} {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 24}
