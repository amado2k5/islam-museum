## Summary
*Al-Sawa'iq al-Mursala 'ala al-Jahmiyya wa'l-Mu'attila* ("The Thunderbolts Sent against the Jahmiyya and the Deniers of the Attributes") is a book by [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya]] (691–751 AH) criticising the interpretation that turns the texts on God's names and attributes away from their apparent sense. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 2} He opens it with a preface holding that tawhid is affirming the realities of the names and attributes in the way of perfection, that "the one who denies the attributes worships a nothing, and the one who likens God to creatures worships an idol", and that the key to the messengers' call is knowing God by His names, attributes and acts. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 3} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 5}

## Attribution
The editors establish its attribution to Ibn al-Qayyim on internal evidence, including his references in it to three of his books – *[[work/ijtima-al-juyush-al-islamiyya|Ijtima' al-Juyush al-Islamiyya]]*, *[[work/hadi-al-arwah|Hadi al-Arwah]]* (as "Sifat al-Janna") and *[[work/miftah-dar-al-saada|Miftah Dar al-Sa'ada]]* – and his quotations from his two teachers [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] and Ibn Taymiyya's brother Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Halim (d. 727 AH). {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 7} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 8}

## The question it answers
Ibn al-Qayyim closes the preface with a question he takes from Ibn Taymiyya: how can these later, bewildered people know God, His names and attributes better than the first forerunners among the Emigrants and Helpers and those who followed them, and how can the heirs of the philosophers know God better than the heirs of the prophets? He then makes the whole book an answer to it, in chapters. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 17} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 18}

## Its chapters
The editors count twenty-four chapters of very unequal length, the last running to hundreds of pages. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 18} The first is on the reality of ta'wil in language and usage: for the exegetes and the early generations it means explanation and clarification, while for the [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazila]], the [[group/jahmiyya|Jahmiyya]] and other theologians it means turning a word from its apparent and literal sense to a figurative one. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 18} The second divides ta'wil into the sound, which agrees with the texts, and the false, which contradicts them, with ten kinds of the false; the third holds that ta'wil reports what the speaker meant and does not create meaning; the fourth distinguishes the ta'wil of reports from that of commands. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 19} Later chapters treat the causes that make ta'wil easy for souls to accept; the inability of the people of ta'wil to establish a scriptural proof against any falsehood; the four causes of ta'wil – a deficiency in the speaker's clarity or a bad intention on his part, and a bad understanding or a bad intention in the hearer; praiseworthy and blameworthy disagreement; and the causes of disagreement among the imams, quoting Ibn Hazm and Ibn Taymiyya. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 26} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 27}

## The four "idols"
The editors call chapter 24 the heart and aim of the book. In it come the "four idols" (tawaghit) by which, Ibn al-Qayyim says, the people of false ta'wil demolished the strongholds of religion: that the words of God and His Messenger are verbal proofs yielding neither knowledge nor certainty; that the verses and hadiths on the attributes are figurative; that the Messenger's authentic reports yield not knowledge but only probability; and that when reason and revelation conflict, reason is followed. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 27} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 28}

- **The first idol** (revealed texts give no certainty): he refutes it in seventy-three aspects. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 28} He begins by quoting a theologian's statement "in the *Muhassal*" that verbal proof gives certainty only when ten things are certain, then gives Ibn Taymiyya's answer, distinguishing certainty about what the speaker meant from certainty that what he meant is true. See [[question/do-the-texts-of-revelation-yield-certainty|Do the texts of revelation yield certainty?]] {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 343} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 344}
- **The second idol** (reason over revelation): here he drew heavily, and openly, on his teacher's *[[work/dar-taarud-al-aql-wal-naql|Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql]]*, giving 242 numbered aspects, of which 234 survive because some are missing in the manuscript; this answer fills about two-thirds of the extant book. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 29}
- **The third and fourth idols**: the discussion of them has not been found in the original. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 29} The editors note that Ibn al-Qayyim put the "verbal proofs" idol later in the chapter's introduction but first in the detailed answer. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 28}

The editors add that Ibn al-Qayyim promised a chapter, after breaking the idols, showing that the verses on the attributes are clear and decisive (muhkam), and that it is not known whether he wrote it, since it is not at the end of the abridgement. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 30}

## Abridgements and editions
The book has two abridgements: [[work/mukhtasar-al-sawaiq-al-mursala|that of Shams al-Din Ibn al-Mawsili]] (d. 774 AH), the older and better known, important because it contains the abridgement of the lost part of the original; and that of Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab (d. 1206 AH), a small selection published by Daghsh ibn Shabib al-Ajmi in Kuwait in 1437 AH / 2016. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 55} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 56} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 57} The first edition of the extant original was by Ali ibn Muhammad al-Dakhil Allah in four volumes, Dar al-'Asima, Riyadh, 1408 AH, from a doctoral thesis at the Faculty of Usul al-Din of Imam University supervised by Muhammad Rashad Salim. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 58} The copy present is the Dar Ata'at al-'Ilm printing in the "Collected Works of Imam Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya", edited by Husayn ibn Ukasha ibn Ramadan, first edition, Riyadh, 1445 AH. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 2} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 3}

## Its place
The editors quote Muhammad Hamid al-Fiqi calling it Ibn al-Qayyim's best and most forceful book on this subject, and their account shows it continuing Ibn Taymiyya's method on the attributes and ta'wil; its judgements on the theologians are Ibn al-Qayyim's and are given here as his. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 29} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-v1 p. 54}
