## Summary

The fourteenth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers pauses before the migration to speak about the Ansar as a group and through individual figures: the Prophet's last sermon on them, their identity in Medina, their steadfastness at Uhud, and the lives of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, Usayd ibn Hudayr, Thabit ibn Qays, Abu Talha, Ubada ibn al-Samit, Umm Haram, Haram ibn Milhan and Mu'adh ibn Jabal. He closes with the lesson that the meaning behind their merit, supporting the religion and its messenger, remains open to later believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Why a session on the Ansar and the weight of the subject

- He says that the previous session reached the second pledge of al-Aqaba and that he had planned this one for the migration, but chose to speak about the Ansar instead because there is no single event called the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that the greatest thing the Ansar ever did in the whole biography was the pledge of al-Aqaba, so this is the fitting place to speak about them, and the next session will return to the migration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that the Prophet, as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari, made love of the Ansar a sign of faith and hatred of them a sign of hypocrisy, and he concludes that the subject touches creed and not only praise of righteous people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### The Prophet's last sermon and charge concerning the Ansar

- He relates, on the authority of Anas ibn Malik in al-Bukhari, that Abu Bakr and al-Abbas passed a gathering of Ansar weeping during the Prophet's final illness because they missed sitting with him, and told the Prophet about it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that the Prophet came out with his head bound, climbed the pulpit for the last time, and spent that sermon commending the Ansar to the people, calling them his closest and most trusted circle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He explains that the two images the Prophet used mean the people nearest to a person, and he calls the statement a decoration that every Ansari should wear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that the Prophet's statement that they had fulfilled what was on them and that what was theirs remained, given eleven years after the pledge of al-Aqaba, is his own witness that they kept the pledge to protect and support him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that the Prophet asked people to accept from their good-doers and forgive their wrongdoers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### The Ansar's identity in Medina and the respect of other Companions

- He says that the Ansar returned from the pledge as people who had, in effect, sold this world for the next, and received the Prophet in Medina with Ansari as their first identity. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that after the pledge and the migration, being Muhajir or Ansari came before tribal identity in the society of the Prophet's time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari and Muslim through Anas, that Jarir ibn Abdullah al-Bajali, older than Anas, insisted on serving him because he had seen what the Ansar did for the Prophet and had vowed to serve any of them he met. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He draws from this story how the Companions themselves viewed the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Women of the Ansar, Umar's charge, and the later hardship

- He says that two Ansari women attended the pledge of al-Aqaba, and that Ansari women were known for eagerness to learn their religion; he relates that Aisha praised them in al-Bukhari for not letting shyness stop them from seeking understanding. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that Umar ibn al-Khattab, when stabbed and near death, charged the caliph after him to treat the Ansar well, to accept from their good-doers and forgive their wrongdoers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar noted that scholars drew from the charge that the Ansar would not hold posts or wealth, since they were being asked to be forgiven rather than given office. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates a report in al-Bukhari that the Prophet wished to grant the Ansar land in al-Bahrayn, that they refused unless the Muhajirun received the same, and that he told them they would meet favouritism after him and should be patient until they meet him at the Basin. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that the Prophet also reported that people would grow many while the Ansar grew few, and he counts this as a sign of the Prophet's prophethood since the Ansar became fewer in tribal numbers afterwards. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### The distribution of spoils and winning hearts

- He relates that after a battle with Quraysh in which the Ansar and Muhajirun fought, the Prophet gave most of the spoils to Quraysh leaders to win their hearts, and the Ansar felt hurt because their swords were still wet with the blood of those men. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that the Prophet was aiming at something distant: he gives worldly goods to those of weaker faith so they come to love him and then their Islam becomes sound, while those with firm faith are left to their hearts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He compares this to someone who first seeks knowledge for a worldly motive and then finds it turns him toward God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that the Prophet gathered the Ansar and asked whether they would be content that people take flocks while they return with the Messenger of God to their homes, saying that if all people took one valley he would take the Ansar's valley, and praying for mercy on them and their descendants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that the speech bound their hearts, since it spoke to the great meaning they lived for, belief in God and support for the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that reformers should learn from this that worldly matters are sometimes kept from those closest to the leader because of the faith in their hearts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### The seven Ansar who defended the Prophet at Uhud

- He says that the Prophet's call to defend him at Uhud is told in Sahih Muslim by Anas and by Ibn Mas'ud, and he reads the account, which he calls one of the greatest showing the truth of the Ansar's pledge. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that when the lines broke and the polytheists pressed from both sides, the Prophet withdrew to the mountain pass and was left with seven Ansar and two men of Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that each time the enemy pressed him he offered that whoever drove them off would have Paradise, and one Ansari after another stepped forward and fought until killed, until all seven had died. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that the Prophet then said to his two remaining companions that they had not treated their companions fairly, and that in Ibn Mas'ud's version Abu Sufyan then shouted for Hubal and the Prophet answered with a call to God's greatness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that there were different stages at Uhud, sometimes with only one or two men beside the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and the reformer's school of the Prophet

- He asks which Ansar come to mind first, and names Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, a chief of the Aws who accepted Islam at the hands of Mus'ab ibn Umayr before the migration and kept close to the Prophet, taking an honoured stand at Badr when the Prophet consulted his companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that a student of the Prophet's school stays a short time and leaves a transformed person, still human with faults, yet truthful, patient, self-giving and close to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says Sa'd lived only six years as a Muslim, and relates that the Prophet said the throne of the Merciful shook at his death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that when silk garments sent by the ruler of Dumat al-Jandal were admired, the Prophet said that the handkerchiefs of Sa'd in Paradise are softer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Usayd ibn Hudayr and Abbad ibn Bishr

- He says that Usayd ibn Hudayr was another chief of the Aws and one of the best Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that Usayd and Abbad ibn Bishr left the Prophet at night and each was given a light to walk by until he reached home, which he calls a miracle granted by God and not a material lamp. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that Usayd was reciting a chapter at night while his horses grew restless, saw something like a cloud with lamps, and that the Prophet told him next day that these were angels who had come down for the recitation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Thabit ibn Qays and Ukasha ibn Mihsan

- He says that Thabit ibn Qays, the Prophet's orator, withdrew to his house when a verse about raising voices above the Prophet was revealed, fearing for himself; the Prophet asked about him and told him he was among the people of Paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that a good tiding of Paradise did not make the Companions idle: Thabit and Ukasha ibn Mihsan, promised entry without reckoning, both kept working and died as martyrs in the wars after the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He explains that someone who tastes the sweetness of faith and closeness to God cannot leave that life, and compares it with a person guaranteed the reward of night prayer in Ramadan who still would not stay home. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that Thabit, at al-Yamama, was found by Anas ibn Malik applying embalming perfume with his thighs uncovered, and when the army gave way he said the Muslims should clear a space so they could fight, criticizing how they had accustomed their opponents to retreat, and was killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Abu Talha and Usayd at Uhud

- He says that Abu Talha al-Ansari, husband of Umm Sulaym and stepfather of Anas, fought well at Uhud as an archer who rarely missed, and that the Prophet told departing men to leave their arrows for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that Abu Talha shielded the Prophet at the pass, begging him not to lift his head and offering his own neck as protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that Sa'd, in al-Bukhari, saw two men in white fighting fiercely for the Prophet at Uhud whom he had never seen before or after, and he takes them to be angels. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that Usayd ibn Hudayr had vowed, after missing Badr, to show God what he would do if he witnessed a battle; at Uhud he advanced beyond Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and was killed so badly marked that only his sister recognised him by a fingertip. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says the Usayd account may be confused in the transcript with a similar story of Anas ibn al-Nadr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Ubada ibn al-Samit and Umm Haram bint Milhan

- He says that Ubada ibn al-Samit was a leader among the Muslims, a jurist among the Companions, one of the twelve leaders chosen at the pledge of al-Aqaba, and the first judge Umar appointed over Palestine. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that his wife Umm Haram bint Milhan, sister of Umm Sulaym, was visited by the Prophet at her house. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that the Prophet once woke smiling and said he had been shown people of his community crossing the sea to fight like kings on thrones, that Umm Haram asked for prayer to be among them and was told she was, and that after a second dream she was told she would be among the first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that she later sailed with Mu'awiya's expedition with her husband Ubada, fell from her mount and died on that campaign. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Haram ibn Milhan at Bi'r Ma'una

- He relates, through Anas, that the Prophet sent about seventy men to Banu Amir, and that Anas's maternal uncle, Haram ibn Milhan, brother of Umm Haram, went ahead asking for safe conduct to convey the message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that while Haram was speaking, one of them stabbed him through, and he said he had won, which the speaker sees as showing what comes out of a person in his last moment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that the rest of the party were then killed except one lame man who climbed the mountain, and that the Prophet prayed against the killers for forty mornings, the best known occasion of that supplication. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates a wording in al-Bukhari that Haram put his blood on his face and head before saying he had won. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Mu'adh ibn Jabal and other Ansar treated elsewhere

- He says that Mu'adh ibn Jabal, whom the Prophet told he loved him, was in his twenties at the Prophet's death and was sent to Yemen as a teacher, at around twenty-five or twenty-six. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He holds that this shows how the Prophet's training produced people mature and independent enough to carry a mission, compared with much upbringing today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He relates that the Prophet advised Mu'adh to ask God after each prayer for help to remember, thank and worship him well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that he has given Umm Sulaym, Ubada ibn al-Samit, Usayd ibn Hudayr and Abbad ibn Bishr their own episodes in his series on lives and lessons for reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He mentions Jabir and his father Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Haram, a martyr at Uhud about whom a verse on the martyrs was revealed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}

### Closing lesson: the meaning behind the Ansar's merit remains open

- He holds that the merits reported for the Ansar were not given for lineage, since Quraysh were nobler in descent, nor for living in Medina, but for supporting the religion and the Prophet and providing the setting that protected the message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that this meaning has not run out, since the religion still needs people who sacrifice and live for it, and whoever imitates them may hope for something of what they received. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He acknowledges that the Companions had a distinction later believers missed, surrounding and defending the Prophet in person, and relates the Prophet's statement that none can match even a measure of a Companion's spending. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
- He says that those who follow the Ansar's path and are patient may hope to meet them and the Prophet at the Basin. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep14}
