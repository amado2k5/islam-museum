## Summary

The lecturer resumes reading Ibn Hisham's recension of Ibn Ishaq, at the years of the siege in the valley, and goes through the chapters that gather the objections and arguments the Meccan leaders raised against the Prophet, with the verses reported to have answered each. He notes the different style of each opponent, comments on Ibn Ishaq's care in marking his chains of report, and ends with the report on Ibn Umm Maktum and the opening of Surat Abasa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### Returning to Ibn Hisham and the value of the Meccan disputes

- He says that the previous sessions were spent on surahs and on the Abyssinia emigrants from Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat, so this session returns straight to Ibn Hisham, the base text of the course. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He holds that any reading of the Meccan period that skips the disputes with the polytheists and the way the Prophet established the case against them is incomplete. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says that Ibn Ishaq places many of these disputes in the years of the siege of the Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib in the valley, which he dates from the seventh to the tenth year, and that this keeps those three years from being an empty gap in the narrative. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He notes that earlier exchanges involving Abu Lahab, Umayya ibn Khalaf and al-As ibn Wa'il were only touched on in an earlier lesson and that he now reads the texts fully. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### Abu Jahl: the threat to insult the Prophet's God

- He relates, following Ibn Hisham, that Abu Jahl met the Prophet and threatened that Quraysh would insult the God he worshipped unless he stopped insulting their gods. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that a verse in Surat al-An'am forbidding the insulting of what others call upon was reported as sent down on this occasion, and that the Prophet then stopped and went on only inviting them to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says that more verses are reported as revealed about Abu Jahl than about any other polytheist, and refers listeners to an earlier lesson on the occasions of revelation for the full survey. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He observes that many verses with no name in them are transmitted in the exegesis and occasions-of-revelation literature as tied to particular Meccan opponents. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### al-Nadr ibn al-Harith and the claim of ancient tales

- He relates that al-Nadr ibn al-Harith would take the seat after the Prophet had preached and recited and warned Quraysh of past nations, and would tell them Persian stories of Rustam, Isfandiyar and the Persian kings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that al-Nadr claimed his tales were better than the Prophet's and that the Prophet's accounts were merely ancient tales he had written down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says this shows how closely the polytheists followed everything the Prophet brought and answered it, and holds that the answers came from God directly, within the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says that Ibn Ishaq lists verses reported as revealed on this charge, and he adds that Ibn Abbas is quoted earlier in the book as saying that every verse mentioning ancient tales concerned al-Nadr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He holds that the Qur'an's accounts of Noah, Moses, Abraham, Hud, Salih and Shu'ayb are far from Persian legend, and points out that several surahs say these stories are reports of the unseen revealed to the Prophet, who was not present for them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates Ibn Hisham's gloss that the word for slanderer means liar, with a line of verse by Ru'ba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### The mentality of the listener and the method of quibbling

- He holds that the polytheists attended the Prophet's recitation to refute it, not to benefit from it, and that the listener's state of mind decides whether truth is accepted however clear it is. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He holds that a mark of this attitude is turning away from the main question while quibbling over details, and that the same pattern recurs today in debates with atheists and deniers of the Sunna. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says the Prophet and the believers were humbled by the verses while the polytheists met the same verses with mockery. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Ibn al-Zib'ara's argument about those worshipped besides God

- He says that Abu Jahl, al-Walid ibn al-Mughira and al-As ibn Wa'il are, by the sheer number of reported occasions of revelation, the opponents most often named, followed by Umayya and Ubayy ibn Khalaf, Utba and Shayba sons of Rabi'a, and al-Akhnas ibn Shariq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that the Prophet silenced al-Nadr in a gathering with al-Walid and then recited verses saying that what they worship besides God is fuel for hell. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that Abdullah ibn al-Zib'ara then argued that the angels, Ezra and Jesus are also worshipped by some, so would they too be in hell, and that al-Walid and the others were impressed by this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that the Prophet answered that whoever is worshipped beside God is with those who worship him, since they in truth worship devils, and that verses were revealed exempting those previously promised good, including angels and the righteous. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates Ibn Hisham's note on the meaning of the word for fuel, with a line of verse by Abu Dhu'ayb, and a note on two readings of a word in Surat al-Zukhruf, one meaning turning away and the other meaning clamouring, which he gives as an example of readings that widen meaning without contradiction. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates two explanations given for the verse calling Jesus a sign of the Hour: the miracles at his hands, or his descent at the end of time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### The different styles of opposition

- He holds that each opponent had a method: al-Nadr used the claim of ancient tales, Abu Jahl used mockery, intimidation and bullying, and al-Walid stood for the arrogant and self-sufficient elite who did not bother with detailed objections. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says that Abu Jahl used the same bullying towards fellow Qurayshi unbelievers who were drawn towards Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says it is a well-known report that the verses about the man who deemed himself self-sufficient and the one created alone were about al-Walid, while noting disagreement on whether the first referred to Utba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He compares such figures in the present day to those who lead campaigns against Muslim revival. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### al-Akhnas ibn Shariq and the debate on the word zanim

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq counts al-Akhnas ibn Shariq al-Thaqafi, ally of the Banu Zuhra, among the great deniers, a notable man who was listened to and who answered the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that verses describing a swearing, slandering man ending with the word zanim were reported as revealed about him, and that Ibn Ishaq says the word was not meant as an insult on his lineage but as a description marking an attached outsider. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says that because the man was an ally and not Qurayshi, this was one reason some exegetes favoured him as the intended person. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates the editors' note that al-Qurtubi says most exegetes held the verse to be about al-Walid, others about al-Nadr, and that Ibn Abbas, in a report in al-Bukhari, says only that it was a Qurayshi man, which excludes al-Akhnas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He holds that the Qur'an describes these men by traits their contemporaries recognised. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### al-Walid ibn al-Mughira and the complaint about who received the Qur'an

- He relates that al-Walid asked why the revelation would come to Muhammad and pass over himself, the master of Quraysh, and Abu Mas'ud Amr ibn Umayr al-Thaqafi, master of Thaqif. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq reports, with the wording of a relayed report, that a verse about a revelation upon a great man of the two towns was sent down about this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He holds that al-Walid exemplifies the wealthy, arrogant opposer who relies on status and not on arguments. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### Ibn Ishaq's care in marking his chains, and the contrast with al-Waqidi

- He holds that Ibn Ishaq is precise in documentation, distinguishing a connected chain, a chain that stops at a successor, and a general report relayed to him, and that a close survey of his reports raises his standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says that al-Waqidi, by contrast, almost never uses a relayed report formula and relates everything with chains, with a great many solitary reports, which led many hadith scholars to judge him harshly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He holds that al-Waqidi's reports are weak by default and that some exceptions may be made under conditions he explained elsewhere, while some scholars went as far as accusing him of lying. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He explains that the early critics judged most narrators they had never met, by comparing their reports, and gives the example of Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Yahya ibn Ma'in. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Ubayy ibn Khalaf and Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt

- He relates that Ubayy ibn Khalaf of the Banu Jumah and Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt were close friends, and that when Ubayy heard that Uqba had sat with the Prophet and listened, he swore he would not speak to him unless Uqba spat in the Prophet's face. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that Uqba did so, and that verses about the wrongdoer biting his hands in regret were reported as revealed about the two. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that Ubayy came to the Prophet with a decayed bone, crumbled it and blew it towards him, asking whether God would raise such a thing, and that the Prophet answered yes, and that God would raise both and then send Ubayy to the fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that verses on God's power to give life to decayed bones were reported as revealed in answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### The offer to share worship and the occasion of Surat al-Kafirun

- He relates that while the Prophet was circling the Kaaba, al-Aswad ibn al-Muttalib, al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, Umayya ibn Khalaf and al-As ibn Wa'il, all older men of standing, proposed that each side worship the other's deity so that both would share in the benefit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that Surat al-Kafirun was reported as revealed about this offer, and he holds that knowing the occasion helps explain the repetition in the surah. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He compares the offer to a mediation between two families in which each gives a little. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### Abu Jahl and the zaqqum tree

- He relates that when the zaqqum tree was mentioned as a warning, Abu Jahl told Quraysh it was nothing but dates of Medina mixed with butter and that he would gladly gulp it down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates that verses describing the tree as food of the sinful, boiling like molten metal in the bellies, were reported as revealed in answer, and another verse saying the cursed tree was only a trial and increased them in rebellion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates Ibn Hisham's gloss that the word for molten metal means anything melted such as brass or lead, with a report from al-Hasan al-Basri that Ibn Mas'ud, as Umar's governor over the treasury at Kufa, had silver melted and pointed to its changing colours as the nearest likeness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates a report that Abu Bakr, near death, asked to be shrouded in two worn garments and answered Aisha's objection by saying the shroud was only for a short while before the molten state, and he notes that it is unclear whether the relayed formula belongs to Ibn Ishaq or Ibn Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### Ibn Umm Maktum and the opening of Surat Abasa

- He says this chapter was treated in detail in the previous session and is only read here. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that the Prophet was speaking with al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, hoping he would accept Islam, when the blind Ibn Umm Maktum asked him to recite to him, and the Prophet turned away frowning, whereupon the opening verses of Surat Abasa were reported as revealed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates Ibn Hisham's note that Ibn Umm Maktum was of the Banu Amir ibn Luayy, named Abdullah or Amr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He relates the editors' reference to a report in al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim, from Aisha, with a note that the chain is sound but scholars differ on whether it is connected or mursal, in which the Prophet is occupied with a leading polytheist when Ibn Umm Maktum seeks guidance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}

### Plan for the coming sessions

- He says that the next chapters in Ibn Hisham concern the return of some emigrants from Abyssinia after hearing that Mecca had accepted Islam, then the tearing up of the boycott document and the names of those who did it, then the account of al-Tufayl ibn Amr al-Dawsi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
- He says that even after the document is annulled Ibn Ishaq goes on recording further occasions of revelation and arguments, such as the objection about the man the polytheists pointed to as a teacher, the demand for an angel to be sent with the Prophet, and the mockery of earlier messengers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep30}
