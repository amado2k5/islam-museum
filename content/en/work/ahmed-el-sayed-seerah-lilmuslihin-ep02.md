## Summary

The second lecture of the series on the Prophet's biography for reformers, on what happened before the sending. The speaker sorts the events into signs that drew people's attention and signs and preparation aimed at the Prophet himself: the Year of the Elephant, his mother's vision, the opening of his chest, the earlier tidings in the scriptures of the People of the Book, his truthfulness, his shepherding and his retreats. He draws from each the lesson that God prepares those He sends for great tasks. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### Opening and the scope of the lecture

- He opens the second session of the series and says that it deals only with what concerns the Prophet himself before the sending, whether proofs of his prophethood or God's preparation of him for it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He says that the next session will take up the condition of the people in the Prophet's time and why knowing it matters for understanding the Qur'an, the Sunna and the biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### The Year of the Elephant and the Prophet's birth

- He divides the events around the birth into two kinds: those that drew the public's attention that something had happened, and those touching the Prophet's own close circle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He says that it is established, and that some scholars report consensus, that the Prophet was born in the Year of the Elephant, and that scholars did not treat this as a mere coincidence of fate but as a purposeful sign with a wisdom behind it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that al-Mawardi explained the destruction of the elephant army by saying that Quraysh, idol worshippers rather than a people of scripture, did not deserve to be saved for their own sake, and that God acted to found prophethood and honour the Kaaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that Ibn Taymiyya argued that, since the neighbours of the House were idolaters worse off than the Christians, the event cannot have been for their sake but for the House, or for the Prophet born that year beside it, or for both, and that in any case it is among the proofs of his prophethood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir called the event a preparation for the sending, and he adds in his own terms that all the lights were turned onto the Kaaba, showing the Arabs and the world that the place mattered to God and that something great was coming after an age of idol worship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that the birth should be studied through firmly established reports, and that the many weak or unchained stories, such as the falling of the battlements of the Persian king's palace at his birth, are not established and should be set aside. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Amina's vision and the importance of dreams for the prophets

- He relates that when the Prophet was asked about fasting on Monday, he explained that it was the day he was born and the day revelation first came to him, and he takes both as things decreed and intended. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that his mother Amina saw in a dream around his birth a great light come out of her that lit up the castles of Busra in Syria, that this is transmitted through several chains, and that he calls it a preparation God made in the Prophet's close circle and beyond. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that the sending of the last of the messengers, with the last book to the last prophet, is a tremendous event in the course of the universe, which is why the signs began at birth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He notes that dreams recur in the preparation of prophets, pointing out that the long story of Joseph begins with a dream of eleven stars and ends with the explanation of that dream. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He says that it is preserved with the soundest chains that every dream the Prophet saw came true like the break of dawn, and he relates from Aisha in al-Bukhari that the first form of revelation for him was the true dream. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He explains one reading of the saying that a good dream is a forty-sixth part of prophethood: prophethood lasted twenty-three years and the period of dreams was about six months, which gives one part in forty-six. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### A map of the events: signs and preparation

- He draws the lesson that God prepares for great matters, and most of all prepares the person who is to carry them, and he calls understanding this one of God's patterns in His dealings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He sorts the pre-prophetic events into two headings, signs and preparation, and splits the signs into those meant to make people take notice and those meant to make the Prophet himself take notice, adding that every sign concerning him is also part of his preparation but not all preparation is a sign. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### Signs concerning the Prophet himself: the opening of the chest and the greeting stone

- He relates from Sahih Muslim that while the child Prophet was playing with other boys outside Mecca at his wet nurse's home, two men laid him down, opened his chest, took something out and said it was the share of Satan in him, then closed it, and he returned with a changed colour that startled the nurse. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that the nurse then brought him back to Amina, and that in a report he thinks is well transmitted, Amina was not surprised and told of the light that had come out of her at his birth, a report he places in Ahmad's Musnad with a chain Ibn Kathir called good and strong, adding that this is not the origin of the story itself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that the opening of the chest is both a sign and a preparation, because God purified His prophet from early on. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates from Sahih Muslim, on the authority of Jabir ibn Samura, that the Prophet said he knew a stone in Mecca that used to greet him before revelation came, and relates from Ibn Abbas that for a period before revelation the Prophet heard a voice and saw a light without being given revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that these signs did not remove the shock when Jibril came down at Hira, which was an overwhelming event after which the Prophet went straight to Khadija asking to be covered and saying he feared for himself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### The story of Bahira

- He says that the story of the monk Bahira, if established, would be a sign both for the Prophet and for the people, that almost every biography book gives it and al-Tirmidhi records it in his Sunan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that there is a deep discussion about whether it is established, that al-Dhahabi criticised it and many others pointed to some criticism, and that one widely known version has Abu Bakr and Bilal with the Prophet and Abu Talib sending him back with them, which is a problem since Bilal was much younger and Abu Bakr somewhat younger than the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that the story may have a basis in the event itself while many of its reported details are doubtful, and he leaves the matter to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Signs for the people: tidings in the earlier scriptures

- He lists the signs aimed at the people as the Year of the Elephant, Amina's vision, the blessing seen in the animals and milk of the nurse's household, and the story of the two angels. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that among the greatest signs is that the coming of the Prophet had been announced in the books of the People of the Book, who knew he would appear at that time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that Heraclius, after questioning Abu Sufyan, told him he knew that a prophet was due but had not thought he would come from among them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He says that the Qur'an itself affirms the basis of this in Surat al-Baqara, where the People of the Book are said to have prayed for victory over unbelievers through him before he came and then rejected him when he did. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates from the biography books, in a report of Ibn Ishaq that Ahmad also records, a story of the Ansar and their Jewish neighbours in which the Jews had told them a prophet would appear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He says that in recent times scholars have studied the tidings more widely because of the many Christian and Jewish sources now available and the greater contact between Muslims and others, and he names a recent book by Sami Amer on the tidings of the Prophet of Islam, which answers Christian objections that a New Testament passage does not refer to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He adds that this field was cultivated by early scholars as well and is not only a recent concern. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### Pre-prophetic character used as proof: no writing or poetry, trustworthiness and the Kaaba

- He holds that certain facts about the Prophet before the sending are not signs in themselves but were used after it as arguments for his truthfulness, and that the Qur'an itself points to them, for example that he had not read or written any book before and had not been taught poetry. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He suggests that the rebuilding of the Kaaba, shortly before the sending, can count as a sign or preparation, because the tribes all accepted him as arbiter and the title they used for him then was the trustworthy one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that when the Prophet climbed al-Safa to open his call, he relied on that standing in their minds, asking whether they would believe a warning of horsemen behind the mountain, and they answered that they had never found him lying. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that Heraclius made the same inference, asking Abu Sufyan whether they had ever found him lying and concluding that a man who never lied about people would not lie about God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### God's protection of the Prophet from idol worship

- He counts among the signs God's protection of the Prophet, and of some around him, from practices of that age, above all idol worship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He cites a well-known report in some Sunna or biography books of an exchange between the Prophet and a man called Zayd, in which the Prophet forbade him to wipe an idol. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### Preparation: shepherding

- He lists direct preparation of the Prophet as the opening of the chest and shepherding. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates the report in al-Bukhari that no prophet was sent who had not herded sheep, and says it concerns all prophets, not only Muhammad, so he takes it as one of God's patterns, as the Qur'an elsewhere stresses similarities among the prophets, for instance in their patience and in the common religion given to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He draws from it that God likes to prepare His righteous reformers before they carry their burdens, and that anyone being formed as a reformer needs training and gradual preparation, since the Prophet was not just a messenger of the message but also a leader of people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar in Fath al-Bari explained the wisdom: herding trains a person for the charge of a community, builds gentleness and compassion, and teaches patience with scattered, weak and differing creatures that need constant care, so that the later burden is easier than if it came all at once. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that this reading, which draws large lessons from a short report, is an example of understanding in religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}

### Preparation: reflection and retreat

- He relates from Aisha that solitude was made dear to the Prophet and that he would retire for a number of nights to worship and reflect before revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He says that some scholars hold that such retreat was already known among the Arabs, and that Ibn Kathir cites a line from Abu Talib's well-known ode mentioning Hira in this context. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that solitude and contemplation calm the soul and restore the proper weighing of things, which daily life and constant company disturb, and that this need is greater today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
- He holds that contemplating the signs in the universe strengthens faith, and that the Prophet's reflection and retreat were among the preparations God gave him to receive the great truths. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep2}
