## Summary

A question about paying for a sacrifice by credit card in instalments, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the sacrifice is a sunna for those who can afford it and is valid from someone who borrows for it; buying it with a credit card backed by funds is unobjectionable, and with an unbacked card it is permitted on three conditions: no interest increase, no late-payment fine, and no price rise by the merchant because of the card commission. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3774}

## The question

The questioner asked about paying for sacrificial animals in instalments by credit card: the customer pays the organisation with his card and repays the bank in three monthly instalments without interest. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3774}

## The answer

The Department answered that the sacrifice is a sunna for those able to afford it and is not asked of others, though if someone burdens himself and borrows for it, his sacrifice is valid. It quoted [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] in [[work/al-majmu-sharh-al-muhadhdhab|al-Majmu]] (8/383) that the sacrifice is an emphasised sunna and a visible rite that those able should keep up, and becomes obligatory only by a vow. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3774} It said that buying the sacrifice with a card backed by sufficient funds is unobjectionable; with an unbacked card the amount paid is effectively a loan, and the purchase is then permitted on three conditions: the issuer does not stipulate any interest increase on the amount, does not stipulate a fine for late repayment, and the merchant does not raise the price because of the commission the card company takes from him. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3774} It concluded that buying sacrifices in instalments through credit cards is generally valid if these conditions are met, while judging a particular card requires examining its terms in detail. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3774}
