## Summary

*Greek Thought, Arabic Culture* by Dimitri Gutas studies the Graeco-Arabic translation movement in Baghdad and early ʿAbbāsid society, from the second to the fourth century AH (eighth to tenth century CE). {cite:openlibrary-greek-thought-arabic-culture-1998} The publisher's description says that from the mid-eighth to the tenth century almost all non-literary and non-historical secular Greek books, on subjects such as astrology, alchemy, physics, botany and medicine, were translated into Arabic. {cite:google-books-greek-thought-arabic-culture} The book examines the social, political and ideological factors in early ʿAbbāsid society that sustained the movement after the ʿAbbāsids came to power and founded Baghdad. {cite:openlibrary-greek-thought-arabic-culture-1998} The same description says Gutas draws on earlier historical and philological scholarship in Graeco-Arabic studies and on the study of medieval translations of secular Greek works. {cite:google-books-greek-thought-arabic-culture}

## Publication and editions

Routledge published it in London and New York in 1998, in 230 pages, with a bibliography on pages 197–215 and indexes. {cite:openlibrary-greek-thought-arabic-culture-1998} Its Google Books page lists an Arabic translation published by Markaz Dirasat al-Wahda al-Arabiyya in 2003 and a French translation published by Aubier in 2005. {cite:google-books-greek-thought-arabic-culture} The page describes Gutas as Professor of Arabic Language and Literature at Yale University. {cite:google-books-greek-thought-arabic-culture}
