## Summary
A daily Ramadan programme by Omar Abdelkafy. Each episode takes one devotional or ethical theme in about ten minutes. The speaker defines the 'path of light' as the path of guidance, that is the Qur'an and the sunna, away from innovation, whims, factionalism and disputes that stir up grudges, and likens revelation to the guide that a maker supplies with a vehicle. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:03:00}

## Episode 1: the five kinds of faith
- He announces the idea of the programme: to walk the path of guidance, the Qur'an and the sunna, and cites that God laid down for the community the religion He enjoined on Noah, Abraham, Moses and Jesus, that people should not split over it [[passage/quran-42-13|Surat al-Shura, verse 13]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:02:30}
- He divides [[concept/iman|faith]] into five kinds: stamped, accepted, rejected, no faith, and the bankrupt's faith. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:04:00}
- The stamped kind is the faith of [[concept/malaika|the angels]], who are set on obedience, do not dispute God's rulings and are inspired to glorify Him as people breathe. He says the faster in Ramadan daytime imitates them by abstaining from food, drink and backbiting. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:05:00}
- The accepted kind is the believer's faith in the articles of faith, together with the pillars of Islam. He mentions that whoever has an atom's weight of faith in his heart comes out of the Fire. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:06:00}
- The rejected kind is, in his account, the faith of the [[concept/bidah|innovator]] who adds to the religion or removes from it. He holds that keeping to what the Prophet brought is better than striving in innovation, and asks whether one who adds believes himself cleverer than Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali. He says repentance from innovation is harder than repentance from a sin because it becomes a whim. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:07:30}
- Speaker's view (opinion): the first days of fasting are hardest for the smoker. He prays that God help smokers to quit and says the angels are harmed by what harms the children of Adam. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:08:00}
- He gives the faith of the [[concept/nifaq|hypocrite]] as an example of faith that does not benefit its holder, and names [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy]], who prayed the five prayers with the Prophet while hiding the opposite of what he showed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:09:00}
- He relates that when [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] took office he asked [[person/hudhayfa-ibn-al-yaman|Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman]], who kept the list of hypocrites, whether the Prophet had named him. (needs_source: the number of hypocrites quoted in the recording is unclear) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:09:00}
- The fifth kind is the bankrupt's faith, that of one who owes wrongs to other people, which he calls the great calamity. (needs_source: around 00:08:30 and 00:09:30 the recording mixes the description of the hypocrite and the innovator, so it is not certain which one is the 'rejected' and which the 'no faith') {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep01 00:09:30}

## Episode 2: what veils the heart from God
- He opens on the hearts of the knowers and other hearts that their owners veiled, and lists signs: one who does not weep when others weep in the tarawih prayer, does not feel awe and has no wish to stay in a gathering of knowledge. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:01:30}
- He limits what veils the heart to three things: joy in what one has, grief for what is lost, and joy in praise. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:02:00}
- On the first, he says wide or narrow provision is not a sign of God's honour or dishonour, citing [[passage/quran-89-15|al-Fajr 15]] and [[passage/quran-89-16|16]]. He says [[person/qarun|Qarun]] was not enriched out of satisfaction, that [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] and Solomon were given wealth and treated it as a trial, and mentions Solomon's words in [[passage/quran-27-40|al-Naml 40]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:02:30}
- He separates praiseworthy joy from blameworthy joy. Blameworthy is joy in the blessing that forgets the Giver, and he cites the people of Qarun in [[passage/quran-28-76|al-Qasas 76]]. Praiseworthy is joy in faith, a righteous child, a good wife, reading the Qur'an and fasting, with a reference to [[passage/quran-10-58|Yunus 58]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:07:30}
- He tells of an imam who was beaten for years over the question of [[concept/khalq-al-quran|the createdness of the Qur'an]] and smiled, saying to those who wept: you saw the hand of the executioner and I saw the hand of the Lord of the servants. (needs_source: the recording names him 'Ahmad ibn Muhammad'; identifying him as [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] is an inference, and the figure of thirteen years is uncertain) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:04:30}
- On the second, he says whoever loses a job, a post or a child grieves, and that the better response is [[concept/sabr|patience]] with God's test and [[concept/shukr|gratitude]] for what He gave. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:08:00}
- On the third, he tells a story from the time of [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj]]: young men boasted of their fathers, and a poor, learned boy said he was the son of the man the angels were ordered to prostrate to, meaning [[person/adam|Adam]]. (the speaker's anecdote, no chain given) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:06:00}
- He advises against joy in people's praise, because they have not seen what the recording angel wrote of one's sins. Were sins to have a smell, no believer would sit beside another. He calls for thanking God who veiled the faults, not those who praised. This does not stop us from advising a sinner. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep02 00:10:00}

## Episode 3: the three kinds of sin
- He says every child of Adam errs and the best of those who err are the repentant, and prays that God make the month a chance for the forgiveness of all sins. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:01:00}
- He divides sins into three kinds: tyrannical, satanic (of Iblis), and animal. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:01:00}
- The tyrannical kind is [[concept/kibr|pride]]. God dislikes the unjust rich and the proud poor, and he likens the proud man to one on a mountain who sees people as specks and forgets that they see him as a speck. He notes three words that betray pride: 'I', 'mine' and 'I have'. He recalls the words of [[person/iblis|Iblis]] in [[passage/quran-7-12|al-A'raf 12]], of [[person/firawn|Pharaoh]] in [[passage/quran-43-51|al-Zukhruf 51]] and of [[person/qarun|Qarun]] in [[passage/quran-28-78|al-Qasas 78]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:03:00}
- He quotes verses saying people are made of one clay and return to it. (needs_source: the poet and the wording are unclear in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:03:30}
- The satanic kind is [[concept/hasad|envy]], love of leadership and self-love. He says the first sin by which God was disobeyed on earth was envy, naming Iblis's envy of Adam and the envy of [[person/qabil|Cain]] for his brother, with a reference to [[passage/quran-5-28|al-Ma'ida 28]]. He says the envier is like a fire that consumes itself and his envy grows as the blessing grows. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:08:00}
- He says no one is above another by lineage or colour, since all belong to Adam and Adam was of dust. He names [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal]] the Abyssinian as an example of high standing, and tells that the Prophet asked him what deed made him hear his footsteps in Paradise. Bilal answered that he never sinned without following it with seeking forgiveness, and never performed ablution without praying two rak'as, and that his own prayer was to ask God for His pleasure and Paradise and to seek refuge from the Fire; the Prophet said it is around these that we hover. (needs_source: what is said about Bilal's call to prayer on the day of the conquest of Mecca is unclear in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:06:30}
- The animal kind is the drives of appetite, revenge, violence, aggression and injustice. He claims the lion kills only when hungry and will not drink from water a dog has lapped. (needs_source: the claim about lion behaviour) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:09:00}
- He ends by calling for repentance from all three kinds and for the month to be the start of the path of goodness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep03 00:09:30}

## Episode 4: five cases where haste is praised
- He says haste is disliked except in five matters, because man was created hasty, and gives the example of a child who insists on a toy or a car that does not suit him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:01:00}
- First: [[concept/tawba|repentance]] from sin at once. He repeats the story of Bilal, forgiveness-seeking and prayer after ablution, warns against settling into a sinful habit such as the café, smoking, the water pipe, songs and series, and compares it to a sick man who admits his illness and obeys the doctor but argues about the lawful and the forbidden. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:03:00}
- Second: paying a debt at once. He criticises pilgrimage and umra on instalments, citing that God placed no hardship in the religion, and holds that one should settle debts rather than open a new one. (needs_source: the details on pilgrimage by instalments are garbled in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:04:30}
- Third: answering the call to prayer. He says a Companion would raise the hammer, hear the call and put it down, and relates that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] missed a man, learned he was ill, knocked on his door and struck him with the whip because he did not answer the muezzin. (needs_source: the Umar story has no chain and its wording is garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:05:30}
- Fourth: marrying off daughters. He raises unmarried women and gives rates of fifty percent in a rich country, one percent in Palestine and nine million girls in one Arab country, and says it is a poverty of hearts, not pockets. (needs_source: every figure is the speaker's, with no source) He urges marrying whoever is approved for character and religion, and prefers a groom from the young man's own milieu and tribe. (speaker's view, opinion) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:08:00}
- Fifth: [[practice/dafn|burying]] the dead promptly. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:08:30}
- He ties the five to the words of Moses 'and I hastened to You, my Lord, that You be pleased', and to God's question about what made him hurry from his people [[passage/quran-20-83|Ta Ha 83]] and [[passage/quran-20-84|84]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep04 00:09:30}

## Episode 5: what a person forgets
- He says the human being is called insan because he forgets, and notes that some trace the word to intimacy. (the speaker's etymology) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:00:30}
- He lists five things people love while forgetting their counterpart: they love the creature and forget the Creator, love money and forget the reckoning, love [[concept/dunya|this world]] and forget [[concept/akhira|the hereafter]], love sins and forget repentance, and love palaces and forget graves. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:05:30}
- On money, he says a servant is asked about two things: where he got it and how he spent it. He sets out four combinations of earning and spending and says the duty is to earn lawfully and spend lawfully. He explains the word mal as from inclining away from the truth. (the speaker's etymology) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:02:30}
- On this world and the next, he gives the example of a man asked to write his will who objects, though the death of one of the couple is not the end of the world. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:03:30}
- On sins, he warns against putting repentance off: to tomorrow, to Ramadan, to the pilgrimage season, to the age of forty, or to marriage, which shuts the doors of good. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:04:30}
- On graves, he says three accompany a person: family, wealth and deed. Family and wealth turn back and only the deed enters the grave, either a garden of Paradise or a pit of the Fire. (the speaker's statement, no chain given) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:05:30}
- He calls on the Muslim to draw a plan for life as one makes a feasibility study for a project, referring to God's command to the Prophet to keep himself with those who call on their Lord and not to obey one whose heart He made heedless [[passage/quran-18-28|al-Kahf 28]], and to God dividing livelihood among people [[passage/quran-43-32|al-Zukhruf 32]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:07:30}
- He mentions that some righteous people prayed for forty years as if each prayer were their last, and says nothing guarantees a person will reach the night prayer or dawn. (needs_source: the righteous man is not named in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:08:00}
- He relates that [[person/al-rabi-ibn-khuthaym|al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym]] dug a grave in his house and entered it when the world distracted him, then refers to [[passage/quran-23-100|al-Mu'minun 100]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep05 00:09:00}

## Episode 6: injustice and its kinds
- He says the worst thing people suffer from is [[concept/zulm|injustice]], defined as putting a thing in the wrong place, or acting in another's right without right. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:01:00}
- He divides it into three: between a servant and his Lord, between him and people, and a person's injustice to himself. He links this to the words 'some wrong themselves, some are moderate, some are foremost in good deeds' [[passage/quran-35-32|Fatir 32]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:07:00}
- The first, he says, is leaving God's commands and committing His prohibitions. He holds that voluntary acts cannot replace obligations however many umras one performs, and that rejecting a command is graver than committing a prohibition, giving Iblis and Adam as the contrast. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:03:00}
- Speaker's view on the [[concept/hijab|hijab]]: he counts a woman's not wearing it as rejecting a command, citing [[passage/quran-33-59|al-Ahzab 59]], and says the sins of those who look at her are counted against her guardian. (the speaker's view, a disputed question; needs_source for what he says about the guardian's burden) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:04:00}
- The second, he says, includes harming by words, insult, belittling, arrogance, taking money and ruining rights. He adds 'moral killing': a wife who kills her husband's ambition, a manager who kills his staff's creativity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:05:00}
- He observes that every party complains of being wronged: manager, employee, husband, wife, neighbour and the two litigants before the judge. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:06:00}
- He says God does not accept repentance from wronging people unless the rights are returned, and that giving taken money away as charity is not enough: it goes back to its owners. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:06:30}
- He mentions that cities and civilisations were destroyed by injustice, and cites the verse on plotting [[passage/quran-14-46|Ibrahim 46]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tariq-al-noor/ep06 00:07:00}
- The third, he says, is burdening oneself beyond one's means, for example spending twenty on an income of five.
