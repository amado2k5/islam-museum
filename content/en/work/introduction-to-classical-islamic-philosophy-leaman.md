## Summary

*An Introduction to Classical Islamic Philosophy* is an English introduction by Oliver Leaman. Cambridge University Press first published it in 1985 as *An Introduction to Medieval Islamic Philosophy* and later reissued it under its present title. {cite:openlibrary-introduction-medieval-islamic-philosophy-1985}

## Publication and editions

In the preface to the first edition the author says his aim is not only to describe Islamic philosophy but to arouse interest in its problems and arguments, by following a number of issues from their origins in theology to their discussion in philosophy, rather than to give a comprehensive history. The second edition is in two parts. The first is on al-Ghazali's attack on philosophy: the creation of the world in Averroes and al-Ghazali, Maimonides, causality, immortality and the active intellect, and God's knowledge of particulars. The second is on reason and revelation in practical reasoning: ethics and happiness, how to read Islamic philosophy, mysticism, illuminationism, and Averroes and Aristotle. {cite:archive-org-intro-classical-islamic-philosophy-leaman-text}
