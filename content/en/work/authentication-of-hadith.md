## Summary

*Authentication of Hadith: Redefining the Criteria* is a book by Israr Ahmad Khan, published by the International Institute of Islamic Thought in Herndon, Virginia, in 2010 in 215 pages. {cite:ajiss-review-authentication-of-hadith} According to a review in *The American Journal of Islamic Social Sciences*, the book tries to set criteria for accepting or rejecting questioned hadiths, and is the first book in English devoted to the subject. {cite:ajiss-review-authentication-of-hadith}

## Publication and editions

The book has an introduction and eight chapters but no conclusion: the first on forgery in hadith, its motives and the hadith scholars' responses, the second on the hadith scholars' criteria for the chain and the text. {cite:ajiss-review-authentication-of-hadith} In chapters three to seven the author proposes his criteria: agreement with the Qur'an, established hadiths, reason, established history and moderation; in the last chapter he applies them to the hadiths of the 'Book of Predestination' in al-Bukhari's Sahih. {cite:ajiss-review-authentication-of-hadith} The reviewer, Ahmed M. Hashim, judges the moderation criterion subjective and short of a rigorous method, while acknowledging the importance of the book's subject. {cite:ajiss-review-authentication-of-hadith}
