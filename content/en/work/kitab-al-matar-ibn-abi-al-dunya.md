## Summary
"Kitab al-Matar wa-l-ra'd wa-l-barq wa-l-rih" ("The Book of Rain, Thunder, Lightning and Wind") by [[person/ibn-abi-l-dunya|Ibn Abi al-Dunya]], who lived from 208 to 281 AH according to Brockelmann, is quoted a few times by [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] in the many chapters of [[work/al-haya-al-saniyya-suyuti|al-Hay'a al-saniyya]] on rain, clouds, thunder and lightning. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 62}

## Manuscript
The book is very rare; Heinen used a copy in the Köprülü library (no. 388/3, ff. 49v–74r), apparently copied in 563 AH, which he believed to be the only one extant. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 62}

## Character
Heinen notes that al-Suyuti passed over the great meteorological authorities of Ibn Abi al-Dunya's day, such as al-Kindi and Ibn Qutayba, choosing Ibn Abi al-Dunya because he was interested in natural phenomena only as far as they had entered the religious tradition of the Muslim community. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 63} Therein lies its originality for Heinen: it preserves elements of the earliest Muslim generations' knowledge of the weather, and the Prophet's attitudes in times of drought, storm and thunder. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 63}
