## Summary

A question from a trader offered insurance by a company on the debts other traders owe him in return for an annual percentage, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that insuring debts is unobjectionable if the insurance is cooperative and the debt arises from a lawful transaction, and not permitted if the insurance is commercial. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4028}

## The question

A trader owed money by a number of other traders asked about a company's offer to insure these receivables, covering 90% of a debt's value if the traders do not pay, in return for an annual percentage he pays the company. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4028}

## The answer

The Department answered that the ruling on insuring debts depends on the type of insurance: if it is commercial, dealing in it is forbidden, because commercial insurance is forbidden in the view of the majority of jurists and the fiqh academies, as a contract built on gambling. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4028} It said that if the insurance is Islamic cooperative insurance, dealing in it is permitted, because it rests on mutual guarantee, cooperation and donation among the members of the fund, which is the view of most fiqh academies and contemporary fatwa bodies. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4028} It concluded that insuring debts is unobjectionable if the insurance is cooperative, since the risk of late payment or non-payment is one that may be insured against, provided the debt arises from a lawful transaction, and that it is not permitted if the insurance is commercial. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4028}
