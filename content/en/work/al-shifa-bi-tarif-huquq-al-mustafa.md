## Summary
*Al-Shifa bi-Ta'rif Huquq al-Mustafa* ("The Cure: Making Known the Rights of the Chosen One") is the book of [[person/al-qadi-iyyad|Qadi Iyad ibn Musa al-Yahsubi]], judge of Ceuta (d. 544 AH), on the Prophet's standing, the reverence and honour his community owes him, and the ruling on one who falls short of that due or disparages him. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.11} Ibn al-Imad reports that nothing like it had been written before. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.619} His biographer in *al-Dibaj al-Mudhhab* says Iyad showed full originality in it, his peers conceded that it was his alone, people carried it from him and "its copies flew east and west". {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.4}

## Why it was written
In his preface Iyad says that a questioner asked him repeatedly for a collection making known the Prophet's standing, the reverence and honour due to him and the ruling on one who does not fulfil that due, gathering what his predecessors and imams had said and illustrating it with cases and examples. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.11} He calls the task a hard climb, since it requires establishing principles and setting out fine points about what is necessary, impossible or possible for the Prophet, and about prophethood, messengership, love and intimate friendship (*khulla*). {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.11} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.12} He took it on in hope of reward, citing with his own chain to Abu Dawud the hadith threatening one who is asked about knowledge and hides it, and so set down "points that unveil the purpose", written in haste. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.13}

## Structure
He named the book *al-Shifa bi-Ta'rif Huquq al-Mustafa* and confined it to four parts. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.15}
- **Part One: the Most High's honouring of the Prophet in word and deed**, in four chapters: God's praise of him (ten sections); God's perfecting of his physical and moral qualities (twenty-seven sections); the sound and well-known reports on his standing with his Lord (twelve sections); and the miracles God showed at his hands. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.15} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.382} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.383} The chapter on his qualities has sections on the cleanliness of his body, the fullness of his intellect, his eloquence and noble lineage, then on forbearance, generosity, courage, modesty, good company and compassion. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.382}
- **Part Two: what people owe him**, in four chapters: the duty of faith in him, obeying him and following his Sunna; the necessity of loving him, its signs, the meaning of love and sincere counsel towards him; revering him and honouring his family and Companions; and the ruling on invoking blessings and peace on him, where and how. It closes with sections on visiting his grave and on what is due from one who enters his mosque. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.385} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.698} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.699}
- **Part Three: what is impossible for him and which human matters may befall him.** Iyad calls this part "the heart of the book and the kernel of the fruit of these chapters", with everything before it serving as its foundations. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.16} Its first chapter treats protection from error (*'isma*) in belief, speech and action, with sections on his forgetfulness, on the prophets' protection before prophethood and on the angels' protection; its second treats his worldly states and the human conditions, such as illness and sorcery, that may befall him. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.17} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.699} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.700}
- **Part Four: the legal rulings on one who disparages or reviles him**, in two chapters — what counts as reviling or disparagement, by allusion or explicitly, and the ruling on the reviler, his punishment, repentance and inheritance — closed by a third chapter on one who reviles God, His messengers, angels and books, and the Prophet's family and Companions. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.17} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.700} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.701}

## Method
Iyad narrates hadith with his own connected chains back to the compilers, for example: "Abu al-Walid Hisham ibn Ahmad the jurist told us, by my reading to him…", up to Abu Dawud. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.13} He adds the views of exegetes, theologians and jurists, reports their disagreements and then chooses. On miracles, he holds that signs of prophethood reported by single narrators join the certain ones when a Companion related them before a gathering who stayed silent, and that whoever calls these famous stories mere single reports does so for lack of reading in the reports. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.263} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.264}

## Some of its questions
- **The inimitability of the Qur'an**: he counts four aspects — the excellence of its composition and eloquence beyond the Arabs' habit, its wondrous arrangement unlike their forms of speech, its news of the unseen, and its accounts of past generations and earlier laws despite the Prophet's being unlettered — then lists further aspects such as the awe that seizes its hearer. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.265} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.271} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.275} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.279} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.281} See [[concept/ijaz-al-quran|the inimitability of the Qur'an]].
- **Sensory miracles**: sections on the splitting of the moon, water flowing from between his fingers, the multiplying of food, trees speaking, the yearning palm trunk, the healing of the sick and his answered prayers. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.383}
- **Protection from error**: he reports the Muslims' consensus that prophets are protected from grave sins and from concealing the message, sets out the disagreement over minor sins, and argues for their protection from those as well. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.527} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.538} See [[question/can-prophets-commit-minor-sins|Can prophets commit minor sins?]].
- **Reviling the Companions**: in his last section he reports that the well-known position of Malik's school on one who insults the Companions is discretionary, painful chastisement, that Malik distinguished between insulting the Prophet and insulting his Companions, and he cites views of Malik, Sahnun, Ibn Habib and others. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.691} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.692} See [[concept/sabb-al-sahaba|reviling the Companions]].

## The printing used here
We used the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya (Beirut) printing in two volumes, which carries at the foot of its pages [[work/muzil-al-khafa-an-alfaz-al-shifa|Muzil al-Khafa' an Alfaz al-Shifa']] by [[person/al-shamunni|Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Shumunni]] (d. 872 AH) and opens with a biography of Qadi Iyad copied from Ibn Farhun's *al-Dibaj al-Mudhhab*. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.2} {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.3} Iyad ends the book by saying he has met the condition he set himself and placed in it points "found strange and original" which he had found no one before him treating at length. {cite:al-shifa-ilmiyya-shumunni p.695}

## For young visitors
In the city of Ceuta, Qadi Iyad wrote a big book explaining how God honoured His Prophet Muhammad, and what Muslims owe him: loving him, following him, respecting him and sending blessings on him. He divided it into four parts, and it became one of the best-known books on the Prophet's life and character.
