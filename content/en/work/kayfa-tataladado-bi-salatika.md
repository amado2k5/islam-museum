## Summary

"How to Savour Prayer" (Kayfa tatalaththadhu bi-al-salat) is a video lecture series by Mishari al-Kharraz, held in the Assabile archive. The harvest register records thirty episodes; the count was not re-read on the series page for this entry. This entry covers the first three episodes, from their machine transcripts. In them the speaker moves through degrees of prayer: presence of heart, then understanding, then hope, then awe. Everything here is the speaker's own speech and is attributed to him. These three episodes contain few legal rulings and none is set out in the museum's voice. Anything that needs a source, or is read from an unclear line of the machine transcript, is marked as such.

## Episode 1: presence of heart and understanding

- The episode opens with a report that Jabir ibn Abd Allah transmits, which the speaker attributes to Abu Dawud's Sunan: two men were posted as guards on an expedition, one of the Muhajirun and one of the Ansar. The Ansari was praying when he was struck by arrows one after another, and did not break off his prayer until his companion noticed the blood and was astonished. The speaker names al-Albani in connection with the report's grading; the word that gives the grading is unclear in the transcript. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:01:00}
- The speaker takes the report as an example of a prayer so sweet to its performer that he does not want to end it, and calls prayer the sweetest worship in the world. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:01:30}
- He quotes Ibn al-Jawzi's description of the prayer of worshippers, in which the humble person's spirit is said to roam elsewhere. He first says this sweetness belonged to the early generations, then says that people today have tasted it too. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:02:30}
- He mentions a course with the same title that, he says, drew more than 25,000 attendees. He reports a survey in which, he says, more than 90 per cent of attendees said their prayer had changed; he says it rests on a documented study that he does not name in this passage. These are the speaker's figures (needs_source). {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:03:30}
- He reads out testimonies from some who took part in the survey: one regrets every prayer she had made before, and another says the evening prayer after the lecture was the finest of his life. He says he wanted viewers to taste what these people tasted. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:04:00}
- He speaks of keys to prayer and its secrets, and attributes to Ibn al-Qayyim the description of this sweetness as one of the secrets. He mentions a "magic key" to humility in prayer, which he promises to open in a later episode; in these three episodes he does not open it. The transcript gives the name as "Ibn Qayyim", read here as Ibn al-Qayyim; the word at 04:30 is unclear in the transcript. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:05:00}
- He speaks to a listener who carries pressures and worries, and says that prayer gives the heart rest. He reports that the Prophet asked Bilal to establish the prayer so that they might find rest in it. The speaker gives this report in his own words; its text is not repeated here. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:06:30}
- He says many people think humility in prayer means only that the mind does not wander. He treats not wandering as the first of the meanings, with deeper levels after it. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:08:00}
- He compares presence of heart with wandering of mind. He says the sweetness of presence is greater than that of wandering, and asks the worshipper to give ten minutes of each prayer to presence of heart instead of struggling with it all the time. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:09:00}
- He works out, by his own arithmetic, that five prayers of ten minutes each come to under an hour out of a day of about twenty-three hours. He then moves to a second level, understanding: the worshipper grasping what is said and what is done. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:10:00}
- He presents a scene in which a pillar in the mosque is said to "compete" with the worshipper, since the pillar glorifies God in its own way. He says that what the worshipper does, in glorifying and reciting, other creatures also do. He cites Qur'anic verses in this passage without naming the surah or verse number; they are not typed here, and no passage link is added. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:12:00}
- He says many people repeat the words of prayer without knowing their meanings, and promises to explain the meanings of phrases such as the tahiyyat, tasbih and hamd in later episodes. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:13:00}
- He addresses the person who says "I cannot" concentrate in prayer. He says God does not ask for humility from someone who cannot give it, and offers the example of a student who knows the answer but drifts during the exam. He cites a verse about striving without naming it here. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep01 00:15:00}

## Episode 2: hope

- The speaker mentions Abu Nuwas, whom he names as al-Hasan ibn Hani. He describes him in his early life as dissolute and a drinker of wine, and says people doubted that God would forgive him when he repented late in life. The verb describing how his life ended is unclear in the transcript. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:00:30}
- He recites verses that, he says, were found written on a sheet after Abu Nuwas died. He summarises them as expressing hope in God's pardon, and says that the hope of the good alone is not enough, since the sinner also seeks pardon. Where the sheet was found is unclear in the transcript. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:01:00}
- He moves to a third degree of prayer, hope, which he calls deeper than presence of heart and understanding: the worshipper hopes for God's mercy and that God will bring him near. He compares a prayer without feeling with a meeting with a friend, in which one feels joy and longing. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:02:30}
- He says that knowing God increases hope, and that whoever thinks well of God finds what he expects. He narrates a sacred report in which God says that He is as His servant thinks of Him, and says that these are God's words and not his own. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:04:30}
- He quotes Ibn al-Qayyim that the Lord has no grudge to settle against His servant so as to take pleasure in punishing him, and that His mercy outruns His anger. He cites a verse about God writing mercy upon Himself without naming it here. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:05:30}
- He invites the worshipper to try expecting mercy in prayer, and distinguishes hope from wishful thinking: hope goes with action, wishing does not. He cites verses on supplication, repentance and righteous action without naming them here. The phrase in which he draws this distinction at 07:30 is unclear in the transcript; the meaning written here is the one the context supports. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:07:30}
- He shows a filmed scene of a lioness in a Kenyan reserve that adopted a young gazelle. He says the filmmakers were astonished that the gazelle survived two weeks without milk. He attributes to Dr Mark Stanley, whom he describes as an expert on gazelles, the view that a gazelle cannot survive more than a few days without its mother's milk. These details of the scene and the expert are as the speaker tells them (needs_source). {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:11:00}
- The speaker says a lion then took the gazelle from its mother, and that the lioness could only watch. He says it is said that the lioness afterwards raised five more gazelles, but that this one was her longest adoption, and that the others lasted at most three days. These are the speaker's figures (needs_source). {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:13:00}
- He narrates a report in which the Prophet says that God has one hundred mercies, of which He sent down one, by which creatures show mercy to one another, and kept ninety-nine to show mercy to His servants on the Day of Resurrection. He says the two Shaykhs recorded it. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:14:00}
- He says God's mercy encompasses everything, and gives the example of a man who, he says, killed ninety-nine people and was still reached by God's mercy. He cites a verse about the breadth of mercy without naming it here. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:14:30}
- He closes by asking the worshipper to ask God for mercy and to enjoy hope in prayer, and promises the next episode. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep02 00:15:00}

## Episode 3: awe

- The speaker begins with a report he attributes to Ali ibn Abi Talib: when prayer time came, his face would change and he would tremble, and when asked why, he said that the time of the trust offered to the heavens and the earth had come. He cites a verse on the trust without naming it here. The speaker gives no chain for this report in the episode (needs_source). {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:00:00}
- He summarises the earlier episodes: presence of heart, then understanding, then hope; he introduces awe, which he describes as a kind of fear. He says the magic key has not yet been revealed and will come in later episodes. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:01:00}
- He attributes to Ibn al-Qayyim a distinction between three terms: fear (khawf) as flight from what is feared, fear with knowledge (khashya), and awe (hayba) as fear with knowledge and veneration. He cites a verse on fear of God and the learned without naming it here. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:02:00}
- He gives examples: a person may fear the Fire without standing in awe of it, but stands in awe of a father, because the feeling towards a father combines fear with veneration. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:02:30}
- He says that hope and fear are combined in our dealings with people: a debtor fears the debt yet hopes for leniency from a kind creditor, and a student fears a poor result yet hopes a kind teacher will pass him. He says the same should hold with the Creator. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:03:00}
- He recites part of a supplication he calls the master of forgiveness, and mentions another supplication in which the worshipper seeks refuge in God's pleasure from His displeasure and in His pardon from His punishment. He attributes to Ibn al-Qayyim the view that few know the tawhid and the return that these words contain. The text of both supplications is not repeated here. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:04:30}
- He says awe is the highest degree of fear, and attributes to Ibn al-Qayyim that it is the greatest fear when the majesty of God is mentioned. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:05:00}
- He narrates a report about the Night Journey in which the Prophet describes Gabriel, out of fear of God, as like a worn-out mat, and says Gabriel has six hundred wings (needs_source). {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:05:30}
- He attributes to Ibn al-Qayyim that some angels are in prostration or bowing from the time God created them until the Day of Resurrection, and that they confess they have not worshipped God as He deserves. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:06:00}
- He reports that Ali ibn al-Husayn, Zayn al-Abidin, would turn pale in his ablution, and when asked what was the matter, would say that he knows before whom he stands. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:06:30}
- He says fear of God grows for two reasons: the worshipper's knowledge of his Lord and his knowledge of himself. Knowledge of the Lord is that He is great, possessed of majesty, and that the whole creation runs by His order. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:07:00}
- On knowledge of the self, he says that a person knows his own deeds best, and cites a verse about a person being a witness against himself without naming it here. He says that whoever delays repentance delays it again and again. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:07:30}
- He describes an ancient town in Italy, which he dates to 74 CE and says a volcano destroyed. He gives figures that he presents as drawn from research: the event lasted about 19 hours; the ash buried people under about 75 feet; 80 bodies were found on the shore; the town lay buried for about 1,700 years; and an eruption in 1944 killed 13,000 people. These are the speaker's figures and have not been checked (needs_source). {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:11:30}
- He presents this destruction as a punishment for the town's conduct, which he describes in moral terms; those descriptions are not repeated here. He draws from it a lesson on fear of God. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:09:00}
- He relates that a man asked al-Hasan al-Basri about companions who frighten him until his heart nearly breaks. Al-Hasan answered that the company of one who frightens you until you are safe is better than the company of one who reassures you until the dangers come upon you. Before this, the speaker says that every fear is painful except fear of God, which leads to Paradise. {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-mishary-al-kharraz kayfa-tataladado-bi-salatika/ep03 00:15:00}
