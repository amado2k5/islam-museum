## Summary
Al-Burhan fi Ulum al-Qur'an ("The Proof in the Qur'anic Sciences") is [[person/badr-al-din-al-zarkashi|Badr al-Din al-Zarkashi]]'s (745–794 AH) book on [[concept/ulum-al-quran|the Qur'anic sciences]], gathering forty-seven of their types. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.2} {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.19} {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.21} The editor of the Dar al-Hadith edition calls it broader and more comprehensive than al-Itqan and Manahil al-Irfan, and says everything written after it drew on it and followed its path. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.5}

## Why it was written
In his preface al-Zarkashi says the Qur'anic sciences cannot be bounded nor its meanings exhausted, so they must be served as far as possible, and that earlier scholars had missed writing a book gathering the types of its sciences, as people had done for hadith; so he sought God's guidance in writing a book collecting what had been said in its disciplines, to be a key to its doors and a help to the exegete, and named it al-Burhan fi Ulum al-Qur'an. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.18} {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.19} He says that anyone who tried to exhaust any one of its types would spend his life on it, so he kept to the roots of each type and pointed to some of its sections. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.21} He opened the book with al-Qadi Abu Bakr ibn al-Arabi's words on the number of the Qur'anic sciences, as Muhammad Rizq Satur notes in his foreword; see [[question/does-the-quran-contain-every-science|Does the Qur'an contain the roots of every science?]]. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.11}

## Its types
Al-Zarkashi lists the types at the start of the book: first the occasions of revelation, then the connections between verses, verse endings, words of several senses, the ambiguous, the unnamed, the secrets of the openings, the endings of suras, Meccan and Medinan, the first revealed, how it was sent down, its collection and the Companions who memorised it, its rare words, the grounds of the readings, pausing and starting, the script, its merits, the etiquette of recitation, its parables, its argument, abrogation, the clear and the ambiguous, the ruling on the ambiguous verses about the attributes, inimitability, the necessity of its mass transmission, the Sunna's support of the Book, literal and figurative, allusion and innuendo, the Qur'an's styles, and last the particles. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.19} {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.20} {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.21}

## Its influence on al-Itqan
An Alexandria University MA thesis finds al-Zarkashi's influence plainly visible in [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]]'s [[work/al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran|al-Itqan]], which built most of its chapters on al-Burhan, and calls al-Zarkashi "the author of the first complete work on the exegete's learning". {cite:thaqafat-al-mufassir-zarkashi-alexandria p.3} {cite:thaqafat-al-mufassir-zarkashi-alexandria p.142} {cite:thaqafat-al-mufassir-zarkashi-alexandria p.143} How to describe that relation is debated; see [[question/is-al-itqan-an-abridgement-of-al-burhan|Is al-Suyuti's al-Itqan an abridgement of al-Zarkashi's al-Burhan?]].

## The edition used
The museum used Dar al-Hadith's Cairo edition (1427 AH / 2006) in one volume, edited by Abu al-Fadl al-Dimyati Ahmad ibn Ali on a complete manuscript from the microfilms of the Arab League manuscripts institute, with additions from Muhammad Abu al-Fadl Ibrahim's Dar al-Turath edition. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.4} {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.6} {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.7} The editor says earlier printings had passed on corruptions and gaps of up to a full page, and that the headings of types twenty-three and twenty-four had swapped places in them. {cite:burhan-dar-al-hadith-2006 p.6}
