## Summary

The sixth session continues the account of the first revelation in the cave of Hira, reading the report of Aisha in Sahih al-Bukhari with commentary from Ibn Hajar, Ibn Kathir and al-Suhayli. It treats the meaning of Jibril's coming, the severity of the first encounter, the scholars' views on the Prophet's fear, and Khadija's three-part response, ending with Waraqa ibn Nawfal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### Recap and the wisdom of preparation before revelation

- He recalls that the previous session covered the start of revelation, the report of Ubayd ibn Umayr transmitted by Ibn Ishaq and the report of Aisha in al-Bukhari, but only up to the Prophet's retreat. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He holds that God, though able to do anything, prepared prophets through gradual means suited to human nature, and compares the upbringing of Musa under Pharaoh's threat. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He counts the preparations of the Prophet as true dreams, the greeting of stones and trees, the retreat, and earlier events such as shepherding and the opening of the chest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He says he will keep returning to the theme of causes and of taking means throughout the series, and that true dreams remained present in the Prophet's life to its end. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### Jibril, the Spirit and the Namus

- He explains, following Ibn Hajar, that the word for the truth that came to him means the angel, and that a parallel version in the book of exegesis within al-Bukhari uses a related word meaning suddenly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates that Jibril is the Holy Spirit and the Trustworthy Spirit who brought revelation to the prophets, and that Waraqa used the word Namus, meaning keeper of the secret, for the same angel who came to Musa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He notes that surat al-Takwir, an early surah, praises Jibril and that these verses served to reassure the Prophet and the believers and to answer the polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He says he will examine the Prophet's relationship with Jibril later in the series. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### Aisha's report on seeing Jibril and the number of sightings

- He relates a version through Abu al-Aswad from Urwa from Aisha in which Jibril first appeared at Ajyad, and notes Ibn Hajar's remark that its chain includes Ibn Lahi'a, who is weak. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates from Ibn Hajar that another report from Aisha in Sahih Muslim says the Prophet saw Jibril in his true form only twice, and that scholars differ over two or three sightings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates that a report through Ibn Mas'ud places the two sightings at the Prophet's request and at the Ascension, and that a report in al-Tirmidhi names the Lote Tree and Ajyad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He mentions the account compiled by Sulayman al-Taymi, in which Jibril taught the opening verses at Hira and then appeared in his form before the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### Day and month of the revelation, and the link with Musa

- He concludes that Jibril came to the Prophet in Hira on a blessed night, on a Monday and in Ramadan, linking this to the Prophet's reply on fasting Mondays. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He holds that the arrival of Jibril ended the stage of preparation and began the stage of prophethood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He observes parallels with Musa: Musa is the person named most often in the Qur'an, the first name the Prophet heard after the revelation was Musa through Waraqa, and both men felt fear at the start of their missions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### The severity of the first encounter: scholars' explanations

- He holds that the first encounter was unusually frightening and not repeated in that form, though later revelation also came with strain such as sweat on a cold day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates from Ibn Kathir that al-Khattabi explained the pressing as a test of the Prophet's endurance and a training for the burdens of prophethood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates that al-Suhayli saw in it a sign of seriousness, taking the Book with strength and leaving leisureliness, and he explains that leisureliness here means no blame for the Prophet but the need for resolve. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He mentions that the early judge Shurayh drew from the three pressings a rule about striking pupils, and calls that deduction remote. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### Wording of the pressing and the sources of the series

- He relates from al-Suhayli and Ibn Hajar the variant words for the pressing and their shared meaning of squeezing and choking, and the explanation of the phrase about reaching the limit of effort with its two readings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He says the series will attend to language because it follows Sirat Ibn Hisham, which explains rare words, and will draw on al-Suhayli and Ibn Kathir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### The first five verses revealed

- He holds that the first five verses state great truths without detailing the mission, its audience or its content. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He suggests a fitting link between the themes of creation and teaching in those verses and the nature of prophethood, which brings people from spiritual death to life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### The Prophet's fear: the twelve views counted by Ibn Hajar

- He relates that the Prophet returned trembling to Khadija, asked to be covered, and said he feared for himself, and that the object of the fear is not stated. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar listed twelve views, among them madness, a passing thought, death from terror, illness, lasting illness, inability to bear prophethood, inability to look at the angel, impatience with harm, being killed, leaving the homeland, being called a liar and being reproached. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar judged death from terror, illness and lasting illness the most sound, and that he rejected the first views, with Ibn al-Arabi rejecting the claim of madness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates al-Isma'ili's view, quoted by al-Suhayli, that the fear came before certain knowledge that the visitor was an angel, and further views that he feared weakness or being killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He prefers to leave the phrase in its general form, as the Prophet himself did not specify it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The report of Sulayman al-Taymi and the question of early opposition

- He relates, through Ibn Kathir, that a report in al-Taymi's biography has Khadija consult Waraqa and a monk, mentions Bahira and the revelation of the opening of surat al-Qalam, and records that Waraqa asked about the angel's appearance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He holds that this report is a mursal report, which cannot establish particular details, though some of its phrases may be taken for support. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He says the report suggests that opposition and talk began early, and that the order in which the polytheists learned of the Prophet and began to oppose him is unclear in many sira books, which he intends to clarify. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He notes scholars differ on whether surat al-Duha came after the pause of revelation, and says he will return to it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Khadija's three stances

- He holds that Khadija acted as a woman of full intellect and that her conduct was a form of divine support for the Prophet, and that she stood by him throughout her life in the period of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He counts three stances in this event: she did not question him but covered him until the fear left, she reassured him that God would never disgrace him, and she took him to Waraqa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He notes that Aisha, who said she was never as jealous of anyone as of Khadija, is the one who transmits these accounts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He explains that the Prophet was shaken, and quotes explanations from Ibn Kathir of words for his trembling. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### Khadija's reassurance and the Prophet's character before the mission

- He holds that Khadija understood the Prophet's words and answered with a reassurance backed by a detailed list of his habits, not just a general promise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates the qualities she named: keeping kinship ties, bearing the burdens of others, giving to the needy, honouring guests and helping those struck by genuine misfortune, with truthful speech and honouring trusts mentioned in other versions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates from Ibn Kathir that she reasoned from the way God treats people of good character, and from Ibn Hajar that her list covers all kinds of kindness, to kin and strangers, by body and by wealth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He says the list sums up the Prophet's life before the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

### Waraqa ibn Nawfal and his words

- He relates that Khadija took the Prophet to Waraqa ibn Nawfal, a Christian convert of the pre-Islamic period who knew reports of a coming prophet and was an old man. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates Ibn Kathir's remark that Waraqa named Musa rather than Isa because the law of Isa completed that of Musa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates that Waraqa wished to be a young man alive when the Prophet's people drive him out, and that he said no one has brought what the Prophet brought without being met with hostility. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He holds that Waraqa chose expulsion among the three outcomes the Qur'an describes, namely imprisonment, killing or expulsion, and that this may have come from scriptural reports about the final prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates from al-Suhayli that leaving the homeland is hard on the soul, and says the three outcomes are the pattern of tyrants towards reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Waraqa's death and reports about his standing

- He relates that Waraqa died soon after this meeting, and quotes Ibn Kathir that his words show belief in what he had learned and a good intention for the future. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates a report through Ibn Lahi'a from Urwa from Aisha about the Prophet's dream of Waraqa in white clothes, and notes that al-Zuhri and Hisham transmit it from Urwa as mursal, making that the stronger form. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
- He relates a report in Abu Ya'la from Jabir through Mujalid and al-Sha'bi about the standing of Waraqa, Zayd ibn Amr, Abu Talib and Khadija, which Ibn Kathir calls acceptable and he describes as weak but admissible in this context. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep6}
