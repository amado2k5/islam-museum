## Summary

A question on a father taking back a gift he made to his child, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that a father may not take back a gift to a close blood relative unless it creates unjustified favouritism, the position the law adopts, while noting that the Shafi'is allow it under conditions. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3867}

## The question

The questioner asked about the ruling on a father taking back a gift he made to his child. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3867}

## The answer

The Department answered that a gift becomes binding on delivery and may not be taken back afterwards, citing the hadith comparing someone who takes back a gift to a dog returning to its vomit. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3867} It said the Shafi'is make an exception for a father's gift to his child, quoting [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]]'s commentary on Sahih Muslim and his Minhaj al-Talibin that the condition is that the gift remains under the child's control. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3867} The Hanafis bar taking back a gift to a close blood relative, including a father's gift to his child; it quoted [[person/shams-al-aimma-al-sarakhsi|al-Sarakhsi]]'s al-Mabsut, article 866 of the Majalla and its commentary Durar al-Hukkam, and noted that Jordan's Civil Code adopts this in article 579. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3867} It concluded that a father may not take back a gift to a close blood relative unless it creates unjustified favouritism among them, the view the law adopts, while the Shafi'is allow a return between ascendants and descendants under conditions. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3867}
