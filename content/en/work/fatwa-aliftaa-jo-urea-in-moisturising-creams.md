## Summary

A question about adding urea to moisturising creams, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that synthetic urea is pure and may be used, while organic urea extracted from urine is impure unless it has been transformed, and may be used as medicine when there is no alternative. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3903}

## The question

The questioner asked about the ruling on adding urea to moisturising creams. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3903}

## The answer

The Department answered that urea is an organic compound produced by animal and human bodies and mostly excreted in urine, and that it is now made industrially and used in animal feed, fertilisers, medicines, plastics and other products. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3903} It said synthetic urea is pure because it is made by reacting ammonia gas with carbon dioxide under high pressure, and the rule is that things are pure in principle. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3903} It said organic urea taken from urine is impure because it is mixed with it, but may be used for treatment when no pure alternative exists, citing [[person/al-khatib-al-shirbini|al-Khatib al-Shirbini]]'s Mughni al-Muhtaj on treatment with impure substances when nothing pure is available. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3903} It said that if other substances are added that remove its properties and turn it into a different substance, it is judged pure by the Hanafis, who hold that impure things become pure through transformation (istihala), citing [[person/ibn-al-humam|Ibn al-Humam]]'s Fath al-Qadir. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3903} It concluded that there is no objection to synthetic urea in cosmetic and medical preparations, nor to organic urea once transformed, and that untransformed organic urea may be used for treatment provided there is no alternative and no harm, as judged by the authorities that oversee drug manufacture. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3903}
