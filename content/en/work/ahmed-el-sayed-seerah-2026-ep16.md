## Summary

The episode opens with a biography of the Badr veteran Bilal ibn Rabah from Sulayman al-Awda's book on the Badr fighters. It then reads from Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham on the harm suffered by the Prophet in Mecca, grades the chains of the reports, tells the conversion of Hamza and Utba ibn Rabi'a's offer, and begins the long report of Quraysh's demands and the three questions sent to the Jewish scholars of Medina, ending at the start of the exegesis of Surat al-Kahf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Biography of Bilal ibn Rabah from the Badr fighters series

- He relates, following Sulayman al-Awda's book on the Badr fighters, that Bilal was a freedman of Abu Bakr, born in the Sarat highlands, with a mother named Hamama who had belonged to a family of Banu Jumah; he notes the report that he was Abyssinian and the other that he was born in the Hijaz. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He says Bilal was among the earliest Muslims and among those tortured for his faith, and that Umayya ibn Khalaf was his tormentor, whom Bilal later killed at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Bilal kept repeating the single word of divine oneness under torture, and that this is firmly reported from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, in a report listing seven who first made their Islam public: the Prophet, Abu Bakr, Bilal, Suhayb, al-Miqdad, Ammar and his mother. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He observes that Umar, a noble of Quraysh, called Bilal a master of the Muslims in a report in al-Bukhari, which he reads as a great overturning of the tribal scale of worth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that those given the good news of Paradise in the Prophet's lifetime were more than the famous ten, and counts Bilal, Thabit ibn Qays and Abdullah ibn Salam among them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Bilal was the Prophet's muezzin and treasurer, took part in all the campaigns, served at Badr with distinction, and after the Prophet's death asked Abu Bakr and then Umar for leave to go on campaign; both urged him to stay, and Umar asked whom to give the call to prayer to, and Bilal named Sa'd al-Qaraz. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates from the author that Bilal went to Syria and died in Damascus in the year 20, aged a little over sixty, and was buried near the small gate, citing Siyar a'lam al-nubala for his last words on meeting the beloved ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Why the museum's Meccan chronology is hard, and the sources he follows

- He holds that the order of Meccan events is much harder to fix than the Medinan ones, where disputes are few and concern a campaign's year, order or a man's presence at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He considers knowing the Meccan order important because it helps in understanding the Qur'an, and conversely Qur'anic study can help to order some events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He counts Ibn Ishaq among the best arrangers of the Meccan period, while admitting that some stories may sit in a place that is not their most exact one; he also recommends a book that arranges events by years of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He raises the question whether the torture of Muslims came in one stage or in waves, and reasons that since the migration to Abyssinia came around the fifth year and the secret preaching lasted three years, some torture must precede it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He announces that the lecture follows Ibn Ishaq's order with comments. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### The Quraysh gathering at the Hijr and the strangling of the Prophet

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's report, through Urwa from Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As, that Quraysh nobles complained they had never endured so much from one man, and that the Prophet, passing them three times while circling the House, was jeered at, then warned them of slaughter, which silenced them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that on the next day they surrounded him, one man seized his cloak, and Abu Bakr stood in front of him weeping and protesting; Abdullah ibn Amr called it the worst he saw Quraysh do to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He explains that al-Bukhari has the second incident only, in which Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt put a cloth round the Prophet's neck while he prayed at the Kaaba and Abu Bakr pushed him away, and that the two chains meet at Urwa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He reads from the quarrelsome speech that the Quraysh had first been patient, and concludes that opposition grew by stages. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Kinds of harm in Mecca: physical, moral, hunger and the boycott

- He holds that the most severe harm of the Meccan years was denial and mockery, and recalls Abu Lahab following the Prophet at the market of Dhu al-Majaz, stoning his heels and calling him a liar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that God protected the Prophet from being killed, and contrasts this with Abu Jahl's failed plan against him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He counts four cases of physical harm he remembers from Mecca: stones thrown by Abu Lahab, the strangling by Uqba, the throwing of camel offal on him while prostrating at the Kaaba, and adds the report that people of Ta'if threw stones at him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that moral harm was harder on the Prophet than bodily harm, citing the Prophet's own answer about the worst he met, which concerned his appeal at Ta'if. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He adds the siege of the Shi'b as a fifth kind, since it brought hunger, and mentions that Hakim ibn Hizam smuggled food to Banu Hashim because Khadija was his aunt. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates a report in which the Prophet says he and Bilal went thirty days with almost no food, and says some scholars place it during his stay away from Mecca, such as the Ta'if trip, which he leaves to God's knowledge. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Grading the report from Umm Kulthum bint Abi Bakr, and the threat of slaughter

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's note from some of the family of Umm Kulthum bint Abi Bakr that Abu Bakr came home with part of his scalp torn from having his beard pulled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He grades it as weak, with unknown narrators and breaks in the chain, but supportive, since reports from within a family about its own events are often passed around at home and lighten some weakness without proving soundness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He reads the Prophet's warning of slaughter as meaning killing, citing al-Bayhaqi, and meets the objection that fighting was not yet known in Mecca by pointing to a verse in al-Muzzammil that mentions fighting, noting some scholars date that verse to Medina for that reason. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that the Quraysh fell silent because they knew in their hearts that he was truthful, and ties the warning to early Meccan verses of threat and prediction of defeat. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He treats it as a statement later borne out, since Quraysh leaders were killed afterwards. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### The conversion of Hamza

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's story that Abu Jahl insulted the Prophet at al-Safa, a freedwoman of Abdullah ibn Jud'an saw it, and Hamza, a hunter returning with his bow, was told of it, went to the mosque and struck Abu Jahl on the head, declaring himself on the Prophet's religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Banu Makhzum rose to help Abu Jahl but Abu Jahl told them to leave Hamza alone, and that after this Quraysh saw the Prophet strengthened and eased some of their harassment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that the story is lovely and fits Hamza's character but is hard to establish, since it rests on an unnamed man of Aslam, no connected chain is known, and later books such as al-Bayhaqi rely on Ibn Ishaq alone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He says that whatever its details, Hamza's Islam was a source of strength for the Prophet and the Muslims, and he looks forward to Hamza's role at Badr and Uhud and the Prophet's deep grief at his death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Utba ibn Rabi'a's offer to the Prophet

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's report that after Hamza's conversion, Utba ibn Rabi'a offered to speak to the Prophet and put proposals: wealth, leadership, kingship, or medical treatment if he was afflicted by a follower from the jinn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that the Prophet listened and then recited the opening of Surat Fussilat to him, which Utba heard silently, and that Utba returned to say he had heard words unlike anything, neither poetry nor sorcery nor soothsaying, and advised Quraysh to leave him alone, which they rejected as him having been bewitched. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that Ibn Ishaq places the story here because of the phrase tying it to Hamza's conversion, which suggests Fussilat was an early sura, and he cannot be sure of this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He judges Utba's advice wise, but says in the end his pride of the pre-Islamic age overcame him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Quraysh's demands at the Kaaba and the Qur'anic echoes

- He introduces a long report of Ibn Ishaq, via Ibn Abbas through Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ikrima, as one he cannot rely on in detail but accepts for background, since one part appears in al-Bukhari from Ibn Abbas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that a group of Quraysh leaders, among them Utba, Shayba, Abu Sufyan, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, Abu Jahl, al-Walid ibn al-Mughira and Umayya ibn Khalaf, met at the Kaaba after sunset and summoned the Prophet to argue with him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that they repeated Utba's offer, the Prophet answered he sought neither wealth nor rank and only conveyed his message, and he would endure until God judged between them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates their further demands: move the mountains, open springs, raise their dead ancestors such as Qusayy, then that he be given an angel, gardens, treasures, drop the sky upon them, and bring God and the angels before them; the Prophet refused each and referred the matter to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya, a cousin of the Prophet, then said he would never believe unless the Prophet climbed to heaven and returned with a book and four angels, and he adds that this man was with Abu Jahl at Abu Talib's death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He links each demand to verses in Surat al-Isra and Surat al-Furqan, and notes that the Prophet went home sad, and that he holds denial to be the harshest harm to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that al-Isra, al-Furqan and al-An'am, with al-Zukhruf, are key suras for understanding the Meccan debates, and says he will return to them in a dedicated session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Abu Jahl's stone, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, and their ends at Badr

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's story that Abu Jahl vowed to crush the Prophet's head with a stone while he prayed, but turned back terrified with his hands stiff on the stone, saying a huge camel stood between them; the Prophet said it was Jibril. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He notes that Ibn Ishaq says or as he said repeatedly, which he takes as a mark of his care. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that al-Nadr ibn al-Harith told Quraysh that Muhammad had been the most trusted among them in youth, and that they could not call him sorcerer, soothsayer, poet or madman, having known all four types; Ibn Ishaq calls al-Nadr one of Quraysh's devils who would tell Persian tales to rival the Prophet's gatherings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Ibn Abbas is reported to say eight verses came down about al-Nadr, including every verse mentioning the tales of the ancients. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He notes that al-Nadr, Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, Abu Jahl and Umayya ibn Khalaf all died at Badr, al-Nadr and Uqba being executed after capture, and he calls Abu Jahl the Pharaoh of this community. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Quraysh claims about angels, the resurrection and the Rahman

- He relates that some Quraysh said they worshipped the angels as God's daughters, and holds the Qur'an refutes this in several suras such as al-Saffat, al-Zukhruf, al-Isra and al-Najm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He holds that the denial of resurrection after death was a great obstacle for them, that the Qur'an answers it by pointing to the first creation and the revival of dead land, and that this denial lay behind their moral excess. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Quraysh claimed to know the Prophet was taught by a man in al-Yamama called al-Rahman and refused the name, and recalls that at Hudaybiyya Suhayl ibn Amr likewise refused the opening formula, which the Prophet accepted changing for the sake of the larger agreement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He suggests, with reservation, that the verse about a hidden veil may be early because it is linked to Abu Lahab's wife, whom he names as the visitor from whom the Prophet sought protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### The three questions sent to the Jewish scholars of Medina

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's report that Quraysh sent al-Nadr ibn al-Harith and Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt to the Jewish scholars of Medina, who told them to ask about three things: youths of the distant past, a world-travelling man, and the spirit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that the Prophet promised to answer the next day without saying if God wills, and that revelation then paused for fifteen nights, people in Mecca talked, and he grew distressed until Jibril brought Surat al-Kahf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates the sayings Ibn Ishaq reports that the Prophet asked Jibril why he had stayed away, and the answer of the verse that angels descend only by their Lord's command; he treats this phrase as weak, a transmission that begins with it was mentioned to me. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He cites other reports, one from al-Bukhari through Ibn Abbas that the Prophet asked Jibril to visit more often, and reports from Ikrima and Qatada, to show the verse's setting, while saying he does not rely on Ibn Ishaq's version in full. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Ibn Ishaq's exegesis of the opening of Surat al-Kahf and Ibn Hisham's glosses

- He points to the way Ibn Ishaq explains the opening of al-Kahf through the events of the sira, and holds this is a strength of the book. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's reading that the opening praises God for the book sent to the Prophet, confirms his prophethood, warns those who say God has a son, meaning Quraysh who worshipped angels as God's daughters, and consoles the Prophet over grief at their denial. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham glosses words, taking the term for consuming oneself to mean destroying oneself, and the term for earth, using lines of Dhu al-Rumma and al-Ajjaj, and he comments that ordinary readers would misunderstand one of the poetic lines. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq then turns to the youths question and the verse about the people of the cave, which he explains as one sign among God's greater ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
- He notes that Ibn Hisham usually follows Ibn Ishaq, stepping in only for language comments. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}

### Plan for the next session and verses ascribed to Hamza

- He says the next session will probably be a Qur'anic one linking the Qur'an and the sira, continuing the Kahf exegesis, the Isra and Furqan passages, and then Ibn Ishaq's section on the hostility of the polytheists to the weak Muslims, which leads on to the migration to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep16}
