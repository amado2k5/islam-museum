## Summary
Kitab al-Masahif by Ibn Abi Dawud al-Sijistani is one of al-Suyuti's sources in the Itqan for type 76, 'on the form of writing and the etiquette of writing it'. The notes of the King Fahd edition refer to the book by the numbers of its reports and credit it with reports on disliking a mushaf written mashq, on the saying that only a man of Mudar should write mushafs, on disliking dotting, division into tens and the openings and closings, and on some early authorities allowing dotting and vowelling. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2243} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2244} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2246} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2248}

## Examples from al-Suyuti
He reports from Ibn Sirin that he disliked a mushaf written mashq because it brings a defect. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2243} He reports from Ibn Mas'ud that only a man of Mudar should write mushafs, and Ibn Abi Dawud comments that this is because of the most eloquent dialects. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2244} He reports from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir that they knew nothing introduced into the mushafs except the three dots at the heads of verses, and from al-Nakha'i dislike of the tens, the openings and making the mushaf small. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2245} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2246} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2247} And from al-Hasan and Ibn Sirin that there is no harm in dotting mushafs, and from Rabi'a that there is none in vowelling them. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2248}

## Other books with the same title
The editors say Ibn Ashta wrote a Kitab al-Masahif that is lost as far as they know, and that Ibn al-Anbari wrote one with the same title, also lost. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 62} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 286} The Fihrist, in its list of books on the differences of the mushafs, names many authors, among them al-Kisa'i, Khalaf and al-Farra', and includes a 'Kitab Ikhtilaf al-Masahif' under the name Abu Dawud al-Sijistani. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 66} This record does not decide whether that is the same book.

## What was not read
No copy of the Kitab al-Masahif itself was read; this record rests on what the Itqan and its notes present, and the report numbers are those of the editor on whom the King Fahd edition relies.
