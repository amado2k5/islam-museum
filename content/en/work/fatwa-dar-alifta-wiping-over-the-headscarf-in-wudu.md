## Summary

A question about a woman who finds it hard at work to remove her headscarf for ablution, in which [[group/dar-al-ifta-al-misriyya|Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya]] answered that the obligation is to convey moisture to any part of the head, even a single hair, which she can do by wiping the front of her head from under the scarf; she should not be content with wiping over the scarf unless the moisture passes through it to the hair, in which case it suffices, and it set out the schools' disagreement on wiping over the scarf. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011}

## The question

The questioner asked about a woman at work who finds it hard to remove her headscarf when performing ablution: may she wipe over it instead of wiping her head? {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011}

## The answer

Dar al-Ifta began with the jurists' consensus that wiping the head is, in general, an essential element of ablution by the text of [[passage/quran-5-6|al-Ma'ida 6]], quoting [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Ibn Abd al-Barr]] in [[work/al-tamhid-ibn-abd-al-barr|al-Tamhid]] (4/31) on their agreement that wiping the head is obligatory, and [[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|Ibn Qudama]] in [[work/al-mughni|al-Mughni]] (1/92) that there is no disagreement on it. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011} It then noted that the jurists differ on the amount that must be wiped, and that the fatwa follows the Shafi'i view that wiping is achieved by passing a wet hand over part of the head, even one hair or part of one within its bounds, while wiping hair that hangs below the head does not suffice, as [[person/al-khatib-al-shirbini|al-Khatib al-Shirbini]] says in [[work/mughni-al-muhtaj|Mughni al-Muhtaj]] (1/176). {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011}

It ruled that wiping only over the scarf does not make ablution valid according to the Hanafis, the Malikis and the Hanbalis in one report, this being also the view of [[person/nafi-mawla-ibn-umar|Nafi]], [[person/ibrahim-al-nakhai|al-Nakha'i]], [[person/hammad-ibn-abi-sulayman|Hammad ibn Abi Sulayman]], al-Awza'i and [[person/said-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz]], because the commanded wiping of the head is not achieved and because a scarf, like a cap, is not normally hard to remove; the Shafi'is agree when the moisture of the hand does not reach the hair, but if the woman places her wet hand on the scarf and the moisture reaches the hair, that suffices on the correct view. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011} It cited [[person/shams-al-aimma-al-sarakhsi|al-Sarakhsi]] in [[work/al-mabsut-sarakhsi|al-Mabsut]] (1/101) that a woman does not wipe over her scarf; [[work/al-mudawwana-al-kubra|al-Mudawwana]] (1/124) that a woman who wiped over her scarf repeats her prayer and ablution; [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] in [[work/al-majmu-sharh-al-muhadhdhab|al-Majmu']] (1/409) reporting [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]]'s words in al-Buwayti that she puts her hand under her scarf so that the wiping falls on the hair, and that the correct view is sufficiency when the moisture reaches it; and Ibn Qudama in al-Mughni (1/222) on the two Hanbali reports and [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]]'s statement that she wipes from under the scarf. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011} It said that a woman who finds it hard in her workplace to remove her covering may content herself with wiping the front of her head from under the scarf as far as her hand reaches, which is what [[person/aisha|Aisha]] used to do, putting her hand under her wrap, as [[person/al-bayhaqi-abu-bakr-ahmad-ibn-al-husayn|al-Bayhaqi]] records in al-Sunan al-Kubra, and what [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-abi-layla|Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla]] and [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata]] said, as reported by [[person/ibn-al-mundhir-al-naysaburi|Ibn al-Mundhir]] in al-Awsat (1/470). {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011}

Dar al-Ifta then noted that the Hanbali school proper and [[person/ibn-hazm|Ibn Hazm]] allow wiping over the scarf, the basis being the hadith of [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal]] in [[work/sahih-muslim|Sahih Muslim]] that the Prophet wiped over the khuffs and the khimar, meaning the turban; it quoted Ibn Muflih in [[work/al-furu-fi-al-fiqh-al-hanbali|al-Furu']] (1/204) that a woman wipes her head-covering wound under the throat, and Ibn Hazm in [[work/al-muhalla-ibn-hazm|al-Muhalla]] (1/303) that wiping over anything worn on the head suffices, and noted that this is reported from [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama]] in Ibn Abi Shayba's Musannaf and from [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan]]. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011} It explained that those who hold it valid require the scarf to be wound under the throat and worn in a state of full purity with water, within the period for wiping over khuffs, a day and a night for the resident and three days for the traveller, as in Shams al-Din ibn Qudama's [[work/al-sharh-al-kabir-ala-al-muqni|al-Sharh al-Kabir]] (1/168) and [[person/al-buhuti-mansur-ibn-yunus|al-Buhuti]]'s [[work/kashshaf-al-qina|Kashshaf al-Qina']] (1/113), which supports the condition of purity with the hadiths of [[person/abu-bakra|Abu Bakra]] and [[person/al-mughira-ibn-shuba|al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba]], and that in this they agree with the position of [[person/abu-thawr|Abu Thawr]], as in al-Muhalla (1/309). {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011}

Dar al-Ifta concluded that the obligatory minimum in wiping the head is conveying moisture to any part of it, however small, which a woman can do by wiping the front of her head from under her scarf; she should not be content with wiping over the scarf but should wipe the hair itself as far as she can, and if she places her wet hand on the scarf and the moisture reaches the hair, that suffices and her ablution is valid. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9011}
