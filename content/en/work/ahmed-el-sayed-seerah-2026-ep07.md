## Summary

The seventh session finishes the account of the first revelation. He reflects on why the opening verses of the first sura revealed speak of God and of human need for the Creator, then studies the reports on Khadija's testing of the visitor and her merits, and Ibn Ishaq's account of the pause in revelation, with language notes by Ibn Hisham and al-Suhayli and the comments of Ibn Kathir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### The first verses speak of God and of human need

- He says that the five opening verses of the first revelation are all about God, his attributes and his acts, and that this should guide anyone who begins teaching or calling to the faith: knowledge of God comes first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that the first words sent to Musa, according to his account, were also a self-declaration of God, and he draws a parallel between the two prophets at the moment of prophethood, noting that Musa is the prophet named most often in the Qur'an and that Waraqa linked the visitor to the one who came to Musa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that these verses teach the creature's need of the Creator in two fields, creation and guidance, and that God made the human being able to learn through hearing, sight and intellect. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that the move of one single person from an embryonic clot to a learner and teacher is among the greatest signs, and the one people forget most. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that the stronger opinion is that the opening of the sura on the clot was revealed first, and that the opening of the sura of the cloaked one, held first by some Companions, is the weaker view. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that Ibn Taymiyya said the first verses made Muhammad a prophet and the later command to rise and warn made him a messenger, and that he mentions Ibn Taymiyya's long treatise on this sura in his collected fatwas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that a scholar he names as Ibn Ashur read a word in Aisha's report as showing that Muhammad had recited the verses; he is not sure of the attribution. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Al-Suhayli on love of homeland

- He relates that in Waraqa's words to Muhammad the only phrase that drew a reaction was the one about being driven out, and that al-Suhayli read this as a sign of attachment to one's homeland. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He says that al-Suhayli seems to have merged two versions of the report, since the exact phrase is not in Ibn Ishaq's text as the class read it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### Khadija tests the visitor: the report and its chains

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's report that Khadija asked Muhammad to tell her when his visitor came, had him sit successively on her left thigh, right thigh and in her lap, and then uncovered herself, at which point he no longer saw the visitor; she concluded that it was an angel and not a devil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that the first chain, through Isma'il ibn Abi Hakim, is a weak report with a long gap, since Khadija died in the Meccan period. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq heard a second version from Abdullah ibn Hasan, who had it from his mother Fatima bint al-Husayn, with the detail that the Prophet was put between her and her shift; he holds that this second chain is also interrupted but milder, and counts as an accepted type of mursal in the seerah because it comes from the family concerned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that together the two chains place the report in the third of the four grades he defined earlier in the series, while the first alone would be the fourth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He says that the report shows Khadija wanted extra assurance that the visitor was Jibril and that devils had no part in it, even though she had already believed him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that al-Bayhaqi, quoted by Ibn Kathir, explained her test as caution for her own faith, while the Prophet himself was already sure. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The family of the Prophet's line who transmit reports

- He explains that Abdullah ibn Hasan was a great-grandson of Ali through al-Hasan, father of the man known as al-Nafs al-Zakiyya, and that his family had long troubles with the Abbasids. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn is al-Baqir, son of Ali Zayn al-Abidin and father of Ja'far al-Sadiq, and holds that these three are leading figures of the Prophet's household and also imams recognised by Sunni scholars. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He says that the Twelver Shia count them among their twelve imams, and he calls that group by a hostile name. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### Khadija's standing in the Prophet's eyes

- He relates from the two Sahih collections that Aisha said she was never as jealous of any wife as of Khadija, who died about three years before the marriage, and that the Prophet was told to give Khadija glad tidings of a house in Paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that when Aisha spoke slightingly of the old woman Khadija, the Prophet answered with anger that no better wife had replaced her, since she believed him when people denied him, supported him with her wealth and bore his children. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that the praise given to Khadija is the same kind of praise given to Abu Bakr for belief in the early period, and that early support then meant belief and not fighting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that the Prophet sent meat and gifts to Khadija's close friends after her death and that Aisha was the main narrator of these merits, and he holds that this shows Aisha's honesty and that the Prophet's private life was fully open to public view, which only deepens love for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that Khadija's sister Hala once asked to enter and her voice startled the Prophet because it resembled Khadija's. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### Ibn Ishaq on Khadija and the house in Paradise

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says Khadija was the first to believe in God and his messenger and that she eased the Prophet's grief whenever people rejected him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates the report in Ibn Hisham's text that the Prophet was told to announce to Khadija a house of hollow pearl free of noise and toil, and that Jibril brought her greetings from her Lord, to which she replied that peace is from God and on Jibril. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that Ibn Hisham's own addition, from an unnamed trusted informant, is a very weak chain, but the substance is sound because it is narrated by a connected chain in the Sahih collections, which he reads out with their chains. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He says that scholars explained the phrase about no noise or toil in her house in Paradise as a reward matching her conduct in this life, and he relates al-Suhayli's version of that explanation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that Khadija's reply, which did not say that peace is upon God, shows advanced understanding. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### Best women and chains of the Sahih reports

- He relates the report, with chains through Hisham ibn Urwa, that the best women of her era are Maryam and Khadija, and he notes the debate on whether this means the people of Paradise or the world and the hereafter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He notes that al-Bukhari varies the chain and wording across the many versions of the merits of Khadija and does not repeat the same chain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates a report in Muslim that perfection among men was common but among women only Maryam and Asiya, and that Aisha's merit over women is like the merit of a dish of bread in broth over other foods. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He gives a sense of the role of chains in the Sahih books, saying that the chains themselves carry meaning for those who know the narrators. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### The start of the Qur'an in Ramadan and the course so far

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq places the start of revelation in Ramadan and ties it to the Badr encounter on the seventeenth of Ramadan, given by him as a Friday morning. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's summary that revelation then followed in succession, with the Prophet believing and bearing the burdens of prophethood, which only people of strength and resolve among the messengers can carry. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He recaps the series so far: the aims of studying the seerah, the chosen book, the Prophet's lineage and the importance of lineage, the pre-prophetic events including the Elephant year, the trade journeys, shepherding, the true dreams, the greeting of stone and tree, the marriage to Khadija, the rebuilding of the Kaaba, the seclusion, and then three sessions on revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He says that the next topics are who accepted Islam first, the start of the prayer duty and the early secret call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### The pause in revelation and the sura of the forenoon

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says the revelation paused for a time, which grieved the Prophet, and that Jibril then came with the sura of the forenoon. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that this is a detailed claim and not a general summary, since scholars differ over what was revealed after the pause, so it cannot rest on Ibn Ishaq alone, and that it is listed among the views on the matter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's explanations of the sura: that the Lord had not abandoned or hated him, that the hereafter is better than the first life, and that the verses recall God's favours in his orphanhood, need and prior unguided state. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq reads the commands in the sura as: do not oppress the orphan, do not rebuff the one who asks, and speak of God's blessing, meaning prophethood, which the Prophet did privately at first to those he trusted among his family. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### Ibn Hisham's language notes on the sura

- He relates that Ibn Hisham glosses a verb for the night growing still and cites a line of Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt and a line of Jarir about a calm eye. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham glosses the word for a needy person as poor, citing a line by Abu Khirash al-Hudhali, and adds other senses of the word: one who supports a family, one who wrongs, and something that overburdens. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that for the wrongdoing sense Ibn Hisham cites a line of Abu Talib, and for the burdensome sense a line of al-Farazdaq praising Sa'id ibn al-As, with the story that Marwan ibn al-Hakam envied the line and teased the poet about the wording. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that a student of the seerah must pass through such language, and that al-Suhayli is strong in language and corrects the philologists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### Al-Suhayli on the meaning of the divine name Peace

- He relates that al-Suhayli treats the name Peace, the one Khadija used in her reply to the greeting, and argues against most language scholars that salam and salama are not the same. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates al-Suhayli's argument that the feminine ending narrows the meaning, comparing pairs such as jalal and jalala, and rida and rida'a. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates al-Suhayli's rejection of the claim that the name means freedom from defects, and his view that the name means that all creation is safe from imbalance and from wrong coming from God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### How long the pause lasted

- He relates that al-Suhayli, after Ibn Ishaq gives no length, says some reports give two and a half years for the pause. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates the reconciling of Anas's ten years in Mecca with Ibn Abbas's thirteen: either the pause plus the six months of true dreams make three years, counted from the earlier point, or the figure follows a report from al-Sha'bi that an angel was assigned to the Prophet's prophethood for a period. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He mentions that Jabir's report counts ten years from when revelation grew intense and continued. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

### The report of grief during the pause

- He relates the report, in Aisha's long account and attributed to al-Zuhri with a phrase that he had heard it, that the Prophet grieved so much during the pause that he set out several times to throw himself from mountain peaks, and Jibril reassured him each time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that this part cannot be called sound, being an unattributed addition by al-Zuhri, even though al-Bukhari includes it, and that al-Bukhari did not give it a connected chain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He holds that it is still useful for its meaning, namely the depth of the Prophet's yearning for revelation, and that nobody is obliged to believe the detail. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Jabir's report and what came first

- He relates, following Ibn Kathir, the report of Jabir in the two Sahih collections about the Prophet seeing the angel who came to him at Hira seated on a throne between sky and earth, being frightened, asking to be covered, and the opening of the sura of the cloaked one being revealed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He explains Ibn Kathir's reading that Jabir and Abu Salama meant the first revelation after the pause, not the first absolutely, since the report itself says the angel had come before at Hira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates Ibn Kathir's rejection of the view that the sura of the forenoon came right after the pause, and his claim that it came after a second, short pause of a few nights, linked to a woman's taunt after the Prophet missed night prayer; Ibn Hajar names her as Umm Jamil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates Ibn Kathir's view that prophethood began with the first verses and the commission to the people with the command to rise and warn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir gave the pause as two to two and a half years, tied it to the angel Mika'il, citing al-Sha'bi, and holds this may be a slip of the name for Israfil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}
- He relates Ibn Kathir's list of the first to accept: Abu Bakr among free men, Ali among boys, Khadija among women, Zayd ibn Haritha among freed slaves, and that Waraqa believed and died during the pause. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep7}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.
