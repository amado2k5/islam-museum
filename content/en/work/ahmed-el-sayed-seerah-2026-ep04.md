## Summary

The fourth session closes the stage before the mission. He explains why pre-Islamic customs matter for reading the Qur'an and hadith, describes the jahiliyya system and what Islam kept of it, reads Hilf al-Fudul and the marriage to Khadija from Ibn Hisham with a grading of Ibn Ishaq's reports, and ends with the seekers of Abraham's religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Opening and recap: why pre-Islamic life matters

- He says this is the fourth session and that it closes the stage before the prophetic mission, with the descent of revelation due next time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He recalls the earlier sessions on the year of the elephant and the winter and summer journey, and holds that events before the mission matter because the Qur'an refers to them directly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He holds that some verses and reports cannot be understood without knowing what the Arabs and Quraysh did before Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Verses and reports that presuppose pre-Islamic custom

- He relates that Quraysh, who called themselves the Hums, would not stand at Arafat but moved on from Muzdalifa, and that this explains the wording of the verse about departing from where the people depart. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates a report from Jubayr ibn Mut'im who, searching for a lost camel, was surprised to see the Prophet standing at Arafat, taking him for one of the Hums. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He explains that the verse on Safa and Marwa lifts blame because some of the Ansar had previously called out to an idol, Manat, and later feared resemblance to the pagans; he mentions Isaf and Na'ila as two idols in another account. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He explains the Prophet's naming of Rajab of Mudar in the sacred-months report as settling a dispute between Rabi'a and Mudar over which month is the fourth sacred month. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He explains that the verse about taking adornment at every mosque arose because some Arabs, apart from the Hums, circled the House unclothed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He cites Ibn Abbas as saying that whoever wants to know the ignorance of the Arabs should read the part of Surat al-An'am after verse one hundred and thirty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### The meaning of jahiliyya and why it is named so

- He holds that jahiliyya is a time period with its social, belief, economic, political and divination systems, not the work of one tribe or merely a lack of knowledge. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He holds that the period was not known by this name before the mission and was named so by God after the reform came, since evil is understood by contrast with good. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He holds that the prophetic biography should not be studied only as a run of events, since one must also know the conditions that existed before. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Features of the jahiliyya system

- He lists burying of daughters, noting that an early sura refers to the girl buried alive, and the many forms of marriage that Aisha describes in a report in al-Bukhari, of which Islam kept only one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He lists cheating in weights and measures, tribal fanaticism and rejection of truth when it did not come through one's own tribe, and cites a saying attributed to Abu Jahl about rivalry between Banu Makhzum and Banu Hashim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He says diviners were not only fortune-tellers but judges and rulers to whom people referred disputes, and recalls the story of Abd al-Muttalib and Quraysh going to a woman diviner in the matter of Zamzam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He mentions divination by arrows and, at the head of everything, idolatry, noting the Prophet destroyed three hundred and sixty idols around the Kaaba at the conquest of Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He counts several reports on jahiliyya practices: a ban on a sale called the sale of the offspring of the offspring, the four things of jahiliyya (boasting of lineage, slander of lineages, seeking rain by stars, wailing), a statement of Umar on how women were regarded, the widow's mourning custom, and a statement of Abu Raja al-Utaridi on worshipping stones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates that in the farewell pilgrimage the Prophet declared the matters, bloodshed and usury of jahiliyya set aside, and reads this as the proclamation of the victory of Islam over jahiliyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### What the Prophet kept from jahiliyya: the pact reports

- He holds that the Prophet did not come to destroy every good thing, and contrasts this with revolutions that discard everything, giving the European break with the church as an example. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates a statement that the best of people in jahiliyya are the best in Islam if they gain understanding. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates a report in Sahih Muslim through Jubayr ibn Mut'im that there is no alliance in Islam while any alliance made in jahiliyya was only strengthened, and explains the apparent tension by saying alliances in support of the wronged were affirmed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He mentions that scholars reconciled this with the brotherhood between the emigrants and the Ansar, which he says is brotherhood, not an alliance in the old sense. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Reflection: the Qur'an as addressing every age

- He reflects that although the Prophet worked to dismantle the jahiliyya system, the Qur'an is read by later generations as speaking to them, unlike what he sees as the time-bound character of earlier scriptures. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He says many non-Muslims accept Islam after reading a translation, and mentions Ibrahim al-Sakran as speaking about this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He notes that the Prophet fought about twenty campaigns in ten years and sent about seventy expeditions, by reports he mentions, yet spoke of events to the end of time, and holds that combining a present struggle with timeless guidance is a sign from God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Hilf al-Fudul according to Ibn Hisham

- He reads from Ibn Hisham, narrating from Ibn Ishaq, that clans of Quraysh gathered in the house of Abdullah ibn Jud'an and agreed to stand with any wronged person in Mecca until his right was restored, and that Quraysh named it Hilf al-Fudul. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates a note by al-Qurtubi that the word fudul is a plural form of fadl. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates that the Prophet said he had witnessed a pact in the house of Ibn Jud'an which he would not exchange for red camels and would answer if invited to a like one in Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates the later story of al-Husayn ibn Ali threatening to call on the pact in a dispute with al-Walid ibn Utba, and of Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, al-Miswar ibn Makhrama and Abd al-Rahman ibn Uthman declaring they would stand with him, after which al-Walid gave way; he concludes the pact stayed alive in memory after Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Grading Ibn Ishaq's reports; the numbers of campaigns

- He recalls a five-level grading of Ibn Ishaq's reports he gave earlier, from chains with reliable narrators down to unattributed reports, and adds a split of the fifth level: detailed unattributed stories are the weakest, while summary information on the order of major events is valuable as the specialist's conclusion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He points out that al-Bukhari, who did not rely on Ibn Ishaq's hadith, opens his book of campaigns by citing Ibn Ishaq on the first campaign and then gives Zayd ibn Arqam's report of nineteen campaigns. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates from Ibn Hajar in Fath al-Bari that another report through Jabir gives twenty-one campaigns, and that Ibn Hajar thinks Zayd ibn Arqam missed two because he was young. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He holds the pact report is a mursal report from Talha ibn Abd Allah ibn Awf and a good one that can be taken as supporting evidence, and notes that the well-known prayer hadith of Arafat also comes through him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Meaning of the saying: no alliance in Islam

- He reads al-Qurtubi's view that jahiliyya allies backed each other even when wrong, which Islam voided, while pacts to support the right remain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He reads Ibn al-Qayyim's view that Islamic brotherhood makes alliance unnecessary, any pact against Islam is void and one consistent with it adds nothing, and that the pact the Prophet praised is probably Hilf al-Mutayyibin, though he notes the more likely view is Hilf al-Fudul, whose participants were the remnants of the Mutayyibun. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Marriage to Khadija, her age and the Prophet's children

- He holds that the details of the marriage story are used only as general support, not firmly established, though the marriage itself is certain, and he notes Ibn Ishaq uses a phrase signalling an unattributed report. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He reads from Ibn Hisham that the Prophet married Khadija at twenty-five, that she was a noble, wealthy woman who hired men in trade, and he reads her genealogy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He says there are two accounts of her age, forty and twenty-eight, and that Akram Diya al-Umari favours twenty-eight, partly because she bore six children. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's list of the children, with al-Qasim, al-Tahir and al-Tayyib dying in jahiliyya and the four daughters reaching Islam, and notes the other view that there were two sons, with Abd Allah titled al-Tahir and al-Tayyib; he mentions Ibn Hisham's order of birth and that Ibrahim's mother was a different woman. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Rebuilding the Kaaba and the Prophet's reputation for truthfulness

- He relates that when Quraysh disputed over placing the Black Stone, they agreed to accept the verdict of the first to enter, who was the Prophet, and he had a cloth held by each clan and set the stone himself; he adds that the story, if sound, prepared Quraysh and notes it has chains and reports he may treat another time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He holds that the Prophet was known among Quraysh as the trustworthy, and that the call to the people on the mountain, where they said they had never known him lie, rested on that standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He says Heraclius reasoned that one who did not lie about people would not lie about God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Seekers of Abraham's religion before the mission

- He reads Ibn Ishaq's account that four men of Quraysh, Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh, Uthman ibn al-Huwayrith and Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl, agreed in secret that their people had abandoned Abraham's religion and dispersed to seek it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates that Waraqa embraced Christianity, that Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh later became Muslim, migrated to Abyssinia with his wife Umm Habiba, then became Christian and died so, and that the Prophet later married Umm Habiba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates that Zayd ibn Amr abstained from idols, carcasses and blood, stood against his cousin al-Khattab's harassment, and travelled as far as Mosul seeking the religion, and cites a chain he calls sound through Asma bint Abi Bakr describing him leaning on the Kaaba declaring himself on Abraham's religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates that Zayd used to save girls from burial by raising them, and cites verses attributed to him rejecting al-Lat and al-Uzza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates that Sa'id ibn Zayd and Umar asked whether to seek forgiveness for Zayd, and that the reply was that he would be raised as a community by himself, noting the chain needs checking. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He notes the Bahira story at age twelve has weak chains and that some scholars call it rejected. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt and Quss ibn Sa'ida

- He relates that the Prophet called a line of the poet Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt the truest word a poet has said, adding that Umayya nearly became Muslim, and that Umayya's verses made a deep impression when a Companion recited a hundred of them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He relates a view found in some chained commentaries that Umayya is the man in the verse of the one who shed the signs, and that he reportedly lamented the Quraysh dead of Badr and did not accept Islam, which he reads as envy or denial. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He mentions Quss ibn Sa'ida al-Iyadi as another such figure and recites lines about the passing of earlier generations. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}

### Closing and the plan for the next session

- He says four sessions covered the main points about Arab conditions and the Prophet's life before the mission, not exhaustively. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
- He says the next session will begin the stage just before revelation, including the true dream and retreat at Hira, and will treat the reports of the stone and tree greeting the Prophet in Sahih Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep4}
