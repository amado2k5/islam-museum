## Summary

*Feminist Edges of the Qur'an* is a book by Aysha A. Hidayatullah, published by Oxford University Press in New York in May 2014 in 278 pages. {cite:openlibrary-feminist-edges-of-the-quran-2014} The catalogue describes it as a study and critique of feminist interpretation of the Qur'an that analyses the major feminist readings by Muslim women from the late twentieth century and traces their shared course in the emergence of feminist tafsir. {cite:openlibrary-feminist-edges-of-the-quran-2014}

## Publication and editions

According to a 2016 review in *sehepunkte*, the author examines the work of Riffat Hassan, Azizah al-Hibri, Amina Wadud, Asma Barlas and Kecia Ali, together with the South African scholar Sa'diyya Shaikh. {cite:sehepunkte-feminist-edges-review-2016} The review says the book has three parts: the first on historical background and the notion of 'feminist', the second on three interpretive methods, namely historical contextualisation, intratextual reading within the Qur'an and the tawhid paradigm. {cite:sehepunkte-feminist-edges-review-2016} In the third part the author reassesses these readings critically and speaks of a 'radical uncertainty'; the review notes that she draws on Nasr Hamid Abu Zayd's approach to the Qur'an as discourse. {cite:sehepunkte-feminist-edges-review-2016}
