## Summary
Al-Muhkam is the great dictionary of Ibn Sida al-Mursi; the author of the Mutammih praised it as the most wondrous and soundest of books, and as a work in which Ibn Sida stood alone. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.31}

## Details
Al-Maqqari records Ibn Sida, author of al-Muhkam, addressing Iqbal al-Dawla in verse: 'Is there no way to kiss your right hand, for in that is safety and the right hand?' — so said the Mutammih. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.31} Al-Maqqari, quoting the Mutammih in Nafh al-Tib, says the jurist Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ahmad, known as Ibn Sida, was an imam in language and Arabic and a leader in letters; he devoted himself to al-Muwaffaq, lord of Denia, where he attained his hopes and found leisure for scholarship, and he was singular in that retreat — above all in his book called al-Muhkam, which was the most wondrous and most perfect of books, on which al-Muwaffaq's touches adorned it and confirmed its brilliant passages. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.31}
