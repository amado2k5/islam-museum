## Summary
al-Muzhir fi Ulum al-Lugha wa-Anwa'iha is the book of [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] in which he set out to gather the sciences of language into ordered kinds, as the sciences of hadith had been ordered. This page presents what was read of its preface, the index of its fifty kinds, the opening passage from Ibn Faris and the first questions of the first kind. Each saying is ascribed to its holder, and what the reading did not reach is stated at the end.

## The aim and the order of the book
al-Suyuti begins by praising God, creator of tongues and languages, then describes his book as a noble science whose arrangement, kinds and chapters he devised, in the sciences of language, their kinds and the conditions of their delivery and hearing, and says that in it he imitated the hadith sciences in divisions and kinds; he says no one preceded him in this compilation, and that he named it al-Muzhir fi Ulum al-Lugha {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.7}. This is the author's description of his work, not the museum's testimony for it. The imitation shows in the naming of the kinds themselves: the first eight go back to language as regards the chain of transmission, namely knowing the sound, what was reported but not established, the mass-transmitted and the single report, the loose and the broken, the solitary, whose report is accepted and whose rejected, the ways of taking and bearing, and the fabricated {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.7}.

## The fifty kinds
After transmission he lists thirteen kinds that go back to language as regards words, the first being the eloquent, among them the regular and the irregular, the rare and strange, the unused and the used, language variants and their overlap and agreement, the borrowed, the Islamic words and the neologism {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.7-8}. Then thirteen as regards meaning, including the properties of language, derivation, literal and figurative, the shared word, the opposites, the synonym, substitution, inversion and word-blending {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.8}. Then five on the delicacies and salt of language, such as proverbs, riddling speech and puzzles {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.8-9}, one kind on likes and counterparts, and eight going back to the men of language and its transmitters, among them the manners of the philologist, the book of language, scribal slips, generations, births and deaths, then the kind of poetry and poets, and last the errors of the Arabs {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.9}. So there are fifty kinds, as the author himself counts them.

## Ibn Faris's passage at the head
Before the kinds al-Suyuti puts a passage by Ahmad ibn Faris from the beginning of his book on the jurisprudence of language, that is [[work/al-sahibi-fi-fiqh-al-lugha|al-Sahibi]]: the knowledge of the Arabs has a root and a branch; the branch is knowing names and attributes such as man, horse, tall and short, the root is the discourse on the setting down of language, its priority and origin, then the Arabs' customs in their addresses; people are of two sorts, one occupied with the branch and one who joins both, and this is the highest rank because by it the address of the Qur'an and the sunna is known {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.9}. Ibn Faris says in the same text that one who does not know rare words such as al-ashaq is not harmed, because he hardly finds any of them in the Book of God, and that if he did not know the breadth of the Arabs in their addresses he would be at a loss over much of the knowledge of the Book and the sunna {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.10}.

## The first kind: the definition of language and who set it down
In the first question of the first kind al-Suyuti says that for Ibn Jinni in [[work/al-khasais-ibn-jinni|al-Khasa'is]] the definition of language is sounds by which each people express their aims, and gives its morphology {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.11}; he then adds the definitions of Imam al-Haramayn, Ibn al-Hajib and al-Asnawi, and that Ibn al-Hajib said it is every word set for a meaning {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.12}. In the second question, on who set down language, whether tawqif or convention, he relays what Ibn Faris said that the language of the Arabs is tawqif, with his proofs {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.12-13}, then says Ibn Faris was of the Sunnis, and presents Ibn Jinni's view, saying that he and his teacher Abu Ali al-Farisi were Mu'tazilis, and relays the chapter of al-Khasa'is on the origin of language {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.14-15}. al-Suyuti here is a transmitter and an ascriber of schools; views of different schools come together on one page because his aim is to gather statements, and each statement should be ascribed to its speaker, not to its gatherer. See [[concept/asl-al-lugha|the origin of language]].

## What this page did not reach
Only the pages named were read, from the beginning of the first volume; the many kinds on the eloquent, the borrowed, derivation, proverbs and the generations of philologists were not. An earlier reading of this source took pages of the first volume on the origin of language and the tribes from whom Arabic was taken, which are not repeated here. Page numbers are those of the Shamela copy that follows the edition of Fu'ad Ali Mansur and were not compared with a paper copy. This page does not judge between the schools on the origin of language.
