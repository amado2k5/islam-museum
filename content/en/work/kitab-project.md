# KITAB project

## Summary

The KITAB project (Knowledge, Information Technology, and the Arabic Book) began as an effort to develop methods for detecting how authors copied from earlier works. Classical Arabic authors, its director the historian Sarah Bowen Savant writes, often drew on past works, cutting them into pieces and reassembling them for their own concerns, and the project set out to uncover the relationships between Arabic texts and the networks in which they circulated. {cite:kitab-project-about}

## Funding and results

It was funded by the European Research Council and the Qatar National Library; its results are in a web application, a blog, many data releases on Zenodo and publications, including two monographs the page says are due from Edinburgh University Press. {cite:kitab-project-about} The earliest archived capture of its site, of 12 September 2017, has pages on the project, its team, partners, corpus, a pilot text, its text-reuse methods and [[work/openiti|OpenITI]]. {cite:wayback-kitab-2017}

## KITAB-Transform

From January 2026 it continues as KITAB-Transform, on a new European Research Council grant, training models to find matches that share a meaning but differ in wording, aligning paraphrases and translations across the OpenITI corpus, and reaching from Arabic to Persian, with the hope of Urdu. {cite:kitab-project-about}

## Partners

Its main partner in computer science is David Smith of Northeastern University, and it is one of the partners of the OpenITI initiative, which builds the corpus it works on. Its director heads the Centre for Digital Humanities at the [[listing/aga-khan-university|Aga Khan University]]'s Institute for the Study of Muslim Civilisations. {cite:kitab-project-about}

## Today

The site is at kitab-project.org. {cite:kitab-project-about}
