## Summary
*Al-Musnad* — or *Sunan al-Darimi*, as it is known today — is the hadith book of [[person/al-darimi|Abu Muhammad 'Abd Allah b. 'Abd al-Rahman al-Darimi]] of Samarqand, arranged by topic. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 48-49} The editors of the Dar al-Ta'sil edition say they found no title given by al-Darimi himself; the name is known from the manuscripts, the biographical sources and catalogues. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 48}

## Opening with the proofs of prophethood
The editors quote [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]]: some compilers of topical Sunan opened with the root of knowledge and faith, as [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] opened his Sahih with the beginning of revelation, and al-Darimi opened his book with the proofs of prophethood, treating them well; Ibn Taymiyya holds these two men far superior to Muslim, al-Tirmidhi and the like, being jurists of hadith in principles and branches. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 32}

## High chains and the three-link hadiths
Al-Darimi was famed for high (short) chains; al-Dhahabi calls his book "the high Musnad", of the level of the *Muntakhab* of 'Abd b. Humayd's Musnad. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 37} This shows in his *thulathiyyat*: fifteen hadiths with only three men between him and the Prophet. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 37}

## His legal comments
Al-Darimi follows some hadiths with his own views, which the editors take as evidence that he reasoned for himself rather than following a school: {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 33-34}
- On *aqra'* (the waiting periods): the people of the Hijaz say they are the intervals of purity, the people of Iraq the menses — "and I say: the menses". {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 34}
- After Anas's report that they began the recitation with "Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds", he says he does not hold the basmala should be recited aloud. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 34}
- On one who eats or drinks forgetfully while fasting: the people of the Hijaz say he makes it up; he says he does not. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 34}
- On praying alone behind the row: he notes that [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad b. Hanbal]] upheld the hadith of 'Amr b. Murra, and says that he himself follows the hadith of Ziyad b. Abi al-Ja'd. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 34}

## The book's title
- **"Al-Musnad"** is its best-known name, used by al-Sam'ani, [[person/ibn-al-athir-al-jazri|Majd al-Din Ibn al-Athir]], Ibn al-Salah, al-Mundhiri, [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]], al-Dhahabi, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Hajar and others, and found in the Köprülü, Leiden and Süleymaniye manuscripts, with additions: "al-Musnad al-Sahih al-Jami'" in Köprülü, "al-Musnad al-Jami'" in Leiden and Murad Molla. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 48-49}
- Ibn Hajar found the name puzzling since the book is arranged by topic; al-'Iraqi objected to Ibn al-Salah's listing it among musnads arranged by Companion, explaining that it is called Musnad as al-Bukhari called his book "al-Musnad al-Jami' al-Sahih", because its hadiths have chains — though it contains many mursal, broken, doubly-broken and cut-off reports. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 49-50}
- **"Al-Sunan"** was used by al-'Ayni, Ibn Hajar, al-Qari, Hajji Khalifa and al-Kattani, became common in modern times and appears on most older printings, such as the Indian edition of al-Matba' al-Nizami, 1293 AH. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 51-52}
- **"Al-Jami'"** is mentioned only by al-Khatib al-Baghdadi and those who followed him; the editors think "al-Jami'" and "al-Musnad" describe one book. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 48}

## Is it a "Sahih"?
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Mughaltay and those who agreed**: a number of hadith masters called it "the Sahih", the last being Ibn Daqiq al-'Id; Ibn al-Qayyim and al-Bulqini used the name, and the Köprülü manuscript titles it "al-Musnad al-Sahih al-Jami'". {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 50-51}
- **Sunni: al-'Iraqi and Ibn Hajar**: al-'Iraqi holds its weak reports, from their narrators or their mursal transmission, to be plain; Ibn Hajar found no reliable scholar who said it, notes that the copy Mughaltay took for al-Mundhiri's hand has a first leaf written by Abu al-Hasan Ibn Abi al-Husni, and says that even had a reliable scholar called it so, its weak, broken and cut-off reports would contradict him. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 51}
- **Sunni: later comments**: [[person/muhammad-ibn-ismail-al-sanani|al-San'ani]] thought Ibn Hajar's answer did not fully refute Mughaltay, and the Dar al-Ta'sil editors add that Ibn Daqiq al-'Id, Ibn al-Qayyim and al-Bulqini did use the name. {cite:musnad-darimi-tasil-2015 p. 51}
:::
