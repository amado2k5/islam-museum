## Summary
*Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum: Bahth fi al-Sira al-Nabawiyya* is a biography of [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] by Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri that took first place in the [[award/muslim-world-league-sira-competition|Muslim World League's sira competition]]. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar} {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en} Its English translation is known as *The Sealed Nectar*, which is what the title means. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en}

## How it was written
The Muslim World League announced the competition at one of its conferences in Pakistan in 1396 AH, and writers from many countries took part. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar} {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en} The results were announced in Shaban 1398 AH: Mubarakpuri's study was first of the 171 submitted, and the prizes were handed over at the League's conference in Mecca in 1399 AH (1979). {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar} The League's secretariat then undertook to print the winning studies and publish them in several languages; this book was one of them, and edition followed edition. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar}

## What it contains
The book follows the events of the life in chronological order; where accounts differ, the author states the one he judges stronger with his evidence, and he names his sources with their page numbers. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en} Its four main parts are: Arabia before Islam, its land, peoples, rule, economy, religions and society; the Prophet's lineage, birth and upbringing; the life of prophethood and the call, in the Meccan and then the Medinan period with its campaigns and expeditions up to the Farewell Pilgrimage; and a final part on his death, his household, and his qualities and character. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar}

## Editions and translations
Arabic editions include Darussalam's of Riyadh in 2002 and one by Uli al-Nuha li-l-Intaj al-I'lami in 1422 AH, of 512 pages. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en} {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar} The author also wrote an abridgement, *Mukhtasar al-Raheeq al-Makhtum*, published in 1424 AH. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar} The English translation, *The Sealed Nectar*, was published by Darussalam, and Abdul Khaleq Rahmani translated it into Bengali in 530 pages. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en} The book has Wikipedia articles in fourteen languages, Urdu, Sindhi, Kurdish and Lezgian among them. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en}

## Reception
The sources describe the book as widely popular in the Muslim world, a reach noted by the scholar Sofia Rehman in a study published by Oxford University Press in 2024. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-en} Mubarakpuri himself wrote that the book met with an acceptance he had not hoped for when he wrote it. {cite:wikipedia-raheeq-ar}

## In the museum
The book is used as a source in a number of the museum's sira records, from a scanned copy whose page numbering has been checked. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
