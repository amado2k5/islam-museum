## Summary

A question about making animal feed by farming and breeding earthworms, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that feeding poultry and animals worms and insects is unobjectionable provided experts confirm it does not harm people, and set out the difference between the majority and the Shafi'is over dead ones. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4015}

## The question

The questioner asked about the ruling on making animal feed by farming and breeding earthworms. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4015}

## The answer

The Department answered that jurists agree there is no objection to feeding birds and animals live worms and insects, since they are pure, quoting [[person/muhammad-ibn-qasim-al-ghazzi|Ibn Qasim al-Ghazzi]] in Fath al-Qarib that every animal is pure except the dog, the pig and what is born of them, which includes worms born from impurity. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4015} It said that feeding them dead worms and insects is unobjectionable according to the majority of jurists, quoting [[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|Ibn Qudama]] in al-Mughni that creatures without flowing blood, such as worms, do not become impure by death, and noted that the Shafi'is consider it disliked. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4015} It said that adding worms to feed depends on the amount: if the feed is entirely or mostly made of worms it is disliked because of their impurity, quoting [[person/ibn-hajar-al-haytami|Ibn Hajar al-Haytami]] in Tuhfat al-Muhtaj that feeding an edible animal something impure is disliked; if the amount is small and the pure ingredients predominate, there is no objection. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4015} It concluded that feeding poultry and animals worms and insects is unobjectionable provided experts confirm it does not harm people; if harm is established, it becomes forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4015}

## About this record

Jordan's General Iftaa Department published this fatwa on aliftaa.jo as no. 4015, dated 23 October 2025. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4015} The museum records this text as a historical document attributed to the body or scholar that issued it; it issues no ruling of its own.
