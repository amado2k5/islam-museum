## Summary

Ahmed Elsayed covers the last stretch of the biography: the march to Hunayn and the flight and rally there, Awtas and the siege of Ta'if, the distribution of the spoils at al-Ji'rana and the Prophet's address to the Ansar, the year of the delegations, the expedition to Tabuk with the three who stayed behind, the declaration at the pilgrimage of year 9, the Prophet's farewell pilgrimage and sermon, and his final illness. He draws lessons for reformers on measuring truth by power, winning hearts, remembering one's past service, the sincerity of intention and the completion of the message. He leaves the account of the Prophet's death untold. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### After the conquest: tribes waiting for the outcome

- He says that the conquest of Mecca was the decisive step in a long struggle and that its effect reached the whole Arabian Peninsula, not only Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from a report of Amr ibn Salima that the Arab tribes held back from Islam until they saw how the contest between the Prophet and his people ended, and that each tribe hurried to accept Islam once it was settled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that this is a mistaken yardstick, one that earlier nations also used against their prophets, and that reformers must correct the standard by which people judge, since truth does not depend on power. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that the believers with Ibrahim and with Musa made the same supplication, that God not make them a trial for the unbelievers, and that scholars explain it as a request not to let the opponents prevail, lest they conclude they are in the right. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The march to Hunayn: Dhat Anwat and the night guard

- He says that after Mecca the Prophet turned to Hawazin and the people of Ta'if, the last power in Arabia still feared, who were already gathering against him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the army numbered ten thousand from Medina plus the Tulaqa, the Quraysh who had just accepted Islam and been pardoned, and that at the lowest estimate it reached twelve thousand. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that on the way some of the Tulaqa passed a tree where the pagans used to hang their weapons and asked the Prophet for a tree like it, and that he compared the request to the one the people of Musa made and said that people follow the ways of those before them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Umm Sulaym later asked the Prophet to kill the Tulaqa who fled at Hunayn and that he answered that God had sufficed and done well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that a man brought news that Thaqif had come out with their animals, women and armies, and that the Prophet camped for the night in a narrow valley and asked who would keep watch. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Anas ibn Abi Marthad al-Ghanawi volunteered, held the mouth of the pass on horseback until morning and saw nobody, and that the Prophet told him he had earned the reward and need not worry about doing more. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He notes that the hadith is in Sunan Abi Dawud and al-Hakim and that some scholars graded it sound, and he adds that those promised Paradise were among the most active in deeds. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The ambush at Hunayn, the flight and the rally

- He relates from Anas ibn Malik that Thaqif had drawn up their forces in a manner he had never seen, bringing their camels, sheep, women and wealth so that nobody could retreat. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that at dawn archers hidden behind rocks in a narrow valley loosed arrows on the Muslims, that the animals panicked and the ranks tangled, and that the Tulaqa were among the first to scatter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that fleeing in such a moment is a natural human reaction when those around one run, and that holding firm needs deep presence of mind. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet stayed on his white mule with Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith holding the bridle, and names al-Abbas, Abu Bakr, Umar and members of his household among those near him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith was the Prophet's cousin, a poet who had satirised him and whom Hassan ibn Thabit answered, and that he accepted Islam shortly before the conquest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that reports put those who held firm at about a hundred or fewer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet called out to the Ansar and told al-Abbas, who had a loud voice, to call the people of the tree under which they had pledged, and that they came back swiftly, as Anas describes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that about seven or eight hundred returned and fought, and that the Prophet remarked that the fighting had now grown fierce. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet advanced declaring that he was a true prophet and the son of Abd al-Muttalib, and that al-Bara ibn Azib said that in the hardest fighting the Muslims took shelter beside him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### Lessons of Hunayn and the victory

- He draws a first lesson that numbers are only a means and that relying on them is relying on a weak support, citing the verses on Hunayn, on the ratio in al-Anfal and on Talut in al-Baqara. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He draws a second lesson that what brought the Muslims back was a call that reminded them of a moment of sacrifice, and that a person's earlier stands for Islam help him stay firm in later crises. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that God sent down tranquillity and unseen forces and that the Prophet threw a handful of pebbles at the enemy as he had at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that al-Abbas said the enemy's edge was blunted after that throw, that Hawazin fled and left behind the spoils they had brought, and that the Muslims gathered them in a separate place. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### Awtas and the death of Abu Amir

- He says that the fugitives split up, some into the valleys and some back to Ta'if, and that the main army went to Ta'if while detachments were sent to Awtas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, in al-Bukhari and Muslim, that his uncle Abu Amir led the force to Awtas, that Durayd ibn al-Simma, an old commander whose counsel the enemy valued, was killed there and his men routed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that an archer struck Abu Amir in the knee, that Abu Musa chased the man down and killed him in a duel after shaming him for running, and that he drew the arrow out for his uncle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Abu Amir made Abu Musa commander and died soon after, and that the Prophet, told of this, prayed for Abu Musa's forgiveness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He observes that in Hunayn, Awtas and Ta'if arrows were the central danger. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The siege of Ta'if

- He says that the Prophet besieged Ta'if for forty days without taking it, while Thaqif shot arrows at the Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet announced that any slave of Ta'if who came out would be free, and that a group did, among them Abu Bakra, who got his nickname from climbing down the wall on a pulley. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that the Prophet said they would return, that his men objected, that he sent them to fight again and they were wounded, and that when he repeated that they would leave the next day they were pleased and he smiled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from al-Tirmidhi that when asked to curse Thaqif the Prophet prayed that God guide them, and he sees in this the Prophet's constant concern for others' guidance, set against the Muslims' fleeing from instinct. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He recalls that years earlier, after Ta'if had rejected him, the angel of the mountains offered to crush its people and the Prophet declined, hoping for believers from their descendants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The distribution of the spoils at al-Ji'rana

- He says that the Prophet went back to al-Ji'rana, near Mecca, where the spoils of Hunayn had been collected, and began to divide them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that the largest shares went to those who had fled and to leaders of the Tulaqa and the Bedouin, whom the Prophet wished to win over, each of the greatest receiving a hundred camels, while giving the Ansar what was greater in their eyes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Uyayna ibn Hisn and al-Aqra ibn Habis received a hundred each and Abbas ibn Mirdas less, and that Abbas protested in verse, as recorded in Sahih Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from Safwan ibn Umayya, in Sahih Muslim, that the Prophet kept giving him until the man he hated most became the one he loved most. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that winning hearts with money is a recognised and wise principle, shown by the share of zakat set aside for those whose hearts are to be reconciled, and that driving hearts away from Islam is therefore a grave matter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates a report that Thaqif later came to Medina as Muslims on condition that they neither give charity nor fight, and that the Prophet accepted, saying they would do both once they were Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The Ansar's feeling and the Prophet's address

- He relates that some of the Ansar felt hurt that they were called in danger yet others received the spoils, though they had fought and gathered the booty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that two men, whom he calls the origin of the Khawarij, accused the Prophet of injustice and of dividing the spoils without seeking God's face, and that the Prophet grew very angry and said that if he was not just nobody was. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from Ibn Mas'ud that the Prophet's face changed colour, and that the Prophet recalled Musa and said he had been hurt worse. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet gathered the Ansar alone in a tent, reminded them that God had guided, enriched and united them through him, and said they could truthfully have answered that they had believed, helped and sheltered him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that the Prophet then asked whether they minded a trifle of this world given to win others, and whether they would not be content that people take sheep and camels while they return with the Messenger of God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet said that but for the migration he would have been one of the Ansar and would have taken their valley if people took another, and prayed for mercy on them, their sons and grandsons, and that they wept and said they were pleased with him as their share. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He draws the lessons that even the best believers may feel something about worldly matters without it showing a lack of faith, and that a reformer should remind the community of its origin and goal when it is shaken. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The fourth umra and Attab ibn Usayd

- He says that biographers count four umras of the Prophet: Hudaybiyya, the fulfilled umra, the umra from al-Ji'rana on the way back from Hunayn, and the one joined to the pilgrimage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet appointed Attab ibn Usayd, about twenty years old, as governor of Mecca and then returned to Medina at the end of year 8, the year of Mu'ta and the conquest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The year of the delegations

- He says that year 9 was the year of delegations, that after Mecca every tribe hurried to accept Islam, and that the delegations kept coming through year 10. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that biographers counted from a dozen and more up to sixty delegations, and that al-Waqidi wrote a book on them which is lost. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates the visit of Dimam ibn Tha'laba, who questioned the Prophet about the Creator, his mission, the five prayers and charity and affirmed each answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the delegation of Abd al-Qays asked for instruction to carry home and that the Prophet gave them four commands and four prohibitions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the delegation from Yemen came and that the Prophet praised them as tender of heart, with faith and wisdom said to be Yemeni. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### Preparing the expedition to Tabuk

- He says that in Rajab of year 9 the Prophet ordered preparation for Tabuk, his last expedition, in the hardship of Medina's heat and the season of ripe dates, and for once named the destination openly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet urged spending on the army and that the wealthy gained distinction, and he notes that in the Prophet's time rich and poor competed for the next world, not out of envy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that poor Companions said the wealthy took the rewards and that the Prophet pointed them to words of glorification as a form of charity, but also warned that the wealthy would face a harder reckoning unless they spent freely. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that some accounts put the army at thirty thousand, against fourteen hundred at Hudaybiyya three years earlier. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that Uthman ibn Affan won the highest honour by equipping the army of hardship with his money. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Surat al-Tawba, which came with Tabuk, was called the exposer of the hypocrites, and he notes that the hypocrites' trouble had returned in year 9 after being strong in years 5 and 6, at the Confederates and the Mustaliq expedition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from Abu Mas'ud in al-Bukhari that when the order to give charity came, the hypocrites mocked Abu Aqil's half measure of grain as little and others' large gifts as show, which the surah answers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The Mosque of Dirar, the march and the test at Tabuk

- He says that the hypocrites' long scheming produced the Mosque of Dirar, a place of plotting built in the form of a mosque. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet marched with the believers and some hypocrites, that most hypocrites stayed behind, and that Ka'b ibn Malik later said that in Medina he saw only men marked by hypocrisy or those excused for weakness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the army passed the dwellings of Thamud, that the Prophet gave a warning there, and that water gushed for the people when he put in his arrow or hand at a meagre well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that a quick force under Khalid sent to Ukaydir of Dumat al-Jandal took some spoils and made a settlement, but that there was no fighting at Tabuk itself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that Tabuk was a trial of whether believers prefer what God and his Messenger love over their own desires, and notes that the longest surah revealed about a campaign came with no fighting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The return from Tabuk and the three who stayed behind

- He relates that about two hundred or two hundred and fifty kilometres from Medina the Prophet told his men he was hurrying ahead, and that at the sight of the city he called it Taba and said Uhud was a mountain that loved them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet's habit was to pray two rak'as in the mosque on returning from a journey and then receive those who had stayed behind, and that about eighty hypocrites came with excuses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that not everyone who mixed with the hypocrites was one of them, and that the verse about those who listen to them shows this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that on the way back the Prophet said some men in Medina were with them at every stage, held back by excuse, and he reads this as showing that reward rests on sincere intention. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that al-Sa'ib ibn Yazid went out with the boys to meet the Prophet at Thaniyyat al-Wada on his return from Tabuk, and he places the site near the exit of the Manakha tunnel. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet sent two men to demolish the Mosque of Dirar, since what matters is reality and not buildings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet accepted the outward excuses of the men who came, but that Ka'b ibn Malik and two others admitted they had no excuse and were boycotted, and that their wives were told to keep away. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that a letter reached Ka'b from the Christians of Syria inviting him to join them, and that he threw it into the oven at once, and he uses this against letting hurt feelings damage one's commitment to Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that God accepted their repentance, that the Companions raced to bring the news, and that the Prophet told Ka'b it was the best day since his birth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### Surat al-Tawba and the declaration at the pilgrimage of year 9

- He holds that the Qur'an came down on events and that believers today need to read it with the events in mind, and he describes Surat al-Tawba as exposing the hypocrites. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that the opening of the surah, the declaration of release, is tied not to Tabuk but to the pilgrimage that followed it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet did not make that pilgrimage but put Abu Bakr in charge and sent Ali with the declaration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Ali announced that no polytheist could circle the House after that year, that none could circle it naked, and that treaties would stand until their term, with four months for those without a set term. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He mentions a view that the Prophet stayed away so that nothing of the old practices would mix with his pilgrimage, which then came pure the next year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Year 10: Musaylima and the sending of Mu'adh and Abu Musa to Yemen

- He relates that the delegation of Banu Hanifa came with Musaylima, who offered to follow the Prophet if he were given the succession, and that the Prophet went out with a small stick and refused, naming Thabit ibn Qays to answer him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates, from Abu Hurayra, that the Prophet explained his dream of two golden bracelets that flew away when he blew on them as two liars who would appear after him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet sent Mu'adh ibn Jabal and Abu Musa to Yemen as teachers and judges, and later Ali, and that Ali and Abu Musa came back for the pilgrimage while Mu'adh stayed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that this shows the value of having many reformers to take up roles as the work spreads, and that Mu'adh was about twenty-five, which shows what age can bring in knowledge and leadership. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet told Mu'adh he loved him, taught him a supplication to say after each prayer, and hinted at a farewell, so that Mu'adh wept, and that Mu'adh was therefore not in Medina when the Prophet died. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The farewell pilgrimage and its sermon

- He relates from Jabir that when the Prophet reached Dhu al-Hulayfa the crowd filled the horizon in every direction, which some estimate at a hundred thousand. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that Jabir's account of the pilgrimage in Sahih Muslim is the longest and describes it in detail, and that Jabir said the Qur'an came down on him, he knew its meaning, and they followed what he did. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He draws from this that many rites, such as the stoning, the boundaries and the stays, come from the Prophet's practice and not from the Qur'an, showing the necessity of the sunna. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that in the sermon the Prophet declared the matters of the Jahiliyya, its blood feuds and its usury, to be under his feet, starting with the blood of a kinsman and the usury of al-Abbas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that this proclaimed the victory of Islam, and that at this gathering the verse about perfecting the religion came. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet urged care for women, in front of tribes that had inherited women as property. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet said he left the Book of God, which leads to the sunna, as a safeguard against error, and asked the crowd what they would say, and that they replied he had conveyed, fulfilled and advised, whereupon he raised his finger and called God to witness three times. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The completion of the message and Surat al-Nasr

- He says that the Qur'an's words about conquest and people entering the religion in crowds describe the change from the fourteen hundred of Hudaybiyya to a hundred thousand and from single converts to whole tribes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that Ibn Abbas understood the surah as announcing the Prophet's approaching death, and he compares the sense of completion with the story of Yusuf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He holds that what mattered most to the Prophet was conveying the message and that the witness of the crowd marked the end of the task. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}

### The last days: his night prayer, Usama's army and the final sermons

- He says that he will not recount the Prophet's death because it is too hard to bear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that in his last years the Prophet prayed the night prayer seated and rose to recite the last fifty verses before bowing, and that one of his wives said the burdens of the people had worn him down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He says that in the last two years the delegations, questions of law such as zihar and the oath of imprecation, and his household affairs made the Prophet the reference for everything, and he recalls the month the Prophet withdrew from his wives and the verses that followed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet appointed Usama ibn Zayd, about eighteen, over an army for Syria and answered critics that they had also criticised his father's command, and that the army stayed in Medina when the Prophet fell ill. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Prophet went to pray for the martyrs of Uhud and gave a sermon there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that in a last sermon the Prophet spoke of a servant who chose what was with God over this world, that Abu Bakr wept and said they would give their fathers and mothers for him, and that the Companions later saw he alone understood the Prophet to mean himself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
- He relates that the Ansar wept in their assemblies, that the Prophet then went to the pulpit, charged his successor to treat them well and told them to be patient until they met him at the pool. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep32}
