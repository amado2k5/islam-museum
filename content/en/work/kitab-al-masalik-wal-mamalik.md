## Summary
The *Kitab al-masalik wal-mamalik* ("Book of Routes and Realms") of [[person/ubaydallah-ibn-khurdadhbih|Abu al-Qasim Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Khurradadhbih]], who calls himself in its heading "client (mawla) of the Commander of the Faithful", is a book "containing the description of the earth, the constitution of creation upon it, the qibla of the people of each country, and the realms and routes to the regions of the earth". {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.12} It is among the earliest books of its kind, represented here by de Goeje's edition printed in Leiden in 1306 AH (1889). {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.9}

## The preface
Ibn Khurradadhbih opens with an address to an enquirer, wishing him long life and calling him "son of the noble lords and righteous imams"; he says he understood the request for a clear account of the routes and realms of the earth, their description, distances, inhabited and waste lands and deserts, and that he found that Ptolemy had set out the boundaries in a foreign tongue, "so I translated it from his language into correct language". {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.11} The addressee is not named.

## The description of the earth
He begins by saying that the earth is round like a ball, placed in the heavens like the yolk in an egg; the air around it draws it from every side towards the heavens, and the earth draws the heavy element in living bodies "as the stone draws iron". {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.12} He gives the earth's circumference at the equator as 360 degrees, each degree 25 parasangs, the parasang 12,000 cubits, the cubit 24 fingers and the finger six barley grains laid side by side, making nine thousand parasangs. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.12}

## The routes
The core of the book is its routes, given stage by stage in parasangs: from Baghdad to the far east, the roads of Khurasan, Transoxiana and Tukharistan, the road to the mountain districts and Wasit, from Suq al-Ahwaz to Fars, and the roads of Fars and Kirman to Sind; {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.26} {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.40} {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.51} {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.62} then the sea route from Basra to the east; {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.69} the road from Baghdad to the west, and the roads of Syria, Egypt and the Maghrib; {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.80} {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.93} the road to Azerbaijan and Armenia; {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.127} and the roads to Mecca and Medina from Iraq, Basra, al-Yamama, Oman, Egypt and Damascus, among them "the road the Messenger of God took" on the hijra. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.133} {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.137} {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.157} In places he gives the land tax of provinces, such as that of Qinnasrin and the Awasim. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p.83}
