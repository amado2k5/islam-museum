## Summary
*Qisas al-Anbiya'* ("The Stories of the Prophets") is by the hadith master 'Imad al-Din Abu al-Fida' Isma'il [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] al-Qurashi of Damascus (d. 774 AH). {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 1} It treats the prophets named in the Qur'an and some not named there explicitly. It begins with Adam and ends with Jesus, and does not take up the story of the Prophet Muhammad. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 6-7}

## Method
- Each story first gives the verses, then the reports and sayings. The chapter on Adam opens with the verses on his creation from al-Baqara, Al 'Imran and al-Nisa'. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 13}
- He examines the chains and points out what he considers to be taken from the [[concept/israiliyyat|Isra'iliyyat]]. Of some reports on Adam he says "much of it is received from the Isra'iliyyat". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 50} Of one report he says it has "a damaging defect", since it is transmitted as the saying of a Companion, "and it seems he took it from the Isra'iliyyat". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 69} Of a story about the taking of Idris's soul he says: "this is from the Isra'iliyyat, and some of it is objectionable". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 78}

## Chapters
In al-Farmawi's edition the book has twenty chapters: {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 769-773}
- [[person/adam|Adam]], Idris, [[person/nuh|Noah]], Hud, [[person/salih|Salih]], [[person/ibrahim|Abraham]], [[person/lut|Lot]], [[person/shuayb|Shu'ayb]], and the descendants of Abraham.
- Wondrous events, peoples destroyed wholesale, and [[person/yunus|Jonah]].
- [[person/musa|Moses]], al-Khidr and Elijah, a group of Israelite prophets, [[person/dawud|David]], [[person/sulayman|Solomon]], another group of prophets, and [[person/zakariyya|Zechariah]] and [[person/yahya|John]].
- [[person/isa-ibn-maryam|Jesus]].

The chapter divisions and some section titles are the editor's, following the author's own division of the material. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 8}

## Its place among books on the prophets
The editor says the first to write on the stories of the prophets was [[person/wahb-ibn-munabbih|Wahb ibn Munabbih]] (d. 114 AH), whom he describes as "learned in the legends of the ancients, especially the Isra'iliyyat". After him came al-Kisa'i the grammarian, Sahl ibn 'Abd Allah al-Tustari, al-Musabbihi, and then Ibn Kathir. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 6}

## The editor's assessment
'Abd al-Hayy al-Farmawi says Ibn Kathir relied on the Qur'an, that his hadith contain no forged reports, and that he rarely relied on weak ones. He calls the book "entirely free of Isra'iliyyat". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 7} Ibn Kathir himself, however, quotes reports in the book and states that they are Isra'iliyyat in order to warn against them, as shown above. The editor's point is most likely that he does not rely on them, not that they are absent. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 50} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 78}

## This edition
The fifth edition (1417 AH/1997 CE), Dar al-Tiba'a wa-al-Nashr al-Islamiyya, Cairo, edited by 'Abd al-Hayy al-Farmawi, professor of exegesis and Qur'anic sciences at al-Azhar. He worked from printed editions; he identified verses, glossed rare words, annotated persons and places and traced the reports. He wrote his preface on 1 Rabi' al-Awwal 1411 AH/21 September 1990. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 1-2} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-farmawi-1997 p. 7-9}
