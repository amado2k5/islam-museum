## Summary

The lecturer closes the topic of the earliest Muslims with a versified aid to memorising their names, then explains why Companions give conflicting ordinal places (fourth, third, and so on) by pointing to the secrecy of the first years. He builds on this a theme of security awareness in the Prophet's biography, retells the conversion stories of Ibn Mas'ud, Amr ibn Abasa and Abu Dharr, presents a later digest of the sira, and opens the next topic, the public call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### A memorising poem on the sira and its chapter on the earliest Muslims

- He recommends the versified summary of the sira by al-Iraqi as an aid for memorising points that are easily confused, such as dates and names, and prefers an annotated edition prepared by Abd al-Muhsin al-Qasim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq counted roughly fifty early Muslims, that Ibn Sa'd added a few and that other scholars added more, which is why a poem helps fix the names. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that the poem names Abu Bakr as the first man to accept Islam, citing a poem of Hassan ibn Thabit, then Khadija as the first woman and Ali as the first boy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He says the poem also notes a view that Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As accepted Islam even before the group converted through Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### Why the ordinal places of the early Muslims conflict

- He relates that Amr ibn Abasa, Abu Dharr and, in other reports, al-Zubayr are each said to have been the fourth, third or similar in order of conversion, and that scholars treated these reports as puzzling. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He counts the groups behind the usual lists: Khadija, Zayd, Abu Bakr and Ali; then those who converted through Abu Bakr, namely Uthman, Talha, al-Zubayr and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf; then Uthman ibn Maz'un, Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah and others named by Ibn Ishaq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He holds that each speaker was reporting only what he knew, and that the secrecy of the early years explains why nobody knew the true number of Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir took the same view of the Prophet's answer to Amr ibn Abasa about who was with him, reading the freeman and slave as kinds of people and not only Abu Bakr and Bilal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas said, according to Sahih al-Bukhari, that he was a third of Islam for seven days, and holds that Sa'd spoke after the Prophet's death still believing this, which shows how strict the secrecy was. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He holds that merely being a Muslim was at first the sensitive security fact, and that this was the most dangerous stage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The security dimension and the letter of Hatib before the conquest of Mecca

- He holds that a security dimension runs through the whole biography and that its object changes: first who is a Muslim, then who migrates and when, as in the departure to Abyssinia, then military movements in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates from Ka'b ibn Malik in Sahih al-Bukhari that the Prophet usually hid his intended destination by naming another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that before the conquest of Mecca a letter about the army's movement was sent to Quraysh by Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a with a woman, and that revelation informed the Prophet of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that the Prophet sent Ali, al-Zubayr and al-Miqdad, who overtook the woman and, after threatening to search her, obtained the letter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He stresses that Hatib was a veteran of Badr and a steadfast fighter at Uhud, and relates that when Umar asked leave to execute him as a hypocrite the Prophet first asked him for his reason. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Hatib explained he was an ally and not a kinsman of Quraysh and wished to protect his family in Mecca, and that the Prophet accepted the explanation, citing a report in the Musnad of Ahmad that he was confident God would support His messenger. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that a passage of revelation about taking enemies as allies was sent down on this affair, which he reads as showing that truth about a mistake is not hidden out of regard for persons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He concludes that the Companions were raised with a security sense from the start. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### Security through Medina: Tabuk, hypocrites and Banu Qurayza

- He holds that the security dimension continued after the migration and the victory at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He says Tabuk was the one expedition whose destination the Prophet stated openly, and explains that the society had not yet separated the hypocrites from the believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He holds that information could pass through hypocrites to the Jews, especially Banu Qurayza, or to Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### Vigilance in revelation: the prayer of fear

- He holds that a passage of revelation legislating the prayer of fear, sent down in Medina, shows vigilance being reinforced even when the Prophet was present, supported by revelation, and aided by angels. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He explains that the prayer is short, and that the rule exists so that worship does not become an opening for the enemy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates a saying that a believer is not bitten twice from the same hole and presents it as an expression of the Prophet's own alertness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### The scout sent before Badr

- He relates from Anas that before Badr the Prophet sent a man as an observer to a chosen spot on the route of the Quraysh caravan to bring back exact information. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He draws from this that every Muslim held a responsible post, and that even women learned vigilance early. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### The conversion of Ibn Mas'ud

- He holds that the story of Ibn Mas'ud's conversion is sound, citing Ibn Kathir's report through Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi, and defends the narrator Asim as a good, truthful transmitter who sometimes erred. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Ibn Mas'ud was a young shepherd for Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt when the Prophet, with Abu Bakr, asked him for milk, and that a young unmated ewe gave milk after the Prophet prayed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Ibn Mas'ud then asked to be taught, was called a boy fit to teach, and learned seventy surahs that nobody disputed with him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that al-Iraqi versified the story in about seven or eight lines. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The story of Amr ibn Abasa

- He relates from Sahih Muslim, through Abu Umama, that Amr ibn Abasa, before Islam, thought people's worship of idols baseless, heard of a man in Mecca, rode there and found the Prophet in hiding because of his people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He holds that Amr's visit probably fell in the secret stage and that the report fits the public call too, since concealment was partial then. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that the Prophet told Amr his message was kinship ties, the breaking of idols and the worship of one God, said a freeman and a slave were with him, and told Amr to return home and come when he heard the Prophet had emerged. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Amr came to Medina after hearing the Prophet had migrated, and asked about prayer times and ablution, and that the Prophet gave an answer on forbidden prayer times and on the reward of ablution. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Abu Umama doubted so long a report from one sitting, and that Amr answered he was old and would not lie, having heard it more than seven times. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The conversion of Abu Dharr

- He mentions that Dimad has his own conversion story, which he leaves for its time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that the story of Abu Dharr's conversion comes in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim from Ibn Abbas, and in Sahih Muslim from Abu Dharr himself, and that the two accounts differ. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He says scholars differ: al-Qurtubi found harmonising them strained, while Ibn Hajar harmonised them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates from Abu Dharr's account that he left Ghifar with his brother Unays and their mother, that Unays heard of a man in Mecca said to be a poet, sorcerer and soothsayer, and judged his words not poetry. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Abu Dharr went to Mecca, was beaten by the people there, stayed thirty days at Zamzam living on its water, and was then met by the Prophet and Abu Bakr, who fed him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Abu Dharr first greeted the Prophet with the Islamic greeting, that the Prophet told him of a land of palms, probably Yathrib, and asked him to carry word to his people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Abu Dharr's brother, mother and part of Ghifar then accepted Islam, led by Khufaf ibn Ima, with the rest following after the Prophet reached Medina, and that Aslam joined as well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He holds that the account shows public preaching already underway but still with partial concealment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### A digest of the sira that adds from other sources

- He presents al-Khazin, the commentator who died in 741 AH by his statement, as author of a digest of the sira of Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham that adds reports from hadith collections. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He values the book because it joins the substance of Ibn Ishaq with additions from other works, and says Amr ibn Abasa's story is absent from Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He names al-Bayhaqi's Dala'il al-Nubuwwa as a basic reference with chains of transmission and says he awaits his copy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that al-Bayhaqi, quoted by al-Khazin, says Khalid ibn Sa'id accepted Islam early and was the first of his brothers, after a dream about standing at the edge of a fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### Ammar's report and Ibn Hajar's account of the slaves and women

- He relates a report from Ammar ibn Yasir in Sahih al-Bukhari that he saw the Prophet with only five slaves, two women and Abu Bakr, and calls it important as a Companion's testimony. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates Ibn Hajar's reading that the slaves were Bilal, Zayd ibn Haritha, Amir ibn Fuhayra and Abu Fakiha, with the fifth possibly Shuqran or Ammar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates Ibn Hajar's reading that the two women were Khadija and Umm Ayman or Sumayya, and that some suggested Umm al-Fadl, which Ibn Hajar found unclear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar understood Ammar to mean those who made their Islam public, since others hid it from relatives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates a report, graded sound by al-Dhahabi and transmitted by Ibn Majah, that seven made their Islam public: the Prophet, Abu Bakr, Ammar and his mother Sumayya, Suhayb, Bilal and al-Miqdad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that in this report the Prophet was protected by Abu Talib, Abu Bakr by his tribe, and the others were tortured, with only Bilal refusing to yield. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}

### Opening the public call: Ibn Ishaq's transition

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says people then entered Islam in groups until it was spoken of in Mecca, and that the Prophet kept his matter hidden for about three years from his mission, by Ibn Ishaq's own report of what reached him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He uses this to say that Quraysh knew of the matter even during the secret stage, though not who had converted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham explains the command to proclaim as a command to distinguish truth from falsehood, and says al-Suhayli will be taken up on its linguistic sense in the next session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep11}
