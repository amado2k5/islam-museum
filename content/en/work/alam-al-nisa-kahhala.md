## Summary
*A'lam al-Nisa' fi Alamay al-Arab wa-al-Islam* ("Notable Women in the Arab and Islamic Worlds") is a biographical dictionary of famous women compiled by [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|Umar Rida Kahhala]], offered "to the speakers of Arabic" as a guide for anyone researching Arab and Muslim women. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.7}

## Purpose
In his preface Kahhala says that a researcher on Arab or Muslim women meets an obstacle that only long digging in printed and manuscript books can overcome, so he tried to gather as many famous women as he could who left a mark in learning, civilisation, letters and the arts, in politics and power, in charity, and in religion and asceticism, "to lift the veil from the different roles women have played in the history of the Arabs and of Islam". {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.7}

## Method and arrangement
He followed each entry with the sources he relied on, so that the reader may trust it, and arranged the women alphabetically like encyclopaedias and dictionaries, cross-referring names beginning with "Umm" or "Ibnat" and multiple names to the best-established and most common one. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.7} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.8} In the enlarged edition he added women found after the first edition, some of them recently deceased, and he dated the preface in Damascus in 1379 AH / 1959 CE. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.8}

## Contents
Entries range from a few lines on women hadith transmitters drawn from works such as *al-Daw' al-Lami'* and *al-Tahbir*, to long entries gathering reports and verse from *al-Aghani*, al-Tabari, Ibn Khallikan and others — the entry on [[person/al-khansa|al-Khansa']] runs to ten pages and ends with some thirty sources. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.6} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.364} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.375} Its women include queens and regents such as [[person/arwa-al-sulayhi|Arwa al-Sulayhiyya]] and [[person/sitt-al-mulk|Sitt al-Mulk]], scholars such as [[person/shahda-al-katiba|Shuhda the Scribe]] and [[person/zaynab-bint-al-kamal|Zaynab bint al-Kamal]], ascetics such as [[person/rabia-al-adawiyya|Rabi'a al-Adawiyya]], and poets and singers such as [[person/ulayya-bint-al-mahdi|Ulayya bint al-Mahdi]] and [[person/azza-al-mayla|Azza al-Mayla']]. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.31} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.434} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.170} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v2 p.313} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.279} {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v3 p.338}

## Edition
The Mu'assasat al-Risala (Beirut) printing, "enlarged, with a supplement" {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.5}, which the imprint page of a second copy of vol. 1 identifies as the fifth edition (1984; see the source record); Kahhala says its first and second printings were due to the efforts of al-Maktaba al-Hashimiyya. {cite:alam-al-nisa-kahhala-risala-1984-v1 p.8}

## For young visitors
This is a big book that gathers the stories of thousands of women in Muslim and Arab history — scholars, poets, queens and devout women — arranged in alphabetical order like a dictionary.
