## Summary

Radd al-Muhtar ala al-Durr al-Mukhtar is the gloss that [[person/ibn-abidin|Muhammad Amin Ibn Abidin]] wrote on [[work/al-durr-al-mukhtar|al-Durr al-Mukhtar]], the commentary of Ala' al-Din [[person/al-haskafi-muhammad-ibn-ali|al-Haskafi]] on the base text Tanwir al-Absar; the title page of volume 3 calls it "the third volume of Radd al-Muhtar ala al-Durr al-Mukhtar on the text of Tanwir al-Absar, by the learned Ibn Abidin". {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.20} The index of the same volume calls it the gloss Radd al-Muhtar by al-Sayyid Muhammad Amin, known as Ibn Abidin. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.3} It belongs to the [[group/hanafi-madhhab|Hanafi school]]: it follows the commentary phrase by phrase, gathers what the authoritative books of the school say, and marks the view on which fatwa is given. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.9} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21}

## How the gloss works

Each note opens with the word qawluhu, "his words", followed by the phrase of al-Durr al-Mukhtar in brackets and then the comment; the text of al-Durr al-Mukhtar is printed in the margin of the page, and short side headings beginning with matlab ("topic") name the subject of each passage; the index of every volume is made of these headings. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.9} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.3} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.3}

The author opens each book by explaining how it connects with what came before. At the start of the Book of Sales he explains that what preceded were the rights of God, which he counts as three, acts of worship, penalties and expiations, and that transactions stand opposite them; acts of worship are those whose purpose in principle is the servant's drawing near to God and gaining reward, and transactions those whose purpose in principle is meeting people's needs, such as sale, suretyship and transfer of debt. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.9} He discusses the order of the chapters, holding that marriage, though a transaction, also carries the sense of worship, and that loans are placed with sales because of their kinship with them. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.9} At the start of the Book of Manumission he explains that manumission follows divorce because both are "releases" (isqatat): releasing a claim to retaliation is pardon, releasing a debt is acquittal, releasing the marital tie is divorce, and releasing slavery is manumission. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.21}

The gloss gives each view with the name of its holder: Abu Hanifa and his two companions [[person/abu-yusuf-al-qadi|Abu Yusuf]] and [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-hasan-al-shaybani|Muhammad ibn al-Hasan]], and books of the school such as al-Hidaya, Fath al-Qadir, al-Bahr, al-Nahr, al-Bada'i', al-Ashbah, al-Qunya, al-Khulasa and al-Bazzaziyya, before the author gives his own opinion with "I say". {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.18} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.31} He sometimes sends the reader to his separate treatises, such as [[work/nashr-al-urf|Nashr al-Urf]] on custom and [[work/tanbih-al-ruqud|Tanbih al-Ruqud fi Ahkam al-Nuqud]] on changes in the value of money. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.31}

He pays attention to his own time: he speaks of buying in piastres "as is the custom of our time", of the key-money (khulu) of shops in the markets of Cairo, of giving up posts for money, and in the chapter on rebels he mentions an event of his own day in 1233 AH. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.31} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.497}

## Volume 3

Volume 3 opens with the Book of Manumission and its chapters: partial manumission, oaths sworn by manumission, manumission for a payment, tadbir and istilad. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.3} Then comes the Book of Oaths, with chapters on oaths about entering and leaving, eating and drinking, divorce and manumission, buying and selling, and striking and killing. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.4} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.5} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.6} Then the Book of Hadd Penalties, with chapters on intercourse that incurs the hadd, testimony to zina, the penalty for drinking, the penalty for false accusation and discretionary punishment. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.7} Then the Book of Theft, the manner of amputation and highway robbery, and the Book of Jihad (siyar), with chapters on spoils and their division, conquest by unbelievers, the foreigner under safe-conduct, and tithe, land tax and jizya. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.8} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.9} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.10} Then the chapters on the apostate and on rebels, the books of the foundling, lost property, the missing person and partnership, and finally the Book of Waqf with its sections on the founder's conditions and the administrator. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.11} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.12} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.13} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.15}

Among its questions the museum has entries on [[concept/itq-fiqh|manumission]], its definition and pillars; [[concept/istilad-umm-al-walad|istilad and the umm al-walad]]; and [[concept/baghy-fiqh|rebels]], distinguished from highway robbers and the Khawarij. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.21} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.74} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3 p.496}

## Volume 4

Volume 4 opens with the Book of Sales, covering the options of stipulation, inspection and defect, the defective sale, the unauthorised agent, rescission, resale at cost-plus and at cost, dealing in the goods and the price, and loans. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.3} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.4} Then come the chapters on riba, appurtenant rights, third-party claims, salam, miscellaneous questions and currency exchange. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.5} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.183} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.210} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.241} Then the books of suretyship, transfer of debt, judgeship, testimony, agency, claims, acknowledgement, settlement, silent partnership (mudaraba) and deposit, ending with the Book of Gifts. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.295} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.303} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.378} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.408} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.426} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.453} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.477} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.488} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.498} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.513}

At the start of the sales he divides sale four ways: by its legal effect into effective, suspended, defective and void; by the thing sold into barter (goods for goods), sarf (price for price), salam (price for deferred goods) and plain sale (goods for a price); by the price into murabaha, tawliya, wadi'a and musawama; and sometimes by the quality of the price as immediate or deferred. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.10} Among its questions the museum has entries on [[concept/mal-fiqh|property and legal value]], [[concept/bay-al-taati|sale by exchange without words]], [[concept/bay-al-istijrar|sale by running account]], [[concept/nuzul-an-al-wazaif|compensation for bare rights and giving up posts]], [[concept/urf|particular and general custom]] and [[concept/kasad-al-nuqud|the collapse and change in value of money]]. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.10} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.18} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.19} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.31}

## The end of volume 4 and its printing

At the end of volume 4 is a note by the author's son Muhammad Ala' al-Din saying that this is the last of what he found on his father's copy and that some of it needs checking against the original it was taken from, which he did not have; it is dated Wednesday 25 Safar 1260 AH. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.528} It is followed by the statement of the corrector of the Egyptian Press that the volume was printed at Bulaq, corrected as far as possible and collated with the author's hand, except for its end from quire 91, which was corrected from a copy full of distortions and omissions, taken from a copy stripped of its marginal notes; printing was completed early in Sha'ban 1271 AH. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.528} The volume closes by saying that volume 5 begins with the Book of Hire. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.528}

## The copies in the museum

The museum's scanned collection has four files under this title. Two are volumes of the gloss: volume 3 (source [[source/radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3|radd-al-muhtar-scan-v3]]) and volume 4 of the Bulaq print ([[source/radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4|radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4]]). The other two are not the gloss itself: one is a later Egyptian fatwa collection that mentions Ibn Abidin and his revision of the Hamidiyya fatwas and dates its questions from 1260 to 1302 AH; the other is a volume whose writer refers to "my master, my father", the author of the Tanqih, and is probably from the son's supplement; neither is used here. Page numbers cited are the pages of the scanned file; the source records give the difference from the printed pages.

## Not covered here

Volumes 1, 2 and 5 of the gloss, and its introduction, in which the author explains why and when he wrote it, were not read in this batch, so the museum gives no date of composition here.
