## Summary
Kashf al-Mahjub ("The Unveiling of the Veiled") is the Persian book on Sufism by [[person/al-hujwiri|Abu al-Hasan 'Ali ibn 'Uthman al-Jullabi al-Hujwiri al-Ghaznawi]]. Badi' Jum'a, introducing the Arabic translation, calls it the oldest work on Sufism in Persian and the first systematic book on the theory and practice of Sufism, and the only one of al-Hujwiri's works to survive. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 11} He thinks the author began it around 435 AH and finished it around 442 AH. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 11} It has twenty-five parts on the theory and practice of Sufism, notices of its imams and shaykhs, the Sufis' sayings and symbols, their schools, and beliefs, worship, dealings and Sufi customs. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 12}

## The question it answers
Al-Hujwiri opens with the form of the question: Abu Sa'id al-Hujwiri asked him to explain truly the Sufi path, the nature of the Sufis' stations, their doctrines and sayings, their symbols and allusions, and their love of God. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 200} He answers that this science "has in truth been effaced in our time", especially in his lands, that the matter has turned from realisation to imitation, and that disciples have given up striving and call their sick fancy contemplation; he says he had earlier written books on the subject that were all lost, and that some pretenders had appropriated sayings from them. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 200} He says he made the book to be a polish for veiled hearts. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 198}

## Sources and place among Sufi books
The introducer says al-Hujwiri sought to harmonise Sufi teaching with Islam, as [[person/abu-nasr-al-sarraj|al-Sarraj]] had attempted in [[work/kitab-al-luma-fi-al-tasawwuf|al-Luma']] and [[person/al-qushayri-abu-l-qasim|al-Qushayri]] in [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|the Risala]], and as al-Ghazali later did in the Ihya'; the book resembles al-Luma' in method and material closely enough to show it relied on it, contains a Persian translation of chapters of al-Qushayri's Risala, and relies heavily on al-Sulami's [[work/tabaqat-al-sufiyya-sulami|Tabaqat al-Sufiyya]] for the notices of the shaykhs. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 12} He says Farid al-Din 'Attar in Tadhkirat al-Awliya' and Jami in Nafahat al-Uns drew on it. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 12}

## The chapters of volume one
Volume one of the Arabic translation contains, after the author's preface, thirteen chapters: {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 396} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 397}
- On affirming knowledge (p. 206), poverty (p. 218), Sufism (p. 230), wearing the patched frock (p. 244), and the dispute over poverty and purity (p. 258). {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 206} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 218} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 230} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 244} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 258}
- On [[group/malamatiyya|blame (malama)]] (p. 262). {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 262}
- Chapters on their imams: among the Companions (p. 270), the Prophet's family (p. 278), the People of the Bench (p. 288), the Successors (p. 294), their followers (p. 300), the later masters (p. 378), and then a chapter on later Sufi men "from the regions" (p. 390). {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 270} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 278} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 288} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 294} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 300} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 378} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 390}

In this last chapter al-Hujwiri names shaykhs he knew or heard of in Syria and Iraq, Fars, Quhistan, Azerbaijan, Tabaristan, Kirman and Khurasan, such as Abu Ja'far al-Saydalani, Ibn Salibah, Akhi al-Zanjani, Abu al-'Abbas al-Sharmaqani and al-Muzaffar son of Shaykh Abu Sa'id, and says Ahmad ibn Hammad al-Sarakhsi was his companion for a long time. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 390} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 391} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 392} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 393}

## Editions and translations
The introducer says the book was first printed in Leningrad in 1926, then in Tehran in 1336 SH, and that Nicholson translated it into English, his translation being published in London. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 12} The Arabic translation used here is by Is'ad 'Abd al-Hadi Qandil, revised and introduced by Badi' Jum'a, published in the National Translation Project in Cairo in 2007 (no. 1042), opening with the translator's long study of al-Hujwiri's age, life and book. {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 3} {cite:kashf-al-mahjub-qandil-2007-v1 p. 13}
