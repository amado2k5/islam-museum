## Summary

The ninth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers closes the first half of the Meccan period. The speaker treats Surat al-Furqan as a case of how the Qur'an steadied the believers and answered the polytheists' objections, the plain way the Prophet conveyed the message, and the migration to Abyssinia: its numbers, the feelings of the emigrants, their contact with Christianity and the Asma bint Umays report. He ends with the conversion of Umar and the protection he still needed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Recap and the aim of the session

- He says that at least three sessions of the Meccan period remain before the series moves to the Medinan period. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He recalls that the previous session covered the stands of the Companions in the fourth and fifth years, the practical harm done by the polytheists, and the verses that steadied the believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that the first theme of this session continues the Qur'anic question: what the revelation did for the believers in giving them firmness and the ability to stand for the truth against the polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that many students of the biography overlook the verses revealed in this stage, although they show the nature of the religion and what occupied the believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Surat al-Furqan: who God is, and the powerless idols

- He says that he chose Surat al-Furqan as a Meccan chapter that had a great effect in treating the condition of the believers, and that he will treat only its meanings and their effect on the biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that the opening of the chapter joins two ideas found throughout the Meccan chapters: the greatness of God as creator, owner of dominion and one without partner, and the helplessness of the gods the polytheists worship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that the Meccan Qur'an came to make God known to believers and polytheists, so that when the Prophet told them to worship God alone there was already a store of verses describing His attributes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that the polytheists took the second idea, that their gods bring neither harm nor benefit, as an assault on their right to worship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Conveying the message plainly, and the report of Amr ibn Abasa

- He holds that the religion did not come in soft form, and he criticises a modern idea that wisdom and gentleness in calling to God mean diluting religious truths to suit general taste. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that the Prophet from the first day, on the hill of al-Safa, warned them plainly, and that concealing from an unbeliever that he has only one path would be deceit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He distinguishes kindness in dealings, neighbourliness and justice from deceit about concepts and truths, and says that mercy means saving people from the fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that the polytheists understood the message better than many people today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates, from Sahih Muslim, the report of Amr ibn Abasa, an outsider who heard in Mecca of a man giving news, came to the Prophet in hiding because his people were bold against him, and asked what he was. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that the Prophet answered that God had sent him to join family ties, break the idols and make God's oneness known, and that Abu Bakr and Bilal were with him then. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He notes that scholars differ on whether the words free man and slave mean one of each or the classes in general. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that the Prophet told Amr he could not yet follow him and should return to his family until he heard that the Prophet had prevailed, and that Amr later came to him in Medina and asked about prayer and purity. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He draws from the report that even in a time of hiding the Prophet stated his message as it was, including the breaking of idols, which was done physically only at the end of his mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Surat al-Furqan: the polytheists' objections and the answers

- He says that this chapter gives a view of a theme not yet treated, the argument with the polytheists: what they said and how the Qur'an met it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that they accused the Prophet of fabricating the Qur'an with the help of others and of having old tales written down for him, and that they objected that a messenger eats food and walks in markets and should have an angel, a treasure or a garden. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that one of the greatest functions of the Qur'an was to correct false standards the polytheists had inherited from their forefathers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that the chapter answers the objection about food and markets by pointing out that earlier messengers whom they accept, such as Moses and Abraham, did the same. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that God sometimes argued with the polytheists from what they believed of earlier messages. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that the chapter repeatedly names denial of the Hour and the hereafter as the root of their various errors and arrogance, and that the more value the hereafter has for a believer, the more readily he answers God's command. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that they also demanded that angels be sent down or God be seen, and asked why the Qur'an was not sent down all at once, and that the chapter answers that it came in parts to steady the Prophet's heart. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Lessons for reformers from Surat al-Furqan

- He draws the lesson that a reformer should choose from the Qur'an the verses that suit his condition and bring him consolation and steadiness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that the chapter reports the polytheists mocking the Prophet and saying he nearly turned them from their gods, and he explains that they boasted of having held fast to al-Lat and al-Uzza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that the chapter answers that they will learn who is furthest astray, and likens those who follow desire and fill the gatherings of Quraysh to cattle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He points to the pride of the Qur'anic address, which came to weak believers who were being tried, and tells them that their mighty persecutors are like cattle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that the chapter alternates speech about God with speech about their gods, ends with praise of the servants of the Merciful and compares the good abode of the believers with the narrow place of the unbelievers in the fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that reading the chapter with the period and its people in mind changes one's reading of the Qur'an and of the biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Summary: the Qur'an healed and exalted the believers

- He says that the conclusion of this part is that the Qur'an treated the effects of hardship on the believers, corrected their understanding and raised their standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that verses of a Meccan chapter, asking whether the one who creates is like the one who does not, and asking the polytheists to show what their gods created, raised the value of firmness and of a believing and methodical sense of superiority. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He calls this an important and delicate matter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### The departure to Abyssinia: numbers and notable emigrants

- He says that as torment grew severe and conditions narrowed, the Prophet allowed his Companions to emigrate to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He counts the first party as about twelve men and four women, followed later by some eighty men and nearly nineteen women, and says this was the second migration to Abyssinia since there were two. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He names among the emigrants Ruqayya, daughter of the Prophet, with her husband Uthman, Abu Salama with his wife Umm Salama, Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, and Ja'far ibn Abi Talib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He notes a question raised by some biographers whether Ja'far went only because he was weak like the others or also as leader of the emigrants appointed by the Prophet, and says he has found no explicit report on it but that it deserves research. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Cut off from the Prophet: prophetic upbringing as provision

- He raises a methodological point for reformers: the emigrants were separated from the Prophet, from the revelation and from new rulings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that prayer had been obligatory from the start, as two prayers, with the five prayers fixed later at the Ascension, while zakat, pilgrimage, fighting, the veil and fasting came later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that the emigrants were away for years, having left around the fifth year of the mission and returned at the latest in the seventh year after the Hijra, though some came back early, including Abu Salama and Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, and some took part in Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that the short prophetic upbringing they received was provision enough to keep them firm for all those years of exile. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that educators and reformers should therefore give a deep and serious grounding rather than a thin one, so that people last through such crises. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### The report of Asma bint Umays and the two migrations

- He relates, from al-Bukhari on the authority of Abu Musa, that Abu Musa and his brothers left Yemen by ship with some fifty men, were carried to the Negus in Abyssinia, joined Ja'far there and reached the Prophet when Khaybar was conquered. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that some people told the ship's company that they had preceded them in emigration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that Asma bint Umays, wife of Ja'far and later wife of Abu Bakr, visited Hafsa, where Umar asked who she was, called her the Abyssinian and sea woman, and said that they had preceded them and were closer to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that she answered angrily that they had been with the Prophet, who fed their hungry and taught their ignorant, while she and her companions were in a distant and hostile land, afraid and harmed for God and his messenger, and she said she would raise it with the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that the Prophet told her Umar was not closer to him than they were, and that Umar and his companions had one migration while the people of the ship had two. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that Abu Musa and the ship's people came to her in groups asking about this report, which pleased them more than anything in the world. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that this report, recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim, shows the feeling of deprivation and anxiety among the emigrants in Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Tension and contact with Christianity in Abyssinia

- He says that the emigrants lived in tension although they were in a place of safety and justice, since Quraysh sent Amr ibn al-As and another man to the Negus to negotiate their handing over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that when a rival contested the Negus's rule, al-Zubayr inflated a waterskin and swam out to watch the fighting, then returned with the good news that the Negus had won, and he says this shows they lived in suspense about the politics of their host country. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that the emigrants also met another culture and the Christian religion, and that this contact demanded a measure of firmness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari on the authority of Aisha, that Umm Habiba and Umm Salama described to the Prophet a church they had seen in Abyssinia with images in it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that the Prophet answered that those people, when a righteous man among them died, built a place of worship over his grave and put such images in it, and he called them the worst of creation before God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Umar and Hamza accept Islam; reliance on the two Sahihs

- He says that the emigrants stayed in groups, some returning early and the bulk of them much later, and that meanwhile events in Mecca changed the balance, chiefly the conversion of Hamza and then Umar, which he dates to the fifth or sixth year of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He explains that he often cites al-Bukhari and Muslim because of the strength of their authentication, not to belittle widespread biographical reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He says that the biography tolerates reports that would not be tolerated in hadith on rulings, and that many biographical reports are mursal reports of specialists in it such as Urwa ibn al-Zubayr and al-Zuhri, both of the generation after the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Umar's strength, and the ruling on speaking in prayer

- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that Sa'id ibn Zayd, husband of Umar's sister, said that Umar had kept him bound on account of Islam, and he reads this as a sign of how harsh Umar was before he accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that Ibn Mas'ud said, in al-Bukhari, that the Muslims had been mighty since Umar accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that Ibn Mas'ud, on returning from the Negus, greeted the Prophet in prayer and received no answer, and that the Prophet said prayer has its own occupation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He explains that speech in prayer had at first been allowed and was forbidden during the period of the Abyssinian migration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}

### Umar still feared Quraysh; al-As ibn Wa'il's protection

- He stresses that Quraysh's power remained very great after Umar accepted Islam and that Muslims were few, so that even Umar was afraid and was threatened. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that al-As ibn Wa'il of Banu Sahm, an ally, found Umar afraid at home and was told that his people meant to kill him for accepting Islam, and that he said no harm would come to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates that al-As then met a crowd flooding the valley looking for the ibn al-Khattab who had left his religion, told them there was no way to him, and that they turned back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He relates a second report from Ibn Umar, as a boy on his roof, that a man in a silk cloak told the crowd Umar had left his religion and that he was Umar's protector, and that the people dispersed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
- He holds that this fits the earlier discussion of protection, and that Umar's conversion strengthened the believers inwardly through his character and standing among his people, but did not end the polytheists' power. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep9}
