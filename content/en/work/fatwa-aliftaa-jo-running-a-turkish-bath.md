## Summary

A question from the owner of a Turkish bath about his business, in which Jordan's General Iftaa Department answered that working there is permissible provided the sharia rules on covering and lowering the gaze are kept. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3801}

## The question

The questioner said he owns a Turkish bath whose morning hours are for women, with an all-female staff, and whose evening hours are for men, and asked for the sharia ruling on this business. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3801}

## The answer

The Department began by noting that Islam gives weight to cleanliness and purity, and that the bath meant in the hadith reports is the public bath used for washing and treatment, which today resembles the steam baths of weight-loss clinics. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3801} It said men may enter with their nakedness covered and their gaze lowered, while it is disliked for women to enter without an excuse such as treatment or a cleanliness that cannot be had otherwise, citing a hadith that censures a woman undressing outside her home and a passage from the Shafi'i work Mughni al-Muhtaj. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3801} Where a woman needs to enter, she should make sure that only women run the place, keep the limits of covering in front of other women, avoid clothing that exposes most of her body or touching private parts without necessity, and lower her gaze. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3801} It concluded that there is no objection to women working in such places provided these rules are kept, lowering the gaze and touching other women only as far as needed. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3801}
