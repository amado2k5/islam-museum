## Summary
The Javahir al-Musiqat is a manuscript on music and dance in the British Library (Or. 12857), which Mark Zebrowski thinks may date from the reign of [[person/ali-adil-shah-i|Ali Adil Shah I]] of Bijapur, about 1570. Fragmentary, it ends abruptly at folio 214 and has forty-eight paintings in a crude but lively version of the [[work/nujum-al-ulum|Nujum]] style. The text is in Deccani Urdu in a clumsy nasta'liq, written with a thick brush on coarse brown paper. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 62}

## Link with the Nujum
The two share a palette — bright orange, moss-green, brownish red and brilliant royal blue — common in later Bijapuri painting, and its third section of single women (dancers?) in arched niches resembles the Nujum's fourth. A perplexing note on folio 4a names [[person/muhammad-adil-shah-bijapur|Muhammad Adil Shah]] (1627–56) as patron; the author thinks it was added later, when the book entered the royal library. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 62} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 63}
