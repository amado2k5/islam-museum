## Summary

A question about cremation where Muslim burial is opposed, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that cremating a corpse is forbidden because it degrades the dead and violates their dignity, and that a way must be found to bury the dead according to the Shari'a, even by moving the body to another area. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4482}

## The question

Muslims living in a foreign country where the dead are cremated, and where some local residents object to Muslim burials, asked whether cremation is forbidden if they are forced into it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4482}

## The answer

The Department answered that Islam honours the human being alive and dead, including by washing, shrouding, praying over and burying the dead, citing [[passage/quran-17-70|Surat al-Isra' 17:70]] and a hadith narrated by [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] in his Sunan whose meaning is that breaking a dead person's bone is like breaking it while alive. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4482} It said burning the corpse is forbidden because it harms, demeans and degrades the dead and erases the features of the body, noting that jurists disallowed carrying out retaliation in a way that ruins the body, and quoting [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|al-Juwayni]] in [[work/nihayat-al-matlab-juwayni|Nihayat al-Matlab]] (16/147) that some Shafi'is disallowed retaliation by poison to protect the corpse, since the body of the dead is inviolable. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4482} It concluded that a way must be found to bury the dead in accordance with the Shari'a, even if that requires moving the body to another area where burial is possible. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4482}
