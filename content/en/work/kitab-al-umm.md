## Summary

Al-Umm is the great law book of the Imam [[person/al-shafii|Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i]] (150-204 AH); its chapters open with "al-Shafi'i said" or "al-Rabi' told us: al-Shafi'i told us", so it comes through his student [[person/al-rabi-ibn-sulayman-al-muradi|al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman al-Muradi]]. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.1} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.391} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.513} In it al-Shafi'i states the question and its ruling and then his argument, often in debate form, "if someone says... it is answered", arguing with opponents from Iraq and from Medina. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.5} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.15}

## Volume 8 in the Dar al-Wafa' edition

The museum's copy is volume 8, edited with references by Dr Rif'at Fawzi Abd al-Muttalib, Dar al-Wafa', Mansura, first edition 1422 AH / 2001. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.1} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.4} The editor numbers the books of al-Umm continuously and numbers the reports, records variants of the manuscripts he marks (b), (s), (z) and (m), and traces reports to the Muwatta', the two Sahihs, the Musannafs and al-Bayhaqi's Ma'rifat al-Sunan. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.5} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.217} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.392}

The volume contains:

- **The Book of Claims and Evidence** (book 65), opening with the chapter on what is and is not decided by an oath with one witness, then "the disagreement over the oath with a witness". {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.5} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.15} See [[concept/al-qada-bil-yamin-maa-al-shahid|judgement on one witness and an oath]].
- **The Book of Testimony** (book 66). {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.107}
- **The Book of Oaths, Vows and Expiations for Oaths**. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.149}
- **[[work/ikhtilaf-al-iraqiyyin|The Disagreement of the Iraqis]]** (book 68): where Abu Hanifa and Ibn Abi Layla differed, as related by Abu Yusuf, with al-Shafi'i's comments. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.217}
- **[[work/ikhtilaf-ali-wa-ibn-masud|The Disagreement of Ali and Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud]]** (book 69). {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.391}
- **[[work/ikhtilaf-malik-wal-shafii|The Disagreement of Malik and al-Shafi'i]]** (book 70). {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.513}

## Al-Shafi'i's method as this volume shows it

At the start of the Disagreement of Malik and al-Shafi'i, al-Rabi' asks al-Shafi'i how a report from the Prophet is established; he refers to his book Jima' al-Ilm and then says: when a trustworthy narrator relates from a trustworthy one back to the Prophet, it is established, and no hadith of his is ever left except for another hadith from him that contradicts it. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.513} When hadiths conflict there are two cases: one abrogates the other, and the abrogating one is followed; or the abrogating one is not known, and the better-attested report is taken, and if they are equal, the one closer to the Book of God and the Prophet's sunna. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.513} A hadith from the Prophet with nothing from him against it gains no strength from agreement by anyone below him, and the view of anyone below him who contradicts it is disregarded. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.514} See [[concept/khabar-al-ahad|the solitary report]].

In the chapter on the oath with one witness he distinguishes property, which is owned, from what is not owned as property is, such as marriage, manumission and hadd penalties, and treats two witnesses as the basic rule for rights and the oath with one witness as a rule for what it was applied to and what is like it. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.6} {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.16}
