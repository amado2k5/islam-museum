# Shadharat al-Dhahab fi Akhbar man Dhahab

## Summary

Shadharat al-Dhahab ("Fragments of Gold, on the Reports of Those Who Passed Away") is an annalistic history by Abd al-Hayy ibn Ahmad, known as Ibn al-Imad al-Hanbali (d. 1089 AH). It gives events year by year and, under each year, the names of those who died in it, scholars, rulers and notable men among them {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 3 p. 9}.

## The edition read

The third volume, which covers the years 350 to 500 AH, has a title page that calls the author a historian, jurist and man of letters. It says that the author's manuscript, kept in the Egyptian National Library (Dar al-Kutub al-Misriyya), was collated in part with a copy belonging to Amir Abd al-Qadir al-Hasani al-Jaza'iri {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 3 p. 9}. The title page gives the year of printing as 1350 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 3 p. 9}. Only the title page and the opening of the 350 AH entry were read for this draft. The later years of the volume were not read.

## How a year is told

Each year opens with a heading and then with the phrase "in it" (wa-fiha). In the entry for 350 AH the text says that Mu'izz al-Dawla built, in Baghdad, a palace called Dar al-Saltana, of great beauty and size, at a cost of thirteen million dirhams; by about 600 AH its traces had been effaced, and only its site and part of its foundations remained {cite:shadharat-dhahab vol. 3 p. 10}. The entry shows how the book joins political building news to its year.

## Limits of this draft

The draft describes the book's structure and reads one of its entries. It does not judge the reliability of any report, and it does not cover the other volumes of the edition (volumes 7 and 8 are held in the same scans). Those volumes need reading before any claim about them is made.
