## Summary
Al-Zuhd wa-l-Raqa'iq ("Renunciation and heart-softening reports") is the book of [[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Abu Abd al-Rahman Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak ibn Wadih al-Hanzali]], Turkic and then of Marw (d. 181 AH). It gathers reports with chains, from the Prophet, from Companions and from later figures, on renunciation and softening of the heart, arranged in chapters beginning with "Urging obedience to God". {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-shamela p. 1} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1} Its editor Ahmad Farid quotes Fuat Sezgin as calling it "the oldest book that has reached us" on renunciation from this period. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 46}

## Its place among books on renunciation
[[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] says that most compilers of the Six Books included a book on renunciation or heart-softening, that a number of scholars devoted separate works to it, and that al-Faryiwa'i counted seventy-six such works; ordering the printed ones by their authors' death dates, he puts Ibn al-Mubarak's first. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 46} He notes that earlier books existed but have not survived, among them the Zuhd of Za'ida ibn Qudama (d. 160 AH). {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 46} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 47} He quotes [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] as counting it among the greatest books written on renunciation. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 53}

The description at the start of the digital file says that sound chains predominate in its material, although its subject is the merits of deeds, and links this with Ibn al-Mubarak's well-known saying "In sound hadith there is enough to keep one from the unsound", as al-Dhahabi reports it in the Siyar. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-shamela p. 1}

## The two recensions
The book has two recensions: that of [[person/husayn-ibn-al-hasan-al-marwazi|al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan al-Marwazi]] and that of [[person/nuaym-ibn-hammad|Nu'aym ibn Hammad]]. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 47} Al-Marwazi's text comes through [[person/yahya-ibn-muhammad-ibn-saaid|Yahya ibn Muhammad ibn Sa'id]] and contains "additions" by Ibn Sa'id and al-Marwazi from their own teachers, not through Ibn al-Mubarak, which the editor marks in the footnotes. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 47} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 127}

The chain of the recension opens the copy: Abu Ghalib Ahmad ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Banna', from Abu Muhammad al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Jawhari, from [[person/ibn-hayyuya-al-khazzaz|Abu Umar Muhammad ibn al-Abbas ibn Hayyuya]], from Ibn Sa'id "on Thursday, six days into Rabi' al-Awwal of the year 315, at the door of his house", from al-Marwazi in the year 245, from Ibn al-Mubarak. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1} In most reports Ibn Hayyuya is paired with [[person/abu-bakr-al-warraq-al-mustamli|Abu Bakr al-Warraq]]. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 2} The editor notes that Ibn Hayyuya heard some parts in 315 and others in Dhu al-Qa'da 309, as the opening of part 7 shows, which al-Jawhari read out on Monday 6 Jumada al-Akhira 454. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 304}

## Structure: parts and chapters
The text is divided by the parts of the original manuscript; among its chapter headings in al-A'zami's text are: {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 303} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 304}

- Urging obedience to God, with the hadith "two blessings in which many people are cheated"; the editor notes that al-Bukhari opened his Book of Heart-Softening with it. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 2}
- Seeking knowledge for worldly gain; warning of the consequences of sins; the merit of worship; grief and weeping; hidden deeds and remembrance; humility and fear; striving in worship; sincerity and intention; the greatness of remembering God. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 15} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 22} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 30} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 40} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 45} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 50} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 58} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 62} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 70}
- Reflection while following funerals; against long hopes; remembering death; taking heed and reflecting; fleeing from sins. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 82} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 85} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 90} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 95} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 100}
- The truthful companion; guarding the tongue; humility; trust in God; the censure of showing off and self-admiration; the repentance of David and mention of the prophets; having little of this world; the world's insignificance before God. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 120} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 125} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 132} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 143} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 151} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 161} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 175} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 177}
- Satisfaction and contentment; poverty; seeking the lawful; charity; kindness to orphans; avarice; intention with little action; reconciling people; the censure of luxury; the taking away of knowledge. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 197} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 199} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 209} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 226} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 229} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 230} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 248} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 256} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 262} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 281}
- Chapters on named ascetics: [[person/uwais-al-qarni|Uways]] and al-Sunabihi; [[person/amir-ibn-abd-al-qays-al-anbari|Amir ibn Abd Qays]] and [[person/sila-ibn-ashyam|Sila ibn Ashyam]]; reports of [[person/abu-rayhana-shimun|Abu Rayhana]]; reports of [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]]. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 293} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 294} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 304} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 308}
- God's mercy; the merit of remembering God. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 312} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 339}

## Method of transmission
Every report is given with its chain from Ibn al-Mubarak through his teacher to its speaker, and the narrators' remarks appear between reports: after the reports on Uways the text quotes Ibn Sa'id saying that "the chains of the hadith of Uways are all sound, transmitted by trustworthy men from trustworthy men". {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 294} Al-Marwazi sometimes adds another teacher's version alongside Ibn al-Mubarak's as corroboration, as the editor notes for the first report, from al-Fadl ibn Musa. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-azami-scan p. 1}

## Editions
- Habib al-Rahman al-A'zami's edition of al-Marwazi's recension with the additions of Ibn Sa'id and al-Marwazi, 1,627 reports, followed by 436 reports in Nu'aym ibn Hammad's recension; first printed in India, then by Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya in Beirut, with a reprint in Alexandria. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 47} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 53}
- Ahmad Farid's edition (Dar al-Mi'raj al-Dawliyya, Riyadh 1415 AH / 1995), which follows al-A'zami's text of al-Marwazi's recension but removes the students' additions not through Ibn al-Mubarak, leaving 1,206 reports; it gives biographies of the book's 1,041 narrators in an appended dictionary and grades the chains. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 2} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 55} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 56} He says reports up to no. 594 are transmitted by two students of Ibn Sa'id, and from no. 595 by one. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 57}
- Adil Murshid's edition of al-Marwazi's recension (Dar al-Faruq, Amman 1443 AH / 2022), based on two manuscripts, which according to its title page restores what earlier printings omitted. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-murshid-2022-v2 p. 1} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-murshid-2022-v2 p. 3}

## The editor Ahmad Farid's view of renunciation (a modern view)
:::perspectives
- **Ahl al-Sunna: Ahmad Farid (the editor's view, modern).** In his introduction he holds that the renunciation he calls for is "Salafi renunciation", and contrasts it with what he calls "the innovated renunciation of the Sufis", which he sees as based on celibacy and giving up marriage; he cites the Prophet's prohibition of celibacy and of excess in worship, and holds that Ibn al-Mubarak's life, as a merchant earning lawful wealth, exemplifies the renunciation he means. {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 20} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 22} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 23} {cite:zuhd-ibn-mubarak-farid-1995-v1 p. 33}

## For young visitors
In this book Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak gathered hadith and sayings that teach a person not to tie the heart to this world, and to remember death and the hereafter. He also told stories of ascetics such as Uways al-Qarani.
