## Summary

A question on selling animal semen and how it differs from the prohibition on stud fees, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that selling semen extracted from a pure animal is permitted, and that the prohibition concerns hiring a stud to produce a pregnancy, because of the uncertainty involved. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3854}

## The question

The questioner asked whether selling animal semen is allowed, and how it differs from the prohibition on paying for a stud's covering. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3854}

## The answer

The Department answered that there is no objection to selling animal semen or charging for inseminating with it if it comes from a pure animal other than a dog or a pig, since if selling the whole is allowed, selling a part is too. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3854} It said the reason for the prohibition on stud fees is that hiring a stud to produce a pregnancy is uncertain, since pregnancy is not guaranteed and the benefit is neither known nor deliverable, as [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani]] stated in Fath al-Bari; none of these problems arise in selling extracted semen itself. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3854} It quoted [[person/ibn-hajar-al-haytami|Ibn Hajar al-Haytami]]'s al-Zawajir and [[person/ali-al-shabramallisi|al-Shabramallisi]]'s gloss on Nihayat al-Muhtaj that someone who hires a stud for a set time to use as he wishes may use it for covering as a by-product rather than as the purpose. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3854} It concluded that hiring a stud for a set sum and a set time for general use is a valid hire, and the hirer may use it for covering as a by-product. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3854}
