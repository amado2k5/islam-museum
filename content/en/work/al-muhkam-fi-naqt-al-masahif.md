## Summary
Al-Muhkam fi Naqt al-Masahif is by Abu Amr Uthman ibn Sa'id al-Dani. The bibliography of the King Fahd edition of the Itqan lists an edition by Dr 'Izza Hasan, Dar al-Fikr, Damascus, second printing, 1407 AH. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 3014} The editor of the Taysir says he will follow the Muqni' with the Kitab al-Naqt. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 10}

## Who first dotted the mushafs
Al-Suyuti, at a passage for which the notes refer to the Muhkam, says there is disagreement about dotting and vowelling the mushaf: it is said that Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali was the first, by order of 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan; others say al-Hasan al-Basri and Yahya ibn Ya'mar, others Nasr ibn 'Asim al-Laythi; and that al-Khalil first set the hamza, shadda, rawm and ishmam; Qatada said they began with dots, then marked fives, then tens. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2245} He reports from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir that they knew nothing introduced into the mushafs except the three dots at the heads of verses. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2245} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2246} The notes say al-Dani gives the report in the Muhkam with a chain to Abu 'Ubayd. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2246}

## Al-Dani's view
Al-Suyuti quotes al-Dani that he does not allow dotting in black ink because it alters the form of the rasm, nor gathering several readings in one mushaf in different colours, which he calls the greatest confusion; he would have the vowels, tanwin, shadda, sukun and madd in red and the hamzas in yellow. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249} He adds that vowelling in the first age was by dots: the fatha a dot above the start of the letter, the damma at its end, the kasra below its start, and that al-Dani followed this. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249}

## A related title
Al-Qalqashandi cites al-Dani's al-Tanbih 'ala Naqt al-Masahif wa-Shaklaha in discussing the origin of Arabic writing. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 12} This record does not decide whether it is the same book or another title.

## What was not read
The text of the Muhkam was not read here, and only the opinions the Itqan reports from it are attributed to it. See [[concept/rasm-al-quran|the rasm of the Qur'an]] and [[person/abu-al-aswad-al-duali|Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali]].
