## Summary
*al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an wa'l-Mubayyin li-ma Tadammanahu min al-Sunna wa-Ay al-Furqan* ("The Compendium of the Qur'an's Rulings, Explaining the Sunna and Verses It Contains") is a commentary on the whole Qur'an by Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Bakr ibn Farh al-Ansari al-Khazraji of al-Andalus, later of Córdoba — [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]] (d. 671 AH) — and is known as Tafsir al-Qurtubi. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.1} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.5} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.8} It is distinguished by its attention to the legal content of the Qur'an, dividing every verse that carries a ruling into questions (masa'il). {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.8} See also [[event/al-qurtubi-composes-jami-670|al-Qurtubi's composition of the Jami']].

## The author's purpose
Al-Qurtubi opens by saying that God entrusted His Messenger with explaining what the Book states briefly and clarifying what is obscure, and then entrusted scholars with drawing out what it points to, "so the Book became the root, the Sunna its explanation, and the scholars' derivation its clarification". {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.7} Since God's Book contains all the sciences of the Law, he resolved to devote his life to it by writing a "concise commentary" containing points of interpretation, lexicon, grammar and readings, rebuttals of the deviant and misguided, and many hadith attesting the rulings and occasions of revelation he mentions, explaining what is difficult by the sayings of the early generations and those who followed them. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.7} He made it "a reminder for himself and a provision for the day of his burial". {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.8}

## His rule and method
Al-Qurtubi writes: "My rule in this book is to attribute sayings to those who said them and hadith to those who compiled them, for it is said that part of the blessing of knowledge is to attribute a saying to its speaker." {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.8} He explains that hadith often appear in books of fiqh and tafsir without attribution, recognisable only to those who know the hadith collections, so the inexpert reader is left unable to tell sound from weak, and a hadith may not be used as proof until it is attributed to the imam who compiled it. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.8} He says he left out many of the commentators' stories and the historians' reports, except what is indispensable, and instead set out the legal verses through questions that reveal their meaning: every verse containing one ruling or more is given numbered questions on its occasion of revelation, rare words and ruling, while verses without a ruling receive interpretation and explanation. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.8}

## His introduction on the Qur'anic sciences
Al-Qurtubi prefaced his commentary with long chapters, among them: the merits of the Qur'an; how it is to be recited and what is disliked; what befits one who carries the Qur'an; its grammatical analysis; the merit of tafsir and its people; the warning against interpreting the Qur'an by opinion; the explanation of the Book by the Sunna; the meaning of the hadith of [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa|the seven ahruf]]; the collection of the Qur'an; the order of its suras and verses, its vowelling, dotting and division into parts and tenths; the meaning of sura and verse; words from outside the Arabs' languages; inimitability; a warning on hadith forged about the merits of suras; and the reply to those who attack the Qur'an. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.9} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.18} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.41} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.56} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.64} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.71} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.83} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.96} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.106} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.110} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.112} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.122} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.126}

Among the positions in these chapters:
- **The order of the suras:** he reports the judge Abu Bakr ibn al-Tayyib's view that the order may have been the Companions' own judgement, and the view of others, among them Malik and Abu Bakr ibn al-Anbari, that it was fixed by the Prophet; see [[question/is-the-order-of-the-suras-fixed-by-revelation|Is the order of the suras fixed by revelation?]]. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.97} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.98}
- **Hadith forged on the merits of suras:** "no attention is to be paid to what the forgers forged" about the merits of the suras and other matters; he classifies the forgers and mentions Abu Isma Nuh ibn Abi Maryam and the long hadith of Ubayy on the merit of each sura in turn; see [[concept/wad-al-hadith|fabrication of hadith]]. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.122} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.123} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.124}

## Its link with Ibn Atiyya's commentary
Majd Makki, introducing [[work/tafsir-ibn-atiyya|al-Muharrar al-Wajiz]], lists al-Qurtubi's commentary among the four books in which Ibn Atiyya's influence is plain, and says that al-Qurtubi followed him in citing some weak and forged hadith. {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.8} {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.9}
