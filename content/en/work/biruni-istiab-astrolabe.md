## Work and section read

[[person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad|Al-Biruni]] authored the full discussion of possible astrolabe constructions, Istiab al-wujuh al-mumkina fi sanat al-asturlab. This entry particularly records the Moon Box section edited, translated and technically discussed by Hill, not a complete reading of every chapter or a certain inventory of every instrument. It must not be merged with Ibn Abd al-Barr's unrelated biographical Istiab: similar titles do not identify one work {cite:hill-biruni-calendar-1985 pp.139–142}.

## Description, inheritance and modification

Hill associates the older machine with Hellenistic/Byzantine antecedents and leaves its original inventor unknown. The account mentions other craftsmen's variants before Hill attributes a concluding simplified proposal to the author. The work therefore links here to [[invention/biruni-simplified-calendar-gearing|al-Biruni's proposed simplified gearing]], not a claim that he invented every box or gear described {cite:hill-biruni-calendar-1985 pp.140,157–158,163}.

## Arithmetic and limits

The basic account lists eight wheels of differing tooth counts, manually advanced weekly input and representation of mean motions. The proposal attaches four teeth to the forty-tooth arbor, then meshes four with fifty-two, yielding364days in the engineering reading. The translation mentions360; this discrepancy is not erased by replacing the supposed author's number with the interpreter's result {cite:hill-biruni-calendar-1985 pp.156–163}.

The28day sidereal lunar period differs from the29.5day mean phase cycle. Both are approximations, not guaranteed crescent visibility or exact daily celestial positions. The basic train yields about366.42days, not the proposal's output; fewer parts do not establish greater accuracy. The invention entry compares these by modern arithmetic while preserving the text's limits {cite:hill-biruni-calendar-1985 pp.159–163}.
