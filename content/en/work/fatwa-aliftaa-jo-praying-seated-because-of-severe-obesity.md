## Summary

A question about praying seated because of severe obesity, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that someone who suffers severe, unbearable hardship or fears harm because of obesity, or loses humility (khushu') by standing, may pray on a chair or on the floor, while performing whatever pillars he is able to. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4515}

## The question

Someone who finds prayer hard because of severe obesity, especially rising from the floor after prostration, asked about praying seated. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4515}

## The answer

The Department answered that standing, when one is able, is a pillar of prayer; someone who suffers severe, unbearable hardship, fears harm to himself or a limb because of obesity, or loses his humility in prayer by standing, may pray on a chair or on the floor, gesturing for bowing and prostration with the prostration lower than the bowing. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4515} It said that whoever is able at any point to perform one of these pillars must perform it fully, since leaving it is a concession limited to inability, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] whose meaning is that one should do as much of a command as one can, and the jurists' maxim that what is possible is not dropped because of what is impossible; it noted that [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|al-Juwayni]] chose as the criterion of inability a hardship that takes away the worshipper's humility. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4515} It added that voluntary prayer may be performed seated even by someone able to stand, but bowing and prostration must be performed by whoever is able to. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4515}
