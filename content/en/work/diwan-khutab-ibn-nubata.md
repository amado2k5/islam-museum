## Summary
The *Diwan al-Khutab* is the collected sermons of [[person/ibn-nabbata|Abu Yahya 'Abd al-Rahim ibn Muhammad ibn Isma'il ibn Nubata al-Fariqi]], called "the preacher of preachers", preacher of [[place/aleppo|Aleppo]] in the service of [[person/sayf-al-dawla|Sayf al-Dawla ibn Hamdan]]. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 14} His notice, taken from Ibn Khallikan, says men of letters agreed that "nothing like them was ever composed". It adds that Sayf al-Dawla campaigned often, "so the preacher composed many sermons on jihad to urge people to it". {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 14}

## Contents
- In this edition the sermons are grouped as follows: sermons on the hereafter; sermons on death; sermons for the seasons, such as the new year and the merit of Ashura; "a sermon recalling the death of the Messenger of God", given in Rabi' al-Awwal; the farewell to Ramadan; sermons on jihad; the sermon of the Day of Sacrifice; and marriage sermons. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 5-13} {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 p. 133-136}
- Each sermon opens with praise in paired rhymed phrases, then the testimony of faith and the blessing on the Prophet, then "O people" and the exhortation, and closes with a verse. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 p. 3} {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 p. 136-137}
- Among the jihad sermons is one "on jihad and calming the people after an alarm and fear that befell them when the enemy took Aleppo in Dhu al-Qa'da 351 AH". In it he reminds his hearers of God's promise of victory. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 p. 191-192}
- The commentator says the preacher composed some sermons at sixteen, namely those he delivered in 351 AH. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 15}

## The "Dream Sermon"
In the notice taken from Ibn Khallikan, Taj al-Din al-Kindi relates with his chain to Ibn Nubata that he composed a sermon about the dead in their graves and gave it on a Friday. That night he dreamt he saw the Prophet among the graves outside Mayyafariqin, and the Prophet called him "preacher of preachers"; the sermon is therefore known as al-Manamiyya, "the Dream Sermon". The story rests on al-Kindi's chain and is given here as a report. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 14-15}

## Commentaries and criticism
- The commentator lists commentaries on the Diwan. They are by [[person/al-ukbari-abu-al-baqa|Abu al-Baqa' al-'Ukbari]] (d. 616 AH), Taj al-Din al-Kindi (d. 613 AH), and [[person/abd-al-latif-al-baghdadi|'Abd al-Latif al-Baghdadi]] (d. 629 AH), who answered al-Kindi's objections. Further ones are by 'Uthman ibn Yusuf al-Qalyubi (d. 644 AH), one called Rawdat al-Nasihin, one by Sari al-Din ibn Hani', and a brief one by Najm al-Din al-Barizi. Al-Qarafi also wrote a book answering questions raised about the sermons. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 16-17}
- He notes that [[person/ibn-al-athir-diya-al-din|Diya' al-Din ibn al-Athir]] quoted the sermons in al-Mathal al-Sa'ir and criticised some of their words. Ibn al-Athir also objected that in most of their rhymed phrases the two members say the same thing. The commentator disputes this requirement, citing Abu Hilal al-'Askari on the schools of eloquence. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 17-18}

## This edition
It was printed at the Matba'at Jaridat Bayrut in 1319 AH at the charge of al-Sayyid 'Abd al-Basit al-Unsi, with a philological commentary by Shaykh [[person/tahir-al-jazairi|Tahir al-Jaza'iri]], and is followed by the sermons of his son Abu Tahir Muhammad. {cite:diwan-khutab-ibn-nubata-beirut-1319 front 25}
