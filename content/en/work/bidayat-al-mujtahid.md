## Summary
Bidayat al-Mujtahid wa-Nihayat al-Muqtasid — Ibn Rushd's book on fiqh, counted by al-Zirikli among roughly fifty works in his biographical entry. {cite:al-aalam p. 849}

## Details
Al-Zirikli's entry describes Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Rushd al-Andalusi, Abu al-Walid — the Cordoban philosopher (d. 595 AH) the Franks called Averroes — who devoted himself to Aristotle's works, translated them into Arabic with many additions, and composed about fifty books, among them Bidayat al-Mujtahid wa-Nihayat al-Muqtasid in jurisprudence. {cite:al-aalam p. 849}

The same entry names his Falsafat Ibn Rushd, al-Tahsil collecting the scholars' disagreements, Fasl al-Maqal on the accord of philosophy and revealed law, al-Daruri on logic, Minhaj al-Adilla on legal theory, and Tahafut al-Tahafut answering al-Ghazali; his books were rendered into Latin and Hebrew. Accused before the caliph al-Mansur, he was exiled to Marrakesh and some of his books burned, then restored to favor; he died at Marrakesh and was carried back to Cordoba. {cite:al-aalam p. 849}
