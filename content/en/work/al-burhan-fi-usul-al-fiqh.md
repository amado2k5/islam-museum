## Summary
Al-Burhan fi Usul al-Fiqh is a work of legal theory by [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|Imam al-Haramayn Abu al-Ma'ali Abd al-Malik ibn Abd Allah ibn Yusuf al-Juwayni]]. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 1} He opens by saying that whoever takes up a discipline should first grasp its aim, the materials it draws on, and its nature and definition, and then states that legal theory draws on kalam, Arabic and substantive law. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 2}

## Structure
Al-Juwayni begins with introductions on the materials of legal theory, reason, knowledge and exposition, then has chapters on commands (p. 83), prohibitions (p. 139), general and specific (p. 162), interpretations (pp. 186-228), abrogation, the Prophet's acts (p. 274) and the laws of earlier prophets (p. 286); then the book of reports (p. 325), the "second book" on consensus (p. 390), then analogy, including the chapter on "the divisions of causes and bases" (p. 551), the "fourth book" on inference (p. 674), the "fifth book" on preference (p. 694), and then fatwa and ijtihad. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 83} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 390} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 551} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 674} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 694}

## The five kinds of legal bases
Al-Juwayni divides the bases of the law, according to how far their meaning can be grasped, into five kinds: {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 551}
1. those whose meaning comes down to an indispensable necessity, such as the obligation of retaliation, explained by the inviolability of protected lives and deterrence from attacking them; the validity of sale belongs here, since if people did not exchange what they hold a manifest necessity would follow; {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 551}
2. those tied to general need short of necessity, such as the validity of hire, because of the need for dwellings; he holds that the need of the whole kind can reach the level of one individual's necessity; {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 551}
3. those tied to neither necessity nor need, but showing an aim of securing a noble trait or removing its opposite, such as purification from ritual impurity and removing filth; {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 552}
4. those resting on neither need nor necessity, where the aim is explicitly recommended, such as the manumission contract (kitaba) that secures freedom; {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 552}
5. those in which no meaning appears to the reasoner at all, such as purely bodily acts of worship and fixed numbers like the count of prayer units. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 552} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 553}
He then explains how analogy operates in each kind, holding that in the first kind a particular analogy that clashes with the universal rule is abandoned for the rule - as in executing a group for killing one person, since strict equivalence would destroy the necessary rule of protecting life. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 553} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 555} He holds that sale rests on necessity or on need equivalent to it, and that whoever says hire departs from analogy "lacks insight". {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 557} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 560} See: [[concept/maqasid-al-shariah|Objectives of the Shari'a]].

## Inference
Al-Juwayni defines istidlal as a meaning that suggests and suits a ruling to rational reflection without an agreed base, reports three positions on it, and adopts al-Shafi'i's - accepting it on condition that it be close to the meanings of the bases. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 674} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 675} See: [[question/is-unattested-inference-istidlal-a-proof|Is unattested inference a valid proof?]]

## Sources and interlocutors
Al-Juwayni often reports and discusses the views of [[person/al-baqillani|al-Qadi Abu Bakr al-Baqillani]], for example on distinguishing trivial from valuable property and on the penalty for a small amount of wine. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 549} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 550} He discusses the schools of [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] and [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] at length. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 559} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 678}

## The ending
Al-Juwayni closes the book with a question on a mujtahid's wavering views, holding that al-Shafi'i settled on a single view in most questions, leaving only eighteen cases undecided, that his old views do not count as his school since he retracted them, and noting that Abu Hanifa took doubt as his position on the leftover water of a donkey. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 854} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p. 855}
