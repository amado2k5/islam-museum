## Summary

The fifth session opens the period of the prophetic mission. The speaker sets out the books he will rely on from here, discusses why the age of forty matters and how commentators read the age of full strength, compares the two long accounts of the start of revelation (Aisha's report in al-Bukhari and Ubayd ibn Umayr's report in Ibn Ishaq), and closes with the preparatory signs before revelation and the Prophet's retreat in Hira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

### Opening the mission and the reference books

- He says that the sira in the narrow sense begins with the mission, even though the books of the genre also cover Arab conditions, lineage and birth before it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that from this session he will read most of Ibn Hisham's recension of Ibn Ishaq, drawing on other books alongside it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He holds that the sira section of Ibn Kathir's al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya is outstanding because it gathers scattered and rare reports in one place, but that it is a poor choice for continuous reading because it piles up chains and long reports of weak standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He names al-Suhayli's al-Rawd al-Unuf, a commentary on Ibn Hisham, and the commentaries on hadith, above all Ibn Hajar's Fath al-Bari, as further companions, together with works of Qur'an commentary for verses tied to events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He says the aim of each session is twofold: gathering the sources, then drawing out lessons and comment from them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

### The covenant of the prophets and the mission at forty

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq begins the mission narrative by saying that Muhammad was sent as a mercy to all people at the age of forty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that God took a covenant from every earlier prophet to believe in him and support him, and that they passed this on to their followers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He says he treats the verse on the covenant only briefly here, having passed it earlier, and moves to the matter of the age. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

### The age of forty and the age of full strength in the commentators

- He says that forty is the well-known age of the mission among the great majority of scholars, while specialist sources discuss details of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that commentators generally take full strength and the reaching of forty, named together in one verse of surat al-Ahqaf, to be different things, and that Ibn Ashur argues this from the repeated verb and the conjunction. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Ashur defines full strength as the peak of mental and bodily powers, not a fixed year, with its time after thirty and its completion at forty, so that the two overlap. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that al-Tabari reports through Mujahid that full strength means thirty-three and that the other verse's word for reaching maturity means forty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that al-Tabari argues from Arabic usage that joining two terms with a conjunction implies they are close in value, so forty is closely linked to thirty-three rather than to the age of puberty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He concludes that the Prophet was sent after reaching full strength and completing forty, and that God chose that age with wisdom, not before it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He draws a practical lesson that age affects maturity and capacity for burdens, so a person might plan major reforming work around the years of full strength, with some decline in bodily strength after forty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Precision about the age and the duration of the Meccan period

- He relates that Ibn Kathir gives forty as the age at the mission and also mentions a report through Ibn Jarir from Ibn Abbas and Said ibn al-Musayyab of forty-three. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Abd al-Barr, in al-Tamhid, cites the report of Anas that the Prophet was sent at forty and stayed ten years in Mecca and ten in Medina, and he says scholars differ on how this squares with the thirteen years usually counted in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir reports from al-Shabi a report that Israfil was joined to the Prophet for three years before Gabriel, and that Ibn Kathir calls its chain sound up to al-Shabi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar, in Fath al-Bari, reads this three-year period as the pause in revelation, meaning a delay in the Qur'an coming down, not Gabriel's absence. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He holds that al-Shabi's report is a respected mursal, not certain, and that the length of the pause may be left undetermined; he defers the pause itself to the next session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He notes that the report of ten Meccan years occurs even in al-Bukhari and that Ibn Hajar's reading may reconcile it with thirteen. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Two accounts of the first revelation and their chains

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq opens with Aisha's report through al-Zuhri and Urwa, giving only its opening lines about the true dream and the love of solitude. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that al-Bukhari gives the full story of Aisha through Yahya ibn Bukayr, al-Layth, Uqayl, Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri and Urwa, so the two chains meet at al-Zuhri. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq takes the long narrative from a second chain, Wahb ibn Kaysan hearing Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr ask Ubayd ibn Umayr how the revelation began. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He holds that this scene shows a Companion asking a senior successor, and so rejects treating every mursal report as equally weak. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He says Ubayd ibn Umayr was born in the Prophet's lifetime, was a preacher in Mecca in the time of Umar according to Thabit al-Bunani, and died shortly before Ibn Umar; he adds that al-Bukhari claimed a sighting of the Prophet for him though most scholars count him a successor. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He holds that Aisha's report in the two Sahihs takes precedence in any conflict, while Ubayd's report is accepted in general and may be used where it does not clash or give offence. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The tahannuth in Hira and the linguistic note of Ibn Hisham

- He relates that between the two accounts Ibn Ishaq inserts a report through Abd al-Malik ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Sufyan that stones and trees greeted the Prophet as a messenger when he walked into the valleys of Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates from Ubayd ibn Umayr that the Prophet spent a month each year at Hira, a practice Quraysh called tahannuth, that he fed the poor who came, and that on leaving he would first circle the Kaaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham notes the Arabs swap the letters fa and tha, so tahannuth and tahannuf both point to the hanifiyya, and he gives the examples jadath and jadaf and a verse of Ruba, and he cites a verse of Abu Talib as a witness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham, via Abu Ubayda, gives a further example of this swap and that some commentators explain a word for food in surat al-Baqara this way. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

### Ubayd ibn Umayr's account of the descent of Gabriel

- He relates that in this account, in Ramadan, Gabriel came to the Prophet while he slept with a silk wrapper carrying writing and pressed him three times before the first words were delivered. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that in this account the Prophet then heard a voice from the sky declaring him God's messenger, saw Gabriel as a man filling the horizon, and stood fixed until Khadija's messengers returned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Khadija took him to her cousin Waraqa ibn Nawfal, who had turned Christian and read the scriptures, and that Waraqa affirmed he was the prophet of this community and would be denied, harmed, expelled and fought. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Waraqa later met the Prophet at the Kaaba, repeated this, and kissed his head. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

### Aisha's report in al-Bukhari

- He says he reads Aisha's report from al-Bukhari, not from Ibn Hisham, because Ibn Hisham gives only part of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that in it the revelation began with true dreams, then love of seclusion in the cave of Hira, where he worshipped for nights before returning to his family for provisions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that the angel came to him in the cave, pressed him three times, and delivered the opening of the revelation, after which he returned trembling to Khadija and asked to be wrapped. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Khadija reassured him by listing his character and took him to Waraqa ibn Nawfal, who wrote in Hebrew from the Gospel, was old and blind, and said this was the same angel sent to Moses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Waraqa wished to be alive when the Prophet's people drove him out and promised support, that he died soon after, and that the revelation then paused. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

### Waking or sleeping: reconciling the two reports

- He holds that the revelation certainly came in wakefulness and that Gabriel truly came to the Prophet at Hira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that al-Suhayli, Ibn Hajar and Ibn Kathir reconcile the reports by supposing a sleeping vision first, as a preparation, before the waking descent; Ibn Kathir cites Musa ibn Uqba from al-Zuhri for this order. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Abu Shama is reported to have discussed whether such preparation by dream is special to Muhammad, and that Abu Nuaym in Dalail al-Nubuwwa gives a saying through Alqama ibn Qays that prophets first receive in dreams until their hearts settle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He holds personally that the better course is to prefer Aisha's report and treat the sleep detail in Ubayd's mursal as an error, not a reconciliation, since Ubayd's story ends with the Prophet waking, while stressing no firm barrier stands against the reconciling view. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Preparation before revelation: dreams and greetings of the stones

- He relates that Ibn Hajar explains the likeness of the dream to the break of dawn as stressing its clarity, and says the dreams prepared the way for the waking vision. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that many scholars take the period of the true dreams to be six months, and some tie the saying of the forty-sixth part of prophethood to this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates the report in Sahih Muslim that the Prophet knew a stone in Mecca that used to greet him, and ties it to Ibn Ishaq's report of stones and trees saluting him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He grades Ibn Ishaq's greeting report as weak: it comes through an unknown teacher, a general account of the Meccan period, placed by him in the fourth of five ranks of Ibn Ishaq's sources. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar says earlier preparations included the elephant incident and the opening of the chest in Banu Sad, and that he separates the people's preparation, such as scriptural prophecies, from the Prophet's own, near or far. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar points to Joseph's early dream and later words as a parallel showing dreams as prophets' preparation, and he cites Umm Ayman weeping after the Prophet's death over the end of revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}

### Love of seclusion as a setting for worship

- He relates that Ibn Kathir explains the Prophet's love of seclusion as a result of seeing his people's idol worship, and says it grew as revelation neared. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Aisha's wording says a love of seclusion was placed in him, so God made it dear to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He holds that seclusion is a setting for worship such as night prayer, supplication and reflection, not worship in itself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Ashur, on the verse about the night hours, explains the word for night prayer and says the daytime was filled with calling, teaching and arguing with idolaters, leaving no seclusion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Taymiyya is reported in his biography to have gone at times to abandoned mosques to pray and supplicate. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep5}
