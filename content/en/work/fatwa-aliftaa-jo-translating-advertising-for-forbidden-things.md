## Summary

A question from a website translator who sometimes translates restaurant and hotel offers involving alcohol or resorts for gay people, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that translation is permitted in principle, but not where it directly helps or promotes what is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3894}

## The question

The questioner said he works as a translator for a website and sometimes translates restaurant and hotel offers involving alcohol, or resorts for gay people; he asked about the ruling on his work. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3894}

## The answer

The Department answered that translation work is permitted in principle, provided its fields are not tied to directly assisting or promoting forbidden things. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3894} It said material that assists the forbidden may not be translated, such as advertising for what the Department regards as vice and corruption, or for drinking alcohol, pointing to the Qur'anic command to cooperate in righteousness and piety and not in sin. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3894} It concluded that translating material tied to or promoting the forbidden is not permitted, while translating what has no such connection is permitted. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3894}

## About this record

Jordan's General Iftaa Department published this fatwa on aliftaa.jo as no. 3894, dated 2 July 2024. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3894} The museum records this text as a historical document attributed to the body or scholar that issued it; it issues no ruling of its own.
