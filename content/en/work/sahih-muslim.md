## Overview
"Al-Musnad al-Sahih al-Mukhtasar bi-Naql al-Adl an al-Adl ila Rasul Allah" (The Concise Authentic Collection of Reports Transmitted by the Reliable from the Reliable to the Messenger of God), by Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj al-Qushayri al-Nisaburi (d. 261 AH) ([[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|his biography]]). It is one of the [[concept/kutub-al-sitta|Six Books]]. {cite:sahih-muslim}

It is regarded as the second most famous book of hadith. It is known for bringing together the different chains of a single hadith in one place. Its introduction on the terminology of hadith became a foundational text in the science of hadith. {cite:sahih-muslim}

## Structure and size
Sunnah.com describes Sahih Muslim as containing roughly 7,500 hadith (counting repetitions) in 57 books. Its English translation is by Abdul Hamid Siddiqui {cite:sunnah-com muslim}. Together with Sahih al-Bukhari it is known as the two Sahihs.

## In «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'» by Abu Ishaq al-Heweny
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] shows viewers a copy of the first edition of Sahih Muslim from the Halabi press, which he calls rare, and says scholars agree that the two Sahihs are the soundest books after the Book of God, but that this does not mean they are free of every error. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:02:30} He mentions a master's thesis on the corroborating chains in Sahih Muslim, and the commentaries and mustakhrajs on Muslim. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:06:30}

He says he explained Sahih Muslim in a Sunday lesson devoted to the chains and Muslim's craft in them, covering ninety hadiths of the Book of Faith, until the lesson was stopped some five or six years earlier, by order of the security authorities as he puts it, and never resumed. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:19:00} He says three or four volumes came out of it, which he hopes to publish, and that he had wished to write something on Muslim like «Fath al-Bari» on al-Bukhari. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:20:00}

He mentions the argument for preferring Muslim over al-Bukhari, that Muslim wrote in his teachers' lifetimes and kept their exact words, and says he wanted to test it by induction, so he gathered the hadiths in which the two agree from the first teacher to the end of the chain and found close to two hundred and forty-five. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:14:00} He says the book stalled because Muslim sometimes gives a chain and refers the text to an earlier version, so he divided it into two parts, and that he knows of no precedent for it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep24 00:20:00} His work on Muslim also includes his edition of [[person/abu-awana-yaqub-ibn-ishaq|Abu Awana]]'s mustakhraj on it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep30 00:18:30}

## In the introduction to al-Nawawi's commentary
- **Transmission:** [[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] says the book is mass-transmitted from Muslim as a whole, while its connected transmission had narrowed in his day to the route of Abu Ishaq Ibrahim b. Muhammad b. Sufyan from Muslim, besides that of Abu Muhammad al-Qalanisi in the Maghrib; he himself transmits it through six men between him and Muslim. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 6} {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 11-12}
- **Its making:** he relays from Makki b. 'Abdan Muslim's statement that he showed his book to Abu Zur'a al-Razi and left out whatever Abu Zur'a flagged as defective, and from al-Khatib Muslim's statement that he compiled it from three hundred thousand hadiths he had heard. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 15}
- **Its distinction:** in al-Nawawi's view Muslim is unique in giving each hadith a single place where its routes and wordings are gathered, unlike al-Bukhari, who scatters the variants across distant chapters — though he holds al-Bukhari's book the sounder. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 14-15}
- **Abu Zur'a's and Ibn Wara's reproach:** al-Nawawi relates from Sa'id b. 'Amr al-Bardha'i that Abu Zur'a objected to Muslim's narrating in the Sahih from Asbat b. Nasr, Qatan b. Nusayr and Ahmad b. 'Isa, saying it would let innovators claim of a hadith that "it is not in the Sahih"; Muslim answered that he had included from them only what reliable men had also transmitted, which had reached him from them by a shorter chain; later Muhammad b. Muslim b. Wara rebuked him in Rayy in similar terms, and Muslim replied that he had called what he included sound, but never said that what he left out was weak. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 25-26}
- **What was built on it:** the [[concept/mustakhraj|mustakhrajat]] on it, and al-Daraqutni's [[work/al-ilzamat-ala-sahih-al-bukhari|"obligations"]] addressed to both Shaykhs. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 24} {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 26-27}
