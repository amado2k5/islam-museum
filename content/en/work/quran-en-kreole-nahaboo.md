# Quran en kreole ek tex arab (Houssein Nahaboo)

## Summary
This rendering of the meanings of the [[work/quran|Qur'an]] into Mauritian Creole, the everyday language of Mauritius, is by Houssein Nahaboo and was published in instalments from 1983. {cite:gloqur-nahaboo-mauritius}

## The translator
Nahaboo (1920–2000) was a successful dentist in Curepipe and the first Mauritian awarded a fellowship in dentistry by the Royal College of Surgeons; Johanna Pink describes him as a pious man engaged in da'wa. He studied Arabic and went to Riyadh to perfect it, where he was asked to revise a well-known French translation, which he did with Mahmud Baballi, though the revision was never published. {cite:gloqur-nahaboo-mauritius}

## What it contains
The work took five years and was published by the Islamic Students Movement and the Islamic Women's Movement in Port Louis, with the Arabic text in a facing column. {cite:gloqur-nahaboo-mauritius} He wrote it in a Creole using French spelling, to make it easier for literate readers, unlike activists who wanted a phonetic, independent Creole; it has no notes, so explanation is carried in the translation itself. {cite:gloqur-nahaboo-mauritius}

## Afterwards
Its good reception encouraged him to make English and French translations, published around 1987; the Creole one was reportedly reissued in a luxury single volume in the 2000s, though his translations are now hard to obtain. {cite:gloqur-nahaboo-mauritius}
