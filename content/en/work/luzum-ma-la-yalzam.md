## Summary
Luzum ma la Yalzam — al-Ma'arri's great diwan, running to about five volumes {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 288}.

## Details
In his biography of Abu al-'Ala' al-Ma'arri, citing Ibn Khallikan, Ibn al-'Imad lists among his verse works "Luzum ma la Yalzam" — a large book of about five volumes — together with Saqt al-Zand, which he also commented himself, Da' ma Daw' al-Saqt, and a work on hamza and radif in more than a hundred volumes {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 288}. The diwan is famed for its self-imposed rhyme constraint: its poet bound himself to requirements the craft does not impose, adding an extra letter before the alif and the like — hence its name and the nickname Luzumiyyat {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 288}. In it al-Ma'arri versified his reflections on the world, mankind and fate. He had studied grammar and philology under his father at Ma'arra and under Muhammad ibn 'Abdallah ibn Sa'd al-Nahwi at Aleppo, and was studied under by Abu al-Qasim ibn al-Muhsin al-Tanukhi and the khatib Abu Zakariyya al-Tabrizi, among others {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 288}.
