## Summary
*Al-Ibana 'an Usul al-Diyana* ("The Elucidation of the Foundations of Religion") is the book of [[person/al-ashari|Abu al-Hasan 'Ali ibn Isma'il al-Ash'ari]]; the title page of the Dar Ibn Zaydun edition says he died in "some twenty-odd and three hundred". {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 1} It treats the foundations of creed. It sets out the views of those it calls "the people of deviation and innovation", then "the view of the people of truth and the Sunna", arguing each point from verses and reports and in the form of question and answer. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 7-9}

## Its structure
- **The author's opening address**, praising God and declaring Him above all imperfection, then urging adherence to the Book and the Sunna. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 4-6}
- **A chapter on the view of the people of deviation and innovation.** He says many of [[group/mutazila|the Mu'tazila]] and the people of qadar interpreted the Qur'an by their own opinions. They contradicted the reports of the vision, denied intercession for sinners and the punishment of the grave, held that the Qur'an is created, and claimed that human beings create evil. Citing a report, he calls them "the Magians of this community". {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 7}
- **A chapter on the view of the people of truth and the Sunna.** Answering one who asks his position, he says it is to hold to God's Book, the Sunna of His Prophet and what is reported from the Companions, the Successors and the imams of hadith, "and we hold what Abu 'Abd Allah Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal held … and keep apart from whoever opposes his view". {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 8-9} He then sets out his creed in summary. God [[concept/istiwa-ala-al-arsh|rose over His Throne]]; He has a Face, two Hands without modality and an Eye without modality, with knowledge, hearing, sight and strength. [[concept/kalam-allah|God's speech]] is uncreated, human acts are created by God, and good and evil are by His decree. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 9-10} Further points: no one of the people of tawhid is assigned to Paradise or the Fire except those the Prophet named; God brings people out of the Fire through the Prophet's [[concept/shafaa|intercession]]; belief in [[concept/adhab-al-qabr|the punishment of the grave]], [[concept/al-hawd-al-nabawi|the Basin]], the Balance and the Bridge; faith is word and deed and increases and decreases; Abu Bakr ranks first, then 'Umar, 'Uthman and 'Ali; and one refrains from judging the disputes among the Companions. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 11}
- **The chapters that follow**: [[concept/ruyat-allah|the vision of God with the eyes in the hereafter]]; the Qur'an as God's uncreated speech, answering [[group/jahmiyya|the Jahmiyya]] and those who suspended judgement on it; the rising over the Throne; the Face, Eyes and Hands; knowledge and power; will; decree and capacity, with questions on obligation, the suffering of children, life-spans, provision, guidance and misguidance; intercession, the Basin and the punishment of the grave; and the imamate of Abu Bakr and the caliphs. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 75-76}

## On the rising over the Throne
In the chapter on istiwa' he says God is risen over His Throne, as He says in [[passage/quran-20-5|Ta-Ha 5]]. He argues from the verses on ascending and raising, from Pharaoh's words to Haman, and from the fact that Muslims raise their hands towards the sky when they pray. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 33-34} He answers the Mu'tazila, Jahmiyya and Haruriyya, who explain istiwa' as "taking control", that God controls all things, so that if istiwa' were control it would not be special to the Throne. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 34}

## On the caliphate and the Companions
The book ends with the chapter on Abu Bakr's imamate. It argues for the imamate of the four caliphs from Safina's report "the caliphate in my community is thirty years". It says that what passed between 'Ali, al-Zubayr and 'A'isha, and between 'Ali and Mu'awiya, arose from interpretation and independent judgement, and that all the Companions are trustworthy imams. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 73-74}

## This edition
Dar Ibn Zaydun of Beirut published it as a first edition, with no editor's name and no date in the scan. Its footnotes record variant readings under "in one copy", and its closing note says the book was printed after collation with corrected originals. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 3} {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 74} The edition offers no study of the book's attribution or of its place among al-Ash'ari's works; later scholars discussed these questions, and their views have not yet been taken here from their own sources.
