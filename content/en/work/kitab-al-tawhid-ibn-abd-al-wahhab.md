## Summary
*Kitab al-Tawhid alladhi huwa Haqq Allah 'ala al-'Abid* ("The Book of Oneness, which is God's Right over His Servants") is by [[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab|Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Wahhab]] (1115–1206 AH), as the title page of the Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University edition gives it. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university} Its subject is [[concept/tawhid|tawhid]] in the sense of devoting worship to God alone, together with what contradicts it — [[concept/shirk|shirk]], greater and lesser — and what the author treats as a means leading to it. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 7-9} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 153-157}

## Method
- The book opens with no preface beyond the basmala and a line of praise. It then says "The Book of Oneness" and brings verses — [[passage/quran-51-56|al-Dhariyat 56]], [[passage/quran-16-36|al-Nahl 36]], and verses of al-Isra', al-Nisa' and al-An'am — with Ibn Mas'ud's saying that the verses of al-An'am are Muhammad's testament, and the report of Mu'adh on God's right over His servants and theirs over Him. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 7-9}
- Each chapter gives verses, reports and sayings of the early generations without chains, briefly attributed ("Ahmad narrated it with an acceptable chain", "the two Sahihs have it"). Each closes with "In it are points" (fihi masa'il) and a numbered list of what the author draws from it. The first chapter has sixteen points and more, among them that worship is tawhid, that a messenger was sent to every community, that the religion of the prophets is one, and that worship of God is attained only by disbelieving in [[concept/taghut|taghut]]. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 9-10} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 27}
- He sometimes quotes others. In the chapter on intercession he quotes "Abu al-'Abbas" [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]], and the publishers explain in a note that this is his kunya. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 51-52}

## Its chapters
The sixty-six chapters, as the edition's table lists them, run as follows: {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 153-157}
- **The merit and meaning of tawhid** (chs 1–5): its merit and the sins it expiates; whoever realises it enters Paradise without reckoning; fear of shirk; calling to the testimony that there is no god but God; and its explanation.
- **Acts that contradict it** (chs 6–14): wearing rings and threads to lift or ward off affliction; [[concept/tawidh-amulets-and-incantations|incantations and amulets]]; [[concept/tabarruk|seeking blessing]] from a tree or stone; sacrificing to other than God; [[concept/nadhr|vows]] to other than God; and seeking refuge with or calling for help on another.
- **Intercession and guidance** (chs 15–17): including the chapter on [[concept/shafaa|intercession]], and the chapter on the verse of al-Qasas revealed about Abu Talib.
- **Excess regarding the righteous and their graves** (chs 18–22): that excess regarding the righteous was the cause of the children of Adam's disbelief; the severity of worshipping God at a righteous man's grave; that excess regarding their graves turns them into idols; the Prophet's guarding of tawhid; and that some of this community will worship idols. (Other sides of the subject are treated under [[concept/ziyarat-al-qubur|visiting graves]] and [[concept/tawassul|tawassul]].)
- **Sorcery and what touches it** (chs 23–29): sorcery and its kinds, [[concept/kahana|soothsayers]], nushra, [[concept/tatayyur-omens|omens]], astrology, and seeking rain from the lunar stations.
- **Acts of the heart** (chs 30–38): love, fear and reliance; feeling safe from God's plan; patience with decree; [[concept/riya|ostentation]]; seeking this world by one's deeds; obeying scholars and rulers in forbidding the lawful or permitting the forbidden; and seeking judgement from other than the law.
- **Names, attributes and forms of speech** (chs 39–58): denying any of God's names and attributes; phrases such as "what God wills and you will"; reviling time; the title "judge of judges"; mocking anything containing God's mention; saying "peace be upon God"; reviling the wind; and saying "if only".
- **Decree, image-making and oaths** (chs 59–66): deniers of decree; [[concept/tasweer-images-and-picturing|image-makers]]; frequent swearing; God's and His Prophet's protection; swearing by God that He will do something; the Prophet's guarding of tawhid. The last chapter, on [[passage/quran-39-67|al-Zumar 67]], ends with points on the greatness of the Throne and the Kursi and that God is above the Throne. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 150-151}

## Examples of its points
- **Amulets**: he narrates reports forbidding hanging them, then notes that some of the early generations permitted an amulet of Qur'an and others, among them Ibn Mas'ud, did not, and that the Prophet permitted incantations free of shirk against the evil eye and venom; among his points he records this as a matter on which scholars differ. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 29-31}
- **Dhat Anwat**: in the chapter on seeking blessing he narrates Abu Waqid al-Laythi's report of new Muslims asking for a tree to hang their weapons on, like the polytheists' tree. He draws from it that shirk is greater and lesser, since they did not leave Islam by this, and that it shows the principle of blocking the means. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 32-34}
- **Intercession**: he quotes Ibn Taymiyya that the intercession the Qur'an denies is the one containing shirk, that God affirmed intercession by His leave in several places, and that it belongs to the people of sincerity; among his points he names the greatest intercession, [[question/al-maqam-al-mahmud|the Praiseworthy Station]]. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 51-53}

## This edition
The book appeared in the series "The Works of Shaykh Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Wahhab" from the College of Shari'a in Riyadh, Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University. A group of professors revised it and collated it with its originals, and it was printed at the expense of Prince Sultan ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz to mark the centenary of the founding of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university} In his foreword the university's director, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Abd al-Muhsin al-Turki, says the university held a symposium on the Shaykh's call. In his words, its aim was "to clarify the reality of this call" and "to dispel the doubts raised about it". One of its recommendations was to re-edit and reprint his works. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 5-6} The edition's notes record differences between manuscript and printed copies. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 7-8}
