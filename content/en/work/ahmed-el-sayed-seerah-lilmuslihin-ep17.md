## Summary

The lecturer covers events of the first two years in Medina: the death of As'ad ibn Zurara, the Prophet's marriage to Aisha and anecdotes of her place in his life, and the first expeditions sent against Quraysh, including the Nakhla patrol of Abdullah ibn Jahsh and the revelation that followed it. He then lists the first raids led by the Prophet himself, outlines the departure for Badr and the Prophet's consultation of the Helpers, and ends with the story of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Umayya ibn Khalaf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### The death of As'ad ibn Zurara and the Jewish reaction

- He says that the lecture has moved from Mecca to Medina and that the first two years there saw events worth noting beyond the mosque, the brotherhood and the change of prayer direction. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that As'ad ibn Zurara, a leading Companion who had taken part in all three pledges with the Medinans, died of a throat illness in the first year after the Prophet's arrival, though he was not old. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that the Jews of Medina at once remarked on the death of the Prophet's companion, and he holds that they watched every event of the Prophet's life for something to comment on, which a reader of the Qur'an should keep in mind. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### The Prophet's marriage to Aisha

- He relates that soon after the arrival the Prophet sent for his family from Mecca, among them Umm Ayman, Usama ibn Zayd and Sawda, and that Aisha was also brought, the Prophet having contracted his marriage to her before the migration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that after the mosque and the brotherhood, his marriage to Aisha was among the first things he did in Medina, and that she held a great place in his life although she was young. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He holds that the marriage was never an objection for anyone through history, because Arabs commonly married at such ages, and that it became a point of attack only recently when that custom faded. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He argues that the Jews and hypocrites, who objected to the smallest act of the Prophet, never raised this marriage or his plural marriages, and that they spoke against Aisha only later in the affair of the slander. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He holds that some Muslims feel unnecessary distress over this matter and that it was never a charge that needed an answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Aisha's closeness to the Prophet

- He describes Aisha as sharp, intelligent and good company, and says the Prophet loved her openly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari that when a man asked the Prophet who was dearest to him, he named Aisha, and among men her father. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that the Companions knew of this special place, though the Prophet was just to all his wives, and so chose her day for their gifts, which annoyed the other wives, a story he leaves for later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that in his final illness the Prophet asked his wives' leave to be nursed in her house, and that she took pride in his dying with his head resting on her chest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates her report that a prophet is offered a choice at death, and that she heard the Prophet choose the highest companionship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates from Bukhari that her brother Abd al-Rahman came in with a tooth stick, the Prophet's eyes followed it, and she softened it for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that a search of Aisha's name in a hadith database brings thousands of results, since her reports cover transmitted sayings and household events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### The lost necklace and the verse of dry ablution

- He relates that on a campaign Aisha's necklace was lost, the Prophet held the army to search for it though there was no water, and it was found under her camel. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Abu Bakr reproached her for delaying the army and that the verse of dry ablution was then revealed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates from Bukhari that Usayd ibn Hudayr remarked that this was not the first blessing from the family of Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that Aisha has many more episodes and deserves a series of her own. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### The first expeditions of Hamza and Ubayda

- He holds that the Prophet turned at once to sending expeditions against Quraysh's caravans after leaving Mecca, as the stage of giving and defending the faith after the years of building. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that the biographers began with Emigrants, not Helpers, and that some explain this by the Helpers' pledge covering protection only inside Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that scholars differ over whether the first expedition was that of Ubayda ibn al-Harith or of Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the two being close in time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that Hamza lived only three or four years after the migration yet is named in many expeditions and was a fighter at Badr, and promises to describe his fighting at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Hamza's patrol went to the coast north of Yanbu near al-Is and met Abu Jahl with a party of polytheists, and that Majdi ibn Amr al-Juhani, an ally of both sides, stepped between them so there was no fighting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Ubayda's patrol went to Rabigh and faced the polytheists, and that Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas shot the first arrow in the cause of God there, a distinction in which Sa'd took pride. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and the complaint in Kufa

- He says that Sa'd faced a trial later in life, and that he accepted Islam in its first days at about sixteen, took part in every battle and was one of the ten promised paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari, through Jabir ibn Samura, that people of Kufa complained to Umar about Sa'd, claiming among other things that he prayed badly, and that Umar, for political reasons, removed him and appointed Ammar ibn Yasir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that a man from Kufa, asked by Umar's envoy, accused Sa'd of not marching with the patrols, not dividing fairly and not judging justly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Sa'd, known for answered prayer, prayed against that man, who was later seen very old, poor and wandering after girls, saying that Sa'd's prayer had struck him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He holds that the story shows how grave it is to be bold against the Prophet's Companions, and that cursing Abu Bakr and Umar and accusing Aisha, as he says some Shia do, is no mere historical quarrel. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that Noah, in the Qur'an, speaks of his followers with respect, and that the Prophet was told not to drive away those who call on their Lord. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The patrol of Abdullah ibn Jahsh to Nakhla

- He relates that the Prophet sent a patrol to Nakhla, on the Taif road between Mecca and Taif and distinct from the earlier visit of the jinn, under Abdullah ibn Jahsh, an early convert who later died at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Abdullah was given a sealed letter to open after two days on the road, which told him to go to Nakhla and intercept a caravan of the polytheists, and that the party numbered about ten or a few more. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that Abdullah told the men they were free to go back, that all continued except two, among them Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, who lost a camel and turned back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that they found a small caravan with four or five men on the last day of Rajab, debated whether to attack in the sacred month, and decided to do so, killing one man, capturing two and letting one escape. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that this was the first polytheist killed and the first captives taken, and that the party returned to Medina with the prisoners. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### Quraysh's outcry and the lesson of proportion

- He relates that Quraysh spread the news that Muhammad had broken the sacred month, and that when the patrol came back the Prophet was troubled by what they had done, paid the blood-money for the dead man and released the two captives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that a verse of Surat al-Baqara was then revealed on fighting in the sacred month, and that it calls the fighting grave while naming greater wrongs by Quraysh, such as barring people from the way of God and expelling people from the Sacred Mosque. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He holds that the verse teaches a balance in answering attacks on the mistakes of Muslims and reformers: the mistake is admitted, but the one who points to it must not hide his own larger wrongs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that a clear mistake by a good person should be acknowledged, while an accuser with a long record of harm should not pose as righteous, and that the verse puts the persecution above the killing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He compares this to Pharaoh's reproach of Musa for the man he killed, set against Pharaoh's own killing of children and enslavement of the Israelites, and says that Musa admitted the act and spoke of his flight and his mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He holds that the episode and its divine correction teach reformers how to treat their own errors and to look at who raises the objection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The route to al-Abwa and the first raid led by the Prophet

- He says that the biographers hold that the first expedition the Prophet himself led went to al-Abwa, where his mother Amina died, some fifty kilometres or more from Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He describes the old pilgrim road he says the Prophet used, not the modern fast road: from Medina through al-Rawha, to a junction where the left fork goes towards Mecca and the right towards Badr, then Wadi al-Qaha, al-Abwa and Rabigh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He notes that al-Rawha is often named in the biography and relates a sound report that the son of Maryam will pronounce the pilgrim call in the pass of al-Rawha at the end of time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that this expedition took place in Safar of the second year and that nothing came of it because the caravan was not met. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### Buwat, Kurz's raid, First Badr and Dhu al-Ushayra

- He relates that in Rabi al-Awwal the Prophet led the second raid, to Buwat, and that soon after his return Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri raided the grazing animals of Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that in two months the Prophet personally led three raids, al-Abwa, Buwat and what is called the first Badr, none of which saw fighting, and that Medina was by then in a state of war. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that the raid of al-Ushayra, towards Yanbu al-Nakhl, aimed at a Quraysh caravan led by Abu Sufyan that slipped away, and that this same caravan was the one the Prophet sought months later in Ramadan at Badr on its return. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that the great Battle of Badr, named by God the day of the criterion, needs a session of its own. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### The departure for Badr and the review of the army

- He says that Surat al-Anfal treats Badr and opens with the leaving of the house, and that some believers disliked the march. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that this time the Helpers marched with the Prophet, three hundred and fourteen men in all. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that the Prophet reviewed the army near the mosque, at a place today called al-Anbariyya, and sent back those too young, and relates from al-Bara that he and Ibn Umar were judged too young. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Umayr ibn Abi Waqqas, brother of Sa'd, was sixteen and was allowed, and was killed at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### Quraysh march out and Abu Jahl's boast

- He says that the Prophet reached the station of Dhafiran, where he consulted his companions after Abu Sufyan's caravan escaped and Quraysh marched out in force. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan had sent to Mecca for help, and that the Meccans set out with singing girls, drums and camels, meaning the march as a military display as well as a fight. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that when Abu Sufyan sent word that the caravan was safe and they could turn back, Abu Jahl refused and said they would stay three days at Badr drinking and feasting before returning. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Abu Jahl did not return, and cites verses of Hassan ibn Thabit about the dead of Badr in the well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### The Prophet's anxiety and the consultation of the Helpers

- He holds that no episode of the biography shows the Prophet bearing greater concern than the eve of Badr, more even than at al-Hudaybiyya, and that his worry was for the faith and not only for the victory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari that Abu Bakr told him to end his pleading as God would fulfil his promise, and relates from Ali that only the Prophet stayed awake that night, praying and weeping under a tree. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He says that Dhafiran lies about thirty kilometres from Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates from Bukhari through Ibn Mas'ud that al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad answered the consultation by saying the Muslims would not tell the Prophet to go and fight alone, as the people of Musa had, but would fight on every side, and that the Prophet's face brightened. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates from Anas, in a sound report, that Abu Bakr and Umar spoke well in turn, and that the Prophet repeated the question because he wanted the Helpers' answer, since their pledge might have covered only Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that a Helper then replied that they would follow him even to Bark al-Ghimad, and he cites Ibn Kathir for the soundness of the chain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}

### Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Umayya ibn Khalaf

- He relates from Bukhari, through Ibn Mas'ud, that Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, chief of the Aws, went to Mecca for the lesser pilgrimage some time before Badr and stayed with Umayya ibn Khalaf, who in turn lodged with him when passing through Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Umayya waited until midday to take Sa'd to circle the House, fearing Abu Jahl, but Abu Jahl appeared and angrily challenged Sa'd for circling in safety after sheltering the Prophet and his companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Sa'd raised his voice and threatened to cut off Quraysh's trade route to Syria, while Umayya begged him to be quiet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Sa'd then told Umayya that he had heard the Prophet say that he would kill him, and that Umayya answered that Muhammad never lies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
- He relates that Umayya told his wife, who reminded him on the day of the march, and that he wanted to stay behind but Abu Jahl urged him to go a day or two, and he was killed at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep17}
