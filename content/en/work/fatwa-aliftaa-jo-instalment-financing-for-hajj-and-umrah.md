## Summary

A question about a hajj and umrah company that agreed with a bank on instalment sales to its clients, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that financing such services is structured as a lease of a described service owed in liability together with a parallel lease, and set out its conditions, including a ban on late-payment penalties. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4023}

## The question

A company that organises hajj and umrah trips asked about its agreement with a bank to carry out a murabaha sale from the bank to the client, because of the high total cost of hajj and umrah and some pilgrims' wish for a flexible monthly instalment payment. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4023}

## The answer

The Department answered that contracts financing services rest on the financing institution agreeing with the travel companies on the terms and specifications the client requires, buying that service from them, and reselling it to the client in instalments, and that this is structured as a lease of a described service owed in liability (ijara mawsufa fi al-dhimma) together with a parallel lease. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4023} Among its conditions it listed that the service be known by its specifications, that it have recognised value in law and custom, and that the financier remain bound to deliver the service to the client to the end, so that if the company refuses to provide the service the client has recourse against the financier, unless the cause of refusal is stated in the contract. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4023} It added that the financier may not impose late-payment penalties on the client, because such penalties are forbidden usury. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4023} It concluded that the contracts must be drawn up as a lease of a described service owed in liability and a parallel lease, governed by these conditions. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4023}
