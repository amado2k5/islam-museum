## Summary

*Principles of Islamic Jurisprudence* is a university textbook on usul al-fiqh in English by Mohammad Hashim Kamali. It first appeared from Pelanduk in Malaysia in 1989 in 524 pages, then from the Islamic Texts Society in Cambridge in a revised edition in 1991 and a third edition in 2003 of 546 pages. {cite:openlibrary-principles-islamic-jurisprudence-1989} {cite:openlibrary-principles-islamic-jurisprudence-1991} {cite:archive-org-principles-of-islamic-jurisprudence-kamali-2003}

## Publication and editions

In his preface the author says English works on Islamic jurisprudence tend to discuss the doctrines of usul in isolation from their sources, so he tried to convey the content of usul al-fiqh as he found it in the Arabic sources, with their tone and spirit. The book runs through nineteen chapters, from the Qur'an, the Sunna and the rules of interpretation to abrogation, consensus, analogy, istihsan, public interest, custom, istishab, blocking the means and the conflict of evidences, ending with ijtihad. {cite:archive-org-principles-of-islamic-jurisprudence-kamali-text}
