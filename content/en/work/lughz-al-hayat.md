## Summary

*Lughz al-Hayat* ("The Riddle of Life") is a book on the wonders of living things by the Egyptian writer [[person/mostafa-mahmoud|Mostafa Mahmoud]]; the copy used here is Dar al-Awda's Beirut printing with rights dated 1986 {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 4}. It presents the popular biology of its day and reads it through faith; its judgements are the author's views, attributed to him.

## The chapters

By the table of contents: Introduction; The Forbidden Tree; A Dracula Called the Virus; The Plant Discovered Its Atom Bomb; Her Majesty; Before the Ants' Nest; The Language Bees Speak; We and the Apes; The Embryo Gives the Story Away; A Gap in Darwin's Theory; And After Evolution?; A Great Exchange Called the Brain; The Self and Freud's Talk; The Question Mark; Was It Chance?; The Key to the Riddle {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 145}.

## The miracle is order

The author opens by saying that "reality is more astonishing than magic", and that the true miracle "is not in breaking order but in establishing order", like the sun rising every day with precision for millions of years {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 5}.

## Darwin

In "We and the Apes" he tells of the 1925 trial of a biology teacher in Dayton, in America, the lawyer Clarence Darrow's exchange with the politician William Bryan, and then Darwin's voyage on the *Beagle* {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 65} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 66}. In "A Gap in Darwin's Theory" he holds that Darwin started from sound premises, the anatomical likeness of living things, but erred in making the causes of evolution "internal", with no "guiding hand" {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 88} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 89}. He argues that the beauty of peacock feathers and butterfly wings "is required by no necessity of life" {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 90}, and says the theory "is not scientific certainty, at most a mere preponderance", "a beautiful theoretical garment, no doubt, but full of holes" {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 89} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 91}. See [[question/is-human-evolution-compatible-with-islam|the question of evolution and Islam]].

## Freud and chance

In "The Self and Freud's Talk" he criticises Freud's sexual reading of infant behaviour and of the Oedipus complex as "an unscientific overreach" {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 113} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 114}. In "Was It Chance?" he asks about bird migration, the chick breaking its shell at the weakest point, and the floats on mosquito eggs: does all this happen by chance or "by the inspiration of an Inspirer"? {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 125} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 126} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 127}

## The key to the riddle

He ends by saying that modern science, in seeing the unity of the cosmos, enters "the forbidden zone the Sufis long kept for themselves", and that at the edge of the fog "religion's role begins... when science has said all it has and falls silent, the prophet's role comes" {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 137} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 138}.

:::perspectives
- **Mostafa Mahmoud's view of Darwin's theory.** He accepts anatomical likeness and the sequence of living things as a preponderance, rejects explaining evolution by internal material drives alone, and holds that "the ape ancestor" is still missing {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 88} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 89} {cite:mm-lughz-al-hayat-awda-1986 p. 91}.
