## Summary

A question on moving surplus duplicate copies from a mosque library to other libraries, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the ruling depends on how each book reached the library, and that the decision rests with the Ministry of Awqaf. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3860}

## The question

The questioner said a mosque library has filled with surplus duplicate copies that take up space, which arrived either by purchase from donations, as gifts from authors, or as endowments, and asked whether they may be moved to other libraries. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3860}

## The answer

The Department answered that what is endowed to a specific mosque should in principle stay there and may not be moved or sold without a lawful reason; if it wears out, or a replacement has been brought and it cannot be used in the mosque, it may be moved to another mosque to fulfil the endowment's purpose, quoting [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]]'s Rawdat al-Talibin and I'anat al-Talibin. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3860} What was bought from donations and not endowed by the administrator, or given by authors without stating it is an endowment, may be sold or disposed of when needed, with the proceeds spent on the library, since it is the mosque's property, as it quoted from al-Nawawi. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3860} Something a person placed in the mosque for worshippers' use without intending an endowment or gift is a loan that remains its owner's property. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3860} It concluded that the decision in these matters rests with the Ministry of Awqaf, Islamic Affairs and Holy Places as the endowments' administrator under Awqaf Law no. 32 of 2001. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3860}
