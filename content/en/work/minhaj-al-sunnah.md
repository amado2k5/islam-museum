## Summary
*Minhaj al-Sunna al-Nabawiyya fi Naqd Kalam al-Shi'a wa'l-Qadariyya* ("The Way of the Prophetic Sunna, in refutation of the discourse of the Shia and the Qadariyya") is a large book by Taqi al-Din Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Abd al-Halim [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] of Harran. It answers *[[work/minhaj-al-karamah|Minhaj al-Karama fi Ma'rifat al-Imama]]*, which [[person/al-allama-al-hilli|Ibn al-Mutahhar al-Hilli]] wrote to set out the Imami doctrine. {cite:minhaj-sunna-bulaq-v2 p. 6} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 51} The book follows al-Hilli chapter by chapter, quoting him with the formula "the Rafidi said" and then answering "in several respects". {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 61} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2376} It is a polemical work in which the author states his view of the Imamiyya in harsh terms; the museum reports those views as his, not in its own voice.

## Why it was written
In his preface Ibn Taymiyya says that a group of Ahl al-Sunna wa'l-Jama'a brought him a book composed by one of the Imami shaykhs of his time, which invited rulers and others to that school. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 17} The person who brought it told him it was among the strongest means of establishing the school with kings inclined to it, and that its author had written it for the well-known king he names in it, "Khudabanda"; they asked him to show what was in it. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 19} The editor's note gives the king's name as it appears in a manuscript of Minhaj al-Karama, "Aljaytu Khudabanda Muhammad", that is the Ilkhan [[person/oljaitu|Öljeitü]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 62}

He adds that they pressed him for a reply, saying that ignoring the book would let the believers down, so he wrote what he could in fulfilment of the covenant God took from people of knowledge, and he gives a section to the prohibition of concealing knowledge. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 25} He holds that proofs are either transmitted or rational, and that the book he answers, though among the best its side has produced in argument, is weak on both counts. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 20}

## The title
The full title on the Bulaq title page is *Minhaj al-Sunna al-Nabawiyya fi Naqd Kalam al-Shi'a wa'l-Qadariyya*. {cite:minhaj-sunna-bulaq-v2 p. 6} The volumes of Muhammad Rashad Salim's edition read "fi Naqd Kalam al-Shi'a al-Qadariyya". {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v8 p. 1} Ibn Taymiyya notes that al-Hilli called his book *Minhaj al-Karama fi Ma'rifat al-Imama* ("The Way of Honour in Knowing the Imamate") and, in a polemical turn, answers it with the name "Minhaj al-Nadama" ("The Way of Regret"). {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 51}

## Structure
The book opens with the author's preface: the opening praise, the reason for writing and the prohibition of concealing knowledge. Then comes a general account of the Imamiyya, in which he compares them to Jews and Christians in a list of traits and states that their later scholars relied on the books of the [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazila]] in rational matters. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 59} He then quotes al-Hilli's introduction and reports that al-Hilli divided his treatise into chapters: the first on the positions about the imamate, the second on why the Imami school must be followed, the third on the proofs of Ali's imamate after the Prophet, the fourth on the Twelve Imams and the fifth on refuting the caliphates of Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 62} The table of contents adds a sixth chapter answering their arguments about Abu Bakr's imamate. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 14}

- **Chapter 1 (the positions on the imamate):** it begins on p. 90 of the Shamela copy; Ibn Taymiyya answers what al-Hilli reported of the Sunni and Imami positions. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 90} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 93} Inside it is a long digression against the philosophers' doctrine that the world is eternal, on infinite regress and circularity, on the views of the Kullabiyya, the Ash'ariyya, the Karramiyya, [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]] and Ibn Malka, against Democritus and Abu Bakr al-Razi, and against the indivisible atom of the Mu'tazila and the Ash'aris. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 3} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 105} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 140}
- **Chapter 2 (that the Imami school must be followed):** it begins on p. 345 and includes replies to the virtues of Ali that al-Hilli cited, among them reports Ibn Taymiyya judges forged and others he accepts as sound, such as "You are of me and I am of you", which he says does not prove the imamate. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 345} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 7}
- **Chapter 3 (proofs of Ali's imamate):** al-Hilli divided it into four methods: rational proofs, then the Qur'an, then the Sunna, then proofs drawn from Ali's own states; the first method begins on p. 1883. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 10–12} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1883} Al-Hilli's first rational proof is that the imam must be infallible; it opens volume 4 of Rashad Salim's edition. {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v4 p. 8}
- **Chapter 4 (the imamate of the Twelve):** it begins on p. 2367 with the way of explicit designation, then the virtues al-Hilli lists for each imam. Ibn Taymiyya answers that a virtue makes its holder fit for the imamate but does not make him imam, just as a man does not become a judge merely by being fit for it. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2367} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2376}
- **Chapters 5 and 6:** that those who came before Ali were not imams, then the refutation of the arguments for Abu Bakr's imamate. Here he dwells on the reports of Abu Bakr leading the prayer during the Prophet's last illness and earlier, while he was well. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2404} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2529} {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v8 p. 575}

## Method
Ibn Taymiyya announces two lines of reply in his preface. The first is criticism of transmission: he asks for chains of narration and judges reports sound or forged according to what he takes to be the verdict of the hadith scholars. The second is criticism of reasoning: he examines the theological premises al-Hilli builds on, such as divine favour (*lutf*) and the necessity of infallibility. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 20} {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v4 p. 11} He often turns an argument back on his opponent: when al-Hilli cites a merit of Ali, he answers with a merit of Abu Bakr of the same kind. For example, if the faith of one's relatives is a merit, he says, Abu Bakr leads in it, since his father, mother, children and grandchildren all believed, which is true of no other Companion. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2367} He draws often on the heresiographies, such as al-Ash'ari's *Maqalat*, al-Shahrastani's *al-Milal wa'l-Nihal* and al-Baghdadi's *al-Farq bayn al-Firaq*, and quotes [[person/qadi-abd-al-jabbar|al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar]], [[person/al-jahiz|al-Jahiz]] and others; the editor's footnotes refer to these sources. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 24} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 61}

## Some of its questions
- **The rank of the imamate among the questions of religion:** he rejects al-Hilli's claim that the imamate is the most important matter of religion. In his view faith in God and His Messenger matters more at every time, and the Prophet accepted people's Islam on repentance, prayer and zakat without mentioning the imamate. See [[question/is-the-imamate-the-most-important-matter-of-religion|the rank of the imamate]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 62} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 63}
- **Infallibility and divine favour:** he holds that the infallible one who must be obeyed at all times is the Messenger, and that the Imamiyya took from the Mu'tazila the doctrine that God must grant favour (*lutf*). See [[question/must-the-imam-be-infallible|the infallibility of the imam]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v4 p. 9} {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v4 p. 11}
- **Abu Bakr and the caliphate:** he holds that the Prophet guided the community to Abu Bakr's caliphate and pointed them to it, without a written appointment. One of the reports he cites is that of the woman the Prophet told: if you do not find me, go to Abu Bakr. See [[question/did-the-prophet-indicate-abu-bakr-as-successor|the indication of Abu Bakr]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v8 p. 575}
- **Ali and kalam:** he rejects al-Hilli's statement that the science of kalam goes back to Ali, and dates its appearance to after the first century. See [[question/did-ali-found-the-science-of-kalam|kalam and Ali]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v8 p. 5}
- **Mu'awiya and the writing of revelation:** he answers al-Hilli's statement that Mu'awiya never wrote a word of the revelation. See [[question/was-muawiya-a-scribe-of-the-revelation|Mu'awiya as scribe]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-bulaq-v2 p. 219}
- **Rank by lineage:** he states that ranking one group above another, such as Quraysh, the Banu Hashim or the Arabs, does not rank every member above every member, and that God honours His servants by piety. He attributes the view that no such group is above another to a party of theologians, among them al-Qadi Abu Bakr ibn al-Tayyib al-Baqillani. {cite:minhaj-sunna-bulaq-v2 p. 265} {cite:minhaj-sunna-bulaq-v2 p. 267}

## Polemical positions attributed to the author
The book contains severe judgements of the Imamiyya. He calls them the most untruthful of the sects in what they transmit and the most ignorant in reasoning, and compares them to the Jews in a list of traits. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 20} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 31} He also accuses a party of them of working to bring the Tatars to Baghdad in the year it fell. {cite:minhaj-sunna-bulaq-v2 p. 263} These are the author's judgements in a work of refutation. The museum presents them as his position and does not adopt them, and it does not describe Imami beliefs from their opponents' words alone.

## Place in its field
It is the most extensive Sunni reply to Minhaj al-Karama. It combines argument on the imamate and on the history of the Companions with rational discussions of predestination, the origination of the world and infinite regress that go beyond the subject of the imamate. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 2} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 3}
