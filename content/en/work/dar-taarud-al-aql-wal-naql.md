## Summary
*Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql* ("Averting the Conflict between Reason and Revelation") is a large book in which [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] answers the "universal law" that gives rational proof precedence over scriptural proof when they conflict. Its editor Muhammad Rashad Salim judges it, in his estimation, "the greatest and most important thing Ibn Taymiyya wrote". {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 3} Ibn Taymiyya states his aim: since explaining what the Messenger meant on these matters cannot be completed without removing the supposed rational objection, he set out in this book the corruption of the law by which people were turned from understanding and believing the Messenger, so that the book is "on showing that there is no rational objection, and refuting those who claimed that rational proofs take precedence absolutely". {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 19}

## The title
The editor notes that Ibn Taymiyya's biographers counted this book his most important and gave it several titles: Ibn Abd al-Hadi in *al-'Uqud al-Durriyya* quotes al-Dhahabi's "a book on the agreement of the rational and the transmitted, in two volumes", adding that it is in four large volumes and that Ibn Taymiyya wrote a volume answering the objections of [[person/kamal-al-din-ibn-al-sharishi|Kamal al-Din Ibn al-Sharishi]] to it. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 4} Al-Bazzar called it *al-Jam' bayn al-'Aql wa'l-Naql* in seven volumes, and Ibn al-Qayyim in *Tariq al-Hijratayn* called it *Bayan Muwafaqat al-'Aql al-Sarih li'l-Naql al-Sahih*. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 5} Ibn Taymiyya himself called it *Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql* in most of his books, and elsewhere *Bayan Dar' Ta'arud al-Shar' wa'l-'Aql*, *Man' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql* and *Radd Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Shar'*. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 6} The manuscripts carry titles such as *Bayan Muwafaqat Sarih al-Ma'qul li-Sahih al-Manqul*, *Fustat al-Insaf wa'l-'Adl fi Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql* and *al-Jam' bayn al-Ma'qul wa'l-Manqul*; the editor chose the title that occurs most often. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 6} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 7}

## Date of composition
The editor draws on Ibn Taymiyya's remark in the book that he had written an early work on the corruption of this view "some thirty years ago", and on the fact that Ibn al-Sharishi, who replied to it, died in Shawwal 718 AH, so that the book was written before then. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 7} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 8} He rules out Egypt, since Ibn Taymiyya writes in it "when I was in the Egyptian lands, someone asked me", and dates it to Damascus between 713 and 717 AH, after his return there at the end of 712. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 9}

## The universal law it answers
After the preface Ibn Taymiyya opens the book with the text of the law: if scriptural and rational proofs conflict, either both are accepted, which is impossible; or both are rejected; or scripture is put first, which is impossible because reason is the root of transmission, and impugning it impugns both; so reason must be put first, and the transmitted text is then either interpreted or its meaning left to God. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 10} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 96} He calls this a "universal law" of [[person/fakhr-al-din-al-razi|al-Razi]] and his followers, anticipated by a group including [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]], the judge Abu Bakr Ibn al-Arabi, [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|al-Juwayni]] and [[person/al-baqillani|al-Baqillani]]. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 10} The editor gives al-Razi's statements of it from *Asas al-Taqdis*, *al-Matalib al-'Aliya*, *Muhassal Afkar al-Mutaqaddimin wa'l-Muta'akhkhirin* and *Nihayat al-'Uqul*, al-Ghazali's in *Qanun al-Ta'wil* and al-Juwayni's in *al-Irshad*. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 11} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 13} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 14} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 15}

Ibn Taymiyya considers these theologians less extreme than the philosophers who hold that the prophets' texts address the common people in terms suited to their minds, such as the law [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]] gave in *al-Risala al-Adhawiyya*, and he groups with them the philosophising Sufis such as Ibn Arabi, Ibn Sab'in and al-Suhrawardi "al-Maqtul", the Isma'ili heretics and the authors of the *Rasa'il Ikhwan al-Safa'*. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 11} The editor quotes Ibn Sina's text that the revealed laws address the masses in terms they understand, and [[person/ibn-rushd|Ibn Rushd]]'s in *Manahij al-Adilla* and *Fasl al-Maqal* that where the apparent sense of revelation conflicts with demonstration it admits of interpretation. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 17} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 18} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 19}

## Structure: forty-four arguments
Ibn Taymiyya answers the law in forty-four "aspects" (wujuh), whose places the editor lists: the first begins on p. 86 of vol. 1; the first nineteen run through vol. 1; he then digresses into questions bound up with the law's corruption – the proof from the origination of motions and accidents, preponderance without a preponderant, infinite regress of events, the eternity of the world – and into God's speech, His rising over the Throne, His elevation and events subsisting in Him; the twentieth begins with vol. 3, and the last is in the Dublin manuscript. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 19} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 20} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 21} He notes that the question of God's elevation takes up a large part of the book, answering al-Razi on "direction" and "aboveness" and Ibn Sina in *al-Isharat wa'l-Tanbihat* on "estimative" judgements. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 21}

Among the first aspects: if the two conflicting proofs are both certain, they cannot conflict; if both are probable, the stronger is preferred; if one is certain, the certain one is preferred because it is certain, not because it is rational; the fourfold division is not exhaustive; and the claim that "reason is the root of transmission", if it means that reason is the root of revelation's truth in itself, is something no rational person says – it is only the root of our knowing its truth. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 178} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 179} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 180} See [[question/must-reason-be-preferred-when-it-conflicts-with-revelation|Must reason be preferred when it conflicts with revelation?]]

## Editions
The editor notes that the book had been known since it was printed in the margins of *Minhaj al-Sunna* at the Amiriyya Press in Bulaq in 1322 AH, and that what was printed there is no more than a third of its real size. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 3} Muhammad Rashad Salim says he found the opportunity to publish it when he was called to the Centre for Heritage Editing at Dar al-Kutub; the copy present is the second printing of vol. 1, by Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University in 1411 AH / 1991. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 1} {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 3}

## Its place
The book joins theology and philosophy and criticises both from a Sunni Hanbali position that holds sound transmission and clear reason to agree; its judgements on the theologians and philosophers are given here as his. {cite:dar-taarud-salim-v1-1411 p. 19}
