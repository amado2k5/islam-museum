## Summary
Milo Cleveland Beach contrasts the [[work/tuzuk-i-jahangiri|Jahangir-nama]], a thoughtful, intensely personal memoir, with the Padshah-nama (or Shah Jahan-nama), an official state biography limited to the events and decisions of [[person/shah-jahan-mughal|Shah Jahan]]'s life as prince and emperor. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 76}

## Origins
There are several contemporary histories of the reign. The earliest was commissioned in 1634 (the eighth regnal year) from Muhammad Amin Qazwini and finished in 1646; titled Padshah-nama, it covered the first ten years. [[person/abd-al-hamid-lahori|Abd al-Hamid Lahori]], author of the most famous Padshah-nama, used Qazwini as the basis of his first part. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 76} Beach notes that these versions were written by imperial commission, not personally, so they are official texts; they do not discuss painting, and so lack the anecdotes that make Jahangir's memoirs so useful. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 15} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 26}

## Its illustrated copy
Its most famous illustrated copy is the [[object/windsor-padshahnama|Windsor manuscript]]. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 76} Beach quotes its description of Shah Jahan's throne for the eighth regnal year — a canopy enamelled outside with gems, set inside with rubies, on twelve emerald columns each topped by two peacocks — and sets it against the accounts of the travellers Bernier and Tavernier. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 78}
