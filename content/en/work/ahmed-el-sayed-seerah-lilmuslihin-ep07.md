## Summary

The seventh session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers treats the move from the secret to the public call. The speaker recounts the proclamation on al-Safa, Abu Lahab's reaction, the early torture of vulnerable converts, the story of Dhimad, the Qurayshi council on how to label the Prophet and their pressure on Abu Talib. He draws two lessons: the duty of conveying the religion and the centrality of patience. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Recap and the secret phase was not fully secret

- He recalls that the previous session dealt with the earliest converts and the exceptional training they received in Mecca, and that the lesson was that any training meant to benefit Islam today must follow the model of the Prophet's upbringing of his Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He says that this session moves to the public call, the passage from the secret call to the open one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that the secret phase, which most biographers place at three years, was not hidden from everyone: Quraysh knew of the Prophet's talk to his followers and that some had converted, but saw the matter as under control. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He suggests that Quraysh may have placed him among the hanifs of the pre-Islamic period, who worshipped God by what remained of Abraham's religion and of earlier prophecies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### The proclamation on al-Safa

- He relates that the command to warn the nearest kin, in Surat al-Shu'ara, was understood by the Prophet as the order to announce the call openly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that the Prophet climbed al-Safa and gave the cry used in Mecca in grave danger, which he compares to a modern air-raid siren, and that people gathered saying it was Muhammad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that the Prophet addressed the clans of Quraysh and asked whether they would believe him if he reported a cavalry force behind the mountain, that they answered that they had never found him lying, and that he then declared himself a warner before a severe punishment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that the Prophet then called out particular kin by name, including his aunt Safiyya, and told them he could not avail them against God, and that he said the same to his daughter Fatima. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that the opening of the public call was a warning rather than good news, and that the Prophet warned because he truly feared for his people, not merely because he was ordered to. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Abu Lahab's reaction

- He relates that Abu Lahab, the Prophet's uncle, spoke harshly to him in the same gathering, before the people dispersed, asking whether this was what he had gathered them for. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that a chapter of the Qur'an was revealed in response to Abu Lahab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He stresses that this was a heavy moment for the Prophet, since the nearest of his kin opposed him at the very start, and that he sees the event as a great shock to Quraysh, the leading city of Arabia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Ibn Mas'ud's report on the first to declare Islam

- He relates a report of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, which he attributes to Ibn Majah through Asim ibn Abi al-Najud and Zirr ibn Hubaysh, and judges its chain to be good. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He says that the report names the first to show their Islam as the Prophet, Abu Bakr, Ammar and his mother, Khabbab, Suhayb, Bilal and al-Miqdad, and that he himself counted eight where the report gives seven. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that the Prophet was protected by his uncle Abu Talib and Abu Bakr by his tribe, while the others were seized, dressed in iron armour and left in the sun. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that all of them yielded under torture to what their tormentors wanted except Bilal, whom the tormentors handed to boys to drag through the ravines of Mecca while he kept repeating a single word of divine oneness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He sums up the start of the open call as a hot one: a warning, an immediate objection from the Prophet's uncle, mockery and torture. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Early revelation answering mockery

- He holds that early Meccan revelation is a key to understanding the biography, because the Qur'an was dealing with the events as they happened. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He says that many early passages, among them verses of Surat al-Hijr, speak of mockery and verbal harm to the Prophet and serve to steady him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He explains that the Qur'an repeats the note that earlier messengers were mocked, and that the revelation came in parts to strengthen the Prophet and those with him, since mockery was among the strongest weapons of Quraysh against the call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### The Prophet's approach to gatherings

- He says that after the proclamation the Prophet went to gatherings of Meccans and visitors and announced monotheism to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates, from the Musnad of Ahmad, that he would address a tribe and promise success to those who accepted the declaration of oneness, while Abu Lahab walked behind him telling people not to believe him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that the main burden in this phase fell on the Prophet, while the Companions were beginners whose task was to believe, stay firm and bear harm without being turned from their faith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Quraysh council and al-Walid ibn al-Mughira

- He says that as the pilgrimage season approached Quraysh feared visitors would question them, and their nobles agreed they must settle on one answer instead of calling him a poet, a soothsayer or a sorcerer each in turn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that al-Walid ibn al-Mughira led this request, rejected the suggestion of soothsayer, and asked for time to think. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that al-Walid privately recognised the Prophet's truthfulness while struggling to suppress it, and that Surat al-Muddaththir records his state of mind in detail. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that they settled on calling him a sorcerer and told pilgrims so, though he judges the agreement was not complete, since some also said he was possessed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### The story of Dhimad al-Azdi

- He relates, from Sahih Muslim on the authority of Ibn Abbas, that Dhimad of the Azd Shanu'a came to Mecca, practised ruqya on the possessed, and heard fools of Mecca say that Muhammad was mad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that Dhimad offered to treat him, saying God cures through his hands whom He wills, and that the Prophet answered with an opening address of praise and testimony. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that Dhimad asked him to repeat it, that the Prophet repeated it three times, and that Dhimad said he had heard soothsayers, sorcerers and poets but nothing like it, then pledged himself to Islam on behalf of himself and his people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that later, in Medina, a detachment the Prophet sent passed Dhimad's people and its commander ordered an item taken from them to be returned, because they were Dhimad's people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that the story shows the first kind of harm the Prophet faced: being called mad, a liar or a sorcerer, and he asks his listeners to imagine how hard that was for someone who was certain of the revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Consolation of the Prophet in the Qur'an

- He says that the Qur'an consoled the Prophet over the denial and mockery, noting that earlier messengers suffered the same and were patient until help came. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He explains, through verses of Surat al-An'am, that the deniers did not truly disbelieve him in their hearts but rejected the signs unjustly, and that the Prophet's task was a charge he could not set aside. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He concludes that the first reception the Prophet met was verbal denial, mockery and public repetition of these charges, followed by meetings of the Qurayshi nobles. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Torture of the vulnerable and the death of Sumayya

- He says that Quraysh resolved to torture the Muslims who had no protection among their people, naming Ammar, Bilal, Sumayya, al-Miqdad and Suhayb. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that Abu Jahl was among the fiercest in hostility and that Sumayya, mother of Ammar ibn Yasir, was his freedwoman and refused to abandon Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that Abu Jahl killed her with a spear, and he counts her as the first martyr in Islam, a woman. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that this shows women a place of responsibility for the religion, with patience and sacrifice, not a position behind the lines. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Quraysh pressure on Abu Talib

- He says that Quraysh saw the main obstacle in Abu Talib, who protected the Prophet more strongly because he stayed on their religion and was a chief of Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that a Qurayshi delegation asked Abu Talib to silence his nephew, who criticised their gods, and that Abu Talib passed their message to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates the well-known reply that the Prophet could no more abandon his mission than a person could take a flame from the sun, and he notes that this account lacks a clear or established chain though it is a famous story. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He says he does not remember its wording exactly and takes its meaning to be that the Prophet was under a binding charge, not following a passing idea. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### First lesson: the duty of conveying the message

- He holds that the greatest burden on the Prophet was the duty to convey the religion as revealed, heavier even than torture and mockery. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He points to a verse commanding the Messenger to convey what was sent down to him, and to the Prophet's sayings urging his community to convey from him, preserve what they heard, and learn rites from him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that, as reported in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet said at the Farewell Pilgrimage that the present should inform the absent, and asked God to witness that he had conveyed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He draws the lesson that a reformer who follows the Prophet must convey the truths of the religion as he conveyed them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that calling to good and forbidding wrong is a communal obligation, so that the whole community is at fault if no sufficient part of it performs it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Quraysh methods: doubt and false claims

- He says that Quraysh used scattering, doubt and objections against the call, such as the claim that a human being taught the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that they named a Roman blacksmith as that teacher, and he explains the Qur'anic reply that the language of the one they pointed to was foreign while the Qur'an is clear Arabic. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He says they also called the revelation tales of the ancients, confused dreams, an invention and poetry, and he finds these charges contradict one another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that the charge of poetry was weak because the Prophet had lived forty years among them and they knew him, as the Prophet himself relied on when he reminded them they had never found him lying. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He says they also demanded signs and objected that the Messenger ate food and walked in markets, and he sees parallels in the demands made of Moses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Obstructing the Qur'an and rival stories

- He says that Quraysh tried to keep people from hearing the Qur'an and urged others to make noise over its recitation so as to prevail. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He relates that al-Nadr ibn al-Harith told people old tales and claimed to bring better ones than Muhammad's, and he says a verse about those who buy idle talk was revealed concerning him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}

### Looking ahead: patience and steadfastness

- He says that the next session will discuss the effect of these objections, mockery and harm on the Muslims and how the Prophet and the Companions met such trials. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He says that the central lesson he draws from this first stage of the prophetic mission is the centrality of patience and steadfastness in a believer's life, with the Prophet and his Companions as the model. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
- He holds that the religion cannot rise unless it has bearers who live for it, convey its truths and bear the harm that comes with that. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep7}
