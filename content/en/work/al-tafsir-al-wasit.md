## Summary
*al-Tafsir al-Wasit lil-Qur'an al-Karim* ("The Middle Commentary on the Noble Qur'an") is a complete commentary on the Qur'an from al-Fatiha to al-Nas by [[person/muhammad-sayyid-tantawi|Muhammad Sayyid Tantawi]], printed by Dar al-Ma'arif in Cairo in fifteen volumes whose covers give its author the title "Grand Mufti of the Arab Republic of Egypt". {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.1} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.6353} The volumes were revised by Dr Abd al-Rahman al-Adawi, professor at the Faculty of Usul al-Din. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2} In his closing words the author says he spent "about fifteen years" writing it, and that he finished it in Nasr City, Cairo, on the morning of Friday 26 Rabi' I 1407 AH, 28 November 1986. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.6903}

## Purpose and method
Tantawi opens the first volume with an introduction on the purposes for which, in his view, the Qur'an was revealed: to guide humans and jinn, to be a miracle attesting the Prophet's truthfulness, and to let people draw near to God by reciting, memorising, pondering and acting on it; here he quotes passages from the introduction of [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]] on the merits of the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.6} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.7} He then notes that scholars served the Qur'an from many sides — its inimitability, narratives, occasions of revelation, readings, script, clear and ambiguous verses, abrogating and abrogated, Meccan and Medinan, and rare words — and that many wrote commentaries because guidance from the Qur'an is only reached by explaining what its words mean. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.8} He cites [[person/iyas-ibn-muawiya|Iyas ibn Mu'awiya]]'s comparison: one who reads the Qur'an without knowing its interpretation is like people who receive a letter from their king at night and have no lamp. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.8}

He quotes [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]]'s ranking of "the best methods of interpretation": the Qur'an by the Qur'an, then by the Sunna, then by the sayings of the Companions, then by those of the Successors such as [[person/mujahid-ibn-jabr|Mujahid]], [[person/said-ibn-jubayr|Sa'id ibn Jubayr]], [[person/ikrima-mawla-ibn-abbas|Ikrima]], [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata']] and [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]]; see [[concept/tafsir-bil-mathur|tafsir by transmitted report]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.8} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.9} He describes the library of commentaries as holding large, middle and concise works; works dominated by transmitted report or by reasoned opinion; works with a juristic, rhetorical, philosophical, Sufi, scientific, social or sectarian colour; some purged of legends and false stories and some stuffed with them. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.9}

He presents his own work as a "middle commentary" into which he put his utmost effort so that it would be "scholarly and verified, purged of weak opinions and false doubts". {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.9} He describes the order in which he treats a verse: first a linguistic explanation of the words and what is meant by them, then the occasion of revelation if one exists and is acceptable, then the overall meaning with its rhetorical features, lessons and rulings, supported by other verses, hadith and the sayings of the early generations; he avoids lengthy grammatical analysis and gives only the preferred view where opinions differ. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.10}

## The sura introductions
Before explaining each sura verse by verse, Tantawi gives an introduction whose aim, he says, is "to give the reader a clear picture of the sura". {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2498} It usually states the sura's place in the mushaf, its number of verses with the differences between the Kufan, Basran, Hijazi and Syrian counts, its names, the time and place of its revelation and any verses held to be exceptions, and then a summary of its themes. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2328} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2498} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2499} In the later volumes these introductions are headed "Introduction and preface" (muqaddima wa-tamhid). {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4392} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5763} What he says about verse counts is recorded under [[concept/add-al-ay|the science of counting verses]], and his introductions to several suras on their own pages, such as [[passage/quran-8|al-Anfal]], [[passage/quran-9|al-Tawba]], [[passage/quran-24|al-Nur]] and [[passage/quran-48|al-Fath]].

Tantawi holds that an enlightened understanding of the verses is helped when the commentator knows their occasions of revelation and "the historical atmosphere in which they were revealed"; so before al-Anfal he summarises the story of the battle of Badr from the Sira of [[person/ibn-hisham|Ibn Hisham]], and before al-Fath the story of the truce of al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2336} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5763}

## His sources
Tantawi quotes most often from the commentaries of [[work/tafsir-ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]], [[person/al-tabari|Ibn Jarir al-Tabari]] and al-Qurtubi, and his footnotes name their editions: Ibn Kathir in the Isa al-Halabi printing, al-Tabari in the Dar al-Ma'arif printing, al-Qurtubi in the Dar al-Katib al-Arabi and Dar al-Kutub al-Misriyya printings. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.12} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2502} He also draws on [[work/tafsir-al-kabir-razi|Fakhr al-Din al-Razi's commentary]], al-Zamakhshari's [[work/al-kashshaf|al-Kashshaf]], al-Alusi's commentary, [[work/tafsir-al-manar|Tafsir al-Manar]] and al-Jamal's gloss on al-Jalalayn, on Sayyid Qutb's *Fi Zilal al-Qur'an*, and on the commentaries of Mahmud Shaltut and Muhammad al-Khidr Husayn. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.15} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.16} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.19} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2329} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2500} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.6901} For hadith he uses the two Sahihs and the Sunan, and for criticism of chains [[person/ahmad-muhammad-shakir|Ahmad Shakir]]'s edition of the Musnad of Ahmad. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.13} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2503}

## Some of his positions
- **Where al-Fatiha was revealed:** he states that the preferred view among careful scholars is that it was revealed in Mecca among the earliest revelations, and reports two other views: that it is Medinan, and that it was revealed twice. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.11}
- **The basmala:** he reports the consensus that it is part of a verse in Surat al-Naml and the disagreement over whether it is a verse of al-Fatiha and of every sura, and refers the detailed discussion to the books of fiqh and the commentaries on the legal verses; see [[concept/al-basmala|the basmala]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.17}
- **The order of the suras and their correspondences:** he holds that the order of the suras is fixed by revelation (tawqifi), but considers that much of what commentators, al-Alusi among them, say about correspondences between neighbouring suras "is not free of strain". {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2329} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2330}
- **The missing basmala at the head of al-Tawba:** he adopts al-Qurtubi's view that it was not written because Gabriel did not bring it down with that sura, and judges the other explanations weak; see [[question/why-no-basmala-in-surat-al-tawba|Why does Surat al-Tawba not open with the basmala?]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2502} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2503}

## When and where it was written
The dated prefaces and closing notes of the suras show the stages of writing: the preface to al-Tawba is dated 19 Shawwal 1395 AH (24 October 1975), and the commentary on Yunus 17 Muharram 1400 AH (7 December 1979). {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2496} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2770} He finished the commentary on Hud in [[place/medina|Medina]] on the morning of Thursday 5 Jumada II 1401 AH (9 April 1981). {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.3061} The later volumes carry dates running from 1984 to 1986, the last being the close of the book in Cairo in 1407 AH / 1986. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4141} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5129} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.6903}

## The edition used here
The museum used a combined 6,906-page copy of the Dar al-Ma'arif printing whose first-volume title page is dated 1412 AH / 1992; the page numbers cited here are pages of that file, not printed folios, because each volume restarts its own pagination. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.1} The harvest library holds two further copies that are byte-identical to it, and a separate copy of the commentary on Surat al-Mulk that was not used because its machine reading is garbled.
