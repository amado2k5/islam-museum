## Summary
The Musnad of Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi is attributed to Sulayman ibn Dawud ibn al-Jarud al-Tayalisi (d. 204 AH). The editor holds that the book is not his own composition. It is a set of sessions that Yunus ibn Habib heard from him, which Abu Mas'ud al-Razi arranged for Yunus, as the editor reports from Abu Nu'aym and al-Dhahabi. {cite:musnad-tayalisi-dar-hajar-v1 p.53}

## Attribution and material
The editor gives three grounds for attributing the book to al-Tayalisi. The chain of transmission at the start of the copies runs back to al-Tayalisi through sound hearing. Many recorded hearings are written at the end of each part. And the reports that the imams cite from al-Tayalisi through Yunus ibn Habib match the musnad exactly, as al-Bayhaqi and Abu Nu'aym show. {cite:musnad-tayalisi-dar-hajar-v1 p.53}

The edition gives about 2,890 reports. Al-Tayalisi is said to have narrated a hundred thousand hadiths in Khurasan, so the musnad is a small part of his transmission. The book has a few reports from Yunus ibn Habib through other shaykhs. {cite:musnad-tayalisi-dar-hajar-v1 p.54}

## The edition used
Dar Hajar for Printing, Publishing and Distribution, Giza, in partnership with the Centre for Arab and Islamic Research and Studies at Dar Hajar, edited by Dr. Muhammad ibn Abd al-Muhsin al-Turki. Volume 1 is dated 1419 AH and volume 2 is dated 1420 AH, both 1999 CE. {cite:musnad-tayalisi-dar-hajar-v1 p.3}

## Volume 2
The scanned portion of volume 2 includes the musnad of Abu Mas'ud al-Badri. Its first numbered report in the scan is 641, about a man who prays behind an imam who prolongs the prayer. {cite:musnad-tayalisi-dar-hajar-v2 p.5}

## For young visitors
The Musnad of al-Tayalisi is a hadith collection by a well-known traditionist. The edition shows how much smaller the surviving musnad is than all he transmitted.

## Research notes
- Because the book is a set of sessions heard by Yunus ibn Habib, the arrangement should not be credited to al-Tayalisi alone without that qualification.
- Hadith numbers are those of the edition scanned.
- The two byte-identical scans of volume 1 were read once.
