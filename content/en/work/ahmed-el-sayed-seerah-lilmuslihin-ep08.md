## Summary

The eighth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers continues the early Meccan years. The speaker treats Abu Bakr's buying and freeing of tortured slaves, Khabbab's complaint to the Prophet, the physical harm done to the Prophet at the Kaaba, the themes of early revelation, and the pressure that led toward the migration to Abyssinia, including Abu Bakr's protection under Ibn al-Dughna. He closes with the centrality of the Qur'an for the reformer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Recap and plan of the session

- He recalls that the previous session dealt with the start of the public call, the verbal harm the Prophet suffered, and how severe that harm was even if it seems light when heard in the biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that the session continues with events of the fourth and fifth years of the prophethood, and he divides the Meccan period into a secret phase of three years, a public phase, and a last phase with other events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He announces that he will treat the stands of some Companions, the physical harm to the Prophet, some early Qur'anic themes, and the peak of torture that pushed Muslims to emigrate to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Abu Bakr frees the tortured slaves

- He says that this is the second early deed he recounts of Abu Bakr, after the earlier one in which notable men accepted Islam at his hands. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that most of those tortured were freedmen and slaves, and that Abu Bakr bought them from their masters and freed them for God's sake. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that some of his own people reproached him for buying weak and persecuted people who could bring him nothing, and that he answered that he sought only what is with God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that books on the occasions of revelation report that the verses of Surat al-Layl about the one who gives and fears God were revealed about Abu Bakr, and that this is a well-known report; he notes that the chapter is among the early ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that sometimes Abu Bakr arrived too late and the victim died under torture, as happened to Sumayya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Khabbab's complaint to the Prophet

- He relates that Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, who suffered much for his faith, came to the Prophet as he rested against his cloak by the Kaaba and asked him to pray to God and seek help for them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He reads the request as a sign that the torture had reached a level beyond endurance, and he places the report, in his own judgement, in the third to fifth years before the migration to Abyssinia, since the report gives no date. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that the Prophet answered by describing men of earlier peoples who were sawn in two or combed with iron combs without turning from their faith, and told the Companions they were hasty, and promised that God would complete this matter until a rider could travel from Sana'a to Hadramawt fearing only God and the wolf for his sheep. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that al-Bukhari and Muslim both record this report and that it is sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He draws three lessons for reformers: stories of earlier prophets and righteous people steady the believer as the Qur'an itself steadied the Prophet; the Prophet opened a future horizon of hope for the Companions; and the promise of victory was given when there was no material support for it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Challenge verses: the Prophet will prevail

- He holds that the promise of victory came in the form of a challenge in early revelation, while the Prophet was materially weak and the call had only begun. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He points to verses of Surat al-Qalam, which many scholars place among the earliest, to Surat al-Tur about waiting for the Prophet's fate, and to a verse in Surat al-Hajj. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates a well-known reading of the Surat al-Hajj verse: whoever thinks God will not support the Prophet may hang himself and see whether that removes his rage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He regards this challenge, matched by the promise to Khabbab, as one of the signs of prophethood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### The sheep entrails thrown on the Prophet

- He says that after the verbal harm he turns to the physical harm the Prophet suffered, in that stage or later in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari on the authority of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, that the Prophet was praying by the Kaaba while Abu Jahl and his companions sat nearby, and that they sent for the afterbirth of a slaughtered camel to put on his back as he prostrated. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that the most wretched of them, named in one version as Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, did so, and that Ibn Mas'ud said he watched without being able to prevent it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that they laughed and leaned on one another, that the Prophet stayed in prostration until Fatima came, still a young girl, threw it off his back and rebuked them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that the Prophet then asked God three times to deal with Quraysh, which distressed them since they thought prayer in that city was answered, and that he named Abu Jahl, Utba and Shayba sons of Rabi'a, al-Walid ibn Utba, Umayya ibn Khalaf and Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, with a seventh the narrator did not remember. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Mas'ud swore he saw those named lying dead in the well of Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Abu Jahl's threat and the angels

- He relates, from Sahih Muslim on the authority of Abu Hurayra, that Abu Jahl asked whether Muhammad put his face in the dust before them, and swore he would tread on his neck if he saw it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that when Abu Jahl approached the praying Prophet he suddenly retreated shielding himself with his hands, saying there was a trench of fire, terror and wings between them, and that the Prophet said the angels would have seized him limb by limb had he come near. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that the narrator links to this episode the revelation of verses of Surat al-Alaq about the one who forbids a servant to pray, and he adds that the report may be the narrator's own addition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He stresses the value of returning to hadith to complete events of the biography, although biographies sometimes include this one too, and that he believes this event also happened early. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Uqba chokes the Prophet; verbal harm weighed against physical

- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that Urwa ibn al-Zubayr asked Abdullah ibn Amr about the worst thing the polytheists did to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that Abdullah described the Prophet praying in the Hijr of the Kaaba when Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt put his garment around his neck and choked him violently, until Abu Bakr seized Uqba's shoulder and pushed him away, asking whether they would kill a man for saying his lord is God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He notes that all three reports set the harm at the Kaaba during the Prophet's prayer, and that some of these events came under Abu Talib's protection and some perhaps after his death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He reasons that those with no protector fared far worse, as with those scorched in the sun. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He gives his own opinion, without certainty, that the verbal harm hurt the Prophet much more than such acts, since much of the Qur'an's steadying of him concerns mockery and ridicule. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Early revelation: the charge of madness

- He says that studying the Qur'anic themes of this stage is a way to understand the biography, and that it deserves its own series. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He compares Surat al-Qalam and Surat al-Takwir, and notes that both deny that the Prophet is mad and both end with the same statement that it is a reminder for the worlds. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He holds that the repetition shows how widespread the charge of madness was and how heavy it was, since it came from the leaders of Quraysh, whom Arabs regarded as their highest model. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Early revelation: glorifying God, and why they denied

- He says that Surat al-A'la is counted by many among the earliest revelations, though he stresses that the order of revelation is a matter of scholarly effort and nothing in it is certain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that the chapter draws attention to glorifying God and being occupied with remembering Him, as did the first revelation in the cave of Hira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He holds that one of the largest Meccan themes is the cause of the polytheists' denial, which he names as above all their denial of the Hour and the resurrection, together with arrogance and desire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that Surat al-Furqan voices their demand that angels be sent down or God be seen, that Surat al-Ma'un points to the one who denies the reckoning and neglects the orphan, and that Surat al-Layl joins both causes in one short phrase of denial and turning away. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Peak of torture and the road to Abyssinia

- He says that when torture became severe the Prophet allowed his Companions to emigrate to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He explains that Abyssinia was not a land of Islam but had a just king under whom no one was wronged, so the departure was a flight to protection and to freedom to worship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that he will treat the Abyssinian migration and its lessons in the next session, and that Uthman was among the first to leave. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Abu Bakr under the protection of Ibn al-Dughna

- He relates, from al-Bukhari on the authority of Aisha, that she remembered her parents always following the religion and the Prophet visiting their house morning and evening, and he reads this as showing Abu Bakr and his wife as among the earliest Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that when the Muslims were tried Abu Bakr set out to emigrate and at Barak al-Ghimad met Ibn al-Dughna, chief of the al-Qara tribe, who said that a man like him should not be driven out or leave, praising his generosity, kinship ties, hospitality and help in hardship, and offered his protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He notes that this praise recalls what Khadija said of the Prophet, and that the protection fits Arab custom. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that Quraysh honoured Ibn al-Dughna's protection but asked that Abu Bakr worship in his house only, fearing that their women and children would be drawn away. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that Abu Bakr then built a place of prayer in the courtyard of his house, and that the women and children of the polytheists crowded round him as he wept while reciting the Qur'an, which alarmed the nobles of Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that they asked Ibn al-Dughna to make Abu Bakr keep within his house or return the protection, and that Ibn al-Dughna, unwilling to be known as one whose pledge was broken, asked Abu Bakr, who returned it and was content with God's protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that the Prophet was then in Mecca and told them that he had been shown the place of their emigration, a palm-covered land between two lava tracts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He holds that the episode shows both the fear the polytheists had of the Qur'an and their keeping of covenants even toward an enemy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### The power of the Qur'an: Surat al-Najm and the prostration

- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that the Prophet once recited Surat al-Najm openly before the polytheists, and that they prostrated as it ended. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He says that this event came later than the third to fifth years, after the Night Journey and the Ascension, and that he cites it to show the force of the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He relates that biographers give the spread of a rumour that Quraysh had accepted Islam, after this prostration, as one reason some returned from Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He holds that the Qur'an was the main pillar by which the Prophet confronted the polytheists, strengthened the truth and resisted falsehood, and he mentions a verse commanding him to strive against the deniers with it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}

### Closing lesson: the centrality of the Qur'an for the reformer

- He says that the closing lesson of all these reports is the centrality of the Qur'an for the believer and the reformer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
- He holds that the reformer's first provision must be the Qur'an, for forming concepts, ideas and creed, steadying the heart, taking pride in the religion, and addressing unbelievers and hypocrites. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep8}
