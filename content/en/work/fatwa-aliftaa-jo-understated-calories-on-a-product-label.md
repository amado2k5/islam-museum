## Summary

A question about understating calories on a product label, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that claiming qualities a product does not have is forbidden as lying and cheating; whoever has done so must repent and correct the information, but need not set aside any profits since the price did not change, though giving charity to purify the earnings is fine. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4463}

## The question

A seller who had labelled a product as about 200 calories when it actually contained more, and had sold large quantities, though the price would have been the same either way, asked what he now owes. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4463}

## The answer

The Department answered that Islam forbids lying and cheating in transactions, citing [[passage/quran-23-8|Surat al-Mu'minun 23:8]] and [[passage/quran-9-119|Surat al-Tawba 9:119]] and a hadith narrated by [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] whose meaning is that whoever cheats the Muslims is not of them, and said that attributing to goods qualities they lack is lying and cheating. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4463} It quoted resolution no. 101 of Jordan's Iftaa Council that a Muslim, whether a trader, a sales representative or in any other position, must be truthful in all he says and always show the truth about every product he promotes, and that lying and misrepresentation are forbidden to him. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4463} It said whoever has fallen into this must hasten to sincere repentance: stopping at once by correcting the published product information and announcing the correction, resolving not to repeat it, feeling remorse, and seeking forgiveness often and doing good deeds. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4463} It noted that because the product's price did not rise owing to the false description, being the same with or without it, there is no need to set aside any of the profits, as there is no difference to return; there is no harm in giving charity to purify the earnings. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4463}
