## Summary

A question about offering a sacrifice on behalf of the deceased, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that a sacrifice on behalf of the deceased is permitted, the Hanbali position and the view of al-Abbadi among the Shafi'is, because the sacrifice is a financial charity whose reward reaches the dead. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4371}

## The question

The questioner asked whether a sacrifice may be offered on behalf of someone who has died. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4371}

## The answer

The Department answered that a sacrifice on behalf of the deceased is permitted, that this is the Hanbali position as in Kashshaf al-Qina by [[person/al-buhuti-mansur-ibn-yunus|al-Buhuti]] (6/428), that al-Abbadi among the Shafi'is held it as reported by Ibn Qadi Shuhba in Bidayat al-Muhtaj (4/358), and that it is also reported from some Malikis and Hanafis. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4371} It noted that [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] gave a chapter of his [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan]] to sacrifice on behalf of the dead, narrating from Hanash that he saw [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] sacrifice two rams and that Ali told him the Prophet had instructed him to sacrifice on his behalf, and narrating from [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abdullah]] that the Prophet sacrificed on behalf of his community, which includes those who have died. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4371} It said the texts show that the reward of fasting and hajj reaches the dead, so the sacrifice, a purely financial act of worship that counts as charity, reaches them all the more, and that scholars agree the reward of charity reaches the dead; for these reasons the Department holds it permitted. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4371}
