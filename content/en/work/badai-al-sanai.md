## Summary

Bada'i' al-Sana'i' fi Tartib al-Shara'i' is a book of [[group/hanafi-madhhab|Hanafi]] law by [[person/ala-al-din-al-kasani|Ala' al-Din Abu Bakr ibn Mas'ud al-Kasani]] (d. 587 AH). {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.1} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.76}

## The author's preface and the title

Al-Kasani opens his preface by saying that after knowledge of God and His attributes no knowledge is nobler than law, the knowledge of the lawful and unlawful and of rules, and that it cannot be known by reason alone without the help of revelation. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.87} He then says that their masters wrote much in this field, all usefully, but did not attend to systematic order, except his teacher [[person/ala-al-din-al-samarqandi|Ala' al-Din al-Samarqandi]], whom he followed, since the aim of writing is to make the goal easy to reach, which needs an order that the craft requires and wisdom demands: going through the divisions and sections of the questions and deriving them from their rules and principles. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.88} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.89} So he gathered the body of law "arranged in a systematic order and a reasoned composition", with clear evidence and strong points, and named it Bada'i' al-Sana'i' fi Tartib al-Shara'i', "marvels of craft in arranging the laws", because he saw it as a marvellous craft and a wonderful order, so that the name would match the thing named. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.89}

## Its method in the chapters

This order shows from the first page: "The discussion in this book rests in principle on two points: first, the definition of purity; second, its kinds", each point then divided into its parts. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.90}

## Its relation to Tuhfat al-Fuqaha'

The editors hold that al-Bada'i' is not a commentary of the usual kind: it does not take the text of [[work/tuhfat-al-fuqaha|al-Tuhfa]] and explain it phrase by phrase, as al-Sarakhsi did in his Mabsut on al-Kafi or Ibn al-Humam on al-Hidaya, nor does it keep al-Tuhfa's order of books or chapters; it rearranges the material while keeping al-Tuhfa's wording, so that a researcher finds al-Tuhfa in al-Bada'i' word for word in another order. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.77} They say al-Kasani relied mainly on al-Tuhfa for his wording, and that the link between the two is well known, even proverbial: "he explained his Tuhfa and married his daughter", as in Kashf al-Zunun. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.77} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.78}

## The edition in the museum

Volume 1, edited by Ali Muhammad Mu'awwad and Adil Ahmad Abd al-Mawjud, Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, Beirut, second edition 1424 AH / 2003. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.2} The editors used nine manuscripts in the Egyptian National Library and two earlier prints, the Khanji press and the Maktabat al-Qahira edition; they traced the verses and hadiths, gave notices of persons, and commented on rare words and on some legal and theoretical questions. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.78} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.79}
