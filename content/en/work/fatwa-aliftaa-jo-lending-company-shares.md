## Summary

A question about borrowing company shares and returning them later against a cheque held as security, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that shares may not be lent, because their value fluctuates and like-for-like return is impossible. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3907}

## The question

The questioner asked whether he may borrow company shares and later return them to their owner, giving him a cheque for their value to be held only as a pledge and security for his right. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3907}

## The answer

The Department answered that a loan is handing over property to someone who benefits from it so that he returns its like, and that it is permitted for the borrower and recommended for the lender. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3907} It said shares are undivided portions of ownership in joint-stock companies whose value changes with the company's assets and activities, so their like cannot be returned; the gap between their value when lent and when returned may be large, causing gross unfairness. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3907} It cited Sharia Standard No. 24 of the Shari'ah Standards for Islamic Financial Institutions, which bases the prohibition on lending shares on the fact that, given the constant change in a company's assets, shares at the time of return are not the like of what they were when lent. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3907} It concluded that company shares may not be lent, given the large fluctuation in their value. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3907}
