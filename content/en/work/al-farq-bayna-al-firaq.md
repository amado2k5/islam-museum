## Summary
Al-Farq bayn al-Firaq wa-Bayan al-Firqa al-Najiya minhum ("The Difference between the Sects, and the Saved Group among Them") is a work on sects and doctrines by Abu Mansur [[person/al-baghdadi-abd-al-qahir-ibn-tahir|Abd al-Qahir ibn Tahir ibn Muhammad al-Baghdadi]] (d. 429 AH / 1037). {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 1} Its editor, Muhammad Uthman al-Khisht, notes that al-Baghdadi's scholarly lineage was Ash'ari: he studied with Abu Ishaq al-Isfara'ini, who studied with Abu al-Hasan al-Bahili, a student of [[person/al-ashari|Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari]]. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 7}

## Why it was written
The editor says al-Baghdadi states at the start that he wrote it at the request of his students, who asked him to explain the hadith on the community's division, show the differences among the sects and describe the saved group, and that he considered this a duty so that sound religion would be distinguished from "the caprices of the misguided sects", in his words. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 8} See: [[concept/al-iftiraq-al-ummah|The division of the community]].

## Its five chapters
The book has five main chapters, each with sections: {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 8}
1. **The transmitted hadith on the community's division**: he gives versions from Abu Hurayra, Abd Allah ibn Amr and Anas with his chains, and holds that the groups meant are groups in doctrine - divine unity, justice, prophecy, the return - not the legal schools. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 9} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 23} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 24} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 25}
2. **How the community divided into seventy-three groups**, with what unites the groups that share the name of Islam; he reports the views of al-Ka'bi, the Karramiyya and others on who belongs to "the community of Islam". {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 9} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 28} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 29}
3. **The doctrines of the sects of caprice** in eight sections: the Rafida, the Khawarij, the Mu'tazila and Qadariyya, the Murji'a, the Najjariyya, the Dirariyya, Bakriyya and Jahmiyya, the Karramiyya, and the anthropomorphists. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 9} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 10}
4. **The groups that claimed Islam without belonging to it**, in al-Baghdadi's view, in seventeen sections, such as the Saba'iyya, the Batiniyya, the Maymuniyya and the Yazidiyya. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 10}
5. **The features of the saved group**, which for him is Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a, in seven sections: its classes, its salvation, its agreed principles, its view of the forebears, its refraining from declaring one another unbelievers, its virtues and leading figures, and its works in religion and the world. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 10} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 271}

## His eight classes of Ahl al-Sunna
Al-Baghdadi counts Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a as eight classes: {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 272}
1. theologians of the "affirmers of attributes" (sifatiyya) who mastered divine unity, prophecy, promise and threat and the imamate, and rejected both anthropomorphism and denial of attributes; {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 272}
2. the leading jurists of both the people of opinion and the people of hadith who held the sifatiyya's doctrines in the principles of religion; he includes the followers of [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]], [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]], al-Awza'i, al-Thawri, [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]], Ibn Abi Layla, Abu Thawr, [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] and the Zahiris; {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 272} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 273}
3. the hadith scholars who knew the paths of transmission and the grounds of criticism and approval; {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 273}
4. the masters of literature, grammar and language in the manner of al-Khalil, Abu Amr ibn al-Ala', Sibawayh, al-Farra' and al-Asma'i; {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 273}
5. the scholars of the Qur'an readings and exegesis in accord with Ahl al-Sunna; {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 273}
6. the ascetic Sufis whose speech, "by way of statement and of allusion", followed the manner of the hadith scholars; {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 273} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 274}
7. those garrisoned on the Muslim frontiers; {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 274}
8. the common people of lands where the marks of Ahl al-Sunna prevail, who follow their scholars, whom - he says - the Sufis called "the filling of Paradise". {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 274}

## Its method as the editor describes it
The editor divides the methods of creed books into rhetorical, dialectical and demonstrative, ranking demonstrative works highest, then dialectical, then rhetorical, and notes that an author may combine them as the context demands. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 10} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 11} His study lists works on sects and doctrines before al-Baghdadi, among them Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali's epistle against the Qadariyya, Wasil ibn Ata's al-Manzila bayn al-Manzilatayn, and Abu Isa al-Warraq's al-Maqalat, which al-Baghdadi used. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 14} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 15} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 16}

## The copy in the museum
The Maktabat Ibn Sina edition, Cairo, studied and edited by Muhammad Uthman al-Khisht, with biographical notes and hadith references in the margins. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 1} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p. 5}

## Not covered here
The sections of chapters 3 and 4 on the sects' doctrines are not read here; the book's descriptions of other groups are al-Baghdadi's, written from his Ash'ari position, and those groups' accounts of themselves are not given here.
