## Summary

A question about a husband who gave his wife gold at the wedding party and later claimed it back as his own, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the gold belongs to the wife, whether it was part of the dower or a gift she had received. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3909}

## The question

The questioner asked about a husband who gave his wife gold during the wedding party and then demanded it back, claiming the gold was his by right. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3909}

## The answer

The Department answered that gold a husband gives his bride at the wedding party is either part of the dower (mahr) or a present and gift. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3909} It said that if it is part of the dower, meaning it was agreed at the marriage contract and recorded in it, it belongs entirely to the wife. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3909} It said that if it was a present, the rules of gifts apply: a gift becomes binding once received and becomes the wife's property, and the husband may not take it back. It cited a hadith reported by Abu Dawud forbidding taking back a gift, except a father's gift to his child. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3909} It noted that Article 579 of the Jordanian Civil Code lists a gift from one spouse to the other among the cases in which a gift cannot be revoked. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3909} It concluded that any other dispute between the spouses is for the courts to settle. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3909}
