## Overview
*Al-Isti'ab fi Ma'rifat al-Ashab* ("The Comprehensive Account of Knowing the Companions") is the dictionary of the Prophet's Companions by [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Abu 'Umar Yusuf ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Barr al-Namari of Cordoba]] (d. 463 AH). {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 2, 15} It is one of the four works on the Companions that Ibn al-Athir exhausted in *Usd al-Ghaba*, marking it with the siglum (ب), and it carries glosses from which the author of *al-Tajrid* benefited. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 398}

## The author's preface
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr begins by saying that, after the Book of God, the most deserving object of study is the Sunna of His Messenger, which explains the meaning of the Book's general statements, and that among the most essential tools for it is knowledge of those who carried it from the Prophet to all people: his Companions. He then states that [[concept/adalat-al-sahaba|the probity of all of them]] is established by the praise of God and of His Messenger, and that there is no fuller vindication than God's choosing them to accompany and support His Prophet, citing the verse of al-Fath describing those who were with him. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 15} He adds that not everyone who saw or believed in him stands with those who hastened to believe and support him, and that God has preferred some of them over others. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 15}

He then gives, with his chains, the views on "the first forerunners": Ibn Sirin and [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]] say they are those who prayed towards both qiblas, [[person/amir-al-shabi|al-Sha'bi]] that they are those who took [[event/bayat-al-ridwan|the Pledge of Ridwan]]. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 15-16} He relates the hadiths that no one who was present at Badr or al-Hudaybiya will enter the Fire, and the reports on how many took the pledge under the tree: fourteen hundred according to Jabir, Ma'qil ibn Yasar and Ibn Abi Awfa, fifteen hundred in what Qatada reports from Ibn al-Musayyab. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 16-17}

## Arrangement
The book follows the alphabet by first name, each letter split into sections by name (the section of Burayda, of Shurahbil, of Nadla…), then a Book of Kunyas for those known by their kunya, then a Book of Women and their Kunyas. The entries are numbered in the Dar al-A'lam edition, the last being no. 3590 (Umm al-Walid al-Ansariyya). {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94, 330, 719, 783, 968} A Companion is often entered under his name with a cross-reference to the fuller notice among the kunyas, as with Abu Umama al-Bahili: "we have given him more fully among the kunyas." {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 348}

## Method
Entries vary in length from a single line giving lineage and who narrated from the Companion to several pages of reports and poetry. He gives lineage, kunya and the variants of the name, and states his preference — of Abu Barza he says "what we gave first is correct", citing Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Yahya ibn Ma'in. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 719, 783} He records conversion, emigration, campaigns, residence and death with its variants, and those who narrated from the Companion, and gives some reports with chains through his Andalusian teachers such as 'Abd al-Warith ibn Sufyan, Qasim ibn Asbagh and Khalaf ibn Qasim. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94, 788}

He states his doubts openly — "his companionship is open to question", "I know no more of him than this", "I have found no lineage for him". {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94, 686, 745} He corrects his predecessors, saying for example that al-Bukhari erred "in a way no genealogist can miss" in making Abu Hind al-Dari the brother of Tamim al-Dari. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 95} He refers readers to his other books: *al-Tamhid*, *al-Istidhkar* and *al-Inbah 'an Qaba'il al-Ruwat*. {cite:istiab-alam-2002 p. 94, 788}
