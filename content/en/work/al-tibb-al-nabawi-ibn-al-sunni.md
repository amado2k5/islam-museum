## Summary
"Al-Tibb al-nabawi" ("Prophetic Medicine") by [[person/ibn-al-sunni|Ibn al-Sunni]] (d. 364 AH) belongs, Heinen suggests, to perhaps the earliest representative of Prophetic Medicine, a branch of Arabic medical writing relying wholly on reports from the Prophet. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 45} [[person/al-suyuti|Al-Suyuti]] drew on it in [[work/al-haya-al-saniyya-suyuti|al-Hay'a al-saniyya]], having himself written a long treatise on Prophetic Medicine. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 45}

## Manuscript
Heinen consulted a copy in the Süleymaniye (Fatih collection no. 3585), 72 folios copied in 775 AH, which he believed the only one extant; Ömer Recep studied it in his Marburg thesis of 1969 together with Abu Nu'aym's Prophetic Medicine. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 45}

## Scholars' views
Heinen notes that some modern scholars, such as Bürgel, saw Prophetic Medicine as a threat to scientific medicine among Muslims; he himself argues that its existence beside scientific medicine was no danger to it, citing Ibn al-Nafis and Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi, who also wrote on the religious sciences, and holding that variety of ideas is worth more than the uniformity of one school. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 45} {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 47}
