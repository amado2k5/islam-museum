## Summary

A question on a doctor withholding the truth of an illness from a patient for his own good, which Egypt's Dar al-Ifta permitted within limits. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 8977}

## The question

The questioner said his sister's husband has cancer, and that when the patient asked the doctor about his condition, the doctor did not tell him plainly. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 8977} The doctor told the questioner instead, explaining that the patient's condition would decline quickly if he learned the truth. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 8977}

## The answer

Dar al-Ifta answered that the basic rule is for the doctor to tell the patient the truth of his illness. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 8977} It held that where the doctor judges from experience that disclosure would bring a serious physical or psychological decline, he may withhold it, and that this is not blameworthy concealment but permitted indirection to the extent needed. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 8977} Its conditions were that only the patient is kept uninformed, that the relatives who need to know are told so that his religious and worldly rights are protected, and that this stays within what necessity requires and harms no one else. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 8977}

## About this record

Egypt's Dar al-Ifta published this fatwa on its site as no. 8977, dated 13 May 2026, in its medicine category, among the fatwas of Grand Mufti [[person/nazir-ayyad|Nazir Ayyad]]. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 8977} The museum records this answer as a historical text attributed to its author; it issues no ruling of its own.
