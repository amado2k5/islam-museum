## Summary

The lecturer opens the sixth year of the mission with Umar ibn al-Khattab. He discusses the reports of the Prophet's prayer for Umar's Islam, the traits that made Umar a source of strength for Islam in Mecca and later as caliph, and Companions' tributes to him. He closes with Umar's clan, Banu Adi, his lineage, relatives and descendants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Setting: Muslims in Mecca and Abyssinia in the sixth year

- He says that this session is devoted almost entirely to one Companion, Umar ibn al-Khattab, because his Islam falls in the sixth year of the mission, where the series now stands. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that the Emigrants to Abyssinia number 82 men and about a dozen women, roughly one hundred Muslims, while around forty Muslims remain in Mecca with the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that these figures are the whole Muslim population of the earth at that stage, a very small number. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### The unique weight of Umar's conversion

- He holds that no conversion during the thirteen Meccan years strengthened Islam as Umar's did, and that the same is true of the whole biography of the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates a statement of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari, that the Muslims have been strong since Umar became Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He notes that some reports place Umar as the fortieth to enter Islam; he cites Ibn Kathir's reservation and prefers the reading that Umar was the fortieth among those who stayed in Mecca rather than emigrated to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that Hamza, though strong and of firm character, and the other Companions, though steadfast under torture, did not bring the same inner strength to the community as Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### The reports of the Prophet's prayer for Umar's Islam

- He relates that the reports about the Prophet praying for Umar come in two groups: one asking God to strengthen Islam through whichever of two men He loves more, Umar or Amr ibn Hisham (Abu Jahl), and one asking for Umar specifically. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He says that al-Tirmidhi graded the first group sound, and that al-Tirmidhi is a respected authority in grading, though the report still needs further study, including how to reconcile it with the second. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that the second wording comes through Hisham ibn Urwa from his father from Aisha with a weakness in the chain, and that some scholars prefer it on the strength of its meaning. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that the first group raises a difficulty: the Prophet had by then prayed against Abu Jahl by name, after the choking incident and the camel entrails incident, so a prayer for Abu Jahl's Islam sits awkwardly after those events. He mentions that some researchers doubt the version naming both men, and says he will return to the chains in a later session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that the second version raises no such difficulty, and that in either case the Prophet prayed for Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Umar before Islam and why he was chosen

- He says that the historical sources give Umar's age at conversion as 26, and he finds it remarkable that so young a man should be singled out in prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that Abu Jahl was the unrivalled leader of Quraysh in opposing the Prophet, obeyed by elders from Banu Abd Shams such as Utba and Shayba sons of Rabi'a and Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, and from his own clan Makhzum such as al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He says that Umar's clan, Banu Adi, had less standing in Quraysh than Makhzum, Hashim or Abd Shams, yet the Prophet prayed for Umar by name and is not reported to have prayed so for other nobles, but against several of them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq, in the version of Ibn Hisham, an account of Umm Abd Allah bint Abi Hathma, who describes Umar, still a polytheist, meeting her party on the way to Abyssinia, showing unexpected tenderness and wishing them well; her husband Amir ibn Rabi'a doubted he would ever convert. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that the report places Umar's roughness toward the Muslims before this meeting, and that Zayd, Umar's own relative from Banu Adi, is said to have been bound by Umar because he became Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Prophetic insight into character and the traits of Umar

- He holds that the Prophet's choice shows his insight into people and his habit of assigning each Companion a role suited to his nature, and he cites the immediate command given to Khalid ibn al-Walid after conversion and the appointment of the very young Usama ibn Zayd over the Syrian army. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that events proved the insight right: Umar's conversion strengthened the Muslims and his caliphate strengthened Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that Umar was not chosen because others would follow him as a leader, as with Abu Jahl or al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, nor for wealth, and he contrasts the case of Ibn Umm Maktum and the later reconciling of hearts at Hunayn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that the secret was Umar's personality: a firm, unyielding self-respect which was both personal and outward-reaching, joined with the strength to carry it, so that he acted on what he judged right. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He says that Umar's strength showed in his dominance over Muslims before Islam, noting that the weakest targets were freedmen such as Sumayya, Ammar and Yasir, then non-Qurayshi allies such as Khabbab, then Qurashis, and that the clans punished their own members to avoid inter-clan feuds, and that Umar is said to have tortured Zinnira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Abu Jahl's influence and Umar's lasting strength

- He holds that Abu Jahl was a dangerous, influential figure, citing how nearly the whole Qurayshi army followed his decision to go on to Badr rather than turn back, and holds that his own pride kept him from Islam even though he knew the truth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that after Uhud Abu Sufyan called out asking whether Muhammad, Abu Bakr and Umar were among the Muslims, and that Umar answered back; the lecturer takes this to show the importance of these three to the community. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that Umar's strength and effect were not confined to the moment of his conversion nor to Mecca or a few episodes in Medina, but showed most fully in his caliphate. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Companions on the loss of Umar

- He relates statements he attributes to Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, through Ibn Hisham and through Ibn Abi Shayba with a sound chain by his judgement, calling Umar's conversion a victory, his emigration a help and his rule a mercy, and saying the Muslims could not pray at the Kaaba until Umar became Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates another report from Ibn Mas'ud, with a good chain according to him, praising Umar's strength, saying that everything on earth mourned his loss, and describing his role in restraining wrongdoing in Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that Umm Ayman is reported to have said on Umar's death that Islam had become weak that day, and he sets this against Ibn Mas'ud's remark about growing strong from Umar's conversion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates from Ibn Abi Shayba that Umar was a strong fortress for Islam: people entered it freely, and after he was killed the fortress was breached so that people left more than entered. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that Ibn Abbas visited the wounded Umar and praised his Islam and his rule as just, that Umar asked him to bear witness to this before God, and that Umar rejoiced when he agreed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates a saying of Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman that Islam in Umar's time was like a man approaching, and after his death like a man withdrawing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Ali's regard for Umar

- He holds that Ali ibn Abi Talib greatly honoured Umar, citing chains he says are sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that those who curse Umar, naming Rafidi and Batini groups, are driven by a hostility he explains as inspired by Satan in revenge for Umar's role; this is his own polemical judgement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He announces a plan for a long series on Umar, possibly covering both Umar ibn al-Khattab and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Umar compared with Hamza and Khalid; his judgment and knowledge

- He holds that Hamza was distinguished by bravery and a front-line fighter's boldness at Badr and Uhud, and that Khalid ibn al-Walid was a specialised military commander, while Umar combined strength of personality, self-respect and leadership in peace, civil affairs, knowledge and law. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He describes Umar as physically big, strong and tall. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that Umar's sound judgment is stressed in the sound texts: he cites the reports that verses came down in agreement with his view, Ibn Mas'ud's remark that an angel seemed to guide him, and a Bukhari report that, if the earlier nations had an inspired man, in this nation it would be Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates statements of Ibn Mas'ud that Umar was the most knowledgeable of them in God's book, the best reader of it and the most discerning in religion, and two dream reports in which the Prophet interpreted milk given to Umar as knowledge and a long garment on Umar as religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### The role of distinguished individuals in the Prophet's mission

- He holds that a single person can shape a community or an age, and that collective bodies never fully replace individual leaders, though leaders should draw on consultation, as the Prophet himself was told to consult. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He holds that most followers of prophets are the weak, as Abu Sufyan told Heraclius, and that the wealthy elite typically oppose them, yet the Prophet was also supported by people of standing, among them Abu Bakr in Mecca and nobles of Yathrib such as Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, through whom others followed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that the Prophet sought to win such leaders, and links this to the zakat category of those whose hearts are reconciled, to the extra gifts at Hunayn, and to the Prophet's declaration at the conquest of Mecca that whoever enters Abu Sufyan's house is safe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He also relates that, alongside this, the Prophet showed lasting care for the weak, and that Abu Bakr is reported to have called the freed Bilal our master. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Umar's clan: location and alliances of Banu Adi

- He holds that Umar belongs to Banu Adi, who meet the Prophet's line far back at Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy, and lays out which Qurayshi clans meet the Prophet nearer: Hashim, Abd Manaf with Abd Shams and Nawfal, and, at Qusayy, Banu Asad, the clan of Khadija and al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates, from a reading of al-Fakihi's history of Mecca, that Banu Adi's homes lay between al-Safa and the Sacred Mosque near Ajyad, and that a mosque gate was named for them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that in the pre-Islamic dispute among Quraysh clans, Banu Adi sold their houses after clashes with Banu Abd Shams and allied with Banu Sahm, and that their chief house was that of al-Khattab ibn Nufayl, which, per al-Azraqi, Umar later gave away as caliph and had demolished to make a resting place for pilgrims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He remarks that pre-Islamic Meccan names such as Abd al-Uzza and Abd Manaf have disappeared in favour of names such as Muhammad, Umar, Uthman, Ali and Abdullah. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}

### Lineage and relatives of Umar

- He relates that Umar's line runs through Nufayl, Abd al-Uzza, Rabah, Abd Allah, Qurt, Razah and Adi to Ka'b, and that Nufayl was a noble to whom Quraysh took disputes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that Nufayl's descendants included Amr, father of Zayd, who in turn was father of Sa'id ibn Zayd, one of the early emigrants, and that Zayd is described as one of those who withdrew from idol worship before Islam and travelled in search of the true religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that Sa'id ibn Zayd is reported to have died in Medina in 51 AH and that al-Waqidi says he died at al-Aqiq and was carried to al-Baqi, with Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Ibn Umar among those present. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates the marriages of Atika bint Zayd, sister of Sa'id: she married Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr, who died after being struck by an arrow at Ta'if, then, in a chain he describes as disputed, Zayd ibn al-Khattab, who died at Yamama, then Umar, then al-Zubayr, then, after a refusal to Ali, al-Hasan; he notes other Companion women who married several leading Companions in turn, such as Asma bint Umays. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that Zayd ibn al-Khattab was older than Umar, accepted Islam before him, was paired by the Prophet as brother with a man of Banu Adi, fought at Badr, Uhud, the Trench and Hudaybiyya, carried the banner at Yamama and was killed there, and that Umar grieved deeply for him; he notes the author he reads from gives no source for this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that Umar had thirteen children, nine sons and four daughters, from several wives including Zaynab bint Maz'un, sister of Uthman ibn Maz'un, who bore Abdullah, Hafsa and Abd al-Rahman, and Umm Kulthum, whom he calls daughter of Fatima al-Zahra, who bore Zayd and Ruqayya; Abdullah ibn Umar was among the most notable. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
- He relates that Salim ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar, a great imam and teacher of al-Zuhri, resembled his grandfather in asceticism, according to Malik, and cites Ibn al-Qayyim for his place among the jurists of Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep24}
