## Summary

The third session of the 2026 seerah series, still on the period before the call to prophethood. He treats the elephant episode and the winter and summer caravans as divine preparation of the Arabs, the re-digging of Zamzam, the Prophet's birth and the debate over its date and its commemoration, the fosterage with Banu Sa'd and the opening of the chest, the other nurses, the orphanhood and the deaths of his mother and grandfather, and the shepherding that every prophet undertook. Along the way he sets out how he grades Ibn Ishaq's reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### Why the pre-prophetic period matters

- He says this is the third session and that the series is still inside the period before the call, which he does not expect to finish in this meeting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that knowing the Arabs' conditions before the call shows which problems the Prophet came to treat, since prophets address the ills of their own time while the final message also stays valid for all later ages. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Surat al-An'am is, in his view, a model presentation of the beliefs of the polytheists and of how the Prophet answered them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that following God's decrees in preparing the people and the Prophet is one of the ways of knowing God, alongside reflection on creation and on revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### Preparation of the people: the scriptures and the Jews of Yathrib

- He relates that earlier scriptures carried announcements of a coming prophet and that this knowledge was preserved among the People of the Book, which he counts as an early preparation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He says the Jews used to tell the Arabs, especially the people of Yathrib, that a prophet would be sent and that they would win with him against them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He mentions the stories of fallen fires and a collapsed palace in Persia at the birth, and says they are told but lack firm foundations. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### The year of the elephant and Abd al-Muttalib

- He says the elephant episode was the most widely known event for the Arabs, drew attention to Mecca and the Kaaba about forty years before the call, and that the Prophet was born in the same year, which he describes as certain and agreed by historians. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Abd al-Muttalib, then the leading man of Quraysh, negotiated with Abraha; Abraha was impressed by his bearing and sat beside him on the ground, and Abd al-Muttalib asked only for his stolen camels, saying the House has a Lord who protects it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He says the best-known report is that the Prophet's father Abdullah died before the birth, and that other reports place the death shortly after it, so that the grandfather took the child in. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that al-Mawardi saw two wonders in the timing: that the unborn Prophet was spared captivity, and that Quraysh did not deserve rescue by their own faith but the event honoured the Kaaba and prepared for Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Ibn Taymiyya held the sign was for the sake of the House, or the Prophet born there, or both, and that Ibn Kathir called it a preparation for the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that the Arabs stoned the grave of Abu Rughal, the guide who led Abraha's army, and that Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham treat the surahs of the elephant and of Quraysh together, with Ibn Hisham adding linguistic notes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that a report preserved by Ibn Ishaq from Aisha says she saw the elephant's driver and its keeper blind and begging in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He mentions that the story of the Prophet's camel halting at Hudaybiyya is, in his reading, a confirmation of the elephant story, and relates the version in which an Arab defiled Abraha's church and angered him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### The winter and summer journeys of Quraysh

- He says the winter and summer caravans, which Hashim established, brought Quraysh prosperity and are mentioned in the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates Ibn Ashur's reading of Surat Quraysh: the Arabs honoured Quraysh as keepers of the Kaaba, so their trade travelled safely across Arabia, Mecca became a trading hub, and food came by sea to Jeddah and from Tabala and Jurash. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Ibn Ashur links the surah to Ibrahim's prayer for a safe land and provision, and counts the safety of the sanctuary as compensation for Quraysh's small numbers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He says Ibn Ashur mentions that Mecca's provision lapsed only after the Prophet prayed against it, in the early Medinan years, when a seven-year famine struck. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### The re-digging of Zamzam

- He relates, following a chain Ibn Ishaq gives back to Ali ibn Abi Talib, that Abd al-Muttalib was told in dreams over several nights to dig at a place described by signs, and that he dug with his son al-Harith, then the only son he had. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Quraysh claimed a share in the well as the well of Ismail, that the dispute was referred to a woman diviner of Banu Sa'd in the Levant, and that on the way the party ran out of water and found it at Abd al-Muttalib's mount, which they took as God's judgement for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that Abd al-Muttalib's role fits the chain of divine preparation, and notes that the watering of pilgrims stayed with his family, which is why al-Abbas was excused from spending the nights at Mina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### How he grades Ibn Ishaq's reports

- He says that when a report has an unbroken chain he stops at its wording, while stories with no chain at all he treats more briefly in his lecturing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He ranks Ibn Ishaq's material in levels: first, chains also found in the hadith collections; second, chains he alone transmits but are connected through acceptable narrators; third, connected chains ending in early mursal transmitters such as Urwa ibn al-Zubayr; then reports from elders of the Ansar, then the weakest, vague phrases such as it reached me. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that the weakest level is only for reassurance and not for argument, and that Ibn Ishaq was not known for careful choice of narrators. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Monday and the date of the birth

- He relates that the Prophet explained fasting on Mondays by saying it was the day of his birth and of the first revelation, a report he places in Sahih Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates a further report in Ahmad from Ibn Abbas, whose chain he calls weak, listing Monday as the day of birth, call, departure, arrival at Medina and death, and notes Ibn Kathir's comments on it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that the Prophet singled out Monday every week, not a fixed calendar day each year, and that later commemoration on a set day of Rabi al-Awwal honours the birth from an angle the Prophet did not take. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that sincere love is not proof of following the Prophet's own method, and that those who fast Mondays can claim to follow the angle he chose. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates Ibn Kathir's list of opinions on the date: the second, the eighth, the tenth, the twelfth and the seventeenth of Rabi al-Awwal, with the twelfth as the majority view and the eighth held by some historians cited by Ibn Abd al-Barr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Ahmad Shawqi's poem on the birth is a famous and finely made text, and that the Companions chose the Hijra, not the birth or the call, as the start of the Muslim calendar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Signs at the birth: light over Syria

- He relates that the Prophet's mother saw a light come from her that lit the palaces of Syria, and relates Ibn Kathir's reading that this points to the religion's later settling in Syria and Ibn Rajab's reading that it points to the light the Prophet brought. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He cites the Heraclius exchange with Abu Sufyan as showing that the People of the Book already linked the Prophet with Syria. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates a chain through Hassan ibn Thabit about a Jew in Yathrib announcing the rising of the star of Ahmad on the night of the birth, which he places at the third level and calls not firmly established. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### Fosterage with Banu Sa'd

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Abd al-Muttalib took the newborn into the Kaaba to give thanks and then sought nurses, and that the child was given to Halima of Banu Sa'd. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's genealogy of Halima's father back to Qays Aylan, and explains that Hawazin and Thaqif belong to that branch of Mudar, while the Prophet descends from the other branch through Ilyas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates Ibn Kathir's account that the blessing on Halima's household later reached Hawazin, whose delegation after Hunayn appealed to the Prophet on the ground of his fosterage and obtained the release of captives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates the speech of the Hawazin spokesman Zuhayr ibn Surad as given in Ibn Ishaq, and Ibn Kathir's figure of six thousand captives freed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### The opening of the chest

- He relates, from a report of Anas ibn Malik in Sahih Muslim, that while the Prophet was a boy among Banu Sa'd the angel Gabriel opened his chest and washed his heart with Zamzam water, and that Anas later saw the mark of the stitching. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that the event was physical, fits the preparation of the Prophet, and kept him from the vices of the pre-Islamic people, and that its memory also served as psychological preparation before his retreats at Hira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### Other nurses: Umm Ayman and Thuwayba

- He relates Ibn Kathir's note that Umm Ayman, named Baraka, cared for the child, was inherited from his father, was later freed, and married Zayd ibn Haritha, bearing Usama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Thuwayba, a freedwoman of Abu Lahab, also nursed the Prophet, and that Abu Lahab freed her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates the Bukhari and Muslim report in which the Prophet declines a marriage to the daughter of Umm Salama because both he and her father Abu Salama were nursed by Thuwayba, and mentions that Hamza is said to have been nursed by her too. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates Urwa's addition in Bukhari that Abu Lahab was shown in a dream after death receiving a little water because of freeing Thuwayba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### Orphanhood and divine care

- He holds that the Prophet's orphanhood, with his father dying before birth, his mother after six years and his grandfather after eight, was part of his preparation, as hardships were for Musa and Yusuf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that hardship sent to those God favours comes with care and not abandonment, and gives the cases of Hajar left in the valley, Yusuf in the well, and the Prophet taken in by his uncle Abu Talib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Amina died at al-Abwa returning from a visit to her kin of Banu Adi ibn al-Najjar in Medina, and that Abd al-Muttalib had a mat laid in the Kaaba's shade where the boy sat, the grandfather defending him against his uncles. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates that Abd al-Muttalib died eight years after the elephant year and left the boy to Abu Talib, whose mother Fatima bint Amr of Makhzum was also the Prophet's father's mother. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He uses Hassan's line about the sons of the daughter of Makhzum to show why genealogy is worth knowing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}

### Shepherding as the prophets' practical training

- He relates a report from Jabir that the Prophet, picking arak fruit, advised taking the black ones and said every prophet has herded sheep, and a report from Abu Hurayra in which the Prophet says he herded for the people of Mecca for small payments. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that herding sheep in particular, not camels or cattle, trained prophets in care of the weak, patience and organisation, since sheep need constant watching and are easily lost to wolves. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He relates Ibn Hajar's view that herding prepares prophets gradually for leading a community, and that the Prophet's mention of it shows humility. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
- He holds that anyone preparing a reformer should include practical training and not only theory, and compares the training of Musa with the staff before facing Pharaoh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep3}
