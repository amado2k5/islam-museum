## Summary

A question about renting farmland for money, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that bare land without trees may be rented for a known sum of money, while land with fruit trees may not be rented, though a sharecropping contract for orchards (musaqat) may be made on its conditions. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3747}

## The question

The questioner asked about renting land for farming at a known sum per dunum paid in advance, with the produce going to the farmer alone and any loss borne by him alone. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3747}

## The answer

The Department answered that bare land without trees may be rented for a known sum of money, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] in which Hanzala ibn Qays asked [[person/rafi-ibn-khadij|Rafi ibn Khadij]] about renting land; Rafi mentioned that it had been prohibited, then said there was no harm in renting it for gold and silver. It quoted [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]]'s [[work/al-minhaj-sharh-sahih-muslim|commentary on Sahih Muslim]] (10/198) that [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]], [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] and many others permitted renting land for gold and silver. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3747} It said that land with fruit trees whose fruit has not ripened may not be rented, since that amounts to selling fruit before it is clearly ripe, which is prohibited, but the worker and the landowner may agree a sharecropping contract for the trees (musaqat) on its Shari'a conditions. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3747} It noted that the Jordanian Iftaa Council distinguished in its resolution 80 between bare land, which may be rented for gold, silver or money, and land with trees, which may not be rented for money for a year, more or less, whether before or after the fruit ripens, nor for a fixed, unshared portion of its produce. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3747}
