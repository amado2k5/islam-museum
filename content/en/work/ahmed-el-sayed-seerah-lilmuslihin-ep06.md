## Summary

The sixth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers examines the earliest Muslims as the core of the Prophet's school. The speaker argues that the Companions' role in Mecca was endurance and preserving the faith, while in Medina they carried many leadership and teaching tasks, and that the early converts held a large share of them. He then discusses the Prophet's concern about the opening of worldly wealth and closes with the foundation that, in his view, explains the rapid conquests. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Recap of the series and aim of the session

- He recaps the earlier sessions: the first was an introduction to the value and centrality of the biography, the second treated the signs preceding prophethood, the third the importance of the pre-prophetic period, then the descent of revelation and the secret phase of the call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says that today's session treats some pillars of the Prophetic school, namely the earliest converts to Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that anyone seeking reform today should look at the Prophet's school and at who was in it, because its model of upbringing is far from much of present educational and missionary practice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### The Companions' role in Mecca versus Medina

- He says that in the Meccan period the Companions' main role was patience, steadfastness and holding to the faith, while the Prophet himself bore the greater burden and no detachments were sent out. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that the migrations to Abyssinia show that their role was preserving the religion rather than confronting the unbelievers, and he names Uthman and Jafar ibn Abi Talib among the leading migrants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says that in Medina the situation changed completely and within the first years every Companion had a particular task. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Seriousness and the range of Medinan tasks

- He names seriousness and work as a clear mark of the Prophet's school, since detachments left Medina continually after the move. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates that some scholars count seventy-seven detachments between the second and eleventh years of the Hijra that were led by Companions, besides nineteen expeditions led by the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He lists the tasks Companions held: deputising over Medina when the Prophet left, being sent as teachers, governing newly entered regions, collecting alms, and carrying letters to rulers such as the Persian and Byzantine emperors. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that Meccan upbringing prepared them for these tasks, and that institutions that raise would-be reformers must instil seriousness, sincerity, sacrifice, patience and firm faith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Who the earliest converts were

- He counts Khadija, Abu Bakr, Ali and Zayd as the first four to accept Islam, among women, men, boys and freed slaves respectively. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He lists those who accepted through Abu Bakr as Uthman, Sa'd, Talha and al-Zubayr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He also names Bilal, Khabbab, Ammar, Suhayb and Abu Salama, together with Uthman ibn Maz'un, Abdullah ibn Jahsh, al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam and Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As, as early converts recorded by the biographers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Abu Bakr and the first detachments

- He says that Abu Bakr was the Prophet's chief supporter, was appointed to lead the people in prayer during the Prophet's illness, and carried the religion after him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates that, according to Sahih Muslim, the Prophet put Abu Bakr in command of a detachment against Banu Fazara, and that the biographers mention a second one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says that the first detachment in Islam was given to Ubayda ibn al-Harith, known as one of the three who fought in single combat at Badr, was wounded and died on the way back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says the second went to Hamza and the third, he thinks, to Abdullah ibn Jahsh, whose detachment to Nakhla fought in a sacred month and about which a verse was revealed, and that all three commanders were early converts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Zayd ibn Haritha and Abu Salama as commanders

- He relates, citing Aisha with a chain that Ibn Hajar called strong, that whenever the Prophet sent Zayd with an army he made him its commander. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says Ibn Hajar found Zayd to be the Companion most often put in command of detachments, perhaps seven or nine of them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad, husband of Umm Salama before the Prophet married her, was wounded at Uhud, was given a detachment after a month's recovery, and died when the wound reopened in the third or fourth year of the Hijra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Early converts sent as deputies, teachers and governors

- He says that Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum, an early convert, was the one the Prophet most often left in charge of Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says the Prophet sent Mus'ab ibn Umayr, also an early convert, to teach and prepare the people of Medina before the move. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says that when Yemen came into Islam the Prophet sent Mu'adh, Abu Musa and Ali, the last of whom was the first boy to accept Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that a reader looking for the Companions' tasks will find that the early converts had a great share of them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### The Prophet's willingness to send those he loved

- He holds that the Prophet did not hold back those he loved but sent them to command armies and into danger, sometimes losing them as martyrs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says Zayd ibn Haritha, whom the Prophet had adopted before adoption was abolished, was with him from the beginning, was given command of each detachment, and was killed at Mu'ta. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says Jafar ibn Abi Talib, a cousin of the Prophet who spent over ten years in Abyssinia, was also put in command and killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says Usama ibn Zayd, who inherited his father's title of beloved of the Messenger, was put in command of detachments while still a young man. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that much present educational and missionary work is far from this serious upbringing, and that change will only come through people raised in that school. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Love of the world, moderation and the Prophet's diagnosis

- He relates a report in which the Prophet foretells that nations will gather against the Muslims despite their numbers, and that the cause is love of the world and dislike of death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that in the Prophet's school the problem was the opposite: the Prophet had to restrain Companions from withdrawing from life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates from Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, in al-Bukhari, that the Prophet refused Uthman ibn Maz'un's wish for celibacy, and the story of three men who thought the Prophet's worship small and who vowed fasting, avoiding meat and avoiding marriage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He adds that women also took part in the hard stages, in migrating to Abyssinia and Medina and in treating the wounded and giving water. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Early converts who died before the worldly fruits

- He says some early converts died before the conquests brought wealth, while others lived to enjoy them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates from Aisha, in al-Bukhari, that they were not filled with dates until Khaybar was conquered in the seventh year of the Hijra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates that Khabbab, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and others reflected on those who died without enjoying their reward, and that Abd al-Rahman wept over Mus'ab ibn Umayr who was killed with too little cloth to cover him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates a story of a detachment given one date a day each, and of the Khabat expedition where the men ate leaves, which Sa'd cited to answer those who criticised him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### The Prophet's fear of wealth after conquests

- He relates that when wealth arrived from Bahrayn and Abu Ubayda brought it at dawn, the Companions rushed toward it and the Prophet smiled and told them he feared more for them once the world opened to them than in poverty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates that near the end of his life the Prophet said he did not fear that they would associate partners with God but that the world would be opened to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates from Sahih Muslim a conversation with Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf in which the Prophet warned that on conquering Persia and Byzantium they would compete, then envy, then turn away from one another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that this foretelling shows the Prophet's insight, since no one then thought those empires could fall. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### Early converts remembered with little fame

- He says Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As was among the earliest converts, even fourth or fifth in some accounts, migrated early to Abyssinia, returned with Jafar in the seventh year, was reportedly made governor of Sana'a, and was killed at Ajnadayn in the thirteenth year of the Hijra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates that before Uhud Abdullah ibn Jahsh and Sa'd each voiced a wish, Abdullah asking to be killed by a fierce enemy who would mutilate him, and that this happened to him at Uhud, so that he was called the one mutilated for God, as Ibn Abd al-Barr says in al-Istiab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He mentions Hatib ibn al-Harith, an early convert who migrated with his wife to Abyssinia and died there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates that Asma bint Umays, who he says was later married by well-known Companions, was with Jafar in Abyssinia, and that on her return Umar told her the Medina emigrants had precedence over her group, whereupon the Prophet told her that the people of the ship had two emigrations. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}

### The foundation behind the conquests

- He concludes that the results achieved in the Prophet's time and after rested on firm ground, which was a doctrinal, faith-based and purifying formation of souls, not material crafts or civilisational products. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He holds that the Prophet did not send people to Persia and Byzantium to learn building, and that such achievements are later reflections that can even become a problem. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari, the words of al-Mughira before the Persian commander, describing the Arabs as former idol worshippers who sucked date stones from hunger and were sent a prophet from among themselves. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says that the Persian and Byzantine empires fell and Muslim armies reached the borders of China, and a century after the Prophet reached about seventy kilometres south of Paris. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
- He says that when this base weakened, deviations appeared even in movements of jihad, and that what changed Arabia, which had three hundred and sixty idols around the Kaaba, was the souls the Prophet formed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep6}
