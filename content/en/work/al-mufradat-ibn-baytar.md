## Overview
"Al-Mufradat fil-Adwiya wal-Aghdhiya" (Simple Remedies and Foods) by [[person/ibn-al-baytar|Ibn al-Baytar]] the herbalist of Malaga. According to "Shadharat al-Dhahab", knowledge of plants, their properties, their uses and where they grow came to its end with this book. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 234}

## Structure and sources

Commonly called the Jami li-mufradat al-adwiya wa-l-aghdhiya, this alphabetical work belongs to the author's later life: Kaya reports composition in 1242–1248. Iranica distinguishes about 1,422 plant, animal and mineral materials from 931 synonymic entries. Their sum, 2,353, counts entries, not newly discovered plants. It describes over 150 sources alongside the compiler's observations {cite:tdv-mufradat-kaya composition and structure} {cite:iranica-ibn-baytar entry counts and sources}.

Accounts retain authorial attribution, prominently including Dioscorides, Galen, al-Razi and Ibn Sina. Kaya reports the preface's policy of observation, experience and rejecting unreliable information. This is a report of the compiler's programme, not an original preface read here or evidence of a modern clinical trial for every entry. The [[invention/ibn-baytar-comparative-materia-medica|comparative-method account]] explains the organization and its limits {cite:tdv-mufradat-kaya preface} {cite:iranica-ibn-baytar quoted authors}.

## Criticism, reception and reading limits

Iranica discusses Arabicized Persian names and errors of interpretation or copying: multiple languages do not imply proficiency in each. Kaya reports criticism of predecessors and botanical additions and classifications from cited studies, not counts we independently repeated on the original. The organ-arranged Mughni, Dioscorides commentary and Ibana remain different works, not automatically parts of this compendium {cite:iranica-ibn-baytar Persian nomenclature} {cite:tdv-mufradat-kaya contributions} {cite:tdv-ibn-baytar-kaya works}.

Kaya describes Turkish and European translations, Leclerc's three-volume French version, the Bulaq printing, its reprint and a later edited adaptation changing arrangement and detail. We have not inspected or collated those witnesses, editions or the original preface.
