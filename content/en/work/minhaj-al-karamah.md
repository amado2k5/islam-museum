## Summary
*Minhaj al-Karama fi Ma'rifat al-Imama* ("The Way of Honour in Knowing the Imamate") is a treatise on the imamate according to the Twelver Imami school, written by [[person/al-allama-al-hilli|Ibn al-Mutahhar al-Hilli]] and answered by [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] in *[[work/minhaj-al-sunnah|Minhaj al-Sunna al-Nabawiyya]]*. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 51} What is shown here is taken from Ibn Taymiyya's quotations and from the footnotes of the editor of that reply, not from an independent copy of the treatise.

## Its writing and the king it was written for
Ibn Taymiyya says that al-Hilli composed the treatise for the king he names in it as "Khudabanda", and that it was offered to kings and rulers inclined to the Imamiyya. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 17} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 19} From the manuscript of the treatise he labels "k", the editor of Minhaj al-Sunna records the king's name as "Aljaytu Khudabanda Muhammad", followed by a prayer for the permanence of his rule; this is [[person/oljaitu|Öljeitü]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 62}

## Introduction and chapters
The treatise begins with an introduction in which al-Hilli calls it "a noble treatise and a subtle discourse" and states that the imamate is the most important matter in the rules of religion and the noblest question for Muslims. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 61} The chapters then follow, as Ibn Taymiyya reports them: the first on the positions about the imamate, the second on why the Imami school must be followed, the third on the proofs of Ali's imamate after the Prophet, the fourth on the Twelve Imams and the fifth on refuting the caliphates of Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 62} The reply's table of contents adds a sixth chapter on the arguments for Abu Bakr's imamate. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 14}

## Its account of the Imami doctrine
As Ibn Taymiyya quotes him, al-Hilli states in the first chapter that God sent the prophets infallible, so that trust in their words would not be lost. God then followed prophecy with the imamate, appointing infallible guardians so that people would be safe from their errors and the world would not be left without His favour and mercy. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 91} He states that the Prophet designated Ali as his successor, then al-Hasan, al-Husayn, Ali ibn al-Husayn, Muhammad al-Baqir, Ja'far al-Sadiq, Musa al-Kazim, Ali al-Rida, Muhammad al-Jawad, Ali al-Hadi, al-Hasan al-Askari and then "the successor, the Proof" (al-Khalaf al-Hujja). {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 91}

In the third chapter the first rational proof is that the imam must be infallible. He grounds it in the claim that man is social by nature and cannot meet his own needs for food, drink, clothing and shelter alone, so society needs someone to uphold it. {cite:minhaj-sunna-salim-v4 p. 8}

## Its account of the Sunni doctrine, as Ibn Taymiyya reports it
In Ibn Taymiyya's report, al-Hilli attributes to the Sunnis positions on predestination and justice. Among them are that God may punish the obedient and reward the sinner, and that the prophets are not infallible. He also attributes to them that the Prophet designated no imam and died without a testament, and that the imam after him was Abu Bakr, by Umar's pledge with the consent of four men: Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, Salim the client of Abu Hudhayfa, Usayd ibn Hudayr and Bashir ibn Sa'd. Then came Umar, by Abu Bakr's designation. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 92} The account ends with the Sunni imamate passing through the Abbasids down to al-Musta'sim. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 93}

:::perspectives
**Sunni: Ibn Taymiyya in Minhaj al-Sunna.** He holds that this report of the Sunni position contains lies and distortion. In his view it is also wrong to bring the questions of predestination, divine justice and divine injustice into the topic of the imamate, because each position on predestination is held by parties of both the Sunnis and the Shia. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 93}

**Imami: Ibn al-Mutahhar al-Hilli (as quoted by his opponent).** The imamate is a necessary divine favour, and the imam is infallible and explicitly designated. The imams are twelve, the first Ali and the last the Proof. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 91} The text of the treatise itself has not yet been consulted in an Imami edition.
:::
