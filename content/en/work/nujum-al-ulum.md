## Summary
The Nujum al-Ulum is a manuscript in the Chester Beatty Library, Dublin, which Mark Zebrowski describes as a compilation on astronomy, magic, animals and weapons with 876 illustrations, some full-page, others marginal decoration. It is the most important work attributable to the reign of [[person/ali-adil-shah-i|Ali Adil Shah I]] of [[concept/bijapur-painting|Bijapur]]. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 60}

## Contents
The pictures follow the chapters: the heavens, angels, the zodiac, talismans and sorcerers; magic diagrams; processions; demonesses, some terrifying and others resembling Hindu deities; animals; and weapons — duelling being a passion among young Muslims at Bijapur and Ahmadnagar, which Firishta deplored. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 60} {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 62} The museum describes the contents without endorsing or judging them.

## Date and style
The date 978 AH/1570–71 appears twice in the text and in a simplified colophon, and a former owner's note says it was in the library of [[person/ibrahim-adil-shah-ii|Ibrahim Adil Shah II]], which suggests a Bijapuri origin. The author calls it a compendium of the motifs of Deccani painting in the following century; its male figures show the influence of Mughal painting of the late 1560s, while its women follow a southern canon of beauty. {cite:zebrowski-deccani-painting-1983 p. 62}
