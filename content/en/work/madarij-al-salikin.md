## Summary
*Madarij al-Salikin bayna Manazil Iyyaka Na'budu wa-Iyyaka Nasta'in* ("The Ranks of the Wayfarers between the Stations of 'You Alone We Worship and You Alone We Ask for Help'") is a book by Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya, Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr al-Zur'i al-Dimashqi (691–751 AH). It is a commentary on *[[work/manazil-al-sairin|Manazil al-Sa'irin]]* by Abu Isma'il [[person/al-ansari-al-harawi-abd-allah|al-Harawi al-Ansari]], built around the verse of al-Fatiha "You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help". {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 1} {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 12}

## Subject
The editors of the Dar al-Sumay'i edition describe it as covering most questions of creed:
- the kinds of tawhid;
- God's names and attributes, affirming them and answering those who deny or reinterpret them;
- divinity and true servanthood;
- predestination and faith;
- the Sunni approach to the acts of the heart and how people differ in them;
- the stations of worship and the description of those journeying to God;
- the link between affirming the revealed law and affirming predestination. {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 5} {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 6}

## Built on Manazil al-Sa'irin
Ibn al-Qayyim follows al-Harawi's stations with explanation and notes. Al-Harawi set out a hundred stations in ten sections of ten. Each station has three degrees: that of the common, that of the elect and that of the elect of the elect. {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 12} The sections are beginnings, doors, dealings, character, principles, valleys, states, friendships, realities and ends. {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 12}

## His stance towards al-Harawi and the Sufis
The editors list among the studies in their edition "Ibn al-Qayyim's objections to al-Harawi in Manazil al-Sa'irin", a comparison of the commentary by [[person/afif-al-din-al-tilimsani|Afif al-Din al-Tilimsani]] with Ibn al-Qayyim's, and "tawhid according to al-Harawi". {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 13} The editor of part 1 concludes that Ibn al-Qayyim was fair to the early Sufi masters known for their love of the Sunna. He did not, the editor says, cancel their merits because of their ecstatic utterances, and he distinguished them from the people of union and indwelling, whose occasional agreement with the Book and the Sunna does not earn them praise. The editor adds that Ibn al-Qayyim knew commentaries on the *Manazil*, especially al-Tilimsani's. He used it to explain al-Harawi's wording and rejected the false readings that al-Harawi's words could be given. {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 802} This is the editor's assessment; the Sufis' own texts on these questions have not yet been presented.

## Questions in part 1
Among his findings, the editor of part 1 lists:
- the greatness of al-Fatiha: the kinds of tawhid it contains, how it points to prophethood and recompense, its healing of hearts and bodies, and its answer to those who leave the straight path;
- the breadth of the idea of servanthood;
- that each duty has stations which a person crosses one after another;
- that, for Ibn al-Qayyim, a servant's repentance is surrounded by God's turning to him before and after it;
- that its reality is not only leaving a sin and regretting it but also resolving to do what is commanded. {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 802} {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 803}

## The edition in the museum
The edition is a joint work by professors of creed and contemporary schools at Qassim University, published by Dar al-Sumay'i, Riyadh, 1st edition 1432 AH / 2011. It has five parts and a sixth of indexes, each part with its own editor:
- part 1, by Nasir ibn Sulayman al-Sa'wi, from the start to the section on minor and major sins;
- part 2, by Ali ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Qar'awi, to the end of the station of refinement;
- part 3, by Salih ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Tuwayjiri, from steadfastness to intimacy;
- part 4, from the station of remembrance;
- part 5, by Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Khudayri, from unveiling to the end. {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 2} {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 11} {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 12}

The editors say they set the text of the *Manazil* in bold between small brackets, and defined each station from the Sufis' own books. They also explained Sufi and theological terms. {cite:madarij-salikin-sumayi-q1 p. 14} The museum's library holds part 1.
