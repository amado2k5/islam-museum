## Summary

The tenth session turns from the early male converts to the early Muslim women of Mecca who pledged allegiance and migrated to Abyssinia or Medina. The lecturer follows the biographical entries of Ibn Sa'd for both well-known and little-known women, noting their kin, marriages and ordeals, and stresses how costly early conversion was, especially for daughters of Qurayshi leaders. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Scope of the session and the sources it follows

- He says this session continues the stage of the first entrants into Islam and moves to the early women converts, those who pledged allegiance and those who migrated to Abyssinia and to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says the women of the Ansar are left for the later discussion of the pledge of al-Aqaba, just as the early Ansari men were postponed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says the series relies on the Sira of Ibn Hisham as its main narrative source and on Ibn Sa'd as its main source for biographies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Ibn Ishaq, in the list he gave of roughly fifty early converts after Khadija, Abu Bakr, Ali and Zayd, included several women. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He states an aim of the series: to make the Companions, men and women, known one by one, though not exhaustively. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### The standing of the early women: Khadija and Sumayya

- He says a verse promising that no worker's deed, male or female, is lost is the guiding text for the whole session, and that the verse naming the first and foremost emigrants and helpers also covers women. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says the first person to believe after the Prophet was a woman, Khadija, and that scholars differ on the first male convert, naming Ali, Abu Bakr or Zayd as views, while the priority of Khadija is close to unanimous. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says he will not retell Khadija's story here because it was covered in the sessions on the descent of revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says scholars and biographers also record a woman, Sumayya, as the first martyr of Islam, and that the first martyr being a woman is something to reflect on. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He holds that the biography presents a balanced picture of women, avoiding both a restrictive view that diminishes them in the name of religion and a feminist view that equates women with men in every field. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Umm Ayman Baraka and her son Ayman

- He says Umm Ayman, named Baraka, was Abyssinian, a servant of the Prophet's father Abdullah, then the Prophet's nurse, and that the Prophet freed her when he married Khadija. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that she first married Ubayd ibn Zayd of the Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj and bore Ayman, then married Zayd ibn Haritha and bore Usama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says her son Ayman stood firm with the Prophet at Hunayn and was killed there, and that al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, in a poem on Hunayn, meant him as the eighth of those who held their ground. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that on her emigration she was thirsty and fasting between al-Munsarif and al-Rawha, was given water from above, and never felt thirst again; he counts this among the marvels of Companions in the Prophet's lifetime. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He explains that al-Rawha lay on the old pilgrim road, and that al-Munsarif was a fork in the road, now the settlement called al-Musayjid. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says a mursal report, which he cannot confirm, has the Prophet commending marriage to Umm Ayman, and that Zayd then married her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates the Prophet's jest with her about carrying her on a camel's offspring, and her playful scuffle with Anas over date palms given to her, a story he places in Sahih Muslim with an Ibn Sa'd chain of transmission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates from al-Waqidi that she attended Uhud giving water and tending the wounded and was at Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that after the Prophet's death she wept for the revelation that had ceased, and that at the death of Umar she said the affairs of Islam had weakened. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Umm Kulthum bint Uqba and the daughters of Quraysh's leaders

- He holds that it is striking that almost every leading Qurayshi opponent had a daughter who became Muslim, some early and some at the conquest of Mecca or later, and he lists daughters of Utba, Shayba, Abu Sufyan and others as recorded one after another in Ibn Sa'd. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Umm Kulthum was the daughter of Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, whom he describes as among the fiercest enemies of the Prophet and the man who laid the camel offal on him in prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says she became Muslim early in Mecca and pledged allegiance before the hijra but migrated only after Hudaybiyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that her brothers came after her demanding her return under the Hudaybiyya terms, and that the verse about testing believing women who migrate was revealed concerning her, creating an exception for women. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that she was the only Qurayshi woman known to have left her parents alone as a Muslim migrant, travelling with a man of Khuza'a as guide. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says she married in turn Zayd ibn Haritha, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Amr ibn al-As, and compares Asma bint Umays, widowed of Ja'far, who then married Abu Bakr and later Ali. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He holds that remarriage of widows and divorced women was easy in that society, unlike modern social pressures, and gives the case of Atika, widow of a Companion, who later married Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Umm Habiba bint Abi Sufyan

- He holds that Umm Habiba's conversion was a deliberate choice, since she was the daughter of Abu Sufyan, then a leader of Quraysh and standard-bearer against the Prophet after Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that she migrated to Abyssinia with her husband Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh, and cites al-Waqidi that she left Mecca pregnant. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Ubaydullah left Islam and became a Christian in Abyssinia and died as one, and calls this well-attested, while Umm Habiba stayed firm in exile. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says the Prophet then sent an envoy to the Negus to propose to her, and Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As gave her in marriage, in the seventh year of the hijra by Ibn Sa'd's account. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that she was in her thirties when she reached Medina, and infers she was about twenty when she first became Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### A riddle on Safiyya and Arwa, and Hassan's satire of Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith

- He sets a hard question: what links the Prophet's aunts Safiyya and Arwa, who had different mothers, Hala bint Wuhayb and Fatima bint Amr of Makhzum. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He explains that the link lies in the mothers' lineages, used in Sahih Muslim's account of Hassan ibn Thabit's satire of Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith, a cousin of the Prophet who was the fiercest poet of Quraysh against him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Hassan was told to satirise Quraysh with the Prophet's blessing, and that attacking a Hashimite cousin's descent was only possible through the mothers, as the Prophet's mother line and Hamza's were not Hashim's own. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Hassan composed lines declaring the peak of glory to be with the Hashimites and slighting the satirised man's mother as of low birth, while excluding the Prophet and Hamza by naming their maternal lines as honourable. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He holds that opposing polytheists and angering them is loved by God, but that justice and kindness toward peaceful non-Muslims remain required. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib

- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Safiyya, sister of Hamza through her mother, first married al-Harith, then al-Awwam ibn Khuwaylid, bearing al-Zubayr, al-Sa'ib and Abd al-Kaaba, became Muslim, pledged and migrated to Medina, and received forty wasqs at Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates a mursal report from Urwa that when the Prophet left Medina to fight, women were placed in Hassan's fortress, and that during Uhud Safiyya killed a Jewish spy on the wall with a pole after Hassan hesitated. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He accepts Urwa's mursal reports as strong in the biography unless something objectionable appears, and notes the weaker chain of a second report. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that at Uhud she came with a spear rebuking the fleeing, and that the Prophet had her son al-Zubayr stop her from seeing her brother Hamza's mutilated body, though she later looked and bore it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says she was strict with her son al-Zubayr, whose courage and boldness he links to her upbringing, and he adds that people differ in temperament; he gives the Prophet's differing treatment of Abu Dharr and Khalid ibn al-Walid in command as illustration, and Khalid's mistake after which the Prophet disowned the act yet kept appointing him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Safiyya was buried at al-Baqi and died in the caliphate of Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Arwa bint Abd al-Muttalib and her son Tulayb

- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Arwa married Umayr ibn Wahb and bore Tulayb, who became Muslim in the house of al-Arqam and told his mother, who said she would watch what her sisters did and then, after his plea, declared her faith and supported the Prophet with her tongue. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that Tulayb struck Abu Jahl when Quraysh harmed the Prophet, was bound, and was freed by his uncle Abu Lahab, and that Arwa said his best day was the one he defended his cousin. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that Abu Lahab then rebuked her for leaving Abd al-Muttalib's faith, and she urged him to support his nephew; he declined. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He complains that his edition of Ibn Sa'd contains many errors in names. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Lesser-known early women of Ibn Sa'd's entries

- He says Ibn Sa'd's phrase that a woman became Muslim early in Mecca marks the earliest converts, and that each such woman faced hardship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that Asma bint Mukharriba, mother of Abu Jahl, became Muslim, pledged and reached Medina, and he marvels that a mother of a man killed at Badr did so. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He lists Harmala bint Abd al-Aswad, who migrated to Abyssinia with Jahm ibn Qays and died there, and Umayna bint Khalaf, who migrated with Khalid ibn Sa'id, bore Sa'id and Ama there, and later married al-Zubayr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates the case of Salama bint Umays, wife of Hamza, whose daughter was claimed at the Fulfilled Umra by Ali, Zayd and Ja'far, and that the Prophet awarded her to Ja'far because his wife Asma was her maternal aunt. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Salama later married Shaddad ibn al-Hadd al-Laythi and bore the tabi'i Abd Allah ibn Shaddad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He lists Fatima bint Safwan, who migrated with Amr ibn Sa'id, and Hasana, who migrated with her son Shurahbil ibn Hasana, the later conqueror in Syria, who carried a message from Umm Habiba to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that Umm Ruman, wife of Abu Bakr, became Muslim early, came to Medina with the Prophet's family, and died in Dhu al-Hijja of the sixth year, with the Prophet praying over her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Umm al-Fadl Lubaba and the dispute over the first woman after Khadija

- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Umm al-Fadl Lubaba bint al-Harith, wife of al-Abbas, was the first woman to become Muslim in Mecca after Khadija, and that the Prophet visited her often. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says her sisters included Maymuna, wife of the Prophet and mother of Ibn Abbas's household ties through the hadith of his night with her, and Lubaba the younger, mother of Khalid ibn al-Walid. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says she bore al-Fadl, Abd Allah, Ubayd Allah, Ma'bad and Qutham, and a poet praised her and her sons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says scholars differ on the first woman after Khadija, giving three views: Umm al-Fadl, Fatima bint al-Khattab, or the Prophet's daughters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### More early women, their husbands and Fatima bint al-Khattab

- He lists Umm Kulthum bint Suhayl ibn Amr, who became Muslim early and migrated to Abyssinia with Abu Sabra ibn Abi Ruhm, though her father only converted later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He lists Fatima bint al-Mujallil, who migrated with Hatib ibn al-Harith, and Fatima bint Alqama, who migrated with Sulayt ibn Amr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He lists Umayra bint al-Sa'di and Layla bint Abi Hathma, who migrated with Amir ibn Rabia, and Sahla bint Suhayl, who migrated with Abu Hudhayfa ibn Utba, killed at Yamama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that Ramla bint Abi Awf became Muslim before the Prophet entered the house of al-Arqam, and that Rayta bint Munabbih and al-Tuwama bint Umayya, daughters of men of Quraysh, converted later or are not marked as early. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Fatima bint al-Khattab and her husband Sa'id ibn Zayd became Muslim before Umar and before the house of al-Arqam, and that her brother Umar's conversion followed his encounter with her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Women tortured in Mecca, the Jahsh family and Hamna

- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Umayma bint Ruqayqa and the sisters al-Nahdiyya, her daughter and Zunayra were early Muslims tortured for their faith, that Abu Bakr bought and freed them against his father's remonstrance, and that when Zunayra lost her sight Quraysh blamed al-Lat and al-Uzza, then called her cure the Prophet's sorcery. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He lists Jariya bint Umar ibn Mu'ammal as tortured in God's cause, and Rayta bint al-Harith, who migrated to Abyssinia with her husband and lost a son there and died on the road back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He lists Baraka bint Yasar and her sister Fukayha, who migrated to Abyssinia with their husbands. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that the Banu Ghanm ibn Dudan, allies of Banu Abd Shams, migrated entirely with their houses locked behind them, including the sisters of Abdullah ibn Jahsh, Zaynab, Habiba and Hamna, Jadhama bint Jandal, Umm Qays bint Mihsan, Amina bint Ruqaysh and Umm Habib bint Nubata. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Nufaysa bint Umayya was the go-between who arranged the Prophet's marriage to Khadija. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that Hamna bint Jahsh, widow of Mus'ab ibn Umayr, was told at Uhud of the deaths of her brother, uncle and husband, grieved most for her husband, later married Talha ibn Ubaydullah and bore Muhammad al-Sajjad and Imran, and received thirty wasqs at Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Ruqayya bint Muhammad in Abyssinia

- He says that he has not yet treated the Prophet's daughters and takes Ruqayya as an example of sacrifice, reporting that she and her husband Uthman are said to be among the first to migrate to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that in the first migration she miscarried, later bore a son named Abd Allah by whom Uthman was called, and migrated to Medina after her husband. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates that she fell ill while the Prophet was preparing for Badr, that Uthman was left to look after her, and that she died in Ramadan seventeen months after the hijra as Zayd arrived with news of victory at Badr while the grave was being levelled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### The cost of early conversion and the migration to Abyssinia

- He holds that these women made sacrifices equal to those of men: one kept her faith when her husband turned Christian, one migrated pregnant, others died in Abyssinia or on the road back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He stresses that the sea voyage from Mecca to Abyssinia and life among a foreign people were far harder than modern travel, and that these women came from noble, comfortable households. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says that in Mecca the main trial was simply to convert, since Muslims were few, whereas in Medina it became fighting and sacrifice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates the report in Sahih Muslim of Amr ibn Abasa meeting the Prophet in Mecca, where the Prophet said only a free man and a slave were with him, and says those who showed their faith early were seven, with the Prophet and Abu Bakr protected by their kin and the rest tortured. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He says Umm al-Fadl became Muslim before her husband al-Abbas, while others converted with their husbands. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He holds that Umm Kulthum bint Uqba's flight alone from Mecca to Medina, chased by her brothers, was firmly established and the occasion of the verse on testing migrant women. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}

### Sumayya, the first martyr, and the hardships of the early community

- He says Sumayya, the first martyr of Islam, was the daughter of Khabbat, not Khayyat on the sounder reading, and that he is not treating her in detail today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He holds that she was an elderly, weak woman who accepted Islam and endured torture with no worldly gain, contrasting the later dates of Khaybar, since the Companions say they were not fully fed on dates until Khaybar was taken. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He relates the account by Utba ibn Ghazwan that he and Sa'd shared one cloak in the early days and that each later became a governor of a province. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
- He summarises the series so far: pre-prophetic period, three sessions on the first revelation, one long session on the first male converts, and this one on women; next he will return to Ibn Hisham with the call and the polytheists' response. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep10}
