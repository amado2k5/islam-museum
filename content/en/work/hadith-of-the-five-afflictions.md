## Overview
A short saying of the Prophet to the emigrants, which Ibn Ishaq transmits in the context of [[event/sariyya-of-abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf-to-dumat-al-jandal|the dispatch of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]]. It comes from Ata ibn Abi Rabah, from a man who heard [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]]: "O company of emigrants, there are five traits which, if they befall you, and I seek God's protection lest you meet them..." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/280}

## The five traits
The saying links each trait to its consequence: the open spread of immorality brings plague and sicknesses unknown to earlier generations; the cheating of measure and balance brings years of drought, hardship of livelihood and the oppression of rulers; withholding alms brings the withholding of rain, so that were it not for the animals, no rain would fall; breaking the covenant of God and His Messenger brings an enemy from outside them who takes some of what is in their hands; and when the leaders do not judge by the Book of God and act tyrannically in what God has sent down, God makes their might a source of conflict among them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/280}

## In the museum
A visitor reads here an early idea of what later writers call "social punishments": plague, famine, injustice, invasion and civil war are presented as consequences of general conduct, not of the sins of individuals. The text is given in sense here; its wording is read in the edition. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/280}

## Notes on the transmission
- The narrator from Ibn Umar is an unnamed man from Basra. Ibn Ishaq transmits it from Ata "from someone I do not suspect". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/279}
- "They were tyrannical" is in two copies, and "they were bewildered" in one copy. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/280}
