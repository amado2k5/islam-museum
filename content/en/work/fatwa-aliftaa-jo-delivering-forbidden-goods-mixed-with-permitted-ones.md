## Summary

A question about delivering forbidden goods mixed with permitted ones to a buyer, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that this is forbidden if the worker knows they are there, that there is no blame if he does not know, and that he is not obliged to inspect the order's contents. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3997}

## The question

The questioner asked about the ruling on delivering forbidden goods to a buyer when they are mixed with other, permitted goods. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3997}

## The answer

The Department answered that Islam forbids any work that assists in what is forbidden, and that delivering forbidden goods is forbidden assistance, quoting [[person/zakariyya-al-ansari|Zakariyya al-Ansari]] in Asna al-Matalib that selling grapes to someone known or thought to make wine of them is forbidden, as is any dealing that leads to sin. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3997} It said there is in general no objection to working as a delivery worker if he does not know an order's contents, and he is not obliged to inspect or ask about them; but if he is certain the order is pork, wine or similar forbidden things, he may not deliver it and should excuse himself. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3997} It concluded that delivering forbidden goods is forbidden, even when mixed with permitted goods, if the worker knows they are there, and that there is no objection if he does not know. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3997}
