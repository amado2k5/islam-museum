## Summary
*Al-Ibana 'an Shari'at al-Firqa al-Najiya wa-Mujanabat al-Firaq al-Madhmuma* ("The Exposition of the Law of the Saved Sect and the Avoidance of the Blameworthy Sects") is a book of creed in the manner of the hadith scholars by the Hanbali Abu Abd Allah [[person/ibn-batta-al-ukbari-ubayd-allah|Ibn Batta al-Ukbari]]. In it he narrates hadiths and reports with his own chains under the headings of doctrine, and answers the sects that departed from them. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 48} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 49}

## Its title
The title appears at the opening and close of every part of the Taymuriyya manuscript and at the head of some parts of the Zahiriyya manuscript. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 31} Biographers call it *al-Ibana al-Kubra* ("the larger Ibana") to distinguish it from the author's short treatise *al-Sharh wa-al-Ibana 'ala Usul al-Sunna wa-al-Diyana*, which came to be called the smaller Ibana. When "the Ibana" of Ibn Batta is named without qualification, the larger one is meant, as in the quotations of Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 32}

## Its attribution
The editor establishes the attribution on four grounds. The first is the connected chain to the author, recorded at the head of the parts, from Abu al-Qasim Ali ibn Ahmad al-Busri by licence from Ibn Batta, then Abu al-Hasan Ibn al-Zaghuni. The second is that scholars quoted it, including Abu Ya'la, his son Abu al-Husayn, Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn al-Qayyim and al-Dhahabi. The third is Ibn al-Jawzi's narration through Ibn Batta. The fourth is the many audition notes recorded on its parts. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 33} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 34} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 38} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 39} The audition notes include a hearing before Ibn al-Zaghuni in Baghdad in Safar 514 AH, hearings in Aleppo in 633 AH and in Jerusalem in 676 AH. They list men, women and children, and one of them names Majd al-Din Abu al-Barakat Abd al-Salam Ibn Taymiyya. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 40} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 41} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 42} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 45} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 47}

## Its divisions
Al-Dhahabi says in *al-'Uluw* that the book fills three large volumes, and elsewhere four; the editor suggests that he saw different copies. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 50} Volume 1, the Book of Faith, has seven parts and answers the [[group/murjia|Murji'a]]. Volume 2, kept in the Taymuriyya library, also has seven parts: the eighth to the eleventh answer the [[group/qadariyya|Qadariyya]], who denied predestination, and the last three answer the [[group/jahmiyya|Jahmiyya]]. Two further surviving parts are in Manchester. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 49}

The editor lists the chapters of the Book of Faith. They cover:
- the reports that prompted the book;
- obedience to the Messenger;
- the division of the community;
- leaving questions that do not concern one;
- warnings against companions who sicken the heart;
- the blame of disputation and quarrels in religion;
- the unbelief of whoever abandons prayer or withholds zakat;
- the signs of hypocrisy and the sins that part a person from faith;
- faith as assent of the heart, affirmation of the tongue and action of the limbs;
- the increase and decrease of faith;
- the exception in faith;
- asking another, "Are you a believer?". {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 50} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 51} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 52}

## Why it was written
In his preface, as the editor presents it, Ibn Batta says he wrote because he saw caprice prevailing and people splitting into factions where the community's affairs had once been united and hearts joined. He also gives a chapter to the reports that moved him to compile the book. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 53} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 54} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 55}

## Method and sources
His first sources are the Qur'an and the hadiths he narrates with his chains, followed by the reports of the Companions, the Successors and their followers. In volume 2 he narrates debates held before kings and princes between Sunnis and others. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 56} The editor notes that Ibn Batta gives the sects' views as they were commonly known, without quoting their own texts. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 57} He often narrates a hadith or report through several chains, and the editor counts about 1,300 texts in volume 1. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 59} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 60}

## Questions in the Book of Faith
- **The definition of faith:** Ibn Batta states that God made knowledge and assent obligatory on the heart, utterance and affirmation on the tongue, and action on the limbs, and that none of these suffices without the others. See [[concept/iman|iman]]. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 760} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 761}
- **The exception in faith:** he holds that it is a mark of people of knowledge to say "I am a believer, God willing", not out of doubt. See [[question/may-one-say-i-am-a-believer-god-willing|the exception in faith]]. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 864}

## Its standing
The editor places it among the narrated books of Sunna in creed. These include the *Sunna* works of Ahmad, his son Abd Allah, Ibn Abi Asim, al-Athram and al-Marwazi, al-Lalaka'i's *Sharh Usul I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna* and al-Baghawi's *Sharh al-Sunna*. He notes that al-Dhahabi quoted from it the creeds of several early imams in *al-'Uluw*. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 48} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 58} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 60}

## The edition in the museum
The Book of Faith, edited with a study by Rida ibn Na'san Mu'ti, Dar al-Raya, Riyadh, 2nd edition 1415 AH / 1994. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 1} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 4} The museum's library also holds two scans of an edition by Adil ibn Abd Allah Al Hamdan (volume 1, 1436 AH), which have not yet been used.
