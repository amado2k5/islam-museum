## Summary

*Islam and the Secular State: Negotiating the Future of Shari'a* is an English book by the Sudanese scholar Abdullahi Ahmed An-Na'im, published by Harvard University Press in Cambridge, Massachusetts, in 2008 in 324 pages. {cite:openlibrary-islam-and-the-secular-state-2008} It opens with an introduction titled "Why Muslims Need a Secular State". Its chapters then treat Islam, the state and politics in historical perspective; constitutionalism, human rights and citizenship; and three cases: state secularism in India, the contradictions of authoritarian secularism in Turkey, and the realities of diversity in Indonesia. It closes with negotiating the future of Shari'a. A bibliography is on pages 295–309. {cite:archive-org-islam-and-the-secular-state-2008}
