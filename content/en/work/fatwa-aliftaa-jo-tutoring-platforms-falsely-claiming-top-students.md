## Summary

A question about tutoring platforms falsely claiming top students studied with them, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that this is forbidden lying and deception, whether it comes from the students or the platform owners, and that the sin is greater when it is paid for. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4180}

## The question

The questioner asked about tutoring platforms that, for commercial and advertising purposes, mislead students into thinking that certain top-scoring students studied with them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4180}

## The answer

The Department answered that a Muslim must hold to truthfulness in word, deed and every circumstance, citing the verse commanding believers to be with the truthful in [[passage/quran-9-119|Surat al-Tawba 9:119]]. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4180} It said that lying and cheating to obtain worldly gain are among the gravest sins, relying on a hadith reported by [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] that whoever cheats is not one of us and on the verse commanding avoidance of false speech in [[passage/quran-22-30|Surat al-Hajj 22:30]], and it counted money taken in return for false statements as forbidden gain (suht) and as consuming people's wealth wrongfully. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4180} It added that platform owners may not attribute to themselves what is not theirs, such as claiming a top student as one of their own when he is not, relying on the hadith of [[person/asma-bint-abi-bakr|Asma bint Abi Bakr]] reported by al-Bukhari that one who boasts of what he was not given is like one wearing two garments of falsehood. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4180} It concluded that lying in media statements, and in every circumstance, is forbidden, whether by students or by platform owners, and that the sin is greater when it is done for payment. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4180}
