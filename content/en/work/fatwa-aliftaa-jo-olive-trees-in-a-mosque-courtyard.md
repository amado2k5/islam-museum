## Summary

A question about olive trees in a mosque's grounds, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the fruit of trees planted for a mosque goes to the mosque's needs, while trees planted for the public may be eaten from by anyone entering the mosque, and that a father who donates the mosque's running costs may not take a return for his donation; the Ministry of Awqaf must be consulted. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4522}

## The question

The questioner said there are olive trees in a mosque's grounds and that his father pays the mosque's electricity, water and other needs, and asked whether his father may take the olive harvest. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4522}

## The answer

The Department answered that fruit trees in a mosque, if planted for the mosque, may have their fruit used only for the mosque's interests; if planted for public use, any Muslim entering the mosque may eat from them; and if the planter's intention is unknown, they count as planted for public use where that is the custom. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4522} It relied on [[work/tuhfat-al-muhtaj|Tuhfat al-Muhtaj]] (6/295) that the fruit of a tree planted for the mosque is not free to take without payment, its value being spent by the imam on the mosque's interests, whereas a tree planted for public use may be eaten from freely, and the imam may uproot a tree if he sees fit. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4522} It said the father is a donor, so he may not take anything in return for his donation to the mosque, and that the Ministry of Awqaf supervises endowment affairs, so it must be consulted along with the religious and administrative instructions governing such matters. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4522}
