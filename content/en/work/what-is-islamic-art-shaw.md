## Summary

*What Is “Islamic” Art? Between Religion and Perception* by Wendy M. K. Shaw explores the perception of the arts, including painting, music and geometry, through the discursive sphere of historical Islam, including the Qur'an, hadith, Sufism, ancient philosophy and poetry. {cite:fu-berlin-shaw-hourani-award-2020} The publisher's description says the book began as an exploration of the image in Islam and ended by exploring how one might set aside concepts like image and art to conceive of perception through Islam. {cite:openlibrary-what-is-islamic-art-2019} It asks what art is if the primary sensory organ is neither the eyes nor the ears, but the heart. {cite:openlibrary-what-is-islamic-art-2019} According to the Freie Universität Berlin release, Shaw puts the experience of reception before the context of production, traces themes through philosophical, religious and literary texts in Arabic, Persian, Turkish and Urdu from the eleventh century to the colonial and postcolonial era, and develops a decolonial approach to the cultures of perception in Islam. {cite:fu-berlin-shaw-hourani-award-2020}

## Publication and editions

Cambridge University Press published it in 2019, in 336 pages. {cite:openlibrary-what-is-islamic-art-2019} It received the Honorable Mention of the 2020 Albert Hourani Book Award, announced at the Middle East Studies Association's annual meeting; it is the second art-historical book so honoured in the award's history. {cite:fu-berlin-shaw-hourani-award-2020} Shaw holds a professorship in the art history of Islamic cultures at the Art History Institute of Freie Universität Berlin. {cite:fu-berlin-shaw-hourani-award-2020}
