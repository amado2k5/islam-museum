## Summary

A question about stipulating that a delivery is free if the delivery worker is late beyond a set time, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that stipulating that the worker loses his whole wage for lateness is forbidden unless he volunteers it himself, while stipulating a known deduction from the wage is permitted. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3990}

## The question

The questioner asked whether he may stipulate to a delivery worker that delivery is free if the goods do not arrive within a set time, and what the ruling is if some workers make this offer themselves to attract customers. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3990}

## The answer

The Department answered that rights must in principle be paid to those owed them, including a hired worker's right, because using a worker without payment is injustice, citing a hadith reported by al-Bukhari about one who hires a worker, takes his full work and does not give him his wage. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3990} It said a delivery worker is a hired worker providing a service, so his right may not be cut by stipulating that he gets no wage if he is late; but stipulating a deduction of part of the wage is permitted, as held by some Hanafis and in the Majalla, quoting [[person/burhan-al-din-al-marghinani|al-Marghinani]] in al-Hidaya that [[person/abu-yusuf-al-qadi|Abu Yusuf]] and Muhammad allowed both conditions where someone says: if you sew it today you get a dirham, and if tomorrow, half a dirham. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3990} It said that if the worker imposes this on himself it is permitted, as a voluntary gift, and noted that some delivery companies impose the condition and so deprive the worker of his due for his effort, which is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3990} It concluded that stipulating that the worker loses his wage for lateness is forbidden unless he volunteers it, and that stipulating a known deduction from the wage for lateness is permitted. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3990}
