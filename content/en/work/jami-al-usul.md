## Summary
*Jami' al-Usul fi Ahadith al-Rasul* ("The Compendium of the Source-Books on the Hadiths of the Messenger") is the work of [[person/ibn-al-athir-al-jazri|Majd al-Din Ibn al-Athir al-Jazari]] (544–606 AH), gathering the hadiths of six books he calls "the source-books" (al-usul): [[work/muwatta-malik|the Muwatta']], [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]], [[work/sahih-muslim|Muslim]], [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Abu Dawud]], [[work/jami-al-tirmidhi|al-Tirmidhi]] and [[work/sunan-al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]]. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 10} Its editor 'Abd al-Qadir al-Arna'ut calls it unique of its kind, with no one following its pattern, and says that whoever owns it can do without the six source-books. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 10-11} The author titled it "the book Jami' al-Usul fi Ahadith al-Rasul". {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 59}

## Three pillars
Ibn al-Athir built the book on three pillars: the first on principles, the second on the aims, the third on the conclusions; the first pillar has five chapters. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 45}
- **Principles:** what moved him to write the book; how it is arranged; the principles and rules of hadith (narrators and modes of receiving, transmitting wording and meaning, criticism and validation of narrators, abrogation, the kinds of sound hadith); and the lives of the six imams. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 46} {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 60} {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 115} {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 131} {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 137} {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 159}
- **Aims:** the hadiths themselves in "books", beginning with the letter hamza and the Book of Faith and Islam. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 179}
- **Conclusions:** including a chapter on the names of all the men mentioned in the book — Companions, Successors and others — in alphabetical order. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 60}

## Why he wrote it: from the Tajrid al-Sihah to the Jami' al-Usul
Ibn al-Athir sorts hadith compilers by aim: some recorded hadith under the names of its narrators, like Ahmad b. Hanbal; some arranged it by topic, like Malik in the Muwatta' and then al-Bukhari and Muslim; some wrote on its rare words, like Abu 'Ubayd and Ibn Qutayba; some added rulings, like al-Khattabi in *Ma'alim al-Sunan*; and some collected hadiths of encouragement, warning and law, like al-Baghawi in [[work/masabih-al-sunna|al-Masabih]]. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 52-54} He holds topical arrangement easier to use, because a seeker knows the subject he wants a hadith for even if he does not know its narrator, and because the hadith's place shows the ruling it is evidence for. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 53}

He then came upon the compilations that combine the source-books, and found that of [[person/razin-ibn-muawiya|Razin]] — the [[work/tajrid-al-sihah|Tajrid al-Sihah]] — the largest and most comprehensive, since it contained all six. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 57} Working through it, he found hadiths placed in chapters where others belonged better, much repetition and still more omission; comparing it with the source-books he had read and heard, he found many hadiths Razin had left out, and others in Razin's book that he could not find in the source-books, because of differing copies and routes; and he saw that Razin had followed al-Bukhari's chapters, keeping some and dropping others. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 57} After hesitation and consultation he resolved to combine the six books himself, working from the source-books rather than from Razin. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 58}

## Method
- **Chains:** he removed the chains of transmission, following his predecessors, since their purpose — establishing the hadith — had already been accomplished by the early scholars; he kept only the Companion who narrates the hadith, or the Successor who narrates from the Companion in the case of a report, unless understanding the text depended on naming a narrator. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 60}
- **Texts:** he kept only the Prophet's hadith and the Companions' reports, and only rarely the sayings of Successors or the views of jurists, following al-Humaydi — unlike Razin, who had included Malik's fiqh and al-Bukhari's chapter headings. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 61}
- **Sources:** for al-Bukhari and Muslim he relied on [[work/al-jam-bayn-al-sahihayn-al-humaydi|al-Jam' bayn al-Sahihayn]] of [[person/muhammad-ibn-fattuh-al-humaydi|al-Humaydi]]; the other four he took from copies he had read and heard and collated with others; he preferred the wording of al-Bukhari and Muslim unless another book added something or explained it; what he found in Razin but not in the source-books he copied as it was, leaving the space for the compiler's name blank in case he should find it. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 61}
- **Chapters:** he built the chapters on the meanings the hadiths convey, not on the compilers' chapters: a hadith with one meaning went to its chapter; one with a dominant meaning to that chapter; one whose meanings were equal to a "Book of Appendices" at the end; and all hadiths on merits to a single "Book of Merits and Virtues". {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 62-63}
- **Alphabetical order:** he ordered the books' titles alphabetically by the first letter of the word, whether radical or not, for the ease of ordinary readers — so "al-I'tisam" and "Ihya' al-Mawat" are under hamza — while keeping topics that belong to one book with it (booty, fay' and misappropriation stay in the Book of Jihad under jim), with cross-references at the end of each letter. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 64-65}
- **Signs:** he wrote the narrator of each hadith in the margin and marked it in red with the sign of whichever of the six compilers recorded it. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 66}

## His view of the history of hadith
Ibn al-Athir holds that the Companions and Successors relied first on memory; as Islam spread, the Companions dispersed and exactness declined, scholars needed to write hadith down, and Ibn Jurayj, Malik and others compiled it — the first book written in Islam being, by different accounts, Ibn Jurayj's, Malik's Muwatta', or the work of al-Rabi' b. Subayh in Basra as the first to compile by chapters. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 49-50} He sees the summit of the discipline in al-Bukhari, Muslim and their contemporaries, after which it declined down to his own time. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 51} He holds that knowing mass-transmitted and single reports, abrogating and abrogated, is the jurist's task, while the hadith scholar's task is to transmit what he heard as he heard it. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 48}

## Manuscripts and editions
- For the first edition al-Arna'ut used manuscripts in the Zahiriyya library in Damascus, the first a complete copy of 875 folios written by Adam b. Muhammad between Muharram 772 and Muharram 774 AH and endowed by As'ad Pasha al-'Azm to his father's madrasa. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 12-13} Among them is a volume in the author's own hand, completed in 586 AH — twenty years before his death — bearing certificates of scholars who read it with him. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 14}
- He notes an earlier printing by Matba'at al-Sunna al-Muhammadiyya in Egypt, in twelve volumes edited by 'Abd al-Majid Salim in 1368 AH/1949 CE, incomplete by about a fifth of the book. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 15}
- The editor discussed every hadith's soundness or weakness, since the author did not; the gradings in the notes are the editor's, not the author's. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 15-16}
- The first edition appeared in 1389 AH/1969 CE; the editor died before the second was complete, and his assistants 'Adnan 'Abd Rabbih, Muhammad Adib al-Jadir and Ma'mun al-Saghirji finished it, collating a manuscript from the Chester Beatty Library in London, adding references to Ahmad's Musnad and Ibn Majah's Sunan, including the Tatimma and compiling an index of rare words comparable to the author's own *al-Nihaya*. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 5-7} The edition used here is the third, Dar Ibn Kathir, 1437 AH/2016 CE. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 4}
