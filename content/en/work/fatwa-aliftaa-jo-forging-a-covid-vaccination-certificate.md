## Summary

A question about forging a COVID-19 vaccination certificate, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that forging a vaccination certificate or COVID-19 test is forbidden lying, cheating and forgery; whoever issues the forged certificate shares in the sin, and the prohibition is greater if a bribe is paid to obtain it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3652}

## The question

The questioner asked about forging a certificate showing that one has received a dose of the COVID-19 vaccine. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3652}

## The answer

The Department answered that a Muslim is bound to truthfulness in word and deed whatever the circumstances, citing [[passage/quran-9-119|Surat al-Tawba 9:119]], and that truthfulness includes presenting genuine medical tests and certificates that honestly show one's state of health, among them vaccination certificates and tests for the virus. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3652} It said that producing a forged certificate showing that the holder has been vaccinated, or a forged test showing that he is not infected, is lying, cheating and forgery, which God has forbidden, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] whose meaning is that false testimony is among the gravest of major sins. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3652} It added that forgery in such medical matters has serious consequences for society and can undo much of the work of the responsible authorities; whoever issues the forged certificate aids and shares in the sin, and the prohibition is greater if a bribe is involved, citing [[passage/quran-5-2|Surat al-Ma'ida 5:2]]. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3652} It concluded that everyone must be truthful in presenting medical certificates and tests and avoid forgery, lying and trickery, especially since the safety of the whole society is at stake. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3652}

## About this record

Jordan's General Iftaa Department published this fatwa on aliftaa.jo as no. 3652, dated 24 October 2021. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3652} The museum records this text as a historical document attributed to the body or scholar that issued it; it issues no ruling of its own.
