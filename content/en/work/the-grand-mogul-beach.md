## Summary
The Grand Mogul is the catalogue of an exhibition shown at the Sterling and Francine Clark Art Institute (September–November 1978), then at the Walters Art Gallery, Baltimore, the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, and Asia House Gallery, New York, until June 1979, written by [[person/milo-cleveland-beach|Milo Cleveland Beach]]. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 3} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 4} It focuses on the central achievement of the [[concept/mughal-painting|Mughal school]] — the exploration of naturalism in the early seventeenth century — drawing entirely on American collections. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 8}

## Structure
An introduction on the patrons, from [[person/babur-mughal|Babur]] to [[person/aurangzeb-mughal|Aurangzeb]], the characteristics of Mughal painting, and painting under [[person/jahangir-mughal|Jahangir]] and [[person/shah-jahan-mughal|Shah Jahan]] is followed by a catalogue of manuscripts and albums — the Allahabad manuscripts, the Akbar-nama of 1604, the [[practice/mughal-muraqqa-album|albums]] of Jahangir, the [[work/tuzuk-i-jahangiri|Jahangir-nama]], the Gulistan of Sa'di, the albums of Shah Jahan and the [[work/padshahnama-lahori|Padshah-nama]] — and then by sixteen painters in alphabetical order, among them [[person/abu-al-hasan-nadir-al-zaman|Abu'l Hasan]], [[person/ustad-mansur|Mansur]], [[person/govardhan-painter|Govardhan]] and [[person/payag-painter|Payag]], each with "further major attributions" the author trusts and "other attributions" he trusts less. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 5} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 28}

## Argument
For the author, painting under Jahangir was naturalistic, absorbed in the individual and close observation, while painting under Shah Jahan used perfected technique to reassert deeper Indian attitudes — the culmination of the style rather than its decline — even though Mughal painting was in decline in that Shah Jahan was not an involved patron. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 25} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 26}
