## Summary

A question on a man's bequest that his undutiful son be denied inheritance, which Egypt's Dar al-Ifta answered is invalid. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032}

## The question

The questioner said a man had died leaving a son who had been undutiful to him, and had made a bequest that the son be denied inheritance. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032} He asked what the ruling on the bequest is, and whether the son inherits from his father's estate. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032}

## The answer

Dar al-Ifta answered that undutifulness to parents is forbidden and a major sin, and that honouring them is an obligation on children. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032} It held, however, that undutifulness does not cancel rights, among them inheritance, which passes into the heir's ownership by force of law without the choice of heir or deceased. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032} The bequest to disinherit the son is therefore invalid in its view, and he takes his share unless one of the recognised impediments to inheritance applies. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032} It advised the son to make up for the duty he failed in by seeking forgiveness for his father, giving charity on his behalf and keeping his ties of kinship. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032}

## About this record

Egypt's Dar al-Ifta published this fatwa on its site as no. 9032, dated 10 August 2026, in its inheritance category, among the fatwas of Grand Mufti [[person/nazir-ayyad|Nazir Ayyad]]. {cite:dar-alifta fatwa 9032} The museum records this answer as a historical text attributed to its author; it issues no ruling of its own.
