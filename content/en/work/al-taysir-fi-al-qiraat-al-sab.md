## Summary
Kitab al-Taysir fi al-Qira'at al-Sab' by Imam [[person/abu-amr-al-dani|Abu Amr Uthman ibn Sa'id al-Dani]] (371-444 AH) is a work on the seven [[concept/qiraat|Qur'an readings]], which its editor Otto Pretzl calls "the authority in this noble science". {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 5} {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 10}

## Structure
The contents of the Pretzl edition show that the book opens with the author's introduction, a chapter on the names of the readers and their transmitters, their lineages, cities, kunyas and death dates, a chapter on the men and one on the chains, then the isti'adha, the basmala and al-Fatiha. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 7} Then come the chapters of general principles: Abu Amr's major assimilation, the pronoun ha', lengthening and shortening, two hamzas in one word and in two, the single hamza, transferring the hamza's vowel, Hamza's and Hisham's pausing on the hamza, clear pronunciation and assimilation, fath and imala, al-Kisa'i's pausing on the feminine ending, Warsh's treatment of ra', the lam's, pausing at word ends and on the written form, the possessive ya's and the ya's omitted in the script; then the particular words. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 7} {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 8} Among the readers it treats are [[person/nafi-al-madani|Nafi']], [[person/abu-amr-ibn-al-ala|Abu Amr]], [[person/hamza-al-zayyat|Hamza]] and [[person/al-kisai|al-Kisa'i]]. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 7} {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 8}

## The editor's aim
In his preface the editor says that the science of reading is the earliest of the Islamic sciences, and that recording it - studying the articulation of letters and fixing them - was among the strongest drivers of the community's care for the fine points of Arabic. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 9} He says he chose to publish al-Taysir to make the best-known readings accessible, and would follow it with al-Muqni' on the script of the regional copies together with the book on dotting, noting in a footnote the projects of Jeffery and Bergsträsser to collect the readings. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 10}

## Transmission of the book
Citing Ibn al-Jazari, the editor says al-Dani relied in al-Taysir for Warsh's reading on his teacher Abu al-Qasim Khalaf ibn Ibrahim ibn Khaqan of Egypt; that among those who heard al-Taysir from him was Abu al-Hasan Yahya ibn Ibrahim Ibn al-Bayyaz of Murcia, that Abu Abd Allah Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Khawlani transmitted it from him by licence, and that Abu al-Qasim Ahmad ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Hamza of Murcia, the last to transmit from him, also did so by licence. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 11} {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 12} In Ibn al-Jazari's ordering, as the editor reports it, it comes second among al-Dani's books after Jami' al-Bayan. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p. 13}

## The edition in the museum
