## Summary

A question on buying a sacrifice or aqiqa from private companies and appointing them to slaughter and distribute it, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that this is permitted if the company owns the animal at the time of contract, and otherwise the contract must be drawn up as a forward sale (salam) with set specifications, or as a promise followed by a sale once the company owns it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3855}

## The question

The questioner asked about buying a sacrifice or aqiqa through private companies and appointing them to slaughter and distribute it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3855}

## The answer

The Department answered that if the customer pays the full price at contract and the company owns the animal at that time, there is no objection, but if the company does not own it, the sale is not allowed because of the prohibition on selling what one does not own. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3855} It said the contract may be drawn up as a salam, with delivery deferred and price paid in advance, the company specifying in the contract the animal's kind, sex, age, colour, weight and location; it quoted [[person/al-khatib-al-shirbini|al-Khatib al-Shirbini]]'s Mughni al-Muhtaj that these specifications are required in a salam for livestock. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3855} Alternatively the parties may agree on an initial promise, and once the company owns the animal it sells it to the customer; the agency to slaughter and distribute is then contracted on the basis of the customer's ownership. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3855}
