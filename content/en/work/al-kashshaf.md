## Overview
"Al-Kashshaf an Haqa'iq al-Tanzil" (The Unveiler of the Realities of the Revelation), a Qur'anic commentary by [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]]. According to Ibn al-Imad in "Shadharat al-Dhahab", no commentary like it had been written before. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 599-600}

## Preface
Ibn al-Imad reports that when the author first composed the preface, he began it with the words "Praise be to God, who created the Qur'an". He was told that people would shun the book in that form, so he changed it to "who made the Qur'an", taking "made" to mean "created". Ibn al-Imad says he has seen in many copies "who sent down the Qur'an", and that this was the copyists' correction, not the author's. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 599-600}

## Why and how quickly it was written
In his preface, as Khalil Ma'mun Shiha quotes it, al-Zamakhshari says that his brethren of his school, who combined Arabic with the principles of religion, would admire what he brought out of a verse whenever they asked him about one, and then proposed that he dictate to them "the unveiling of the realities of revelation and the best sayings on the ways of interpretation"; he excused himself, then dictated a discussion of the opening letters and part of the realities of Surat al-Baqara. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.12} When he resolved to return to residence by God's House in Mecca, he found in every town he passed people eager for that dictation, and at Mecca the Sharif Abu al-Hasan ibn Hamza ibn Wahhas the most eager of all; so he took a shorter way than the first and finished it in about the length of Abu Bakr's caliphate, though he had reckoned it would take more than thirty years. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.12} He finished it on the morning of Monday 2 Rabi' II 528 AH; see [[event/al-zamakhshari-kashshaf-528|the writing of al-Kashshaf]]. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.12}

## Its standing among scholars
The editor notes that al-Zamakhshari's biographers agree in attributing al-Kashshaf to him, from al-Sam'ani's *al-Ansab* to Ibn Khallikan, al-Dhahabi, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Khaldun, and that al-Zamakhshari himself praised it: "Commentaries in this world are beyond counting, yet none of them, by my life, is like my Kashshaf." {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.11} He holds it among the best references for rhetoric despite its Mu'tazili bent, its value showing above all in the sciences of ma'ani and bayan, and says most later commentaries drew on it. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.12} He quotes the praise of Ibn Khaldun and Taj al-Din al-Subki, and Ibn al-Munayyir's acknowledgement, for all his severity, of al-Zamakhshari's rhetorical insight. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.13}

## Its Mu'tazilism
The editor gives a section to "al-Zamakhshari's championing of his Mu'tazili creed in al-Kashshaf", with examples: his figurative readings of verses that conflict with his school, his denial of the reality of magic in explaining Surat al-Falaq, his doctrine of free will and the creation of acts, and his reading of "those endowed with knowledge" as "the scholars of justice and divine unity". {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.13} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.14} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.15} He says al-Zamakhshari calls his Sunni opponents determinists and literalists (hashwiyya) — the description and judgement are the editor's. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.15} He notes that with Israelite reports al-Zamakhshari usually prefaces them with "it is reported", signalling weakness, or leaves their truth to God, or notes their grade. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.15} Scholars' approaches to it are set out under [[question/how-did-sunni-scholars-read-al-kashshaf|How did Sunni scholars treat al-Kashshaf?]].

## Works written on it
Many glosses were written on al-Kashshaf, among them: Ibn al-Munayyir the Maliki's *al-Intisaf*, answering its Mu'tazilism; the glosses of Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi; al-Tibi's, in six large volumes; *al-Kashf* by Umar ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Qazwini; two glosses by Yahya ibn Qasim al-Alawi; Ibn Hisham's abridgement of al-Intisaf; the gloss of Qutb al-Din al-Razi al-Tahtani; al-Babarti's commentary; al-Taftazani's gloss, which reached Surat al-Fath; al-Bulqini's *al-Kashshaf 'ala al-Kashshaf*; the gloss of al-Sayyid al-Sharif al-Jurjani; and al-Firuzabadi's. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.16} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.17} The editor quotes verses by Shaykh Haydar al-Harawi, one of its annotators, satirising its bending of words to its doctrine. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.16}

## The edition used
The museum used Dar al-Ma'rifa's (Beirut) one-volume printing prepared by Khalil Ma'mun Shiha, carrying the comments of Ibn al-Munayyir's *al-Intisaf*; 3rd edition 1430 AH / 2009. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.2} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.3}

## Al-Kashshaf and al-Intisaf in two places
[[person/al-zamakhshari|Al-Zamakhshari]]'s way of reading verses on [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazili]] principles through figurative sense and question-and-answer, and [[person/ibn-al-munayyir|Ibn al-Munayyir]]'s replies in the footnotes, show clearly in two places: the sealing of hearts in [[passage/quran-2-7|al-Baqara 7]], where al-Zamakhshari makes the seal figurative to keep God clear of doing evil and Ibn al-Munayyir answers that nothing comes to be except by God's power {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.41} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.42} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.43}; and Moses' request to see God in [[passage/quran-7-143|al-A'raf 143]], where al-Zamakhshari holds the vision incompatible with God's attributes and Ibn al-Munayyir holds it possible {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.385}. See [[question/who-seals-the-hearts-in-al-baqara-7|Who seals the hearts?]] and [[question/does-lan-tarani-deny-that-god-can-be-seen|Does 'You will not see Me' deny the vision?]].
