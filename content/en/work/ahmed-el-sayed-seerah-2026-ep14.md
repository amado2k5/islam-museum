## Summary

The speaker presents the biography of Abu Salama al-Makhzumi from the book on the Badr veterans, then a survey of the Makhzum clan and the line of al-Mughira from a Quraysh genealogy book. He reflects on how Islam changed Arab naming and returns to Ibn Hisham's account of al-Walid's council at the pilgrimage season, the reasons for Quraysh's rejection, and al-Suhayli's linguistic notes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Opening and the biographies of the Badr veterans

- He says this is the fourteenth session and that each session begins with one biography from the book on the Badr veterans by Sulayman al-Awda. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that the series pays special attention to the Companions who fought at Badr and to the earliest converts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He mentions a view held by many scholars that everyone who took the pledge of al-Ridwan counts among the earliest converts, and notes the other known views, namely those who prayed toward both directions of prayer and the Badr veterans. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad: kinship, emigration and the separation from Umm Salama

- He relates from the book that Abu Salama of the Makhzum clan was a cousin of the Prophet through an aunt and also his foster brother. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that he was among the earliest converts, accepted Islam before the Prophet entered the house of al-Arqam, and is said to have been the first to emigrate to Abyssinia, together with Umm Salama; their children were born there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates the account, in Umm Salama's own telling, that her family kept her and the family of Abu Salama pulled the child so hard his arm was dislocated, so that husband, wife and son were parted for about a year before her clan relented and let her follow. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Sa'd that Abu Salama was the first to reach Medina, around the tenth of Muharram, and that the whole emigration took about two months. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Uthman ibn Talha escorted Umm Salama to Medina and that she spoke of his generosity. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### The change of Arab names after Islam

- He proposes that names are historical evidence in their own right: genealogies are an independent science passed down within tribes, not dependent on chains of transmission like hadith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He holds that the family trees show names such as Abd al-Uzza, Abd Allat and Abd Shams dying out, while Abd al-Rahman, Abd Allah, Muhammad and Ahmad become very common, and that the names of Companions and their wives spread widely too. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He says this spread reached Turkic lands, Azerbaijan, Armenia, the Syrian frontier towns, North Africa and al-Andalus within about a century, and among peoples regardless of their religious practice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He suggests checking this in biographical dictionaries and indexes, noting that the name Muhammad was rare among the Arabs before Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He cites as supporting evidence a recently found inscription near Mahd al-Dhahab mentioning Umar ibn al-Khattab, and the astronomical dating of the solar eclipse that matches the death of the Prophet's son Ibrahim in the Medinan period. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Abu Salama at Uhud, the expedition to Qatan and his death

- He relates that Abu Salama fought at Badr and Uhud, was wounded in the arm at Uhud, and recovered for a time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that the Prophet sent him in Muharram, thirty-five months after the Hijra, on a raid against Banu Asad at Qatan, the same raid the previous session mentioned for Abu Sabra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that the wound reopened after his return and he died in Jumada al-Akhira, was washed, carried into Medina and buried there; he places the death around four years after Uhud as the sounder view. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Umm Salama later said the supplication taught by the Prophet for calamity, doubted who could be better than Abu Salama, and was then given the Prophet as her husband in his place. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Genealogy of Banu Makhzum

- He relates, following the book on Quraysh genealogies, that Makhzum is a branch descending from Murra ibn Ka'b, who is the Prophet's sixth grandfather, so the two lines meet there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Makhzum's line ran through Abd Allah ibn Umar to al-Mughira, grandfather of Abu Jahl and of Khalid ibn al-Walid, and that the clan held command of the cavalry banner and one of the ten Qurayshi duties at the sanctuary. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He explains that opponents from the Abd Shams or Nawfal branches would name Hashim when attacking the Prophet, whereas a Makhzumi such as Abu Jahl speaks of Abd Manaf as a whole, because he stands outside it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that the clan's houses lay on the southern side of the Sacred Mosque near Ajyad, that a gate was named after them, and that the house of al-Arqam, near al-Safa, belonged to the clan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He describes how the Prophet's house by al-Marwa and Abu Bakr's house lay at opposite ends of Mecca, to help the listener picture the city. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### House of al-Arqam and the lecturer's reference tiers

- He relates from al-Salihi that the Prophet worshipped in secret in the house of al-Arqam until about forty men had joined, the last being Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He says al-Arqam was a Makhzumi, an early convert and Badr veteran, aged about sixteen or seventeen, and notes that many early converts such as al-Zubayr, Sa'd and Talha were also teenagers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He explains that his school sorts references into set texts, main references such as al-Rawd al-Unuf and al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, and secondary references such as Subul al-Huda wa-l-Rashad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Branches of Makhzum: Banu Rabi'a and Banu al-Mughira

- He relates that the sons of al-Mughira include Abu Hudhayfa, Abu Rabi'a, Abu Umayya, Hashim, Hisham and al-Walid. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Abu Hudhayfa was one of the four leaders of Quraysh, that Abu Rabi'a clothed the Kaaba one year alone and Quraysh the next, and that his descendant Abd Allah ibn Abi Rabi'a was made governor of Yemen by Umar, and his grandson was the poet Umar ibn Abi Rabi'a. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Abu Umayya, nicknamed the provision-giver of travellers, was married to the Prophet's aunt Atika, and that his children included Abd Allah, Zuhayr, al-Muhajir, who was sent to the Himyarite king and later governed Yemen, and Umm Salama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Hanatma, mother of Umar ibn al-Khattab, descended from Hashim ibn al-Mughira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Hisham, father of Abu Jahl, led Makhzum in the Fijar war, and that his son al-Harith accepted Islam at the conquest, was protected by Umm Hani, and later left for Syria and died in the plague; the Prophet gave Abu Jahl his nickname in place of Abu al-Hakam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that al-Harith's sons included Abd al-Rahman and Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Rahman, known for his devotion and learning. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### The line of al-Walid ibn al-Mughira

- He relates that al-Walid was a leader of Quraysh, father of Khalid and of al-Walid ibn al-Walid, that he fed pilgrims and clothed the Kaaba alone in alternate years, and built one corner when Quraysh restored it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that a descendant of his brother's line, Hisham ibn Isma'il, governed Medina under Abd al-Malik, and that a later palace in the Urwa district of Medina is mentioned by al-Samhudi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that al-Walid ibn al-Walid was captured at Badr and ransomed for four thousand dirhams, accepted Islam only after release so that none would think he feared captivity, was prayed for by the Prophet in the supplication, then escaped and rejoined him after al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that his letter to his brother Khalid was a cause of Khalid's conversion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Al-Walid's council at the pilgrimage season

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham that al-Walid gathered a group of Quraysh before the season and urged them to agree on one description of the Prophet to give the arriving Arab delegations. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that each proposed label was rejected by al-Walid in turn, and that he settled on sorcery as the nearest, arguing it divides a man from his family; the group then warned visitors along the roads. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that the Qur'an passage of al-Muddaththir about a man made rich and arrogant is reported to have been revealed about al-Walid, with another verse about those who split the Qur'an into parts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He holds that the plan spread the Prophet's name across Arabia, and that it reveals Quraysh's concern with a unified public message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Why Quraysh rejected the message: lineage and the case of Abu Talib

- He holds that the central standard for Quraysh was ancestors, tribe and lineage, and that the message declared their beliefs and even their names mistaken. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates, from the account in al-Bukhari, that at Abu Talib's death Abu Jahl and another man pressed him to stay on the religion of Abd al-Muttalib, and he refused to say the declaration of faith though the Prophet urged him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that the Prophet once offered the chiefs a single statement that would bring them the Arabs and non-Arabs, and they objected to making the many gods one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He holds that the people took idols to be sacred, not mere stones, and compares this to modern idols of nationhood, persons and laws. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Early moral content of the message

- He holds that alongside monotheism the Prophet called early on to noble character, above all kinship ties, giving and truthfulness, and chastity. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He lists early Meccan chapters that, in his reading, stress generosity and care for the poor, among them al-Layl, al-Ma'un, al-Duha, al-Fajr and al-Muddaththir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates a report in which Amr ibn Abasa asks the Prophet what he was sent with and is told kinship ties, breaking idols and worship of God alone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He observes that legal rulings such as the ban on wine came late, even in Medina, after Uhud, and suggests the early emphasis on giving concerned the purification of the self rather than social welfare. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Dhimad al-Azdi and Quraysh's media message

- He relates the story of Dhimad al-Azdi, who came to Mecca meaning to cure the Prophet of madness, heard a short address from him, asked for it to be repeated three times, and then said he had never heard such words and accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He holds that this shows Quraysh's description of the Prophet was spread among visitors deliberately, as a recurring habit of unjust rulers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Abu Bakr's three great roles in the Meccan period

- He compares Abu Bakr's role to a star beside the full moon, the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He counts three main works of Abu Bakr in Mecca: supporting and believing the Prophet including with his money, inviting early converts to Islam, and buying and freeing enslaved Muslims who were being tortured. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates that Abu Bakr's father reproached him for freeing weak slaves, and that many exegetes connect the chapter of al-Layl with Abu Bakr's giving. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}

### Al-Suhayli's linguistic notes on the passage

- He relates from al-Suhayli, in al-Rawd al-Unuf, explanations of the terms hazaj, rajaz, zamzama, adhq against ghadaq and the verb for splitting, noting that Ibn Ishaq's wording is the more eloquent. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He relates al-Suhayli's reading that the Qur'anic term for the hard ascent is a slope in hell, with the traditional exegesis that he says lacks a firm raised chain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
- He recommends the study of Arabic lexicons, naming al-Nihaya by Ibn al-Athir, Gharib al-Hadith by Abu Ubayd, Tahdhib al-Asma' wa-l-Lughat by al-Nawawi, Lisan al-Arab and Ibn Faris's Maqayis al-Lugha. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep14}
