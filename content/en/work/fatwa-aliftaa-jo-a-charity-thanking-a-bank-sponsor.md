## Summary

A question about a charity accepting support from a commercial bank in return for thanking it in posts, videos and advertisements, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that donations from interest-based banks may be accepted if no promotion is required, that thanks given orally or by letter are fine, and that thanks through advertisements and posts are forbidden because they amount to promotion. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3995}

## The question

A charity that helps the poor and needy asked whether it may receive support from a commercial bank in return for thanking the bank by mentioning it in its videos, posts and advertisements, as appreciation rather than advertising. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3995}

## The answer

The Department answered that promoting and advertising banks and financial institutions that deal in usury is forbidden, because it is assisting in what is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3995} It said there is no objection to dealing with someone whose wealth is mixed between lawful and unlawful, or even mostly unlawful, so his gift or donation may be accepted unless the specific money given is known to be unlawful, provided this is not tied to promoting the donor; it quoted [[person/ibn-hajar-al-haytami|Ibn Hajar al-Haytami]]'s answer in al-Fatawa al-Fiqhiyya al-Kubra that dealing with someone most of whose wealth is unlawful is disliked unless the unlawful item itself is known. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3995} It distinguished ways of thanking a supporting bank: oral thanks to its management, or a letter of thanks, are unobjectionable; thanks published in advertisements and videos are not permitted, because they amount to promotion. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3995} It concluded that donations from interest-based banks may be accepted if they do not require promotion, but are forbidden if given in return for promotional work, such as thanks in posts and advertisements. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3995}
