# Lisan al-Arab

## Summary

Lisan al-Arab ("The Tongue of the Arabs") is a large dictionary of the Arabic language by Ibn Manzur (Muhammad ibn Mukarram, d. 711 AH). Ibn Hajar's biographical notice in al-Durar al-Kamina says that he gathered in it the Tahdhib, the Muhkam, the Sihah, the Jamhara, the Nihaya and the margins of the Sihah, and arranged it in the order of the Sihah {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 4}. This draft reads only the front matter of the Dar Sadir edition. The dictionary entries and the indexes were not read.

## The edition read

The copy is volume one of the Dar Sadir edition in Beirut. The title page gives no date of printing in the OCR {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 1}. The publishers' note says that the edition is a new one of the first edition, which the Khedive Muhammad Tawfiq's government funded and which was printed at the Bulaq press. Specialists in Arabic lexicography were consulted. The corrections of the first edition's corrector, printed in its margins, were kept word for word. The publishers planned to issue the work in parts, with an index of poets and a supplement of modern terms approved by Arab language academies {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 3}. Some had advised changing the book's arrangement; the publishers kept it, because ordering entries by their last root letter helps a poet find a rhyme. They added separators between entries to make searching easier {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 3}.

## The author

Ibn Hajar records that the author was Muhammad ibn Mukarram ibn Ali ibn Ahmad al-Ansari al-Ifriqi, later of Cairo, who traced his descent to Ruwayfi ibn Thabit al-Ansari. He was born in Muharram 630 AH, heard hadith from several teachers, was fond of abridging long books of adab, served in the chancery (diwan al-insha) and was judge of Tripoli. He died in Sha'ban 711 AH {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 4}. Ibn Hajar also records that he had a Shi'i inclination without rejection (rafd); the draft attributes this to Ibn Hajar's notice and does not state it as its own judgement {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 4}. Al-Suyuti's notice in Bughyat al-Wu'at describes him as learned in grammar, lexicography, history and writing, and says that he abridged about a quarter of the History of Damascus {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 4}.

The preface of the first edition gives his birth as 690. A footnote on the same page corrects it to 630, and gives the death as 711, citing al-Safadi's al-Wafi, Ibn Hajar's al-Durar and al-Suyuti's al-Bughya {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 5}.

## The preface of the first edition

The preface reproduced in this edition is signed by Ahmad Faris al-Shidyaq, editor of al-Jawa'ib, and dated 17 Rajab 1300 AH. It presents the dictionary as a compilation of al-Jawhari's Sihah with Ibn Barri's margins, al-Azhari's Tahdhib, Ibn Sida's Muhkam, Ibn Duraid's Jamhara and Ibn al-Athir's Nihaya {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 6}. It thanks Husayn Husni Bey, supervisor of the Bulaq press, and Husayn Effendi Ali al-Deek, who bore the cost of the printing, and says that the editors gathered sound manuscripts from several places {cite:lisan-al-arab-dar-sadir p. 6}.
