## Summary

The lecturer surveys the events of the fifth and sixth years after the Hijra: the prohibition of wine, the legislation of the hijab and the other laws of the early Medinan years, and the growing plotting of the hypocrites. He tells the dispute on the road of the Banu al-Mustaliq expedition, Abdullah ibn Ubayy's words, Zayd ibn Arqam's report and sura al-Munafiqun, links the slander against Aisha to the same journey, and begins the Hudaybiyya story with the lesson of the fourteen hundred. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### The prohibition of wine

- He says that the prohibition of wine was a great event in the biography, because for the Arabs wine was not only a drink but a culture bound up with horsemanship, love poetry, raiding and war. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that the old poems show this, since most of them praise wine, its vessels, its colour and its drinking. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says wine was not forbidden at the start of Islam, in Mecca or in early Medina, and that after Uhud some Companions worried about brothers killed there who had drunk it, so a verse came down about them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He relates that Anas described wine running in the lanes of Medina after people emptied their large storage jars, and he draws the lesson of submission to God and His messenger that these believers had reached. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says the prohibition came after Uhud, in the fourth year, not in the fifth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### Hijab, the marriage to Zaynab and the legislation of these years

- He says the legislation of the hijab came down with the Prophet's marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says the date of that marriage is famously disputed: the usual view places it after the Confederates, since the verses are in sura al-Ahzab, but he says a very strong view puts it at the end of the fourth year, and that both sides have evidence. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He counts fasting, zakat and jihad among the laws given between the second and fifth years, and calls those years full of legislation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that this legislation is reflected in the Medinan suras of the Qur'an, which matters for jurisprudence and for reflection on the Book. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The rise of the hypocrites after Badr

- He says that by the fifth to sixth year the Jews were finished with the fall of Banu Qurayza, apart from those now farther away at Khaybar, so the plotting that rose was that of the hypocrites. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that strong hypocrisy began after Badr, when Islam's word was raised and some joined the Prophet's banner while hiding disbelief, and that there were no hypocrites in the battle of Badr itself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says their plotting showed at Uhud when they withdrew with a third of the army, and he notes that the Qur'an speaks of them more in Al Imran, about Uhud, than in al-Anfal, about Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that after Uhud their plotting rose clearly and showed more experience and skill. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### The call to tribes on the road and Abdullah ibn Ubayy

- He places the expedition of Banu al-Mustaliq, also called al-Muraysi, at the end of the fifth year or the start of the sixth, and says two events about the hypocrites belong to it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He relates that on the road a Muhajir struck an Ansari, each called on his own group, and the Prophet told them to leave the call since it stank. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that the Prophet said this although the names are legitimate and mentioned in the Qur'an, because the call drew people into division, and he draws two lessons: names inside the Muslim ranks are not forbidden when nobody rallies around them in partisanship, and names that call to splitting are forbidden and must be fought. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He relates that Abdullah ibn Ubayy, whom he calls a hypocrite of the greater kind, said that on returning to Medina the mightier would drive out the weaker, meaning the Ansar would expel the Prophet and the Muhajirun. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says Umar asked to strike the man's neck and the Prophet refused so that people would not say Muhammad kills his companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### Weighing benefits and harms

- He holds that the Prophet's reply lays the foundation of the jurisprudence of weighing benefits against harms: killing the man had a benefit, but a greater harm followed, so the Prophet preferred to avert the harm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that this shows the Prophet took account of people's feelings and states, although he had revelation and proof. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that a caller and reformer must do the same, so long as he does not make lawful what is forbidden or the reverse, and that of two competing benefits the higher is preferred, and of two harms the lesser may be accepted to avert the greater. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### Zayd ibn Arqam and sura al-Munafiqun

- He says Zayd ibn Arqam was among the youngest Companions, and that he heard Abdullah ibn Ubayy's words and reported them to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He relates that the Prophet called Abdullah ibn Ubayy, who swore he had said nothing, and the Prophet took his outward statement and left what Zayd said, as the Qur'an says of the hypocrites using oaths as a shield. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He relates, from Zayd's own account, that his uncle blamed him, that he fell into a grief like no one's, and that on the road the Prophet pulled his ear and smiled at him, which Zayd would not trade for eternal life in this world. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He relates that Abu Bakr and then Umar met Zayd and gave him good news, and the next morning the Prophet recited sura al-Munafiqun, which came down about this event and confirmed Zayd. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He relates a report in al-Bukhari that the Prophet said of Zayd that God had made good his ear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that naming a sura after the hypocrites shows their plotting was at a very high point at that time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### The slander against Aisha and its lessons

- He says the slander against Aisha happened on the same expedition, and that Abdullah ibn Ubayy, still full of anger, was the one who stirred it up. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says he will not retell the well-known story but names lessons: hypocrites never tire of making harm and weakening the Muslims, and he calls spreading rumours and lies, dividing the ranks and inciting unbelievers a specialism that always exists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that the Qur'an speaks at length of the hypocrites so that reformers know their ways, and that workers for the faith who are unaware of them are in great danger. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says the presence of hypocrites in the ranks also affected some people of merit, since some Companions repeated the rumours, and the Prophet applied the penalty for slander to those who spoke. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that a Muslim must watch his sources and from whom he hears, and that hypocrites today are, in his view, more skilled at producing rumours aimed at reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### The vision at the start of Hudaybiyya

- He says Hudaybiyya took place in the sixth year after the Hijra and began with a vision of the Prophet, in which he and his Companions circled the House in safety, with heads shaved or trimmed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says the Companions, kept from the Kaaba for years and knowing a prophet's vision is revelation, were eager, and about fourteen hundred set out from Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says he will only begin the story today, calling it very long and one of the longest reports in al-Bukhari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}

### Quality over number: the fourteen hundred

- He holds that fourteen hundred was very few after about nineteen years of the call, thirteen in Mecca and six in Medina, and that these men were the essence of Islam, the bearers of the faith raised by the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He says that within two years, after the conquest of Mecca, the Arabian Peninsula had entered Islam, and he notes a report that the Farewell Pilgrimage numbered about a hundred thousand or more. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that after the Prophet's death many of those who had joined quickly, most of them in his view, left Islam, and that the fourteen hundred were the ones who held firm, fought the apostates and brought people back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that those who joined after the conquest, drawn in by gifts, were waiting for the stronger side, and that only Mecca, Medina, Ta'if and Juwatha in Bahrain remained Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
- He holds that the lesson is that what counts is the kind of people and how they were raised, not their number, and that Muslims today need an elite of reformers raised on Islam, in view of the report about being like the froth of a flood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep25}
