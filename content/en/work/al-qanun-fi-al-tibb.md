## Summary
*The Canon of Medicine* (al-Qanun fi al-Tibb) is a medical encyclopedia written by [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]]. In his introduction he divided it into five books. The first begins with the general principles of the science of medicine, in four sections, and the second begins with simple drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.10}.

## Sections of the book
The first book has four sections: the definition of medicine and its subject matter; then general diseases, causes and symptoms; then the cause of health and sickness and the necessity of death; then the classification of the methods of treatment. These sections begin on pages 13, 103, 200 and 267 of the first volume {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.11} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.13} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.103} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.267}.

The second book, on simple drugs, begins on page 313 of the first volume and ends on page 728 {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.313} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.728}.

The third book, on diseases affecting the parts of the body one organ at a time, begins on page 3 of the second volume. The fourth book, on diseases not specific to one organ and on cosmetics, begins on page 3 of the third volume and ends on page 393. The fifth book, on the compounding of drugs, which is the Aqrabadhin, begins on page 395 of the third volume {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.3} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v3 p.3} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v3 p.393} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v3 p.395}.

## Note on the edition
The three volumes are from the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya edition in Beirut, first edition 1420 AH / 1999 CE, with notes by Muhammad Amin al-Dannawi. The edition has not yet been checked against a library catalogue, so the date rests on the title page alone. The point where the third book ends is not identified in the second volume, and the text of the third volume breaks off before the end of the fifth book. The page numbers here are scan numbers, which match the printed numbers at the points we checked.

## The experimental chapter in Book 2
In the first general division of Book 2, the second maqalah presents the [[invention/ibn-sina-seven-drug-assessment-conditions|drug-assessment conditions]]; both page images 318–319 were inspected, and their printed numbers match the scan numbers at these particular locations {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.318–319}. The author names experience and analogy as two routes to knowledge, discussing the former's conditions before turning to analogy in the third maqalah; that arrangement prevents treating the passage as a rejection of all non-experimental knowledge {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.318–319}.

The conditions concern acquired qualities of the substance, a simple condition, contrary cases, relative power, timing, consistency or usual occurrence, and differences between human and other bodies; intrinsic and incidental action are distinguished in humoral language throughout {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.318–319}. The passage supplies no observation count, outcomes table, randomization or masking procedure: it argues about interpretation rather than reporting implementation of a modern protocol {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.318–319}.

## Two witnesses and translation limits
The 2009 study presents Aida Tibi's translation from a Rome 1593 witness in the Sibbald Library, whereas our inspected images are from 1999; the witnesses' dates and pagination remain distinct {cite:nasser-tibi-savage-smith-canon-rules-2009 pp.78–79} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.318–319}. In rule four, “dosage” is bracketed in translation, while the Arabic discusses power, the condition and the weaker case; that interpretive addition remains visible, neither attributed to the author's wording nor converted into treatment advice {cite:nasser-tibi-savage-smith-canon-rules-2009 p.80} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.318}. The fifth condition's Arabic continuation also retains the complication of an acquired quality acting before a natural power, so timing alone does not become a guarantee of intrinsic action {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.319}.

This is a reading of one chapter, not a claim to have read the complete work or original manuscripts; the older structure and edition evidence above remain without unsupported revision {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.318–319}. The Canon's known circulation does not alone establish that every reader implemented the conditions or every later protocol descended directly from them {cite:nasser-tibi-savage-smith-canon-rules-2009 pp.78–80}.
