## Summary

At the midpoint of the series he turns from Mecca to Medina and tells the story of the Hijra from two Sahih al-Bukhari narrations, one by Aisha and one by Abu Bakr through al-Bara ibn Azib. He draws lessons on prayer before reform work, on taking practical precautions while trusting God, and on trials that come to reformers, and he closes with Anas ibn Malik on the welcome in Medina and the city's new meaning. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### The Hijra as the start of the Hijri calendar

- He says that the series has reached its fifteenth session, in the middle of Ramadan, which he takes as the middle of the Prophet's biography and the point of passage from Mecca to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He holds that the Hijra still touches Muslim life because the Hijri calendar, a marker of Muslim identity, is tied to it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that the Companions chose the Hijra, rather than the Prophet's birth, mission or death, as the start of the calendar, and he reads this as showing how decisive the event was. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He praises the Companions, scholars and narrators who preserved the details of the Prophet's life, and he holds that no other figure's life and conduct were preserved so fully. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### Signs that preceded the Hijra: the pledge of Aqaba and the supplication

- He says that the first sign of the coming move was the pledge of Aqaba, after years in which the Prophet looked for people to shelter him and found them only at the end, in the Ansar who undertook to protect him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that a second sign was a supplication revealed in the Meccan chapter al-Isra, asking for a good entry and a good exit and for a helping authority from God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir reads this as God guiding the Prophet to ask for relief and a quick way out, after which God allowed the move to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Ahmad in his Musnad, with Ibn Abi Shayba, reports from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet was ordered to migrate while in Mecca and the supplication was revealed, and that Qatada took the entry to mean Medina and the exit Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He draws the lesson that a believer should surround reform work with supplication before, during and after it, and he gives the Prophet, Ibrahim with Ismail, and Nuh as the examples for each of the three moments. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### Aisha's account: the Prophet's visit to Abu Bakr and the precautions

- He says that the day rests mostly on two Sahih al-Bukhari narrations of the Hijra, one from Aisha and one from Abu Bakr as told by al-Bara ibn Azib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates from Aisha that the Prophet came to Abu Bakr's house every morning and evening, and that he came one noon, covered, at an unusual hour, which made Abu Bakr sure something serious had happened. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that the Prophet asked that others be sent out, that Abu Bakr answered that only family was present, and that the Prophet then said he had been given leave to leave Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He holds that the Prophet took many material precautions during the move and that following him in taking such means is part of worship, since sentiment alone is not enough. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He draws the lesson that God directs the stages of the defence of his religion, so that the Prophet awaited permission rather than deciding to migrate alone, and he compares this with Musa at the sea. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Abu Bakr asked to accompany him and was told yes, that Abu Bakr offered one of his two mounts, and that the Prophet insisted on paying for it though Abu Bakr was his close friend. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He observes that thirteen years after the start of Islam Abu Bakr still spoke to the Prophet with ardent devotion, and he takes it as a lesson in love and respect. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### Preparing the journey: Asma, Abdullah, Amir and the guide

- He relates from Aisha that the family prepared the travel provisions in a bag, that she was about seven or eight years old, and that Asma cut a piece of her waist band to tie the bag, which is why she was called the woman of the two waist bands. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that the two went to the cave on Mount Thawr and stayed three nights, while Abdullah, Abu Bakr's young son, spent the night with them and returned to Mecca before dawn to follow what Quraysh planned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Amir ibn Fuhayra, a freed slave of Abu Bakr, grazed sheep nearby and brought milk each evening, and he describes Amir as an early Muslim who later died at Bir Maona. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that they hired a man of Banu al-Dil, then still on the religion of Quraysh, as an expert guide for an unusual route. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He draws the lesson that Muslims should not treat all unbelievers alike in dealings, since here expertise was the main requirement and trustworthiness also counted, and he compares Umm Salama's remark about the Negus in Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### The route out of Mecca

- He relates that after the three nights the two left the cave with Amir and the guide, who took them along the coastal road. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He describes the route as first heading south, the opposite of the road to Medina, then west toward the coast, then north, passing near Hudaybiyya and keeping al-Jumum on the right, and he says the road and the Prophet's steps along it are well known. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### In the cave: trial, Quraysh's reach and certainty

- He says that the Prophet and Abu Bakr stayed three days in the cave of Thawr, which he describes as small, high on a large mountain, with a low entrance that hides the inside unless one bends down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He holds that God's custom is not to spare a person he honours from trials, and he reads the Hijra as an example of the trials that come to reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Quraysh set a large reward for the Prophet, alive or dead, and he reasons that their reaching the mouth of the cave shows strong tracking and information power. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Abu Bakr told the Prophet that if one of them looked at his own feet he would see them, and that the Prophet answered by calming him with the thought that God was with them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He holds that the Prophet's calm in crises is a proof of prophethood for believers, and he links the scene with the Qur'anic report of the cave and with Musa's certainty at the sea. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He draws the lesson that a reformer needs full certainty in God to rely on in crises. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### Abu Bakr's account through al-Bara ibn Azib: the saddle and the rest stop

- He relates that al-Bara ibn Azib told how Abu Bakr bought a saddle from his father Azib and that Azib refused to let it be carried until Abu Bakr told how he had left with the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Abu Bakr said they travelled through the night and the next day until the noon heat, and that he found a rock with some shade, leveled the ground and spread a fleece for the Prophet to lie on. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He praises the language of the hadith collections as a source for Arabic eloquence beside poetry. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Abu Bakr then met a shepherd of a Quraysh man, had him wipe the udder and his hands clean, milked a little milk and cooled it with water, and brought it to the Prophet when he woke. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He points to the line in which Abu Bakr says he was pleased when the Prophet drank, and reads it as a lesson in caring for the Prophet's comfort. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### Suraqa ibn Malik's pursuit

- He relates from Abu Bakr that only Suraqa ibn Malik caught up with them, that Abu Bakr wept out of fear for the Prophet, and that the Prophet told him God was with them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that the Prophet prayed against Suraqa, that his horse sank in hard ground, and that Suraqa asked for relief and promised to turn pursuers back, offering provisions and an arrow, which the Prophet declined. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates Suraqa's own account in Bukhari: Quraysh offered the blood-money of each of the two to whoever killed or captured them, and a man of Banu Mudlij reported dark shapes on the coast. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Suraqa dismissed this to keep the prize for himself, went out on his horse, drew lots with arrows, an act he says God forbade, and got the result he disliked yet rode on. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that the Prophet recited as he walked and did not look back while Abu Bakr looked often, that the horse's forelegs sank twice, and that Suraqa called for safe conduct. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Suraqa concluded the Prophet's cause would prevail, asked for a document of safety, which Amir ibn Fuhayra wrote on a piece of hide, and that he turned other pursuers back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### Arrival in Medina and the stay with Banu al-Najjar

- He relates from Abu Bakr that people came out onto the roads and roofs of Medina, servants and boys cried out that the Messenger had come, and the people disputed over who would host him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that the Prophet said to stay with Banu al-Najjar, the maternal relatives of Abd al-Muttalib, to honour them, and he sees in this the Prophet's loyalty to a distant family tie. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### Anas ibn Malik on the order of arrival and Medina's joy

- He relates from Anas ibn Malik in Bukhari that the first to reach Medina were Musab ibn Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum, who taught the people to recite, then Bilal, Sa'd and Ammar ibn Yasir, then Umar ibn al-Khattab with about twenty Companions, and then the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that Anas saw the people of Medina rejoice at nothing as much as at the Prophet's arrival, to the point that slave girls spoke of his coming. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He says that the Prophet's entry into the city and the reception by its people will be treated more fully in the next session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}

### What the Prophet's coming gave to Medina

- He relates from Anas, in a report he describes as sound or with a good chain, that the day the Prophet entered Medina everything in it was lit and the day he died everything grew dark. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He holds that before his coming Medina was known for palms, the two lava fields, the valley of al-Aqiq and the dwellings of the Aws and Khazraj, and that it took on a new spirit and structure afterwards, being one of the pagan settlements like any other Arab town before. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates two hadiths in Bukhari that faith gathers in Medina as a snake to its hole and that Medina is better for those who leave it for conquered Syria and Iraq if they knew. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates that the Prophet on returning from travel would hurry his mount at the sight of Medina, calling it Taba, and said of Mount Uhud that it loves them and they love it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He relates a Bukhari report that Jibril told the Prophet to pray in the blessed valley of al-Aqiq, and he holds that every part of Medina had been walked by the Prophet and touched by revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
- He says that the next sessions will cover the Prophet's founding acts in Medina, the raiding parties before Badr, and then the Battle of Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep15}
