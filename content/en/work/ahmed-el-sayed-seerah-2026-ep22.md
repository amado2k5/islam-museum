## Summary

He opens with a biography of Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, then completes Ibn Ishaq's list of the emigrants to Abyssinia by clan: Jumah, Sahm, Adi, Amir ibn Luayy and al-Harith ibn Fihr. He dwells on Hisham ibn al-As and on families of the early Muslims who came out of houses of Quraysh's leading opponents, and closes with poetry of Abdullah ibn al-Harith and a jurisprudential note of al-Suhayli on emigrating to protect one's religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Aims of the series and focus on lesser-known Companions

- He says that the series follows the major events of the Prophet's life while pausing at length on the Qur'anic passages revealed around them, as with the passages from Surat al-Nahl in the previous session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He states that a main aim is to study the Companions, above all the less famous ones who entered Islam at its start, whether among the emigrants in Mecca or the Ansar in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that he begins each session with a biography of one early Muslim, the last two being Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Khabbab ibn al-Aratt: lineage and order of conversion

- He relates, following the author of the book on the Badr participants, that Khabbab was taken captive in the pre-Islamic period, sold in Mecca, bought by Umm Anmar of Khuza'a, and was an ally of Banu Zuhra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates a report that Khabbab was the sixth of six to accept Islam, and another from Ibn Ishaq that he came after nineteen people, making him about the twentieth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He explains the waves of early converts: Khadija, Ali, Abu Bakr and Zayd as the first group, among whom he holds Khadija to be first absolutely, then a group of seven or eight who became Muslim at Abu Bakr's hands, including Uthman. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He says that three names are reported as having entered Islam between the first four and the rest, namely Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Khabbab, and that he does not give a final ruling but treats these reports as evidence of very early conversion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He holds that merely becoming Muslim in Mecca in those early years was itself support for the Prophet, since there was no fighting or army at that stage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Khabbab's torture, his dispute with al-As ibn Wa'il, and his death

- He relates an account from Ibn Sa'd via al-Sha'bi in which Khabbab tells Umar that he suffered more than Bilal, because he had no protector among the polytheists, and describes being burned over a fire, leaving scars on his back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates from the two Sahihs that Khabbab worked as a blacksmith and made swords for al-As ibn Wa'il, who refused to pay him unless he renounced Muhammad; al-As answered that he would settle the debt after being resurrected with wealth and children, and a passage of Qur'an was revealed about him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He recalls the well-known story in which Khabbab asked the Prophet to pray for help, and the Prophet answered that they were hasty, then spoke of the future security and honour to come. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates Khabbab's later remark that he once owned neither coin nor dirham but at the end of his life had a large sum stored at home, and feared that his good things had been hastened for him in this world. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He states that Khabbab settled in Kufa and died there in the year 37, aged seventy-three, on Ali's return from Siffin, that he was the first buried outside Kufa and that Ali prayed over him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### How Ibn Ishaq organises the list of emigrants by clan

- He says that the reading in Sirat Ibn Hisham has reached about page 220 in the twenty-second lesson, and that he split the list of emigrants to Abyssinia over three sessions so that the many names do not bore listeners. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He holds that names come alive when one knows each person's story, and counts the list as eighty-two men and a dozen or so women. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He explains that Ibn Ishaq arranges them by Quraysh clans, with allies listed under each, and that the clans left are Jumah, Sahm, Adi ibn Ka'b, Amir ibn Luayy and al-Harith ibn Fihr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He explains the closeness of each clan to the Prophet's line: al-Harith ibn Fihr is the farthest, meeting him only at Fihr, then Adi and Makhzum, then Zuhra at Kilab, then Abd al-Dar and Asad at Qusayy, and Abd Shams at Abd Manaf, nearest of these. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Emigrants from Banu Jumah

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq lists from Banu Jumah the family of Uthman ibn Maz'un: Uthman, his son al-Sa'ib and his brothers Qudama and Abdullah, four people in all. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He lists also Hatib ibn al-Harith with his wife Fatima bint al-Mujallal and their sons Muhammad and al-Harith, Hattab ibn al-Harith with his wife Fukayha, and Sufyan ibn Ma'mar with his sons Jabir and Junada and his wife Hasana. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Seven sons of al-Harith ibn Qays of Banu Sahm and their fates

- He relates that from Banu Sahm Ibn Ishaq names Khunays ibn Hudhafa, Hisham ibn al-As, and a family of seven brothers, the sons of al-Harith ibn Qays, who all emigrated, plus a half-brother through their mother. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He says that Abdullah ibn al-Harith and Abu Qays were killed at al-Yamama, that al-Sa'ib was killed at Fihl in the conquest of Syria, and that al-Hajjaj or al-Harith and Sa'id were killed at the Yarmuk. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He explains a naming difference: Ibn Sa'd names al-Hajjaj where Ibn Ishaq names al-Harith, and for the brother called Ma'mar or Ma'bad he holds Ma'mar to be likelier because Ibn Ishaq and al-Waqidi agree on it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that nothing is recorded of Bishr's death and that Tamim is said by some to have died at Ajnadayn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He notes that this clan is the one to which Amr ibn al-As, Quraysh's envoy to recover the emigrants, belonged. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Sons of Quraysh's opponents who became early Muslims

- He holds that almost every house of Mecca's leading opponents produced early Muslims among its sons, daughters or brothers, and draws from this a reforming lesson that new generations carry hope even from criminal households. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He gives as examples the house of Abu Jahl, from which Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a and Salama ibn Hisham became Muslim, the daughter of Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, al-As ibn Wa'il's sons, and Sa'id ibn al-As Abu Uhayha, whose sons Khalid and Amr emigrated to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that Abu Uhayha, on his deathbed, vowed that the Prophet's god would not be worshipped if he recovered. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He describes Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt as the man who threw the camel's offal on the Prophet and who strangled him while he circled the Kaaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Hisham ibn al-As: detained in Mecca, his brother's testimony, and death

- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Hisham's mother was Umm Harmala, sister of Abu Jahl, and notes that Ibn Sa'd is the source for such family details absent from Ibn Hisham and Ibn Ishaq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Hisham was an early Muslim, emigrated to Abyssinia, came back to Mecca when he heard of the Prophet's move to Medina, was held by his father and people, and reached the Prophet only after the Trench; he was younger than his brother Amr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He grieves for this group of emigrants held in Mecca, who missed Badr and Uhud, among them al-Walid ibn al-Walid, Hisham, Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a and Salama ibn Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that the Prophet in Medina prayed by name in the qunut for al-Walid, Salama and Ayyash and the weak believers, and that two of them were once held in a single shackle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates a report through Ibn Sa'd that the Prophet called the two sons of al-As, Hisham and Amr, believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates an account from Ibn Sa'd that Amr ibn al-As told a circle of Quraysh that he and Hisham prayed at the Yarmuk for martyrdom, that Hisham received it and he did not, and that Amr then told them to make room for the young men and teach them hadith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He notes that other reports place Hisham's death at Ajnadayn, that he holds the Yarmuk account the sounder because it comes from Amr himself, and that historians often confuse the two battles. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates from al-Waqidi through Ibn Sa'd that at Ajnadayn Hisham charged into the enemy and was killed, and that Amr had the army ride over his body where it blocked a narrow passage, then afterwards gathered his remains and buried him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that Umar, on learning of Hisham's death, said that he had been a good support for Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He holds that Hisham's life is a model: an early Muslim, son of a man condemned in the Qur'an, brother of Quraysh's envoy to Abyssinia and nephew of Abu Jahl, who was jailed, joined the fighting after the Trench and died a hero in Syria. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Emigrants from Banu Adi ibn Ka'b

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq names from Banu Adi Ma'mar ibn Abdullah ibn Nadla, Urwa ibn Abd al-Uzza, Adi ibn Nadla and his son al-Nu'man, and Amir ibn Rabi'a, an ally of the family of al-Khattab, with his wife Layla bint Abi Hathma. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Ma'mar emigrated in the second emigration, stayed in Mecca, and joined the Prophet at al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that Adi ibn Nadla died in Abyssinia, the first emigrant to die there and the first heir in Islam, and that his son al-Nu'man inherited from him and was later appointed by Umar over Maysan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He holds that the first emigration had ten men and four women, followed some months later by the second. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Emigrants from Amir ibn Luayy; Suhayl ibn Amr's family and al-Sakran

- He relates that the first name from Banu Amir is Abu Sabra ibn Abi Ruhm, who returned from Abyssinia and fought at Badr, with his wife Umm Kulthum bint Suhayl ibn Amr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that five people of the house of Suhayl ibn Amr emigrated: his daughter Umm Kulthum, his son Abdullah, and his three brothers al-Sakran, Salit and Hatib; he stresses that Suhayl was among the fiercest opponents before he became Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that al-Sakran ibn Amr emigrated with his wife Sawda bint Zam'a, and that Ibn Sa'd says all sources agree on this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that Musa ibn Uqba and Abu Ma'shar say al-Sakran died in Abyssinia, whereas Ibn Ishaq and al-Waqidi say he returned to Mecca and died there before the move to Medina, after which the Prophet married Sawda, the first wife he took after Khadija. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He says that three wives of the Prophet were among the emigrants to Abyssinia, Umm Salama, Sawda and Umm Habiba, and recalls that Umm Habiba's husband Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh died in Abyssinia as a Christian, as the sira books widely report. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that some researchers doubt that Ubaydullah converted to Christianity, citing the exchange in which Heraclius asks Abu Sufyan whether anyone leaves Islam in resentment, to which he answered no, though he says the sira tradition holds the report as established and gives this only as a point for deeper study. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Emigrants from al-Harith ibn Fihr and the total count

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq names eight men from al-Harith ibn Fihr, among them Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, Suhayl ibn Bayda, Amr ibn Abi Sarh, Iyad ibn Zuhayr, Uthman ibn Abd Ghanm, Sa'd ibn Abd Qays and al-Harith ibn Abd Qays. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Poetry of Abdullah ibn al-Harith, called al-Mubriq

- He relates that Ibn Hisham records verses by Abdullah ibn al-Harith, one of the seven brothers, composed when the emigrants felt safe under the Negus's protection, urging believers to leave a land of oppression because God's earth is wide. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that in another poem the poet says Quraysh denies God's right as earlier peoples did, and threatens that he will make a bold show of resolve, which earned him the nickname al-Mubriq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that al-Suhayli cites the verse as evidence against al-Asma'i, who rejected the form abraqa beside ar'ada, and that al-Suhayli treats the poet as an Arab whose speech counts as a reliable authority. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He holds that Abdullah was wounded at al-Ta'if with the Prophet and later died at al-Yamama, rather than dying at al-Ta'if as al-Suhayli says. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that Abdullah's father al-Harith was among the mockers of the Prophet, while his grandfather Qays was the most honoured man of Quraysh in his time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}

### Quraysh's two envoys, and al-Suhayli on emigrating to protect religion

- He recalls that Quraysh sent Amr ibn al-As and Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'a, the latter a maternal half-brother of Abu Jahl and father of the poet Amr ibn Abi Rabi'a. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates al-Suhayli's legal point that leaving one's homeland, even Mecca, is allowed to flee for the sake of one's religion even if the destination is not Muslim land, since the Abyssinians were Christians, and that those who did so were praised as emigrants of both migrations. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He relates that al-Suhayli treats this as a permanent ruling: wherever evil prevails and a believer is persecuted, leaving for any land where he can practise his faith is required of him, and this emigration never ends. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
- He says that this closes the series' treatment of the Abyssinia migration after three or four sessions, leaving a passage of Ibn Hisham for next time, then the conversion of Umar and the boycott of the Banu Hashim in the valley. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep22}
