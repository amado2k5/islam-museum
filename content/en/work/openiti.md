# Open Islamicate Texts Initiative (OpenITI)

## Summary

The Open Islamicate Texts Initiative (OpenITI) is a multi-institutional academic effort led by researchers at the [[listing/aga-khan-university|Aga Khan University]]'s Institute for the Study of Muslim Civilisations in London, the Roshan Institute for Persian Studies at the University of Maryland, College Park, and Universität Hamburg, building the digital infrastructure for the study of Islamicate cultures. {cite:openiti-about}

## Its work

The initiative says that since its founding in 2016 it has worked on better optical character recognition and handwritten text recognition for Arabic script, on standards for their output and for text encoding, and on platforms for building text corpora and digital editions together. {cite:openiti-about} It states that, with researchers at Northeastern University, Université Paris Sciences et Lettres and the University of California, San Diego, it has reached what it calls the most accurate results to date on Arabic-script texts, on the open-source Kraken engine, with funding from the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, the National Endowment for the Humanities and the National Science Foundation. {cite:openiti-about}

## The corpus

Its second aim is a machine-actionable scholarly corpus of Islamicate texts, covering a growing number of Persian, Arabic, Ottoman Turkish and Urdu works, offered as plain text, in its own mARkdown and in TEI XML, with as much verified metadata as possible. {cite:openiti-about} The [[work/kitab-project|KITAB project]] works on this corpus. {cite:kitab-project-about}
