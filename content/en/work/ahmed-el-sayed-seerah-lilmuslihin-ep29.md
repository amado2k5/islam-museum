## Summary

He completes the Khaybar chapter with the return of the Abyssinia emigrants under Jafar and the Ashari group, the lessons of the slave Mid'am and of honesty with public funds, and the return to Medina. He then covers Asma bint Umays and Umar, the Prophet's marriages of the seventh year, his letters to the kings and the seal he adopted, and the small expeditions before the Fulfilled Umrah. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Who shared in the Khaybar spoils: the Abyssinia emigrants

- He recalls that the previous session dealt with Khaybar, which he describes as a conquest announced in the Qur'an beforehand and as a promise to those present at Hudaybiyya, bringing them wealth after years of hardship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says the Prophet gave a share of the Khaybar spoils to a small group who arrived after the fighting was over, the people of Abyssinia, a name that refers to where they had lived and not to their descent. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says this group had two parts: the first left for Abyssinia in the early years of the mission under Jafar ibn Abi Talib, and the second was led in fame by Abu Musa al-Ashari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that Abu Musa's party had set out from Yemen to reach the Prophet, but their ship was driven off course to the Abyssinian coast, where they joined Jafar, and the two parties travelled together to the Prophet, reaching him at the end of the Khaybar events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Jafar's return and his place with the Prophet

- He describes Jafar as the Prophet's cousin, among the people dearest to him and most like him in character and appearance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says Jafar had been away more than twelve years, having left around the fifth year of the mission and returned only in the seventh year after the hijra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He holds that the Prophet did not keep his close cousin for himself but placed him at once in the work of raising the banner of Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He notes that Jafar stayed in Medina only about a year before dying as a martyr, sent as one of the commanders of the army at Mu'ta, and he sees in this a lesson about the Prophet putting the cause above personal feeling. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates, from a mursal report going back to al-Shabi found in several hadith books, that the Prophet said he did not know whether he was happier at the conquest of Khaybar or at Jafar's arrival. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### The Asharis of Yemen

- He says the Ashari group of Abu Musa had accepted Islam in Yemen, received a share from the Prophet, and then settled together in houses in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates a report in which the Prophet says he recognises the Asharis' houses at night by their voices reciting the Qur'an, even though he had not seen the houses by day; he takes this as showing they were people of the Qur'an and night prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He adds as a side point that al-Bukhari, known for fine deduction, placed this report under a chapter heading on the testimony of a blind person, because the Prophet identified a place by sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says he will return to Abu Musa's group shortly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Wadi al-Qura and the death of Mid'am

- He says that after conquering Khaybar the Prophet did not go straight to Medina but went to the Wadi al-Qura area, in the direction of today's al-Ula, and besieged the Jews there for four days, and he mentions Fadak as well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that near Wadi al-Qura a young servant of the Prophet, named in his later remarks as Mid'am, was struck by a stray arrow and killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that the Companions congratulated him on martyrdom, but the Prophet answered that a cloak he had taken from the spoils before division would burn on him as fire. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He stresses that the Prophet gave no other reason for the man's punishment and did not call him a hypocrite, and that the item was only a cloak, so the lesson lies in trust and not in the amount taken. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Honesty with public funds

- He holds that a believer working in an Islamic or reform effort should not be lulled by that fact, because he is accountable like anyone else, and he treats money as one of the gravest matters on which a person may be punished. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says this matters especially where donations and large sums pass through such work, and that small pilfering, not only large theft, can be a cause of punishment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that the Prophet was strict about money, and tells how a man sent to collect alms brought the funds and set aside part as a gift to himself; the Prophet rose and objected that he should have sat at home to see whether gifts came to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says the rightly guided caliphs were also careful, and relates from al-Bukhari that Abu Bakr, after eating food given to him, learned it came from a questionable earning of the giver's past, and made himself vomit it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that Umar paid the emigrants and Badr veterans more than others but paid his own son Abdullah less, saying he had not emigrated of his own will but had been brought by his father. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says Abdullah ibn Umar was around eleven at the hijra and exactly fifteen when he took part at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says that carelessness and loose interpretation about money are as dangerous as theft, and that Islamic work needs a sound culture of standards on this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Return to Medina and the Prophet's love for it

- He relates that as the Prophet neared Medina and saw Mount Uhud he voiced love and longing for his city, and he thinks this happened more than once on his returns. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates a saying of the Prophet that this mountain loves the Muslims and is loved by them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says the Qur'an also honours Medina, and notes that some interpreters take a word in one verse about those who settled the abode and faith before the emigrants as a title for Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Asma bint Umays and Umar on who has the greater claim

- He says that Jafar's wife, Asma bint Umays, married Abu Bakr after Jafar's martyrdom and Ali after Abu Bakr's death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that on her return from Abyssinia Asma visited Hafsa, and Umar, seeing her, asked who she was and called her the woman from the sea. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that Umar told her the emigrants from Mecca had come first and so had a greater claim to the Messenger than the Abyssinia party. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that Asma answered angrily that Umar's group had been with the Prophet, who fed their hungry and taught their ignorant, while hers had lived far away among people who hated them, and she vowed to report the matter to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates from Sahih Muslim that the Prophet replied that Umar and his companions had one emigration while the people of the ship had two, and that the ship party came to Asma in groups to hear this again, delighted by it above all else. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He holds that these episodes show human feeling within the early community. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### The Prophet's marriages of the seventh year

- He says the Prophet, on the road back, married Safiyya bint Huyayy ibn Akhtab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He counts three marriages of the Prophet in the seventh year: Safiyya, Maymuna and Umm Habiba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says Umm Habiba, daughter of Abu Sufyan, had been in Abyssinia, where her husband died, before the Prophet married her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Letters to the kings and the seal

- He places the start of the Prophet's letters to rulers either just after Khaybar or between Hudaybiyya and Khaybar, two months apart, since Hudaybiyya was in Dhu al-Qada of year six and Khaybar in Muharram of year seven. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He holds that Hudaybiyya was a turning point, since once the fighting with Quraysh calmed, the Prophet widened the call to wider circles. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He says the rulers written to included Heraclius, Khosrow, al-Muqawqis and the ruler of Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that the Prophet was told rulers accept only sealed letters, and so he had a ring made to seal them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He holds that this shows regard for custom and for the approaches people accept, and that reformers should not say it is enough to speak the truth and leave people to take it or not. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Raids of the year and Abu Bakr as military commander

- He says that the Prophet sent several raiding parties in that period and went out himself on some light expeditions, since there were no idle moments in his life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that he sent Zayd ibn Haritha's party to al-Is, where Abu Basir had lain in wait for Quraysh, about eighty kilometres from the coast. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He holds that Abu Bakr led some parties and was not only the devout, learned Siddiq but also a fighter, and says he led an assault on some forts of Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He holds that the Prophet's training could produce people of many talents, and that differences between people must be respected but those who can should combine several qualities, as Abu Bakr did, and not say others already are known for fighting or learning, for example Khalid ibn al-Walid and Muadh ibn Jabal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### The raid on Banu Fazara and Salama's captive

- He says Abu Bakr was also the commander of the party against Banu Fazara, in a story told by Salama ibn al-Akwa in one of the two sound collections. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that spoils, captives and women were taken, and a woman fell to Salama's share. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He relates that on returning to Medina the Prophet asked Salama more than once to give her to him; Salama, who seemed attached to her, finally gave her up, and the Prophet sent her to Mecca to ransom Muslim captives held there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}

### Closing: the Fulfilled Umrah comes next

- He says that the year held a series of small raiding parties and expeditions until the Fulfilled Umrah came at its end, which he leaves for the next session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
- He holds that the biography is complete and many-sided, and stays a living source for reformers and believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep29}
