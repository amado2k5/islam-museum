## Summary

Ahmed Elsayed covers the Umrat al-Qada of year 7 AH, the marriage to Maymuna at Sarif, the expedition of Dhat al-Riqa, and the Battle of Mu'ta against a vast Byzantine and Arab Christian force. He draws lessons for reformers on psychological strength, sequencing, forgiveness and the youth of the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### Umrat al-Qada and the display of strength

- He relates that the Prophet set out with his Companions for Mecca in the seventh year, performing the umrah agreed with the Quraysh at Hudaybiyya, and that this umrah came to be called Umrat al-Qada. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He explains that Quraysh had refused entry in the sixth year so that the Arabs would not say he entered by force, and that they wanted to show they still held a word and standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the Quraysh withdrew from the sanctuary area, and some of them climbed the hills to watch, which he reads as a sign of the Muslims' dignity and height. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the polytheists spread the claim that a people weakened by the fever of Yathrib was coming, and that the Prophet therefore wanted the Muslims' strength to show during the circumambulation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says the Prophet bared the shoulder and ordered the first three circuits to be done at a brisk pace, a practice that remains a sunna today, even though the Muslims had come from a long journey. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Umar later said the brisk walk had been done only to impress the polytheists, and he draws the lesson that psychological factors, image-making and dispelling rumours are part of the struggle between truth and falsehood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### Ibn Rawaha's verses and struggle by the tongue

- He relates that Abd Allah ibn Rawaha, a poet and one of the Ansar's heroes, walked before the Prophet as he entered the sanctuary reciting verses of defiance, and that this was his last year in the biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Umar objected to reciting poetry before the Prophet and in the sanctuary, and that the Prophet told him to let Ibn Rawaha continue because the verses struck the polytheists more than arrows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He links this to a report in which the Prophet urged Hassan ibn Thabit to answer the polytheists with verse, and he counts these as sound reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He holds that struggle by the tongue is a recognised form of jihad, and cites a report from Anas in which the Prophet names tongues alongside wealth and lives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He draws the lesson that reformers should diversify their means and not confine their horizons to a single method, nor sit idle when that one method is unavailable. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### Leaving Mecca on time and not breaking the idols

- He relates that after the three agreed days Huwaytib ibn Abd al-Uzza told the Prophet to leave Mecca, and that the Prophet left, which he counts as keeping the covenant. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He contrasts this with the conquest of Mecca a year later, when the Prophet broke the three hundred and sixty idols around the Kaaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He asks why the idols were not broken during the umrah, and answers that it was not the right stage, noting that the Prophet had about three thousand with him then and few in Mecca before the migration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He holds that a reformer is not required to do everything at every time, and that calling to Islam and changing wrongdoing have their own jurisprudence and sequence. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He adds that the Prophet did not choose at the conquest to keep the idols for conciliation or as heritage, but broke them all at once, one year after the umrah. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### Marriage to Maymuna at Sarif

- He relates that on the way back, at Sarif near the sanctuary boundary beyond al-Tan'im, the Prophet married Maymuna bint al-Harith, whom he counts as his last wife. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Maymuna died decades later at the same place and that her grave is known there today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that al-Bukhari records a report from Ibn Abbas that the marriage took place while the Prophet was in the state of ihram. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He presents this as a model of criticising the text rather than the chain: scholars largely accept the report as sound in transmission but hold that Ibn Abbas erred, because Maymuna herself and Abu Rafi, who carried the messages between them, reported that it occurred after he left the state of ihram. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### The expedition of Dhat al-Riqa and the Bedouin who forgave

- He says scholars differ on when Dhat al-Riqa took place, and that he holds the sounder view to be after the Trench, in the seventh year, partly because Abu Musa al-Ashari, who had come back from Abyssinia to Khaybar, narrates it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the Prophet led the Companions to Najd and visited several tribes, with no major fighting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates the story that a Bedouin took the sleeping Prophet's sword, and that the Prophet was unharmed and forgave him, and he counts this report as sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He compares this forgiveness to that of the Jewish woman who poisoned the Prophet and was pardoned until a Companion died of it, after which she was put to death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says the expedition is also known for prayer in fear, and that this supports placing it after the Trench, where prayers were delayed instead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Abu Musa explained the name by saying that groups of six shared one camel in turn until their feet were worn, so they wrapped them in rags. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Jabir's camel and conditions within a sale

- He relates that on the return Jabir lagged behind on a feeble camel, the Prophet struck it with a stick so that it became strong, and then asked to buy it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the price rose until it reached an uqiyya, which he gives as forty dirhams, and that Jabir stipulated riding it to Medina before handing it over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says jurists use this report as a main proof for stipulations added to a sale, distinct from the basic conditions of a sale such as consent and ability to deliver. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the next day in Medina the Prophet returned both the camel and the price, and he reads this as a warm gesture toward his Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### The army sent to Syria and its three commanders

- He relates that the Prophet sent a detachment to Syria, which became known as Mu'ta, and that no one expected to meet an army of that size. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He notes two signs of some anticipation of difficulty: the force numbered about three thousand, far above the usual detachment of tens or hundreds, and the Prophet named three successive commanders. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the order was Zayd, then Ja'far if Zayd fell, then Abd Allah ibn Rawaha. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says Zayd ibn Haritha was the Prophet's freedman and adopted son before adoption was abolished, the first freedman to accept Islam, and the commander most often appointed, as Aisha is reported to say. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says Ibn Kathir gives Ja'far's age at death as thirty-three, which he takes to mean Ja'far was about seventeen or eighteen when he migrated to Abyssinia, and he draws the lesson that the Companions were young and given room by the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the sources mention a Ghassanid envoy of the Prophet being killed, and reports of Byzantine movements, as reasons for the expedition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### The vast enemy army and Ibn Rawaha's encouragement

- He relates that the force entered the land of Syria, today in Jordan, and heard of the movements of Heraclius and the Arab Christians. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says that all the biographers he has seen give the enemy as about two hundred thousand, and he considers such a gathering easy for Heraclius. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He contrasts this with the largest earlier army, the polytheist coalition at the Trench, which he gives as about ten thousand. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the Companions debated whether to write to the Prophet, and that Ibn Rawaha settled it by saying that what they dreaded, death in battle, was what they sought, namely martyrdom. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He lists the senior veterans of Badr, Uhud, the Trench and Hudaybiyya in the army, with Abd Allah ibn Umar at eighteen and Khalid ibn al-Walid, who had just become Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### The fall of the three commanders and Khalid's command

- He relates that fighting began at once, and Zayd ibn Haritha fell early, pierced by spears. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Ja'far took the banner and fought fiercely, and that when his hand was struck he held it with the other, which he links to Ja'far's epithet of the two wings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that Abd Allah ibn Umar counted over ninety wounds on Ja'far's body. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Ibn Rawaha then took the banner and fell, and an unnamed veteran of Badr lifted it and asked the people to agree on a commander, and they chose Khalid ibn al-Walid. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He cites a statement of Khalid in al-Bukhari that nine swords broke in his hand that day, leaving only a Yemeni blade, and takes it as a sign of the fighting's ferocity. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### Withdrawal or victory: the two views

- He says the better-known view is that Khalid withdrew, while another, less known view that some early specialists held, among them al-Zuhri, al-Bayhaqi and Ibn Kathir in the Bidaya, is that the Muslims fought and prevailed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq took the Prophet's phrase about God granting victory to mean Khalid saved the Muslims without heavy loss. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He holds that even a withdrawal was itself a victory from God, since a three thousand army that lost its commanders would otherwise have been destroyed in enemy territory with nowhere to flee. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham, who lists the Muslim dead for each expedition, gives about twelve for Mu'ta, and he reads this as evidence of extraordinary courage and of Khalid's skilled withdrawal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that the sources describe Khalid dividing the army and moving men to the rear to return as apparent reinforcements, frightening the Byzantines. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The Yemeni auxiliary and the dispute over spoils

- He relates a story from Sahih Muslim of a man from Yemen who joined the force as reinforcement, armed with a sword, and who made a shield from camel hide. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that he killed a gilded enemy leader and took the arms and gear, and that Khalid took them away from him afterward as too much. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Awf ibn Malik protested, reminding Khalid that the Prophet had ruled the killer's gear belongs to the killer, and then brought it before the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Khalid said he thought it too much, the Prophet ordered it returned, and he was angry at those who would not leave his commanders alone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says Ibn Kathir uses this story as evidence of real slaughter and victory in the battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}

### News in Medina and the grief of Ja'far's family

- He relates that before the army returned, the Prophet announced from the pulpit the fall of the three commanders and the banner passing to a sword of God, and that Anas reports his eyes shed tears. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He counts this as a miracle, since the news came before any word of the army. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that when the army approached, the people went out to meet it, and the Prophet took Ja'far's children, carrying Abd Allah ibn Ja'far behind him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He relates that Asma bint Umays grieved deeply and that the Prophet is reported to have told people to prepare food for the family of Ja'far. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
- He says Ibn Hisham is the main source for the poems on this, including verses by Hassan and Ka'b ibn Malik mourning the dead of Mu'ta. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep30}
