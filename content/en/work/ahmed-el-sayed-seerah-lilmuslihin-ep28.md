## Summary

He recalls the Dhu Qarad story of Salama ibn al-Akwa and follows Salama's report linking Hudaybiyya, Dhu Qarad and Khaybar. He then treats the Khaybar campaign: its causes, the siege of the forts, the killing of Marhab by Ali, the poisoned sheep, and the fiqh rulings he says belong to this expedition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### Lessons from Dhu Qarad: initiative and individual character

- He recalls that the previous session covered the expedition of Dhu Qarad and the role of Salama ibn al-Akwa, who pursued the raiders himself and recovered the Prophet's camels. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He holds that the story teaches the value of personal initiative and responsibility, since the Companions were people of character and not mere numbers around the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He cites Abu Bakr as an early example of initiative: he approached Uthman, Talha, al-Zubayr and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, and they accepted Islam through him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He holds that the Prophet did not mould his Companions into one type: he let the shy, the eloquent, the military commander and the jurist keep their own traits, and only trimmed what was excessive so it fitted the balance of Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### Salama's report joining Hudaybiyya, Dhu Qarad and Khaybar

- He says that the report of Salama ibn al-Akwa joins three events in one narration: Hudaybiyya, Dhu Qarad and Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that Salama says the army left for Khaybar only three nights after returning to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says that the span from Hudaybiyya to Khaybar was roughly two to three months, and he stresses how many large events the Prophet's life held in so short a time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### Salama's narration of the road to Khaybar, Amir's death and the duel with Marhab

- He relates that on the way to Khaybar Salama's uncle Amir chanted verses to the troops, and the Prophet asked for forgiveness for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that Salama notes the Prophet never asked forgiveness for a particular person in that setting except that the person was martyred, and that Umar asked why Amir had not been left to them longer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that when the army reached Khaybar its king Marhab came out boasting with his sword and Amir stepped forward to fight him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that Marhab's blow struck Amir's shield, and when Amir swung low his own sword rebounded and cut a vein in his leg, which killed him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that some Companions then said Amir's deeds were void because he had killed himself; Salama went to the Prophet in tears, and the Prophet rejected that claim and said Amir would receive his reward twice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that the Prophet then sent Salama for Ali, who had sore eyes, and Salama led him by the hand because he could hardly see. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that the Prophet applied saliva to Ali's eyes, which healed, and gave him the banner; Marhab came out again, Ali answered him in verse, struck his head and killed him, and the conquest came through Ali. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### Khaybar and Surat al-Fath: the promised nearby conquest

- He says that Surat al-Fath, revealed over Hudaybiyya, alludes to Khaybar in more than one place, such as the promise of a near conquest and of many spoils given in advance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He holds that the passage about those who stayed behind and wished to join the spoils shows that the promise was for the people of Hudaybiyya alone, so only they took part at Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He adds that, in his reading, weak-hearted people and hypocrites keep away from hardship and hurry toward easy gain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### Why the Prophet marched on Khaybar

- He lists as a first cause that the Jews of Khaybar kept plotting against the Prophet and his Companions, and that they had played the main part in mobilising the tribes for the Battle of the Trench. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says that those settled at Khaybar included people expelled from Medina, among them Banu al-Nadir, and he also names Banu Qurayza in the same context. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He holds that the Prophet meant to cut off the source of the plots by going to their home ground, which he describes as many fortresses and not one, many of which still stand today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says the siege was long because the forts were strong, and that a number of Companions took the banner and failed before the Prophet announced the banner would go to another man the next day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He lists as a second cause that God was compensating the believers of Hudaybiyya who had pledged to the death with little means, so that Khaybar was a gift to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Food and plenty after the conquest

- He relates that Aisha and Ibn Umar are reported in sound hadith to have said the Muslims were fully fed on dates only after Khaybar fell. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says that after the conquest the Muhajirun returned to the Ansar the gifts of date palms the Ansar had lent them on arrival in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He sums up Khaybar as a spoil that brought ease and blessing to the Muslims and also ended the threat of their enemies there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### Arrival at Khaybar and the siege of the forts

- He relates that the Prophet reached Khaybar at night and camped outside, and in the morning the farmers came out with their tools and were startled to see the army. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that they cried out the name of Muhammad and the army, and that the Prophet answered with a declaration of greatness and said Khaybar was ruined. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He describes the forts as built on hills with hidden channels bringing fresh water, so that the defenders were well prepared. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says some forts fell at once while others held out, and that Mahmud ibn Maslama was killed beside a fort when Marhab dropped a heavy millstone on him from above. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says that as forts fell, survivors fled to the next fort and gathered there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### The banner given to Ali and Sahl ibn Sad's report

- He relates that the Prophet announced he would give the banner next day to a man loved by God and his Messenger, at whose hands God would grant victory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates from Sahl ibn Sad that the people spent the night hoping to receive it, and he holds that their eagerness was for that testimony and not for command. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that the Prophet asked for Ali, was told his eyes were sore, and had Salama bring him, after which the Prophet's saliva healed his eyes at once. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He notes that the Prophet did not forbid medicine and encouraged it, and holds that God sometimes shows his favour through such events to strengthen the believers, like the healing of the leg of Abd Allah ibn Rafi after the killing of Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### The call to Islam before fighting and Ali's killing of Marhab

- He holds that although everyone already knew victory would come next day, the Prophet told Ali to advance calmly, to camp near them and to invite them to Islam first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says the Jews of Khaybar had already heard of Islam from the time they lived near Medina, and that the invitation was nevertheless a mercy and a way of removing any excuse. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He holds that had they accepted, the fighting and the spoils would have ended and the army returned to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says the rule is general and not tied to that age: enemies are fought until they accept Islam or pay the poll tax and submit to its rule, and he contrasts this with wars fought only for domination and economic gain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He holds that the lesson for reformers is that even in strength and near victory a Muslim remains a caller to God in every situation, and he cites Yusuf calling his fellow prisoners in prison. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that Ali then came down to them with the banner, Marhab came out as before, and Ali killed him, which broke the Jews' power. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He counts the stand of Ali at Khaybar among the noblest of his deeds in the Prophet's biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Treatment of Khaybar's people after the conquest

- He says the Prophet did not treat the people of Khaybar as he had treated Banu Qurayza: he let them remain and work the palm groves, and the produce came to him as a levy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says they stayed in their lands until Umar ibn al-Khattab expelled them, and later expelled them from the whole Arabian Peninsula. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### The poisoned sheep

- He relates from al-Bukhari, on the authority of Abu Hurayra, that after the conquest a Jewish woman from Khaybar sent the Prophet a cooked and roasted sheep as a gift, and that it contained poison. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says the Prophet noticed after he and some Companions had eaten, gathered the Jews present, and asked whether they had poisoned it; they said they had, to be rid of him if he was a liar, or to cause him no harm if he was a prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He extends this judgement to the present, saying the same malice remains in them toward reformers and those who follow the prophets' path; this is his own generalising opinion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that Anas said he could still see the trace of the poison in the Prophet's mouth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He holds that this shows the Prophet's forbearance in a matter that touched him personally. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He relates that later, when Bishr ibn al-Bara died from the poison, the Prophet ordered the woman killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}

### Fiqh rulings tied to Khaybar

- He says that fiqh scholars look at the biography through the rulings it contains, and that the Khaybar expedition holds several rulings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says that at Khaybar the Prophet forbade the meat of domestic donkeys, and ordered the pots the hungry men had cooked it in to be overturned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says that the Prophet also forbade temporary marriage at Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
- He says that the Prophet's marriage to Safiyya also yields rulings that scholars derived, and he leaves these for a later session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep28}
