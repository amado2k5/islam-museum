## A book of calculation

[[person/ibn-munim-al-abdari|Ibn Munim al-Abdari]] divides this work into integers and fractions. Its subject is knowledge of calculation, not a book of rulings, although he connects exposition with purposes of learning arithmetic. The overview names Euclid and part of al-Mutaman's Istikmal as preparatory reading and describes analysis and synthesis in proofs {cite:arrabita-fiqh-hisab book structure and introduction}.

SectionXI of the integer part uses [[invention/combinatorics-ibn-munim|combinations and arrangements]] in counting words. Models start with ten silk colours and selections of different sizes, then include repeated letters and their markings. A model's twenty-eight letters and maximum length are not a census of every meaningful word or language in use {cite:arrabita-fiqh-hisab combinatorial section} {cite:djebbar-ibn-munim-combinatoire pp9–13,19–23}.

## Method and development

Djebbar identifies inductive and combinatorial construction, not just gathered answers. He compares al-Khalil's linguistic and al-Karaji's algebraic work, questioning absence of a connection between the triangle and binomial coefficients in root extraction. Modern notation permitting that connection does not let us complete a historical claim on the author's behalf. Scholarly explanation and original wording are different layers {cite:djebbar-ibn-munim-combinatoire pp10–13}.

Djebbar reports a practical section and tables added after copies circulated among students. A surviving version need not be a single composition layer produced on a known day. Prefatory praise of al-Nasir suggests1207–1212 to the historian, not a dated scribal ending. Reported praise of Ibn Tumart is authorial rhetoric, not museum endorsement {cite:djebbar-ibn-munim-combinatoire pp5–8,12}.
