## Summary

Ahmed Elsayed first gathers Ibn Kathir's compilation of reports on how revelation came to the Prophet, then treats the early prayer and Ibn Ishaq's account of its institution. He then reads the opening of surat al-Muddaththir as the first commands of the call, and finally reads Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham on the first Muslims before the public call, supplemented with biographical notes from Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Opening: the sources of the session and the chain of Urwa

- He says that this is the eighth session of the series and that the previous three sessions dwelt on the report of the first descent of revelation in the cave. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that the session adds a chapter that Ibn Kathir supplies and that does not come from Ibn Hisham or Ibn Ishaq, and he presents the variety of sources as a benefit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that Urwa ibn al-Zubayr was a great Madinan jurist and a leading authority on the sira, and that his mursal reports on the sira are in principle accepted and are among the strongest of the sira mursal reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says that Malik is the most reliable transmitter of both main routes to Urwa, through Urwa's son Hisham and through al-Zuhri. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### How revelation descended: the forms and the heaviness

- He relates that Ibn Kathir collects reports on the states of revelation, beginning with the report in which al-Harith ibn Hisham asks the Prophet how revelation comes to him, with two modes described: a ringing sound and an angel taking human form. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir then adds the account of Aisha about the Prophet sweating in cold weather, the report of the Slander incident, and reports from Umar, Ubada ibn al-Samit, Zayd ibn Thabit and Yala ibn Umayya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates the story of Zayd ibn Thabit and Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum, in which the weight of a short phrase of revelation almost crushed Zayd's thigh on which the Prophet's thigh rested. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir cites al-Nasai's judgement that one report via Yunus ibn Sulaym is munkar, and he praises this habit of commenting on chains as the mark of a hafiz. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that revelation was always heavy and had a physical effect, in several forms rather than one, yet that the Prophet did not lose awareness, as shown by the incident of Sawda's outing at night, which he places after the rule on the veil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir also treats the verses about not moving the tongue to hasten the recitation, and he reports from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet used to move his lips in his eagerness until God assured him of gathering and explaining the revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Prayer before the Isra: Ibn Ishaq's placement and the scholars' views

- He says that Ibn Ishaq inserts a chapter on the institution of prayer right after the early revelation material, and that this placement has been criticised because the five prayers were only imposed after the Isra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that some prayer certainly existed before then, and points to the Prophet praying at the Kaaba and to the Qur'anic passages on night vigil and on praising God morning and evening. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says that no firmly established report fixes the times, form or number of units of that early prayer, and that scholars differed: some said only night prayer without fixed limits, others two prayers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar mentions the view that nothing was obligatory before the Isra except night prayer, and a view ascribed to al-Harbi of two units in the morning and two in the evening. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He lays out Ibn Ishaq's order of material as the first descent of revelation, the pause, the surah of al-Duha, then the prayer chapter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Ibn Ishaq's reports on the institution of prayer and his reliability

- He relates Aisha's report, cited by al-Bukhari and Muslim, that prayer was first prescribed in two units and later completed in residence but kept as two on journeys, and notes scholars differ on whether this refers to the Isra or to an earlier time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's report from an unnamed scholar that Jibril showed the Prophet ablution at a spring in the valley of Mecca and prayed with him, and that the Prophet then taught Khadija. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that this report is weak, ranking it at the lowest of the five grades he proposed for Ibn Ishaq's reports, because the source is anonymous and from the generation of Ibn Ishaq's teachers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He argues that such weak reports actually support Ibn Ishaq's honesty, since he gives full chains where he has them and admits vagueness where he does not, and that his chain for Aisha's report matches the chain of al-Bukhari through Malik and Salih ibn Kaysan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq's report from Ibn Abbas that Jibril led the Prophet in the five prayers on two successive days to define their times, holds that this account is sound, but that it belongs after the Isra and so is out of place here, citing Ibn Abd al-Barr that the scholars did not differ that prayer was imposed in Mecca at the Isra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir distinguishes this lesson in prayer from the two-day leadership at the House and says the principle of prayer was already incumbent during Khadija's life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Surat al-Muddaththir: the first commands of the call

- He says that Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Kathir speak of the Prophet becoming a prophet with the first revelation and a messenger with the command to rise and warn, and he calls this an interpretive expression, holding that prophethood began with the first revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says that the pause in revelation may have been short, about a month by a well known view, or longer, with views of two or two and a half years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He observes that the opening verses of both al-Alaq and al-Muddaththir are short and consist of commands addressed to the Prophet himself, seven in al-Muddaththir by his count, and concludes that the bearer of the message is first trained and then sent. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He compares this with the first address to Musa and concludes that a reformer in the prophets' path must first learn, obey, be trained and worship himself before setting out. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that the address with the name of the one wrapped in a cloak shows God's knowledge of his private state, and that the contrast with the command to rise shows the moment was for work rather than rest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that warning is central to the prophets' call, that the Prophet's first public words on al-Safa were about warning, and that he called himself the naked warner. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says that the command to magnify the Lord covers heart and tongue and prepared the Prophet to face the powerful of Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that al-Tabari reports most early authorities took the command about the garments in a figurative sense, of purifying deeds and heart, while al-Tabari himself leaned to the literal sense, and that the word for impurity was explained by many as idols. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates two readings of the command not to give while seeking more: not to count the revelation as a favour to people, or not to count one's own deeds as a favour to the Lord. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that the Prophet understood the first warning as private and the later verse about warning his nearest kin as public, and offers that this may come from his reading of the situation rather than a separate revelation, which he says is not known. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Ibn Sa'd's placement of a verse interpretation of Ibn Sirin

- He says that reading Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat teaches humility about the distance between a student and the early scholars. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Sa'd opens his list of the Emigrants with Muhammad as the first emigrant, and he calls this a beautiful touch. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that in the section on the Prophet's call to Islam Ibn Sa'd places a report, with a sound chain to Muhammad ibn Sirin, that Ibn Sirin took the verse about the best of speech to refer to the Prophet, and he admires the choice of this report at this point. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He notes that Ibn Sa'd's volume on the sira consists almost entirely of chains and rarely of his own commentary. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### The first Muslims in Ibn Ishaq: Ali, Zayd and the household

- He says that the fourth topic of the session is the first to accept Islam, before the stage of Dar al-Arqam, and that Ibn Ishaq opens it with Ali ibn Abi Talib, whom he places after Khadija. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq, citing Mujahid, that Ali had been raised in the Prophet's household because Abu Talib had many dependants in a hard year, and that the Prophet and al-Abbas each took a son of Abu Talib, Ali and Jafar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq gives Ali's age as ten at the time, and he says this claim on being first is open to challenge by scholars. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham on Zayd ibn Haritha: Hakim ibn Hizam brought him from Syria as a slave, Khadija chose him, the Prophet asked for him, freed and adopted him before revelation, and Zayd became the first male to accept after Ali. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates the poem that Ibn Ishaq attributes to Zayd's father mourning the lost son, and says he does not vouch for the poem's authenticity, remarking that the poetry in the sira is of uneven reliability and that Ibn Hisham himself omitted some of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham corrects Ibn Ishaq on Abu Bakr's name, holding it to be Abdullah and Atiq a title. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Abu Bakr's call and the list of about fifty early Muslims

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's portrait of Abu Bakr as an amiable, well liked merchant and genealogist of Quraysh who called trusted people to Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that, by Ibn Ishaq, those who accepted through Abu Bakr were Uthman ibn Affan, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Sad ibn Abi Waqqas and Talha ibn Ubaydullah, and that Ibn Ishaq counts these with Abu Bakr and others as the first group of eight. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq cites the Prophet as saying that everyone he invited hesitated except Abu Bakr, and that Ibn Hisham glosses a rare word in it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates the long list that follows: among others Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, Abu Salama, al-Arqam, Uthman ibn Mazun and his brothers, Ubayda ibn al-Harith, Said ibn Zayd and his wife Fatima bint al-Khattab, Asma bint Abi Bakr, Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, Abdullah ibn Masud, Ammar ibn Yasir, Suhayb, Jafar ibn Abi Talib, Amir ibn Fuhayra, Khalid ibn Said ibn al-As, Abu Hudhayfa and the four sons of al-Bukayr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that Ibn Ishaq's mention of Aisha in this list is probably an error, since she may not yet have been born at the start of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that these names together, about fifty, are those who accepted in the three years of the private call, that Ibn Ishaq's list can be supplemented in some names, and that the list should be learned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says people then entered in groups until Islam became known in Mecca, and that the command to proclaim openly came after three years in his account. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Who was first to accept Islam: Ibn Kathir's reconciliation

- He says that the question of the absolute first has drawn the attention of scholars, with Ibn Kathir collecting many chains and views. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates the views reported by Ibn Kathir: Ali first, per Muhammad ibn Kab and Ibn Ishaq; al-Waqidi saying the scholars agreed Ali accepted a year after the prophethood began, with Ali ten years old per Mujahid; al-Nakhai and others saying Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates Ibn Kathir's reconciliation: Khadija first of all, Zayd first of the freed, Ali first of the boys, and Abu Bakr first of free men, whose acceptance was of greatest benefit because of his standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir cites the report in al-Bukhari in which the Prophet recalls Abu Bakr's confirmation, as nearly decisive for Abu Bakr, and Ibn Abbas citing Hassan's verses on Abu Bakr as first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir says this is the majority view among Ahl al-Sunna, and he himself says the matter allows disagreement and God knows best. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates the report from Ibn Masud, in Ahmad and Ibn Majah, that seven first declared their Islam, and that Bilal alone gave himself up, and he adds that Ibn Ishaq seems to have omitted Bilal, Sumayya and al-Miqdad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates Sad ibn Abi Waqqas's statement that he was a third of Islam for seven days and Abu Dharr's statement about being fifth, and holds that the secrecy of the call explains the differing counts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat as a source for the Companions

- He holds that Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat is distinctive among biographical works for giving social detail on the Companions and organises them by generation and then by tribe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says that much of Ibn Sa'd's material comes from his teacher al-Waqidi, who gave many chained reports that were criticised, whereas Ibn Sa'd himself is reliable and a critic of narrators. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He contrasts Ibn Ishaq, who gives chains to Companions that match the sound collections and rarely stands alone, with al-Waqidi, who often stands alone with many chained reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says that the volume on the Companions who fought at Badr is the best place to look for most of Ibn Ishaq's fifty names, some of whom are missing even from works such as al-Istiab, and that the Isaba of Ibn Hajar is better than that. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says he will not give full biographies but will pick, for the early Muslims, when they accepted and personal details from the Tabaqat. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam in the Tabaqat

- He relates that Ibn Sa'd gives al-Zubayr's mother as Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib and lists his children by their mothers, eleven sons and nine daughters, and he notes this habit of recording children and wives for each Companion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates from Urwa that al-Zubayr named his sons after martyrs hoping they would be martyred, citing the names of Abdullah ibn Jahsh, Hamza, Jafar, Musab, Ubayda and others. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates a story from Urwa that al-Zubayr as a boy broke a man's arm in a fight and Safiyya asked her son's opponent how he found al-Zubayr, which he takes as a sign of her firm upbringing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates reports that al-Zubayr was fourth or fifth to accept Islam, aged sixteen, that he migrated twice to Abyssinia, and was paired in brotherhood with Talha or with Kab ibn Malik, and he notes that the Prophet seems from Ibn Sa'd to have paired Emigrants among themselves and then with the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that al-Zubayr commanded a wing at the conquest of Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### Musab ibn Umayr, Amir ibn Fuhayra, al-Arqam and Amir ibn Rabia

- He relates that Musab ibn Umayr belonged to Banu Abd al-Dar, the keepers of the banner of Quraysh, that he carried the Muslim banner at Uhud, and that the Prophet stood by his body and recited a verse about men who kept their pledge. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates a report via Urwa about the Prophet looking with compassion at Musab in a worn cloak after a life of comfort, and he says Musab was the first teacher sent to Madinah, with Ibn Umm Maktum. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Amir ibn Fuhayra, a freed slave of Abu Bakr, accepted before the Prophet entered Dar al-Arqam, was tortured in Mecca, and was killed at Bir Mauna. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates from Uthman ibn al-Arqam that his father was among the first seven and that his house on al-Safa was where the Prophet called people, and that Umar accepted there the next morning after the Prophet's night prayer for Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Amir ibn Rabia accepted before Dar al-Arqam, migrated to Abyssinia twice and to Madinah, was present at Badr and other battles, and died in the days after the killing of Uthman after a night prayer asking to be spared the turmoil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He notes that Abu Hudhayfa and Salim, freed slave of Abu Hudhayfa, early Muslims, were killed at Yamama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}

### The four sons of al-Bukayr and the families who emigrated whole

- He relates that Ibn Sa'd reports that the four sons of al-Bukayr, Aqil, Khalid, Amir and Iyas, left Mecca together to Madinah, locking their houses behind them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He says that two families are known to have emigrated entirely: the family of Uthman ibn Mazun and the sons of al-Bukayr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He relates that Aqil was paired in brotherhood with Mubashshir ibn Abd al-Mundhir and was killed at Badr aged thirty four, Khalid was killed at al-Raji in Safar of the fourth year, with Hassan ibn Thabit's verses on him recalling Khubayb and Asim, Iyas was present at Badr and later battles, and Amir at Badr and later battles. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
- He holds that the early Muslims stood by the Prophet when there was no protection or gain, were tortured, emigrated to Abyssinia and Madinah, and died at Badr, al-Raji, Uhud and elsewhere, and he names Abdullah ibn Jahsh and Abu Salama among the dead of Uhud and its aftermath. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep8}
