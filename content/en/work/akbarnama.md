## Summary
The Akbarnama is Abu'l-Fazl ibn Mubarak's official history of the reign of the Mughal emperor Akbar in India, written in Persian and covering the events of the state and the men who served it. {cite:akbarnama}

## Details
Abu'l-Fazl ibn Mubarak — minister and state secretary at Akbar's court — composed the work as an official record of his sovereign's days, gathering the affairs of the state, the reports of its servants and the biographies of its notables. The Persian text was published, edited by Abd al-Rahim, in the Bibliotheca Indica series (no. 79) of the Asiatic Society of Bengal in Calcutta, in parts issued between 1877 and 1886. {cite:akbarnama}

The book ranks among the most important Persian historical texts of the Mughal period: its author had access to the state's papers and records by virtue of his office, and it has remained a primary reference for the study of Akbar's reign, his administration and his fiscal and military institutions. {cite:akbarnama}
