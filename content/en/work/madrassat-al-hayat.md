## Summary
"Madrasat al-Hayat" (The School of Life) is the title [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] gave to his commentary on [[work/sayd-al-khatir|Sayd al-Khatir]] by [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]]. He opened it every Ramadan, choosing one reflection (khatira) from the book, reading it, explaining it, and moving from it into hadith, the Sira and the lives of scholars. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:02:00} The Assabile archive keeps twelve video episodes under this title, each between about fifty and seventy-eight minutes long, given by al-Heweny in the mosque before his audience; in one of them he says that some people regard this mosque of his, among the mosques of Kafr el-Sheikh, as they would the Sacred Mosque. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:09:30}

Not every episode belongs to the Sayd al-Khatir commentary. Episode 2 is a lesson on Umm Salama's hadith of the migration to Abyssinia, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:01:30} episode 6 a lesson from the Book of Knowledge of Sahih al-Bukhari, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:01:30} episodes 11 and 12 two parts of another lecture series on hearing and obeying, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:20:30} and episode 7 repeats the recording of episode 4. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep07 00:02:00} Some episodes are dated by al-Heweny himself: episode 3 on the night of 27 Ramadan 1432, and episode 10 on Wednesday 3 Ramadan, in August 2011. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:02:00} The archive's pages give no recording dates.

## The episodes
**Episode 1: Misunderstanding God and His Messenger.** He opens the season by presenting Ibn al-Jawzi as a preacher to the common people and comparing his book with Ibn al-Qayyim's al-Fawa'id, then reads a reflection in which Ibn al-Jawzi seeks refuge from misunderstanding, above all among those who claim knowledge, and illustrates it with a saying of [[person/abu-abd-al-rahman-al-sulami-al-muqri|Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami]] about the hadith of the people of Badr. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:06:00} He gives al-Sulami's biography, tells [[event/hatib-letter-to-quraysh|the story of Hatib's letter]] in full, and then disagrees with Ibn al-Jawzi on the hadith's meaning. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:24:30} He draws on Uthman's equipping of the Army of Hardship, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:31:00} on the dream and humility of [[person/abdullah-ibn-salam|Abd Allah ibn Salam]], {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:39:00} and on Umar's fear of God, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:42:00} and closes on the unnamed narrator in al-Bukhari's chains and how he is identified, and on al-Suyuti's remark in the introduction to Tadrib al-Rawi that hadith science is mastered only by the most skilled. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:54:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:55:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:56:30}

**Episode 2: Umm Salama's hadith of the migration to Abyssinia.** He returns to a hadith he had explained years before, which he calls established, narrated by Ibn Ishaq in the Sira and by Ahmad in the Musnad: the Quraysh embassy to [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]], the speech of [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far ibn Abi Talib]], the Negus's return of the gifts, and [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]]'s crossing of the Nile. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:17:00} He then describes the state of the Arabs at the coming of the message through the words of al-Miqdad in al-Adab al-Mufrad and of al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba in al-Bukhari, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:21:30} cites Jabir's hadith that the Prophet spent ten years following people to their camps and markets, asking who would shelter and support him, and tells of the pledge of al-Aqaba and the warning of As'ad ibn Zurara, the youngest of the seventy. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:40:30} He ends with the conversion of [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] at the hand of the Negus. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep02 00:49:30}

**Episode 3: The reality of beneficial knowledge.** On the night of 27 Ramadan he begins with [[practice/laylat-al-qadr|Laylat al-Qadr]], then reads Ibn al-Jawzi's reflection on scholars who indulge in legal licences and on the worshipper who presumes upon his deeds before God; he says this is the ninth lesson on this reflection. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:11:30} He digresses on God's blessings in the body, drawing on what doctors taught him during his treatment, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:20:00} then gives the biography of [[person/sila-ibn-ashyam|Sila ibn Ashyam]] with his miracle and the story of the lion, and tells the story of [[person/khubayb-ibn-adi|Khubayb ibn Adi]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:43:00}

**Episode 4 (repeated as episode 7): On the friends of God.** He reads Ibn al-Jawzi's description of those God chooses for His friendship, defines the [[concept/wali|wali]], and disagrees with Ibn al-Jawzi that beauty of form is a condition, citing the stories of [[person/julaybib|Julaybib]] and of Zahir; he then speaks of the friends of God's fear of a bad end and of the death of [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:28:30} He turns to guarding one's time: al-Bukhari, who woke seventeen times in one night to write the chapter headings of his Sahih, as his student Muhammad ibn Abi Hatim al-Warraq reports, and who began seeking hadith only at eleven; [[person/ibn-aqil|Ibn Aqil]] and his book al-Funun; and he ends with Ibn Abbas's hadith on health and free time, with which al-Bukhari opens the Book of Softening Hearts. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:49:00}

**Episode 5: The third man of the cave.** He completes the hadith of the three men shut in by the rock and reaches the man whose labourer's wage he invested; he says the two Shaykhs narrate it only from Ibn Umar, and that Ali, al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, Abu Hurayra, Uqba ibn Amir and Abd Allah ibn Abi Awfa narrate it with additions. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:04:30} He draws from it that one who holds another's money may invest it if he is confident of profit, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:09:00} and moves on to the story of al-Zubayr's debt, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:10:00} to Ibn Taymiyya's saying that it is recommended to leave a recommended act in order to bring hearts together, which he reads as a temporary leaving, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:25:30} to Abu Hamza al-Sukkari, Muhammad ibn Maymun, and the neighbour who priced his nearness at two thousand, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:46:30} and to A'isha's vow not to speak to her sister's son Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and its expiation with forty slaves freed. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:53:00} He presents the hadith as an answer to Ibn al-Jawzi, who made light of those three men's deeds, and asks his audience: if you were the fourth, which deed would you plead? {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:56:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep05 00:57:00}

**Episode 6: From the Book of Knowledge of Sahih al-Bukhari.** He explains the chapter "whoever is asked about knowledge while busy speaking, finishes what he is saying and then answers the questioner", sets out his seven-point method for explaining a hadith, and the kinds of al-Bukhari's chapter headings and their three pillars, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:13:00} tacit approval, the hadith of [[person/khuzayma-ibn-thabit|Khuzayma ibn Thabit]] and Jabir's camel, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:19:30} and then Abu Hurayra's hadith of the Bedouin who asked about the Hour and the loss of trust. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:32:30} From the chain he draws lessons on the forms of transmission and their abbreviations, the letter of switching between chains, the difference between al-Bukhari's craft and Muslim's, high and low chains and the three-man chains, and a son's narration from his father. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:36:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:53:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep06 00:59:00}

**Episode 8: Evasion (al-hayda).** He defines hayda in the language as swerving from the path and divides it into evasion used to disparage in the criticism of narrators, evasion to escape answering out of scruple, as when Yahya ibn Ma'in was asked by a woman about a hen that died in a well, and evasion by way of fallacy. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:07:30} His example of the last is Pharaoh in his exchange with Moses as told in Surat al-Shu'ara', {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:09:00} [[passage/quran-26-23|al-Shu'ara' 23]], and he pauses on Pharaoh's first word, "what", which is used for things without reason. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:13:00} He draws two lessons: a caller to God does not spend his time defending his own honour, for the Prophet was called mad, a sorcerer, a poet and a soothsayer and never stopped to say he was not mad; and a caller needs alternative plans, as armies prepare a plan for victory and a plan for defeat. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:16:00} He then speaks at length about Egypt's modern wars and about his own experience of restrictions. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep08 00:44:30}

**Episode 9: The blessing of perspicacity.** He says that the previous Ramadan he gave eight lectures on the reflection "Nafthat masdur" and four remain, then takes a short reflection on how the intelligent man escapes a predicament while the heedless man harms himself, with Joseph's brothers as the example. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:02:30} He speaks of [[concept/firasa|firasa]]: whether it is innate or acquired, al-Shafi'i's saying that he learned it before jurisprudence, and the need of muftis and judges for it, then gives examples from Solomon, [[person/iyas-ibn-muawiya|Iyas ibn Mu'awiya]], [[person/yahya-ibn-aktham|Yahya ibn Aktham]], al-Hajjaj and [[person/wahshi-ibn-harb|Wahshi]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:38:00}

**Episode 10: Clothes and adornment that breed self-admiration.** He reads Ibn al-Jawzi's examples and says Ibn al-Jawzi spoke too generally, since what corrupts one soul may set another right; he grades the ring hadith sound and gives the sources of the hadith of the man swallowed by the earth as he strutted. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:07:00} He then speaks at length on trailing the garment and on [[concept/kibr|kibr]], judges the report of A'isha's garment false, and tells the hadith of the patterned cloak the Prophet sent back to Abu Jahm, from which he draws that one may accept a gift returned to him but may not take back a gift. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:42:00} He explains the prohibition of silk by its softness and luxury, not its price, since pure wool may cost more than silk and diamonds more than gold, and both are permitted. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep10 00:49:30}

**Episode 11: Hearing and obeying (part two).** He opens with the words of one pledging to the Prophet that he pledges to hear and obey in what is pleasant and what is disliked, and says that abandoning this "law" is the community's one disease. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:03:00} He narrates from Sahih Muslim, from Abu Hurayra, that the Companions fell to their knees when the end of Surat al-Baqara was revealed, [[passage/quran-2-284|al-Baqara 284]], and that when they said "we hear and obey" the verse of relief came down, [[passage/quran-2-286|al-Baqara 286]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:14:00} His examples include Ukkasha ibn Mihsan; Abu Hurayra's prayer for knowledge that is not forgotten, which he says al-Nasa'i narrates in al-Sunan al-Kubra with a good chain; Abu Mas'ud al-Badri, who dropped the whip and freed his slave; and Mu'awiya ibn al-Hakam al-Sulami, whom the Prophet taught gently. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:47:30} He ends by taking the hadith of the slave girl as proof that a solitary report is authoritative in matters of creed. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep11 00:53:00}

**Episode 12: Hearing and obeying (part three).** He opens with the cry "Wa Mu'tasimah", the campaign against Amorium and Abu Tammam's ode, then with Hafiz Ibrahim's verses from Mustafa Kamil's campaign to found the National University. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:10:00} He says all the community's ills come down to one word, explains the meaning of the two testimonies as "nothing is worshipped by right but God, and no one is followed by right but the Messenger", and gives Sufyan ibn Uyayna's saying that the Messenger is the great scale. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:22:30} He then gives three descriptions of the Jahiliyya by Ja'far, al-Mughira and al-Miqdad, and notes that whatever al-Bukhari narrates outside the Sahih must be cited with its grading. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:39:30} Among his examples of stopping at the Prophet's word are Imran ibn Husayn and Bushayr ibn Ka'b on modesty, Ibn Umar and his son Bilal on women going to the mosques, and Abd Allah ibn Mughaffal on flicking pebbles, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep12 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny urjuza by Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Hanafi, one of the later Hanafi shaykhs of Medina as he describes him, on the four imams' instructions that their words not be put before the Prophet's.
