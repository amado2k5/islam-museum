## Summary
Al-Fusul al-Thalathin ("The Thirty Chapters") is a work on astronomy by Muhammad ibn Kathir al-Farghani, cited by al-Mas'udi among the authors in this field. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 519}

## Details
Al-Mas'udi, in Muruj al-Dhahab, in the course of his account of celestial influences of the sun and moon, names the books of those concerned with this science: Ptolemy's Almagest and other ancient works, then, among those who came after the rise of Islam, al-Kindi, Ibn al-Munajjim, Ahmad ibn al-Tayyib, Abu Ma'shar, al-Khwarizmi, and Muhammad ibn Kathir al-Farghani for what he related in his book al-Fusul al-Thalathin, along with Thabit ibn Qurra, al-Tabrizi, Muhammad ibn Jabir al-Battani and others devoted to the sciences of the heavens. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 519} He adds that he conveys from these into his own book only in a general way, seeking brevity. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 519} For al-Mas'udi, then, al-Fusul al-Thalathin stands among the standard astronomical treatises produced after the advent of Islam. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 519}
