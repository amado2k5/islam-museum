## Summary
*Nur al-Yaqin fi Sirat Sayyid al-Mursalin* ("The Light of Certainty on the Life of the Master of Messengers") is a concise biography of the Prophet by Shaykh [[person/muhammad-al-khudari|Muhammad al-Khudari]], inspector at the Ministry of Education and formerly teacher of Islamic history at the Egyptian University, as the title page of its fourth edition (1344/1926) describes him. {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.1}

## Why it was written
In his preface al-Khudari says that from his youth he enjoyed reading the histories of earlier peoples, and saw in the life of the Prophet — the harm he suffered from his people and his patience until he left his homeland — the greatest teacher for Muslim minds: it holds what rulers need to win hearts, what commanders need to prepare men and equipment, and what the public needs to unite. {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.3} He regretted that people had stopped reading it, excusing them by the length of the books written on it. {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.3} When he came to al-Mansura he met Mahmud Bek Salim, judge of the Mixed Court, who longed for "a sira free of padding and complication from which ordinary Muslims can benefit"; the leading men of the city pressed him too, and he wrote it. {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.3} {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.4}

## Sources and method
He says his sources were the Qur'an and the sound Sunna as recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim, going beyond them only where needed to explain expressions, with help from Qadi Iyad's [[work/kitab-al-shifa|al-Shifa]], [[work/insan-al-uyun|al-Sira al-Halabiyya]], al-Qastallani's [[work/mawahib-al-laduniyya|al-Mawahib al-Laduniyya]] and al-Ghazali's [[work/ihya-ulum-al-din|Ihya' Ulum al-Din]]. {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.4} He begins with the Prophet's lineage, naming each forefather together with his mother. {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.4} {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.5} {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.6}

## Structure
The book follows events from the lineage and birth to the Hijra, then arranges what follows year by year, giving for each year its campaigns, expeditions and delegations, up to the eleventh year with the Prophet's illness, Abu Bakr's leading the prayer and the Prophet's death; it closes with two sections on his qualities and his miracles, as the contents of the fourth edition show. {cite:nur-al-yaqin-tijariyya-1926 p.272}
