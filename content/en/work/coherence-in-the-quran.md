## Summary

*Coherence in the Qur'an* by Mustansir Mir is, in the words of its publisher's description, the first detailed study of [[person/amin-ahsan-islahi|Amin Ahsan Islahi]]'s contribution to Qur'an exegesis. {cite:archive-org-coherence-in-the-quran-mir} The description says Islahi rejected the view that the Qur'an is disjointed and, developing the ideas of his teacher [[person/hamiduddin-farahi|Hamid al-Din al-Farahi]], argued in his multi-volume Urdu commentary *Tadabbur-i Qur'an* that the Qur'an has a *nazm*, or coherence, that is aesthetically pleasing and of deep hermeneutical significance. {cite:archive-org-coherence-in-the-quran-mir}

The book begins with a history of the idea of *nazm* among traditional and modern writers, then sets out the exegetical principles and the *nazm* principle of Farahi and Islahi. {cite:archive-org-coherence-in-the-quran-mir} Two chapters treat the sura as a unity, the second comparing the *nazm* views of Tabataba'i and [[person/sayyid-qutb|Sayyid Qutb]] with Islahi's; chapters on sura pairs and sura groups follow, then conclusions, two appendices and a bibliography. {cite:archive-org-coherence-in-the-quran-mir}

In his preface the author says the book is a revised version of his 1983 doctoral dissertation at the University of Michigan, whose committee was chaired by James A. Bellamy and included [[person/fazlur-rahman-malik|Fazlur Rahman]] of the University of Chicago, and that it aims to introduce Western scholars of Islam to a major twentieth-century Urdu commentary. {cite:archive-org-coherence-in-the-quran-mir} American Trust Publications published it in 1986; the author had earlier published parts of it in *The Muslim World*, *Hamdard Islamicus* and *Islamic Quarterly* in 1983–1984. {cite:archive-org-coherence-in-the-quran-mir} {cite:openlibrary-coherence-in-the-quran-1986}
