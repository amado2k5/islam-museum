## Summary

The lecturer gives a biographical entry on Amir ibn Fuhayra, then opens the account of the Quraysh boycott of Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib following Ibn Ishaq's narration in Ibn Hisham's recension. He discusses its dating, the order of events Ibn Ishaq places in these years, seven lessons he draws, and begins Ibn Ishaq's list of Qurayshi opponents about whom Qur'anic passages were revealed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Series recap and the book used for the Companion entry

- He says this is the twenty-sixth session and that the previous two dealt with the conversion of Umar and the Prophet's supplication for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says the series is leaving the sixth year of the mission and moving to the seventh, with a possible return to a few missed events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says the Companion entries come from a book on the Badr veterans by Sulayman al-Awda. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Entry on Amir ibn Fuhayra

- He says Amir ibn Fuhayra was a freed slave of Abu Bakr, dark-skinned, of Azd origin, who had earlier served a master from the family of Aisha's maternal half-brother before accepting Islam and being bought and freed by Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Amir grazed Abu Bakr's flock and used it to cover the traces of the Prophet and Abu Bakr during the emigration, bringing them food while they hid in the cave, and then travelled with them to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He notes a difference over whether his earlier master was al-Tufayl ibn al-Harith or Abdullah ibn al-Tufayl, and says it matters little. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Ibn Sa'd places Amir's conversion before the Prophet entered the house of al-Arqam, so he counts among the earliest converts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He holds that the Prophet's use of the house of al-Arqam may belong to the fifth or sixth year, the open phase, rather than the secret phase as commonly said, adding that neither phase was absolute. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Amir was among the tortured weak believers in Mecca, fought at Badr and Uhud, and was killed at Bi'r Ma'una in year four of the Hijra at about forty years of age. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates a report in al-Bukhari that after Amir was killed, a survivor named Amr ibn Umayya heard the enemy leader Amir ibn al-Tufayl say Amir's body had been lifted toward the sky and set down again. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar judged this version sounder than another saying the angels buried him, and sees it as honouring Amir and warning the enemies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says there may be a topic of miracles of the Companions in the Prophet's time, some in life and some after death, and does not know whether anyone has compiled it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The earliest converts and the place of Ibn Ishaq's framing

- He counts the first four converts as Khadija, Abu Bakr, Zayd and Ali, and says scholars differ on who followed them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says Ibn Ishaq died in 151 and that he is distinctive among early authors for giving events a narrative framework rather than only chains of reports, and that later biographers depend on it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Quraysh decide on the boycott and the document

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Quraysh acted after seeing the Muslims find safety in Abyssinia under the Negus, Umar and Hamza join the Prophet, and Islam spreading among the tribes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that they agreed in writing not to marry into or from Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib and not to buy from or sell to them, which he labels social and economic boycott. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that the document was hung inside the Kaaba and that its scribe was Mansur ibn Ikrima, while Ibn Hisham mentions al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, and that the scribe's fingers withered after the Prophet's supplication. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib gathered with Abu Talib in his ravine, while Abu Lahab left them and sided with Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He stresses that it was not a physical enclosure of the clans, though Quraysh did try to stop food reaching them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates a report that Abu Lahab spoke with Hind bint Utba to confirm his support, and calls such reports with the weak phrasing of a transmitter among the lowest-grade material in Ibn Hisham's work. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### How Ibn Hisham edits Ibn Ishaq

- He explains that Ibn Hisham's work is a scholarly refinement of Ibn Ishaq, mostly adding linguistic glosses, short corrections or alternative views, sometimes extra reports, and sometimes omitting poems he judges unreliable. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Abu Talib's poem during the boycott

- He counts this as the fourth or fifth poem of Abu Talib in the biography, in which Abu Talib backs the Prophet, criticises Quraysh and their allies, and declares he will not hand Muhammad over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says the poem refers to the document as a source of ill fortune for those who wrote it and warns of war if they persist. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Hardship and secret supplies; Hakim ibn Hizam and Abu Jahl

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that they stayed two or three years in great hardship, with supplies reaching them only secretly through relatives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates the story that Abu Jahl stopped Hakim ibn Hizam carrying wheat for his aunt Khadija, who was with the Prophet in the ravine, and that Abu al-Bakhtari intervened, struck Abu Jahl and let the man pass, as Hamza watched. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He notes that wives of the clans came from other clans, such as Banu Asad, which gave a tribal way to send help. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Dating and length of the boycott

- He says, following Sulayman al-Awda's summary of sources, that al-Waqidi dates the start to the night of the new moon of Muharram of year seven of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that the biographies give two or three years, while al-Waqidi is firm on three and places the exit in year ten, which he reads as implying more than three full years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says the end of the boycott, the annulment of the document, belongs to year ten and will be treated later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Gap between years seven and ten and Ibn Ishaq's arrangement

- He holds that many biographies, especially modern ones, jump from year seven to year ten as if nothing happened, and that this is a defect. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He lists the usual outline of Meccan events, from revelation, secret and open calls, torture, the two Abyssinian migrations, the conversions of Hamza and Umar, the boycott, then the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadija, the Night Journey, Ta'if and the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says Ibn Ishaq instead fills the boycott years with the opponents about whom revelations came, naming among them Abu Lahab, Umayya ibn Khalaf, al-As ibn Wa'il, Abu Jahl, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, al-Akhnas ibn Shariq, Ubayy ibn Khalaf and Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, then the episode of Ibn Umm Maktum, the return from Abyssinia, the annulment of the document, and later items up to the Night Journey. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says upcoming sessions will therefore focus on the Qur'an and the polemics with the polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Seven lessons from the boycott

- He holds that opponents of the truth use recurring methods such as threats of expulsion, imprisonment, killing and mockery, and that the Quraysh methods were neither the first nor the last of their kind. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He holds that Quraysh gave the boycott a sacred legal colour by hanging the document in the Kaaba, even though it cut kinship ties and targeted two clans of their own tribe, many not Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He points out that Quraysh saw the Prophet as the one who severed kinship, and that Abu Jahl's prayer at Badr reflects this view. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He holds that an organised economic, social and security boycott was a new method after torture. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He holds that the boycott was a new kind of trial that came after earlier hardships. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He holds that opponents are most angered when the person they target has protection, and cites Abu Talib's protection and the Abyssinia emigrants as examples where Quraysh tried negotiation, delegations and then pressure. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He holds that a preacher needs firm trust in his protectors, that the tie here was tribal, that the Prophet did not forge it but found it in place, and that after Abu Talib's death he sought support elsewhere, such as at Ta'if. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He recalls that Amr ibn al-As carried a prepared message of Quraysh to the Negus in the Abyssinia episode. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### The boycott in al-Iraqi's didactic poem

- He says al-Iraqi's rhymed summary of the biography links the boycott with the Abyssinia migration, dates its start to year seven, and says the document was eaten away by the earth so that only God's name remained, after which the clans left in year ten. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He notes the poem allows that the stay may have been two years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Abu Lahab and Umm Jamil, and al-Suhayli's reflections

- He explains that Ibn Ishaq lists the mockers about whom revelation came, a topic that books on Qur'anic sciences treat as the unnamed figures of the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Umm Jamil, sister of Abu Sufyan, was called the carrier of firewood because she is said to have thrown thorns in the Prophet's path. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates from al-Suhayli's commentary on Ibn Hisham that the choice of the word for neck rather than the usual one links her rope to the ornaments women wear, that the rope is compared to a well rope, and that the thorns and rope are connected. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Umm Jamil came to the Prophet in the mosque holding a stone while Abu Bakr was with him, could not see the Prophet, spoke a rhyming insult, and left. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham marks part of her words as coming from someone other than Ibn Ishaq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates that Quraysh called the Prophet by a reversed name in their abuse, and that the Prophet said they were reviling someone else while he was Muhammad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Umayya ibn Khalaf

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Umayya ibn Khalaf used to mock the Prophet whenever he saw him, and that a Qur'anic chapter on slanderers is said to concern him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates Ibn Hisham's glosses on two words for slanderer with poetic examples. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says Umayya was killed at Badr, with Bilal, whom he had tortured, involved, and that the story will come later along with Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says some also hold that Umayya is among those meant in the opening of Surat Abasa, with others naming Utba ibn Rabi'a or al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}

### Al-As ibn Wa'il and Khabbab

- He says al-As ibn Wa'il was the father of Amr ibn al-As and, as an ally of Banu Adi, had sheltered Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, a swordsmith in Mecca, asked al-As for payment, who answered by scoffing at the promised rewards of Paradise and saying he would pay in the next world, and that a Qur'anic passage is said to have been revealed about him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
- He says the session ends here and the list of those about whom revelation came continues next time, and mentions a plan to read Surat al-An'am for its refutation of polytheist claims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep26}
