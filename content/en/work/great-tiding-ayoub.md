# The Great Tiding: An Annotated Translation of the Thirtieth Part of the Qur'an (Mahmoud Ayoub)

## Summary
*The Great Tiding* is an annotated English translation of Juz' Amma, the last part of the [[work/quran|Qur'an]], together with al-Fatiha, by Mahmoud Ayoub, published by the Islamic Call Society of Tripoli, Libya, in 1983. {cite:gloqur-ayoub-great-tiding}

## The translator
Ayoub (1935–2021) was born in South Lebanon into a practising Shia family; blind, he attended a school for blind children run by British missionaries and became a Christian missionary in youth, returned to Islam during his studies, earned a Harvard doctorate in 1975 and became professor of Islamic studies at Temple University. {cite:gloqur-ayoub-great-tiding}

## How it was made
Libya founded the Islamic Call Society in Tripoli in 1972 to publish multilingual Qur'an translations; it commissioned Ayoub to translate Juz' Amma, whose suras are so often recited in prayer. {cite:gloqur-ayoub-great-tiding} In 1997 Ayoub self-published a second edition in Iowa with small corrections and a new title, *The Awesome News*. {cite:gloqur-ayoub-great-tiding}

## What it contains
It is bilingual, with a general introduction, an introduction to each sura and footnotes; Ayoub called it "a textbook" meant to give direct access to the Qur'an's meaning. {cite:gloqur-ayoub-great-tiding} Its English is modern, clear and elegant, and its commentary rests on the classical tafsir tradition, setting out its range of opinions without claiming certainty; Johanna Pink finds it drawn largely from Sunni commentaries with no discernible Shia leanings. {cite:gloqur-ayoub-great-tiding}

## Reception
Pink sees it as an attempt to continue madrasa-style tafsir in English, which seems not to have met a wide response; Ayoub never extended it beyond Juz' Amma. {cite:gloqur-ayoub-great-tiding}
