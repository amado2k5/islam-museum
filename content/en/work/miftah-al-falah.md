## Summary
Miftah al-Falah wa-Misbah al-Arwah ("The Key of Success and the Lamp of Spirits") is a book on remembrance of God (dhikr). The two title pages of its first printing attribute it to "Taj al-Din ibn 'Ata' Allah al-Iskandari", the first giving the title as "Miftah al-Falah fi Tahdhib al-Nufus". {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 1} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 3} In his preface the author says that remembrance of God is "the key of success and the lamp of spirits", the mainstay of the path, and that he had seen no complete and sufficient book on it, so he began this one at the suggestion of "a righteous brother". {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 4} See [[person/ibn-ata-allah-al-iskandari|Ibn 'Ata' Allah al-Iskandari]] and [[concept/dhikr|dhikr]].

## Arrangement
The author arranged it in two parts: the first of an introduction, sections, chapters and principles; the second explaining the formulas of remembrance, with sections and a conclusion. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 4} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 53}

- Introduction on what dhikr is (p. 5). {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 5}
- Sections on the fruits of the formulas, on the reports on the merit of dhikr and of gathering for it, on the warning against neglecting it, and on the sayings of the early generations (pp. 6-18). {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 6} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 10} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 15} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 16}
- A section on the rules of conduct of dhikr (p. 19), a chapter on its benefits in general (p. 21), and a chapter on the benefits of formulas used by the travelling disciple, among them the Beautiful Names of God (pp. 24-33). {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 19} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 21} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 24}
- A section on how the disciple is set between seeking forgiveness and invoking blessings on the Prophet according to his state (p. 34). {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 34}
- A chapter on unification (p. 41), a chapter on gnosis (p. 42), a section on whether dhikr or Qur'an recitation is better (p. 43), and a section on the hindrances on the way to God (p. 48). {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 41} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 42} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 43} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 48}
- A chapter on what the people of the path must hold themselves to (p. 49). {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 49}
- Part two: grammatical and doctrinal discussions of "there is no god but God", its merit and its names (pp. 53-81), then a chapter on healing invocations and seeking forgiveness (p. 82), the remembrances of morning and evening (p. 83), what is said on travel (p. 90) and on visiting the sick (p. 92). {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 53} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 69} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 82} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 83} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 90} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 92}

## The definition and kinds of dhikr
The author defines dhikr as freeing oneself from heedlessness and forgetting through the constant presence of the heart with God, or, it is said, repeating the name of the One remembered with heart and tongue; he widens it so far as to say that the theologian, the student of law, the teacher, the mufti, the preacher, the one who reflects on God's greatness and the one who obeys His command are all rememberers. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 5} He divides it into dhikr of the tongue, of the heart and of the limbs; dhikr bound to a time or place, as in prayer, pilgrimage and before sleep; and unrestricted dhikr such as glorifying, praising, declaring God's oneness and greatness, and "there is no power and no strength save in God". {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 5} He quotes [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]] that the reality of dhikr is the One remembered taking possession of the heart, and that it has three husks, the outermost being dhikr of the tongue, with which the rememberer strives to make the heart present until heart and tongue join. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 6}

## Rules of conduct and benefits
The author divides the rules of dhikr into those that come before, during and after it: before it are repentance, discipline of the soul, acquiring the obligatory knowledge of religion and body, and purifying intentions so they are "lawful, not habitual", with lawful, clean clothing and lawful food; during it are sincerity, perfuming the gathering and sitting facing the qibla. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 19} Here he mentions rules laid down by the people of the Sufi path, among them that a disciple under a shaykh's care "draws with his heart" on his shaykh's spiritual resolve at the start of dhikr; these are Sufi rules the author sets out, not statements of the museum. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 19} He then lists the benefits of dhikr in general: it drives away Satan, pleases the Merciful, removes worry, brings the watchfulness that leads to the station of ihsan, and makes the rememberer among the heedless like a dark house with a lamp in it. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 21} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 24}

## The Beautiful Names as remedies
The author says that the Beautiful Names of God are "remedies for the diseases of hearts and the ailments of travellers", and assigns each name to the states it suits, some for the heedless, some for beginners, some for those seeking to detach from secondary causes; this is a Sufi ordering of formulas that the author presents. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 24} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 26} He sets the disciple's course by his state: one with many sins begins by seeking forgiveness until its fruit appears, then moves to invoking blessings on the Prophet, and does not rise from one formula to another until its proper fruit appears to him awake or in sleep. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 34}

## Unification, gnosis and the Qur'an
The author quotes al-Ghazali that unification has two husks and a kernel: the saying of the tongue; then the belief of the heart, "the unification of the common people", guarded by the theologians; then seeing all things as from God in a way that cuts one's attention away from intermediaries. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 41} He defines gnosis as perceiving a thing in its essence and attributes as it is, and divides knowledge of God into the general knowledge required of every responsible person — affirming His existence, declaring Him transcendent and describing Him as He has described Himself — and a special knowledge. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 42} On whether dhikr or Qur'an recitation is better he quotes al-Ghazali: recitation is better for all people except the one travelling to God in the states of his beginning and some states of his end, since the Qur'an contains every kind of knowledge and state. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 43}

## The people of the path
In the chapter on what the people of the path must hold to, the author says the way of God is far from contention, and that its people must do justice to others against themselves and not seek it from anyone, accept excuses without making them, and treat people with mercy and compassion. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 49} At the end of the first part he distinguishes the miracle as the common people understand it, a breaking of the usual order, from the miracle as the elect understand it: the divine care that gave them strength to break the habits of their own souls. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 53}

## The word of unification
The second part opens with discussions of "there is no god but God": the grammarians' account of "la" denying a whole genus, and the dispute over the implied predicate. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 53} In it he quotes from al-Hakim al-Tirmidhi a hadith on one who dies bearing witness that there is no god but God, with the shaykh's explanation that the witness at death is sincere because desires have died and outward and inward are one, followed by a completion from Fakhr al-Din al-Razi. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 64} He then gives a section on the names of this formula, the first being "the word of unification". {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 69}

## The edition
The edition used is "the first printing", at the expense of Shaykh Mustafa Sayyid Ahmad Taj and his son Ibrahim Taj, booksellers of Tanta, printed at Matba'at al-Sa'ada beside the Cairo Governorate and completed on 15 Safar 1332 AH. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 3} {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 95} It closes with a formula of blessing on the Prophet that prays for God's good pleasure on "al-Shadhili our shaykh"; see [[person/abu-al-hasan-al-shadhili|Abu al-Hasan al-Shadhili]]. {cite:miftah-al-falah-saada-1332 p. 95}
