## Summary

The fourth session opens the account of the coming of revelation to the Prophet. He stresses the awe and weight of the event, then reads Khadija's words of reassurance, the role of Waraqa ibn Nawfal, and the pause in revelation, drawing lessons for reformers about the gravity of their task and the certainty of trials. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### Opening: the earlier sessions and the grandeur of the descent of revelation

- He says that the earlier sessions dealt with the time before prophethood and that this session begins the account of revelation reaching the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He holds that the descent of revelation was an awesome event after which history changed, and that even matters in the heavens changed with it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that the jinn found their access to news of the heavens cut off, searched the regions, and came upon the Prophet leading dawn prayer with his companions at the valley of Nakhla, where they listened to the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He points to the jinn's remark that they had heard a book sent down after Musa, and asks the audience to keep it in mind for later in the session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### Revelation as the last descent from heaven, and Umm Ayman's grief

- He holds that without revelation people would not know how to pray, worship, secure rights or reach the best character and speech, and that the event is the more weighty because it is the final prophecy and message to descend. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that after the Prophet died Abu Bakr and Umar visited Umm Ayman, as the Prophet used to do, and she wept; he says she explained that she wept because revelation from heaven had ended, not because she doubted what lay with God for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He says this report is found in a sound hadith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### The weight of revelation: reports of its physical effect and its reflection for reformers

- He holds that revelation came with a heavy and frightening beginning so that the manner of its arrival would reflect the greatness of what God says, and he adds that God's speech to the angels is likewise awe-inspiring. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates from Abu Hurayra, in al-Bukhari, a report describing the angels' humble reaction when God decrees a matter in heaven. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that Jibril pressed the Prophet hard three times in the cave, and that the Prophet then went on foot from Hira to Khadija's house in Makka, a distance of several kilometres, afraid for himself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates from Aisha that the Prophet's brow would run with sweat on a bitterly cold day when revelation came, from Ubada ibn al-Samit in Sahih Muslim that his face changed colour, and from al-Bukhari that Abdallah ibn Umm Maktum felt the Prophet's thigh grow heavy on his own when a verse came down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He draws for reformers the lesson that great matters must be given their weight, and that anyone taking the path of calling to God should feel it as a serious charge with burdens and trials, not a casual decision. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### Khadija's reassurance and her reasoning from God's past favours

- He says that Khadija's reaction was not mere spousal comfort but showed that God had chosen her for the Prophet, and that she reasoned in a remarkable way. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that she listed the Prophet's qualities, such as keeping kinship ties, bearing others' burdens, hosting guests and helping those in true need, and concluded that God would never disgrace him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir, in al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, explains that she knew God's fine customs with His creation: a person marked by good qualities is not disgraced in this world or the next. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He holds that reasoning from what God did earlier, in moments of confusion or fear, is a valid kind of evidence, and compares it with the verses of surat al-Duha sent when revelation paused, where God recalls the Prophet's orphanhood, guidance and provision. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He draws the point that kindness to others and helping the needy are signs of good and of divine success. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### Why the angel pressed the Prophet

- He relates that al-Khattabi explains the pressing as a test of the Prophet's patience and a training to bear the burdens of prophethood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that others say it was meant to rouse him to the weight of what would be conveyed to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He concludes that a noble path tied to the afterlife and full of burdens must hold a place of high regard in the heart of the Muslim and the reformer, and that God prepared someone to tell the Prophet of the path's burdens from the first day. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### Waraqa ibn Nawfal and the link to Musa and the earlier scriptures

- He relates that when the Prophet went to Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Waraqa called what had come the same angelic messenger that was sent to Musa, and he links this with the jinn's remark about a book after Musa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He holds that this shows a deep tie between the Prophet and Musa and between the Qur'an and the Torah, and that the Qur'an confirms both the Torah and the Gospel. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He says that early revelation, such as surat al-Muzzammil, already mentions Musa and Pharaoh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates from Ibn Kathir that Waraqa did not mention Isa because Isa's law completed and in part abrogated that of Musa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### Reports on Waraqa's standing and on Zayd ibn Amr

- He relates that Ibn Kathir cites reports, with some weakness, that the Prophet saw Waraqa in white garments in a dream or in paradise, and he notes that one chain is mursal through al-Zuhri and Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He explains that when Ibn Kathir calls such chains good he means a general acceptability for supporting use, not strict chain-grading, because they include weaker narrators such as Mujalid and al-Sha'bi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that in the same report the Prophet is asked about Zayd ibn Amr, Abu Talib and Khadija, and that Zayd, father of Sa'id ibn Zayd, is said to rise as a community on his own. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that Zayd ibn Amr abstained from the idolaters' rites, saved baby girls from burial, and told Quraysh before the Kaaba that none of them followed the religion of Ibrahim but himself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Lesson one from Waraqa: divine kindness and varied sources of assurance

- He holds that God showed kindness to the Prophet by preparing someone to confirm him at the start of prophethood, and that evidence from several sources strengthens certainty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He counts the Prophet's own experience, Khadija's reasoning from God's past favours, and Waraqa's confirmation as successive sources of reassurance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He says some reports indicate Waraqa had expected the Prophet earlier, tied to the Bahira incident, though that story's chain is disputed, and that people of knowledge from the earlier scriptures awaited a prophet then. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that a verse in surat al-Baqara says the People of the Book used to seek victory over the idolaters through a coming prophet, and that this fits his point; he also says Muhammad Abdullah Draz dwelt at length on it in al-Naba al-Azim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Waraqa's words about expulsion and the Prophet's reply

- He says that Waraqa's certainty was striking and that he must have kept a remnant of true knowledge from earlier prophets. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that Waraqa wished to be alive when the Prophet's people expelled him, and that the Prophet asked in surprise whether they would drive him out, since they had just made him judge in the rebuilding of the Kaaba and called him trustworthy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He holds that Waraqa knew from the histories of earlier prophets and from living among Quraysh that expulsion would follow, and that the Prophet's reply shows what had not occurred to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that Waraqa said no one had ever brought the like of this without being opposed, and promised strong support, which the speaker reads as support by argument and proof rather than by arms, given Waraqa's age and blindness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### Lesson two: trials of reformers

- He holds that the second lesson from Waraqa's story is that reformers must understand the divine pattern of trial. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He says that whoever follows the prophets' path will meet some of what they met, though not to their degree, since the prophets are tried most and then those most like them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates that the Prophet returned from the cave with his heart trembling, and explains an alternate wording about a muscle between shoulder and neck. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

### The pause in revelation and the report about the mountain peaks

- He relates that Waraqa died soon after and revelation then paused, and he pictures the Prophet's state after such rapid, shaking events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He says a report that the Prophet went to mountain tops in distress, comforted each time by Jibril, is not established by its chain: it is a mursal of al-Zuhri and appears in al-Bukhari only as a relayed note after Aisha's long hadith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He holds that a sound meaning may still be drawn from it, namely the Prophet's attachment to revelation and his need of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Return of revelation and Ibn Ishaq's closing passage

- He says that revelation returned with grandeur, relating the Prophet's words about seeing the angel of Hira seated on a throne between heaven and earth, after which revelation intensified and followed continuously. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He says he will treat the wisdom of the pause and the later modes of receiving revelation in coming sessions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
- He relates a passage of Ibn Ishaq in the Sira, which he recommends memorising, saying that revelation followed on the Prophet while he believed in it and bore its burdens regardless of people's pleasure or anger, and that only the resolute among the messengers can carry prophethood's weight. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep4}
