## Summary
Kitab al-Sunna by [[person/abu-bakr-al-khallal|Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Harun al-Khallal]] (234–311 AH) is, in its editor 'Adil Al Hamdan's words, one of the best-known and largest Sunni books of creed with chains of transmission. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 3} {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 7} In it al-Khallal gathered what [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] said on these matters, arranged it under chapter headings and commented in places; he did not keep to Ahmad's words alone but filled it with texts and the sayings of the early authorities. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 3}

## Its standing
- The editor quotes [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] in Majmu' al-Fatawa: "It is the fullest book recording Ahmad's views on the foundations of religion, though he has views beyond what is in it." {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 3}
- And [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn al-Qayyim]] in Ijtima' al-Juyush: a great book no scholar can do without. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 4}

## Works it contains
The editor notes that al-Khallal included whole books and letters, or much of them: {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 4}
- Ahmad's book "On Faith and the Refutation of the Murji'a", in full.
- Ahmad's [[work/al-radd-ala-al-jahmiyya|al-Radd 'ala al-Zanadiqa wa-al-Jahmiyya]] on the ambiguous verses, which several scholars who saw complete copies say he gave in full.
- [[person/abu-bakr-al-marrudhi|Al-Marrudhi]]'s book on the question of the "utterance" of the Qur'an, and much of his book against those who rejected [[person/mujahid-ibn-jabr|Mujahid]]'s report on [[question/al-maqam-al-mahmud|the Praiseworthy Station]].
- Letters of Ahmad, among them his letter to al-Mutawakkil on the Qur'an and his two letters on faith as word and deed.

## What survives
His biographers say the Kitab al-Sunna filled three large volumes; the first survives, in seven parts. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 4-5} Its surviving chapters cover: {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 5}
1. Obedience to rulers, civil strife, [[group/khawarij|the Kharijites]] and robbers.
2. The Prophet's merits and the Praiseworthy Station denied by [[group/jahmiyya|the Jahmiyya]].
3. The Companions, the caliphate and precedence.
4. Predestination and the refutation of the Qadariyya.
5. Faith and the refutation of [[group/murjia|the Murji'a]].
6. Some chapters on the Qur'an as God's uncreated speech and the refutation of the Jahmiyya, the Lafziyya and the Waqifa.

The editor thinks most of what is lost concerned the refutation of the Jahmiyya and the attributes, and cites a quotation in Tabaqat al-Hanabila from "the thirteenth part" refuting al-Karabisi on the utterance question. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 5}

## Manuscript and title
The editor knew of only one manuscript, British Museum no. 2675, of 199 leaves, copied by 'Abd al-Hadi ibn 'Abd al-Malik ibn al-Qasim, holding the first volume (seven parts), with the second and third parts lost. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 14} Its title reads "al-Musnad min Masa'il Abi 'Abd Allah Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal, in the transmission of Abu Bakr … al-Khallal", but the editor holds that the correct name is Kitab al-Sunna, as al-Khallal himself called it. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 14} It bears audition notes, among them one of 506 AH through a chain passing Abu Ishaq al-Barmaki from [[person/abu-bakr-ghulam-al-khallal|Abu Bakr 'Abd al-'Aziz Ghulam al-Khallal]] from al-Khallal. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 15}

## Reading it in Tarsus
Al-Khallal relates that he read Kitab al-Sunna in [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]] many times over the years in the congregational mosque and elsewhere; when he read it in the year ninety-two and reached the chapter on the Praiseworthy Station, he learned that some newcomers to Tarsus, followers of a man he calls "al-Tirmidhi the innovator", had rejected it; he shunned them and wrote to his teachers in Baghdad, who sent a letter that he read to his companions in Tarsus and that people copied. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 171}

## Editions
It had been printed twice before: by Dar al-Raya in 1410 AH, a doctoral thesis the editor finds full of misreadings, and by Dar al-Faruq al-Haditha in 1428 AH. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 11} Al Hamdan re-edited it and added an appendix gathering some 263 quotations from the lost parts, Ahmad's refutation of the heretics and the Jahmiyya, and a fragment of Ghulam al-Khallal's al-Sunna on affirming the attributes and passing them on as they came. {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 5-6} {cite:kitab-al-sunna-khallal-al-hamdan-2018 p. 949}
