## Summary
The Assabile archive lists this series among the video lessons of [[person/yusuf-al-qaradawi|Yusuf al-Qaradawi]], in twenty-three episodes, each on verses from one sura, from al-Fatiha to the last part of the Qur'an (juz' 'Amma) and Surat Ibrahim. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep23} The episodes are lessons al-Qaradawi gives himself, after a short opening nasheed, with no presenter. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:01:30} In some he says they fall in the last ten nights of Ramadan; he calls episode 22 the closing night of the month, and in the last episode says the gathering resumes after the Ramadan sessions. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep19 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep22 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep23 00:04:00} In one of them he says he has spent forty-six Ramadans in Qatar and that these lessons have been recorded for a long time, and asks anyone with old or rare tapes of them to contact his office. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep22 00:28:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep22 00:28:30}

The machine transcripts read clearly for the most part; what reads clearly is written here, and names and numbers that came out garbled are left out. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01}

## Episode 1: al-Fatiha
He holds that the first revelation was the five opening verses of al-'Alaq rather than al-Fatiha, and says great undertakings begin in God's name, not in the name of an emperor or a leader, as Noah began boarding the ark and Solomon his letter to the queen of Sheba. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:03:30} He holds that al-Rahman is a proper name of God, and contrasts the Torah's "Lord of Israel" with the Qur'an's "Lord of the worlds", seeing in it the universality of Islam. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:09:00} He reports his teacher Muhammad Abdullah Draz of al-Azhar as calling [[concept/al-fatiha-umm-al-kitab|al-Fatiha]] a request to God for the divine constitution, and says its plural "we", said even by someone praying alone seventeen times a day in the obligatory prayers, plants a sense of community and umma. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:26:30} He explains the straight path as the middle in creed, worship, morals and law, and divides people into three: those who knew the truth and followed it, those who knew it and turned away from it out of desire and partisanship, and those who never found it. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep01 00:30:00}

## Episode 2: the verse of fasting
He says the address to believers by the name of faith was known only in Medina, once Muslims had land and a community, that it classed people by faith rather than race and tribe, and that it occurs more than ninety times in the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:10:00} Explaining the verse prescribing fasting ([[passage/quran-2-183|al-Baqara 183]]), he says it was known to earlier nations and trains the will by giving up the lawful. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:17:30} On the traveller's [[practice/sawm-ramadan|fast]] he notes the jurists' disagreement and adopts the view of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz that the better is whichever is easier, with the example of a traveller flying west to America whose day stretches to about twenty hours. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:23:30} On the fidya verse ([[passage/quran-2-184|al-Baqara 184]]) he sets out Ibn Abbas's view on the elderly, the pregnant and the sick, and the other view that it was abrogated, and says the Qur'an takes every chance to feed the poor, as in the [[practice/kaffarat-al-yamin|expiation for oaths]]. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:28:00}

## Episode 3: the Throne Verse
He recalls that as children, when they finished al-Baqara, they held a party with sweets and sharbat and called it "the small khatma", and called finishing the whole Qur'an "the big khatma". {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:04:00} He cites the hadith of the Prophet asking Ubayy ibn Ka'b which verse of God's Book is greatest, and says verses differ in merit by how much praise and affirmation of God's oneness they hold. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:06:00} He reports the Yemeni scholar Ibn al-Wazir as holding that God's names are not limited to ninety-nine, and agrees. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:09:30} He says philosophers and the Mu'tazila describe God by detailed negation and general affirmation, while the Qur'an does the reverse. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:14:30} On [[concept/shafaa|intercession]] he rejects the pagan kind in which the intercessor imposes his will, notes that the Mu'tazila of old and Dr Mustafa Mahmoud in our time denied intercession in the hereafter, and holds that God has favour and mercy alongside justice. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:21:30} On the Kursi he reports al-Hasan al-Basri's view that it is the Throne and inclines to it, mentions a report from Ibn Abbas that it is knowledge, and says the question should not become a battle between the early and the later scholars. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep03 00:27:30}

## Episode 4: the inheritance verses
He says the pre-Islamic Arabs treated women as property, and that these rights came to women from God without any demonstration of women demanding them. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:02:30} Explaining the inheritance verse ([[passage/quran-4-11|al-Nisa' 11]]), he says the Qur'an gives detail where things do not change with time and place, and rejects "new readings" and "Islamic feminism", saying in his words that Islam has no men against women. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:08:00} He notes that some Arab laws dealt with the exclusion of grandchildren whose parent died before the grandparent through the obligatory bequest, and considers this ijtihad correct. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:13:00} With an example of an estate in Qatar he argues that a son's larger share is offset by the dower and gifts he pays, so he sees no favouring of men. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:17:00} He recalls a man he knew in his village who signed his land over to one fawning son and cut out the others; that son abandoned him and only the disinherited cared for him. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep04 00:25:00}

## Episode 5: standing firm for justice
Explaining the verse on standing firm for justice and bearing witness for God ([[passage/quran-4-135|al-Nisa' 135]]), he says justice is the aim of every revealed message. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:07:30} He cites the hadith of Bashir ibn Sa'd, who wanted the Prophet to witness a gift to his son al-Nu'man, and the Prophet's refusal to witness unequal treatment of children. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:09:30} He relates that the Prophet sent [[person/abd-allah-ibn-rawaha|Abdullah ibn Rawaha]] to assess the date crop of Khaybar; the Jews of Khaybar offered him a bribe, he refused and promised them no injustice, and they said that by such justice the heavens and the earth stand. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:21:30} He says nine verses of al-Nisa' came down in defence of a Jew wrongly accused ([[passage/quran-4-105|al-Nisa' 105]]), and sees in it God's justice to all His servants. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep05 00:27:30}

## Episode 6: apostasy, and a people God loves
Explaining the verse on whoever turns back from his religion ([[passage/quran-5-54|al-Ma'ida 54]]), he cites the hadith of Heraclius asking Abu Sufyan whether anyone leaves the faith out of dislike for it. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:04:00} He relates that claimants to prophethood rose after the Prophet's death, among them [[person/musaylima|Musaylima]] of the Banu Hanifa, Sajah, al-Aswad al-Ansi and Tulayha, that the Arabs apostatised except the people of Mecca, Medina and Ta'if, and that al-Hasan al-Basri said the verse refers to Abu Bakr and his companions. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:07:30} He gives his view on [[concept/ridda-fiqh|apostasy]]: he distinguishes one who keeps apostasy hidden in himself from one who declares it and calls others to it, which he sees as a great trial that must be resisted. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:15:00} He says missionaries met in 1977 in Basel, Switzerland, to Christianise the world, gives Afghanistan as an example, and mentions a treatise by Abu al-Hasan al-Nadwi, whom he says he knew and lived alongside, titled "An Apostasy, and No Abu Bakr for It". {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:21:30} He says the disbelievers of the verse are those hostile to this religion, not all non-Muslims. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep06 00:23:00}

## Episode 7: the closing verses of al-Hijr
He says the closing verses of al-Hijr console the Prophet for the harm the polytheists did him in Mecca ([[passage/quran-15-85|al-Hijr 85]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:01:30} He rejects a statement he attributes to the Pope of the Vatican, that in Islam God's will is absolute and bound by neither reason nor wisdom, saying Muslim scholars agree it is bound by wisdom. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:05:00} He holds that "the seven oft-repeated" are al-Fatiha rather than the seven long suras, since al-Hijr is Meccan and the long suras Medinan ([[passage/quran-15-87|al-Hijr 87]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:11:00} He says the mockers targeted weak believers who had no clan to protect them, such as Ammar ibn Yasir, Bilal ibn Rabah and Khabbab ibn al-Aratt. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:21:00} He explains "certainty" at the end of the sura as death ([[passage/quran-15-99|al-Hijr 99]]), and calls misguided the claim of some extreme Sufis that certainty is a knowledge that ends religious duties. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep07 00:30:30}

## Episode 8: those who want this world and those who want the next
He says Surat al-Isra' is also called Surat Bani Isra'il, and that a rich man is not one of the people of this world if the world is in his hand and not his heart ([[passage/quran-17-18|al-Isra' 18]], [[passage/quran-17-19|19]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:03:30} He says the Qur'an ties outcomes to what a person wills. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:04:00} He recalls country people in his village content with bread and old cheese, and tells of a Bedouin who told al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf that hunger and good health, not the cook, made the food good. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:09:00} He cites a saying attributed to Ali describing the world as beginning in weeping, its middle toil and its end perishing, and says wealthy Companions such as Uthman and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf owned the world without it owning them. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:18:00} This is what [[concept/zuhd|renunciation]] means for him. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:18:00}

## Episode 9: the prophets who called and were answered
He says Surat al-Anbiya' is Meccan and treats the story of Abraham in more detail than any other sura. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:02:30} He says Noah's trial was a trial of his mission, while [[person/ayyub|Ayyub]]'s was personal, as if he had set "the world record" in patience ([[passage/quran-21-83|al-Anbiya' 83]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:06:30} He rejects the story of worms falling from his body as one of the Isra'iliyyat, unfit for the station of prophethood. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:08:00} He says the time and place of Idris are unknown, rejects an Egyptian minister of religious endowments' claim that Idris was Osiris for want of evidence, and holds Dhu al-Kifl to be a prophet because he is listed with the prophets. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:15:30} He says [[person/yunus|Yunus]] left his town of Nineveh near Mosul, finds three principles in his prayer in the darkness ([[passage/quran-21-87|al-Anbiya' 87]]): God's oneness, declaring God free of flaw, and admitting one's fault; and says a person in distress should call not on "my master so-and-so" but on God alone. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:26:30} He notes that Ibn al-Qayyim put the prayer of Yunus first among the prayers for distress in Zad al-Ma'ad. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep09 00:30:00}

## Episode 10: the slander affair
He says Surat al-Nur is a Medinan sura of rulings and manners, then tells the story of the slander (ifk) ([[passage/quran-24-11|al-Nur 11]]): a lie invented about [[person/aisha|Aisha]] and [[person/safwan-ibn-al-muattal|Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami]] on the campaign of al-Muraysi', spread by the head of the hypocrites, Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:12:30} He tells of her lost necklace, of her litter being loaded without anyone noticing she was absent, and of Safwan finding her and not speaking a word to her. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:11:00} He says revelation came only after a full month, and sees in this an answer to those who claim Muhammad made up revelation. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:19:00} He lists the three penalties for slander: flogging, rejection of testimony and the name of wrongdoer ([[passage/quran-24-4|al-Nur 4]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:23:00} He says Aisha narrated more than a thousand hadith, that Ibn al-Qayyim counted her among the Companions who gave many fatwas, and that al-Zarkashi wrote a book on her corrections of other Companions. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:32:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep10 00:33:00}

## Episode 11: the servants of the Most Merciful
He says reflecting on the Qur'an is among the greatest acts of worship, lists the Qur'an's portraits of the God-fearing, the believers and the people of understanding, and then explains the portrait of the servants of the Most Merciful ([[passage/quran-25-63|al-Furqan 63]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:02:30} He says a person's walk reveals his character, cites Abu al-Ala al-Ma'arri's line about treading lightly because the earth's surface is made of bodies, and says that in his forty-five years and more in Qatar cemeteries have filled and been closed. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:08:30} He distinguishes squandering, spending on the forbidden even a little, from excess, going beyond the limit in the permitted ([[passage/quran-25-67|al-Furqan 67]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:19:00} He tells how Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz ordered a relative who had bought a ring stone for a thousand dirhams to sell it, feed the hungry with its price and wear an iron ring instead. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:21:00} He says a child who comes top at school but does not pray is no comfort to the eyes ([[passage/quran-25-74|al-Furqan 74]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep11 00:30:00}

## Episode 12: the People of the Book who believed

## Episode 13: the miracle of the Qur'an

## Episode 14: the verse of choice

## Episode 15: Abraham and the son to be sacrificed
He says al-Saffat alone tells the story of the sacrifice ([[passage/quran-37-102|al-Saffat 102]]), and defines the sound heart as one free of association, hypocrisy, showing off, arrogance and love of the world. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:05:30} He says the one weak in argument resorts to violence. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:13:00} He says the dreams of prophets are true and are revelation, and that the son's answer to his father, to go ahead with what he was commanded without mentioning himself, is stronger than Moses' words that he would be patient. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:23:00} He holds that the son was [[person/ismail|Isma'il]], saying that Isaac's name was added to the Torah's text out of envy of the Arabs and that some Muslim scholars took it from People of the Book such as Ka'b al-Ahbar. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:28:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:29:00} His evidence is that the event took place at Mecca and that the good news of Isaac comes after the story of the sacrifice, and he calls weak the hadith attributed to the Prophet that he is the son of the two sacrificed. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep15 00:31:00}

## Episode 16: the believer of Pharaoh's family

## Episode 17: Surat al-Shura
He says Surat al-Shura stresses consultation as a foundation of Islamic life. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:02:00} He reports al-Ghazali that sincere repentance erases the trace of sin ([[passage/quran-42-25|al-Shura 25]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:03:30} As an example of plentiful provision that breeds arrogance he gives America, a whole continent, against Bangladesh with a hundred and seventy million on a small piece of land ([[passage/quran-42-27|al-Shura 27]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:09:00} He says Islam prescribed the prayer for rain because God alone sends rain, and rejects the jahiliyya belief in rain-stars ([[passage/quran-42-28|al-Shura 28]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:14:00} He describes the galaxies and the expanding universe, disagrees with Ibn Kathir in counting angels among the "creatures", and says the plain sense of the verse allows living beings in the heavens ([[passage/quran-42-29|al-Shura 29]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep17 00:23:00}

## Episode 18: the rules of fighting and of captives

## Episode 19: al-Rahman and the balance

## Episode 20: fay' and self-sacrifice

## Episode 21: Blessed is He in whose hand is the dominion
