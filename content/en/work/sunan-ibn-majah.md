## Overview
The Sunan of [[person/ibn-majah|Ibn Majah]], Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Qazwini (d. 273 AH). For most later scholars it is the sixth of the [[concept/kutub-al-sitta|Six Books]]. {cite:sunan-ibn-majah}

It is distinguished by its hadiths on asceticism and tribulations, and by a chapter not found in the two Sahihs. Its best-known edition is that of Muhammad Fu'ad Abd al-Baqi (Cairo, 1952–1953 CE). {cite:sunan-ibn-majah}

## Structure and size
Sunnah.com gives Sunan Ibn Majah as 4,341 hadith in 37 books, and calls it the sixth of the six canonical collections {cite:sunnah-com ibnmajah}.

## In «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'» by Abu Ishaq al-Heweny
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says the first book he began work on was a takhrij of Sunan Ibn Majah, in Shaykh Hamid Ibrahim's library during his military service, because the shaykh told him Ibn Majah had not been served and asked him to do the takhrij and leave his copy for the library's readers. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep10 00:15:30} He then turned from it to [[work/sunan-al-nasai|Sunan al-Nasa'i]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep11 00:11:00} In the second-last episode he says he completed six volumes of his medium-length takhrij of Ibn Majah, up to the end of the Book of Prayer, and stopped. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep29 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep29 00:14:30}
