## Summary

Ahmed Elsayed turns to the events between Uhud and the Trench. He treats the tragedy of Bi'r Ma'una, the Prophet's grief and the prayer of supplication that followed, and then the expulsion of Banu al-Nadir, with the lessons he draws for reformers, ending on the hypocrites who abandoned their allies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### No empty stretches in the Prophet's life

- He says that this series aims at the methodological and reforming lessons of the Prophet's biography, and that a student's formation is incomplete if the biography is not central to it, however much law and creed he studies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He holds that the biography shows no empty periods: each year holds many events, and biographers mostly record only the large public ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He draws the lesson that a reformer who follows the Prophet should not leave gaps of idleness in his own life, and notes that after a blow like Uhud, with the city wounded, material reckoning would suggest a year of rest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### Events between Uhud and the Trench

- He relates that about two or three months after Uhud the Prophet sent a detachment under Abu Salama, who had been wounded at Uhud; the wound reopened on his return and he died. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He says that the Prophet then married Umm Salama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He says that Bi'r Ma'una and the affair of Banu al-Nadir are the two large events he will treat, and that scholars differ on their dating. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that Urwa ibn al-Zubayr and al-Zuhri placed Banu al-Nadir before Uhud, between Badr and Uhud, while Ibn Ishaq placed both events after Uhud; he follows Ibn Ishaq, who also makes the first event a cause of the second. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The request that led to Bi'r Ma'una

- He relates, following the biographers, that Abu al-Bara Amir ibn Malik came to the Prophet and asked for a group of Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He says that some reports give the purpose as teaching his people, while another report says they asked for help against an enemy and that seventy were sent; he adds that some researchers prefer the teaching account because the men were known as the readers, and that he thinks the teaching report is in Sahih Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He says that the readers were people of worship and knowledge who cut wood by day and prayed by night, and cites Ibn Hajar for the readers being scholars given to worship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### Specialisation among reformers

- He draws the lesson that reformers have different specialities: some Companions were known for one field and others for another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He notes that the Prophet repeatedly chose the same men to lead expeditions, naming Zayd ibn Haritha, who led around nine to eleven, and Khalid ibn al-Walid after he accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He holds that formation should allow variety rather than put everyone at the same level and in the same field. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### The treachery at Bi'r Ma'una

- He relates that the party was led by al-Mundhir ibn Amr, whom he names with hesitation, and included Haram ibn Milhan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that on the way groups of Banu Sulaym, Ri'l and Dhakwan among them, surrounded the smaller party, which had set out for teaching and not for fighting, and betrayed and killed them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that Haram ibn Milhan was the first to be struck, with a spear, and that his last words declared his success. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that the Companions supplicated that their word reach the Prophet, and that one man survived who had been left for dead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri and a companion, tending camels, saw birds circling over the bodies; the companion went down and was killed, and Amr was captured by Amir ibn al-Tufayl, who freed him on learning he was of Mudar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that on his return Amr killed two men of Banu Amir who held a safe-conduct from the Prophet, believing he was avenging the seventy, and that the Prophet told him he would pay their blood-money. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### The Prophet's grief and the supplication prayer

- He holds that a person may suffer more through losses among others than through his own, and asks whether the loss of his companions weighed more on the Prophet than the death of his children, all of whom except Fatima died in his lifetime; he says he knows no text settling it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that Anas reported the Prophet grieved over nothing as much as the people of Bi'r Ma'una. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that the Prophet made the supplication of calamity for a month against Ri'l and Dhakwan, in the dawn prayer according to the sound reports, and in all prayers in a report in the Musnad of Ahmad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that a verse about these men was revealed and recited by the Companions and later abrogated, and that this is reported in the sound collections. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### Trials of the prophets and loved ones sent forward

- He says that Bi'r Ma'una, though smaller than Badr, Uhud or the Trench, matters for its emotional weight. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates a report from Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, issued by Ahmad, that the prophets are tried most severely and each person according to his religion, and counts the loss of loved ones among the Prophet's trials. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He holds that the Prophet did not keep his dearest people back from danger, citing Zayd ibn Haritha, whom he describes as beloved to him and the father of Usama, advanced in command until he was killed at Mu'tah. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that at Badr the Prophet put forward Ali, Hamza and Ubayda ibn al-Harith, all kinsmen, and that Ubayda was wounded in the duel and died on the way back. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He draws the lesson of putting the aims of the next world before the desires of the self and of patience in grief. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### Banu al-Nadir and the plot against the Prophet

- He relates, following Ibn Ishaq, that the Prophet went to Banu al-Nadir to seek their help with the blood-money for the two men, since they had an alliance with Banu Amir, and that he did not then have means to feed his Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that they welcomed him but then proposed that someone climb the wall he leaned on and drop a millstone on him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that the Prophet rose and went back to Medina without telling Abu Bakr and the others, who followed after a man told them he had entered the city, and that he informed them that God had revealed their plot. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He places the Banu al-Nadir quarter near Quba, less than a kilometre from the Medina district he names, and now within the city. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### The siege and the palm trees

- He holds that the question of the place of direct revelation in the Prophet's worldly actions, especially in fighting, deserves separate treatment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He says that the Prophet marched and besieged Banu al-Nadir, who thought themselves wealthy and learned and the Arabs ignorant, and trusted in their fortresses; he notes that Medina itself had no encircling wall. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that the siege lasted six or fifteen days, and that the Muslims cut and burned some of their palms. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that Surat al-Hashr was revealed about Banu al-Nadir and that Ibn Abbas called it the surah of al-Nadir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He explains that the verse on cut or standing palms means permission both in law and in decree, and distinguishes this from divine permission in decree alone, as in what is said of magic harming no one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### The expulsion and the fay

- He relates that the besieged surrendered quickly and asked for exile, and that the Prophet allowed it on condition that they carry no weapons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that they took what they could, even door frames, and tore down their own houses, which the Muslims partly wrecked too, and he links this to the terror the Prophet was granted according to a sound report. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He explains that the property was fay and not spoils, since fay is what is gained without fighting and is not shared among the fighters as spoils are, apart from the fifth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that the Prophet gave it to the poor and the Emigrants, keeping a year's provision for his family, and that Abu Dawud has a report that he gave it to the Emigrants and not the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He says that afterwards the Emigrants began returning what the Ansar had lent them, and mentions the story of Umm Ayman and the returned palms. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}

### The hypocrites who failed their allies

- He holds that wherever work for the faith appears, hypocrites appear too, and that the Qur'an often mentions them together with fighting, as in Surat al-Tawba, Al Imran on Uhud and Surat al-Hashr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He relates that in Surat al-Hashr the hypocrites promised the People of the Book to leave with them if expelled and to support them, and did neither. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
- He characterises the hypocrites as cowardly and loud, and concludes that these events ended in victory, honour and fay for the believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep22}
