## Summary
'Fi Hubb al-Mustafa' ('In the Love of al-Mustafa') is a short television programme presented by [[person/abla-al-kahlawi|Abla al-Kahlawi]]; the Arabic Wikipedia says it was broadcast in the month of Ramadan 1428 AH on the al-Risala channel. {cite:wikipedia-abla-al-kahlawi-ar} The Assabile archive keeps twenty-seven video episodes of it, each about ten minutes long, which she opens with the blessing on the Prophet and closes with a greeting, followed by a song that returns at the end of every episode. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:10:00} The episodes move from the meaning of love to the life of [[person/muhammad|the Prophet Muhammad]] from his birth to his death, and then to what she calls the 'essential axes' of his message: the hijra, ethics, education and women. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:02:30}

Episodes 5, 10, 12 and 24 repeat in the recording the text of episodes 4, 9, 11 and 23 respectively, so each of those lessons is described here only once. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep05 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep10 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep12 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep24 00:00:30} The events of the Prophet's life on this page are the speaker's account as recorded and are attributed to her; the independent accounts of these events are on the pages of the persons and events linked here.

## The episodes
**Episode 1: what love is.** She begins by saying that the Prophet came with a message of love that taught people how a brother loves his brother and how people love one another, recalling the hadith on the sanctity of a Muslim's blood, property and honour and the hadith that makes wanting for one's brother what one wants for oneself part of faith. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:01:00} She defines love as one of the gentlest kinds of divine mercy, the basis of life's movement, of the harmony of existence and of the bonds between people, and says: 'love builds, and hatred ruins'. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:02:30} She sets against this what she calls 'love in the bad times', the age of globalisation and genetic engineering, which in her view wears the mask of mercy while it stirs up conflict and offers new means of death, and she calls on people to learn love from the Prophet. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep01 00:09:30}

**Episode 2: love and living together.** She speaks of love as a call to coexistence, recalling the hadiths that people are not won over by wealth but by a cheerful face and good character, and that goodness is an easy thing. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:01:00} She gives the example of an employee passed over for promotion who spends his days in rancour, and works out his life for him: sixty years on average, of which thirty are sleep, six are siesta and fourteen come before religious responsibility, leaving only ten. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:02:30} Love, she says, is pure only when it passes through its source, which is God; whoever loves his children for God's sake will not feed them what is unlawful, and whoever loves money for its own sake is wretched. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:08:00} She describes the Prophet sharing the work of his household and relates from Aisha that he smiled and laughed often and that his character was the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep02 00:06:00}

**Episode 3: the essence of love.** Love, she says, is a gift from God that joins hearts; it is a love of souls that time does not cut off, so that later generations pray for the earlier ones. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:02:30} She relates the hadith of the Prophet's longing for 'his brothers' who would believe in him without having seen him, and a young man of the generation after the Companions who wished he had known the Prophet so that he could have carried him on his shoulders. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:04:30} She tells the story of Abu Idris al-Khawlani and the young man in Syria to whom he said 'I love you for God's sake', and the hadith of the call in heaven when God loves a servant. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep03 00:07:00} (See [[concept/hubb-allah|love for God]].)

**Episode 4 (repeated as episode 5): why we love the Prophet, and his choosing.** Muslims love him, she says, because God loved him, and she lists what she finds in the Qur'an in his praise: the vindication of his reason, heart, tongue and sight, the opening of his breast, the oath by the town where he was born, and the many names given to him; many names, she says, show the honour of the one named. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep05 00:03:00} In her view he was chosen unlettered and orphaned so that no one could say he took his knowledge from an earlier culture or civilisation, and without any teacher he would climb to [[place/ghar-hira|the cave of Hira]] to worship, reflect and look towards the Ka'ba. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep04 00:06:30}

**Episode 6: Amina and Thuwayba.** Before speaking of why people love the Prophet she begins with his mothers: [[person/amina-bint-wahb|Amina bint Wahb]], her light pregnancy after her husband's death, her visions and the light she saw on the day of his birth, and then [[person/thuwayba|Thuwayba]], who nursed him. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep06 00:08:00}

**Episode 7: Halima, Baraka and Fatima bint Asad.** She tells the story of [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima al-Sa'diyya]], who took the orphan and saw blessing come with him, then Amina's journey to Medina and her death at al-Abwa', the care of [[person/umm-ayman|Baraka (Umm Ayman)]] for him on the road, and [[person/fatima-bint-asad|Fatima bint Asad]] in the house of Abu Talib. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:08:30} She ends by saying she wishes she could have been one of his mothers. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep07 00:09:30}

**Episode 8: truthfulness.** His Companions loved him, she says, for his truthfulness and trustworthiness, the clarity of his life and his commitment to what he preached, and she cites the witness of his opponents, among them Abu Jahl's saying that they did not call him a liar but denied what he had brought, and the call from [[event/safa-proclamation|al-Safa]]. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:04:00} For her the strongest proof of his truthfulness is the love and trust of his Companions, some of whom had known him for ten years before the mission, merchants who travelled to Syria and Yemen and people skilled in reading faces who could tell an honest man from a liar. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep08 00:07:00}

**Episode 9 (repeated as episode 10): certainty that does not bend.** From the start, she says, he faced his people with the heart of the message, and his followers did the same: Abu Bakr; [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr al-Ghifari]], who declared the testimony of faith in the assembly of Quraysh at the Ka'ba and was beaten; and [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Mas'ud]], who recited to them the opening of the sura al-Rahman. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:02:30} She relates the message of [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]] asking him to spare himself and his uncle, and his answer that he would not leave this matter even if the sun were set in his right hand and the moon in his left. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:05:30} She speaks of the torture of Bilal, of Ammar and his mother Sumayya, and then of [[event/boycott-of-banu-hashim|the boycott]] and the Year of Sorrow. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:08:30}

**Episode 11 (repeated as episode 12): the axes of the message, and the start of the hijra.** She announces the four axes, saying of the fourth that the one who truly freed women was the Prophet. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:03:00} Migration, she says, was the lot of all the prophets, and she lists Adam, Noah, Abraham, Lot, Ishmael, Joseph and Moses; the Prophet migrated after thirteen years spent among Quraysh. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:04:30} In her reading the hijra began on the day of [[person/khadija|Khadija]] and of [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa ibn Nawfal]]'s words that his people would drive him out, and she distinguishes between the 'launching' of the call in Mecca and its 'setting out' from Medina. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep11 00:08:30}

**Episode 13: al-Aqaba and Mus'ab.** She speaks of the group of mockers led by al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, of the Prophet offering himself to the tribes after al-Ta'if, then of his meeting with a few men from Medina, [[event/first-pledge-of-aqaba|the first pledge]], the sending of [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]] and [[event/second-pledge-of-aqaba|the second pledge]]. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:10:00} The faith, she says, found in the Ansar people who gave it their time, their lives, their land and their wealth, and the people of Medina welcomed him with the song 'Tala'a al-badru 'alayna'. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:11:00}

**Episode 14: the meaning of the hijra, Abyssinia and Abu Bakr.** An event, she says, needs a time and a place; the Arabs dated by events such as the bursting of the dam of al-Arim and the Year of the Elephant, and the calendar was dated from the hijra rather than from the mission because the hijra began the abode of faith. She sees in Medina the three elements of a state: a people of Emigrants, Ansar and some Jews, a territory, and an authority, the Prophet. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:02:30} One should say that he 'emigrated' (hajara) and not that he 'abandoned' (hajara as desertion) Mecca, she says, because he did not leave it of his own will but was driven to migrate. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:03:00} She then tells of [[journey/first-migration-to-abyssinia|the migration to Abyssinia]], Abu Bakr's joy at accompanying him on the hijra to Medina, the money he took and Asma's device with her blind grandfather Abu Quhafa. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep14 00:10:30}

**Episode 15: the cave, and Ali.** She tells what Abu Bakr did in [[place/ghar-thawr|the cave of Thawr]], Ali's night in the Prophet's bed on the night of [[event/hijra-night-escape|the escape]], and the banner of [[event/battle-of-khaybar|Khaybar]]; she recalls Ali's conversion as a boy, the report of the merchant who saw the Prophet, Khadija and Ali at prayer, and Ali's knowledge of his future killer, Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:12:00}

**Episode 16: ethics in worship.** She does not like, she says, dividing the message into creed, worship, dealings and ethics, because ethics runs through all of it from tawhid itself: tawhid is justice and associating partners with God is wrongdoing. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:02:00} She finds an ethical core in every act of worship: prayer restrains from indecency and wrong, and is a support the weary turn to, as the Prophet would ask Bilal to give them rest by it; fasting forms the will and aims at God-consciousness, and only God sees the one who fasts; zakat purifies wealth, refines the soul and brings blessing; and the hajj is a 'rehearsal' of life and of the passage to the hereafter. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep16 00:10:00}

**Episode 17: ethics in economy and politics.** The tragedy of the age, in her view, is that economic dealings have been emptied of ethics, and she criticises what she calls 'instrumental reason', which invents a tool without asking what it will be used for. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:03:30} She recalls the prohibition of hoarding and of cheating, the duty of a merchant to show the defect in his goods, and the hadith that on the Day of Judgement a servant will be asked about his life, his youth and his wealth. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:07:00} Politics in Islam, she says, is ethical and not 'Machiavellian': no end justifies the means; she recalls the duties of returning trusts, judging with justice, keeping covenants, not transgressing in war and feeding the captive. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep17 00:08:30}

**Episode 18: the marks of the message's ethics.** She says that its ethics are reasoned and universal, binding towards Muslims and others alike, so that cheating and theft are lawful towards no one, and she warns Muslims living in non-Muslim countries against cheating those countries' laws. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:04:00} She calls them suited to reality, measuring necessity by its extent, and realistic, not opposing human nature in its love of adornment and possession. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:05:30} She relates from Ali the counsel to hold to good manners, and divides patience into three: patience in keeping from the forbidden, patience in obedience and patience under trial. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep18 00:07:30}

**Episode 19: humility.** Tribal society, she says, boasted of lineage, wealth and horses, and the Prophet forbade arrogance and lived humbly although the world lay open to him. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep19 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep19 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep19 00:03:00} She relates that Umar found him asleep on a mat that had marked his side, and that he said he was in this world like a rider who rests in the shade of a tree and then leaves it; and that he told a man who trembled before him that he was no king but the son of a woman who ate dried meat in Mecca. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep19 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep19 00:05:00} She describes his courtesy in a gathering, in a handshake and in listening, his service to his household and his mending of his own sandals. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep19 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep19 00:06:00}

**Episode 20: generosity.** Wealth, she says, belongs to God and people are trustees of it, and charity does not lessen wealth but adds blessing to it. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:03:00} She recalls that the Prophet's house might hold nothing but dates and water, as Aisha said, and that Abdullah ibn Umar would not eat without an orphan or a poor man beside him. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:05:00} She tells the story of [[person/said-ibn-amir-al-jumahi|Sa'id ibn Amir]] in Syria, the detachment of Abu al-Darda', who was a merchant, and that the Prophet was most generous in Ramadan. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep20 00:09:30}

**Episode 21: education.** The tragedy of the age, she says, is that people learn a great deal without being formed, while the Prophet defined his mission as being sent as a teacher; purification comes before instruction, since one who has not been formed cannot receive knowledge. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:02:30} She measures the perfection of an educator by three things: his power to raise a person from a lower state to a higher one, the breadth of the circle of those he changed, and the constancy of his method and its fitness for the generations. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:07:00} She says that some orientalists counted him the greatest reformer in history, without naming any of them. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep21 00:08:00} (needs_source: the speaker names neither these orientalists nor their books.)

**Episode 22: 'the school of Muhammad'.** She gives two examples of people changed by the Prophet's formation. The first is Umar ibn al-Khattab, who had been harsh and became the just Commander of the Faithful who called his governors to account for their wealth before and after their appointment, asked Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman whether he was among the hypocrites, accepted that people might set him straight with their swords and gave way to a woman's objection. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:08:00} The second is [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Mas'ud]], the thin hired labourer whose legs the Prophet said weighed more with God than Uhud, who became the dean of the Qur'an's reciters, took seventy suras from the Prophet and founded a school of fiqh from which Abu Hanifa drew. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep22 00:10:30}

**Episode 23 (repeated as episode 24): women.** Islam, she says, honoured woman as mother, daughter, wife, sister and widow. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:08:00} She recalls the counsel on the mother and on parents; for the daughter, the hadith on treating children equally in gifts and even in kisses; for the wife, that marriage is a contract of consent concluded only with her agreement, that she may set conditions in the contract that do not contradict its basis, such as that he not take her travelling or not marry another wife, and that she has the right of khul'. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:08:00} She relates the hadith of the woman who hurries to the gate of Paradise with the Prophet because she devoted herself to her orphans, and says that in his time no widow or divorced woman remained unmarried. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:09:00} She ends with the Muslim woman who lost her father, brother and husband and, seeing the Prophet safe, said that every misfortune after him was light. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep23 00:10:30} (See [[concept/huquq-al-walidayn|the rights of parents]].)

**Episode 25: the fruits of the message: tawhid and the hereafter.** The first fruit, she says, was tawhid in a world that worshipped idols, trees, the sun and the moon; the second was reminding people of the Last Day, so that they work for the hereafter without forgetting this world. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:05:00} She relates the hadith of the one who makes the hereafter his concern, the generosity of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf with his caravans, Abu al-Darda' sleeping on a hide, and Umar's counsel to call oneself to account. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:09:00} She quotes Ali telling the world to go and deceive someone else. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep25 00:13:30}

**Episode 26: personal responsibility, equality and clear-sighted obedience.** The message, she says, brought the principle that every soul is pledged for what it has earned, so that no one bears another's burden, and a single humanity in a world of ranks; she mentions the castes of India and the Persian kings' claim to divine blood. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:02:00} She recalls that the first Companions joined the nobles of Quraysh with Suhayb the Roman, Salman the Persian and Bilal the Abyssinian, Abu Bakr's address on taking office, and Abu Dharr dressing as his servant dressed. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:06:00} She sees in the prohibition of wine an example of 'clear-sighted obedience': wine was the life of the people and their greatest trade, and when the prohibition came they poured it out until it ran in the streets of Medina. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:09:30} Among the institutions of the Prophet's time she names the leaders (nuqaba'), the Seventy and the waqf. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep26 00:11:00}

**Episode 27: the death.** She tells of [[event/death-of-the-prophet|the Prophet's illness and death]] and the grief of his Companions, and closes with a call for love of the Prophet to mean reviving his Sunna and his way, not only praise poems and birthday celebrations; for people to love one another and stop the bloodshed; and to spend on education and on scientific and technical excellence because they are 'the language of the age'. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:10:00}

## About these recordings
The machine transcripts of the episodes are clear for the most part, with errors in some names and numbers; what did not read clearly has not been written, and the verses and hadiths in the recordings are given by their meaning, not their text. The numbers and dates the speaker gives are her statements as recorded.
