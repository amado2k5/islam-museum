## Summary

A question from a YouTube creator who earns from ads and filters them, in which Jordan's General Iftaa Department answered that the ruling follows whether forbidden ads predominate or are rare. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3804}

## The question

The questioner said he posts videos on YouTube and earns by allowing ads, filtering them to block pornographic and immoral material, though some forbidden content may still appear incidentally; he asked whether his earnings are lawful. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3804}

## The answer

The Department answered that Islam forbids anything that directly aids what is forbidden, and promoting a forbidden ad is such aid, so the creator must filter the ads and remove forbidden ones. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3804} It said that if he cannot control the removal of forbidden ads, or they appear predominantly despite filtering, earning from them is forbidden, since the ruling follows what predominates. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3804} If they appear only rarely after filtering, or the forbidden element is incidental, such as images in ads for lawful products, it held that this is at the least doubtful and better avoided, and advised giving part of the earnings in charity to clear one's account. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3804}
