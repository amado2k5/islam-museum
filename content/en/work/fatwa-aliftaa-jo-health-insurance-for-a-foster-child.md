## Summary

A question on adding a foster child to health insurance, in which Jordan's General Iftaa Department answered that there is no sharia objection if the regulations allow it, and that doing so carries great reward. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3827}

## The question

The questioner asked whether a foster child may be added to health insurance. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3827}

## The answer

The Department answered that there is no sharia objection to adding foster children to the health insurance and education the state offers the fostering family, if the regulations and laws allow it, and that the family earns great reward, since the sharia urges care for such children. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3827} It quoted al-Nawawi's Rawdat al-Talibin that the rules on foundlings oblige the finder to protect and care for the child, and that jurists use the word hadana here in the sense of protection and upbringing. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3827} It said that easing procedures for fostering families, especially those without children of their own, is one way of protecting these children from neglect, and that if they have no means their maintenance falls on the public treasury, and failing that on the Muslims as a communal obligation, as it quoted from al-Nawawi. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3827} It closed by urging official bodies and citizens to work together to care for foster children and ease procedures for fostering families. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3827}

Jordan's General Iftaa Department published this fatwa on aliftaa.jo as no. 3827, dated 9 November 2023. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3827} The museum records this text as a historical document attributed to the body or scholar that issued it; it issues no ruling of its own.
