## Summary
*Kitab al-Tawhid wa-Ithbat Sifat al-Rabb* ("The Book of God's Oneness and the Affirmation of the Lord's Attributes") is the book of [[person/ibn-khuzayma-muhammad|Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Ishaq ibn Khuzayma]] of Nishapur (223–311 AH); this is its title in the manuscripts. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 45} In it he brings, with full chains, the Qur'anic verses and reports by which he affirms God's attributes, giving each attribute chapters whose headings state the point to be proved, in the manner of the hadith scholars who wrote on creed before and after him. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 57-60} His editor counts more than 750 reports besides dozens of further chains, and notes that many come by routes outside the six books, so that the work acts almost as a parallel collection to them. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 61-62}

## Why it was written
In his introduction Ibn Khuzayma says that for a long time he disliked writing anything touched by theological disputation, gave most of his effort to books of law, and thought the debates with "the people of whims" held in his gatherings were enough without books on the subject. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 9} Then something happened in his affairs that kept him from appearing in public and teaching, and he heard from some young students of hadith, who perhaps attended the gatherings of those he calls the Mu'attila and the Qadari Mu'tazila, things that made him fear they would turn from the truth; so he resolved to write a book joining two kinds of knowledge: the affirmation of God's prior decree, and faith in God's attributes. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 9-10} He says: "I began the Book of Decree and dictated it, and this is the Book of Oneness." {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 48}

The editor holds it to be one of his last works, written in 309 AH, two years before his death. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 68} The manuscript chain agrees: his grandson Abu Tahir Muhammad ibn al-Fadl says his grandfather transmitted it to them in Rabi' al-Akhir 309 AH. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 53}

## His condition and method
- Ibn Khuzayma states that on God's attributes he argues only from what is in the Qur'an or reported from the Prophet with firm, sound chains, upright from upright and unbroken, and that he does not argue from mursal reports, from weak reports, or from opinion and analogy; the same condition is written on the first page of the Kastamonu and Köprülü manuscripts. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 65-66}
- He begins with the Self (nafs), then the Face, then the Hand and two Hands and the creation of Adam, then God's highness and His being in the heaven, the descent, speech, God's speaking to Moses and the manner of revelation, the Qur'an as God's speech, and then the vision and intercession. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 11} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 24} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 254} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 346-404}
- He sometimes sets out his opponents' views and answers them; in the chapter on the Self he says [[group/jahmiyya|the Jahmiyya]] disbelieved in these verses and reports, and that some of them claimed that God's ascribing a self to Himself is the ascription of a created thing. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 60}
- He repeats one report under several chapters, according to the phrase in it that bears on each heading, and reconciles reports that seem to conflict. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 60}
- He composed by dictation, and often refers to what he had dictated in his other books: the Book of Decree, the Description of the Bliss of Paradise, the Book of Faith and the chapters on bequests. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 60-61} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 437}
- On [[concept/nuzul|the descent]] he says he bears witness to what the reports contain, as one who affirms and believes, "without describing the manner", because the Prophet did not describe it. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 39}

## The vision and intercession
One chapter is on the believers' vision of their Lord on the Day of Resurrection, with the Prophet's comparison of it to seeing the moon in this world ([[concept/ruyat-allah|the vision of God in the hereafter]]). {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 437} Chapter 47 is on [[question/did-the-prophet-see-his-lord|the Prophet's seeing his Lord]] and chapter 48 on the reports from [[person/aisha|'A'isha]] denying it; in them he leans towards affirming the vision as [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn 'Abbas]] held, and his editor disagrees. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 477-479} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 548} Then come the chapter on God's laughter and the chapters on [[concept/shafaa|intercession]]: the intercession God gave the Prophet alone, and its being the first of the intercessions. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 563} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 589-600}

## Its standing and its readers
- The editor counts it among the most important Sunni books of creed, for its author's early date and his connected chains, and notes that many who later set out the creed of the early generations relied on it. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 61}
- Among the witnesses he gathers to its attribution are quotations by [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] in Fath al-Bari, [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] in al-Mizan, [[person/ibn-abi-al-izz-al-hanafi|Ibn Abi al-'Izz]] in his commentary on al-Tahawi's creed, al-Suyuti, Hafiz al-Hakami and al-Saffarini; Brockelmann, Sezgin, Kahhala, Hajji Khalifa and al-Baghdadi list it. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 51-52}

## Criticisms
:::perspectives
- **Sunni, following the early generations — the editor's view**: while praising the book, al-Shahwan lists points against it. In form, it multiplies chapters for one subject, as with fourteen chapters on the Hand, some holding a single report. In substance: it narrates from some weak, unknown and abandoned transmitters despite its stated condition, though mostly as supporting chains; it interprets the report [[question/the-hadith-adam-created-in-his-form|"God created Adam in his form"]]; it includes some very weak reports; it explains the negation in [[passage/quran-6-103|al-An'am 103]] in a way he rejects; and it hesitates in judging some reports. He says these points "do not lessen the book's standing". {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 62-71}
- **The view of [[person/fakhr-al-din-al-razi|Fakhr al-Din al-Razi]] (Sunni, Ash'ari), as the editor quotes it**: commenting on [[passage/quran-42-11|al-Shura 11]], al-Razi said Ibn Khuzayma had cited and objected to his school's argument from this verse in the book "he named al-Tawhid, which is in truth the book of shirk", and described its author in harsh words. This is taken from the editor's study and has not yet been checked in al-Razi's tafsir itself. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 39-40}
- **The view of [[person/muhammad-zahid-al-kawthari|Muhammad Zahid al-Kawthari]] (Sunni, Hanafi-Maturidi), as the editor quotes it**: in his notes to al-Bayhaqi's al-Asma' wa-al-Sifat he held that Ibn Khuzayma had harmed himself by writing this book. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 40}
- The editor answers both sharply, arguing that the book says nothing beyond what is in the books of al-Bukhari, Muslim, Ahmad, al-Darimi, Ibn Manda and al-Lalaka'i. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 40-41}
:::

## Manuscripts and editions
- The editor knows five manuscripts: two in Turkey (Kastamonu no. 3025, and Köprülü), one in Egypt (the Taymuriyya), part of a copy in Germany and part of one in the Escorial in Spain. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 75}
- The book is transmitted through [[person/al-sabuni|Abu 'Uthman Isma'il ibn 'Abd al-Rahman al-Sabuni]] from the author's grandson Abu Tahir Muhammad ibn al-Fadl; among its chains are that of [[person/jamal-al-din-al-mizzi|al-Mizzi]] from Zaynab bint Makki, that of Ibn Hajar, and a hearing note at the end of the German copy records al-Mizzi's reading of the whole book before Umm Muhammad Zaynab bint 'Umar in sessions in Baalbek, the last on Friday 19 Shawwal 693 AH. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 52-54}
- It was first printed in 1353 AH at the Muniriyya press, without editing and checked against only a few pages of the Taymuriyya copy, then reprinted from that printing; the editor describes both as full of omissions and corruptions. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 75} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 80-81}
- 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Ibrahim al-Shahwan then edited it from the manuscripts, and Dar al-Rushd in Riyadh published his first edition in 1408 AH/1988 CE, with a foreword by Salih al-Fawzan. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 6} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988-study p. 4}
