## Summary

*The Cambridge Companion to the Qur'an* is a collective volume edited by Jane Dammen McAuliffe and published by Cambridge University Press in December 2006. {cite:openlibrary-cambridge-companion-quran-2006} The lending-library record describes it as xv + 332 pages, with indexes. {cite:archive-org-cambridge-companion-quran-2006}

## Publication and editions

It is divided into five parts: the formation of the Qur'anic text, description and analysis, transmission and dissemination, interpretations and intellectual traditions, and contemporary readings. {cite:archive-org-cambridge-companion-quran-2006} Its contributors include Fred M. Donner on the historical context, Claude Gilliot on the creation of a fixed text, Harald Motzki on alternative accounts of the Qur'an's formation, Daniel A. Madigan on themes and topics, and Angelika Neuwirth on structural, linguistic and literary features. {cite:archive-org-cambridge-companion-quran-2006} Later parts include William A. Graham and Navid Kermani on recitation and aesthetic reception, Sheila Blair and Jonathan Bloom on inscriptions in art and architecture, Andrew Rippin on Western scholarship, Asma Barlas on women's readings and Abdulaziz Sachedina on the Qur'an and other religions. {cite:archive-org-cambridge-companion-quran-2006}
