## Summary

A question about financing a Ramadan iftar invitation in instalments, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that maintaining kinship ties is according to one's means and it is better not to burden oneself with debt for it; an interest-bearing loan is forbidden, an interest-free loan or compliant murabaha is not forbidden though burdensome, and borrowing is forbidden if it leads to stinting on one's dependants or the feast is for showing off. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3761}

## The question

The questioner asked whether the costs of a Ramadan iftar invitation may be paid in instalments through a finance company. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3761}

## The answer

The Department answered that Islam encourages maintaining kinship ties, citing a hadith agreed upon by [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] and [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]] whose meaning is that whoever wishes his provision to be expanded and his life extended should maintain his ties of kinship; such ties are according to one's means: inviting relatives to iftar is good for whoever can afford it, but whoever cannot should not overburden himself or borrow, and visiting and greeting them suffices. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3761} It said that if someone does borrow for the iftar feast, the means matters: an interest-bearing loan is forbidden, citing a hadith narrated by Muslim from [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abdullah]] about the curse on whoever consumes riba, pays it, records it or witnesses it; an interest-free loan or a murabaha meeting Shari'a standards is not forbidden, though he has burdened himself beyond his capacity, especially if his finances do not allow repayment. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3761} It added that the loan is forbidden if it leads to stinting on spending for his family, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] whose meaning is that it is sin enough for a person to neglect those he must provide for, and the feast is likewise forbidden if held for showing off, boasting or display. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3761}
