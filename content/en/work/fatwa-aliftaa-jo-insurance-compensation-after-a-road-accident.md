## Summary

A question from someone injured in a road accident about taking compensation from the insurance company, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that compensation for harm is lawful, so there is no objection to taking it for real harm, without deception or lying. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3884}

## The question

The questioner said he was in a road accident, fell on his back and suffered muscle bruising, and asked about taking money from the insurance company as compensation for these injuries. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3884}

## The answer

The Department answered that compensation for material harm is lawful in fiqh and that jurists have set out the grounds that require it; it pointed to the Qur'an's establishing blood money for accidental killing, and quoted [[person/al-mawardi|al-Mawardi]]'s al-Hawi al-Kabir that whoever destroys something is liable for it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3884} It said compensation falls on whoever directly caused the harm or brought it about, since the act is what creates liability, quoting [[person/shihab-al-din-al-ramli|al-Ramli]]'s Nihayat al-Muhtaj that whoever destroys another's property or misuses it owes its replacement and the hire. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3884} It concluded that there is no objection to taking compensation from insurance companies when it is for real harm caused by the accident, with no deception or lying involved. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3884}
