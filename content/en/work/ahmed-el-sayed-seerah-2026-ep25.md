## Summary

The lecturer closes the account of the conversion of Umar ibn al-Khattab in the sixth year of the mission. He weighs the hadith about the Prophet's prayer for Islam to be strengthened through one of two men, reads Ibn Ishaq's two narrations (Medinan and Meccan) and al-Bukhari's reports, then turns to Ibn Sa'd for scenes from Umar's later life, and previews the boycott years that follow. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### More on Umar's character: envoy of Quraysh and his age

- He recalls the traits listed in the previous session: self-respect, strong opinion, bodily and psychological strength and leadership. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates, citing Zubayr ibn Bakkar and what he thinks Ibn Abd al-Barr transmitted in al-Istiab, that Umar served as an envoy of Quraysh in pre-Islamic times, and he reads this as a sign of skill in negotiation and decision making at an early age. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He states that the best-known report puts Umar's age at conversion at 26, and that another report has him born thirteen years after the Year of the Elephant, which would make him about 33 when the Prophet was 46; he holds that either way he was a distinctive figure from youth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Grading the hadith of the prayer for the more beloved of two men

- He recalls that al-Tirmidhi graded as sound the report in which the Prophet asks that Islam be strengthened through Umar ibn al-Khattab or Amr ibn Hisham, and that it has several routes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that the best-known chain runs through Kharija ibn Abd Allah, whom Ahmad and al-Daraqutni weakened while Ibn Ma'in and Ibn Adi in al-Kamil strengthened him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He holds that Ibn Adi's including the report in Kharija's entry does not here signal weakness, and concludes that the report is good or acceptable in chain; he adds that the wording about the two Umars seems not established. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that the separate report from Aisha, naming Umar alone, goes back to Muslim ibn Khalid al-Zanji, whom some scholars weakened strongly. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### The many narrations of Umar's conversion and Ibn Ishaq's method

- He holds that the accounts of Umar's conversion are numerous, some cannot be combined and so must be weighed, and none is entirely free of difficulty by the strict standards of hadith scholars. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He rejects the view that the story should be dropped because no fully sound chain exists, and says the sira is not judged as severely as legal hadith but still requires preference among accounts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham transmits two accounts from Ibn Ishaq: the Medinan account, and the Meccan account from Abd Allah ibn Abi Najih through Ata and Mujahid, and that Ibn Ishaq says God knows which was so, which the speaker takes as a sign of Ibn Ishaq's care. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He notes that al-Bukhari has reports under the chapter on Umar's conversion that many sira writers omit, perhaps because Ibn Ishaq missed them or because they do not state outright that they caused the conversion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Al-Bukhari's report of the calf and the jinn, and Umar's insight

- He relates a report in al-Bukhari from Ibn Umar that Umar's guesses about people were always borne out, and takes it as evidence of Umar's keen insight, which he says aids sound judgement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates the connected story of a former soothsayer who told Umar of a jinn voice and of a cry heard beside idols at a sacrificed calf announcing a messenger; Umar says this was heard shortly before news came of a prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He classes the story among the signs preceding the mission that came through soothsayers and jinn, as against signs tied to earlier nations, stones and trees. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar in Fath al-Bari says al-Bukhari hinted this story was a cause of Umar's conversion, citing a report in Abu Nu'aym's Dala'il in which Umar, setting out to kill the Prophet for a reward offered by Abu Jahl, hears the calf's cry and then goes to his sister's house. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He notes Ibn Hajar's commentary took twenty-five years to complete. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### The common core of the narrations: conversion through hearing the Qur'an

- He holds that all or most narrations share the point that Umar became Muslim through hearing the Qur'an, whether from Khabbab or Sa'id ibn Zayd, by reading a sheet at his sister's house, or by hearing the Prophet recite in prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He holds that Umar, being a man of judgement, honesty and courage, accepted once outside pressure from companions and Quraysh nobles was lifted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He contrasts Umar with al-Walid ibn al-Mughira and Utba ibn Rabi'a, who were moved by the Qur'an but did not bear the consequences of a decision, and says Umar's resolve later showed in his caliphate. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### The Medinan account: the sister's house and Dar al-Arqam

- He reads Ibn Hisham's account, citing Ibn Ishaq: Umar's sister Fatima, wife of Sa'id ibn Zayd, had accepted Islam secretly, and Khabbab ibn al-Aratt visited to teach her the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar set out armed to find the Prophet at a house near al-Safa, which the speaker identifies as Dar al-Arqam, where about forty people stayed, including Hamza, Abu Bakr and Ali, those who had not gone to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He states that Nu'aym ibn Abd Allah, who hid his Islam, met Umar, told him his own sister and brother-in-law had accepted Islam, and sent him there instead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar struck his sister and wounded her, then felt remorse, asked for the sheet, washed on her insistence, read a portion of Surat Ta-Ha, and judged it beautiful. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Khabbab then came out and told Umar of hearing the Prophet pray for Islam to be strengthened by Abu Jahl or Umar; Umar asked to be led to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Hamza told those inside to let Umar in, that the Prophet seized him by his cloak and asked why he had come, and that Umar declared he came to believe, at which the Prophet called out the takbir and the household knew. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He holds that the Companions felt strengthened, having Hamza and Umar, and that Umar's boldness before Islam against Muslims matches his boldness after it against the idolaters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He says he knows no single sound chain for this narration, that Ibn Sa'd has a parallel through Anas whose narrator al-Qasim ibn Uthman was weakened and whom al-Dhahabi faulted, but that it reached through several routes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He holds that Umar's pause on remorse and his waiting once the truth was clear show a trait he already had before Islam, and that a person of six years in Mecca had not read what Muhammad brought, as many Meccans had not heard it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### The Meccan account: listening to the Prophet at the Kaaba

- He reads the Meccan account Ibn Ishaq took from Ibn Abi Najih: Umar, who was fond of wine, went at night to the Kaaba after missing his drinking companions and a wine seller. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar found the Prophet praying between the Black Corner and the Yemeni Corner, crept beneath the Kaaba's covering to listen, was moved to tears and felt Islam enter him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar followed the Prophet home, was first thought to mean harm, then said he had come to believe, and that the Prophet praised God, said God had guided him, and prayed for his steadfastness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He notes that the Prophet's home was the house later held by Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He holds that the two accounts are hard to combine at the moment of conversion, and follows Ibn Ishaq in leaving it to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Umar announces his conversion and the protection of al-As ibn Wa'il

- He holds that Umar's character did not change with Islam but was purified and strengthened, and that without Islam he would have remained a chief like others. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates, from Ibn Ishaq via Nafi and Ibn Umar, that Umar asked who was the best carrier of news in Quraysh, was told Jamil ibn Ma'mar al-Jumahi, and told him of his conversion; Jamil ran to the Kaaba and shouted that Ibn al-Khattab had left his religion, and Umar called out behind him that he had accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that the people attacked Umar until midday, that he told them that with three hundred men they would leave the city to the Muslims or the Muslims to them, and the speaker links this to the three hundred at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that an older man in fine dress, whom Abd Allah ibn Umar later named as al-As ibn Wa'il al-Sahmi, told the crowd to leave Umar alone, citing the alliance between Banu Sahm and Banu Adi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He states that Ibn Hisham transmits that Umar's son said of him, with a different wording about reward, and that the speaker links al-As to the one criticised in a Qur'anic passage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar then went to Abu Jahl, the cousin or maternal uncle depending on whether his mother Hantama was daughter of Hashim or Hisham ibn al-Mughira, preferring with Ibn Abd al-Barr that she was daughter of Hashim; Abu Jahl shut the door in his face. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Al-Bukhari's accounts of Umar's fear and the kinds of support given to the Prophet

- He cites al-Bukhari for Ibn Mas'ud's remark that the Muslims had been strong since Umar's conversion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates al-Bukhari's report that Umar, fearful at home after converting, was told by al-As ibn Wa'il that no harm would come to him, after which al-As turned the crowd away; he holds this and the report of Ibn Umar and Amr ibn Dinar show tribal custom in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He compares Umar's fear to that of Moses, holding that fear may be felt without keeping a person from the truth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He sets out stages of support for the Prophet: first belief, from Khadija, Abu Bakr, Zayd ibn Haritha, Ali and the early group; then protection from physical harm, by Abu Talib and Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, seen most in the boycott; then the pledge and city of refuge, which he calls the beginning of establishment; and finally the conquest of Mecca as full establishment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He holds that Abu Talib could shield the Prophet from physical harm because he was the heir of Abd al-Muttalib, chief of Mecca, and that some scholars place the thrown-offal episode only after Abu Talib's death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He holds that in Mecca the Prophet looked not for victory or establishment but for security, and that this holds also for later reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Ibn Sa'd's summary of Umar's firsts and reign

- He relates reports of Hudhayfa that Islam advanced while Umar lived and receded after his death, and of Umm Ayman that Islam weakened when he died, placing Umar's life between these and Ibn Mas'ud's remark. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He reads Ibn Sa'd's summary, in which Umar is first called Amir al-Mu'minin, first dates events from the Hijra in the month of Rabi al-Awwal in year 16, and first gathered people for the Ramadan night prayer in year 14. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates from the summary that Umar punished wine drinking with eighty lashes, banished Rabi'a ibn Umayya, patrolled Medina at night, and carried the whip. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates the conquests listed there: Iraq, Azerbaijan, Basra and Ahwaz regions, Persia, Syria, the Jazira, Mosul, Egypt and Alexandria, with Ajnadayn under Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates the tax figures the summary gives: forty-eight, twenty-four and twelve dirhams for the rich, middling and poor, and the founding of garrison cities and the register of stipends, favouring the veterans of Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar expelled the Jews from the Hijaz and the people of Najran to the Kufa area, was at Jabiya for the conquest of Jerusalem, turned back from Sargh because of the plague, and lived through the plague of Amwas and the year of the Ramadah famine. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He states that the claim Umar was first to collect the Qur'an into sheets is open to question. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Ibn Sa'd's sources and Umar's firmness and gentleness

- He says Ibn Sa'd gives many reports on Umar through al-Waqidi, whom he calls wide in transmission though sometimes solitary, and that al-Dhahabi judged Ibn Sa'd's selection from al-Waqidi to be good. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Ali, Uthman, Talha, al-Zubayr, Abd al-Rahman and Sa'd sent Abd al-Rahman, the boldest with Umar, to ask him to ease his manner because petitioners were too awed to speak; Umar asked whether they had sent him, then said he feared God for his gentleness and for his severity, and Abd al-Rahman left weeping. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar, sitting after prayers to hear petitions, once received Uthman and Ibn Abbas, offered them money to distribute, and when Ibn Abbas objected, spoke of the Prophet's hard life; Ibn Abbas answered that had wealth come to the Prophet he would have eaten and fed others, at which Umar wept and wished to be free of the matter with neither credit nor blame. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Umar's worship, manner and self-reproach

- He relates reports through Ibn Sa'd that Umar liked prayer in the middle of the night, fasted much and used the toothstick often. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates a sound report that Umar said he would have called the adhan himself were it not for the burdens of the caliphate, and explains the wording as meaning the multitude of its affairs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar said he would wish to meet God were it not for travelling in God's way, prostrating, and sitting with people who pick the best of speech. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that al-Shifa bint Abd Allah, seeing young men walk slowly and speak softly, was told they were ascetics, and said Umar, who spoke so as to be heard, walked briskly and struck so as to hurt, was the true ascetic. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates the report that goodness was not recognised in Umar and Ibn Umar until they spoke or acted, and Ibn Umar's explanation that they were neither affected nor feigning weakness; he draws a contrast between the natural manner of the early generations and the affectation of some later ones, which he says is distinct from humility visible in the face. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### Scenes of Umar's rule: the shepherd, self-address, justice and Hormuzan

- He relates a report that Umar, on the road to Mecca near al-Rawha, turned aside to a shepherd, told him he had passed a richer pasture, and reminded him that every shepherd answers for his flock. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates a very sound report from Anas that Umar, inside an orchard, spoke to himself by name and as Amir al-Mu'minin, saying he must fear God or be punished; he adds that Umar sometimes recalled herding for his rough father al-Khattab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that the senior follower Abu Uthman al-Nahdi said Umar was an even balance that never tilted, and takes this as a sign of his justice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates, from Anas, that Hormuzan, the captured Persian commander brought to Medina who became Muslim, saw Umar sleeping in the Prophet's mosque and called him a king safe from fear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates a report that Umar cleared the mosque after the evening prayer except for those praying, but sat with a group including Ubayy ibn Ka'b who were making supplication, each in turn; one young man was seized by trembling, and Umar then supplicated and wept more than all. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Umar vowed to give the people whatever the treasury held, and that when he asked Salman if he was a king or a caliph, Salman said a caliph takes only what is right and spends it rightly, whereas a king oppresses; Umar fell silent. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}

### End of this stage and preview of the boycott years

- He says the account of the early Meccan stage ends at the close of the sixth year of the mission, and that some reports date Umar's conversion to the month of Dhu al-Hijja of that year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He announces that the next session will begin the seventh year, with the boycott in the ravine and the Quraysh pact against the Prophet, his Companions and Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, Muslim and unbeliever alike. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq, after the conversion, treats the pact and boycott briefly, then moves to the mockers including Abu Lahab and Umayya ibn Khalaf, the story of Ibn Umm Maktum and Surat Abasa, the annulment of the pact and Surat al-Kawthar, before reaching the Night Journey. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep25}
