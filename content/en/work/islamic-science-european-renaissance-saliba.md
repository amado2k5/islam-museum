## Summary

*Islamic Science and the Making of the European Renaissance* by George Saliba describes the rise and fall of the Islamic scientific tradition and the relationship of Islamic science to European science during the Renaissance. {cite:muslimheritage-saliba-islamic-science} It sets out the conventional accounts of Islamic science, discusses their shortcomings and proposes an alternative narrative. {cite:muslimheritage-saliba-islamic-science} Taking astronomy as a template for the progress of science in Islamic civilization, Saliba details the innovations of Islamic astronomers from the thirteenth to the sixteenth century, including new mathematical tools, and offers evidence that Copernicus could have known of and drawn on their work. {cite:muslimheritage-saliba-islamic-science} Rather than view the rise and fall of Islamic science through politics and religion, he focuses on scientific production itself and the social, economic and intellectual conditions that made it possible. {cite:muslimheritage-saliba-islamic-science}

The Muslim Heritage notice reproduces two endorsements from the MIT Press site, by N. M. Swerdlow of the University of Chicago and Gül A. Russell of Texas A&M University; Swerdlow writes that the book shows there was no decline of Arabic science well into the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries. {cite:muslimheritage-saliba-islamic-science}

## Publication and editions

MIT Press published the book in April 2007 in its series Transformations: Studies in the History of Science and Technology. {cite:muslimheritage-saliba-islamic-science} Open Library records the edition at 315 pages and catalogues a first MIT Press paperback edition of 2011. {cite:openlibrary-islamic-science-european-renaissance-2007} The notice describes Saliba as Professor of Arabic and Islamic Studies at Columbia University, where he has taught since 1979. {cite:muslimheritage-saliba-islamic-science}
