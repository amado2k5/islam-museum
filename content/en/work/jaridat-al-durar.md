## Summary

Jaridat al-durar wa-kharidat al-fikar is a zij by [[person/taqi-al-din-ibn-maruf|Taqi al-Din ibn Ma'ruf]], described by TDV as a small work containing tables based on decimal fractions. Ihsan Fazlioglu's biography explains its place in applying decimal arithmetic to trigonometry and astronomy. This description uses scholarship actually read, not a purported inspection of a Jarida manuscript {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid work3} {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea decimal-zij discussion}.

## What does the zij do?

A zij is a tool for calculation and tables, not solely descriptive writing about the sky. Fazlioglu discusses decimal fractions in trigonometric functions, tangent and cotangent tables, and decimal fractional degrees and calculations in the Jarida and Tashil zij al-ashariyya. He also retains a fixed-star-table exception in the description of decimal tables. That qualification belongs to the definition, rather than being an inconvenient detail to remove {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea decimal-zij discussion}.

This is the [[invention/taqi-decimal-trigonometric-tables|particular contribution]] linked to the book. It does not mean every decimal fraction began here, or that decimal representation determines an astronomical model's correctness. Notation, measurement and approximation are connected but distinct questions.

## Author and setting

Taqi al-Din worked in teaching and judicial service in Egypt and later astronomy in Istanbul, benefiting from books, instruments and learning networks described by Fazlioglu and TDV. Fazlioglu connects his arithmetic with al-Kashi's work and the Samarqand school. The book therefore belongs within a long tradition of computation and observation, not isolation from predecessors {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea biography and mathematical works} {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid biography}.

The reading used here does not establish an exact composition date or precise writing location. Neither observatory construction, the author's death nor a modern study's publication supplies a book date without evidence. Fazlioglu states that his books were written in Arabic; the modern translation's language and history must remain separate from composition {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea works}.

## Not the Sidra

Sidrat muntaha al-afkar concerns correcting Ulugh Beg's zij, with functions described by Fazlioglu as sexagesimal. The Jarida is discussed in a decimal context. Tekeli's inspected article compares the Sidra with Copernicus: its diameter120, radius60 and sine definition are not values extracted here from the Jarida {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea Sidra and decimal-zij discussions} {cite:tekeli-trigonometry-1986 pp.250,257–258}.

Different books by one author cannot be collapsed. Likewise, an unfinished observational programme does not alone establish that every book remained a draft. Each work has its own purpose, extent, chronology and witnesses.
