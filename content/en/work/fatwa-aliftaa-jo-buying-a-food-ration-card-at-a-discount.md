## Summary

A question about buying a food ration card at a discount, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that selling a ration card before taking possession of the goods it represents is not permitted, because of the gross uncertainty involved and because it exploits the needs of the poor; once the holder has received and owns the goods, he may sell them at any price. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3667}

## The question

The questioner asked whether he may buy a food ration card worth 100 dinars for 85 dinars. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3667}

## The answer

The Department answered that selling a ration card involves gross uncertainty, since the type and quantity of what is sold are unknown, and uncertainty invalidates a sale, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] prohibiting the pebble sale and sales involving excessive uncertainty (gharar). {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3667} It said that the card is allocated by the body that issues it to help those in need and to subsidise essential foodstuffs; selling it exploits the needs of the poor, defeats the purpose of issuing the cards and turns aid into profit for those not in need. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3667} It said the prohibition is strengthened by a report narrated by [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] in [[work/muwatta-malik|al-Muwatta]] that food vouchers were issued to people in the time of [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]] and they traded them before receiving the food; [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]] and another Companion objected to Marwan, who sent guards to take the vouchers back from people and return them to their owners. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3667} It concluded that selling ration cards before owning the actual goods is not permitted, but once the cardholder has received his entitlement and owns the goods the coupons represent, he may sell them at whatever price he wishes. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3667}
