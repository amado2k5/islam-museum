## Summary

He closes his treatment of the Battle of Badr by adding scenes he had left out and then turns to its consequences: the council on the captives, the quarrel over the spoils, the shock in Medina and Mecca, the open hypocrisy, the thirst for revenge that led to the incident of al-Raji and to Uhud, and the status of the Badr veterans. He then covers events of the second year, the story of Ali's two she-camels and Hamza, the gradual prohibition of wine, and the two kinds of criterion between truth and falsehood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Badr had consequences, not a single event

- He says that this is the nineteenth session of the series and that it completes what remains of Badr before moving to other matters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that Badr was not an isolated event: it led to consequences in the stance of the polytheists and in several legal rulings about spoils. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that he will take some of these consequences today, after adding a few quick scenes from the battle that he had omitted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Umayr ibn al-Humam and the dates

- He relates, citing Sahih Muslim on the authority of Anas ibn Malik, that when the Prophet called the fighters towards a paradise as wide as the heavens and earth, Umayr ibn al-Humam al-Ansari asked whether it was true and rejoiced at the answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that the Prophet told Umayr he was among its people, after Umayr explained that he spoke only out of hope of being among them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Umayr took some dates from his quiver and ate a few, then judged that finishing them would mean too long a life, threw the rest away, fought and was killed, counted among the martyrs of Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He compares this with the case of Thabit ibn Qays, who also received a testimony of paradise, and argues that by worldly reckoning Umayr would have left the line, yet he advanced instead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that the story shows the certainty of the Companions' faith and the way they received the realities of religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Al-Zubayr and the polytheist known as Abu Dhat al-Karsh

- He relates, citing Sahih al-Bukhari, that a fully armoured polytheist nicknamed Abu Dhat al-Karsh taunted al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, who struck him in the eye with a short spear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that the Prophet asked for that spear after the fighting and that it then passed, as he recalls it, to Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman and finally to Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, in whose hands it remained when he was killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He explains that he often quotes the two Sahih collections because many authentic reports cover parts of the biography, and that this is not a deliberate avoidance of the biographical works. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### The consultation about the captives

- He relates that after the battle the Prophet consulted Abu Bakr and Umar, and, in the report he cites from Sahih Muslim on the authority of Ibn Abbas from Umar, Ali as well, about the seventy captives. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that the consultation teaches that a reformer who follows the Prophet should share opinion with others, since the Prophet had no revelation on the matter and only general principles. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Abu Bakr favoured taking ransom as a source of strength against the unbelievers and in hope that the captives might be guided, being kinsmen. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Umar instead wished each Muslim to execute a close relative among the captives to show that there was no leniency towards the polytheist leaders. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that the Prophet inclined to Abu Bakr's view and took ransom, and that the next day he and Abu Bakr were found weeping because of a punishment shown to him, after which a revelation addressed the matter and permitted the spoils as lawful. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He draws the lesson that at that stage the context called for subduing the enemy, while in other stages the Prophet pardoned polytheists, which he says requires careful understanding. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Dispute over the spoils and the principle of referring disputes to revelation

- He relates that the Prophet gathered the spoils, went back towards Medina and divided them at a valley he names as Wadi Sayr, not far from Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that the Companions had disagreed among themselves over the spoils, and cites Ubada ibn al-Samit as saying that God removed the matter from their hands and placed it with the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that the dispute teaches that even excellent people may differ during work of calling, fighting or reform, and that the way out was to refer to revelation and submit to the Prophet's authority. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He counts this among the elements of the jurisprudence of disagreement and links it to the opening of Surat al-Anfal, which he says belongs to Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### News of the victory reaches Medina and Mecca

- He relates that the Prophet sent Zayd ibn Haritha ahead to Medina with the good news, and that Zayd named the slain polytheist leaders such as Abu Jahl, Utba, Shayba and Umayya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that naming those men first shows how large a problem they had been for the Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that the hypocrites in Medina did not believe Zayd, saying he had lost his mind, and that, as the reports have it, Usama ibn Zayd asked his father whether it was true while the Muslims were burying Ruqayya, the Prophet's daughter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that in Mecca the first messenger who announced the dead leaders was doubted until Safwan ibn Umayya was found sitting in the sanctuary, which made the people take it as true. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that the disaster was beyond belief for the polytheists and the hypocrites alike. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Open hypocrisy in Medina and the earlier clash with Abdullah ibn Ubayy

- He holds that one consequence of Badr was that polytheists in Medina declared Islam out of hypocrisy, having been open before, because Islam's strength had risen. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates, citing Sahih al-Bukhari on the authority of Anas, an earlier scene in which the Prophet rode a donkey to visit Abdullah ibn Ubayy, who insulted him over the smell of the animal, and the supporters of both men came to blows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He says he believes this took place before Badr, though God knows best. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Revenge oaths and Abu Sufyan's raid on Medina

- He holds that after Badr the polytheists of Quraysh swore to take revenge, some for personal losses against particular Companions and others in general against the Prophet and his followers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan was among those who swore an oath of abstention until he took revenge, and that after a short while he moved on Medina with a group. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that they entered stealthily from the east, killed an Ansari man and another with him, did some damage and fled, and that the Prophet noticed and sent a force in pursuit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### The ten scouts and the story of Khubayb

- He holds that the Prophet gave great care to intelligence and constant movement and was not idle, pointing to the many patrols sent in the second and third years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates, citing Sahih al-Bukhari on the authority of Abu Hurayra, that the Prophet sent ten men as scouts under Asim ibn Thabit and that they were tracked near al-Hada between Usfan and Mecca by about a hundred men of the Hudhayl clan called Banu Lihyan, who recognised them by the date stones from Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Asim refused to come down under a pledge from unbelievers and was killed with arrows, while three men came down on a promise of safety and were bound at once, one of whom refused to go and was killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Khubayb and Zayd ibn al-Dathina were sold in Mecca after Badr, Khubayb to the sons of al-Harith ibn Amir, whom he had killed at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Khubayb was held captive, borrowed a razor from a daughter of al-Harith, held it near her child without harming him, and that she later said she had never seen a better prisoner and had seen him eating grapes when none could be found in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that when they took him out of the sanctuary to kill him, Khubayb asked to pray two rakas, was killed by Uqba ibn al-Harith, and is held by tradition to have set the practice of prayer for any Muslim facing execution. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that the Prophet told his Companions of their fate, that Quraysh sent for part of Asim's body in revenge for the leader he had killed, and that a swarm of hornets protected it so nothing could be cut. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that the story shows both the high spirits of the Muslims and the hatred of the polytheists after Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Uhud as a consequence of Badr and the standing of the Badr veterans

- He holds that the Battle of Uhud was itself a consequence of Badr, since the polytheists marched to take revenge for Badr and Surat Al Imran links the two events in more than one place. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Umar paid the Badr veterans more from the public treasury than others, and that people were identified as Emigrant or Helper Badr veterans. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that this shows the weight of a person in the Prophet's time came from the days on which God gave the religion victory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that al-Bukhari, in the book of expeditions under Badr, gathered reports of very different legal subjects because their narrators had attended Badr, and likens this to a modern search engine. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that attending Badr served as a defining mark of a Companion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Events of the second year after the migration

- He holds that the second year was full of events, including patrols before Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He says that fasting was made obligatory in that year and that the fixed zakat with its set thresholds was then required, while general almsgiving had been commanded since Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He says that the Prophet married Aisha either at the end of the first year or the start of the second, and that Ali married Fatima in that year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Ali's two she-camels, Hamza and the gradual prohibition of wine

- He relates, citing al-Bukhari, that Ali received an old she-camel from the Badr spoils and the Prophet gave him another, and that he meant to gather a fragrant grass to sell, with a goldsmith of Banu Qaynuqa, to fund his wedding feast for Fatima. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib was drinking in a house nearby, before wine was forbidden, and that while intoxicated he cut open the two animals with his sword and took part of their livers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that Ali wept at the sight and told the Prophet, who went to Hamza and was angry, and that Hamza answered him roughly, so the Prophet withdrew. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that this shows the gradual stages of legislation, noting that wine was not yet forbidden even at Uhud and that some who died there had drunk before. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He lists, as he recalls them, stages beginning with a hint that intoxicants are not good provision, then a ban on praying while intoxicated, with Umar praying each time for a clear ruling, and finally the full prohibition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He relates that after the prohibition was announced in Medina the wine was poured out until it ran in the streets, and that those who drank were afterwards flogged in the Prophet's time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that such a sudden change of a society devoted to wine was remarkable, comparing it with failed attempts at prohibition in other countries in modern times, and thinks the gradual approach reflects divine wisdom. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

### Badr as a practical criterion between truth and falsehood

- He holds that Badr was a day on which God separated truth from falsehood in practice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that God made two kinds of criterion: a theoretical one of proof and clarification, which is the Qur'an and the name of Surat al-Furqan, and a practical one in which truth is seen to triumph. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that the practical criterion completes the theoretical one and adds to the proof, and that this may be the most important point of the session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He argues that the verse about the day of the criterion, saying that those who perish do so after clear evidence, shows that the day of Badr was connected with clear proof. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that the proof existed before Badr and that the practical proof was added to it afterwards, so whoever kept to unbelief after Badr perished after more evidence. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}
- He holds that reformers should combine both kinds of criterion and both kinds of proof, the knowledge-based and the practical. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep19}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.
