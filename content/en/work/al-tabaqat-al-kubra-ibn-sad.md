## Summary
*Al-Tabaqat al-Kubra* ("The Great Generations", also called al-Tabaqat al-Kabir) is the work of [[person/muhammad-ibn-sad|Muhammad ibn Sa'd]], secretary of [[person/al-waqidi|al-Waqidi]]. The sources agree in ascribing it to him, and the original ran to fifteen volumes. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 55} It opens with the reports about the Prophet, which Ibn al-Nadim lists as a separate book, Akhbar al-Nabi, and which Ibn Sa'd closes with the words "the end of the report of the Prophet". There follow the generations of Companions, Successors and those after them down to his own time. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 55-56} Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi said he wrote a great book on the generations of the Companions, the Successors and their followers to his day "and did it excellently", and Hajji Khalifa called it "the greatest work written on the generations of transmitters". {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 57}

## How a notice is built
The editor of the completing section notes that Ibn Sa'd gives a man's lineage through father and mother and lists his children and their mothers, tracing lineages back before Islam, so that the book is rich in genealogy. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 65} He usually gives the kunya and title, sometimes the trade, and details that show a man's character, learning or creed and the main events of his life. He describes the person's appearance: the dye on hair and beard, the kind and colour of clothes, the turban, the ring and its inscription. He records offices held, governorship or judgeship, and where a man lived in Medina or its outskirts, giving scattered information on the city's quarters, then follows his travels. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 65} On transmitters he often says only "of few reports" or "of many reports", though he also grades men as reliable or weak. The editor places him among the moderate critics, and quotes al-Sakhawi that his judgements of transmitters are "good and accepted". {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 7-8} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 77}

## Transmission and printings
- It was transmitted from him by al-Harith ibn Abi Usama (d. 282 AH) and al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Fahm (d. 289 AH). {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 54}
- It was printed at Leiden in eight volumes with a ninth of indexes, then in Beirut on the basis of the Leiden edition, and by Dar al-Tahrir in Egypt. None of these three printings contains the section published by Ziyad Muhammad Mansur. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 55} The foreword by the vice-president of the Islamic University says the orientalists' edition was incomplete in many places because of gaps in their manuscripts, gaps they themselves pointed out. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 5}

## The completing section on the Successors of Medina
- In 1408 AH/1987 CE (second edition) Ziyad Muhammad Mansur published the part missing from volume 5, from a unique manuscript photographed at the Institute of Manuscripts in Cairo. It begins with the end of the notice of [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz]] and covers three-quarters of the third generation of Medinan Successors, the whole of the fourth and fifth, and about half the sixth. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 3} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 77-78}
- It holds 407 notices. The earliest to die is 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Miswar (90 AH) and the last Shu'ayb ibn Talha (174 or 175 AH). Among them are [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri]], whose notice is the longest, [[person/ibn-abi-dhib|Ibn Abi Dhi'b]] and [[person/rabia-al-ray|Rabi'at al-Ra'y]]. Also included are the three brothers of the Munkadir family, among them [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-munkadir|Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir]], and 'Abd Allah ibn Hasan with his sons [[person/muhammad-al-nafs-al-zakiyya|Muhammad al-Nafs al-Zakiyya]] and Ibrahim. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 6-7}
- A rarity is the notice of Qurin ibn al-Muttalib ibn Abi Wada'a, son of the man to whom Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab married his daughter, for whom the editor found no other mention. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 7}
- The fourth generation opens with al-Zuhri (notice 70). From al-Waqidi, Ibn Sa'd relates how al-Zuhri sought learning: he left 'Abd Allah ibn Tha'laba ibn Su'ayr for Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, 'Urwa, 'Ubayd Allah ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Utba and Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman, then travelled to Damascus and was brought before 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 157-159}
