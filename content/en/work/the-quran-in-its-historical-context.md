## Summary

*The Qur'an in Its Historical Context* is a collective volume edited by Gabriel Said Reynolds and published by Routledge in 2008 in 294 pages. {cite:google-books-quran-in-its-historical-context} Reynolds opens it with an introduction on Qur'anic studies and its controversies, preceded by a foreword by Daniel A. Madigan. {cite:google-books-quran-in-its-historical-context}

## Publication and editions

Part 1, on linguistic and historical evidence, has chapters by Fred M. Donner on the Qur'an in recent scholarship, Robert Hoyland on epigraphy and the linguistic background, and Gerhard Böwering, Claude Gilliot and Sidney Griffith. {cite:google-books-quran-in-its-historical-context} Part 2, on the religious context of the late antique Near East, has chapters by Samir Khalil Samir, Suleiman A. Mourad, Kevin van Bladel, Manfred Kropp and Abdul-Massih Saadi. {cite:google-books-quran-in-its-historical-context} Part 3, on the critical study of the Qur'an and the Muslim exegetical tradition, has a chapter by Devin J. Stewart on medieval and modern emendations and one by Andrew Rippin on classical Muslim theories about Syriac in the Qur'an. {cite:google-books-quran-in-its-historical-context}
