## Summary

*Muhammad and the Believers: At the Origins of Islam* is a book by Fred M. Donner, published by the Belknap Press of Harvard University Press in May 2010 in 280 pages. {cite:openlibrary-muhammad-and-the-believers-2010} In his preface Donner argues that Islam began as a religious movement, not a social, economic or national one, concerned above all with attaining salvation through righteous behaviour, against earlier writers who explained it by motives other than religious conviction. {cite:archive-org-muhammad-and-the-believers-scan}

## Publication and editions

The book has five chapters: the Near East on the eve of Islam, Muhammad and the Believers' movement, the expansion of the community of Believers, the struggle for leadership of the community from 34 to 73 AH (655–692), and the emergence of Islam under the Umayyads, followed by two appendices on the umma document and the inscriptions of the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem. {cite:archive-org-muhammad-and-the-believers-scan}
