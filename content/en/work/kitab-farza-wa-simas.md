## Summary
Kitab Farza wa Simas is a Persian book of tales containing accounts of the kings of India and their viziers, named by al-Mas'udi alongside Hazar Afsana and the Sindbad. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 555}

## Details
Al-Mas'udi mentions in Muruj al-Dhahab, among the translated books and tales, the book Farza wa Simas with its stories of the kings of India and the viziers. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 555} It appears in his discussion of the book Hazar Afsana — whose rendering from Persian into Arabic is Alf Khurafa ("A Thousand Tales"), khurafa being the Persian afsana — the book people call Alf Layla wa-Layla, the tale of the king, his vizier, and their daughters Shirzad and Dinazad; and like it the book Farza wa Simas and the book of Sindbad and other works of this kind. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 555} For al-Mas'udi it belongs to the class of story and legend books circulating in this genre, testifying to the currency of tales of Indian kings in the translated Persian literature. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 555}
