## Summary
Fasl al-Maqal fima Bayna al-Hikma wa-l-Shari'a min al-Ittisal — Ibn Rushd's printed treatise on the connection between philosophy and revealed law, counted by al-Zirikli among his works. {cite:al-aalam p. 849}

## Details
In his al-A'lam entry on Ibn Rushd al-Andalusi (d. 595 AH), al-Zirikli says he composed about fifty books, among them the printed Fasl al-Maqal on the accord of wisdom and the revealed law — one of the works circulating in print in al-Zirikli's own day. {cite:al-aalam p. 849}

Its very title announces its aim: to join philosophy and the religious law and show their connectedness rather than their conflict, in a milieu where the quarrel between rational inquiry and transmitted scripture was at its sharpest. In the same list it keeps company with Tahafut al-Tahafut, Bidayat al-Mujtahid, Minhaj al-Adilla and other works of his, some of which were rendered into Latin and Hebrew. {cite:al-aalam p. 849}
