## Summary

A question about zakat on gas cylinders in a wholesale warehouse, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that if the warehouse's trade is confined to selling gas and exchanging cylinders, zakat is due on the value of the gas alone, the cylinders being containers; if it sells cylinders both empty and full, they are trade goods and zakat is due on their value together with the gas. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3795}

## The question

The owner of a warehouse selling household gas wholesale asked whether zakat is due on the empty gas cylinders or only on the liquid gas inside them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3795}

## The answer

The Department answered that in principle zakat is due on everything held for trade, citing [[passage/quran-2-267|Surat al-Baqara 2:267]] and [[person/mujahid-ibn-jabr|Mujahid]]'s explanation of it as referring to trade, as reported in [[work/tafsir-ibn-kathir|Tafsir Ibn Kathir]] (1/697). {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3795} It said that if the warehouse only fills cylinders, sells them full and exchanges them for empty ones, the trade is confined to the gas, so zakat is due on the value of the gas and not on the cylinders, which are containers for the goods and lack the condition of growth. It cited a hadith narrated by [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] whose meaning is that a Muslim owes no alms on his horse or his slave, and Umayra's gloss that zakat is due only on growing wealth. The trader values the gas he owns at the end of the zakat year, adds his other cash, and if the total reaches the nisab pays 2.5% on all of it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3795} If, however, the warehouse sells cylinders both empty and full and earns from both, they are trade goods, and zakat is due on the value of the empty cylinder plus the value of the gas in it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3795}
