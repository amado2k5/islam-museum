## Summary

The equation work of [[person/sharaf-al-din-al-tusi|Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi]], called al-Muadalat in Ozel's entry, survives through an anonymous abridgement. It treats classes through degree three, distinguishing classes with positive solutions from those depending on conditions. The associated [[invention/sharaf-tusi-positive-cubic-roots|positive-root analysis]] is a particular contribution, not a claim that all algebra originated in this book {cite:tdv-sharaf-tusi-ozel works1} {cite:sharaf-tusi-mactutor equations}.

## Structure and approach

The accounts describe twelve lower-degree types, eight positive-soluble cubic types and five conditional ones. MacTutor illustrates x³+a=bx with positive parameters through a maximum condition. The symbols are modern exposition; the biography states that Sharaf used words. It also discusses numerical approximation and differing interpretations of the relation between maximum reasoning and derivatives {cite:sharaf-tusi-mactutor equations-and-derivative-discussion}.

The classification counts are not a modern inventory of complex roots. Nor does a Horner-method comparison document a transmission route. The invention article separates historical attribution from independently checked teaching calculations and explains positive roots, repeated roots and distinct-root counts {cite:sharaf-tusi-mactutor numerical-approximation}.
