## Overview
*Tadrib al-Rawi fi Sharh Taqrib al-Nawawi* is a commentary by the hafiz Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti (849–911 AH) on al-Nawawi's [[work/al-taqrib-wal-taysir|al-Taqrib wa-l-Taysir]], itself an abridgement of [[work/muqaddimat-ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]]'s book. It follows the sixty-five types in order and blends al-Nawawi's wording with the commentary. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 1} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 5}

## Method
Al-Suyuti opens each type with Ibn al-Salah's words and then those of later scholars. The thirtieth type shows the pattern:
- he quotes Ibn al-Salah's remark that the meaning of "well-known" is self-evident, so he gave no definition;
- then the definition of al-Bulqini and of the legal theorists, a report whose transmitters number more than three;
- then Ibn Hajar's definition.
{cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 5}

On disputed questions he gathers the positions, their holders and where they are found. On [[question/awwal-man-aslama|who first accepted Islam]] he sets out about ten views before reporting the reconciliation chosen by Ibn al-Salah and al-Nawawi. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 211} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 222}

He marks what al-Nawawi added to Ibn al-Salah. An example is al-Nawawi's remark on Khadija: "this is correct according to a group of verifiers". {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 217} He also passes judgement on views, as when he says that the views dissenting from the uprightness of the Companions are all incorrect. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 186}

## The knowledge of the Companions
The thirty-ninth type, the knowledge of the Companions, fills about ninety pages of volume 5 in Awwama's edition. Al-Nawawi calls it a great and very useful science, by which the connected chain is told apart from the hurried (mursal) one. Among its books al-Suyuti names those of Ibn Hibban and Ibn Manda. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 148} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 238}

Its points include:
- the uprightness of the Companions (see [[concept/adalat-al-sahaba|the uprightness of the Companions]]);
- which of them was most excellent;
- who first accepted Islam;
- who was the last of them to die: [[person/abu-al-tufayl-amir-ibn-wathila|Abu al-Tufayl]].
{cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 171} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 199} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 223}

## The edition used
The edition used is Muhammad Awwama's (Dar al-Minhaj, Jeddah, first edition, 1437 AH / 2016), with the gloss of Ahmad ibn Ahmad ibn al-Ajami (1014–1086 AH). Each page separates four layers:
- al-Nawawi's base text;
- al-Suyuti's commentary;
- Ibn al-Ajami's gloss;
- the editor's extensive notes.

In those notes the editor sometimes answers the commentator. For example, he finds al-Suyuti's description of the dissenting views on the Companions' uprightness as merely "incorrect" too soft. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 1} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 4} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 186}
