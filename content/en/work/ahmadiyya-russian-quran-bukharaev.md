# The Ahmadiyya Russian translation of the Qur'an (Khalid Ahmad and Ravil Bukharaev)

## Summary
This is the official Russian rendering of the meanings of the [[work/quran|Qur'an]] of the Ahmadiyya (Qadiani) community, first published in 2006, a joint work: Khalid Ahmad translated it from the Arabic with Rustam Khamatvaleev, and Ravil Bukharaev revised and edited it, wrote its preface and translated its commentary. {cite:gloqur-bukharaev-russian}

## Ravil Bukharaev
Bukharaev (1951–2012) graduated from the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics at Kazan State University and did postgraduate study at Moscow State University; he wrote more than thirty books in Russian, Tatar, English and Hungarian, worked for the BBC World Service for over fifteen years, and is regarded as a major Tatar writer of his time. {cite:gloqur-bukharaev-russian} The author reports that he joined the Ahmadiyya community in the late 1980s and moved to London in the early 1990s. {cite:gloqur-bukharaev-russian}

## What it contains
In Ahmadiyya interpretation, on which this translation openly draws through Mirza Tahir Ahmad's Urdu commentary, the verses on Jesus are read differently from the Sunni majority: *mutawaffika* is rendered as causing to die and the raising as a raising in rank, and narratives read as miracles are explained otherwise, as Elvira Kulieva describes. {cite:gloqur-bukharaev-russian} The preface takes a mild polemical stance against Muslims it sees as privileging reports over the text or relying on classical tafsir alone. {cite:gloqur-bukharaev-russian}

## Reception
The author writes that the Tatar secular intelligentsia saw Bukharaev's Ahmadiyya affiliation as one more layer of a complex personality, while the mainstream clergy and many traditional Sunnis of Tatarstan saw his affiliation and his translation as dangerous. {cite:gloqur-bukharaev-russian}
