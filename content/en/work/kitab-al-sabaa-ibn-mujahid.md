## Summary
Kitab al-Sab'a fi al-Qira'at is the book of Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Musa Ibn Mujahid of Baghdad. Ibn al-Jazari says Ibn Mujahid was the first to gather the readings of the seven well-known imams, during the fourth century AH. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 34} The Fihrist says he was the last to whom the leadership in the readings passed at Baghdad, and gives his birth as 245 and death as 324 AH. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 59}

## The seven
Ibn al-Jazari, quoting al-Mahdawi, lists the readers of the cities on whom most people came to limit themselves: Nafi', Ibn Kathir, Abu 'Amr, Ibn 'Amir, 'Asim, Hamza and al-Kisa'i. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 46} He says al-Kisa'i was added to the seven only in the days of al-Ma'mun and later, that the seventh had been Ya'qub al-Hadrami, and that Ibn Mujahid, about 300 AH, put al-Kisa'i in Ya'qub's place. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 47}

## The criticism of a limit of seven
Ibn al-Jazari reports that many early imams disliked Ibn Mujahid's limiting himself to seven and faulted him, because the public heard the hadith 'the Qur'an was sent down in seven ahruf' and the phrase 'the readings of the seven' and took the seven to be the ones referred to; they said that had he reduced or increased the number, or explained his intention, he would have removed the confusion. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 46} He quotes Makki ibn Abi Talib that people had named in their books more than seventy imams of higher rank than these seven, that Abu Hatim and others left some of them out, and that al-Tabari, Abu 'Ubayd and Isma'il al-Qadi added to them. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 46} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 47} He also quotes a passage, which begins on the preceding page, explaining that the number matched the number of ahruf sent down, not a belief that the seven readings are the seven ahruf. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 49} See [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa|the seven ahruf]].

## Related books
In giving the chains of the readers, Ibn al-Jazari refers to 'the three paths given earlier in the chains of Kitab al-Sab'a' and attributes one of Ibn Abi Mihran's paths to Ibn Mujahid's Kitab al-Sab'a. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 113} The notes of the King Fahd edition of the Itqan say that al-Hujja lil-Qurra' al-Sab'a by Abu 'Ali al-Farisi (d. 377 AH) is a commentary on 'Ibn Mujahid's Sab'a'. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 536} See [[work/al-hujja-lil-qurra-al-saba|al-Hujja lil-Qurra' al-Sab'a]].

## What is not settled
The Fihrist's list of Ibn Mujahid's books, as read in the Cairo copy here, names the large and the small Kitab al-Qira'at, books on the ya's and the ha's, a book on the reading of each imam and one on the Prophet's reading, but no book titled al-Sab'a. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 59} {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 65} This record does not decide whether these are one book under two titles; and the book itself was not read.
