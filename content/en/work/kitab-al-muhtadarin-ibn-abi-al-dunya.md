## Summary
Kitab al-Muhtadarin ('The Book of the Dying') is a work on the dying attributed to Abu Bakr ibn Abi al-Dunya, and its title indicates its subject. From the pages I read there is no description of its arrangement and no text from it; what the books give is its name among the author's works and the fact that it was read and heard through connected chains. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2502} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 49 p. 126}

## What the books say
In his entry on Ibn Abi al-Dunya, al-Dhahabi lists the works of his that he had come across, among them al-Muhtadarin, alongside other books on death and renunciation such as al-Mawt, al-Qubur, Qisar al-Amal and al-Tawba. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2502} That company suggests that to al-Dhahabi it was one of the author's group of books on death and what follows it, but he does not describe its contents.

## Its transmission and hearing
Al-Dhahabi quotes Ibn al-Sam'ani that he heard from a shaykh in a village near Balkh the Kitab al-Muhtadarin of Abu Bakr ibn Abi al-Dunya, which the shaykh transmitted by licence from Abu Ali al-Wakhshi. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 41 p. 65} In another entry he quotes Ibn al-Najjar that a man heard al-Muhtadarin among other books of Ibn Abi al-Dunya, namely Hilm Mu'awiya, al-Riqqa wa'l-Buka', al-Mawt and al-Tahajjud, and that he heard al-Riqqa and al-Muhtadarin from Abu al-Khayr al-Baghbani. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 49 p. 126} So the book was in circulation in the hearing sessions of the sixth and seventh centuries AH.

## What I did not find
In the books read I found no count of its chapters, no list of the reports it contains, no names of the persons it mentions and no edited text of it. I therefore cannot describe its arrangement and leave it for a reading of the full text or of the catalogues that describe it.
