## Overview
"Tarikh Madinat Dimashq" (The History of the City of Damascus) is the great history by Ibn Asakir al-Dimashqi. Ibn al-Imad reports that it runs to eight hundred volumes in eighty books. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 719}

Author: [[person/ibn-asakir|Ibn Asakir]]. The details of his method and his biographies need a further source.

## Title and the edition in the museum
The title as given on the Dar al-Fikr title page is "The History of the City of Damascus, and mention of its merit and the naming of the distinguished who settled in it or passed through its districts, of its visitors and its people", by Abu al-Qasim Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn Hibat Allah al-Shafi'i, known as Ibn Asakir (499–571 AH). {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 1} The edition is the study and edition of Muhibb al-Din Abu Sa'id Umar ibn Gharama al-Amrawi, Dar al-Fikr, Beirut, 1415 AH / 1995 CE; the museum holds its fifth volume. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 1} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 2}

## Volume five
Volume five holds entries for men named Ahmad, ordered by their fathers' names, beginning with Ahmad ibn Utba ibn Makin, Abu al-Abbas al-Sulami al-Jawbari, and continuing through Ahmad ibn Uthman and Ahmad ibn Ali to the Ahmad ibn Muhammads. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 3} Each entry gives the subject's lineage, whom he heard from in Damascus and who narrated from him, then one or more hadiths of his with Ibn Asakir's chain, then scholars' sayings about him and the date of his death. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 3} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 252} The longest entry in the volume is that of [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] (no. 136), with his lineage and the dispute over it, his journey to Syria, the reports of the Mihna, and the reports of his death and funeral. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 252} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 335} See [[event/death-of-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|the death and funeral of Ahmad ibn Hanbal]].

## Ibn Asakir's method in an entry
Ibn Asakir relates reports through his own chains, gathers the different reports of a single event, and sometimes comments — as when he says of Abu Umayya al-Tarsusi's date for Ahmad's death: "Abu Umayya was not followed in saying the year 242." {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 329} The editor adds in the notes comparisons with Ibn Manzur's abridgement, *Hilyat al-Awliya'*, *Tarikh Baghdad* and al-Dhahabi's books. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 327} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-amrawi-v5 p. 333}
