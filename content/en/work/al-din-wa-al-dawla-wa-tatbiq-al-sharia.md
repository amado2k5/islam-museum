## Summary

*Al-Din wa-l-Dawla wa-Tatbiq al-Shari'a* (Religion, the State and the Application of the Shari'a) is a book by the Moroccan thinker Mohammed Abed al-Jabri, first published by Markaz Dirasat al-Wahda al-'Arabiyya in Beirut in February 1996, as number 29 of its series al-Thaqafa al-Qawmiyya and number 4 of Qadaya al-Fikr al-'Arabi. In his introduction al-Jabri observes that most writing on religion, the state and the application of the shari'a is polemical: it seeks to refute opponents and builds no knowledge. Some who call for a "contemporary reading", he adds, start from hypotheses with no support in the texts or in historical experience, or load the texts with more than they can bear. {cite:archive-org-al-din-wa-al-dawla-jabri-1996} The first part begins with religion and the state in the "heritage frame of reference": the execution of legal rulings, the caliphate and the balance of power, the caliphate's "constitutional gaps", and sultanic ideology and Islamic ethics. It then turns to the "Nahda frame of reference": sectarianism and democracy, and democracy and rationality in place of "secularism"; and then to religion, politics and civil war. Later chapters treat awakening and renewal and the question of the reference point, salafism or the historical experience of the community, and call for an ijtihad that keeps pace with the times. They take up the rationality of legal rulings, how rulings follow their grounds, the maxim of averting fixed penalties in cases of doubt, and the call for the "full application of the shari'a". {cite:archive-org-al-din-wa-al-dawla-jabri-1996}
