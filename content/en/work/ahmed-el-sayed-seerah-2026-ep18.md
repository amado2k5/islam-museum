## Summary

The lecturer opens with a short biography of the Badri Companion Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a, then finishes the section of Ibn Ishaq's Sira on the Quraysh's challenges to the Prophet, with Ibn Hisham's language notes and the Qur'anic answers. He then reads Ibn Ishaq's chapter on the torture of the weak among the early Muslims, the Khabbab report, and the lineage of Banu Zuhra from the book he follows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Biography of Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a, a Badri Companion

- He relates, from the biographical dictionary of the Badri Companions he reads from, that Hatib was an ally of Banu Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza, a well-known early emigrant who fought at Badr and in all the later campaigns. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that al-Hakim described him as a food merchant of short stature, and that al-Marzubani called him one of the horsemen and poets of Quraysh before Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd and al-Hakim that Hatib killed Utba ibn Abi Waqqas, who had wounded the Prophet's face at Uhud, and that the Prophet returned the spoils to him and prayed for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates the report in Sahih Muslim that the Prophet rejected a servant's accusation that Hatib would enter the Fire, because Hatib had been at Badr and al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says that the verse addressing the believers about taking enemies as allies came down about Hatib's letter to Mecca before its conquest, and that it still addresses him as a believer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Ali, al-Zubayr and al-Miqdad intercepted the letter, and that sources differ on its wording: one version warns Quraysh of a large army, another says only that Muhammad has set out and they should be careful. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says the case teaches that great men can err, and that early service in Islam and a store of good deeds weigh against a mistake. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Hatib was the Prophet's envoy to al-Muqawqis in the year 6 AH and brought back Maria the Copt as a gift, that Abu Bakr later sent him to Egypt again, and that he lived into Uthman's caliphate and died in Medina aged sixty-five. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says these short biographies are meant only as an identity record of each Badri Companion, not as a place for lessons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Ibn Ishaq on the Cave youths and the Qur'anic way of answering the Quraysh

- He recalls that the previous session was spent on the relation between the Qur'an and the Sira, mainly the reported occasions of revelation, especially verses tied to Abu Jahl, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith and Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq tells how Quraysh, after consulting the Jews, put three questions to the Prophet: the youths who vanished in time, Dhu al-Qarnayn, and the spirit. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He holds that the Qur'an does not answer such a question with a bare piece of information: the Cave story is shaped to answer the Quraysh's own beliefs and to steady the Prophet, and the number of years comes only at the very end. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He holds that the same method appears in al-Tabari's reading of the story of Ibrahim and his father, where al-Tabari keeps the Prophet's own dispute with Quraysh in mind throughout, and that this is the best way to read Qur'anic stories. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says Ibn Ishaq explains an expression in the Cave account as meaning that the youths did not associate partners with God as the Quraysh do, so Ibn Ishaq too attends to the setting, though he explains only verses tied to the Sira, and al-Tabari knows the Book of God far better. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq treats the Dhu al-Qarnayn question briefly, passes on some material from the lore of non-Arabs and some mursal reports, and then expresses doubt as to whether any of it goes back to the Prophet, which the lecturer takes to mean Ibn Ishaq did not rely much on it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Ibn Hisham's language notes with poetic evidence

- He relates that Ibn Hisham, as is his habit, explains rare words in the Cave and later passages and supports each with a line of Arabic poetry, naming poets such as al-A'sha, Dhu al-Rumma, al-Kumayt and others. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He counts among the words glossed the Cave account's terms for excess, for deviating, for the entrance, and for open space, and later the spring, the fragments of punishment, the face-to-face meeting, and gilded ornament. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says the use of old Arabic verse to explain unusual Qur'anic words is very heavy in both Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham, and that al-Suhayli is a different level again in language. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Quraysh's demands and the Qur'anic replies; the case of Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq continues the long report from Ibn Abbas listing Quraysh's demands: moving the mountains of Mecca, receiving treasures and angels, rising to heaven, and bringing the dead back, followed by the verses sent in reply. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says the exegetes differ on whether the 'better than that' promised to the Prophet means better than walking in the markets or better than the palaces they asked for, and that al-Tabari, as he recalls, preferred the second. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya of Banu Makhzum, the man who stood with Abu Jahl at Abu Talib's deathbed, told the Prophet he would not believe even if he ascended to heaven with a book and four angels as witnesses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says this man later became a Muslim and was killed as a martyr at Ta'if. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### The 'Rahman' objection, Abu Jahl and the guardians of the Fire

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq reports Quraysh saying a man in al-Yamama named al-Rahman taught the Prophet, and that a verse was sent in reply. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq places verses about the man who forbids a servant to pray in connection with Abu Jahl, and that Ibn Hisham glosses the words for seizing by the forelock, the assembly, and the guardians of Hell with verses by Salama ibn Jandal, al-Kumayt, Ibn al-Zib'ara and Sakhr al-Ghayy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Abu Jahl mocked the report that nineteen guardians keep people in the Fire, asking whether a hundred of them could not overcome one such guardian, and that a verse about the angels as keepers was sent in answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says this is the third session spent on God's answers to the objections of the polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Envy, mockery and secret listening to the recitation

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq attributes their stubbornness to envy and quotes a saying among them to drown out the recitation with noise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that when the Prophet recited aloud in prayer they dispersed, and some listeners would secretly listen from a distance and slip away if noticed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates a report through Ikrima from Ibn Abbas that a verse on the loudness of prayer was revealed because of this situation, giving a second explanation beside the earlier one in which it meant hiding from the unbelievers, and notes the editor's reference to al-Bukhari's work on the deeds of servants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Abdullah ibn Mas'ud recites aloud at the Maqam

- He relates, through Yahya ibn Urwa from his father Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, that Abdullah ibn Mas'ud was the first after the Prophet to recite the Qur'an aloud in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that the Companions gathered and asked who would let Quraysh hear it; Ibn Mas'ud volunteered, though they feared for him since he had no clan to protect him, and he went to the Maqam in the morning while Quraysh sat in their gatherings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that he began the chapter called al-Rahman aloud, that the Quraysh men then struck him on the face, that he returned to his companions with marks on it, and that he offered to do the same again the next day, which they declined. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He holds that this implies that chapter was revealed early, that Urwa's mursal reports on the Sira are good and can be relied on, and that the story shows the steadfastness of the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Night listening of Abu Sufyan, Abu Jahl and al-Akhnas

- He relates, through al-Zuhri whose report he calls weaker than Urwa's, that Abu Sufyan, Abu Jahl and al-Akhnas ibn Shariq each went secretly at night to listen to the Prophet praying at home, met on the road at dawn, and for three nights agreed not to repeat it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that al-Akhnas then asked Abu Sufyan and Abu Jahl what they made of what they heard; Abu Sufyan said some of it he understood and some he did not, and Abu Jahl said rivalry with Banu Abd Manaf over honour made him refuse to believe in a prophet from them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says the same pattern of denial out of pride and envy is described in the Qur'an for earlier peoples as well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Their claims of closed hearts and denial of resurrection

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq reports them mocking the recitation by saying their hearts were covered and a screen stood between them and the Prophet, with a verse sent in answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq explains the replies, and says one explanation of Ibn Ishaq's on the closed hearts is open to objection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates a sound report from Ibn Abi Najih through Mujahid that Ibn Abbas explained the phrase about something greater in their breasts as death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says the subject of the Qur'an's replies to the polytheists is vast and he does not know if he will complete it even in twenty years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### How Ibn Ishaq arranged the Sira and the proportion given to disputes

- He says that after the public preaching Ibn Ishaq first gives the negotiations with Abu Talib, then Hamza's conversion, and then mostly details of the Quraysh's disputes with the Prophet and the Qur'an's replies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He observes that in the edition he uses the public preaching begins at page 172 and the debate with the polytheists starts near page 193, so about half of what they read since is such disputes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says this is the last topic from Ibn Ishaq before the emigration to Abyssinia, which he plans for the next session, and that he will then close this main source and move on to the other sources. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Torture of the weak among the early Muslims: Bilal, slaves freed by Abu Bakr, Umar

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that each clan seized its Muslims, imprisoned them, and tortured them with beating, hunger, thirst and the heat of Mecca, some yielding and some holding firm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Umayya ibn Khalaf laid Bilal on his back in the midday heat with a heavy stone on his chest, demanding he give up Muhammad, and that Bilal kept repeating the word for the One. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates, through Hisham ibn Urwa, that Waraqa ibn Nawfal passed by and said he would treat Bilal's grave as sacred if he were killed, but says this detail is doubtful because Waraqa died before the torture, which belongs to the public phase of the preaching. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Abu Bakr bought Bilal from Umayya in exchange for a stronger black slave of his own and freed him, and that with Bilal Abu Bakr freed six others, among them Amir ibn Fuhayra, Umm Ubays and Zinnira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Zinnira lost her sight and Quraysh said their gods had caused it, that she denied it and her sight was restored, and says this oath formula may predate the ban on swearing by the gods or the report may be unestablished. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Abu Bakr also freed al-Nahdiyya and her daughter from a woman of Banu Abd al-Dar, and finished grinding her flour before returning it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Umar, still a polytheist, beat a Muslim slave girl of Banu Mu'ammil until he tired, and that Abu Bakr later bought and freed her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that Sa'id ibn Zayd said Umar had once bound him over his Islam, and the lecturer says Umar was harsh on Muslims before he accepted Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Abu Quhafa told Abu Bakr to free strong men rather than weak slaves, that Abu Bakr answered he did it for God, and that tradition ties a verse about the one who gives and fears God to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Torture of the family of Yasir and the Makhzumite youths

- He relates that Banu Makhzum took Ammar ibn Yasir with his father and mother out in the midday heat, and that Ibn Ishaq has the Prophet passing and speaking words of patience and promise of Paradise to the family. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says Ammar's mother was killed, and that Abu Jahl incited the clan against anyone of standing who became Muslim, threatening ruin of their trade. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates a report from Ibn Abbas that the polytheists tortured believers so hard they could not sit upright and some said their gods were their lords, even of a beetle passing by. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Makhzumite men approached Hisham ibn al-Walid about his brother al-Walid ibn al-Walid, Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a and Salama ibn Hisham, and that Hisham warned he would kill their noblest man if his brother were killed, so they desisted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He distinguishes the Makhzumite youths from Ammar's family, who were clients of Banu Makhzum. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Khabbab's report and Ibn Kathir's reading of the Muslim version

- He says the report of Khabbab ibn al-Aratt asking the Prophet to pray for them is among the important accounts Ibn Ishaq does not give here. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates the version in Muslim where they complained of the heat of the ground and the Prophet did not answer, which some took to concern delaying the noon prayer, a matter he explains with the practice of cooling the noon prayer in Medina's heat. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir in al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya reads the Muslim version as an abbreviation of the Bukhari one: the complaint was about torture and a request for help against the polytheists, and the Prophet's non-response meant he did not pray for it immediately. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates the Bukhari report through al-Humaydi, in which Khabbab came to the Prophet in the Kaaba's shade, asked for prayer, and the Prophet, with his face reddened, spoke of earlier believers combed with iron combs or sawn in two without leaving their faith, and promised the matter would be completed, adding that they were hasty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says al-Bukhari alone has some wording of it, and names al-Humaydi as a teacher of al-Bukhari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Reflections on torture, the strength of souls and the Companions' steadfastness

- He holds that the Khabbab report states plainly that earlier believers were tortured in brutal ways, and that the Prophet cited this to comfort the Companions that they were not the first and had it easier. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He holds that the Companions' torture reached death in the case of Sumayya, whom he says Abu Jahl stabbed, and of Yasir, and severe torture for Ammar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He holds that the severity can be measured by the fact that some of them were driven to utter words of unbelief under compulsion, about whom a Qur'anic verse was revealed, while others such as Bilal refused. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He holds that the gulf between the cruelty of tyrants and the endurance of the faithful shows that the matter turns on souls, since the bodies are alike. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### al-Suhayli on those tortured and on the Companions' names

- He relates that al-Suhayli names Sumayya, mother of Ammar, as the first martyr of Islam, killed by Abu Jahl, and records her as a client of Abu Hudhayfa ibn al-Mughira, Abu Jahl's uncle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that al-Suhayli corrects Ibn Qutayba on her later marriage and gives a note on Ammar's descendants, including one killed in al-Andalus by Abd al-Rahman ibn Mu'awiya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Lineage of Banu Zuhra, the Prophet's maternal kin

- He relates that Banu Zuhra descend from Zuhra ibn Kilab, whose line meets the Prophet's in Kilab, the Prophet's fifth ancestor, and that Amina bint Wahb, the Prophet's mother, belongs to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that two of the ten promised Paradise, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, were from Banu Zuhra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Zuhra had two sons, Abd Manaf and al-Harith, and says the branches are easily confused because of repeated names. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates among the line of Abd Manaf: Wahb, the Prophet's maternal grandfather; al-Aswad ibn Abd Yaghuth, a leading mocker who is said to have been blinded and bereaved after a prayer against him; and Abdullah ibn al-Arqam, who accepted Islam at the conquest, served as scribe, and held the treasury under Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates Makhrama ibn Nawfal, a genealogist who became Muslim at the conquest and died in Medina in 54 AH, and his son al-Miswar ibn Makhrama, born two years after the hijra, who sided with his uncle Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf in the shura. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates the line of Malik ibn Uhayb, called Abu Waqqas: his sons Sa'd, Umayr who died at Badr, Amir who emigrated to Abyssinia, and Utba, whom he says has no evidence of Islam and who broke the Prophet's tooth at Uhud and was cursed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates that Utba's son Hashim, called al-Mirqal, fought at al-Yarmuk and al-Qadisiyya and was killed at Siffin carrying Ali's banner. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He relates the other branch from al-Harith ibn Zuhra, where Abd ibn al-Harith was the line of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and of Talha ibn Abdullah ibn Awf, a judge of Medina under Yazid and a noted generous man, not to be confused with Talha ibn Ubaydullah of Banu Taym. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf was born ten years after the Year of the Elephant, accepted Islam before the Prophet entered Dar al-Arqam, and was brothered by the Prophet with Sa'd ibn al-Rabi'. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He remarks that many early Muslims were young, citing ages for al-Zubayr, al-Arqam and others, and that he plans to do the same for other Qurayshi clans and the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}

### Recap and pointer to the Qur'anic stances on the polytheists

- He summarises that the session covered Hatib's biography, Ibn Hisham's language notes on the Cave and later verses, the Qur'anic replies to the polytheists, the torture of the weak, and the lineage of Banu Zuhra. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He says the Meccan chapters, among them al-Isra, al-Zukhruf, al-Mu'minun, Saba and Sad, hold dozens of sayings of the polytheists, each met with a reply, and that al-An'am alone contains forty-four commands to the Prophet to answer in the form of a set response. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
- He refers listeners to an earlier session titled the stances of the Qur'an, which applied this reading to the chapter Fatir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep18}
