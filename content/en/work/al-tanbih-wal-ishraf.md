## Summary
Al-Tanbih wa'l-Ishraf is al-Mas'udi's compendious summary of astronomy, nature, geography and chronicles including the caliphs' lives to 345 AH, which he describes at the end of Muruj al-Dhahab. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab}

## Details
At the close of Muruj al-Dhahab al-Mas'udi writes that al-Tanbih wa'l-Ishraf contains digests on the celestial spheres and their configurations, the stars and their influences, the elements and their combinations, the divisions of times and the seasons and their mansions, the winds and where they blow, the earth's form and extent, the regions and horizons and their effect on inhabitants, the boundaries of the seven climes, latitudes and longitudes and the mouths of rivers. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab} It also treats the seven ancient nations with their languages and dwellings, then the kings of Persia by their generations, the Romans and their affairs, compendia of world and prophetic chronology, the reckoning of lunar and solar years, the sira of the Prophet and the rise of Islam, and the lives, ages and merits of the caliphs down to 345 AH — containing, as he describes it, much found in no other book of history. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab} In the same place it is noted that the book was printed at Leiden in 1894 within the Geographical Library in five hundred pages. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab}
