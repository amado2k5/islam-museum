## Summary
Kitab al-Zuhd ("The Book of Renunciation") of Imam [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] (164–241 AH) is a collection of reports with chains on renunciation, transmitted from him by his son [[person/abdullah-ibn-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Abd Allah]] together with additions from Abd Allah's own teachers. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 7} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 12} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-juz13-rayahin-2026 p. 18} The publisher of the Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya edition describes it as setting out the renunciation of the prophets, foremost the Prophet Muhammad, then of the Companions, then of the Successors, and quotes [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]]: "the best work written on it is the Kitab al-Zuhd of Imam Ahmad". {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 7}

## Transmission and chain
The printed text opens with a chain beginning with a reading to Ibn al-Munhar of Damascus in 708, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi al-Fahm al-Badani, from Abu al-Qasim Yahya ibn As'ad ibn Bawsh, from Abd al-Qadir al-Yusufi, from al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn al-Mudhhib, read in Rabi' al-Awwal 443, from Abu Bakr al-Qati'i, from Abd Allah ibn Ahmad, from his father. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 12} It begins with reports on the Prophet's life and work at home, such as A'isha's saying that his practice was "constant", that he would patch his garment and mend his sandal, and that he died with his armour pledged to a Jew for thirty measures of food. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 12} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 p. 13}

## Structure

The reports about prophets are transmitted with chains to Companions, Successors and those they quoted, and are presented here as attributed to their narrators.

## The printed book and its larger original
Abu Umar Mahmud ibn Shawqi ibn Muflih holds that the current printed Kitab al-Zuhd "is only an abridgement of this great, large work", relying on Ibn Khayr al-Ishbili's note in his Fahrasa, after giving his chain for the book: "it is twenty parts". {cite:zuhd-ahmad-juz13-rayahin-2026 p. 7} He says he had earlier edited a manuscript titled al-Muntakhab min Kitab al-Zuhd li-l-Imam Ahmad and found reports in it that are not in the printed text, so he searched until he found parts of the original. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-juz13-rayahin-2026 p. 7}

From these he published "Part 13" from a fragment in the Zahiriyya Library in Damascus (hadith section, no. 1131), of 105 folios with the beginning lost; at its end it reads "end of the thirteenth part", followed by parts 19 and 20, which he published separately with Dar al-Rayahin. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-juz13-rayahin-2026 p. 17} The part holds 592 reports, most from Abu al-Darda', [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]] and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz; the fragment is in the transmission of Abu al-Qasim Yahya ibn As'ad ibn Bawsh al-Azaji, and all its chains run through Abd Allah ibn Ahmad from his father, or are Abd Allah's additions. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-juz13-rayahin-2026 p. 17} {cite:zuhd-ahmad-juz13-rayahin-2026 p. 18} The first surviving report is Abu Hazim's saying that no servant makes good what is between him and God without God making good what is between him and people. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-juz13-rayahin-2026 p. 24}

## For young visitors
In this book Imam Ahmad collected reports about prophets, Companions and Successors who lived simply and did not cling to this world, such as how the Prophet patched his own clothes.
