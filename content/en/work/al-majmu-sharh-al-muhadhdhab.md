## Summary

Al-Majmu' Sharh al-Muhadhdhab is Imam [[person/al-nawawi|Yahya ibn Sharaf al-Nawawi]]'s commentary on Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi's [[work/al-muhadhdhab-fiqh-shafii|al-Muhadhdhab]] in [[group/shafii-madhhab|Shafi'i]] law. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.5} The museum's copy is volume 8 of "the only complete edition", corrected, annotated and "completed after it had been incomplete" by [[person/muhammad-najib-al-mutii|Muhammad Najib al-Muti'i]] of Omdurman Islamic University, published by Maktabat al-Irshad, Jeddah. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.5}

## Al-Nawawi's method

Al-Nawawi explains the author's wording and sets out the school's doctrine and the views of its masters; for example, he says circumambulation is valid only in a state of purity, for every kind of tawaf, "so al-Shafi'i and the masters stated decisively in all the lines of transmission", then reports a weak view from Abu Ya'qub al-Abiwardi and the Imam al-Haramayn's rejection of it. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27} He then gives a section headed "on the views of the scholars", naming the Companions, Successors and imams who agree with the Shafi'i position and those who differ, the evidence of each side and his colleagues' replies to the opponents. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.28} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108} He draws often on Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Mawardi for the schools' views. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.434} He grades hadiths, saying for example that the hadith of Safiyya bint Shayba on the sa'y was narrated by al-Daraqutni and al-Bayhaqi "with a good chain", and that the sound view of the report "circumambulation of the House is prayer" is that it stops at Ibn Abbas. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.28} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108}

## Some "views of the scholars" sections in this volume

Such sections are frequent in volume 8; among them are the views on entering the Ka'ba, raising the hands on seeing it, purity in [[practice/tawaf|tawaf]], questions of tawaf, the status of the [[practice/sai|sa'y]], standing at Arafa, combining sunset and night prayers at Muzdalifa, stoning the Jamrat al-Aqaba, whether shaving is a rite, being prevented from completing the pilgrimage, the [[practice/udhiyya|sacrifice]] with its time, age, defects, the naming of God over it and eating from it, the [[practice/aqiqa|birth sacrifice]], and vows to commit a sin. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.13} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.16} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.83} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.146} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.168} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.182} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.197} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.322} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.358} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.363} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.370} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.383} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.391} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.400} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.434} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.446}

## Al-Muti'i's additions

At the start of the volume al-Muti'i places an "editor's addendum" on the ruling of the talbiya, quoting Ibn Hajar in Fath al-Bari that there are four views on it, which can be extended to ten, and correcting al-Nawawi's account of Malik's view. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.6} What al-Muti'i writes is kept distinct from al-Nawawi's text in the edition.
