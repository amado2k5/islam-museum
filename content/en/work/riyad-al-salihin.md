## Overview
Al-Nawawi's book of encouragement, restraint and manners, arranged under chapters bearing on conduct, in which he gathered chosen hadiths from the books of the Sunna.

## What the Encyclopaedia of Islam says
The second edition of the Encyclopaedia of Islam treats the book only in passing, inside its article on its author, the Shafii jurist [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]]. It names the Riyad al-salihin among the works that it says owe their existence to his mystical tendencies, and it dates its completion to 670/1271-2. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1097}

## Place among al-Nawawi's mystical writings
The article links these tendencies to the fact that al-Nawawi attended lectures on the Risala of [[person/al-qushayri-abu-l-qasim|al-Qushayri]] and transmitted that work; see [[work/al-risala-al-qushayriyya|al-Risala al-Qushayriyya]]. Besides the Riyad al-salihin, it lists in the same group his Kitab al-Adhkar on the prayers, finished in 667/1268-9, and the unfinished [[work/bustan-al-arifin|Bustan al-arifin]], on renunciation and Sufism. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1097}

## Editions and translation
The article records printings of the Riyad al-salihin at Mecca in 1302 and 1312 of the Hijri calendar, and an English translation by Muhammad Zafrulla Khan, published in London in 1975. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 1097}

## Limits of this entry
The encyclopaedia entry gives no account of the book's chapters, its sources or its reception, and this page does not go beyond it. The Overview above is the museum's own short description of the work.
