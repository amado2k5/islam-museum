## Summary
*Fada'il al-Sahaba* ("The Merits of the Companions") is a book on the virtues of the Prophet's Companions compiled by [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] (164–241 AH); Ibn al-Nadim lists it among his works as *Kitab al-Fada'il* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.29-30}. It reached us in the transmission of his son [[person/abdullah-ibn-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Abd Allah ibn Ahmad]], and then of [[person/al-qatiqi|Abu Bakr al-Qati'i]] from Abd Allah {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.53}. In the edition of Wasi Allah ibn Muhammad Abbas it contains 1,962 numbered narrations, the last of them on the merits of Banu Hashim {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1255-1256}.

## Three layers of narrations
The editor points out that the book's material is of three kinds: what Abd Allah narrated from his father; what Abd Allah added from his own teachers other than his father; and what al-Qati'i added from his teachers other than Abd Allah {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.46}. After studying the chains, the editor judges that weak material is rare in the first kind, most of which is sound; that the second kind has more weak material; and that most of the third is weak and contains many fabricated reports from abandoned transmitters {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.46}. He supports this with [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]]'s remark in *Minhaj al-Sunna* that al-Qati'i's additions contain many fabricated hadiths which some people mistook for Ahmad's own transmission {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.46-47}.

## The chain to the author
The book opens with its connected chain: Abu al-Fadl [[person/ibn-nasir-al-sulami|Muhammad ibn Nasir al-Salami]] read it to [[person/abu-al-husayn-al-tayuri|Abu al-Husayn al-Mubarak ibn Abd al-Jabbar al-Sayrafi]] in Rajab 500 AH; he had heard it from [[person/ibn-al-allaf-abu-tahir|Abu Tahir Ibn al-Allaf]] in Dhu al-Qa'da 436; Ibn al-Allaf read it to al-Qati'i on Saturday 16 Jumada al-Akhira 368; al-Qati'i had it from Abd Allah, from his father {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.37}. The editor states that all the men of this chain are known and trustworthy, and gives each a short biography {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.37-42}. The title page records that some parts, such as the merits of al-Hasan, al-Husayn, Fatima and Khadija, came by other routes to Ibn Malik al-Qati'i, including those of Abu Muhammad al-Jawhari, [[person/ibn-al-mudhhib|Abu Ali Ibn al-Mudhhib]] and [[person/abu-ishaq-al-barmaki|Abu Ishaq al-Barmaki]] {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.53-54}.

## The chapters
The text begins with hadiths forbidding the reviling of the Companions and urging that their due be kept, among them "Do not revile my Companions", whose chain the editor grades sound {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.58-60}. Then come the merits of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] (p. 76), the chapter on the saying of [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and others, "The best of this community after its Prophet are Abu Bakr and Umar" (p. 90), and the hadiths of the *khulla*, of Abu Bakr leading the prayer, and of the first to accept Islam {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1381}. Next are the merits of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] and his conversion (pp. 299 and 339), the "chapter on the best of this community after its Prophet" (p. 367), then the merits of [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] (p. 547), then the reports on Ali, his lineage, his mother's name and his merits, with al-Qati'i's additions (pp. 648–908) {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1381-1382}. After these come chapters on Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, al-Zubayr, Abu Ubayda, Talha and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas; then Fatima; al-Hasan and al-Husayn; the Ansar; Khalid ibn al-Walid; Sa'd ibn Mu'adh; Haritha ibn al-Nu'man; Suhayb; the Arabs; Usama ibn Zayd; Ibn Mas'ud; Ibn Abbas; Khadija; Ammar; the people of Yemen; Aisha; Ghifar and Aslam; Ja'far; Jarir ibn Abd Allah; Ibn Umar; a group of the people of Syria; Amr ibn al-As; Mu'awiya; al-Abbas; and finally Abd Allah ibn Abbas {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1382-1383}. The text closes with reports on love for Banu Hashim and the words "End of the Fada'il, praise be to God, Lord of the worlds" {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1256}.

## The manuscript
The edition rests on a unique manuscript, noted by Brockelmann and Fuat Sezgin, in the Yeni Cami library, no. 778 {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.33}. It has 201 folios, the text ending on folio 200, nineteen lines a page in a clear naskh hand; its copyist was Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Qurashi {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.33}. From a reading-note at its end the editor infers that it was copied before 544 AH {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.34}. Its margins show that it was collated with an original in the hand of Ibn al-Furat and with the copies of al-Khatib, Ibn al-Mudhhib and al-Barmaki; one margin note reads, for example, "end of the first part of Ibn al-Furat's original" {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.35-36}. The editor found no other complete copy, only a part, "The Merits of Ali", photographed from a Yemeni manuscript finished on 22 Rajab 1027 in Sanaa, 62 folios with many errors; he collated it with the main copy and marked it with the letter *ya'* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.36-37}.

## The reading-notes (sama'at)
Reading-notes at the end of the manuscript show how it circulated. One records that a group heard the whole book from Ibn Nasir al-Salami, read aloud by Diya' al-Din Abu Ahmad Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ali al-Amin, in sessions ending on Tuesday 6 Shawwal 544, and Ibn Nasir certified this in his own hand {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1258}. Another records the reading of the reports and merits of Ali to the chief of the nobles (*naqib al-nuqaba'*) Abu Abd Allah Ahmad ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Mu'ammar al-Alawi in 564, read by Abd al-Mughith ibn Zuhayr al-Harbi {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1258-1260}. The last reading-note the editor reports belongs to sessions that ended in Safar 667 at the Ribat al-Sadri {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.1261}.

## Authenticity and use by later scholars
The editor lists those who mention the book or quote from it: al-Baghawi in *Mu'jam al-Sahaba*, Ibn al-Nadim in the *Fihrist*, Ibn Abi al-Hadid in *Sharh Nahj al-Balagha*, [[person/muhibb-al-din-al-tabari|al-Muhibb al-Tabari]] in *Dhakha'ir al-Uqba* and *al-Riyad al-Nadira* (who calls it "Ahmad's *Manaqib*"), Ibn Taymiyya in *Minhaj al-Sunna*, Sibt Ibn al-Jawzi in *Tadhkirat al-Khawass*, al-Dhahabi in *Siyar A'lam al-Nubala'*, Ibn al-Jazari in *al-Mas'ad al-Ahmad* and al-Sakhawi in *al-Maqasid al-Hasana* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.42-43}. He observes that al-Muhibb al-Tabari loosely ascribes to Ahmad narrations that are in fact additions by Abd Allah or al-Qati'i {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.42-43}.

## The edition
[[person/wasi-allah-abbas|Wasi Allah ibn Muhammad Abbas]] edited the book as a doctoral thesis; it was first printed by the Centre for Scientific Research and the Revival of Islamic Heritage at Umm al-Qura University in 1403 AH, and he later revised it and gave it to Dar Ibn al-Jawzi, whose second edition carries his preface dated 25 Rabi' II 1420 {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.5-6}. The editor says he wrote detailed biographies of more than 1,762 transmitters, not limited to *al-Taqrib* or *al-Kashif*, graded every chain as sound, good or weak, traced each hadith with its corroborating routes, and made indexes of hadiths, Companion musnads, persons, places, tribes and rare words {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.44-46}. His introduction has four chapters: the definition of a Companion and their uprightness; books written on the Companions; Ahmad's biography and works; and the description and attribution of the manuscript {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.8}.

## Its place in the field
The editor places the book in the line of works on the Companions and their merits, naming those of Ibn Sa'd, Khalifa ibn Khayyat, al-Bukhari, Muslim, Ibn Abi Dawud, al-Baghawi, Ibn Qani' and Ibn Hibban, the *Fada'il al-Sahaba* of Khaythama ibn Sulayman and of al-Daraqutni, Ibn Manda's *Ma'rifat al-Sahaba*, and then *al-Isti'ab*, *Usd al-Ghaba* and *al-Isaba* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.21-24}. He explains his interest in the book by the fact that the Companions transmitted the Qur'an and the Sunna, and that setting out their merits answers those who attacked them, quoting [[person/abu-zura-al-razi|Abu Zur'a al-Razi]] from al-Khatib's *al-Kifaya* {cite:fadail-sahaba-ahmad-wasi-allah-1999 p.7-8}.
