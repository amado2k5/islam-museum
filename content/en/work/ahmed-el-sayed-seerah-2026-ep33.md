## Summary

The lecturer closes the Meccan period by following Ibn Ishaq's late-Meccan material: the revelation setting of the Kawthar chapter, the mockers and their fates, the deaths of Khadija and Abu Talib, the journey to Ta'if and the jinn at Nakhla. He then treats the splitting of the moon, the Roman-Persian prophecy and Abu Bakr's wager, and ends with the Night Journey and Ascension as reported in the two Sahih collections. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### Opening and Ibn Ishaq's ordering of late Meccan events

- He says this session nears the end of the Meccan phase and aims to finish the remaining Meccan events so the next session can open the preparation for Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He recalls that the previous session covered the tearing up of the boycott document and the conversion of al-Tufayl ibn Amr al-Dawsi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that Ibn Ishaq is the main authority for the order and presentation of the biography, yet that his arrangement of this stretch is open to discussion because he places the Night Journey early and relies on mursal reports for it rather than on chains. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He says he will skip some of Ibn Ishaq's items, such as the long story of the man from Irash who sold camels to Abu Jahl, because it lacks reliable chains. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### The revelation setting of the Kawthar chapter

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq reports al-As ibn Wa'il al-Sahmi calling the Prophet cut off, without male descendants, and that the Kawthar chapter came down in answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Ibn Hisham adds the lexical note that the word means abundance and cites poetry by Labid and al-Kumayt to illustrate it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates a report from Anas ibn Malik, through Ibn Ishaq, describing Kawthar as a river and a basin whose vessels match the stars in number. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that Ibn Ishaq's Meccan placement of the chapter is doubtful and that the chapter is more likely Medinan, relying on a report in Sahih Muslim in which the Prophet is in the mosque in Medina when it is sent down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates a report in Musnad al-Bazzar from Ibn Abbas, graded sound by Ibn Kathir in his account, in which Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf comes to Mecca and Quraysh complain to him about the Prophet; the chapter's hater is then understood as Ka'b rather than al-As. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He praises Ibn Ishaq for tying Qur'anic revelation to the events of the biography, calling that a key way of understanding the sira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Other revelation settings Ibn Ishaq lists

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq links a verse of the Bee chapter about a foreign-tongued teacher to a Christian youth, Jabr, whom the idolaters accused of teaching the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He says other reports name the man Bal'am and say he was a non-Arab servant the Prophet taught, and that al-Tabari transmits this from Ibn Abbas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He notes that the Qur'an records many sayings of the idolaters and answers them, and that Ibn Ishaq also lists a setting about weak believers and one about the demand that an angel accompany the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He recalls that he gave a long earlier session, the seventeenth, to the revelation settings of Meccan verses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### Outline of the tenth year and the order of events

- He says the tenth year of the mission opens with the tearing up of the boycott document, which had held the Prophet and the Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib under siege for three years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that relief was followed at once by new trials: the death of Khadija, the death of Abu Talib, the trip to Ta'if, the jinn episode on the way back, then the Night Journey, then the approaches to tribes and the Ansar leading to the migration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that Ibn Ishaq's placing of the Night Journey before Ta'if and before the two deaths is questionable, and that Ibn Sa'd, al-Nawawi and Ibn Hazm put it about a year before the migration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He says that if it came a year before the migration, Khadija would not have lived to see the five prayers laid down, and that one report supports this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The mockers and the harassers of the Prophet

- He relates, from Ibn Ishaq via Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, five noble mockers of Quraysh: two men named al-Aswad, al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, al-As ibn Wa'il and al-Harith ibn al-Talatila. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says each met a bodily end after Jibril pointed toward them while the Prophet was at the Ka'ba, and treats this as a tangible form of God shielding his Prophet, noting the report is mursal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq names neighbours who harassed the Prophet at home, among them Abu Lahab, al-Hakam ibn Abi al-As and Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, and that the Prophet used a screen while praying. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He notes admiringly that Ibn Ishaq often gives chains even for small details. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### Deaths of Khadija and Abu Talib

- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says Khadija and Abu Talib died in the same year, about three years before the migration, and describes Khadija as a faithful aide and Abu Talib as a support and protector. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates from Urwa that after Abu Talib died Quraysh went further in harming the Prophet, and a foolish man threw dust on his head, which his daughter washed away weeping. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He says Abu Talib died without declaring the faith, with Abu Jahl and Abd Allah ibn Abi Umayya, brother of Umm Salama, beside him, and his last words tied him to the creed of Abd al-Muttalib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that kinship alone does not entail faith, comparing Abu Lahab, and that Abu Talib was held back by loyalty to his forefathers rather than arrogance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds, citing a report in the two Sahih collections, that Abu Talib's defence of the Prophet lightened his punishment only. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that Abd Allah ibn Jud'an, praised for generosity, is not helped in the hereafter without faith, citing a report in Sahih Muslim from Aisha, and that the matter belongs to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He ties two Qur'anic verses to the episode, one that the Prophet cannot guide whom he loves and one forbidding asking forgiveness for idolaters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Marriages in the tenth year and the memory of Khadija

- He says that in the tenth year the Prophet married Sawda bint Zam'a and contracted marriage with Aisha, who by her own account was six at the contract and nine at consummation in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that nobody replaced Khadija for the Prophet, who kept mentioning her, shared meat with her friends and was moved at the voice of her sister Hala. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Aisha said she had never been as jealous of any woman as of Khadija. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He says Khadija was the first Muslim after the Prophet, and that he marvels at her support despite having seven children by his count. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that losing Khadija and Abu Talib at once removed the Prophet's inner and outer support. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### The journey to Ta'if

- He says Musa ibn Uqba, cited as an early sira author whom Malik preferred over Ibn Ishaq, relates from al-Zuhri that the Prophet went to Ta'if after Abu Talib died hoping for shelter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He distinguishes this hope of shelter from what the Ansar later offered, namely protection backed by arms. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that the Ta'if leaders refused the Prophet and set their fools on him, and that he left distressed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds, from a report in the two Sahih collections from Aisha, that the day at the pass was the hardest the Prophet faced, harder than Uhud, and that he came to himself only at Qarn al-Tha'alib, which he identifies with Qarn al-Manazil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He offers his own reading that the event was so heavy because it came as the last resort after the two deaths. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### Thaqif, the vineyard and Addas

- He relates Ibn Ishaq's mursal account, via Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, of three Thaqif brothers who answered the Prophet with scorn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He notes that some commentators name men of Thaqif, among them one of the same family, as possible referents of the verse about a great man from the two towns. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that the Prophet asked them to keep the matter quiet, but they set fools and slaves on him until he took refuge in the vineyard of Utba and Shayba, sons of Rabi'a. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq gives a prayer of the Prophet there, complaining of his weakness and lowly standing with people, and describes the episode as one about his humiliation before people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that the Christian servant Addas from Nineveh brought grapes, recognised the speech as unlike local talk, learned the Prophet spoke of Yunus as a prophet and kissed his head, hands and feet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### The jinn at Nakhla

- He says that on the way to Mecca the road passes Wadi Nakhla, where the story of the jinn took place, and that he treated its possible repetition in an earlier session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq says the Prophet was praying at night at Nakhla when seven jinn from Nasibin listened, believed and returned to warn their people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### The splitting of the moon

- He says the splitting of the moon is an event of late Meccan days attested in a Qur'anic verse and in Sahih al-Bukhari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Ibn Mas'ud reported the idolaters asked for a sign at Mina and the moon was shown in two parts, which he counts among the signs given to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### The Roman-Persian prophecy and Abu Bakr's wager

- He says the opening of the Rum chapter belongs to the late Meccan period, and notes the date of the Roman victory is placed by some at Badr and by others at Hudaybiyya, so the revelation cannot be later than about the tenth year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that it was a striking prophecy because the Persians had overrun Syria and Palestine and nothing then suggested a Roman recovery within a few years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates from a report in al-Tirmidhi, graded good, sound and strange there, that Abu Bakr wagered the idolaters on the outcome before the wager ban, fixed six years, lost the stake, then the Romans won in the seventh year, and many people became Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He notes that Ibn Ishaq omitted this story, while al-Khazin in his Rawd wa-Hada'iq added it to Ibn Hisham's material. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He says the Persian-Roman wars are recorded in sources of every region and faith, and that the length of years is disputed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

### The Night Journey and the Ascension, from Sahih Muslim

- He says al-Khazin notes the Night Journey came a year before the migration and gives this as the view of Urwa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He holds that the Night Journey carried comfort, signs, knowledge and legislation, joining Mecca and Jerusalem and the prophets, and that it came when Muslims were few and weak. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He reads the report of Anas in Sahih Muslim: the Prophet rides the Buraq to Jerusalem, prays two units, and chooses milk over wine, which Jibril calls the natural disposition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates the sequence of heavens: Adam, Isa and Yahya, Yusuf, Idris, Harun, Musa, and Ibrahim at the Frequented House, then the Lote Tree. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that fifty prayers a day were prescribed and, by repeated return to Musa on the way down, reduced to five, with the reward of fifty kept. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates added details from the two Sahih collections, via Abu Dharr and Malik ibn Sa'sa'a: the Prophet's chest was opened and washed with Zamzam, he heard the scratching of pens, saw Paradise, and four rivers at the Lote Tree, and Musa wept that a younger prophet's followers would exceed his. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Reactions after the Night Journey

- He relates that the Prophet told the idolaters what he saw, and that one account says Abu Jahl gathered people to hear it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that Abu Bakr affirmed it on hearing it and was thereafter called al-Siddiq, and that some scholars say the event was also a trial for certain Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He says he takes the vision in the Isra chapter to be one of the eye and not of sleep, and links the cursed tree to the zaqqum. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
- He relates that when the idolaters asked him to describe Jerusalem he could not recall details until God made it visible to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep33}
