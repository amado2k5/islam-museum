## Summary

Musnad al-Bazzar is the work of al-Hafiz [[person/al-bazzar-abu-bakr-ahmad-ibn-amr|Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Amr ibn Abd al-Khaliq al-Bazzar]] of Basra (d. 292 AH), who gathered his teachers' hadiths arranged by the Companions who related them and discussed their defects. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.98} Its author gave it no title; it became known as Musnad al-Bazzar, and was later called "the Large Musnad" to distinguish it from his smaller one, "the Annotated Musnad" (al-Mu'allal), as in al-Dhahabi and al-Suyuti, and "the Overflowing Sea" (al-Bahr al-Zakhkhar); the thesis author prefers its best-known name, Musnad al-Bazzar. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.98} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.99} The printed edition from Maktabat al-Ulum wa-l-Hikam in Medina bears the title al-Bahr al-Zakhkhar al-Ma'ruf bi-Musnad al-Bazzar. {cite:musnad-bazzar-ulum-hikam-2005-v13 p.1} {cite:musnad-bazzar-ulum-hikam-2005-v13 p.3}

## Arrangement

Al-Bazzar arranged it by the Companions in order of merit and rank: first the four caliphs, then the rest of the Ten, then al-Abbas, al-Hasan and al-Husayn, and so on; within each Companion's section he grouped the hadiths by those who related from him — Thabit from Anas, then Qatada from Anas — and then by those who related from each Follower. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.70} The thesis notes that he did not always keep to this order, since the method of exposing defects sometimes required gathering all the routes of a hadith in one place to show its defect or its corroborations. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.70}

## His method of exposing defects

Among its marks are his statements of sole transmission — "we know of no one who related this hadith from Anas except Qatada" — at the level of the Companion, the Follower, or every layer of the chain; his counting of the hadiths one narrator related from another; his choice of the route giving a hadith in full; and his notes on corroborating routes and differing wordings. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.71} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.72} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.73} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.74} The thesis quotes Ibn Kathir's remark that Musnad al-Bazzar contains discussion of defects found in no other musnad. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.36} {cite:musnad-bazzar-ulum-hikam-2005-v13 p.1}

## Its features, as the study counts them

The thesis lists among its features: its many hadiths not found in the Six Books — citing Dr Abd Allah al-Lihyani's count of 3,698 beyond the Six Books and 1,052 beyond the Six Books and Ahmad's Musnad; its selection, which leaves out weak hadiths unless for a reason it states; its being an annotated musnad; al-Bazzar's own judgements on narrators; its many singular and rare reports, so that Ibn Hajar counted it among the places to look for them; its inclusion of 122 Companions whose hadiths do not appear in the great musnads such as Ahmad's; its good arrangement; and the rarity of fabricated reports in it. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.36} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.37}

## Scholars' attention

[[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] made it one of the ten books whose hadiths he gathered in Jami' al-Masanid wa-l-Sunan, and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-bakr-al-haythami|al-Haythami]] extracted its hadiths not in the Six Books in Kashf al-Astar an Zawa'id al-Bazzar, then combined them with the additions of other works in Majma' al-Zawa'id wa-Manba' al-Fawa'id. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.35} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.36}

## Transmission and manuscripts

Al-Bazzar related it in Egypt from memory, and there Muhammad ibn Ayyub ibn al-Samut, the transmitter of the Large Musnad, related it from him. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.17} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.100} For the section of the Musnad of [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]] the thesis relies on the Azhar Library manuscript (604/1, Hadith 924), the only copy containing the Musnad of Anas: 296 folios, copied in 509 AH, containing the Musnad of Ibn Umar, the whole Musnad of Anas divided into eight parts, and a large part of the Musnad of Abu Hurayra. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.101} The section edited in the thesis has 266 hadiths, most of them related by [[person/qatada-ibn-diama|Qatada]] from Anas, which al-Bazzar received from 105 teachers. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.1} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.39}
