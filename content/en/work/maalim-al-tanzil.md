## Summary
Ma'alim al-Tanzil ("Waymarks of the Revelation") is the Qur'an commentary of [[person/al-baghawi-al-husayn-ibn-muhammad|al-Husayn ibn Mas'ud al-Baghawi]], called Muhyi al-Sunna ("reviver of the Sunna", d. 516 AH), and is known as Tafsir al-Baghawi. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.3} The editors of the Dar Tayba edition describe it as among the most eminent commentaries, the closest to the Qur'an and Sunna, and the freest of innovation and weak hadith. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.3}

## Why it was written
He wrote it at the request of a group of his companions, following the early generations in recording knowledge for those who come after; he aimed at a middle course between the tediously long and the deficiently short, hoping it would benefit whoever set out to learn from it. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.3}

## Method
The editors say al-Baghawi explains the Qur'an by the Qur'an, by hadith or by reports, in brief, plain wording without strain or length; brings together verses of one meaning; attends to the readings; sets out and defends the Sunni view without neglecting its opponents' views; and treats legal rulings, sometimes setting out the jurists' views at length and sometimes briefly. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.3} He gives hadith and reports with his own chains, and has a neat device for his chains to the Companions and Successors: he lists them at the start of the book, sparing repetition when he quotes them. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.3} It thus belongs to [[concept/tafsir-bil-mathur|tafsir by transmitted report]], the heading under which the King Fahd National Library catalogues its abridgement. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.2}

An example of his manner is the opening of al-Fatiha: he gives its names — the Opening of the Book, the Mother of the Qur'an, the Seven Oft-Repeated — with the reason for each, then says it is Meccan according to most, that Mujahid said Medinan, and that it is said to have come down twice; see [[question/where-was-al-fatiha-revealed|Where was al-Fatiha revealed?]]. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.7}

## Editions and abridgement
Dar Tayba of Riyadh has published a critical edition since 1409 AH, in six printings of eight volumes; the edition was then revised and reset in four volumes in 1423 AH. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.3} The same editors, Sulayman Muslim al-Harsh, Uthman Jum'a Dumayriyya and Muhammad Abd Allah al-Nimr, then prepared the one-volume Tahdhib Tafsir al-Baghawi in 1430 AH, keeping the author's wording and style: they kept only some of the sound and acceptable reports where several exist, shortened the chains while keeping the Companion's name, and dropped the [[concept/israiliyyat|Isra'iliyyat]] and the legal views and readings the general reader does not need. {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.1} {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.2} {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.3} {cite:tahdhib-tafsir-baghawi-tayba-2009 p.4}
