## Summary

The speaker recaps what the series has covered and announces a fixed segment on a Meccan sura in each session, then reflects on Sura al-Najm. He then turns to Ibn Sa'd's volume on early Muslims who did not fight at Badr and gives short biographies, clan by clan, of those who emigrated to Abyssinia, with notes on where each died. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Recap of the series and new format

- He says this is the twenty-seventh session and that earlier sessions mixed a reading of Ibn Hisham's Sira from the mission onward, reflection on Qur'anic passages, biographies of the early Muslims and lessons drawn from events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that fifteen lessons were drawn from the first six years, and that the Abyssinia episode and the siege in the ravine received long pauses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He says that from now on each session will include a Qur'anic sura or passage tied to the years under discussion, without claiming precise dating, and that a full session on Sura al-An'am is planned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He lists suras he links to events: al-Rum with Abu Bakr's wager with the polytheists, al-Najm, al-Isra, al-Qamar with the splitting of the moon, and al-Kahf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Sura al-Najm and the prostration of those present

- He relates that the Prophet recited Sura al-Najm to the polytheists and that all who heard it prostrated at its end, Muslims, polytheists and, according to a sound report, jinn as well. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He notes that it was said this episode was the reason the emigrants returned from Abyssinia, and holds that it therefore falls in the sixth year or the end of the fifth, before the seventh year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He holds that the sura was revealed whole, and describes its structure as moving from the unseen world, to the polytheists' errors, to the Creator, to earlier nations, to warning and the command to prostrate. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He says the sura mentions Jibril and his sighting, as does Sura al-Takwir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The idols named in the sura and the polytheists' reliance on conjecture

- He holds that the sura strips the three goddesses of any inherent sanctity, calling them mere names given by the polytheists and their forefathers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He points out that several Qurayshis, including Abu Lahab, bore names compounded with al-Uzza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He explains that the sura contrasts the polytheists' guesswork with proofs held by the Muslims, and draws the lesson that a person on the side of truth should ask opponents for their evidence and history. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He observes that Meccan suras commonly speak at length about God's knowledge, power and sovereignty, often as a comparison with the deities, and cites the opening of al-An'am as a summary. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Ibn Sa'd's volume on early Muslims who missed Badr

- He says the series so far drew only on Ibn Sa'd's volume on Badr veterans and overlooked his separate volume on emigrants and Ansar of early Islam who did not attend Badr but fought at Uhud and later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He holds that not everyone in that volume counts as an early convert, unless early is taken to mean before the conquest of Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He recalls that session ten listed 86 early emigrants who fought at Badr by clan, and says the new names must not repeat them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He says he will skip those already treated at length in the Abyssinia sessions, such as Ja'far and Khalid ibn Sa'id, and will keep only those described as early in Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Allies of Banu Abd Shams: the Jahsh family, Qays and Mu'ayqib

- He relates, from Ibn Sa'd, that Abu Ahmad ibn Jahsh, a son of the Prophet's paternal aunt Umayma and brother of Zaynab bint Jahsh, became Muslim with his brothers before the Prophet entered the house of al-Arqam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He notes that the date of Dar al-Arqam, before or after Abyssinia, is disputed in the sources. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Qays ibn Abdullah of Banu Asad emigrated to Abyssinia with his wife Baraka, was a companion of Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh, and stayed Muslim when Ubaydullah turned Christian and died there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Mu'ayqib ibn Abi Fatima of Daws, an ally of Banu Abd Shams, emigrated to Abyssinia on the account of Ibn Sa'd and the sira authors, and says al-Waqidi's variant is outweighed by consensus. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He holds that the Ibn Sa'd story of Mu'ayqib's leprosy and treatment is soundly transmitted because it avoids al-Waqidi, and says Umar sought a cure for him, sharing his vessel and food, which the speaker reads as trust in God rather than doubt about contagion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He says two men from Yemen treated him with colocynth fruit on the feet to stop the disease from advancing, and he lived without worsening until death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Banu Asad: the sons of Khuwaylid and their line

- He says al-Sa'ib ibn al-Awwam, full brother of al-Zubayr through their mother Safiyya, fought at Uhud, the Trench and later battles and was killed at al-Yamama in the year 12. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He suggests compiling lists of early Muslims killed at al-Yamama, al-Yarmuk, al-Qadisiyya and in the Prophet's lifetime. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Khalid ibn Hizam emigrated to Abyssinia and died on the way, and notes that a verse about those who die while migrating is said to concern him, a claim he treats as questionable. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that al-Aswad ibn Nawfal emigrated in the second migration, notes Ibn Sa'd's correction of Musa ibn Uqba's wrong naming of him, and says his descendant Abu al-Aswad Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman, called orphan of Urwa, is a known narrator. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Umar ibn Umayya ibn al-Harith, not al-Damri, emigrated and died in Abyssinia by consensus of the accounts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Yazid ibn Zam'a emigrated and was killed at al-Ta'if, where his horse bolted toward the fort, with another version saying he was granted safety and then shot. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Banu Abd al-Dar and their allies

- He relates that Abu al-Rum ibn Umayr, brother of Mus'ab, was early in Islam, emigrated in the second migration, fought at Uhud and left no descendants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Firas ibn al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, son of the Qurayshi opponent, emigrated and was killed as a martyr at al-Yarmuk. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Jahm ibn Qays emigrated with his wife Harmala and two sons, and that Harmala died in Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Abu Fakiha, an ally of the clan, was tortured by them at midday heat until the emigration to Abyssinia, and explains that each clan tortured its own members, allies and freedmen. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Banu Zuhra and Banu Taym

- He relates, from Ibn Sa'd, that Amir ibn Abi Waqqas was the eleventh to convert, suffered harm from his mother Hamna bint Sufyan and emigrated to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that al-Muttalib ibn Azhar emigrated with his wife Ramla, and their son Abdullah was born in Abyssinia, and that his brother Tulayb ibn Azhar is reported only by Ibn Ishaq and al-Waqidi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Abdullah al-Asghar ibn Shihab emigrated, returned to Mecca and died before the Hijra, and that Ibn Sa'd judges him a maternal grandfather of al-Zuhri. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Utba ibn Mas'ud, brother of Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, emigrated, fought at Uhud and later battles and died under Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Shurahbil ibn Hasana emigrated and later commanded a banner in the conquest of Syria. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that al-Harith ibn Khalid of Taym emigrated with his wife Ryta and their children, and that on the return journey, according to Musa ibn Uqba and Abu Ma'shar, the mother and most children died at a spring. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Amr ibn Uthman of Taym emigrated and was killed at al-Qadisiyya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Banu Makhzum: the three held in Mecca and those near Abu Jahl

- He lists Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a, Salama ibn Hisham and al-Walid ibn al-Walid as the three the Prophet prayed for by name because they were held in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Ayyash, returning from Abyssinia, emigrated with Umar, was lured back from Quba by his half brothers Abu Jahl and al-Harith, was bound and imprisoned, then escaped to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Salama ibn Hisham returned from Abyssinia and was imprisoned and beaten by Abu Jahl before the Hijra, and holds al-Walid counts as an early Muslim only in the broad sense, having converted before al-Hudaybiyya on one account. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Hashim ibn Abi Hudhayfa emigrated in the second migration, and that Ibn Sa'd corrects Ibn Ishaq's reading of the name as Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Habbar ibn Sufyan and his brother Abdullah ibn Sufyan emigrated and were killed, one at Ajnadayn and the other at al-Yarmuk. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He holds that conversion by Makhzumis was a great feat, since Abu Jahl terrorised Mecca and, he relates, killed Sumayya, and clans punished their own converts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Banu Adi: Nu'aym, Ma'mar, Adi ibn Nadla and others

- He relates that Nu'aym ibn Abdullah al-Nahham, reported to have concealed his Islam, delayed his Hijra until the time of al-Hudaybiyya and was killed at al-Yarmuk, on a report through al-Waqidi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He suggests collecting the figures said to have been tenth or near the first ten converts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He argues that those who emigrated early and fought at Badr, Uhud and al-Khandaq went on to fall in the Ridda and conquest wars, countering the idea that only new converts fought there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Ma'mar ibn Abdullah ibn Nadla emigrated, returned to Mecca, delayed his Hijra and is a narrator of a known hadith on hoarding. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Adi ibn Nadla was the first Muslim to die in Abyssinia, and that Urwa ibn Abi Uthatha is listed by Ibn Sa'd, Musa ibn Uqba, Abu Ma'shar and al-Waqidi as an emigrant to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Mas'ud ibn Suwayd, of Umar's clan, was killed at Mu'ta, and that Abdullah ibn Suraqa is said by Ibn Ishaq to have fought at Badr but not by others. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Banu Sahm: Hudhafa's sons, Hisham ibn al-As and the Harith brothers

- He says Abdullah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi, brother of Khunays who fought at Badr and was Hafsa's first husband, was early in Islam, emigrated to Abyssinia and carried the Prophet's letter to the Persian emperor; a third brother is Qays ibn Hudhafa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He says he will not give Hisham ibn al-As a full entry here, and relates that Amr ibn al-As, asked who was better, spoke of a night before al-Yarmuk or Ajnadayn when both asked for martyrdom and only his brother was granted it, and that Umar praised Hisham as a great help to Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He lists seven Sahmi brothers sons of al-Harith, all said to have emigrated: Abu Qays and Abdullah killed at al-Yamama, al-Sa'ib who died at Fihl in year 13 after being wounded at al-Ta'if, al-Hajjaj killed at al-Yarmuk, Tamim, Sa'id killed at al-Yarmuk, and Ma'bad or Ma'mar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Sa'id ibn Amr al-Tamimi and Umayr ibn Ri'ab, killed at Ayn al-Tamr, and Mihmiyya ibn Jaz', an ally, emigrated to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}

### Banu Jumah, Banu Amir and Banu Fihr

- He holds that Umayr ibn Wahb is not an early convert in the narrow sense, having fought at Badr on the polytheist side before converting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Hatib ibn al-Harith of Jumah emigrated with his wife Fatima and sons and died in Abyssinia, and that Sufyan ibn Ma'mar and Nubayh ibn Uthman also emigrated. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that Salit ibn Amr of Banu Amir emigrated, was sent by the Prophet with a letter to Hawdha ibn Ali al-Hanafi and was killed at al-Yamama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates that his brother al-Sakran ibn Amr, husband of Sawda bint Zam'a, emigrated, and by one report died in Abyssinia and by another back in Mecca, after which the Prophet married Sawda, the first wife after Khadija. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He lists as emigrants Malik ibn Zam'a with his wife, and Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum, who he says came later to Medina after Badr, called the Prophet's muezzin and often left in charge of Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He relates Ibn Sa'd's view that Suhayl ibn Bayda converted early, and that a report calling him a Badr veteran confuses him with his brother. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
- He lists Umar ibn al-Harith, Uthman ibn Abd Ghanm and Sa'id ibn Abd Qays as early Muslims who emigrated, and says Ibn Sa'd then opens a chapter on other Arabs such as Amr ibn Abasa and Abu Dharr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep27}
