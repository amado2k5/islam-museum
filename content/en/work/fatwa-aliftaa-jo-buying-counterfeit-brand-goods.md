## Summary

A question about buying counterfeit brand goods, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that goods bearing a stolen trademark may not be dealt in; goods bearing a look-alike mark may be bought for personal use if the seller does not lie or deceive, but not for resale; and copying a design alone is governed by intellectual-property law. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3676}

## The question

The questioner asked about buying goods from companies that imitate international companies and steal their trademarks. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3676}

## The answer

The Department answered that Islam commands honesty in dealings and forbids cheating and forgery, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/ibn-majah|Ibn Majah]] and [[person/al-tirmidhi-muhammad-ibn-isa|al-Tirmidhi]] whose meaning is that the honest, truthful merchant will be with the martyrs on the Day of Resurrection; in principle everyone should have his own innovation in his field and avoid imitation as far as possible. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3676} It distinguished between kinds of imitation. Goods bearing a stolen trademark are forbidden, since they involve lying and misrepresenting reality and infringe trademark rights, which are valued financial rights that must be protected. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3676} Goods bearing a mark that resembles the original and may deceive people are judged by the purpose of purchase: buying them for personal use is permitted provided the seller does not lie or deceive to promote them, but buying them to trade and sell to consumers is not permitted, because many people are taken in and believe them to be genuine. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3676} Imitating only the design, without any trademark, is governed by the laws on intellectual-property rights. It quoted [[group/international-islamic-fiqh-academy|Islamic Fiqh Academy]] resolution 43 (5/5) that trade names, trademarks, authorship and invention are private rights of their owners with recognised financial value that may not be infringed, advised the public to tell genuine from counterfeit goods, and reminded traders to fear God in their dealings. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3676}
