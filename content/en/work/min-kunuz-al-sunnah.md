## Summary
A series by Omar Abdelkafy titled "Min Kunuz al-Sunna" (From the Treasures of the Sunna), pausing at Prophetic hadiths, explaining them and drawing lessons. This set holds episodes from the Assabile archive, and everything in it is the sheikh's speech as it stands in the machine transcript; what is not traced to a chain or is garbled in the transcript is marked as needing a source.

## Episode 12: do not envy one another and be brothers

## Episode 20: whoever believes in God and the last day, let him say good or keep silent

## Episode 22: where provision and the term are written, and deeds are by their endings
- He relates that the Prophet said whoever wants to hear the Qur'an fresh as Jibril brought it should hear it from Ibn Umm Abd, meaning Ibn Masud, and says Ibn Masud recited al-An'am to the Prophet from memory after hearing it once. See [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Masud]] and [[person/jibril|Jibril]]. (needs_source: hadith quoted by sense without chain, and the claim about al-An'am has no reference) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:01:00}
- He relates from Ibn Masud that the Prophet said each of you is gathered in his mother's womb for forty days, then a clinging clot, then a lump of flesh, then God sends an angel commanded with four words: to write his provision, his term, and whether he is wretched or happy. The report goes on that a person may act like the people of paradise until only a cubit is left and then what is written overtakes him so he acts like the people of the fire, and the reverse, and that deeds are by their endings; he says Bukhari and Muslim report it. See [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Masud]] and [[concept/qadar|divine decree]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:02:30}
- He lays out the stages as forty days a drop, forty a clot and forty a lump, so that at 120 days the angel is sent and writes four things down for the person, the first being provision. This is his view. (needs_source: the day counts are given without a reference) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:03:30}
- He says provision is an encyclopedia and money is only its last page: the first provision is life itself, a sound human being growing in the womb and brought from its darkness into the light of the world. He notes that scholars say a believer shown the grave would not want to come back, relates a saying that one sign of the hour is a man passing his brother's grave and wishing he were in his place, and relates that the Prophet taught asking God to keep one alive while life is good and to take one while death is better. This is his view. (needs_source: the scholars' saying, the sign of the hour and the supplication taught are quoted by sense without chain) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:05:00}
- He says sleep is a kind of death and that waking is a provision to be thanked, since God takes the souls at death and in sleep, keeps those for whom death is decreed and sends the others back until a set term, while the body stays turning in its sleep, and he mentions the waking supplication "praise be to God who gave us life after causing us to die". See [[concept/sleep|sleep]], [[passage/quran-39-42|al-Zumar, verse 42]] and [[practice/dua|supplication]]. This is his view. (needs_source: the waking supplication is quoted without chain) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:06:30}
- He says the human child is the weakest of newborns, unlike a chick that pecks grain or a young camel that reaches for its mother soon after birth, and that God provides it mercy in the mother's heart and care from the father, who toils for the new life. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:08:30}
- He lists the further provisions that come before material ones: movement, sight, hearing, circulation, digestion, oxygen and balance, the mind and the ability to learn in any measure, and the greatest, love of religion, of scholars and of study circles, with heart states such as certainty, trust in God, fear and hope. He says believer and unbeliever are both provided for, but the believer's provision is blessed. See [[concept/tawakkul|trust in God]]. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:10:30}
- He says provisions are distributed in the morning, so that whoever prays the dawn prayer before sunrise has blessing in provision and whoever prays after sunrise loses it, and he argues that offices in hot Arab countries should start work after dawn and get most of their working hours done by the noon prayer. He says night was made for rest and day for livelihood, claims the happiness hormone is released only in sleep at night, and says reversing night and day leaves people low in spirits. This is his view. - He says the term of life is also a provision: God blesses the age of some who live few years yet leave great output, while others live long without blessing or lasting legacy such as a righteous child who prays for them, continuing charity or knowledge that benefits others, and he quotes a saying that whoever's death is decreed in a place will not die elsewhere. See [[concept/sadaqa-jariya|continuing charity]]. This is his view. (needs_source: the saying about the place of death is quoted without chain) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:15:00}
- He says believer and unbeliever alike are sure that life ends in death, but the believer treats this world as a bridge to the hereafter and works to leave it with God pleased with him, while another sees only a single chance. See [[concept/akhira|the hereafter]]. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:16:00}
- He recites the second half of the same hadith, in which a person may act like the people of paradise until a cubit remains and then the writing overtakes him, and the reverse, and says that since provision is written from 120 days in the womb, a person should make it a means and not an end and aim at the pleasure of God and His messenger. This is his view. - He asks whether a person is compelled or free and answers that he is compelled in what he will not be reckoned for, such as when he is born and dies, and free in what he is reckoned for, such as praying, fasting and taking what is lawful or forbidden, so that no servant is wronged on the day of judgment. See [[concept/qadar|divine decree]]. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep22 00:19:00}
- He explains the words as it appears to people as a person doing paradise deeds only for show, and relates that on the day of judgment a scholar, a fighter and a generous giver are each asked about their deeds and each says he acted so that it would be said of him, and each is thrown on the face into the fire; he adds a saying that God leaves any share given to a partner to that partner. See [[concept/riya|showing off]], [[concept/niyya|intention]], [[concept/ikhlas|sincerity]] and [[concept/shahid|martyr]]. This is his view. - He says reckoning is on the intention settled in the heart and confirmed by deed, not on passing thoughts, and recalls that faith is a word of the tongue, belief of the heart and action of the limbs. He says someone who looks lax outwardly but has a sincere heart may be given a good ending, since deeds are by their endings, and he teaches the supplication that the best of our deeds be their endings. See [[concept/husn-al-khatima|a good ending]] and [[practice/dua|supplication]]. This is his view. - He relates that a woman who prayed, fasted and gave but harmed her neighbours with her tongue was said by the Prophet to be of the fire, while another with fewer acts who did not harm her neighbours was of paradise, and concludes that deeds lose value when the intention seeks people's approval.
