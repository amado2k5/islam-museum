## Summary
A series by Omar Abdelkafy titled "Min Kunuz al-Sunna" (From the Treasures of the Sunna), pausing at Prophetic hadiths, explaining them and drawing lessons. This set holds episodes from the Assabile archive, and everything in it is the sheikh's speech as it stands in the machine transcript; what is not traced to a chain or is garbled in the transcript is marked as needing a source.

## Episode 12: do not envy one another and be brothers

- He says removing harm from the road is a branch of faith, mentioning by sense that faith has some sixty or seventy branches and its lowest is removing harm from the road, and that one should not litter from a car window. He says he thanks street sweepers, whose work is a branch of faith, and that people should say work rather than trade, citing the verse that God and the believers will see your work. See [[concept/iman|faith]], [[concept/work-in-islam|work in Islam]] and [[passage/quran-9-105|al-Tawba, verse 105]]. This is his view. - He says that inner watchfulness before God matters more than man-made traffic penalties, and criticises those who leave a car across the road at prayer and block others, including a non-Muslim living among us needing a hospital, advising them to park elsewhere and walk, since the steps to prayer count. He understands removing harm broadly, as not harming people. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep13 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep13 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep13 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy min-kunuz-al-sunnah/ep13 00:25:00}
- He relates that the Prophet warned against sitting on the roads and, when the Companions said they had no alternative, said to give the road its right, which he lists as returning the greeting, holding back harm, lowering the gaze, enjoining the right and forbidding the wrong. He applies it to drivers who sound their horns and trouble others. See [[practice/greeting-salam|greeting]], [[concept/ghadd-al-basar|lowering the gaze]] and [[concept/amr-bil-maruf|enjoining the right]]. This is his view. - He mentions by sense that one who judges unjustly between two people will come with one side leaning, and that people crossing the Sirat are of three kinds, those who pass, those scratched and those piled up, and advises fairness between two wives and among children, citing a man who told the Prophet he had given a gift to his son. See [[concept/adl-bayn-al-zawjat|justice between wives]] and [[concept/sirat|the Sirat]]. This is his view.
