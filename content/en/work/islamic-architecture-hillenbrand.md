## Summary

*Islamic Architecture: Form, Function and Meaning* is a reference work by Robert Hillenbrand, published by Edinburgh University Press in 1994. {cite:google-books-islamic-architecture-hillenbrand} According to the publisher's description, it surveys the major building types of the Islamic world: religious architecture (the mosque, the minaret, the madrasa), the mausoleum, and the caravanserai and the palace on the secular side. {cite:google-books-islamic-architecture-hillenbrand} Each is discussed in art-historical terms, with the interplay of form and function as the underlying theme, across a range from Spain to Afghanistan and from about 700 to about 1700. {cite:google-books-islamic-architecture-hillenbrand}

It contains 24 colour plates, 300 black-and-white photographs and 1,246 line drawings, with a section of composite drawings and ground plans. {cite:google-books-islamic-architecture-hillenbrand} The description says it won the American Publishers Association's award for an outstanding professional and scholarly title and the 1996 Alice Davis Hitchcock Medallion of the Society of Architectural Historians of Great Britain. {cite:google-books-islamic-architecture-hillenbrand}

## Publication and editions

It first appeared in hardback in 1994, in 645 pages, and then in a revised paperback edition in 2000 that adds a previously unpublished index. {cite:google-books-islamic-architecture-hillenbrand} Open Library records the Edinburgh edition under ISBN 0748604790, and another 1994 edition from Columbia University Press in New York. {cite:openlibrary-islamic-architecture-hillenbrand-1994}
