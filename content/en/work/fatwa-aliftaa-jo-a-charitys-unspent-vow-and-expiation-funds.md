## Summary

A question from a charity that collects vows and expiations and has held some of the money for years without spending it, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the charity is an agent forbidden to delay delivery without excuse; if delayed, the money must be delivered at once with repentance, and if lost it must be made good. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4029}

## The question

A charity that collects donations for vows and expiations asked about sums that entered its account several years ago and have not been spent, and how it should deal with them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4029}

## The answer

The Department answered that charities act as agents for those who owe expiations and vows in delivering them to those entitled, so those running them must hasten to do so and may not delay without excuse; the money is a trust that must be safeguarded. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4029} It cited al-Shirwani's glosses on Tuhfat al-Muhtaj, reporting from al-Tatimma that an expiation is due immediately if the broken oath was itself a sin, and [[person/al-khatib-al-shirbini|al-Khatib al-Shirbini]] in Mughni al-Muhtaj that the recipients' need is immediate, so one who delays is sinful and liable if the money is lost. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4029} It said that an agent who delays delivering any expiation or vow without excuse is sinful and must hasten to deliver it to clear his liability, with repentance and seeking forgiveness, and is liable to make it good if it is lost through his failure. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4029} It concluded that delaying delivery of expiations and vows to those entitled without a legal excuse is forbidden; if they have been delayed, they must be delivered immediately with repentance, and if lost they must be made good. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4029}
