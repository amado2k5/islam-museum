## Summary

The lecturer follows the Prophet's arrival in Medina: the waiting of the Medinans, the stay at Quba, the purchase of the site and the building of the Prophet's Mosque, the beginning of the call to prayer, the fever of Medina, and the brotherhood between the Emigrants and the Helpers. He closes with the make-up of Medinan society, the Prophet's treaty with the Jews, and the change of the prayer direction as it is treated in Surat al-Baqara. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The Medinans wait and the Prophet's arrival at Quba

- He says that the lecture turns from the road of the migration to the viewpoint of the people of Medina, following the long report of Aisha in Sahih al-Bukhari, which she transmitted through her nephew Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, from Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the Muslims of Medina, having heard of the Prophet's departure from Mecca, went out each morning to the edge of the lava field and waited until the midday heat sent them home. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He estimates that the journey took about eleven or twelve days plus three days in the cave, roughly fifteen days in all. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that a Jewish man, watching from one of the fortified towers of Medina, saw the Prophet and his companions in white clothes emerging from the mirage and called out to the Arabs that their awaited good fortune had come. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the Muslims rushed to their weapons and met the Prophet at the edge of the lava field, and that the Prophet turned to the right and stopped among Banu Amr ibn Awf on a Monday in the month of Rabi al-Awwal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that Abu Bakr stood to receive the people while the Prophet sat silent, that Helpers who had never seen the Prophet greeted Abu Bakr instead, and that they recognised the Prophet only when Abu Bakr shaded him with his cloak from the sun. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The joy of Medina and the attachment of the Helpers

- He relates that the day of the arrival was one of great rejoicing, with women and slave girls on the rooftops and the people proclaiming the greatness of God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the Prophet's presence gave Medina a light that its people felt inwardly, and he compares it to how a place changes in a person's eyes when a loved one is present or leaves. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates a saying of Anas ibn Malik that everything in Medina brightened on the day the Prophet entered and everything darkened on the day he died, so that the Companions felt their hearts change even before they had shaken the dust of his burial from their hands. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates from a sound report that during the Prophet's final illness Abu Bakr and al-Abbas found the Helpers weeping in their gathering because they missed the Prophet's company, and that the Prophet then came out with his head bound and, on what was his last ascent of the pulpit, commended the Helpers to the people's care. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the arrival made Medina seem to come alive, and that this is why some reports speak of a mountain that loves the Muslims and is loved by them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The layout of Medina and the stay at Quba

- He says that Medina in the Prophet's time was not one continuous town but a set of separate quarters, each clan in its own district with open distance between them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He places Banu al-Najjar near the later mosque and Banu Haritha far off to the north-east, and says that knowing this layout helps in understanding reports such as the Prophet's remark to Banu Haritha about the edge of the sacred area and his advice to Banu Salima, who lived far from the mosque, that their footsteps are recorded. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that Quba was the first quarter met by anyone arriving from Mecca, which lies to the south, and that this is why the Prophet halted there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the Prophet stayed at Quba for some ten and more nights, which he also gives as fourteen according to Anas, and that his first act there was to build the Quba Mosque. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the Prophet lodged with a Companion named Kulthum ibn al-Hidm, though he notes that others name a different host and that some scholars prefer this name. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### From Quba to the quarter of Banu al-Najjar

- He says that the walk between Quba and the Prophet's Mosque takes about an hour today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates the well-known account that the Friday prayer overtook the Prophet on the way and that he prayed at a spot now marked by a mosque called the Friday Mosque. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the clans along the route each invited the Prophet to stay, and that he told them to leave his camel alone because she was under orders until she knelt. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates from Anas, in Bukhari and Muslim, that the Prophet sent for Banu al-Najjar, who came girded with swords, and that they surrounded him as he arrived on his mount with Abu Bakr riding behind him until he dismounted at the courtyard of Abu Ayyub. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the sword-bearing arrival shows how ready the Helpers were in spirit to defend the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that Abu Ayyub at once took the Prophet's baggage into his house, and that a report has the Prophet say a man goes with his baggage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### Buying the site and building the Prophet's Mosque

- He relates that the Prophet liked to pray wherever the time found him, even in sheepfolds, and then ordered a mosque built and asked Banu al-Najjar to name a price for their walled plot. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that they said they sought its price only from God, while another report says the Prophet insisted and bought it from them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates from Anas that the plot held graves of polytheists, ruins and date palms, and that the Prophet ordered the graves dug up, the ruins levelled and the palms cut, the trunks being set in line toward the prayer direction with stone for the door posts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the men carried the stones while chanting verses, with the Prophet among them saying a couplet that asks for forgiveness for the Helpers and the Emigrants and puts true good in the next life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates from the long migration report of Aisha that the Prophet stayed at Banu Amr ibn Awf some ten nights and founded the mosque that was founded on piety. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the camel then knelt at a drying yard for dates that belonged to two orphan boys, Sahl and Suhayl, in the care of As'ad ibn Zurara, and that the Prophet said this was the place to stay, if God wills. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the boys offered the plot as a gift, but the Prophet refused to accept it as one and bought it, and then carried bricks with the builders while saying a rhyme that this load is better than the loads of Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that scholars of Qur'anic commentary disagree on which mosque the verse about a mosque founded on piety from the first day means: some say the Prophet's Mosque, others Quba Mosque, and that those who choose Quba rely on its being his first foundation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates a subtle point that some scholars drew from the Quba reading, namely that the Companions' choice of the migration as the start of the calendar may be linked to the phrase from the first day, though he says they did not rely on it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The simple structure of the mosque and the Prophet's rooms

- He relates from Abdullah ibn Umar in Bukhari that in the Prophet's time the mosque was built of mud brick with a roof of palm fronds and columns of palm trunks. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that Abu Bakr added nothing, that Umar enlarged it and rebuilt it on the same plan with new wooden columns, and that Uthman then changed it greatly, using carved stone and plaster for the walls, carved stone columns and a teak roof. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that the mosque floor was bare earth with no carpets, and that part of the courtyard was open to the sky. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He links this to the report of the Prophet's prostration in water and mud on the morning after a rainy twenty-first night, saying that a palm-frond roof would not hold back rain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that rooms were built beside the mosque for the Prophet's household, each called a hujra, among them the room of Aisha, and he describes them as very small. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The centrality of prayer and gathering for worship

- He holds that building the mosque first, at Quba and again in Medina, teaches that prayer is the central axis of the community from which its other works begin. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that reports in Sahih Muslim, in a Sunan collection from Buraida, and in al-Tirmidhi from Abd Allah ibn Shaqiq speak of the weighty status of abandoning prayer, and that he cites them to show the place of prayer in the Islamic vocabulary and not to settle the legal question. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the mosque as a place of assembly founds the idea of gathering for worship and for good, and that the Prophet tied order in the prayer rows to the unity of hearts, citing a sound report on straightening the rows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He points out that the closing greeting, the greeting on the righteous servants in the sitting, and the shared amen and following of one imam express mercy and fellowship among the worshippers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### Priorities in building the Muslim community

- He holds that the simplicity of the mosque and the rooms was not forced by weakness, as it might have been in Mecca, but a matter of priority in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that the Prophet built the community on doctrine, faith, values and ethics rather than on material construction, and that the mosque and rooms stayed in their simple state until his death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that material building is not wrong in itself, but that founding structures before founding hearts and faith is a misreading, and that building minds on the correct reference of revelation is what yields a lasting civilisational sequence. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The beginning of the call to prayer

- He says that once the mosque stood the Muslims faced the question of how to gather for prayer, and that a report in Bukhari has the Prophet consider a bell like the Christians' or a horn like the Jews'. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He adds that other reports outside the sound collections mention raising a flag at prayer time, and concludes that no fixed means of calling yet existed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the call to prayer was instituted after a dream seen by Abd Allah ibn Zayd ibn Abd Rabbih al-Ansari, in which a man in two green garments offered to teach him something better than a bell. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that Abd Allah told the dream to the Prophet, who said it was a true vision if God wills, and told him to relate its words to Bilal because Bilal's voice was more carrying, and that Bilal then gave the call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the example shows how rulings were laid down one at a time in Medina, not all present on arrival, and that it also shows the value of the true dream in the Islamic frame even though the Prophet received revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The call to prayer and the authority of the Sunna

- He holds that the call to prayer, now a great sign of Islam in every Muslim land, proves the value and authority of the Prophet's Sunna, since its words are not found in the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that when a rejecter of the Sunna objects that such practices were passed down by continuous practice, the reply is to ask for their final source, which is the Sunna and not the Qur'an, so the Sunna can stand alone in laying down rulings whether it is reported by many chains or by single ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The fever of Medina and the rapid walk in the circuits

- He says that the Companions found Medina's climate unhealthy at first, with a known fever called the fever of Yathrib, a reputation that was also known among the people of Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that on the Prophet's later umrah the polytheists said that a people weakened by the fever of Yathrib were coming, so the Prophet ordered his Companions to walk briskly in the first three circuits to show strength, and that the rule remained after its reason had gone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that Umar later said that they had only done it to show off to the polytheists, which he explains as a plain-language meaning of displaying strength, not a legal one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He draws from this that a Muslim should show strength and not leave room for mockery when he can prevent it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates from Aisha that Abu Bakr, Bilal and Amir ibn Fuhayra all lay in bed with the fever, and that each answered her question about his state with a line of verse, Bilal longing for the valleys and mountains near Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the Prophet prayed for his Companions and for Medina, asking that its fever be moved to al-Juhfa, and that he then had a vision, which he interpreted, of a black woman with tangled hair leaving Medina, meaning the plague had gone out of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that Medina has stayed healthy since. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The brotherhood between the Emigrants and the Helpers

- He says that the brothering of the Emigrants and the Helpers was among the first things the Prophet did in Medina, and that Bukhari records that it took place in the house of Anas ibn Malik. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that souls whose main project is helping the Prophet reach an extraordinary purity, though he adds that they did not lose their human nature and that mistakes still occurred among the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates from Bukhari that the Prophet paired Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf with Qays ibn al-Rabi, who offered him half his wealth and offered to divorce whichever of his wives he liked so that Abd al-Rahman could marry her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that Abd al-Rahman declined, asked only to be shown the market, traded there, and soon after was seen with a trace of perfume, having married a Helper woman with a small gold marriage gift, and that the Prophet told him to hold a wedding feast even with one sheep. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that at first the brothering reached as far as mutual inheritance without kinship, and that he sees an allusion to this in the end of Surat al-Anfal, which treats the bonds of allegiance among those who believed and migrated, those who sheltered, and kinsfolk. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that the brothering continued after the early period but without the inheritance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### Brotherhood among believers as a basis for reform

- He holds that the brothering teaches the centrality of brotherhood among believers in any reforming effort. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that among the first acts of the Prophet with his Companions, brotherhood was one of the great starting points that gave later events a firm base. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that division and disagreement are a main problem among Muslims, that the causes need deep analysis, and that the community cannot be set right unless brotherhood among believers returns to the centre. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He adds that he will devote several later sessions to brotherhood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### The make-up of Medinan society and the treaty with the Jews

- He says that Medinan society on the Prophet's arrival comprised the Aws and Khazraj, who split into believers and non-believers, and the Jews, and that some of those who entered the faith did so outwardly and were given the name hypocrites, which lasted to the end of the prophetic mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that the Prophet made a treaty with the Jews as soon as he arrived, and he holds that in the early period the Prophet made treaties with tribes and did not treat all polytheists as being in a basic state of fighting, using such steps for political aims such as neutralising some groups and winning the loyalty of others. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that the treaty carried duties beyond non-hostility, such as helping with blood-money, and that the expedition against Banu al-Nadir came when the Prophet went to them to seek help with a payment, while the expedition against Banu Qurayza after the Confederates came because of a breach of the treaty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that despite the treaty the Jews of Medina worked against the mission, by questions meant to cast doubt and by objections to everything, and that a close link existed between them and the hypocrites, who heard the Muslims and passed it on to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates from a sound report that Abd Allah ibn Salam, a Jew who became Muslim, asked the Prophet to question the Jews about him before they saw him, and that they called him their best and the son of their best but called him their worst after he appeared and spoke. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The change of the prayer direction and the Qur'anic context

- He says that about seventeen or eighteen months after the arrival the direction of prayer was changed, a major event whose weight many Muslims no longer grasp. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the biographical books give it only a brief mention, and that one must read the biography together with the books of the Sunna and the Qur'an, because the Qur'anic treatment shows how heavy the event was for Medinan society. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He relates that when the direction changed, the polytheists said the Prophet would return to their religion since he had turned to the Kaaba, the Jews and the hypocrites asked how both directions could be right, and the Jews led the doubts about the abrogation of the first direction. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the Jews and the hypocrites were raising an objection against revelation and its firm rulings, as in the objection over the cow in the story that gives its name to the second surah. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

### Reading Surat al-Baqara in light of the change of direction

- He says that Surat al-Baqara is among the first revealed in Medina and advises reading its first part and the start of the second with the change of direction in mind. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the surah lays the ground for how hypocrites and Jews would receive such news and rulings, with believers answering by submission, faith and following. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that the surah first speaks of the hypocrites and their link with the Jews, then of abrogation, then of the claims of the People of the Book against each other, and then of Ibrahim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that the surah ties the Kaaba to Ibrahim because the Jews, who claimed descent from him, were the ones objecting, so the Kaaba was shown to be tied to the man they claimed, and he cites Ibn al-Qayyim for the view that the stress on Ibrahim being a leader for people extends to his house. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He says that the direct command to turn then comes, together with the description of the Muslims as a middle community, a statement that the earlier direction was a test of who follows the Messenger, and the point that the faith of the Muslims in the earlier direction would not be lost, meaning their earlier prayers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}
- He holds that reading the surah with these events in mind deepens understanding of the Qur'an, and that reading the biography and the Sunna with the verses in mind deepens knowledge of the Prophet's life and Sunna. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep16}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.
