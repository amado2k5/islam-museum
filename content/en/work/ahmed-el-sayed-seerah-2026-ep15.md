## Summary

The speaker opens with a biography of al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam, whose house served the early community, drawn from Sulayman al-Awda's book on the Badr veterans. He then reads Abu Talib's long poem of protection from Ibn Ishaq's Sira with al-Suhayli's linguistic commentary, turns to the open call and the abuse the Prophet met in the markets and at prayer, and closes with Abu Bakr's role in freeing the tortured and with the clan of Banu Taym. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Method of the series and the book on the Badr veterans

- He says this is the fifteenth session of the series and that each session opens with the biography of an early Muslim who fought at Badr, taken from Sulayman al-Awda's book on the Badr veterans. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says he will take only the Emigrants for now, since the series is still in the Meccan period, and will move to the Helpers later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He explains that the series gathers several sciences around each event: biographical works, genealogy, the Qur'an and language. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says he chose that book because it traces every statement about a Companion to an original source, not because it is the most comprehensive, and he adds that tracing a statement to a source does not make it sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Biography of al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam and his house

- He relates, from the book, that al-Arqam belonged to Banu Makhzum, his mother came from Khuza'a, and he was among the earliest Muslims, his house near al-Safa serving as the hidden meeting place of the Prophet and the believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a report through Ibn Sa'd from al-Arqam's son that his father was the seventh to accept Islam, and a report cited to al-Hakim that the Prophet stayed in that house until forty men had joined. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He holds that the early date of the house shows that many people had already accepted Islam before it, and he stresses that al-Arqam was only sixteen or seventeen when his house became the base of the call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that al-Arqam endowed the house to his descendants as inalienable property, that al-Mansur later bought it, and that it afterwards passed to al-Khayzuran and was called by her name; he adds that it survived in recognisable form until recent times. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that al-Arqam fought at Badr and the later battles, received a sword from the Badr spoils, and died around the year 55 as the last of the Badr veterans to die, with Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas leading the funeral prayer after a dispute with Marwan ibn al-Hakam over waiting for him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a report cited to al-Tabarani in which al-Arqam wished to travel to Jerusalem for prayer and was told of the merit of prayer in the Prophet's mosque, and he judges that report weak. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Quraysh pressure on Abu Talib and the clans' protection of the Prophet

- He recalls that Quraysh first went to Abu Talib as leader of Banu Hashim, as tribal custom required, and that Abu Talib took on protecting the Prophet whatever Quraysh thought once he saw the Prophet could not stop. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, Muslim and unbelieving alike, rallied to defend him, the Muslims out of faith and the others out of tribal loyalty, and he sees this as God's provision for the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He recalls that the previous session dealt with media efforts by Quraysh to mislead people about the message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Abu Talib's long poem and Ibn Kathir's praise of it

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Abu Talib composed the poem when he feared the Arabs at large would side with his own people against him, invoking the sanctuary of Mecca and stating that he would never hand the Prophet over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that Ibn Kathir in al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya calls it a great and eloquent poem that only its attributed author could have written, finer than the seven Mu'allaqat, and notes that Ibn Hisham accepted only part of it while some poetry scholars rejected most of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He explains that the poem calls on many sacred things in the way of Abu Talib's ancestors, including the Lord of mankind, the House, the Black Stone, the station of Ibrahim, the rites between al-Safa and al-Marwa and the pilgrim stations, then ends by swearing that the Quraysh will not take Muhammad without a fight. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says the poem also names those who deserted Abu Talib, such as Mut'im ibn Adi of Banu Nawfal, and he notes the poet's reproach to kin close in descent. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He holds that the poem is worth memorising in part, with the caveat that it contains invocation of things besides God because its author was not a Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Abu Talib's fate and the two kinds of protection

- He says Abu Talib shielded the Prophet from the harm Quraysh wanted, though he died without accepting Islam because tribal custom and reverence for Abd al-Muttalib outweighed him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates the report in Sahih al-Bukhari that Abu Talib's punishment was lightened because of his support, and the report of his last moments when Abu Jahl and another man reminded him of his forefathers' religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He holds that Abu Talib's case shows guidance is not given by sentiment, citing the verse he understands as revealed about Abu Talib, and he criticises those who insist that Abu Talib was a Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says the Prophet had two kinds of protection: in Mecca a bare shelter that let him deliver the message, and in Medina the armed support of the Helpers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says a line in the poem about a man whose face brings rain recalled a rain-seeking prayer made by the Prophet in Medina, according to Ibn Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Al-Suhayli's language notes on the poem

- He relates from al-Suhayli's al-Rawd al-Unuf that the word for inheritance in the poem comes from the root of inheriting, with an initial letter that changed, and that the poet likened his forefathers to kings although none of them was a king. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates al-Suhayli's list of terms for brand marks on camels, and the comment that Arabic has long chapters of near-synonyms such as names of winds, of the hours of night and of swords. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that the shell beads used as ornaments take their name from the sea leaving them on the shore, that marble is a white stone, and that the cluster-stalks of the date palm are the image behind the last word of the verse. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He explains that the name of the hill of Arafa, al-Ilal, is said to come from pilgrims hastening to reach the standing place, and that the closing line means the clans will not give the Prophet up. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Poem of Abu Qays ibn al-Aslat urging Quraysh to restraint

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that news of the Prophet spread to Medina, where Aws and Khazraj knew of him from their Jewish allies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that Abu Qays, a poet married into Quraysh, composed a poem urging them to refrain from war and from harming the Prophet, reminding them of how God protected them from the elephant army. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Open call, accusations and Abu Lahab in the markets

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Quraysh stirred up their fools against the Prophet, calling him a poet, a sorcerer, a soothsayer and possessed, while he kept proclaiming the message openly and criticising their religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He recalls the proclamation from al-Safa, when the people testified they had never found him lying, and the earlier session's account of al-Walid ibn al-Mughira calling the message sorcery out of stubbornness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says the Prophet also went to seasonal markets such as Dhu al-Majaz, Majanna and Ukaz, so that the call was not limited to Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a report through Tariq al-Muharibi, which he calls sound, that at Dhu al-Majaz he saw the Prophet calling people to affirm God's oneness while a man followed throwing stones until his heels bled, and that the man was Abu Lahab, whose name was Abd al-Uzza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a second report through Rabi'a al-Dili in which Abu Lahab, squint-eyed, followed the Prophet from camp to camp telling people not to be led away from their fathers' religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He counts four sources of hardship in this scene: it came at the start of the call, it included physical harm, the accuser was an uncle, and the accusation was shameless. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says the surah about Abu Lahab was revealed regarding him, and he links the report of his wife Umm Jamil, daughter of Harb and sister of Abu Sufyan, who came with a stone to the Prophet and Abu Bakr but could not see him, as a sign of divine protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Abu Jahl's threats at prayer and the divine protection

- He relates a report in Sahih Muslim through Abu Hurayra that Abu Jahl swore to trample the Prophet's neck while he prayed and then turned back in fear, which the Prophet explained by the angels' protection, and that verses were revealed about it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a longer version cited to al-Bayhaqi from Ibn Ishaq in which Abu Jahl planned to strike the Prophet with a stone, was frightened by a vision and later the Prophet identified it as Jibril. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He notes that the Prophet prayed between the Black Corner and the Yemeni Corner so the Ka'ba lay between him and the north. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He holds that the episode shows both divine protection and real threat, so being protected does not mean being spared testing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a report cited to al-Tirmidhi through Anas in which the Prophet speaks of having been frightened and harmed for God's sake beyond anyone else. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Torture of the vulnerable and Umar's fear in Mecca

- He says some Companions were beaten, dragged over the hot ground of Mecca and weighed down with stones, Bilal among them, and that the Prophet suffered through each of their trials. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He holds that those without clan protection were tortured most, though strong men were also harmed, and he relates the report in Sahih al-Bukhari that Umar stayed afraid in his house until al-As ibn Wa'il of Banu Sahm gave him protection and turned back the crowd. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He says Sumayya, mother of Ammar ibn Yasir, was the first person to die for Islam, and that a group of women were tortured. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Abu Bakr's support of the Prophet and his freeing of the tortured

- He holds that Abu Bakr was among the greatest supporters of the Prophet in Mecca, and cites the Prophet's late statement that he would have taken Abu Bakr as a close friend and that Abu Bakr was the most generous of men to him in companionship and wealth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a report in Ibn Abi Shayba's Musannaf, through Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, whose chain he calls sound, that Abu Bakr freed seven people being tortured for their faith: Amir ibn Fuhayra, Bilal, Zinnira, Umm Ubays, the woman of Banu Nahd and her daughter, and a slave woman of Banu Amr ibn Mu'ammil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr said the verses of Surat al-Layl, about the one who gives and fears God, were revealed about Abu Bakr, and says this is the commonly held view of the commentators. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates a report through Ibn Hibban that A'isha said Abu Bakr spent forty thousand on the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He lists Abu Bakr's later roles: the first man to accept Islam, companion in the emigration, present at the battles, and the man the Prophet put forward to lead prayer during his final illness despite objections from A'isha and Hafsa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He attributes to Shia currents a history of cursing Abu Bakr and Umar and criticises that, and he describes a misrepresentation he says they make of Sunni Muslims as followers of Yazid, whom he holds not a Companion and not revered by Sunnis. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}

### Banu Taym, the clan of Abu Bakr

- He relates from the book on Quraysh genealogies that Banu Taym descend from Taym ibn Murra, who meets the Prophet's line at Murra ibn Ka'b, and that they lived near Jabal Qu'ayqi'an, where Qusayy granted them land. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that they joined Banu Abd Manaf in the alliance of the Mutayyibin, listed by al-Suhayli as five clans, and later took part in Hilf al-Fudul, held in the house of Abd Allah ibn Jud'an, their leader. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates the report in Sahih Muslim that when A'isha asked about Ibn Jud'an's generosity the Prophet replied that it did not help him because he never asked God's forgiveness for the Day of Judgement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He names other Taymis: Qunfudh ibn Umayr, named in Abu Talib's poem, and Talha ibn Ubayd Allah, a cousin of Abu Bakr whose marriages to sisters of the Prophet's wives he mentions as a curiosity. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
- He relates that Abu Bakr's descendants were called the Bakriyyin and spread to many lands, naming his sons Abd al-Rahman and Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, the latter born at the Farewell Pilgrimage to Asma bint Umays and later a governor of Egypt. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep15}
