## Summary

*The Prophet and the Age of the Caliphates: The Islamic Near East from the Sixth to the Eleventh Century* is a book by Hugh Kennedy, published by Longman in London and New York in 1986 in 424 pages; a second edition followed in 2004. {cite:openlibrary-prophet-age-of-caliphates-1986} In his preface the author says he wrote it as an introduction for readers coming to the history of the early Islamic Near East for the first time, giving a continuous chronological framework while making room for long-term social and economic change and for the achievements of government in the period. {cite:archive-org-prophet-age-caliphates-scan}

## Publication and editions

In the later edition that was read, the book has twelve chapters, from the Near East in the early seventh century through the Rashidun, Umayyad and Abbasid caliphs to the Buyids, the Kurds, the Hamdanids and the Bedouin dynasties, ending with early Islamic Egypt and the Fatimids; a postscript on the coming of the Seljuks and a survey of the principal sources for 600–1050 follow. {cite:archive-org-prophet-age-caliphates-scan}
