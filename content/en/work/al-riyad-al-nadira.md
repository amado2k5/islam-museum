## Summary
*Al-Riyad al-Nadira fi Manaqib al-'Ashara* ("The Fresh Gardens on the Virtues of the Ten") is a book by [[person/muhibb-al-din-al-tabari|Muhibb al-Din Abu Ja'far Ahmad ibn Abdullah al-Tabari]] of Mecca, a Shafi'i (d. 694 AH), on the virtues of [[concept/ashara-mubashshara|the ten Companions promised Paradise]]. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 p.1} {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.65} The editor of the Dar al-Gharb edition notes that its attribution is secure, since his biographers list it, and that Ibn Taghri Birdi and Taqi al-Din al-Fasi describe it as two volumes. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.66}

## Aim and method
In his preface the author says he gathered what was reported on the virtues of the ten from many books "in abridged form and with the chains removed" so that it would be easy to use, referring each hadith to the book that records it, and that he ordered the material from general to particular: what covers the ten and others, then what concerns the ten, then fewer than the ten, then what is specific to the four caliphs, then to more than one, then the merits of each. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 p.7} He then lists the works he drew on, most of which he says he holds by transmission: musnads and sunan such as Ahmad's Musnad and the Sunan of Abu Dawud and al-Daraqutni; books of virtues such as Ahmad's Manaqib Ali and Khaythama's Fada'il al-Sahaba; dictionaries of Companions; history, tafsir and lexicography; and more than a hundred hadith fascicles such as the Khil'iyyat, Thaqafiyyat, Ghaylaniyyat, Silafiyyat and the collections of forty. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 p.7} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 p.8} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 p.9} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 p.10}

The editor describes his method: he gives the hadiths without chains but with their sources, explains rare words, sets out the legal content and discusses and weighs the evidence where needed. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.67} One example of his weighing reports is that he preferred the historians' figure for the length of the four caliphates — twenty-nine years, five months and some days — over the hadith of Safina that gives Ali's caliphate as six years. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.86} He cites the reports of Abu Bakr leading the prayer during the Prophet's illness as evidence of his caliphate. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.89}

## Structure

## Standing and criticism
The editor of the Dar al-Gharb edition considers it one of the most important hadith works on the virtues of the ten and among the richest in material, preserving rare hadiths from books and fascicles whose originals are lost or still in manuscript. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.68} On the other side he quotes Abu Hayyan's remark that al-Muhibb included in some of his hadith books weak and fabricated reports on the merits of deeds and of the Companions without warning and without the chains by which their status could be known, saying only "recorded by so-and-so". {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.51} The editor himself notes slips of attribution, such as ascribing to both al-Bukhari and Muslim a hadith only one of them recorded. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.90}

## Editions
The editor records that the book was printed in four parts, edited by Muhammad Mustafa Abu al-'Ula, by Maktabat al-Jundi in Cairo in 1970, and that Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya in Beirut reissued it in two volumes in 1405 AH, dropping the editor's introduction and the book's conclusion, with many misprints. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.55} The two copies used here are the complete Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya edition of 1405/1984 {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 p.2} and the Dar al-Gharb al-Islami edition of 1996 of the section on Abu Bakr, with a long study of the author and the book. {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.4} {cite:riyad-nadira-dar-al-gharb-1996 p.9}
