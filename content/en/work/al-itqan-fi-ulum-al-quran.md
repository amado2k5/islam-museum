## Overview
"Al-Itqan fi Ulum al-Quran" (Mastery in the Sciences of the Qur'an), a volume by Shaykh Jalal al-Din Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr al-Suyuti ([[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]]), who died in 911 AH. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 177}

## As Hajji Khalifa relates it
Hajji Khalifa says it is among al-Suyuti's most useful and most nearly complete works. Al-Suyuti noted the classification of his teacher al-Kafiyaji and found it too brief, and he judged al-Balqini's "Mawaqi al-Ulum" insufficient. Then, after composing his "Al-Tahbir", he found al-Zarkashi's "Al-Burhan" to be a comprehensive book, so he resumed the work and added to it until it reached eighty kinds. He made it an introduction to his large commentary on the Qur'an, which he began and called "Majma al-Bahrayn". For most of the kinds there are separate works as well. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 177}

## Plan of the book and what it holds on the readings
In the introduction al-Suyuti gives the index of the eighty types. After the types on the Meccan and Medinan, the occasions of revelation, and the names of the Qur'an and its suras (1-17), the eighteenth is on its collection and arrangement, the nineteenth on the number of its suras, verses, words and letters, the twentieth on its memorisers and narrators. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 15} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 16} Then come the types on readings: the twenty-second the mutawatir, the twenty-third the mashhur, the twenty-fourth the ahad, the twenty-fifth the shadhdh, the twenty-sixth the mawdu', the twenty-seventh the mudraj, followed by pausing and starting, imala and fath, idgham, izhar, ikhfa' and iqlab, madd and qasr, easing the hamza, the manner of receiving it, and the etiquette of reciting it. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 16}

## The rasm and the etiquette of writing the mushaf
Type 76, 'on the form of writing and the etiquette of writing it', stands in volume six of the edited text. It says it is recommended to write the mushaf, to beautify, clarify and verify its hand, without mashq or ta'liq, that writing it with something impure is forbidden and that gold is good according to al-Ghazali. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2240} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2243} Then comes the question of dotting and vowelling the mushaf and who began it, what the early generations said for and against, al-Nawawi's statement that dotting and vowelling a mushaf are recommended to guard it from error and distortion, Ibn Mujahid's that only what is ambiguous should be vowelled, and al-Dani's opinion on colours for vowels and hamzas. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2245} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249} In his introduction he also counts al-Dani's Muqni' and the commentaries of al-Sakhawi and Ibn Jubara on the Ra'iyya among the 'books of rasm'. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 37} See [[work/al-muqni-fi-rasm-masahif-al-amsar|al-Muqni']], [[work/al-muhkam-fi-naqt-al-masahif|al-Muhkam]], [[work/kitab-al-masahif-ibn-abi-dawud|Kitab al-Masahif]] and Abu 'Ubayd's [[work/fadail-al-quran-abu-ubayd|Fada'il al-Qur'an]].

## The edition read here
The Itqan was read here in the edition of the King Fahd Complex for the Printing of the Noble Mushaf, Centre for Qur'anic Studies, a critical edition with heavy notes that identify people and books and trace the reports; page numbers in the citations are the markers of the scan, not printed page numbers. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 5}
