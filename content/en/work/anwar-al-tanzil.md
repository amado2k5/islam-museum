## Overview
"Anwar al-Tanzil wa Asrar al-Ta'wil" (The Lights of Revelation and the Secrets of Interpretation), a Qur'anic commentary by the judge Nasir al-Din Abu Sa'id Abdullah ibn Umar al-Baydawi the Shafi'i, who died in Tabriz. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.672}

Hajji Khalifa says that the book was well received by the majority of scholars, who devoted themselves to studying it and writing glosses on it. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.672}

## The author's aim
In his preface [[person/al-baydawi-abd-allah-ibn-umar|al-Baydawi]] says that tafsir is the chief of the religious sciences and the foundation of the law's principles, fit only for one skilled in the religious sciences, roots and branches, and in Arabic and the literary arts; that he had long meant to write a book holding the best of what had reached him from the great Companions, the scholars of the Successors and the early generations after them, together with fine points and subtleties drawn out by himself and by the best later scholars and verifiers, and setting out the well-known readings ascribed to the eight imams; and that he named it Anwar al-Tanzil wa-Asrar al-Ta'wil ("The Lights of Revelation and the Secrets of Interpretation"). {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.5} The text is read in Fleischer's printed edition, whose OCR is rough in places; the museum used only what is clear.

## A sample: al-Fatiha and the basmala
Al-Baydawi opens al-Fatiha with its names: the Mother of the Qur'an, as its opening and beginning; the sura of Praise and of Prayer; and the Seven Oft-Repeated, being seven verses by agreement. He reports that it is said to have come down in Mecca when prayer was prescribed and in Medina when the qibla was changed, and holds that it is Meccan; see [[question/where-was-al-fatiha-revealed|Where was al-Fatiha revealed?]]. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6} He then sets out the disagreement over counting [[concept/al-basmala|the basmala]] as one of its verses and argues that it is; see [[question/is-the-basmala-a-verse-of-al-fatiha|Is the basmala a verse of al-Fatiha?]]. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6}

## Editions
- A European edition in Leipzig prepared by H. O. Fleischer and printed by Vogel; its first volume opens with the author's preface. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.5} {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.166}
- The Cairo printing of Dar al-Kutub al-Arabiyya al-Kubra at the expense of Mustafa al-Babi al-Halabi and his brothers, in parts with the gloss of Abu al-Fadl al-Qurashi al-Siddiqi, known as al-Kazaruni, in the margin; the cover of part 4 says al-Azhar's High Council prescribed it for ninth-year students. Printing was finished early in Jumada II 1330 AH. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-halabi-1330-v4 p.1} {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-halabi-1330-v4 p.392} Its corrector Muhammad al-Zuhri al-Ghamrawi writes that the great verifiers and the best later scholars agree it is the commentary that gathers the cream of interpretation and the one relied on to understand the secrets of revelation. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-halabi-1330-v4 p.392}
