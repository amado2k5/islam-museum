## Summary
*I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in 'an Rabb al-'Alamin* ("Informing Those Who Sign on Behalf of the Lord of the Worlds") is a book by Shams al-Din Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya]], the Hanbali of Damascus, who died in 751 AH. Its subject is the foundations of the fatwa, independent reasoning and [[concept/taqlid|following authority]]. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p. 518} {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 1} "Those who sign" on God's behalf are the muftis who convey His rulings, as the book's closing "benefits" on the conduct of the mufti and the rules of the fatwa show. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 40}

## The end of the discussion of following authority: following the Companions
The last volume of the Dar Ibn al-Jawzi edition opens with the remaining arguments Ibn al-Qayyim gives for the obligation to follow the Companions. Among them is the report of Hudhayfa, narrated by al-Tirmidhi, about following Abu Bakr and Umar. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 5} He then states that the Companions' interpretation of the Qur'an comes before every other in all its kinds. He also discusses whether a Successor is treated in the same way, noting that [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] said of one ruling on the hajj that he held it in deference to Ata. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 38} He ends with the order of proofs for those who hold a Companion's opinion to be a proof: the Qur'an, then the Sunna, then the Companion's opinion, then analogy. See [[question/is-a-companions-opinion-a-legal-proof|the Companion's opinion as a proof]]. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 39} {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 40}

## Benefits concerning the fatwa
"Let us close the book with benefits concerning the fatwa," Ibn al-Qayyim writes, and he then sets out more than seventy of them. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 40} {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 605} The first is that questions are of four kinds: about the ruling, about its proof, about how the proof indicates it, and about answering what opposes it. If the one asked does not know the ruling, it is forbidden for him to give a fatwa without knowledge. If he knows what people have said but cannot see which is right, he reports the disagreement. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 41} He notes that when one of the early generations was asked a question, he would ask whether it had actually happened, and would not answer if it had not, because a fatwa based on opinion is allowed only out of necessity. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 41}

The volume's index gives the headings of these benefits. They include:
- that a mufti may turn from the question asked to something more useful, or answer more than was asked;
- that he should give the ruling with its proof and give fatwas in the words of the texts;
- that he should not attribute a ruling to God without a text;
- that he gives the fatwa he believes correct even when it differs from his own school;
- that he must not leave the questioner confused;
- fatwas on the conditions of endowment founders;
- whether one who follows authority may give fatwas;
- the qualities a mufti needs: intention, knowledge, forbearance, dignity, calm, sufficiency and knowledge of people;
- the prohibition on seeking out legal stratagems;
- a mufti taking a fee or a gift;
- that all the imams follow a hadith once it is sound. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 605} {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 606}

He divides muftis into four kinds. The highest is the one who knows God's Book, His Messenger's Sunna and the sayings of the Companions and reasons independently on new cases; his independent reasoning does not preclude his sometimes following someone else. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 125} He takes up the question whether a layperson must keep to a single school, and holds that this is not required. See [[question/must-a-layperson-follow-one-madhhab|following one school]]. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 203}

## The Prophet's fatwas
Ibn al-Qayyim then gathers the Prophet's own fatwas by subject. The subjects are creed, purity, prayer, death and the dead, zakat, fasting, hajj, the merits of suras and deeds, earnings and property, sales, pledges and debts, lost property and gifts, and then marriage, suckling, divorce, khul', zihar, li'an and more. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 607} {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 608}

## The edition in the museum
This is the sixth and last volume in the Ibn al-Qayyim Library series, published by Dar Ibn al-Jawzi, 1st edition Rajab 1423 AH. It is annotated with references for the hadiths and reports and collated with manuscripts. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 1} {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 4} At its end it reproduces the closing note of Muhammad Muhyi al-Din Abd al-Hamid's copy, which says that the fourth part, and with it the book, was complete, and the closing words of the Muniriyya printing. {cite:ilam-muwaqqiin-ibn-jawzi-v6 p. 603}

## Not covered here
The earlier volumes have not yet been read in this edition. They contain his criticism of analogy and legal stratagems and his discussion of fatwas changing with times and places.
