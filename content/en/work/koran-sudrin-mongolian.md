# Koran Sudrïn (the Mongolian translation of the Qur'an)

## Summary
*Koran Sudrïn* is a Mongolian rendering of the meanings of the [[work/quran|Qur'an]] published by the Turkish Presidency of Religious Affairs in 2020, probably the first Mongolian translation published outside Mongolia by a well-known Muslim institution. {cite:gloqur-koran-sudrin-mongolian}

## Context
Muslims are around five per cent of Mongolia's population, most of Kazakh origin and living in Bayan-Ölgii province, and the idea of the translation came from this minority. {cite:gloqur-koran-sudrin-mongolian} An earlier translation by the politician and writer Tserenpiliyn Gombosüren appeared in 2008, but relied mainly on secondary sources and had a limited print run. {cite:gloqur-koran-sudrin-mongolian}

## Who made it
It was translated by Marhabagayin Salih, who holds an MA in religious studies and teaches Arabic, with Bezhzhegiyin Battogtoh as Islamic proofreader and Taukein Sultan, a writer, translator and former member of parliament, as editor. {cite:gloqur-koran-sudrin-mongolian}

## What it contains
Its short introduction calls it a gift from the Turkish state to the people of Mongolia and says it was translated from the Arabic. {cite:gloqur-koran-sudrin-mongolian} It has no footnotes or commentary, only short bracketed insertions naming addressees or explaining meanings, and an index of terms; some names of prophets and scriptures appear in the forms Mongolian took under Russian influence. {cite:gloqur-koran-sudrin-mongolian}

## Its reach
It was printed in significant numbers and is distributed free of charge, and Mykhaylo Yakubovych expects it to hold a notable place in the history of Islam in Mongolia. {cite:gloqur-koran-sudrin-mongolian}
