## Summary
When the Prophet resolved to march to Mecca, [[person/hatib-ibn-abi-baltaa|Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a]] wrote to Quraysh informing them — and the news came from heaven {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/40}. [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]] overtook the carrier at [[place/al-khaliqa|al-Khaliqa]] and drew the letter from the plaits of her head {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

## What happened
Hatib wrote to Quraysh telling them what the Prophet had resolved — the march upon them — and gave the letter to a woman, who put it in the plaits of her head — her hair — and set out with it {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/40}. On her identity there are two accounts: Ibn Ishaq, from Ibn Ja'far, from Urwa says she was a woman of Muzayna; in another account she was [[person/sara-al-mawla|Sara]], client of Banu Abd al-Muttalib {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/40}.

The Prophet sent [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]] and [[person/al-miqdad-ibn-amr|al-Miqdad ibn Amr]], saying: "Go to Rawdat Khakh; there is a woman travelling there with a letter — take it from her" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/40-41}. They overtook her at al-Khaliqa — Banu Abi Ahmad's barren land — and she denied it; they left her till nightfall, and she brought the letter out of the plaits of her head {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

## The hearing
Hatib was brought, and Umar said: "Messenger of God, let me strike this hypocrite's neck" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}. Hatib excused himself: there was no unbelief or apostasy in me; but I am a man with no root or clan among the people — my children and family are among them, so I made them a deed by which they would protect my family {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

The Prophet said to Umar and Abu Bakr: "Believe Hatib", then said: "How do you know, Umar — perhaps God looked upon the people of Badr and said: Do as you will, for I have forgiven you" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}. The opening of Surat al-Mumtahana descended concerning his affair in the account {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

## The Qur'an and the affair
The account links this incident with [[passage/quran-60-1|the opening of Surat al-Mumtahana]]: alliance with the warring enemy who fights over God, and the distinction between that and kindness to those who did not fight {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/41}.

## For young visitors
A man wrote a secret letter to Mecca's people telling them the army was coming. The Prophet learned of it and sent men who stopped the woman carrying it. The Prophet asked the writer why he did it; he said: I wanted your people to protect my family and children in Mecca — and he accepted his excuse.
