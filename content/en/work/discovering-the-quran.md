## Summary

*Discovering the Qur'an: A Contemporary Approach to a Veiled Text* is a book by Neal Robinson, published by SCM Press in London in 1996 in 332 pages. {cite:google-books-discovering-the-quran} The jacket of a later edition, to which the author added a new preface, describes it as a guide used by students for reading a translation of the Qur'an. {cite:openlibrary-discovering-the-quran-work}

## Publication and editions

Its opening chapters treat the Qur'an as Muslims experience it, then the anchoring of the revelations in space and time, then an alternative account of the rise of Islam. {cite:google-books-discovering-the-quran} The jacket description says the book covers the Qur'an's rhythm and rhyme, the setting in which it is heard, the part played by memorisation, the importance of calligraphy, the Qur'an's relationship to Muhammad and the ordering of the suras. {cite:openlibrary-discovering-the-quran-work} Terms that recur in it, according to the catalogue page, include Meccan and Medinan suras, the work of Nöldeke and Schwally and of Bell, and the work of Islahi. {cite:google-books-discovering-the-quran}
