## Summary

A question from a sick pilgrim who stood at Arafat, stayed at Mina, stoned and shaved, then went home without the circumambulation of ifada or the running, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that he remains partly in the state of ihram and is not 'prevented' (muhsar), and must complete the rites himself, appointing a proxy only if he becomes permanently unable. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3826}

## The question

The questioner said he stood at Arafat, stayed overnight at Mina, stoned the pillars and shaved, but was ill and could not perform the circumambulation of ifada, the running between Safa and Marwa or the farewell circumambulation; he had stipulated on entering ihram that he would leave it wherever he was held back, returned home, and cannot travel; he asked whether he counts as prevented. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3826}

## The answer

The Department answered that the circumambulation of ifada is a pillar without which the pilgrim does not leave hajj, and its time never runs out, quoting [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] and [[person/al-khatib-al-shirbini|al-Khatib al-Shirbini]]'s Mughni al-Muhtaj that whoever still owes it remains in ihram. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3826} It said the questioner has made the first release, which lifts every restriction of ihram except those relating to women; if able, he must return to Makkah, owing nothing for passing the miqat since he is still in ihram, perform the circumambulation and the running if his hajj was tamattu', and so make the second release. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3826} If he is unable and has become permanently incapable (ma'dub), he may appoint someone to perform the circumambulation and running for him, quoting [[person/shihab-al-din-al-ramli|al-Shihab al-Ramli]]'s fatwas that this is allowed and indeed required, since if a proxy suffices for the whole rite, it suffices all the more for part of it. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3826} On the farewell circumambulation, it noted that scholars differ on whether it is obligatory or recommended and took the recommended view for ease, so no sacrifice is due; and it said he is not prevented, because illness is not a cause of prevention and he had already stood at Arafat and continued his hajj. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3826}
