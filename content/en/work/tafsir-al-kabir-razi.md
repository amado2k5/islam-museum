## Summary
Al-Tafsir al-Kabir ("The Great Commentary"), or Mafatih al-Ghayb ("The Keys to the Unseen"), is the commentary of [[person/fakhr-al-din-al-razi|Fakhr al-Din al-Razi]]; the cover of the Dar al-Fikr printing names him Muhammad al-Razi Fakhr al-Din, son of the learned Diya' al-Din Umar, known as the Preacher of Rayy, with the dates 544–604 AH. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.1} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 p.1}

## Method: the 'questions'
Al-Razi opens his discussion of a verse with "know that there are questions here", then takes them one by one. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.2} On the verse of al-Baqara about [[person/adam|Adam]] being settled in the Garden there are at least seven: whether the command to dwell is an obligation or a permission, which he resolves as containing both; when Adam's wife was created, where he gives two conflicting reports and ends "God knows the truth best"; that the wife is Eve; where the Garden was; the meanings of the words, quoting the author of [[work/al-kashshaf|al-Kashshaf]]; why the verb is joined with 'and' here and with 'so' in Surat al-A'raf; and what kind of prohibition the tree was. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.2} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.3} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.4} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.5} On the location of the Garden he sets out four views with their arguments; see [[question/where-was-the-garden-of-adam|Where was the Garden in which Adam was settled?]]. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.3} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j3 p.4}

## Rational argument
The book often argues in the theologians' terms. In the commentary on Surat al-Waqi'a, the denial that God has any like is argued thus: if He had a like, it would share something with Him and differ in something, so He would be composite and contingent, not necessarily existent; affirming a partner therefore leads to denying God. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j29 p.156} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 p.159}

## Did al-Razi finish it?
At that same place in the commentary on al-Waqi'a the writer says that after writing this he found something like it "in the words of Imam Fakhr al-Din al-Razi", whose thought matched his own, and acknowledges having gained countless benefits from him. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j29 p.156} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 p.159} The editors of the Dar al-Fikr printing note that this suggests the passage is by an author other than al-Razi — either one of his students who completed it after his death, or a later scholar who filled a gap in the original; the Dar al-Hadith printing reproduces the same note. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-fikr-1401-j29 p.156} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 p.159} The pages read do not name who completed it.
