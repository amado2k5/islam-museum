## Summary
The Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri, or Jahangir-nama, was written by [[person/jahangir-mughal|Jahangir]] himself and covers the years from his accession to the nineteenth year of his reign (1605–24); the last two years were written out by Mu'tamad Khan, since from 1622 Jahangir could no longer continue unaided. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 58} Milo Cleveland Beach calls it lively and highly informative reading, in which Jahangir describes scientific experiments he conducted, birds and flowers he admired, historical events and a wide range of observations. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 14}

## For the history of painting
It preserves Jahangir's judgements of his painters: his claim to name the master of every face in a composite picture {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 19}; his praise of [[person/abu-al-hasan-nadir-al-zaman|Abu'l Hasan]] and his title "Nadir al-Zaman" in 1618, when the painter brought the accession picture for the frontispiece of the book {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 84}; his frequent mentions of [[person/ustad-mansur|Mansur]], "Nadir al-Asr" {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 137}; and his sending of [[person/bishan-das|Bishan Das]] with Khan Alam's embassy to [[person/abbas-i-safavi|Shah Abbas]] to take likenesses {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 107}.

## Its illustrated copy
No complete imperial illustrated copy survives, but several known paintings were probably meant for one, among them The Birth of Jahangir attributed to Bishan Das and the Darbar of Jahangir attributed to [[person/manohar-painter|Manohar]], about 1620. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 58} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 60} Jahangir refers to the work in the memoirs themselves. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 58}
