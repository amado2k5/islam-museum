## Summary
Ruh al-Ma'ani fi Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim wa'l-Sab' al-Mathani ("The Spirit of Meanings in the Commentary on the Mighty Qur'an and the Seven Oft-Repeated") is the commentary of [[person/mahmud-al-alusi|Abu al-Thana' Shihab al-Din Mahmud al-Alusi]], teacher in the sultan's college and mufti of Baghdad, as he describes himself in his preface. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.4} {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.5} A doctoral thesis on his work in rhetoric says he spent fifteen years on it, often working through the night, that it runs to thirty parts and is among the largest and richest commentaries, and that it was first printed at Bulaq in 1301 AH. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.34}

## The dream and the beginning
Al-Alusi says he was drawn to tafsir from childhood, reading by moonlight when candles ran short, and began speaking on its finer points before he was twenty, but put off writing, pleading a troubled mind and straitened means. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.4} {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.5} Then on a Friday night in Rajab 1252 AH he dreamt that God commanded him to fold up the heavens and the earth and join what was split in them; he raised one hand to the sky and lowered the other to the water's resting place. He found in some books that this pointed to writing a commentary, and began it on the night of 16 Sha'ban of that year, the thirty-fourth of his life; see [[event/al-alusi-begins-ruh-al-maani-1252|al-Alusi begins Ruh al-Ma'ani]]. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.5} He says he wrote it in the reign of [[person/mahmud-ii-uthmani|Sultan Mahmud Khan son of Abd al-Hamid Khan]]. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.5}

## Its name
As it neared completion he could find no name that pleased him, so he put the matter to the vizier Ali Rida Pasha, who named it on the spot "Ruh al-Ma'ani fi Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim wa'l-Sab' al-Mathani". {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.5}

## The opening 'benefits'
Al-Alusi set several 'benefits' (fawa'id) before the commentary, the first on the meaning of tafsir and ta'wil and the need for and nobility of the discipline. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.5} There he defines tafsir as the science of how the Qur'an's words are pronounced, what they signify, their rulings and meanings, with complements such as abrogation and occasions of revelation, and lists the views on how tafsir and ta'wil differ: the same for Abu Ubayda; tafsir broader and more used of words for al-Raghib; tafsir as certainty about God's meaning and ta'wil as preferring one possibility for al-Maturidi; tafsir as what concerns transmission and ta'wil as what concerns understanding. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.5} {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.6} He then says all these views differ from the usage of his own day. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.6}

## Author and approach
For the book's relation to its author's creed and school, see [[question/was-al-alusi-salafi-in-creed|Was al-Alusi Salafi in creed?]]. The thesis notes that al-Alusi says in Ruh al-Ma'ani that he was a Shafi'i and moved to the Hanafi school only in transactions. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.57} His son [[person/noman-al-alusi|Nu'man Khayr al-Din]] completed some of his father's books, among them his gloss on Sharh al-Qatr. {cite:juhud-al-alusi-al-balaghiyya-thesis p.35}

## The edition used
The museum used volume 1 of Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya's Beirut printing (1415 AH / 1994), edited and corrected by Ali Abd al-Bari Atiyya. {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.1} {cite:ruh-al-maani-ilmiyya-1994-v1 p.2}
