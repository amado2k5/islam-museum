## Summary
A lesson series by Omar Abdelkafy, given during the blessed month he calls the month of mercy. Each episode takes one story from the Qur'an or from a sound hadith, tells it, and then draws the lessons from it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:01:30}

## His method for Qur'anic stories
He says the Qur'anic story differs from human stories: it often does not name its people, places or times, because its purpose is the lesson and not the wording or the name. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:03:30}

## Structure of an episode
Each episode tells a story from the Book and from the sound Sunna, then pauses on the lessons and admonitions a listener can take from it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:11:30}

## Stories in the first episode
- The story of the two sons of Adam, which he describes as the first killing in human history. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:05:00}
- The story of Nuh and his son, who said he would seek a mountain to protect him from the water; he says the son was among the drowned. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:06:30}
- The story of Ibrahim: his leaving Ismail and his mother Hajar in Makka, the exchange about the sacrifice and its ransom, and his raising of the foundations of the House with Ismail. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:09:00}
- The story of Musa's mother, who was told to nurse him and then to set him on the river if she feared for him; he then describes his being taken into Pharaoh's household. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep01 00:10:00}

## Episode 2: the story of Musa and al-Khidr, and other stories
In the second episode he continues his method: the Qur'anic story is present as if seen, and its lesson matters more than the names of its figures. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:01:30}
- The story of Musa and al-Khidr: Musa asks to learn from him; al-Khidr makes a condition that Musa not ask about anything until it is explained; then the boat is broken and a wall is repaired for a miserly people, and Musa objects each time. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:04:30}
- His lesson: when one person breaks with another over three matters, the outcome is "this is parting between me and you"; he applies this to whoever disobeys God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:05:00}
- The story of Maryam: he presents her labour at the palm trunk as a miracle, then the infant's speech in the cradle affirming his servanthood to God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:07:00}
- The story of Zakariya: he says Zakariya was chosen by lot to care for Maryam, then found out-of-season fruit with her, and she said it was from God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:08:00}
- The story of Sulayman and the hoopoe: the hoopoe was missed, returned with news from Sheba, and he describes Bilqis entering a palace with a glass floor and submitting. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:09:30}
- The story of Yunus in the whale: his prayer and his rescue. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:11:00}
- He addresses those who indulge in what God forbade, hoping Ramadan will be a decisive turning point for them. This is his exhortation, not a ruling on any person. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep02 00:10:00}

## Episode 3: Ibrahim in the valley of Makka
The third episode tells the story of Ibrahim, and he reads it as showing that civilisation grows from inside a person, not from the resources of a place. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:02:00}
- He asks whether civilisations arise outside or inside a person, and answers, as his own view, that civilisation comes from a person's connection with his Lord, even in the middle of a desert. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:04:00}
- He notes the common idea that civilisations arise on riverbanks, and says the story of Ibrahim in a valley without farmland goes against it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:02:30}
- He takes from the House that it calls back the wanderer to his Lord, and explains the takbir at the start of ihram as meaning that God is greater than everything. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:05:30}
- He says a test can come as blessing or as hardship, and that a blessing may look good and still be a trial. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:06:30}
- He describes Ibrahim's giving: his body to the fire, his food to guests, and his son as a sacrifice. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:10:30}
- He advises parents this month to tell a child they hope he will become an imam who guides people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep03 00:11:30}

## Episode 4: the owner of the two gardens
He opens by saying that people are either afflicted or spared, and asks listeners to thank God for wellbeing and to be merciful to the afflicted: someone who in Ramadan is still afflicted with smoking, drink or drugs should not be shamed but prayed for, and he warns against shaming a brother for a sin lest God heal him and afflict you. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:01:00} He criticises the habit of each person treating himself as an angel and the other as a devil. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:01:30}
He then sets out the Qur'anic parable of two men, one of whom was given two gardens of vines surrounded by palms, with crops between them and a river running through them; he describes the scene as if a camera were showing it, and notes that their irrigation was in the owner's hands and did not wait for rain. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:02:30} The lesson he draws is that the rich man forgot that the Giver is God, and slid from one sin to a worse one, from denying the favour to denying the Hour; the danger is to forget the Giver and remember only the gift. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:07:00} See [[concept/qissat-sahib-al-jannatayn|the story of the owner of the two gardens]].
- He compares the man's boast “I have more wealth than you" with some developed nations telling others that they produce and the rest consume; he holds that Muslims fall short if they remain consumers, and reports the scholars' view that all Muslims are in sin if others excel in a useful science and they do not delegate some of their sons to excel in it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:04:00}
- He likens the man's trust that his garden would never perish to the claim of the capitalist system that it will never stop, then mentions the financial crisis. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:05:00}
- He says that one who is not guided grieves over this world because it is his only chance, whereas the believer is in a temporary training stage; he says he collected 120 situations of the Prophet in joy and sorrow and found that he rejoiced only for religion and grieved only for religion (the number is his statement; needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:06:00}
- He mentions the poem “The Clay” (al-Tin) by Iliya Abu Madi, saying it was written when a brother of the poet became a minister and grew proud towards him, and urges listeners to read it in his diwan (the occasion is his account; needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:08:30}
- He closes: giving is not a sign of God's pleasure nor withholding a sign of His anger; both are a trial, so the rich should give thanks, the poor be patient, and the rich not look down on the poor; he cites a hadith that an atom's weight of pride bars a person from paradise. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:10:00}

## Episode 5: the people of the town and Habib al-Najjar
He says that truth never lacks supporters, urges listeners to raise the banner of truth and never back falsehood, and tells how a man asked al-Hasan al-Basri whether he sinned by sewing al-Hajjaj's clothes, and was told: you are like al-Hajjaj. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:01:30} He then tells the story of [[concept/qissat-ashab-al-qarya|the people of the town]]: two messengers were sent to them and rejected, a third was sent to reinforce them, and the people answered that they were only human like themselves; the first thing messengers meet, he says, is denial. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:02:30}
- He digresses to the story of [[person/abdullah-ibn-salam|Abdullah ibn Salam]] and his Jewish community, who praised him when asked about him and reviled him once he declared his Islam. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:04:00}
- He says the man who came urging his people to follow the messengers is named in the commentaries Habib al-Najjar, “the man of Ya Sin”; his people killed him, he was admitted to paradise, and the speaker presents him as a model of steadfastness and a good end. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:06:00}
- He holds that many messengers sent to one people is no sign of their goodness but of their many ills, as many doctors point to many diseases. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:05:30}
- On [[concept/al-rizq-al-rububi|provision]]: it is in heaven, not in the central bank or the company; he tells the story of Sulayman and the ant that ate one grain and kept the other once its provision lay in a creature's hand, and asks how the unborn child was provided for in the womb. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:08:00}
- He reports the scholars' saying that a person is born crying while his family laughs, so he should work until he leaves the world laughing while they weep. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep05 00:08:30}

## Episode 6: Dhu al-Qarnayn
He tells the story of [[person/dhu-al-qarnayn|Dhu al-Qarnayn]], whom God established in the land and gave a means to everything, and who followed the means; he reports the scholars' saying that the world was ruled by two believers and two unbelievers: Dhu al-Qarnayn and Sulayman, Nimrod and Nebuchadnezzar. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:01:30}
- Faith, he says, is not wishful thinking but what settles in the heart and is confirmed by deeds; a Muslim who lies, takes bribes and backbites turns non-Muslims away from Islam. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:02:30}
- At the mention of Gog and Magog he lists [[concept/ashrat-al-saa|the major signs of the Hour]] as he narrates them, and says that once the sun rises from the west repentance is closed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:04:00}
- His lesson: the people waited for Dhu al-Qarnayn to build their barrier although the materials and the manpower were theirs; today, he argues, Arabs and Muslims wait for foreign experts while they have the minds and the materials, which he calls “the complex of the vanquished”, and he urges the rich to sponsor gifted children in modern sciences as a continuing charity. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:06:00}
- He says that one sincere in his unbelief prevails over one false in his faith, and closes by asking: have we honoured our religion? He calls for reading the Qur'an with reflection, not as one reads a newspaper. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep06 00:09:30}

## Episode 7: Qarun
He presents the story of [[person/qarun|Qarun]] as the story of corruption through wealth, the use of a favour to disobey the One who gave it, and says that a servant will be asked where his wealth came from and on what he spent it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:02:00} He holds that “hoarding Qarunism” joined to “tyrannical Pharaonism” in a country is corruption itself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:02:30}
- [[concept/shukr|Gratitude]], he says, is the tether of a blessing; what is not tied by gratitude slips away, for the individual and for the state. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:03:30}
- He distinguishes praiseworthy joy (in obedience, in memorising the Qur'an, in a sinner's repentance) from the blameworthy joy that leads to insolence and arrogance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:05:00}
- He tells that Qarun, asked for the zakat, said he had been given his wealth through knowledge of his own, and that he turned base metals into gold (his account; needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:05:30}
- He distinguishes [[concept/hasad|envy]] from ghibta, wishing for the like of another's blessing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:08:30}
- He says that blessing is stripped from the provision of one who prays the dawn prayer after sunrise, and that ample provision is no sign of God's pleasure nor scant provision of His anger. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:10:30}

## Episode 8: Sulayman, the hoopoe and Bilqis
He begins with God's apportioning of livelihoods: there are heirs of knowledge, of prophethood and of wealth, and the greatest thing given to Dawud and Sulayman, beyond the wind, the jinn and the birds, was beneficial knowledge, not certificates without practice. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:02:30} He then tells how [[person/sulayman|Sulayman]] inspected the birds and missed the hoopoe, and draws lessons in management from it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:03:30}
- A manager, he says, must stay alert to his staff, reward and punish, and not treat the diligent and the flatterer alike; and whoever sees another angry should withdraw until he calms down. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:04:00}
- Sulayman did not take the hoopoe's report at face value but checked it, and the small hoopoe in his kingdom became the cause of a whole nation's guidance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:10:30}
- He describes [[person/bilqis|Bilqis]] as shrewd: she consulted her people and sent a gift to test Sulayman, and her envoys found the road to his throne paved with gold. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:09:00}
- From her entry into the palace he draws the lesson that civilisation lies in modesty and in keeping to values: she was a queen, and she wore long garments. This is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:10:00}
- He closes by asking listeners never to belittle a person or a trade, and to keep humility and leave pride and self-admiration. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep08 00:11:00}

## Episode nine: Solomon and the ants
- The episode opens by describing Solomon as the king whom God gave what He had not given anyone in the world, and whom his father David was set beside as a model of God's wealthy, grateful servants; a blessing, he says, must be tied with the tie of gratitude as a beast is tied. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:02:00}
- It recalls his march past the ants' town with his hosts, and the ant calling her sisters: "O ants, enter your dwellings lest Solomon and his hosts crush you unknowing," at which Solomon smiles, for he understood the tongues of birds, insects, animals, jinn and devils — every creature has a language. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:06:00}
- He recounts four birds chattering in a tree: "Would that mankind had not been created," "would that, having been created, they knew why they were created," "would that, knowing why, they acted upon it," and "would that, having acted, they were sincere in it" — and he reads into "He did not create me to waste my life in dancing, song, disorder, prayerlessness and harming people" the meaning of why creation exists. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:07:30}
- He says that of all God's creatures there is a glorification man cannot comprehend, and attributes this to God having sealed our ears against hearing their praise so that we benefit from it, closing by asking God to accept repentance in this month and to guide us to what He loves. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:10:00}
- He tells of an Arab man who came to [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], in whom Umar saw roughness and rigidity; Umar tested him, and when the man offered his service, Umar said there was no need for the governorship of a man who is like that with his own sons — how could he be with the sons of the Muslims; the dialogue in the recording is garbled. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep09 00:08:30} (needs_source: the details of the exchange between the man and Umar are unclear in the recording)

## Episode ten: the mother of Moses
- The episode opens by saying that all the Qur'anic stories are lessons containing creed, sincere reliance on God and certainty in His victory, and that every grant is born from inside a trial. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:01:30}
- It stands at the story of [[person/umm-musa|the mother of Moses]]: God commanded her to nurse him and then cast him into the river if she feared for him, and she said "fear not and do not grieve," while the baby went to the palace of Pharaoh, who had ordered the killing of the Israelite newborns — yet Pharaoh's tongue said what fate's tongue would not let him contradict: Moses would only be raised in his own lap. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:11:00}
- He says a human mother melts if she sees a fly on her child's face — how then a mother commanded to cast her infant; and that it was God's bonding on her heart that made her a believer, and that at calamities there are two kinds of people: those with a store of faith from which they draw, and those with none who collapse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:08:30}
- He illustrates the tug between divine law and human nature in Ramadan: the law says rise and pray at night while nature sleeps, the law says give charity while nature makes excuses — man stands between two forces that pull him apart. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:10:00}
- He says the infant went to the enemy of Moses who was the first scourge of God, yet Asia loved him after God's favour — and in Ibn al-Qayyim's words he was a child born without a mother — while Pharaoh's calamity bore the grant for Moses. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep10 00:11:00}

## Episode eleven: Ibrahim and Ismail (the sacrifice)
- He opens with the complaint of unfilial children — sons and daughters — in homes, telling of a boy who asked his father for an hour of his time, was refused because the hour cost a hundred dirhams, then returned days later with ninety-five dirhams, handed his father a hundred to buy the hour he would spend with him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:02:30}
- He says the true orphan is not the one whose father died but the one whose mother abandoned him or whose father was busy; then he moves to the story of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]] with [[person/ismail|Ismail]]: "O my son, I see in a dream that I am sacrificing you — see what you think," noting that the dream had already occurred but Ibrahim says "I see" because he is conveying God's command to his son. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:03:30}
- He recounts Ismail's answer "O my father, do what you are commanded; you will find me, God willing, among the patient," his instruction to bind him firmly and not to see his face lest compassion seize him, and his giving his father his shirt to take to his mother. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:05:30}
- He says Satan did not let him carry out the command, so he came to Ibrahim at the third stone-throwing saying "O Ibrahim, have you gone mad? Have you lost your mind, man? You sacrifice your own father," whereupon Ibrahim cried "God is greatest" to cover the voice of outward falsehood and let the voice of truth rise from his obedient heart. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:08:00}
- He tells of a man who wanted to give charity; a scholar told him seventy devils stood in his way, so he carried flour to his wife to distribute to the poor and his mother overcame them; and he says the devil of mankind is harder than the devil of the jinn, for the former commands immorality and adorns it for him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:07:30}
- He says at the third stone-throwing relief came from God: "You have fulfilled the vision; thus We reward the doers of good," and draws the lesson of kindness to parents: be kind to your fathers and your sons will be kind to you, and children signing the dawn prayer by waking for it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep11 00:09:30}

## Episode twelve: the People of the Trench
- He opens with Surat al-Buruj: "By the heaven holding constellations, by the promised Day, a witness and one witnessed," and describes it as true stories because God says "We relate to you the best of stories." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:05:30}
- He recounts the king who had a sorcerer raising a boy in sorcery; the boy would pass a monotheist hermit and claim he was with his parents, so he learned monotheism and truth from the hermit; when the sorcerer died the boy succeeded him, and when the king fell ill he was cured by God's leave and spoke "I worship none but God, Lord of the worlds." The king ordered him cast on the seashore; the mountain shook and everything around him fell except him, then he was put in a ship on the sea, which overturned and nothing survived but him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:03:30}
- He says the boy made the king gather all his people, and had himself crucified on a palm trunk while an arrow was aimed at him and he said "In the name of God, Lord of this boy" — the boy was martyred and all the people of the kingdom spoke the testimony of monotheism. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:04:30}
- He narrates that the king dug the trench, lit the fire, and cast into it everyone who did not believe, until only an infant remained on her mother's shoulder; he cast her in, and she spoke "There is no god but God" before being thrown. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:05:00}
- He comments that the martyrs feel no pain of death — the blow and the fire are like a thorn — and that God swears by the heaven of constellations and the promised Day. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:06:00}
- He lists the five days: the lost day — yesterday that perished in obedience or sin; the witnessed day — today in which we witness our dead; the apportioned day — tomorrow known only to God; the promised day — when the angel of death comes to the servant; and the extended day — the Day of Resurrection, the day of eternity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:08:00}
- He tells of al-Hajjaj telling a fasting man "fast tomorrow" and the man answering "does the commander guarantee me I will live to tomorrow?", and of the Bedouin who asked a prophet "when is the Hour?" and was asked "what have you prepared for it?" — "love of God and His Messenger"; and he says we are not like al-Hajjaj but like Uthman, Umar and the righteous who stood firm on truth until the angel of death came to them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep12 00:10:00}

## Episode thirteen: Ibrahim and the four birds
- He describes Ibrahim as al-Khalil, father of the prophets, claimed by every community, who was neither Jew nor Christian but a hanif Muslim, and notes we pray for him in the tashahhud as "as You blessed Ibrahim and his family." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:02:00}
- He recounts al-Khalil's question "My Lord, show me how You give life to the dead," God's answer "Do you not believe?" — "Yes, but to reassure my heart" — and the command to take four birds, cut them up, place a part of each on every mountain, then call them so they come to him swiftly, that he may know God is able over all things. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:06:00}
- He explains why God chose birds: the peacock for pride, the vulture for pouncing, the rooster for dominating the weaker flock, the crow for hoarding; slaughtering them thus reads as a verdict on those blameworthy traits in every soul — vainglory, pouncing on people's honour, wealth, secrets and the weak, and greed for money. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:05:30}
- He says every mercy in man carries peacock-like thoughts — of being greater, nobler, richer than others — so one must slaughter pride and false vainglory in oneself, not dominate the weak by force, and not be a crow greedy for money; the generous is near God, Paradise and people, far from the Fire, and the miser the reverse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:05:30}
- He draws that nothing harms the seeker of truth; God the Generous does not ask who gave or how much, but gives, grants, forgives and pardons; and no request is impossible — so one prays for the rebellious child that his life and trust be revived, for the wayward wife that piety return to her heart, and for the one half-dead in life that his breast be opened and light placed in his heart. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:08:30}
- He says the truly dead is not the one who died but the one who lived gloomy and hopeless; he recounts the Bedouin who asked the Prophet about the Hour and was asked "what have you prepared for it?" — "love of God and His Messenger" — and "he who wishes to look at a man of the people of Paradise should look at him whose tongue stays moist with God's remembrance morning and evening with no sin upon him." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep13 00:10:30}

## Episode fourteen: the tongue
- He opens with "spend of what is between your two hands and hold back what is between your two lips," and says the scholars read it: spend of what is between you and yourself, hold back what is between your lips — yet people hoard with their hands and lose with their tongues. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:01:00}
- He lists the tongue's grave sins: lying, false testimony, false oath, backbiting, tale-bearing and obscenity, and describes meddling in people's honour as the worst kind of tearing society apart — like a great ox that cannot return through a needle's eye. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:02:30}
- He recalls the slander story in which the Righteous was accused, and says a man owns his word once spoken; in that story we see the speaker assumes intimacy with the person he gossips about, that tale-bearing betrays a trust, and that whoever gossips about you today will gossip about you tomorrow; what is haram in Ramadan's day is haram in its night and outside it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:04:00}
- He cites the figure that the Arab man speaks some 7,500 words a day and the Arab woman some 11,500, asking how much of the Qur'an hides among them; we resemble a man who built a minaret then asked the contractor to demolish it — like the soul that unwove its spun yarn after strength became frailty — tearing down good deeds with backbiting, tale-bearing and abuse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:05:30}
- He calls for holding the tongue and giving charity hidden from the left hand to the poor neighbour's son; religion is not shortcoming but manifestation — the scholar must not sit in an ivory tower but teach people, the man of status should open his office and solve people's problems, and the rich should be rich in lawful wealth and distribute his alms. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:07:00}
- He closes with the question posed to every listener: what have I presented to Islam so that I may meet God with it? — and says this blessed month is a chance for a pause between the rich and the provider, and between the man of status and his status. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep14 00:08:00}

## Episode fifteen: the owners of the garden
- He opens by saying a blessing is bound only by the tie of gratitude — "spend of what is between you and yourself, hold back what is between your hands" — and tells the story of generosity and miserliness: three brothers inherited a huge garden in the north of Sanaa, as tafsir books record, and their father had been generous to the poor. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:02:00}
- He says the brothers swore they would not sell it in the morning and would not invest it, and swore to give nothing to the poor — so God tested them as He tested the owners of the two gardens, the harvest almost ready while the town slept. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:05:30}
- He recounts that a band from their Lord passed over it while they slept and it became like al-sarim; He who grew the fruit is He who wastes it, He who gave is He who takes, He who bestowed is He who withholds — yet the brothers think their intellect, rank and chair protect the blessing from fading. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:06:30}
- He says the poor came whispering, not knowing that inside the garden there was "no power nor strength but in God" and "do not let the poor enter," and went away sullen; when they saw the garden it had become like al-sarim — a bare wooden trunk with no branches, leaves or fruit. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:09:00}
- He quotes one of them saying "we were indeed wrongdoers" as they blamed one another, and says glorification is by action not words: God gave, so one gives thanks by spending; wealth belongs to God — "the rich are My trustees and the poor My family" — and their strength lies in enriching their poor brothers; he cites the UN figure that nearly half the world's poverty lies in the Muslim world. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:11:00}
- He says the blessing waits for gratitude until it turns to wrath, and calls for alms given from lawful, hidden wealth rather than unlawful money, for the scholar to serve people rather than sit in his ivory tower, and for the man of status to solve people's problems — closing with the question "what have I presented to Islam so that I may meet God with it?" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep15 00:11:30}

## Episode sixteen: Moses in Midian
- He describes [[person/musa|Moses]] after he had had his fill of Pharaoh's tyranny and turned thirty — the royal who was raised in Pharaoh's palace — then descended to an Egyptian city where an Egyptian of Pharaoh's people struggled with an Israelite; Moses pushed him and the Egyptian fell dead, and the next day a man from the city's edge came to him: "Moses, the great ones plot to kill you, so leave; I am one of those who advise you in honesty," and he went out wary. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:03:00}
- He draws the first lesson: God will not abandon a sincere servant; if people forsake you, the Lord of people will not forsake you, for He is more merciful to you than your father and mother, and if the earth forsakes you the heaven will not; a narration says God calls to Him with the tongues of your righteous brothers and sisters who never disobeyed Him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:04:00}
- He describes Midian: shepherds covering the well with a heavy lid only ten or twenty men could lift, and he found two women standing guard over the flock who said "we will not water until the shepherds come," so he watered for them — an act of manly chivalry — then turned to the shade and prayed "my Lord, for whatever good You send down to me I am in need" — never dispense with God's favour in small or great matters, and do not forget God in ease so He remembers you in hardship. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:06:30}
- He says manliness is not raising one's voice or assaulting the weak but containment, intellect and chivalry, and that a woman must be honoured and should not leave for work except out of necessity, and then mix only as much as necessity requires. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:06:00}
- He recounts that one of them came walking shyly and said "my father invites you to be paid for what you watered for him"; Moses accepted, told his story, and was told "fear not, you are saved from the wrongdoers" — the recompense is of the same kind as the deed: what you sow you reap, and whoever does good to people, God brings out of them one who honours his family and children after his death. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:08:30}
- He says the best hire [[person/shuayb|Shu'ayb]] offered was "the strong, the faithful," proposing that Moses marry one of his two daughters on condition of serving him eight years — ten if he wished — so that Moses became of the household after having been alone, poor and without family; he stayed with his father-in-law ten years so that the killing penalty would lapse under ancient Egyptian law, then returned to Sinai where the tree burned and he was called "I am God," and was given the staff that became a serpent and sent as messenger to Pharaoh. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:11:00}
- He concludes that trials bear grants: distress brings relief, grief brings victory, helplessness brings might, illness brings health, straitness brings spaciousness; "with hardship is ease, so when you are free, strive, and to your Lord turn your longing." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep16 00:12:00}

## Episode seventeen: blessing in Jabir's house
- He opens with [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abdullah]]'s account at the Trench: he saw the Prophet famished — "khams" — so he came to his wife, who said there was only a sa' of barley and a small domestic animal; they slaughtered the sheep and ground the barley, and Jabir told the Prophet "you and ten are eating at my house," for the Trench had lasted over a month while the confederates stood on the other side and the Jews of Medina had broken their pact. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:02:00}
- He describes the Prophet saying "people of the Trench, Jabir has made food — it is upon all of you," meaning upon your houses, then placing his hand in the dough; the blessing settled on Jabir's house and the whole army ate in tens until the food was gone. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:03:30}
- He says blessing has not been lifted from the earth while there are truthful people, excellent women and those connected to God who eat the lawful; he tells of a worker who complained to [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] that five dirhams were not enough, so Abu Hanifa had him ask his employer for four, then three — and the three sufficed; his work was worth only three, and the excess had been unlawful, so haram mixed with halal and scattered it; when he took what his work was worth, blessing came. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:06:30}
- He mentions that [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Mas'ud]] used to say "the dearest person to me is the guest," for the guest's provision is on God and his reward falls on the household; and that the generous are near God, Paradise and people, far from the Fire, and the miserly the reverse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:10:00}
- He recounts that Ibn Mas'ud's coins were stolen in the market; when they wanted to curse the thief he said "I shall pray and you say amen": "O God, if You know the one who stole my coins needed them, bless him in them; and if he did not need them, make it the last sin he commits against You" — and from the theft he gained reward with God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:09:30}
- He says dignity belongs only in a Muslim's house, and closes by noting provision is of two kinds — the positive that is given and the negative that is withheld, as in preventing illness or accidents — and both are provision. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep17 00:10:00}

## Episode eighteen: every moist liver has a reward
- He opens by saying a person may despise a small deed, waiting until five becomes fifty; he cites "fear the Fire even with half a date" and "God raises up your charity by your right hand and nurtures it as you nurture your foal" — the servant should not consider small goods insignificant. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:01:00}
- He recounts [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]'s hadith of the thirsty man who came down a well and found a panting dog licking dust from thirst; he removed his fear, went down, scooped water in his mouth, climbed up and gave the dog to drink, and was forgiven — "in every moist liver is a reward." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:02:30}
- He says this hadith answers those who falsely claim Muslims do not spread animal rights; first seek human rights — he recalls a French actress's nervous breakdown in the 1970s because sparrows were dying in Lebanon's civil war while the deaths of men, women and children went unremarked. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:03:30}
- He tells of a camel that complained to the Prophet that its owner carried it beyond its strength, and the Prophet said "fear God regarding the dumb beasts"; and that in 3rd–4th century Hijri Egypt there were endowments for stray dogs and cats, a "yogurt-pot waqf" of clay vessels so a servant who broke one would not be punished by his master, and waqfs for storytellers who told tales of hope to the incurably ill in hospitals. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:05:30}
- He says the Muslim knows optimism not pessimism, and cites the man who visited a relative saying "what is wrong, uncle? You brought me chest tightness" — "there is no god but God, it is the same illness my father died of" — which is pessimism, not optimism; and every seed a person plants earns reward. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:06:30}
- He recalls the woman who entered the Fire over a cat she confined — neither feeding it nor letting it eat of the earth's vermin — and says there is no injustice on the Day: "no injustice today," for this world runs on injustice, the strong oppress the weak, yet the scales are set aright then. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:08:30}
- He closes with [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]]'s dream as told by Ibn Abbas: his throne nearly fell until he remembered he had freed a sparrow children had tied with thread, and was told "you freed the sparrow's captivity; We free your captivity from the Fire and admit you to Paradise" — let no one despise any good deed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep18 00:09:30}

## Episode nineteen: Pharaoh drowned
- He opens by saying wrongdoing has a grim consequence and pride upon the earth must remember there is a capable Lord; God told Moses "go to Pharaoh, he has transgressed" — not "he disbelieves," for unbelief is a matter between servant and Lord — and "transgressed" warns against wronging God's servants by office, wealth, clan or social standing; wronging the weakest is double wrong because he has no helper but God — like the aged woman whose father and brother died and who remains under your care. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:02:30}
- He describes the sea before the Children of Israel and punishment behind them when Moses' companions said "we are overtaken"; he answered "my Lord is with me; He will guide me" — the first lesson: however narrow the causes, be with God and you have won all; lose God's side and you have lost all. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:04:00}
- He says one confident in God was commanded to strike the sea with his staff and it parted, each part like a great mound; the Children of Israel had twelve tribes so God made them twelve bridges they crossed seeing one another; when the last soldier reached the far shore and Pharaoh came to catch them, the bridges collapsed and Pharaoh and his hosts drowned. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:07:00}
- He says man does not look only at outward causes but also adopts and abandons them; Maryam in the prayer-niche was not commanded to abandon means in health, but in weariness was told "shake toward you the trunk of the palm; it will drop ripe dates upon you" — the Lord of causes prepares the causes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:06:30}
- He quotes a righteous man: "were the heaven of silver and the earth of iron, and not a drop rained nor a plant grew nor a child between east and west, I would know God would provide them, for I know God provides" — certainty guards from every harm; when provision in one place stops, God opens another door of provision; "none despairs of God's mercy except the unbelieving people." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:09:00}
- He tells of the shepherdess who gave her last morsel of bread and salt to a stranger; when the wolf seized her infant she ran after it and heard a voice — "a morsel for a morsel, but how great the difference between the two morsels" — and her child was returned; and he says give even a dirham or two in a financial crisis, for you do not know whence good comes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:10:00}
- He says the second lesson is "nothing moves in this universe except by its Lord's command" — "what did not miss you was not destined to reach you, and what reached you was not destined to miss you"; the third that the affair is never cut off from God even far from the path; and he recalls the Prophet left the young Ali in his bed then emigrated with Abu Bakr, so Ali slept under God's protection, while those who sought the Prophet's life were veiled from sight — He who gives sight is He who veils it, and blindness is the heart's work, not the eyes' going dark. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep19 00:11:30}

## Episode twenty: deeds end with their endings
- He recounts from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] that two men of the Children of Israel were brothers in God — one diligent in worship, the other sinful — and the diligent one kept seeing him in sin and saying "turn back"; until he found him in a sin and said "leave me be" — "by God, God will not forgive you nor admit you to Paradise." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:01:30}
- He says both were taken and met before God; the diligent one was asked "were you able over what is in My hand?" — could you have stopped him? — then the sinner was sent to Paradise and the diligent one to the Fire, for a word can reach the degree of God's displeasure or pleasure that it admits its owner to Paradise or Fire, and who can determine that? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:02:30}

- He distinguishes the sinner who confesses and seeks forgiveness — "my father and mother never taught me prayer; God willing, repentance is open" — from the prayer-denier who says "none prays but one who eats usury"; the first is a believer who returns, the second a stubborn denier. - He says deeds end with their endings, and that Pharaoh's sorcerers who believed in their last moment entered Paradise with a single prostration; a good ending comes from a pure heart, and God accepts repentance even at the death-rattle; there is no door to God and no doorkeeper — the answered call is near: "O servant, if you obey, I am your Lord and you are Mine; if you disobey, I am still your Lord and you are Mine." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:07:00}

- He closes by saying cursing the wrongdoer is not required — the wronged is dispersed by it; "do not supplicate against yourselves, your offspring or your wealth," for your prayer against him may hasten what you do not intend — "his wrong reaches him faster than your supplication"; and we ask God for a good ending. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep20 00:10:00}

## Episode twenty-one: the People of the Cave
- He opens by saying the struggle between truth and falsehood is an eternal struggle that will not end until God is pleased with the earth and those upon it — the cosmic law of mutual striving; and truth always triumphs though people grow impatient: a wronged brother's unjust judge convinces him justice is lost, yet God says "which of you is nearer in benefit?" — He may withhold from you a harm that would have afflicted you. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:02:00}
- He recounts the story of [[concept/ashab-al-kahf|the People of the Cave]]: their first youth fled with their religion, and "know who you keep company with" is the greatest lesson — the shepherd's dog is mentioned in the Qur'an because he was the companion of the righteous, while [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]], the Prophet's uncle, is not named as such because his companions were the dregs and rabble, so he died on unbelief; so with whom do you associate? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:04:30}
- He says spaciousness is the spaciousness of chests, not of palaces; he cites the verse "blessed are those who lodge beneath the protection of life, and guests beneath the shelter of the protected, and the best earth is what the heart loves, loftiest are the threads with beloveds as field" — the world cannot hold those estranged within one continent, and [[person/al-shafii|Imam al-Shafi'i]] used to say of a heavy man "I feel the earth tilting from his side." {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:07:00}
- He explains their sleep: [[person/musa|Moses]] prayed first "my Lord, expand for me my breast and ease my task"; they were cast into the cave and remained three hundred and nine years — the difference between the two calendars — the sun veering right of their right hands and left of their left as if patients in a hospital being turned so their skins would not ulcerate; then they woke thinking they had slept a day or part of a day. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:09:00}
- He says the story teaches that whoever finds a place that keeps him from God should migrate to a place where he worships Him, and he lists five conditions for the companion you keep: that seeing him reminds you of God, that his state — not merely his words — points to God, that he increases your knowledge through his intellect, that he alerts you when you are heedless, and that he aids you when you remember. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep21 00:11:30}

## Episode twenty-two: who is the most knowledgeable?
- He describes [[passage/quran-18-60|the verse]]: "when Moses said to his servant, I will not cease until I reach the junction of the two seas or travel for ages" — and the fish was forgotten when they reached their meeting place; then they passed a town whose people worshipped idols and he wished to save them but they refused.
