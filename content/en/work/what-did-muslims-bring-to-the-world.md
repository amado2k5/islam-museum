## Summary
The Assabile archive keeps fifteen video episodes of a television programme titled Madha qaddama al-muslimun li-l-alam ("What did Muslims bring to the world"), whose regular guest is [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]], in conversation with a host who introduces him as professor at the Faculty of Medicine of Cairo University and supervisor of the Islam Story website. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep13 00:01:30} Each episode opens with questions put by the host, which the guest then answers, with breaks marked by a song that recurs at the start and end of the episodes. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:15:00} The subject of the programme is what Islamic civilization gave the world: the definition of civilization, the state of the world before Islam, the sources of Islamic civilization, the society of Medina, the rights of human beings, of minorities and of animals, and the ethics of war. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep13 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:02:00} The programme carries the title of a book of his of the same name, which won the prize of the Supreme Council for Islamic Affairs in 2009. {cite:wikipedia-ragheb-al-sergani-ar}

The machine transcripts of the episodes read clearly for the most part, with garbled places in names and numbers that are not written here. In the fifteenth episode the host refers to an earlier episode on war and peace that is not among the episodes in the archive, so the fifteen episodes are part of the programme, not the whole of it. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:01:30} The guest's views, figures and dates are attributed to him, and the history he narrates is his account.

## Episode 1: why study history, and what civilization is
He says Islamic civilization was wronged in how it was recorded and written about, and that studying history is the way out of the community's crises because God's patterns in nations are fixed, citing a book by al-Sakhawi answering those who disparage history. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:11:00} He then sets out definitions of civilization among Muslims and Westerners and gives his own: man's relationship with the environment, with other people, and with his Lord. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:20:30} See: [[concept/ilm-al-tarikh|the science of history]], [[concept/sunnat-allah|Sunnat Allah]], [[concept/islam-and-the-rise-of-civilisation|Islam and the rise of civilisation]].

## Episode 2: the three levels, the best community, and witness over mankind
He completes his definition and applies it to present-day civilizations, saying the three criteria came together only in the Muslim community, and reads the verse of "the best community" in Surat Al Imran and the verse on witness over people in that light. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:24:30} He cites what he attributes to [[person/ribi-ibn-amir|Rib'i ibn Amir]] before Rustam, to the effect that God sent the Muslims to bring people from the worship of fellow servants to the worship of the Lord of servants. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:18:00} See: [[concept/ummat-wasat|the middle community]].

## Episode 3: the world on the eve of Islam
He says that about a hundred years before the mission the great civilizations had sunk to a degree of decay that threatened the ruin of the world. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:04:00} He describes Greek civilization as attending to thought while neglecting practice and morals, and cites Aristotle's justification of slavery. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:10:00} He says Harun al-Rashid and al-Ma'mun, in their treaties with the Eastern Roman state, exchanged prisoners for books. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:12:30} He describes the Roman state as draining the wealth of its provinces and treating them as granaries, and its arenas where beasts tore slaves apart before the crowd, and then the extent of the Persian state from Iraq to the borders of China. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep03 00:21:00}

## Episode 4: Arabia before the mission
He says that whoever reviews the history of Arabia finds no earlier civilization there, except the civilization of Saba in Yemen, which had ended long before. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep04 00:03:00} He illustrates the Arabs' smallness before Persia and Rome with Abu Sufyan before Heraclius. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep04 00:10:00} He then lists Arab virtues such as truthfulness, generosity, chivalry and courage. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep04 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep04 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep04 00:19:30} See: [[concept/jahili-virtues|the virtues of the Jahiliyya Arabs]].

## Episode 5: Arab society before Islam
He describes tribal society, the forms of marriage, vendetta and military weakness. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep05 00:13:00} He says at the end that the minds of the Arabs in that age were ordinary minds and that it was Islam that transformed them. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep05 00:24:30} See: [[concept/social-condition-jahiliyya|the social condition of the Jahiliyya]].

## Episode 6: the Qur'an and the Sunna as the springs of civilization
He asks how the Muslim community grew so far in so few years, and cites Gustave Le Bon to the effect that Europe owes its civilization to the Muslims. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:03:00} He says the difference between the Companions and later generations lay in acting at once on what they heard, with examples such as Abu Talha, who gave away his most beloved property on hearing the verse, and a Companion at Badr who threw away the dates he was eating and fought until he was killed. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:18:30} He then makes the Sunna the second spring, saying that God's preserving the Qur'an includes preserving the Sunna. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:22:30}

## Episode 7: Arabs and other peoples in Islam
He says Islam judges individuals by piety and deeds, not by race, distinguishes love of one's people from blameworthy tribalism, and recalls how the Prophet gave Bilal the call to prayer and said of Salman that he was of the household. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:25:30} See: [[concept/asabiyya|asabiyya]].

## Episode 8: the contribution of non-Arabs
He tells the report of Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik and al-Zuhri about the mawali who led the regions, and estimates the share of non-Arabs among Islam's great figures. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:23:30} See: [[concept/mawali|the mawali]].

## Episode 9: the society of Medina
He makes brotherhood the distinctive mark of Medinan society, speaks of the brotherhood pact, and then of self-sacrifice, which he says took root in the community until a Muslim would give even to those he did not know. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep09 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep09 00:20:30} See: [[event/brotherhood-of-muhajirun-and-ansar|the brotherhood of the Muhajirun and the Ansar]].

## Episode 10: the balance of Islamic civilization
He says Muslim society is balanced: ascetic from one angle, busy with trade and building from another. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep10 00:02:00} He contrasts this with civilizations swinging between extremes, naming in Persia Zoroaster, Mani and Mazdak. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep10 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep10 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep10 00:05:30} He mentions the Prophet's search for a site for the market of Medina at its founding, and sets Nietzsche's school of the individual against Durkheim's school of society. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep10 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep10 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep10 00:21:00}

## Episode 11: human rights
He rejects the idea that the first declaration of human rights was the United Nations' in the twentieth century, saying Islam regards the human being as honoured whatever his creed or colour, and that his rights were legislated by God so that no ruler can change them. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:11:00} He counts freedom of belief the most important right, then mentions equality in the rows of prayer, the Prophet's rebuke of Abu Dharr for taunting a man about his mother, and the right of sufficiency. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:24:30} See: [[concept/freedom-in-islam|freedom in Islam]].

## Episode 12: the rights of minorities
He speaks of the treatment of minorities, tells the story of Ali and the Jew before Shurayh, and defends the jizya. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:23:00} See: [[concept/ahl-al-dhimma|ahl al-dhimma]], [[event/shurayh-armor-judgment|Shurayh's judgement on the armour]], [[concept/jizya|jizya]].

## Episode 13: the rights of animals
He sets out Islam's view of the animal, the hadiths of mercy towards it and the manners of slaughter. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep13 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep13 00:21:30} See: [[concept/hayawan-animal|hayawan]], [[person/abu-ishaq-al-shirazi|Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi]].

## Episode 14: animals in fiqh and in society
He speaks of the rulings on animals in the books of fiqh, and then of kindness to them in society: Adi ibn Hatim and the ants, Amr ibn al-As and the dove, the Damascus endowments for cats and old horses, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, before criticizing bullfighting. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:23:30} See: [[person/adi-ibn-hatim|Adi ibn Hatim]], [[place/al-fustat|al-Fustat]], [[concept/waqf|waqf]], [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]].

## Episode 15: the ethics of war
He says morals are an inseparable part of Islamic civilization even on the battlefield, and sets out his count of those killed in the Prophet's campaigns, the forbidding of killing non-combatants, safe-conduct, the charges given to armies and the treatment of captives. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:23:30} See: [[concept/akhlaq-al-harb|the ethics of war]], [[concept/aman|aman]], [[event/badr-prisoners|the prisoners of Badr]], [[event/capture-and-islam-of-thumama-ibn-uthal|the capture and Islam of Thumama ibn Uthal]].
