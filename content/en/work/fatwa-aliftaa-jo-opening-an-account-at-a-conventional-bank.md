## Summary

A question about opening an account at a conventional bank for personal use, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that if he has a choice, a Muslim may not open an account at an interest-based bank, even without interest, because it aids usury, but that it is permitted in pressing need when an Islamic bank is not possible. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4282}

## The question

The questioner asked about opening an account at a commercial bank to deposit money and use it for personal matters such as transfers and electronic payments. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4282}

## The answer

The Department answered that if a Muslim has a choice, he may not open an account at interest-based banks even if it pays no interest, because doing so aids usury (riba), citing the verse commanding cooperation in righteousness and forbidding cooperation in sin and aggression in [[passage/quran-5-2|Surat al-Ma'ida 5:2]]. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4282} It explained that these banks invest money in forbidden interest-based dealings, so depositing with them aids what is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4282} It said that if there is a pressing need to open an account at an interest-based bank and it cannot be opened at an Islamic bank, there is no objection, and it referred to its fatwa no. 471 on bank interest and prizes. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4282}
