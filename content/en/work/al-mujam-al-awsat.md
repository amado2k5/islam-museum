## Overview
Al-Tabarani's middle Mu'jam, one of his three; Ibn Nasir al-Din reports al-Tabarani used to say of it: "it is my spirit, for I toiled on it" {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 30}.

## In «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'» by Abu Ishaq al-Heweny
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says he loves books in which the authors criticise the hadiths they collect, such as al-Bazzar's Musnad, al-Tabarani's «al-Awsat» and Ibn Adi's «al-Kamil», and reads them for pleasure, noting their benefits. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep27 00:20:00} He says al-Tabarani is the scholar he criticises most in his «Tanbih al-Hajid», so he gathered those criticisms, added to them, and made a book whose title ends «...on the errors that occurred in al-Tabarani's al-Awsat». {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:23:30} He says the texts in «al-Awsat» are mostly sound and the difficulty lies in the chains, since al-Tabarani compiled it for the chains; so when a well-known hadith comes with a munkar chain, one may not write under it that al-Bukhari and Muslim narrated it, but rather "munkar with this chain", with the reason. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:26:00} He says that if he stood before al-Tabarani he would wash his feet, but no one is free of error; he has withdrawn many of his criticisms in the new edition, and the book is ready for print. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep28 00:26:30}

## Subject and method in the Dar al-Haramayn edition

The editors of the Dar al-Haramayn edition hold that the subject of al-Awsat is gathering rare and noteworthy hadiths and stating their rarity and where the sole transmission or the divergence lies, which makes it in their view a key source for the hidden defects of hadith; they add that it lay buried in libraries so long that it was thought lost. {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.23} They quote Yaqut that al-Tabarani wrote [[work/al-mujam-al-kabir|al-Kabir]] on the names of the Companions, al-Awsat on the rare reports of his teachers and [[work/al-mujam-al-saghir|al-Saghir]] on the names of his teachers; al-Dhahabi, that it follows his list of teachers, giving for each the rare and remarkable reports he had from him; and Yahya ibn Manda, that it was in twenty-four parts. {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.83} {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.84} They note that al-Tabarani sometimes adduces a hadith from someone other than the teacher of the entry in order to expose a defect in the rare hadith he gave for that teacher; they leave such reports unnumbered and treat them as his own commentary. {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.12} They used al-Haythami's Majma' al-Bahrayn as if it were a further manuscript for the hadiths it contains. {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.11}

## Manuscripts and the critique of an earlier edition

They relied on two manuscripts: the base, in two volumes (309 and 310 folios) from Turkey, whose first leaf is lost from each volume, and whose owner they identified from his seal as Husam al-Din Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Kutahi al-Rumi al-Hanafi, known as Qara Chelebi Zade (d. 1007 AH), who had a library in Bursa; and a Köprülü manuscript. {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.84} {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.85} They criticise Dr Mahmud al-Tahhan's edition of the first volumes, finding in it many misreadings, distortions and omissions, and object to his use of Majma' al-Zawa'id to correct the text, since al-Haythami drops the chains altogether; this is their assessment. {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.29} {cite:mujam-awsat-haramayn-1415-v1 p.30}

## Al-Tabarani's silence on defective reports: a modern study

An article in Tahdhib al-Afkar by Fazl al-Rahman and Abd al-Hamid Kharrub of the International Islamic University, Islamabad, examines hadiths in al-Awsat on which al-Tabarani is silent, and concludes that he had no special method of silence on defective hadiths, that most of what he passed over in silence is weak, abandoned or objectionable, and that he is silent on hadiths with absolute sole transmission or with weak and unknown narrators. {cite:tahdhib-al-afkar-tabarani-sukut p.1} {cite:tahdhib-al-afkar-tabarani-sukut p.9}
