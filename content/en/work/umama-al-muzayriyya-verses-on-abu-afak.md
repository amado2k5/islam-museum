## Overview
Two verses that Ibn Ishaq gives after [[event/killing-of-abu-afak|the killing of Abu Afak]], in the words of [[person/umama-al-muzayriyya|Umama al-Muzayriyya]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/285} In them she addresses [[person/abu-afak|Abu Afak]]: he denies the religion of God and the man who is most praised; she swears by the one who bore him that what he bore was evil; and then she says that Hanif, who is the Muslim as the editors explain, struck him at the end of the night with a spear, so let him take it despite his old age. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/285} The sense is given here; the text is read in the edition.

## In the museum
It is rare in the biography for the consequence of a killing to be told in the words of a woman poet. The two verses carry the attitude that the account is meant to be read with: the spear is the price of denial. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/285}
