## Summary

*Al-Mawaqif wa-l-Mukhatabat* ("The Stations and the Addresses") is a Sufi book by [[person/al-niffari|Muhammad ibn Abd al-Jabbar al-Niffari]] {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}. The description here comes from Mostafa Mahmoud's [[work/raaytu-allah|Ra'aytu Allah]], which quotes and explains passages from it.

## The nature of the book

According to Mostafa Mahmoud, the book is "a collection of scraps" that al-Niffari left at his death and his followers gathered, containing "a number of lofty religious insights", speaking of the spirit, the body and the self, and explaining tawhid, Islam and the Qur'an {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}. He holds that it is a book "for the elite of the elite" who love contemplation, not for general readers who read for passing pleasure {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 21}.

## The form

Its passages run in forms such as "my Lord said to me", "my Lord stayed me before Him and said" and "my Lord addressed me", hence its name: the Stations (mawaqif) and the Addresses (mukhatabat) {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}. Mostafa Mahmoud opens his selections with a chapter on tawhid cast in the form "O servant" {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 22}.

:::perspectives
- **Mostafa Mahmoud's view.** These forms are the Sufis' language for what is cast into their hearts "in moments of complete clarity": instead of saying "this thought flashed into my mind", one of them says "my Lord said to me", "believing that the source and inspirer of truth is God alone"; it is not a claim to prophethood {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}.

- **Academic view.** Not yet documented.
:::

## For young visitors

This is a book of short sayings written by a Sufi, which his students gathered into one book after he died {cite:mm-raaytu-allah-maarif-1976 p. 20}.
