## Summary

A question from an organ-donation awareness initiative about a person bequeathing all his organs for donation after death, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that donating organs after death to relieve harm to others is permitted by the sharia, counting it as cooperation in righteousness. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3895}

## The question

An initiative to spread awareness of organ donation asked for the ruling on a person making a will to donate all his organs after death. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3895}

## The answer

The Department answered that God commanded cooperation in righteousness and piety, quoting [[person/al-mawardi|al-Mawardi]]'s Adab al-Dunya wa-l-Din that joining piety with righteousness brings together God's pleasure and people's. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3895} It said that a person's donating one of his organs at death to relieve harm to another is permitted by the sharia, falling under two agreed maxims: necessity makes the prohibited permissible, and necessity is measured by its extent. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3895} It cited hadiths on relieving people's distress, said that a patient's need for an organ, or a blind person's need for sight, is greater than a poor person's need for money, and said that whoever bequeaths a donation to serve needy patients ends his life with a noble deed. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3895} It noted that the Department had earlier issued fatwas permitting organ donation, including one on donating the cornea, and praised Jordanian doctors' success in transplanting corneas, skin and other organs. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3895}
