## Summary
*al-Tamhid li-ma fi al-Muwatta' min al-Ma'ani wa-al-Asanid* ("The Preparation: on the meanings and chains of transmission in the Muwatta'") is the book of [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Abu 'Umar Ibn 'Abd al-Barr]] of Cordoba (368–463 AH) on the hadiths of [[work/muwatta-malik|Malik's Muwatta']]. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 1} In it he gathered every report from the Prophet found in the Muwatta' in the recension of [[person/yahya-ibn-yahya-al-laythi|Yahya b. Yahya al-Laythi]] — connected, broken and mursal alike — and arranged them under the names of [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik's]] teachers in alphabetical order. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 199} Ibn Bashkuwal says no one had preceded him to anything like it, and that it runs to seventy parts. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 17-18} {cite:wafayat-ayan p. 67}

## Why he wrote it
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr opens with an observation: everyone before him who had extracted the hadiths of the Muwatta' aimed at the musnad (fully connected) reports and turned away from the broken and the mursal, and then failed to keep his own condition, slipping some broken reports in among the connected. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 191} He says that the Malikis, when asked about the mursal reports of the Muwatta', call them sound because of the trustworthiness of those who transmit them — and then contradict themselves by leaving them aside; for him, the root of Malik's position is that the mursal of a trustworthy transmitter is binding proof just as the musnad is. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 191-192} So he set out to bring everything together in one book, so that the reader could see where the reports of the Muwatta' stand in fame and soundness. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 199-200}

## The method, in the author's words
- **Order:** within each teacher he ranked the reports — the connected first, then those whose connection is disputed, then the broken and the mursal — and set the teachers in alphabetical order "so that it is easier to reach". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 199}
- **Connecting:** he connected every broken report that comes connected outside Malik's transmission, and every mursal that comes with a full chain by another route, as far as his knowledge and his own transmission reached. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 199-200}
- **Fiqh:** he set out the meanings and rulings of the reports that the jurists rely on, the scholars' views on their interpretation, abrogating and abrogated, supporting reports from his memory, and explanations of rare words limited to the lexicographers' statements. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 200}
- **Narrators:** he pointed to the narrators' circumstances, lineages, ages, standing and dates of death, aiming at brevity. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 200}
- **Recension:** he relied on Yahya b. Yahya's recension alone, because of Yahya's standing among the people of al-Andalus and their long use of his recension, inherited from their teachers — unless a key legal hadith had dropped out of it, in which case he cites it from another recension. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 201} He transmits the Muwatta' from his teacher Abu 'Uthman Sa'id b. Nasr, from [[person/qasim-ibn-asbagh|Qasim b. Asbagh]] and Wahb b. Masarra, from [[person/muhammad-ibn-waddah|Muhammad b. Waddah]], from Yahya; he has other routes too, among them 'Ubayd Allah b. Yahya from his father, and he recorded the letter-level differences between 'Ubayd Allah's and Ibn Waddah's transmissions. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 201-202}

The editor of the al-Furqan edition notes that al-Laythi's recension was not well known in the East — none of the compilers of the great hadith collections relied on it — and explains this by Yahya's limited reputation as a hadith student, the not inconsiderable errors in his recension, and the availability of more precise recensions such as those of al-Qa'nabi, al-Tinnisi and Abu Mus'ab. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 201}

## The author's introduction: the mursal and the single report
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr prefaces the book with chapters on the principles of transmission. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 191-257}
- On the [[concept/khabar-al-ahad|single report]] he reports the agreement of the people of fiqh and athar in all regions on accepting the report of a single upright transmitter and acting on it unless another report or consensus abrogates it, excepting the Kharijites and some groups of innovators. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 192} His own choice is that it obliges action but not certain knowledge, like the testimony of two witnesses; he attributes to al-Husayn al-Karabisi and others the view that it yields outward knowledge and action together. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 199}
- On the [[concept/irsal-fiqh|mursal]] he sets out the Maliki positions: some hold the mursal of trustworthy men to be stronger than musnad reports, some hold the two equal, and some give the musnad a merit of excellence while still requiring action on the mursal; then the view of al-Shafi'i and the hadith scholars that a break in the chain is a defect that removes the obligation to act. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 194-197}
- Then comes the "Chapter on knowing the mursal, the musnad, the broken, the connected, the mawquf and the meaning of [[concept/tadlis|tadlis]]", and a chapter on whose transmission, mursal reports and tadlis are accepted and whose are not. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 203} {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 217}

## The chapters of the teachers
After the introduction comes the "Chapter alif: the names of Malik's teachers from whom he narrated the Prophet's hadith". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 258} Under each teacher there is a short biography, then the number of his reports in the Muwatta' and their status, then the reports one by one under headings such as "First hadith of Isma'il b. Abi Hakim, musnad". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 320-321} Volume 1 of the al-Furqan edition thus holds:
- [[person/ibrahim-ibn-uqba|Ibrahim b. 'Uqba]], with one report in the Muwatta', mursal in most recensions, on the pilgrimage of a child. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 258-259}
- [[person/ismail-ibn-abi-hakim|Isma'il b. Abi Hakim]]: four reports, one musnad and three mursal. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 320}
- [[person/ishaq-ibn-abd-allah-ibn-abi-talha|Ishaq b. 'Abd Allah b. Abi Talha]]: fifteen reports, ten of them from Anas. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 401}
- [[person/ayyub-al-sakhtiyani|Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani]]: two musnad reports in Yahya's recension, and two more on the pilgrimage in the other recensions. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 600}
- [[person/ayyub-ibn-habib-al-jumahi|Ayyub b. Habib]], client of Sa'd b. Abi Waqqas: one musnad report forbidding blowing into a drink. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 667}

The Moroccan edition describes the whole sequence: it begins with those named Ibrahim, then Isma'il and Ishaq, then Ayyub, and ends with Yahya, Yunus and Ya'qub, then those known by kunya, then the balaghat (reports introduced "it reached me"). {cite:tamhid-awqaf-maghrib-1967 p. 5}

## What it is limited to
The Tamhid confines itself to the Prophet's hadith in the Muwatta' — connected, broken, mawquf or mursal — and leaves aside its legal opinions and the reports of Companions and later authorities, which the author treated in a separate book, [[work/al-istidkar|al-Istidhkar li-Madhahib 'Ulama' al-Amsar]]. {cite:tamhid-awqaf-maghrib-1967 p. 5-6} Ibn Hazm calls al-Istidhkar "the abridgement of the Tamhid". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16}

## Thirty years and two recensions
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr spent more than thirty years on the Tamhid; the Moroccan edition takes this from two lines of his verse in which he calls the book "the companion of my heart for thirty years". {cite:tamhid-awqaf-maghrib-1967 p. 6} The editor of the al-Furqan edition, Bashar 'Awwad Ma'ruf, holds that the author first wrote a draft from which most of the copies found in the Maghrib, Syria and Iraq were made, then produced a fair copy with very many additions and some deletions. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 41} In his view the copy in the Köprülü library in Istanbul, of which eight of eleven volumes survive, represents the final recension. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 19}

One of his examples of the difference: the author had written a long biography of Malik at the head of the book, then moved it to his *al-Intiqa' fi Fadl al-Thalatha al-Fuqaha'* and removed it from the Tamhid, as a marginal note in the base manuscript records. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 42} The al-Furqan edition keeps that chapter as an appendix at the end of volume 1 for its usefulness, noting that it is not part of the book in its final recension. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 677}

## What scholars said of it
The al-Furqan editor collects the scholars' judgements on the book. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 15}
- [[person/ibn-hazm|Ibn Hazm]], the author's companion and friend, in his epistle on the merits of al-Andalus: "a book whose like on the fiqh of hadith I do not know at all — so how could there be a better one?" {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 16}
- [[person/al-qadi-iyyad|Qadi 'Iyad]]: it is twenty volumes, and "a book the like of which no one has made in its manner". {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 17}
- [[person/ibn-bashkuwal-khalaf|Ibn Bashkuwal]]: he arranged it by the names of Malik's teachers alphabetically; no one preceded him to its like; it is seventy parts. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 17-18}

In his foreword to the al-Furqan edition, Ahmad Zaki Yamani calls it one of the greatest and best-known commentaries on the Muwatta' and a foundational book of comparative fiqh. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 5}

## Editions in the library
- **The Moroccan edition:** issued in volumes by the Ministry of Awqaf and Islamic Affairs of Morocco; volume 1 was edited by Mustafa b. Ahmad al-'Alawi and Muhammad 'Abd al-Kabir al-Bakri in 1387 AH/1967 CE. {cite:tamhid-awqaf-maghrib-1967 p. 1} Its editors worked from a microfilm of nine parts of a copy in Maghribi script in an Istanbul library, two parts missing, the first opening with the introduction and the last ending with the words "we have reached what we intended"; they filled the gaps from scattered parts in the Royal Library and the General Library in Rabat. {cite:tamhid-awqaf-maghrib-1967 p. 6-7}
- **The al-Furqan edition:** edited by Bashar 'Awwad Ma'ruf with Muhammad Bashar 'Awwad, published by the al-Furqan Islamic Heritage Foundation, London, first edition 1439 AH/2017 CE. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 1} {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 4} Its foreword says the editor spent more than twenty years on it and made indexes of hadiths and reports, a legal index that rearranges the book by the chapters of the Muwatta', and indexes of the author's teachers and of places. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 7-8}
