## Summary
The notes of the edited Itqan identify the Kashf as al-Kashf 'an Wujuh al-Qira'at al-Sab' wa-'Ilalihā, in which Makki explains his own al-Tabsira, and say it is printed. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 536} The bibliography lists it as al-Kashf 'an Wujuh al-Qira'at al-Sab' wa-'Ilalihā wa-Hujajihā by Abu Muhammad Makki ibn Abi Talib al-Qaysi, edited by Dr Muhyi al-Din Ramadan, Mu'assasat al-Risala, Beirut, fourth printing, 1407 AH. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 3008}

## Explaining the readings
Al-Suyuti says it is important to know the grounds of the readings, that the imams cared for this and wrote separate books on it, among them al-Hujja of Abu 'Ali al-Farisi, al-Kashf of Makki, al-Hidaya of al-Mahdawi and al-Muhtasab of Ibn Jinni on the anomalous readings. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 536} He gives al-Kawashi's account of its use, with his warning against preferring one reading over another so strongly that it nearly cancels it, since each is mutawatir. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 536} See [[work/al-hujja-lil-qurra-al-saba|al-Hujja]] and [[work/al-muhtasab-fi-tabyin-wujuh-shawadhdh-al-qiraat|al-Muhtasab]].

## What may be recited
In the introduction to al-Nashr Ibn al-Jazari quotes Makki, 'in his treatise which he attached to the book of al-Kashf', answering which of what is reported of the Qur'an is accepted and recited. Makki divides the reports into three: those with a transmission from reliable narrators from the Prophet, a sound form in Arabic and agreement with the script of the mushaf, which are recited and certain; those with a sound individual transmission and a sound form in Arabic that disagree with the mushaf's script, which are accepted but not recited, because they were not taken by consensus and because they contradict what was agreed; and those transmitted by an unreliable narrator, or by a reliable one but with no ground in Arabic, which are not accepted even if they match the script. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 23} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 24} This is a root of what became Ibn al-Jazari's own criterion of a valid reading; see [[work/al-nashr-fi-al-qiraat-al-ashr|al-Nashr]].
