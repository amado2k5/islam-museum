## Overview
This is the book on the hadith sciences by Abu Amr Uthman ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Salah al-Shahrazuri (577–643 AH). In his preface he writes that the study of hadith had once been great. Its people had since become a small band who, in receiving it, cared for little more than hearing it, and in recording it for little more than writing it down. So he wrote *Ma'rifat Anwa' Ilm al-Hadith* to clear up its difficulties and gather its scattered sciences. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 5} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 6}

## The sixty-five types
He opens with an index of the types:
- first the knowledge of the sound (sahih), then the good (hasan), then the weak;
- then the supported, the connected, the raised, the stopped, the cut-off, the hurried (mursal), the broken and the problematic chains (mu'dal), concealment (tadlis), the anomalous and the denounced;
- then the qualities of those whose transmission is accepted or rejected, how hadith is heard and the kinds of licence (ijaza), and the writing and checking of hadith;
- then the knowledge of transmitters.

The sixty-fifth and last is the homelands and towns of transmitters. That, he says, is not the last possible type, for the subject can be divided without limit, since the states of transmitters and of texts are beyond counting. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 7} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 8}

## The definition of the sound hadith
He defines the sound hadith as a supported hadith whose chain is connected, through upright and precise transmitters on the authority of upright and precise transmitters, to its end, and which is neither anomalous nor defective. These qualities, he says, exclude:
- the hurried, broken and problematic chains;
- the anomalous;
- reports with a damaging hidden defect;
- reports whose transmitter has some flaw.
{cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 11} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 12}

The editor notes that others, such as al-Nawawi, preferred to drop the word "supported" (musnad) from the definition. The judgement of soundness is applied both to reports traced to the Prophet and to others. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 11}

On the good hadith he quotes two definitions:
- al-Khattabi: a report whose origin is known and whose men are well known;
- al-Tirmidhi: no one in the chain is accused of lying, it is not anomalous, and it is transmitted by more than one route.

He then says: all this is unclear and does not satisfy, and nothing in what al-Tirmidhi and al-Khattabi say separates the good from the sound. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 29} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 30}

## Positions he is known for
- **Independent authentication.** He held that in his age independent recognition of the sound by examining chains had become impracticable. The matter had come to rest on what the imams stated in their recognised works. Others disagreed (see [[question/tashih-al-hadith-fi-al-usur-al-mutaakhkhira|the question of authentication by later scholars]]). {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 16} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17}
- **The two Sahihs.** He divided the sound into seven classes, the highest being what al-Bukhari and Muslim agree on, and held that this class is certainly sound. Here too he was opposed (see [[question/ifadat-ma-ittafaqa-alayhi-al-shaykhan-al-qat|the question of certainty for the agreed hadith]]). {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 27} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 28}
- **The Companions.** He states that the hadith scholars' known practice counts every Muslim who saw the Prophet as a Companion. He adds that the Companions as a whole share one distinction: no one's uprightness among them is questioned (see [[concept/adalat-al-sahaba|the uprightness of the Companions]]). {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 293} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 294}

## The close
He ends the sixty-fifth type with hadith he transmits through his own chains, following the example of al-Hakim Abu Abd Allah. One of them he read at Nishapur to Abu al-Hasan al-Mu'ayyad ibn Muhammad ibn Ali al-Muqri, once at the head of the grave of Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj. For each chain he names the towns of its transmitters: men of Basra, Baghdad and Nishapur. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 405} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 406} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 407}

## The edition used
The edition used is Nur al-Din Itr's, with his commentary in the footnotes, collated with manuscripts marked 'آ', 'ع' and 'ق'. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 3} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 5}
