## Summary

The speaker follows the months between Badr and Uhud: the expedition to Banu Sulaym, the raid of al-Sawiq, the confrontation with Banu Qaynuqa, and the Medinan routine of the Prophet. He then narrates the battle of Uhud in the third year of the Hijra from Quraysh's preparations to Abu Sufyan's departure, with the terrain, the archers, the reversal, the defence of the Prophet and the burials. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### After Badr: the death of Ruqayya and the expedition to Banu Sulaym

- He recalls that the previous episode ended with the return from Badr and the mission of al-Raji, in which Khubayb ibn Adi was killed as revenge for Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the Prophet came back from Badr to find that his daughter Ruqayya, the wife of Uthman, had died, so two heavy events fell together, one of victory and one of family grief. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that about seven days later, according to the biographers, the Prophet marched against Banu Sulaym after hearing of their movements, and he stresses how closely the Prophet followed enemy movements. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He places the site east of Medina, roughly eighty to a hundred and twenty kilometres away, at a place called Qarqarat al-Kudr; he says that the tribe scattered into the hills, nobody was met, and the Prophet returned in Shawwal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### Abu Sufyan's raid and the expedition of al-Sawiq

- He says that about two months after Badr Abu Sufyan, who had sworn to take revenge, entered the outskirts of Medina with a small detachment, coordinating with a Jewish host. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the raiders struck a farm east of the city, killed an Ansari man and another with him, burned some palm trees and fled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the Prophet marched out with an army at once and could not catch them, but took the large amount of roasted barley flour that the fleeing men had thrown away to travel light, which is why the episode is called al-Sawiq. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He reads the raid as a bid by Abu Sufyan to restore Quraysh's standing after Badr rather than a real battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The Prophet's full life in Medina between campaigns

- He holds that the Prophet's life had no idle stretches: the Qur'an was being revealed, and he recited, taught and trained his companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He lists the social roles he says Medina gave the Prophet: attending weddings, taking newborns to place chewed dates in their mouths, visiting the sick, mourning, and reconciling quarrelling groups, and he cites a report in al-Bukhari of reconciling Banu Amr ibn Awf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Arabs around Medina came to ask whether he was a messenger and what he brought, and that at the same time he had to track enemies through informants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that at home the Prophet appeared devoted to worship, and he cites a report in al-Bukhari from his wife, which he thinks is Hafsa, about long night prayer, including prayer seated in his later years before standing for the last portion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### Banu Qaynuqa and the question of Banu al-Nadir

- He describes three Jewish groups living south of Medina, Banu Qaynuqa, Banu al-Nadir and Banu Qurayza, and says Banu Qaynuqa were the nearest and the first with whom the Prophet came into military contact. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that biographers differ on the cause: a boast that Quraysh had met an inexperienced force, an incident in their market in which a woman was humiliated and a man was killed in the dispute, and an attempt by one of them to stir strife between the Aws and Khazraj. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He adds that some scholars see these acts as grounds for ending the treaty, linking it to a Qur'anic instruction revealed after Badr about giving notice when treachery is feared. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the Prophet besieged them and wanted the fighters put to death, but Abdullah ibn Ubayy, their ally, held on to the Prophet's armour and pleaded until the Prophet granted them to him, so they became the first group expelled from Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Ubada ibn al-Samit, also their ally, declared that his pact with them was void. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that he postpones Banu al-Nadir, the subject of Surat al-Hashr, because scholars dispute whether it came before or after Uhud: Ibn Ishaq places it after, al-Bukhari before, and he notes a verse in that sura that some exegetes including Ibn Kathir applied to Banu Qaynuqa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The year between Badr and Uhud, and Uhud in the Qur'an

- He says that a year passed between the two battles and recalls that fasting and zakat were made obligatory in the second year, that Ali married Fatima, and that Ruqayya died. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He holds that events given detailed treatment in the Qur'an are marked as important: Surat al-Anfal for Badr, verses of al-Tawba for Tabuk, al-Hashr for Banu al-Nadir, and many verses of Al Imran for Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He notes that he already devoted about six sessions, around five hours, to those Uhud verses in an earlier series on how the Qur'an treated the souls of reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### Quraysh's preparations for revenge

- He says that after Badr Quraysh feared for its caravan trade, which he calls its economy, and tried an inland route guided by a hired expert, but the Prophet learned of it, reportedly through a drunken Qurayshi's talk overheard by a sympathiser. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the Prophet sent Zayd ibn Haritha, who captured the caravan while its men escaped. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Quraysh then judged that its leaders were dead and its route closed, so the choice was submission or a decisive war, and it chose war. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says they mobilised heavily, drew in allies from Tihama and elsewhere, and brought their women along to deepen the fighters' resolve. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The enemy camp, the council and the march out

- He says the Quraysh army of three thousand camped near Medina facing Mount Uhud, while patrols in Medina watched the entrances. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the Prophet consulted his companions on staying in the city or going out; he describes the advantage of street fighting against a large mass and says this was the Prophet's own leaning, and also that of Abdullah ibn Ubayy for a different reason. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that the elders favoured staying while the young men who missed Badr, with Hamza, pressed to go out, and the Prophet agreed and put on his armour. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that when some then regretted pressing him, the Prophet answered that a prophet who has put on his war gear does not take it off until God judges between him and the enemy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that the army spent the first night at al-Shaykhayn, about halfway to Uhud, where the Prophet sent back boys he judged too young, among them Ibn Umar, who had also been sent back at Badr and fought at the Trench. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### Terrain, the archers and Khalid's tactic

- He says that the Prophet camped between Mount Uhud and the hill of the archers, less than a kilometre apart, with his back to Uhud and the Quraysh camp nearer to Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He explains that the terrain was meant to shrink the enemy's frontage and force a head-on fight, covering the right and rear with the mountain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the Prophet posted fifty archers on the open side and told them plainly not to leave their place, even if they saw the Muslims winning, to block any encirclement from behind the hill. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Khalid ibn al-Walid, who commanded two hundred horsemen with Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl, was known for outflanking, and used the same tactic at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says the banner was given to Mus'ab ibn Umayr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He adds that the Quraysh mood differed from Badr: pride and boasting then, a drive for revenge now. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### Ibn Ubayy's withdrawal and the two wavering clans

- He says that Abdullah ibn Ubayy withdrew with about a third of the army, claiming his advice had not been followed and that he did not expect real fighting, and he links this to verses of Al Imran about those who stayed behind. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He argues that such a withdrawal also shakes those who remain, who begin to recalculate. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that two groups of believers were close to faltering, which he identifies as Banu Salima and Banu Haritha, and that Jabir ibn Abdullah, who belonged to one of them, said he was glad the verse came down because it ends by saying God was their protector. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The opening duel, the banner bearers and the first phase

- He says that the battle began as at Badr with single combat, and that Hamza killed a Qurayshi challenger named Siba, a story he places in al-Bukhari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan blamed the standard-bearers of Banu Abd al-Dar for the defeat at Badr, and they died one after another holding the banner, which he reads as proof of their resolve. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He describes a fierce close fight of seven hundred Muslims against three thousand, and a clear Muslim advantage at first, and he cites the verse of Al Imran that says God fulfilled His promise while they were killing the enemy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates from al-Bara that the Quraysh women, among them Hind bint Utba, fled up the mountain so fast that their anklets showed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The killing of Hamza by Wahshi

- He says that Hamza had a central role in the fighting and that Wahshi describes him as striking down all before him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that Wahshi, a slave, had been promised freedom by the family of a man Hamza killed at Badr if he killed Hamza, and that he did not fight but hid behind a rock watching. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Wahshi threw his spear when Hamza was off guard, wounding him in the abdomen, and Hamza tried to rush him but fell as a martyr, in the area on the eastern side of the archers' hill. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The archers leave their post and Khalid strikes from behind

- He says that the Quraysh camp began to fall back and its goods lay open, so that some Muslims turned to collecting spoils. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that some of the archers on the hill said the victory was settled and came down for the spoils, which went against the Prophet's explicit order. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Khalid ibn al-Walid saw the hill nearly empty, charged with his cavalry, killed Abdullah ibn Jubayr and those with him, and came down on the Muslims from behind. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that a Qurayshi picked up the fallen banner, the scattered fighters rallied around it, and the Muslims were caught between two forces. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### Confusion in the ranks and Anas ibn al-Nadr

- He relates from a report in al-Bukhari that Satan cried out an audible warning to the Muslims about their rear, which he says deepened the confusion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that in the confusion Muslims killed al-Yaman, the father of Hudhayfa, though Hudhayfa called out that it was his father. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that some of the best companions fled, some towards Medina, while others fought on, and a rumour spread that the Prophet had been killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates the story in al-Bukhari of Anas ibn al-Nadr, who had vowed after missing Badr to fight hard if given the chance, apologised to God for the flight of the others, spoke with Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, charged into the enemy and was so mutilated that only his sister knew him, by his fingertips. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The Prophet's steadiness and the withdrawal to the glen

- He holds that severe trials can make people lose sight of their calling, but that the Prophet never did, and compares Hunayn, where the Prophet called out his name and lineage, with Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that the Prophet called the Muslims to himself at some risk, as the verse of Al Imran about the Messenger calling them from behind describes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He argues that the Prophet also judged that the field was no longer fit and withdrew towards Mount Uhud so that the mountain covered three sides, which he calls the most dangerous moment of the battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that during this withdrawal the Prophet fell into a pit dug by a hypocrite and took blows that broke his helmet, broke a tooth and wounded his face, with very few men around him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The defenders of the Prophet, Uthman's flight and the women's support

- He says that once the Prophet reached the glen the Quraysh concentrated their attack on that point, and names Talha ibn Ubaydullah and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas among the most prominent defenders. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates a report in al-Bukhari from Abu Uthman al-Nahdi that in one phase only Sa'd and Talha remained, and another from Qays ibn Abi Hazim that Talha's hand, which shielded the Prophet, was crippled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that Abu Talha al-Ansari, a strong archer, broke two or three bows, and tried to seal the open side while warning the Prophet not to expose himself to arrows. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that a group from the Kharijites asked Ibn Umar about Uthman's flight at Uhud, his absence at Badr and at the Pledge of al-Ridwan; he says Ibn Umar replied that God had forgiven the flight, that Uthman was nursing the Prophet's daughter at Badr and was given a share, and that the Pledge was taken on his account. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that women took part in support roles: according to al-Bukhari Aisha and Asma bint Abi Bakr carried water skins, women treated the wounded, and Umar later gave a garment to Umm Sulayt, who had carried water at Uhud, rather than to Umm Kulthum bint Ali, his wife. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### Abu Sufyan's call and the departure of Quraysh

- He says the Companions gradually regrouped around the Prophet, and relates that at one point two Qurayshi Muslims and seven Ansar were with him, and the Ansar fell one after another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that after Abu Bakr, Umar and others rejoined him, the Quraysh could no longer reach the Prophet, and Abu Sufyan, judging by military balance that he had won, wanted to stop there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that Abu Sufyan called out from the mountain asking whether Muhammad, Abu Bakr and Umar were among them, and the Prophet told his men not to answer; he argues that Abu Sufyan chose those two because of their leadership weight. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that Umar then answered Abu Sufyan's claim that they were dead, and that Abu Sufyan called out the name of Hubal, whereupon the Prophet told his men to reply that God is higher, and the exchange ended with Abu Sufyan claiming not to have ordered the mutilation of the dead. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Abu Sufyan then led the army back to Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}

### The aftermath: the dream, Mus'ab, the burials and the farewell visit

- He relates that the Prophet had seen a dream before the battle and interpreted its parts as slain companions, a notch in his sword as a loss among his own family, and the strong armour as Medina; he says the family loss was Hamza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that Mus'ab ibn Umayr, an early convert, was found with only a short cloak too small to cover him, and the Prophet ordered his head covered and grass laid on his feet; he adds that Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Khabbab recalled him with tears in later years according to al-Bukhari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He names Abdullah ibn Jahsh, who was mutilated, and the father of Jabir among those killed, and says that graves ran short so several men were buried together, with the Prophet putting forward the one who knew more Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He relates that a woman who was told of the deaths of her kin asked only about the Prophet and, on hearing he was well, said every other loss was small. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He says that shortly before his death the Prophet went to Uhud and prayed over its martyrs, which Uqba ibn Amir compared to a farewell to the living and the dead, and cites a short report in the Musnad from Jabir in which the Prophet wishes he had stayed with those at the foot of the mountain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
- He mentions in passing, without telling them, the scene of Abu Dujana with the sword and the story of Amr ibn al-Jamuh, who was lame, accepted Islam late and was killed there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep20}
