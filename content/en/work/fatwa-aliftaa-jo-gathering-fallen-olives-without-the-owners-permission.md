## Summary

A question about gathering fallen olives without the owner's permission, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that in principle one may not take another's fruit without permission, but gleaning what is left after harvest is tolerated on four conditions: it is after the owners have harvested, the land is not fenced or near a house, the owner's consent can be presumed, and the trees are not damaged. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3689}

## The question

The questioner asked whether he may gather olives that have fallen on the ground without the landowner's permission, given that the land is not fenced. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3689}

## The answer

The Department answered that in principle one may not eat from fruit trees planted at homes or in orchards without the owner's permission, citing a hadith narrated by [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] in al-Musnad whose meaning is that a person's property is not lawful to others except with his willing consent. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3689} It said that leftovers after the fruit harvest may be tolerated on four conditions: the gleaner takes what remains only after the owners have harvested, not before; the land is not fenced or close to the precincts of a house, in which case permission must be sought; the owner's forgiveness is likely, for example where custom is known to tolerate gleaning; and the gleaner takes care of the trees and does not damage or break them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3689} It quoted [[work/mughni-al-muhtaj|Mughni al-Muhtaj]] (3/592) that gleaning ears of grain and the like at harvest time is permitted if one presumes the owner has abandoned them or is content for them to be taken, whether or not the gleaner is eligible for zakat, although [[person/badr-al-din-al-zarkashi|al-Zarkashi]] differed on the latter point, since this small amount is overlooked, as the early and later generations practised. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3689}
