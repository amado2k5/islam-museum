## Summary
Omar Abdelkafy's series "Muktatafat min al-Sira": Monday lessons presenting excerpts from the life of the Prophet Muhammad — from the state of the Arabs before Islam to the hijra, the founding of the state and the conquests — and drawing lessons from them for Muslims. The first episode introduces the series, poses the question "why was Arabia chosen as the home of the message?" and surveys pre-Islamic Arab character. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep01 00:07:00}

## Episode one: an introduction to the sira and the rise of the Arabs
- He opens his Monday lesson after an absence of about four years from this appointment and announces a new series, "Muktatafat min al-Sira", in which he presents the life of the Prophet Muhammad: the state of the Arabs before Islam, his mission, the hijra to Medina and the founding of the Muslim state, and the conquests — then draws lessons for Muslims, among them raising the youth, Salah al-Din al-Ayyubi as military leader and jurist, and the character of the Companions and the Prophet's wives. - He pauses on the question "why was the Arabian peninsula chosen as the home of the message?" and surveys the surrounding nations — Persians and Romans — and the pre-Islamic Arab character connected to generosity: a generosity that surpassed the wind's speed, courage, and jealousy over honour to the point that they used to bury their daughters alive for fear they would be looked at, and marriage to close kin — a daughter, a paternal aunt, a sister — was not forbidden among them. - In this first episode he alludes to the episode of Ta'if: the severity of the trial upon the Prophet, Jibril's offer to turn one of the two mountains upon him to gold, and his prayer "my Lord, guide my people for they do not know"; and he mentions that Abu Talib and Khadija died while the Prophet's companions were left to torture and coercion. ## Episode two: the world into which the Prophet was born
- The second episode surveys the world into which the Prophet was born: the state of Quraysh, Mecca and the Kaaba; the role of Abd al-Muttalib in raising [[person/abdullah-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|Abdullah]], the Prophet's father; the year of the elephant in which Abraha came to demolish the Kaaba and failed; the death of Abdullah before his son's birth and the vows of his mother Amina; and the life of his grandfather, one of Quraysh's chiefs. (needs_source: the transcript of this section is severely garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:29:30}
- He mentions [[person/bahira|Bahira]] the monk who saw the sign of prophethood in the boy when he accompanied him to Syria, and his conversation with his uncle Abu Talib; and the story of [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman al-Farisi]] who asked about the true religion among Magianism and Christianity until he reached a prophet to be sent — finding in the Arabs' character something that reassured him. (needs_source: details of these stories are unclear in the transcript) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy muktatafat-mena-al-seera/ep02 00:27:30}
- He surveys the names of the pre-Islamic Arab months and the sanctity of the sacred months, then returns to the first episode's question: why were the Arabs chosen? He sees them as a people of trustworthiness who did not lie or betray and kept covenants; the prophets before him — Musa and Isa — came to eastern peoples, while the Arabs were nearest to [[concept/sirat|the Prophetic biography]] because they carried character that qualified them for the message: generosity, trustworthiness and good neighbourliness.
