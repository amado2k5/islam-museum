## Summary

A question about compensating a patient for a doctor's mistake, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that a doctor is liable for harm caused by his negligence or ignorance, and not liable if he took full care and unavoidable complications occurred. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4301}

## The question

The questioner asked about compensating a patient when a doctor makes a mistake in treatment. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4301}

## The answer

The Department answered that, in principle, any mistake by a doctor that harms another through negligence or lack of knowledge makes him liable to compensate the person harmed, citing the maxim that the direct agent is liable, and quoting [[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|Ibn Qudama]] in [[work/al-mughni|al-Mughni]] that a skilled doctor whose hand errs, such as cutting beyond or outside the proper place, is liable. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4301} It said that if the doctor did what was required in the fullest way and took every precaution, and complications then occurred that specialists consider unavoidable, he is not liable, quoting [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] in [[work/rawdat-al-talibin|Rawdat al-Talibin]] that a cupper, a circumciser and a veterinarian are not liable if they were not negligent. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4301} It quoted al-Khattabi in Ma'alim al-Sunan that he knew of no disagreement that a practitioner who transgresses and causes a patient's death is liable, and that one who practises a science or craft he does not know is a transgressor who owes blood money but not retaliation, and it concluded that if the doctor's negligence or transgression is established, he must compensate the patient or seek his forgiveness. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4301}
