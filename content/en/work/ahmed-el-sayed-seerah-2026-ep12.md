## Summary

The lecturer reflects on what the Qur'an may have brought down during the three years of the quiet call, offering tentative judgements on which chapters are early, and on what the polytheists said about the Prophet in that phase. He then turns to the public call from al-Safa as reported in al-Bukhari, draws out the theme of fulfilling a trust, and closes with language notes and a clan-genealogy book. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### Link with the quiet call and the length of the pause in revelation

- He says the previous session dealt with caution and security in the quiet call, and that this one continues from there to the move to open preaching. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates that the versified summary of the sira by al-Iraqi describes the Arqam house, secret prayer in the mountain paths for three years, and then the public call after the command to warn the close kin. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates, for the third time in the series, the passage in which Ibn Ishaq says the Prophet hid his affair for about three years from the start of his mission before being ordered to proclaim it; he also cites Ibn Hisham's gloss that the verb means to separate truth from falsehood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that the three-year figure for the quiet call is not firmly established, since it has no particular sound chain, but is a widely repeated statement of the biographers and so a reasonable working basis. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that the opening of Iqra and of al-Muddaththir certainly came before the open call, and that al-Muddaththir came after the pause in revelation, as the sound report in al-Bukhari states. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that the pause in revelation cannot well have lasted three years, because six years before the open call would not fit the dating of the first migration to Abyssinia and the conversion of Umar; he leaves its exact length open, perhaps days or weeks. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He treats the report from al-Shabi about Israfil accompanying the Prophet for a time before Jibril as a strong support but a lone report, and suggests it belongs to the preparation before revelation, not to the descent of the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### What the Qur'an brought during the quiet call, and the limits of revelation-order lists

- He says the Qur'an was the main material of the call, alongside the Prophet's own spoken warning, and that one purpose of its descent was to steady the Prophet's heart. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He asks whether what came down in these three years dealt only with reassurance or also with the polytheists and deniers, and holds that if the latter, the quiet call was not secret in the full sense. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that there is no sure way to know which chapters came in that phase and that any answer is a probable inference only. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that full lists ordering every chapter by time of descent rest on no solid foundation, and that building detailed methods or reform programmes on them is unsound, though some scholars and reformers do so. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He gives one example of such a list placing al-Muddaththir fourth, which he says conflicts with the sound report that it was the second to come down after Iqra, and he treats such lists as at most a faint, partial aid. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He distinguishes the part from the whole: judging that a particular chapter is probably early is useful if well argued, while a complete order cannot support knowledge or practice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### Early reports from Mujahid and Ubayd ibn Umayr, and the books that hold them

- He relates that a sound report from Mujahid names Iqra and then Nun as the first to come down, and holds it strong though mursal, since Mujahid was a great Qur'an scholar who said he had asked Ibn Abbas about each verse and when it came. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that this report is to be reconciled with the report in the two Sahihs that Iqra came first and then al-Muddaththir, by taking Nun as one of the earliest chapters and perhaps the third. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He says the Companion Jabir's report in the two Sahihs about what came first is read as meaning first after the pause in revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He describes Ibn Abi Shayba's Musannaf as a great book of early reports from the generation after the Companions, with high chains, and names it along with Abd al-Razzaq's collection and Abu Ubayd's book on the merits of the Qur'an as where such reports are found. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He later reads two chains from Ibn Abi Shayba, via Waki and Shuba from Ubayd ibn Umayr, and via Waki and Sufyan from Mujahid, both naming Nun as coming after Iqra, and calls both sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that Nun was probably not the second but one of the early chapters, and that perhaps only its first verses came then, because the later part speaks of polytheists in detail, resembling the passage about the man created alone in al-Muddaththir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Reasons to think al-A'la, parts of al-Qiyama and al-Fatiha are early

- He offers, as a tentative guess, that chapters addressing the Prophet directly on how to receive revelation belong to the earliest stage, and cites the passage in al-Qiyama about not hurrying the tongue. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He lists four indications that al-A'la is early: it instructs the Prophet about revelation, it was among the first taught to the Ansar before the migration, its theme is glorifying God, and it appears in lists of early chapters; he still declines to say this is certain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates the report in al-Bukhari from al-Bara ibn Azib that Mus'ab ibn Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum arrived first in Medina teaching the Qur'an, followed by Ammar, Bilal, Sa'd, Umar with twenty riders and then the Prophet, and that al-Bara had learned al-A'la among similar chapters by then. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He observes that al-A'la has no direct argument with the polytheists, which fits a stage when believers were being nourished in faith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that al-Fatiha is probably early too, since prayer existed before the five daily prayers, but says reports calling it first are not sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He says scholars differ on the early prayer, a strong view being two prayers, morning and evening, and that the meaning of A'isha's report on two rak'as is disputed: whether it refers to the prayers prescribed after the Night Journey or to an earlier time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### What the polytheists said before the open call: charges of madness and sorcery

- He notes that the chapter of al-Shu'ara, which holds the command to warn the close kin, also answers charges and sneering from the polytheists, and holds that this shows talk against the Prophet existed before the open call, short of general hostility. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He says many scholars place the verse about proclaiming openly in al-Hijr, which likewise rebuts charges including madness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that the polytheists knew of the call and spoke about it, and that at first Quraysh treated it like the isolated monotheist seekers they already knew. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates the stories of Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl, who declared to Quraysh that none but he followed Abraham's way, objected to slaughter in another name and offered to raise girls others meant to bury alive, yet did not confront Quraysh as the Prophet later did; he adds that Zayd was pressed by his kin and driven to leave. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He suggests that Quraysh first placed the Prophet among such seekers, such as Waraqa ibn Nawfal who turned Christian, and that clashes grew as followers appeared. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds as a probable inference that the first charge was madness or possession by spirits, then later sorcery, since al-Qalam, al-Hijr and al-Shu'ara mention madness while al-Isra, a late chapter, mentions the charge of being bewitched. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He notes that the charge of madness is also put in Pharaoh's mouth against Moses in the same chapter, and holds that the Qur'an keeps tying the Prophet's story to Moses from the beginning. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates the account in which Quraysh asked al-Walid ibn al-Mughira for a verdict on the Qur'an, and Abu Jahl feared al-Walid might embrace Islam and dissuaded him; he compares this with Abu Jahl at Abu Talib's deathbed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that al-Shu'ara, with its refrains, probably came down as one unit, including the verse about the close kin, though he knows no text settling it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The jinn chapter and the date of the jinn listening at Nakhla

- He suggests the chapter of al-Jinn may be early, though not necessarily in the quiet phase. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates that Ibn Hajar discussed the matter at length because a report sets the jinn's listening on the way back from Ta'if near the Night Journey, and Ibn Hajar insisted these were two separate events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He gives two points Ibn Hajar found hard if the event is early: the trip toward Ta'if and the dawn prayer, and says the dawn prayer can mean the prayer then performed at that hour, since there were then two prayers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that the main reason for an early date is that the jinn went searching because news from heaven had been cut off at the start of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates the report in al-Bukhari from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet was going with companions toward the market of Ukaz, that shooting stars had barred the devils from heavenly news, that the group heading to Tihama met him at Nakhla leading dawn prayer, listened, believed and told their people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He says the jinn event again links the Prophet with Moses, through the passage in al-Ahqaf about a book sent down after Moses, and he recalls Waraqa's comparison with the law revealed to Moses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He says the jinn in one report were from Nasibin, a town in the Upper Mesopotamia region now on the Turkish border opposite al-Qamishli, and the story of Salman also mentions it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He draws on Muhammad Hasan Shurrab's geographical dictionary of places named in the sira and hadith, which says there are two valleys named Nakhla, al-Shamiyya and al-Yamaniyya, running in parallel and joining some 43 kilometres from Mecca to the north-east on the old Ta'if road. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### A report on public recitation in Mecca and a note on al-Isra

- He relates the report in al-Bukhari from Ibn Abbas that a verse about neither raising nor lowering one's voice in prayer came down when the Prophet was in hiding in Mecca, because loud recitation led the polytheists to curse the Qur'an and its bringer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates Ibn Hajar's comment that hiding here refers to the first period of Islam, and suggests that some verses of al-Isra may have come early so the chapter may have come in parts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### The public call from al-Safa as reported in al-Bukhari

- He relates, from the chapter in al-Bukhari on the command to warn the close kin, that the Prophet climbed al-Safa and called the clans of Quraysh by name until they gathered, asked whether they would believe a warning of cavalry, and then declared himself a warner before a severe punishment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates that in this report Abu Lahab replied with a curse, and the chapter on his ruin came down after that. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates a second report in al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayra, in which the Prophet addressed Quraysh, the Banu Abd Manaf, his uncle al-Abbas, his aunt Safiyya and his daughter Fatima, telling each that he could not shield them from God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He says the Prophet could foresee the difficulty of that moment, having heard the charges of the quiet phase, and that for the first time he faced denial before everyone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that the Prophet chose al-Safa himself as the way to fulfil the command, and that the loud call and the cavalry comparison were a persuasive way to open, while the warning stayed general because a warning is not a lesson and because Quraysh already knew his call to the worship of one God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### The theme of fulfilling a trust, and the report of Quraysh at Abu Talib's

- He holds that the heart of the Prophet's stand on al-Safa was fulfilling the trust of delivering the message, whether people believed or mocked, and that this matters more to him than his eagerness for his kin's guidance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He sets his listeners a question about evidence for this theme and says the famous saying about the sun in his right hand and the moon in his left has no strong chain and does not fit his point by its wording. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates another report, which he says al-Bayhaqi and al-Bukhari in his history transmit and Ibn Hajar called good, in which Quraysh complained to Abu Talib that the Prophet harmed them in their gathering place; Abu Talib sent Aqil to fetch him, and the Prophet answered that he could no more give up his task than they could take a flame from the sun, after which Abu Talib said he had never known him to lie. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that this wording suggests an early stage, before the later offers and pressure. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He cites the verse about being nearly drawn toward them as showing the Prophet himself was among those bound by the message, and holds that he understood the command to warn as requiring openness, as he had earlier understood the command to rise and warn as quiet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### Ibn Ashur on the name and place of the chapter al-Shu'ara

- He relates that Ibn Ashur says the chapter is called al-Shu'ara and also Ta Sin Mim, and that Ibn al-Arabi in Ahkam al-Qur'an and al-Suyuti in al-Itqan record the name al-Jami'a on authority attributed to Malik. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates that Ibn Ashur finds the reason for that name unclear and guesses it was the first chapter to bring together the stories of the messengers who brought laws, leading to the Muhammadan message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He holds that its longest story is that of Moses, and that the chapter invites reflection on the likeness of the Prophet's story to Moses and on being denied as Moses was. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### The meaning of the verb for proclaiming, after al-Suhayli

- He relates Ibn Hisham's gloss on the verb as separating truth from falsehood, with a poetic line as proof. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates that al-Suhayli, in his commentary on the sira, calls Ibn Hisham's gloss sound and adds that the verb is figurative, comparing doubt and ignorance to night and the Qur'an to light that splits it, as dawn splits the night. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates that al-Suhayli cites a line of al-Shammakh and a note of Qasim ibn Thabit comparing the white under a mourner's black garment, when torn, to dawn breaking, and he praises how the images are joined. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}

### Clans of Quraysh and a study book on their lineages

- He announces that a book on the lineages and glories of Quraysh will be a core reading text in his planned study circle, and reads a sample entry on Abd Manaf, the Prophet's third grandfather. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates from that book that Abd Manaf, whose name was al-Mughira, was the second son of Qusayy, took charge of Mecca and Quraysh after his father, and had four sons, Hashim, Abd Shams, al-Muttalib and Nawfal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates that Banu Hashim and Banu Abd Shams shared leadership while al-Muttalib and Nawfal's descendants were allies, and that leadership passed from Hashim to Abd al-Muttalib and then to Harb ibn Umayya, after which it divided. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
- He relates from Ibn Sa'd that when the verse to warn the close kin came down the Prophet stood on al-Safa, called Quraysh by clan, and said he could give them nothing of this world or the next except through the declaration of one God, and that Abu Lahab replied with a curse, whereupon the chapter on him came down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep12}
