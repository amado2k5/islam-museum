## Summary

A question about a loan whose interest is paid by another party, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that any loan repaid with a stipulated increase is an interest-bearing loan whether the borrower or someone else pays the increase, so one may not sign an interest-bearing loan even if another institution bears the interest, since the contract is void. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3657}

## The question

The questioner asked about taking a loan in which he repays the same amount he received, but signs a contract carrying interest that is paid by an institution affiliated with the education sector. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3657}

## The answer

The Department answered that any loan repaid with a stipulated increase counts as an interest-bearing loan, whether the increase is paid by the borrower or someone else, since jurists agree on the well-known maxim that every loan that brings a benefit is riba. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3657} It said there is no Shari'a justification for a Muslim to sign an interest-bearing loan contract whose interest someone else pays, because an interest-bearing loan contract is void, citing [[passage/quran-2-278|Surat al-Baqara 2:278]] and [[passage/quran-2-279|2:279]]. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3657} It concluded that taking an interest-bearing loan is not permitted even if another body pays the interest, because a loan contract that stipulates interest is void in the Shari'a. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3657}
