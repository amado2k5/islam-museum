## Overview
The Jami, in full "al-Jami al-Mukhtasar min al-Sunan an Rasul Allah" or the Concise Collection of Sunan from the Messenger of God, is the hadith collection of Abu Isa [[person/al-tirmidhi-muhammad-ibn-isa|Muhammad ibn Isa ibn Sawra al-Tirmidhi]], who died in 279 AH. It is one of the [[concept/kutub-al-sitta|Six Books]]. {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}

Its distinctive feature is that its author states the grade of each hadith, sound, good or strange, and mentions the views of the jurists on each topic. The author also wrote [[work/al-shamail-al-muhammadiyya|al-Shama'il al-Muhammadiyya]], on the description of the Prophet. {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}

## The author in brief
The Encyclopaedia of Islam calls al-Tirmidhi a great collector of hadith and the author of one of the Six Books. He is said to have been born in 210/825 near the town of [[place/tirmidh|Tirmidh]]. He travelled widely in search of traditions through Khurasan, Iraq and the Hijaz, and was, among others, a pupil of [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]]. The article reports that late in life he became blind, allegedly from excessive weeping, and that he died in Tirmidh in 279/892. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## Place among the Six Books
The article describes the Jami as the work for which al-Tirmidhi is famous in the first place, under the title al-Jami al-sahih. It says the collection is generally considered to hold the fourth rank, after those of al-Bukhari, [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] and [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## Size
In the Cairo edition of Ahmad Muhammad Shakir and colleagues, published between 1937 and 1965, the collection contains 3,956 traditions, according to the article. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579} Sunnah.com describes Jami' al-Tirmidhi as containing roughly 4,400 hadith, counting repetitions, in 46 books, and as one of the six canonical collections. {cite:sunnah-com tirmidhi} The two figures come from different counts and editions, and the sources do not reconcile them.

## Traditions with a single chain
Like his predecessors, the article says, al-Tirmidhi included in his collection many dozens of traditions that he provided with single-strand chains of transmission, for which he alone may be held responsible. They can be identified through the Tuhfat al-ashraf of Yusuf ibn Abd al-Rahman [[person/jamal-al-din-al-mizzi|al-Mizzi]], who died in 742/1341, where they are marked by a single Arabic letter, ta. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## The Kitab al-Ilal and the grading terms
At the end of the Jami an early and, in the article's judgement, extremely important theoretical work on common defects is appended, the Kitab al-Ilal. In it al-Tirmidhi sets out his methods of classifying hadith by means of three technical terms, sahih, hasan and gharib, which he often used in combinations that seem contradictory. The article says these methods met with some criticism, as well as bewilderment, among his fellow traditionists. It points readers to the commentary of [[person/ibn-rajab|Abd al-Rahman ibn Ahmad ibn Rajab al-Hanbali]], who died in 795/1393, called Sharh ilal al-Tirmidhi, for a detailed survey of these reactions. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## The Shama'il
Al-Tirmidhi also made a collection of traditions devoted solely to the characteristics of the Prophet, entitled K. al-Shama'il. The article refers readers to Sezgin for editions and commentaries, and suggests that the book was probably modelled on the latter part of the biography of Muhammad by [[person/muhammad-ibn-sad|Ibn Sa'd]], who died in 230/845, in his [[work/al-tabaqat-al-kubra-ibn-sad|al-Tabaqat al-kabir]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}

## Note on the sources
The Encyclopaedia of Islam entry on al-Tirmidhi is short and treats the Jami in a few sentences. This page therefore records only what the entry states, together with the earlier Sunnah.com figures. The page numbers, chapter counts, individual rulings and the reception of the work beyond the points above are not covered in it.
