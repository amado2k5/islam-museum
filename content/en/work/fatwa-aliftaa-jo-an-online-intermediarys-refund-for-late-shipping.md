## Summary

A question from an intermediary for an online shop who pays in advance for customers' orders, where the shop offers a refund as compensation for late shipping, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the refund belongs to the customer if the intermediary acts as the customer's agent, and to the intermediary if she buys the goods for herself and then sells them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4026}

## The question

The questioner said she works as an intermediary for an online shop, ordering products for customers and paying for them in advance, then collecting the money from customers on delivery; because shipping is sometimes delayed, the shop offers to refund the amount paid as compensation. She asked whether the compensation is hers or the customers'. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4026}

## The answer

The Department answered that the ruling depends on the nature of the contractual relationship between the intermediary and the customers. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4026} It said that if the intermediary is an agent who delivers orders and takes a delivery fee and an agency commission, the rule that an agent acts for the principal's benefit applies, so the agent may not keep any cash refund from the supplier, and the refund belongs to the customer. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4026} If the intermediary buys the goods with her own money and then sells them to customers, she may keep the refunds, because the goods entered her ownership and risk before she sold them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4026} It concluded that the refund belongs to the customer when the intermediary is the customer's agent in buying and delivering, and to the intermediary when she buys the goods for herself and then sells them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 4026}
