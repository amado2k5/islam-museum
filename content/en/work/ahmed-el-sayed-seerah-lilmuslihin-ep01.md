## Summary

The opening lecture of the series on the Prophet's biography for reformers. The speaker explains why the study of the Prophet's life is an act of gratitude and a duty, why his guidance must be read as a whole drawing on both the Sunna and the biographical books, and which three fruits he wants from the series: guidance, understanding of the religion, and firmer faith in the Prophet's mission. He also describes how he will read the biography through the school the Prophet ran and the Companions who were its students. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### Opening: the stature of the Prophet and the covenant taken from the prophets

- He announces a new series titled The Prophetic Biography for Reformers and calls any talk about the Prophet and his life a weighty and honourable matter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that following the Prophet's conditions in order to draw lessons and benefits is itself an act of worship, since a person worships God by knowing the state of His prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that God praised the Prophet at length in His book and swore by the Prophet's life, which he takes as a sign of the Prophet's standing with his Lord. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates a verse about the covenant God took from the prophets to believe in and support a later messenger who confirms what they have, and says that well-known exegetes read the messenger as Muhammad alone, while others read it as prophets in general confirming one another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### First point: gratitude for the grace of the Prophet's sending

- He holds that God has reminded believers of the favour of sending the Prophet, who recites, purifies and teaches them, and has asked for gratitude in return, and he points to two consecutive verses that carry both the favour and the command to give thanks. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that a long span of time can make people forget the size of this favour, leaving only love, reverence and blessings on the Prophet, so that people stop valuing what the sending gave them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that studying the Prophet's life is among the best ways to bring the favour back to mind and so to thank God, and he counts gratitude as one of the greatest acts of worship and as a central one for which the messengers were sent. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates that human beings are described as either grateful or ungrateful, that some early imams said religion has two halves, patience and gratitude, and that a verse joining the two appears four times in the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that thanks for the religious favour of guidance, the sending of the Prophet and the revelation outranks thanks for worldly gifts such as health, safety and children. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He explains that the series is aimed at reformers, who should recognise that each step on the path of calling to God and reform rests on the light of the Prophet's school and would not exist without his sending. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### Second point: the duty to take the Prophet's guidance as the measure

- He holds that honouring the Prophet's life is not a favour that people grant but a duty, and that his guidance must be the standard by which matters are measured, adopted with conviction that it is the best guidance. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates a report in which the Prophet ranks God's book as the best speech and his own guidance as the best guidance, and says the Qur'an denies faith to anyone who does not accept the Prophet's judgement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He argues that one of the main causes of deep and contradictory divisions among Muslims, including charges of innovation and unbelief, is that the full Prophetic biography was pushed out of its central role as the reference. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says the place it left was taken by another cultural model, for example Greek philosophy in the past and humanist Western culture today, and calls this one of the greatest causes of error. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds, citing a verse against putting anything ahead of God and His messenger, that scholars read it as lasting beyond the Prophet's lifetime, so that nothing may be put ahead of his Sunna and guidance after his death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Third point: the whole of the Prophet's guidance, Sunna and biography together

- He holds that people often reduce the Prophet's guidance, either by caring for the Sunna books while neglecting the biography, or by caring for the biography books while neglecting the Sunna. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He describes the biography and campaign books as giving the overall frame of events across his life, while the Sunna books give the detailed content, especially the rulings, and he compares the first to the structure of a building and the second to its interior and decoration. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that a person who wants to study the Prophet properly should not rest on biography books alone, and students of the Sunna should not give the biography a low place. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He groups both under the general name of Prophetic guidance, with detailed guidance known from the Sunna books and comprehensive guidance known from the biography and campaign books. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says the problem can also lie in the reader, who comes with narrow, fixed moulds such as memorising dates, names and places, or hunting for set answers, and he says this series will watch above all for the Prophet's reforming method and for what a reformer can draw from it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that learning from the Prophet covers knowledge, action and faith, and relates that a Companion named Jundub said the young Companions learned faith before they learned the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates that the Qur'an gives the Prophet three tasks, reciting the verses, purifying the believers and teaching the book and wisdom, and recommends reading the biography with these three headings in mind, especially purification. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates that this mission was announced in the prayer of Ibrahim and that the Prophet is reported to have spoken of his own prophethood as the answer to that prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### Reading the biography as a school: the Companions as its students

- He says he prefers the word school for the Prophet's community and wants readers to watch what happened among the Companions during the Prophet's life, the way one judges a project both by its programme and by what its participants gained. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that the Prophet was not only a teacher delivering knowledge but also a leader and an educator, and that purification works largely through education. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says he will briefly cover the lives of the earliest converts so readers can see who entered this school, and will ask why the Prophet chose particular men as commanders of the many small expeditions he sent out. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He asks why the Prophet sent Mu'adh and Abu Musa to Yemen as teachers, and holds that such choices can be studied for what they teach. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that the study should continue after the Prophet's death, and gives one example, that innovations such as the doctrine of divine decree, Shi'ism and the Khawarij appeared in the Companions' own time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that although the Companions had social weight that any innovator would have welcomed, and although they differed among themselves on some matters, they were agreed in keeping away from these currents, and he holds that this result must be traced back to what the Prophet's school gave them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Recap of the three themes and the first fruit: guidance

- He sums up the three introductory themes as the favour of the Prophet's sending, the duty to refer to his guidance and put it first, and the comprehensiveness of that guidance across Sunna and biography, with faults sometimes in the reading and sometimes in the reader. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He names guidance as the first fruit he wants from the study, meaning reading the biography as someone seeking to be guided by it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that many Muslims, even students of knowledge, think only of the detailed guidance such as what the Prophet did around the prayer or how he dressed, and that they overlook other centres of guidance in his life, among them reforming guidance, which the series will stress. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### Second fruit: understanding of the religion beyond the tools of legal theory

- He names understanding of the religion as the second fruit, with all applications in the series taken from the reforming side. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that the tools of legal theory, such as the meanings of wordings, are helpful but are wrongly treated by some, even among specialists in the field, as a guarantee of reaching correct guidance and understanding. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He lists further requirements he regards as outside theory, namely the belief that understanding is a light from God, sincerity of intention, detachment, submission and honesty, and adds that long keeping company with the Prophet's guidance in both its detailed and general forms is another path. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He observes that specialists tend to build a sense from long familiarity, giving the example of hadith critics who, through constant contact with the Prophet's wording, can sometimes judge a report not to resemble his speech and more like the speech of a pious later figure. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates a story from Bukhari in which the Prophet spoke of a servant offered the choice between worldly splendour and what is with God, and holds that Abu Bakr wept at once and said that he and the others would give their parents for the Prophet, while the narrator says they were surprised, since the Prophet died soon afterwards. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that Abu Bakr understood a deeper meaning from the Prophet's circumstances rather than from the wording, because of his close companionship, and offers this as proof that some understanding does not come from tools alone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that the early large works on legal theory after al-Shafi'i were mostly written by Mu'tazilis who used the rules to interpret God's words and support their doctrine, and holds that students err when they treat the Islamic sciences as pure instruments or think they must first learn outside cultural sciences in order to learn the religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Third fruit: firmer faith in the Prophet's mission and the meaning of the testimony

- He names the third fruit as stronger faith and certainty that Muhammad is God's messenger. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He observes that every Muslim says the testimony in the prayer, and holds that there is a great difference between saying it from habit and saying it while aware of every word. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that testimony requires knowledge, and that a verse in which God, the angels and people of knowledge witness the divine oneness shows the honour given to the learned for this testimony. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that the testimony of oneness and of Muhammad's messengership is the dividing point between the Garden and the Fire, and calls it the ticket to the Garden and the highest truth of the religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says two things make a person give the testimony well, knowledge of God and of his messenger, and a present, living heart free from heedlessness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates a report in Bukhari and Muslim of a campaign during which hunger was relieved when food multiplied after the Prophet prayed over it, and the Prophet then renewed the declaration of oneness and his own messengership, which he takes as showing that this meaning is renewed for believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says he prefers to read the Qur'an with a certain awareness that it is the speech of the Creator, given as guidance to human beings who are in a temporary state and will pass to the next world, and holds that half a page read in this way differs greatly from long portions read only for review, memorisation or finishing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### Evidences of prophethood: his character and conduct, not only miracles

- He holds that the proofs of the Prophet's truthfulness should not be limited to physical miracles, and that knowing his circumstances is one of the strongest, because prophethood is a report and a reporter is either truthful or lying. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He relates that when Heraclius questioned Abu Sufyan to test whether Muhammad was a prophet, he asked about the man's conduct and family, for example whether he had ever lied and whether any of his forefathers had been a king, and not about miracles. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He cites two Qur'anic passages as pointing to this method, one asking whether the pagans did not know their messenger and one where the Prophet is told to point to the long life he had spent among them before the revelation, with another noting that he had neither read nor written any book before it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He quotes a saying credited to Ibn Abi al-Izz al-Hanafi and before him to Ibn Taymiyya that only the most truthful or the most false of people would claim prophethood, and the two cannot be confused except by the most ignorant. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He holds that knowing how the Prophet behaved at home with his several wives, how he worshipped there and how he acted in great crises are all reasons to increase faith in him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### Format of the series

- He says that the lectures of this series will generally run between half an hour and an hour, many of them near half an hour, and that this introduction is somewhat longer because the detail helps readers enter the biography aware of its dimensions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### The Sunna as an indirect explanation of the Qur'an

- He holds that knowing the Prophet's life and Sunna is among the surest ways to understand the Qur'an, and that the Sunna explains God's book. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says the explanation he will stress is not the direct kind found in books on Qur'anic sciences, where the Prophet is reported to have glossed a word, as in the case of the word for wrong in a verse that he explained as polytheism. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He describes the other kind as one that emerges only after tracking the Prophet's conduct over a long course of his life, and likens it to the saying credited to Aisha that his character was the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He says that the Qur'anic description of the Prophet purifying his people is not clarified by a single saying of his but by studying how he dealt with the Companions in matters of faith and how he trained them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
- He gives the command to be patient as the messengers of resolve were patient as a second example, saying the wording needs no explanation but following the Prophet's own acts of patience reveals the dimensions of the command, and he files all of this under understanding of the religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}

### Closing: the Prophet's physical description deferred

- He says he had meant to end the introduction with the Prophet's physical description but time has run on, and that he will treat it in a separate session or in one of the later sessions on the biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep1}
