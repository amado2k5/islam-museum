## Summary
Mughni al-Labib an Kutub al-A'arib is the book of [[person/ibn-hisham-al-ansari|Ibn Hisham al-Ansari]] on i'rab, on the particles of meaning and what goes with them, which he meant to spare the student of i'rab the long books of earlier parsers. This page sets out what was read of its preface: what the author says of why and when he wrote it, its eight chapters, what he saw as the cause of length in earlier books of i'rab, and the title and the readers addressed. Each saying is ascribed to its holder as it stands.

## The author and the aim of i'rab
The edition opens the author's preface by naming him: Jamal al-Din Abu Muhammad Abd Allah ibn Yusuf ibn Hisham al-Ansari {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.12}. Ibn Hisham says that what the gifted most wish for is what makes the understanding of God's revealed Book and the sense of His Prophet's hadith easy, and that the root of this is the science of i'rab, the guide to what is right {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.12}. So he ties the aim of grammar here to understanding the text, Qur'an and hadith. This is his own saying; others give grammar other aims, as on the page [[concept/ilm-al-nahw|Arabic grammar (nahw)]].

## When he wrote it
He says that in the year 749 AH he composed a book on this at Mecca, that he then suffered what he suffered over it and other things when he went back to Egypt, and that when in 756 God granted him to return to the Sanctuary and live beside it he took up the work again and wrote this book {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.12}. The two years are Hijri; they correspond roughly to 1348 and 1355 CE, a conversion made here and not found in the book. He says he went through the locked-up questions of i'rab and opened them, made clear the knotty ones, and warned of mistakes that had occurred among a group of parsers, and that no mind had yet allowed anything like it for this purpose {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.12}. This is the author's claim in his book, not the museum's testimony. He says he had earlier written on the same subject a small introduction named al-I'rab an Qawa'id al-I'rab, which students received well {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.12-13}.

## The eight chapters
He holds the book to eight chapters: the first on explaining single words and their rulings; the second on explaining sentences, their kinds and rulings; the third on what hovers between words and sentences, that is the adverb and the preposition with its noun, and their rulings; the fourth on rulings that recur often and whose ignorance shames a parser; the fifth on the ways in which fault enters the parser; the sixth on warning against matters well known among parsers where the right is the opposite; the seventh on how to parse; and the eighth on general matters from which countless particular cases are derived {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.13-14}. In the first chapter he means by single words the particles and the nouns and adverbs that include their meaning; he arranges them by the letters of the alphabet so they are easy to reach, and begins with the letter alif {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.17}.

## Why books of i'rab run long
He says he examined the books of i'rab and found three causes of their length. First, much repetition, because they were set down to speak about particular cases and not to give general laws, so whenever a similar construction came up they repeated what they had said; he gives the relative pronoun and the separate pronoun in different verses, where the same options are listed each time, and says he gathered these questions into the fourth chapter {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.14-15}. Second, bringing in what does not concern i'rab, such as discussing whether the word ism is derived from simah, as the Kufans say, or from sumuw, as the Basrans say, arguing for both parties and weighing the stronger, or whether the alif of the demonstrative dha is added, as the Kufans say, or turned from ya', as the Basrans say. He wonders that [[person/makki-ibn-abi-talib|Makki ibn Abi Talib]] put such things in his book made to explain difficult i'rab when they have nothing to do with i'rab {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.15-16}. Third, parsing the obvious, such as subject and predicate, agent and its deputy, the one most thorough in this being [[person/al-hawfi-al-grammarian|al-Hawfi]]; he says he avoided both and put in their place Qur'anic parallels, poetic witnesses and some of what occurred in the sessions of the grammarians {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.16}. This talk of Kufans and Basrans touches the page [[concept/madrasat-al-nahw-al-basriyya-kufiyya|the Basran and Kufan school]].

## The title and those addressed
He says that when the work was complete he named it Mughni al-Labib an Kutub al-A'arib, and that he addresses it to one who is beginning to learn i'rab and to one who holds firm to the surest of its means {cite:mughni-labib-mubarak-1985 p.16}.

## The two grammarians the preface names
The preface names two earlier authors of books on the i'rab of the Qur'an. A research summary supplied to the museum (the museum did not open the pages it names; the founder approved its use on 21 November 2026) gives their full names and death dates from al-Dhahabi's Siyar A'lam al-Nubala', vol. 17. [[person/makki-ibn-abi-talib|Makki]] is Abu Muhammad Makki ibn Abi Talib Hammush ibn Muhammad ibn Mukhtar al-Qaysi, al-Qayrawani and then al-Qurtubi, who died in Muharram 437 AH (July-August 1045 CE) {cite:islamweb-siyar-makki-ibn-abi-talib p.591-592}. [[person/al-hawfi-al-grammarian|Al-Hawfi]] is Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ibrahim ibn Sa'id al-Hawfi, who died in 430 AH (1038-1039 CE) {cite:islamweb-siyar-al-hawfi p.521-522}. The identification of the two names in the preface with these men is the summary's, not the preface's own.

## What this page did not reach
Only the preface and the start of the first chapter were read; the entries on the single words and the chapters after them, which are most of the book, were not, nor anything said of its influence and commentaries. The Shamela card says the copy has 918 pages and that its numbering agrees with the printed edition; this was not compared with a paper copy. The page that ends the preface shows at its head the number 1316, between pages 15 and 17, and it is cited here as page 16. At the time of reading this was an inference from its position; a research summary supplied to the museum (approved for use by the founder on 21 November 2026, not re-verified by the museum) now answers that the page after page 15 is page 16 and that the 1316 in the Shamela copy is a digitisation typo for 16. The source record states this.

## Place in the canon

Mughni al-Labib an Kutub al-A'arib appears in a research summary supplied to the museum: a list of sixty of the most-cited works on the Arabic language, twelve in each of its five sciences, where it stands in the section "Grammar". The summary ascribes the work to [[person/ibn-hisham-al-ansari|Ibn Hisham al-Ansari]] and gives its date as: author died 1360 CE / 761 AH. It names the Arabic Wikipedia page “مغني اللبيب عن كتب الأعاريب” as the page documenting the work {cite:wikipedia-mughni-al-labib-ar}. On its presence in the Shamela library the summary says: Yes, it is indexed in the Shamela library, through a Shamela PDF link {cite:shamela-mughni-al-labib}.

The museum took this from the summary as it stands. The founder approved using the summary without re-verification on 21 November 2026, and the museum did not open the pages it names. The museum has read the author's preface and the start of the first chapter, pages 12 to 17 of the edition named on this page, and nothing beyond them. This record therefore says nothing of the book's contents, chapters, editions or commentaries beyond what other sections of this page cite. The Shamela book number is a bibliographic pointer only; no text was copied from it.
