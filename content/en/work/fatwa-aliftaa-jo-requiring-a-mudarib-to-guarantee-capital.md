## Summary

A question about a capital owner requiring the working partner to guarantee the capital against loss, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that this is not permitted in a mudaraba: the owner loses his money and the worker his effort, and the worker is liable only if he transgresses or is negligent. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3933}

## The question

The questioner asked about a capital owner stipulating that the working partner must guarantee the capital in the event of a loss. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3933}

## The answer

The Department answered that a mudaraba is a contract in which the capital owner hands money to a person or company to trade with, the profit being shared between them in an agreed proportion of the whole. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3933} It listed conditions for its validity: the capital may not be guaranteed by the working partner but is exposed to profit and loss; it must be present and handed over, not a debt; profits must be shares of the profit actually made, not a fixed sum or a percentage of the capital; profit must be genuinely realised; and the investment must be in permitted business, not forex, network or pyramid marketing, or a Ponzi scheme. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3933} It said that in a loss the owner loses his money and the worker loses his effort and time, and the worker bears no capital loss unless he transgresses or is negligent, which experts assess; it cited [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] in Rawdat al-Talibin that the worker's dealings are bound by the owner's interest, like an agent's. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3933} It cited article 628 of the Jordanian Civil Code, under which the capital owner alone bears the loss and any contrary condition is disregarded, and concluded that if the worker's transgression or negligence is established by a sound procedure, he must make good the loss to that extent. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3933}
