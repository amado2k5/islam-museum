## The treatise and its question

TDV lists a Treatise on Ratio by [[person/al-mahani|al-Mahani]], with additional title forms transliterated as Risala fi al-shakl min amr al-nisba and fi al-mushkil min al-nisba. We give neither every form a separate book ID nor claim to have collated identical copies. Dold-Samplonius lists three ratio texts while questioning whether they differ. That uncertainty describes the witnesses, not permission to inflate a catalogue {cite:tdv-mahani-fazlioglu work1} {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb works}.

The subject belongs to the ratio tradition around Euclid's fifth book. Dold-Samplonius describes a [[invention/mahani-successive-quotient-ratios|successive-quotient definition]] comparing the behaviour of magnitudes during a greatest-common-measure process. This does not mean the treatise invented the Euclidean algorithm or every continued fraction {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb ratio}.

## Definition and limits

In the historian's account these mathematicians accepted Euclid's definition as correct, but found that its synthetic presentation did not explain how ratio theory arose. The proposed account follows successive comparison quotients, making matching sequences a criterion for proportionality. Its pre-Eudoxian background and al-Mahani's reference to Thabit ibn Qurra remain visible: ratio was not created from nothing {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb Euclid-V-and-ratio}.

Dold-Samplonius says al-Mahani and al-Nayrizi did not establish the connection with Euclid's definition; Ibn al-Haytham did so later. Correct museum examples involving rational numbers do not remove that limitation. Our arithmetic is a modern explanation, not a complete proof recovered from an unread original {cite:dold-samplonius-mahani-dsb ratio}.
