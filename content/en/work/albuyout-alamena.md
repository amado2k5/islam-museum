## Summary
'Safe Homes' (al-Buyut al-Amina) is a weekly programme by Omar Abdelkafy on the Dubai channel, in forty-seven episodes, on building the Muslim home from good choice and engagement through married life and the raising of children, with viewers' phone-in questions throughout. It is a guidance programme and not a fatwa programme, and the lecturer's views on disputed matters are recorded as his views. This page covers the first episodes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:32:00}

## Episode 1: good choice
- He announces a new weekly programme on the Dubai channel called 'Safe Homes'. - In his preface he says a society is safe only when its homes are safe, and that homes without firm faith are easily shaken by storms. - The topic of the first episode is good choice in building the Muslim home. - He explains that the word 'zawj' in Arabic is used for a man and for a woman, and means one who needs his like. He holds that the Qur'an uses the word for a spouse where the two are alike in righteousness, such as [[person/adam|Adam]] and his wife, Zakariya and his wife, and the people of Paradise and their spouses. ## Episode 2: choice between children and parents, and the rulings on marriage
- He opens by saying the first episode drew many reactions through the week, and that the subject of choice is sensitive for young and old. He announces a common fault he will address today: parents choosing their son's wife from their own sisters' and friends' daughters instead of the young man choosing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:01:30}
- He cites children's complaint of a mother who says: if you do not marry your cousin I am angry with you until the Day of Resurrection. He says anger does not extend like that, and that a young man may see his cousin only as a sister. He holds that children's objection to parents' obstinacy is legitimate when marriage is delayed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:03:00}
- He sets out the five rulings of [[practice/nikah|marriage]]: obligatory for one who is certain he will fall into the forbidden if he does not marry, required for one who fears it, and recommended when both are equal, citing the warning against turning away from the Sunna. He says whoever obstructs marriage is a 'key to evil and a lock on good' and, in his own phrase, one who should see a psychiatrist, and he notes that unmarried women over the usual age number in the millions in some countries. These are his formulations and are not quoted from a particular school. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:06:30}
- He says marriage becomes disliked if one fears he will wrong his spouse, and forbidden if he is certain of it, giving examples of a withdrawn, ill-tempered young man or one lost in cafes, and a girl who cannot run a home. He says compatibility ([[concept/kafaa|kafa'a]]) exists in marriage but is not about tribe, country or family, and that the faults that matter are faults of behaviour. He points to the boasting of [[person/amr-ibn-kulthum|Amr ibn Kulthum]] about his tribe as a sign of tribal arrogance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:09:30}
- He recommends a medical check before marriage and that neither party hides an illness from the other, since marriage is a solemn covenant and not an economic partnership. He suggests, as a matter of public interest (maslaha mursala), that the lawgiver also impose a psychological and behavioural examination on the suitor, with psychologists and sociologists working alongside jurists and doctors. This is the lecturer's own view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep02 00:12:00}
- On the parents' role: some choose a domineering husband for their daughter, though they would not want for her what they accept for other people's daughters. He holds that a generous man does not wrong, so the father should look for the groom's and his family's generosity, and he cites a saying attributed to an early figure that a daughter should be married to one who fears God, who will honour her if he loves her and not wrong her if he dislikes her. On a son's marriage the parents' opinion is advisory, and they should encourage his choice when compatibility, character and people's testimony are present. See [[concept/wali|the guardian]]. - If the parents are not observant and the son has chosen a righteous girl, or if they approved of the girl at first and then magnified her faults after the engagement, he advises children not to press their parents except by persuasion, and advises parents that sons and daughters past twenty have their own standing and independence, so dialogue and consultation are needed. - He qualifies what he said in episode one after a discussion with scholars: the word 'imra'a' (woman or wife of) in the Qur'an can mark a difference in creation or conduct, or widowhood, or barrenness, such as the wife of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]] and the barren wife of [[person/zakariyya|Zakariya]], who became his 'zawj' after God set her right for him. ## Episode 7: the first year of marriage
- He announces the subject of the episode: the first year of marriage, the hardest in planting either the tree of affection or the seed of discord: the couple sow either a seed of affection that grows into love and good, or a seed of discord and fixing on the other's faults. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep07 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep07 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep07 00:02:00}

## Episode 8: the language of dialogue between spouses and the obstacles to affection
- He opens with a parable: an Arab who knows only Arabic sits two hours with a Chinese who knows only Chinese, and what does either understand of the other? He says many spouses speak, each, a language the other does not: reason and emotion, order and disorder, temper and coldness, social intelligence and dullness, humour and criticism. If they sit together forty years, what is the result, and will there be the affection God mentions? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:02:30}
- He says one should neither belittle good deeds nor magnify bad ones, that a good deed is to be published and a bad one concealed. He recalls that his senior teachers used to see visitors off at the front door saying: conceal whatever faults you saw at our house, for homes are private, and says everyone has weak spots and gaps, and that a husband is no angel nor a wife. This is the lecturer's personal recollection. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:04:30}
- He says a study, as he puts it, of newborns shows that in the first two months the baby tests his mother and father, and if left to cry for two or three days and certain there is no way out, falls silent, and that Westerners leave him to cry to widen his lungs while Arabs are emotional. He concludes that spouses should learn from this child not to mock the other's gifts. The study is the lecturer's claim with no source. - He says pure rationality harms life because life is not a mathematical equation, that Islam addressed both reason and emotion, and he distinguishes reflection (tafakkur), a thought that is then acted on, from pondering consequences (tadabbur). He asks young men and women to ponder the consequences of marriage so that it is not just a white dress, a wedding and gifts. The first obstacle to affection, to him, is mocking the other's qualities. This is the lecturer's view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy albuyout-alamena/ep08 00:09:30}
