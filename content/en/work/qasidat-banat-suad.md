## Summary
[[person/kab-ibn-zuhayr|Ka'b ibn Zuhayr]]'s famous qasida "Su'ad has departed, and my heart today is love-sick", which he recited before the Prophet in excuse and praise — it came to be known as the Burda {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 446-447}.

## The poem's account
Ka'b recited it to the Prophet in the mosque after declaring his Islam and seeking safety {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 446-447}. In it he excused his former lampooning and praised the Prophet, describing him as "a light by which guidance is sought, and a drawn Indian blade of God's swords" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 447}. In it he praised the Muhajirun of Quraysh and hinted at the Ansar — for one of them had sought leave to strike his neck — then made amends with them after his Islam in a poem of praise {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 447}.

## For young visitors
A long, beautiful poem by a famous poet, excusing himself to the Prophet and praising him; it became one of the Arabs' most famous poems.
