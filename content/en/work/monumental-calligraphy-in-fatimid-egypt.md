## Summary
A chapter by [[person/bernard-okane|Bernard O'Kane]] subtitled "Epigraphy in Stone, Stucco, and Wood". It begins by observing that [[concept/floriated-kufic|floriated Kufic]] was not a Fatimid invention and then follows the inscriptions of Fatimid Cairo in order. {cite:okane-monumental-calligraphy-fatimid-egypt p. 1}

## Its course
It first presents pre-Fatimid examples: a tombstone carved by Mubarak al-Makki (Dhu'l-Hijja 243/March 858) in the Museum of Islamic Art, the tomb inscription of Khadija al-Tara'ifi in the Lesser Qarafa shortly before the Fatimid conquest, and the panel of the Ikhshidid vizier Ibn al-Furat in the Saliba district (355/966). Then the stucco inscriptions of [[building/al-azhar|al-Azhar]] (completed 972), the stone and stucco of the [[building/mosque-of-al-hakim|Mosque of al-Hakim]] (completed 1013), and the works of [[person/badr-al-jamali|Badr al-Jamali]]: the Mashhad al-Juyushi on the Muqattam (487/1085), his son al-Afdal's mihrab in the Mosque of Ibn Tulun, and the inscriptions of the Cairo gates [[building/bab-al-futuh|Bab al-Futuh]], [[building/bab-al-nasr|Bab al-Nasr]] and Bab al-Tawfiq; finally the facade of the [[building/al-aqmar-mosque|Aqmar Mosque]] (519/1125–6), the most comprehensive scheme of facade inscriptions to that date. {cite:okane-monumental-calligraphy-fatimid-egypt p. 1} {cite:okane-monumental-calligraphy-fatimid-egypt p. 2} {cite:okane-monumental-calligraphy-fatimid-egypt p. 5} {cite:okane-monumental-calligraphy-fatimid-egypt p. 10}

## Notable observations
The author notes that on Badr al-Jamali's gates his titles take up much more space than those of the caliph al-Mustansir, "a nod to the real holder of power", and that the fourth line at Bab al-Nasr — the Shi'i form of the shahada — is carved on the voussoirs of the relieving arch and is longer than the three lines above, evidence of a hasty afterthought. At al-Aqmar the foundation inscription in the names of the caliph and his vizier al-Ma'mun appears twice on the facade, the upper floriated and the lower plain against a foliate scroll, with the caliph's name placed exactly in the middle above the doorway. {cite:okane-monumental-calligraphy-fatimid-egypt p. 10}
