## Summary

The lecturer opens with a biography of the early Companion Abdullah ibn Jahsh, from the book on the Badr veterans, covering the expedition to Nakhla and his death at Uhud. He then takes the opening of Surat Abasa and its occasion, the Prophet's encounter with the blind Abdallah ibn Umm Maktum, and draws lessons on divine criteria, the Qur'an's divine origin, and the honour God and the Prophet gave to humble believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Opening: early Companions and the value of early Islam

- He says the biographies placed at the start of sessions serve the series' aim of knowing the Companions, especially the early and lesser-known ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He holds that merely being early in Islam in the Meccan period was already great support for the Prophet, since before the migration helping him meant accepting Islam, while after it help meant fighting and protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Abdullah ibn Jahsh: lineage, early Islam and migrations

- He presents Abdullah ibn Jahsh as a well-known early Companion, though not among the very top tier such as the ten promised Paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that he was Abu Muhammad Abdullah ibn Jahsh of the Asad tribe, an ally of Banu Abd Shams, living in Mecca without being a Qurayshi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates from the author of the book on the Badr veterans that he migrated to Abyssinia and took part at Badr, and explains that many who went to Abyssinia missed Badr while others returned to Mecca and migrated to Medina before it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He says Ibn Sa'd devoted one volume to Badr participants and another to early Muslims who missed it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that Ibn Sa'd places his Islam before the Prophet entered Dar al-Arqam, and that he was the brother of Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh, who is said to have turned Christian in Abyssinia, a matter discussed in earlier sessions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### The expedition of Abdullah ibn Jahsh to Nakhla before Badr

- He relates that the Prophet paired Abdullah ibn Jahsh with Asim ibn Thabit, sent him with a group of emigrants only, appointed him commander and gave him a sealed letter to open after two days of travel. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He places this expedition before Badr, among the first detachments alongside those of Hamza, Ubayda ibn al-Harith and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, and notes it was made up purely of emigrants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates from Ibn Hisham that the letter told them to go to the valley of Nakhla between Mecca and Ta'if, watch Quraysh and bring back news. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that the party killed a man of a Quraysh caravan, Amr ibn al-Hadrami, on the last day of Rajab, a sacred month. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He says the polytheists used this to claim the new religion violated the sacred month, and that a media campaign followed, with Jews joining in. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### The Qur'anic response on fighting in the sacred month

- He says the verse revealed on this occasion grants that fighting in the sacred month is grave, yet treats Quraysh's own conduct, expelling people, torturing the weak and barring access to the Sacred Mosque, as graver. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He holds that the Companions erred here, out of confusion over the day or month and not out of aggression, and that the verse does not endorse the act while distinguishing a reformer's mistake from persistent obstruction of God's path. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He compares this method to Pharaoh's words to Moses about a killing and about fearing a change of religion, as told in the Qur'an, saying tyrants attack a single error while hiding their larger crimes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Seeing the whole scene: judging by complete criteria

- He holds that any assessment of mistakes or good deeds is incomplete without the full picture, citing the Badr veteran Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a, whose message to the polytheists is weighed against his past at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He says Pharaoh's people, described in the Qur'an as defiantly sinful, judged him by security, prosperity, buildings and roads, and not by the killing of children, which fell on a weak class they cared little about. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He holds that the same pattern repeats in every age and that the question is which measure one uses: establishing God's religion and justice, or material prosperity and construction. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He argues earlier generations also had great technical achievement, giving old bridges and fortresses still standing as examples. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Quraysh's standing and the difficulty of accepting Islam

- He describes Quraysh as the most honoured Arab tribe, custodians of the House, serving pilgrims, holding Dar al-Nadwa, party to the pact of al-Fudul and keeping the neighbour-protection customs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He concludes that accepting Islam was a hard decision, which explains why the Qur'an repeats proofs and answers doubts one after another. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He holds that each age has its own trial: in the previous century Muslim thinkers were troubled by miracles under European material progress, while today's difficulty lies in modern humanist values on rights, marriage and apostasy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Abdullah ibn Jahsh at Uhud

- He relates, citing Ibn Sa'd through Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, that before Uhud Abdullah ibn Jahsh was heard asking God that his enemies mutilate him and that he say it was for God's sake, and that a listener judged the wish granted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that he was killed at Uhud with his belly cut open and his nose cut off, and that Ibn Sa'd names his killer as Abu al-Hakam ibn al-Akhnas ibn Shariq, which he says shows errors in the printed edition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that Abdullah was buried with Hamza, who was his maternal uncle, since his mother was an aunt of the Prophet, making Abdullah the Prophet's cousin. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He states that Abdullah died aged a little over forty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Surat Abasa: its occasion and the Prophet's eagerness

- He explains the plan of pairing a Meccan sura and a Medinan sura with the biography, noting the previous session covered all of al-An'am for the Meccan method of dialogue with polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He says Surat Abasa was revealed about Abdallah ibn Umm Maktum of Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy of Quraysh, who was blind and an early Muslim; he reports it was said that his mother's name referred to blindness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that the Prophet, intensely eager for the conversion of Meccan leaders in hope that their followers would follow, was addressing some of them when the blind man came asking to be taught a verse and repeating his request. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He notes it is disputed who the leaders were, with Ubayy ibn Khalaf and Utba ibn Rabi'a named, and says the Prophet frowned and turned away because the interruption seemed untimely. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He says this happened amid hardship, mockery and obstruction in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Divine criteria beyond practical planning

- He holds that the Prophet acted with good intent, concerned with the call and not with worldly gain, and that the reproach still came to teach the community. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He argues that by planning logic one would judge the blind man a distraction from strategic goals, yet the revelation shows the eager weak believer outweighs an arrogant leader. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He gives parallel cases of Divine correction after the fact: the three who stayed behind from Tabuk and the ransom taken from the Badr prisoners against what God wished, and holds that weighing interests must be tied to what God revealed, not only to practical data. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that Ibn Ashur wrote at great length on the opening ten verses, treating them as the Prophet's own judgment corrected by revelation, and calls the passage a major gateway to the jurisprudence of weighing interests. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He holds that no leader, however experienced, may neglect divine criteria, and that reform requires understanding of religion, including knowing priorities and the central matters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### The divine origin of the Qur'an and the gentleness of reproof

- He holds that the reproach in the sura is itself a proof that the Prophet conveyed what he received and did not speak from himself, and that God teaches him both by revelation beforehand and by correction after events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates from Ibn Ashur that the opening speaks of the Prophet in the third person so that he realises only gradually it concerns him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He notes other examples of gentleness: the pardon mentioned before the reproach in al-Tawba, and the fact that al-Ahzab, which contains a severe reproach about the Zayd episode, is the sura with the most honours for the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He lists these honours as the address to him as Prophet, his priority over believers, his wives as their mothers, his place beside the five great prophets in the covenant, and the protection from harassment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that Aisha is reported to have said that had the Prophet concealed anything of revelation he would have concealed a reproach verse, and he thinks it was from al-Ahzab, citing the sound collections without naming a report. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Reading the remainder of the sura

- He explains the sura's first verses as saying that the blind man might be purified or reminded, whereas the self-satisfied leader whom the Prophet pursued is not his responsibility if he is not purified. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He says the sura then turns to the human being's ingratitude, creation, death, resurrection and the sustenance God provides, and to the scene of the Last Day, a part he says was treated at length with al-An'am. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### The Prophet's lasting honour to Abdallah ibn Umm Maktum

- He relates that after this sura the Prophet honoured Abdallah ibn Umm Maktum greatly until his death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He says he made him one of his muezzins alongside Bilal, citing the report that one called in the night and the other at dawn. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that the Prophet left him in charge of Medina when going on campaigns, several times. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He relates that Abdallah was excused from fighting, and that when a verse on those who sit out versus fighters came down he asked about himself, whereupon the phrase excusing the disabled was added. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He notes that Meccan verses also instruct the Prophet to be gentle with believers, keep company with those who call on God, and not drive them away. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}

### Lessons on attending to the eager and the humble

- He holds that confrontation with tyrants and unbelievers must not make leaders neglect the sincere believers within their ranks. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
- He holds that teachers should treat the eager seeker as more important than a noble or leader, especially a leader known for arrogance, while not abandoning the policy of winning over notable people, which the Prophet kept up to the end of his life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep29}
