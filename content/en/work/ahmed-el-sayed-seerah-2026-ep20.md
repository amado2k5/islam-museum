## Summary

The second session on the migration to Abyssinia. The speaker profiles Khalid ibn Said ibn al-As and his family from al-Dhahabi and al-Suhayli, resumes Ibn Ishaq's list of the emigrants clan by clan, then reads Umm Salama's long account of the Quraysh embassy to the Negus, Ja'far's speech and the Negus's verdict, drawing lessons on prophetic upbringing and leadership. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Recap of the Abyssinia migration and its scriptural echo

- He says the previous session covered about five lessons from the migration to Abyssinia and that this session and probably the next will still be needed to finish the event. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that Qatada took a Qur'anic verse about those who emigrated for God after being wronged as referring to the emigrants to Abyssinia, and he adds that some interpreters read the promised good in this world as the later settlement in Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that biographers count two migrations to Abyssinia, the first in the fifth year with the second some months later, and that scholars differ on why the first group came back, the best known reason being a rumour of the Quraysh accepting Islam linked to a prostration at the recitation of a sura. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Reference books used for the biographies of the early emigrants

- He explains that the series usually reads Badriyyun by Sulayman al-Awda for short identity cards of Companions with references to original sources, but that book covers only veterans of Badr, while some early emigrants to Abyssinia never fought there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He says Ibn Sa'd in al-Tabaqat al-Kubra gave a separate section to early Muslims who missed Badr, and that for this session he chose instead the book al-Shamil fi al-Ta'rif bi-al-Sahaba al-Amathil, which copies the entries of earlier authors such as Ibn Sa'd, al-Dhahabi in Tarikh al-Islam and Siyar A'lam al-Nubala', and Ibn Hajar in al-Isaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He says al-Suhayli's al-Rawd al-Unuf, a commentary on the Sira in Ibn Hisham's version, is worth owning for its language notes and for further remarks on persons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Khalid ibn Said ibn al-As and his daughter Umm Khalid

- He identifies Khalid ibn Said as an Umayyad of the Abd Shams branch, distinct from the Sahmi family of Amr ibn al-As, and relays al-Dhahabi's description of him as a noble early convert. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays reports that the Prophet appointed Khalid over Sana'a, that Abu Bakr gave him a command in the Syrian campaign, and that he died as a martyr at Ajnadayn, with Marj al-Suffar given as another place. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays a report that Khalid emigrated to Medina with Ja'far at the time of Khaybar, and the daughter's own statement that her father was the fifth to accept Islam and spent some ten years or more in Abyssinia, where she was born. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates the Bukhari story of the girl Umm Khalid being given a patterned garment by the Prophet, who spoke to her in two Abyssinian words meaning something like good, as a gentle joke, and he says she lived until about the year 90. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He says one report ties Khalid's early conversion to a dream in which he stood at a fire and found it matched the new call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### The family of Sa'id ibn al-As (Abu Uhayha) from al-Suhayli

- He argues that some Companions must be studied as families because family links explain events in the Sira and in the later caliphate and Umayyad periods, and he names the families of Sa'id ibn al-As, Uthman ibn Maz'un, Abdullah ibn Jahsh, Abu Bakr and Uthman ibn Affan among those worth tracing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He says a link between the families of Uthman and of Sa'id ibn al-As shows in the Hudaybiyya episode, where Aban ibn Said, brother of Khalid, gave Uthman protection, and that Uthman went to Mecca because no Companion was more respected among Quraysh clans. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays al-Suhayli's account that Khalid's vision showed him at the edge of a blazing fire with the Prophet holding him back, and that when he declared his faith his father beat him until a stick broke on his head, swore to cut off his support and incited his brothers, who drove him out. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays that the father, Abu Uhayha, was so esteemed in Mecca that when he wore his turban in a special way nobody else wore one like it, with verse attributed to al-Jahiz in support, and that he died in the Fijar war before the Prophet's mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays that four sons accepted Islam, Khalid, Amr, Aban and al-Hakam, whom the Prophet renamed Abdullah, while al-As, another son, died an unbeliever at Badr; he says Umar once told al-As's son he had not killed his father. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays that Umm Khalid married al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam and bore him two sons, and that Khalid is said to be the first to write the basmala in letters; the Prophet had made him governor of Sana'a and Yemen, and he declined a post from Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays a story that Abu Uhayha, ill, vowed to stop worshipping the Prophet's God if he recovered, and that his son Khalid prayed that he would not recover and he died. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Resuming Ibn Ishaq's list of emigrants: the count and Ammar

- He says he will not rush through the list of emigrants because a community should know its exemplars, though he will pause only at some names and spread the list over sessions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He recalls that Ibn Ishaq gave ten men and four women for the first migration and eighty-three for the second, and relays al-Suhayli's note that Ibn Ishaq doubted whether Ammar ibn Yasir went at all, the sounder view of al-Waqidi and Ibn Uqba being that he did not, which leaves eighty-two. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He recaps that Ibn Ishaq began with the Hashimites, naming Ja'far and his wife Asma bint Umays, then the Umayyads, naming Uthman and Ruqayya, Amr ibn Said and his wife Fatima bint Safwan, and Khalid with his wife, then allies of Banu Asad such as Abdullah and Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh, Umm Habiba and others. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Emigrants of Abd Shams, Nawfal, Asad, Abd, Abd al-Dar, Zuhra, Taym and Makhzum

- He lists from Abd Shams Abu Hudhayfa ibn Utba and, as an ally of that house, Abu Musa al-Ashari, and says scholars differ on whether Abu Musa came from Yemen directly by a ship blown off course, which he finds likelier from the Bukhari account, so that he stayed with Ja'far until arriving in Medina in the seventh year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He names Utba ibn Ghazwan as the ally counted with Nawfal, and from Banu Asad four emigrants including al-Zubayr, adding that a report says al-Zubayr had no kin among the emigrants and waited for a brother who died on the road, with a verse about dying while emigrating said to refer to that man. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He lists from Abd al-Dar, the clan that carried the Quraysh banner at Uhud, Mus'ab ibn Umayr, Suwaybit ibn Sa'd, Jahm ibn Qays with his wife and two sons, Abu al-Rum ibn Umayr and Firas ibn al-Nadr, son of the Quraysh enemy al-Nadr ibn al-Harith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He remarks that the great Quraysh elders mostly did not accept Islam early while many of their children did, and generalises that elders and the wealthy are the hardest groups to change while the young, the poor and the freed are the easiest. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He lists from Banu Zuhra Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Amir ibn Abi Waqqas and al-Muttalib ibn Azhar with his wife Ramla, who bore Abdullah in Abyssinia, and as allies Abdullah ibn Mas'ud and al-Miqdad ibn Amr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He lists from Banu Taym al-Harith ibn Khalid with his wife Rayta and four children born in Abyssinia, and Amr ibn Uthman, noting that Abu Bakr had a story with the migration which he will tell next time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He lists from Banu Makhzum Abu Salama and his wife Umm Salama, whose daughter Zaynab was born in Abyssinia, Shammas ibn Uthman, Habbar and Abdullah sons of Sufyan, Hisham ibn Abi Hudhayfa, Salama ibn Hisham, Ayyash ibn Abi Rabia and the ally Mu'attib ibn Awf, eight in all. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Al-Zuhri and the chain of the story

- He announces two themes for the topic: the story itself, and then the rulings on emigration and residence among non-Muslims, which he postpones to the next session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He notes that al-Zuhri, teacher of Ibn Ishaq and Malik, had an ancestor among the emigrants to Abyssinia, Abdullah ibn Shihab, whom Ibn Ishaq omitted and al-Suhayli supplied; he relays that this man was renamed Abdullah by the Prophet and died in Mecca after the conquest, while his brother fought at Uhud with the pagans before converting. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He lists al-Zuhri's students and teachers, among them Malik, Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Ma'mar, Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman, and calls him a small-generation Successor and a pillar of the transmission of the Sunna. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He says the chain of Ibn Ishaq runs through al-Zuhri and Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Makhzumi to Umm Salama, and that he regards the story as well supported and of Ibn Ishaq's top tier of reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Umm Salama's account: safe refuge and reconciling it with Asma

- He relates Umm Salama's account that the emigrants found in the Negus the best of neighbours, safe in their religion and free to worship without harm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He sets this beside Asma bint Umays's report in Bukhari, given after the return when Umar remarked to her that his group had more claim on the Prophet, that her group had lived in a distant hostile land in fear and harm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He holds that the two reports do not conflict because one speaks of the general condition and the other of a specific occasion, and he illustrates with a person abroad who may describe his stay as comfortable or as hard according to the question, saying that understanding context lets texts agree rather than preferring one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### The Quraysh embassy to the Negus

- He relates that when Quraysh heard of the emigrants' safety they decided to send two firm envoys with gifts, chiefly leather, for the Negus and for each of his bishops, and he names the envoys as Abdullah ibn Abi Rabia and Amr ibn al-As. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He reads the mission as planned diplomacy by Quraysh and carried out by Amr, a shrewd figure before his conversion: gifts first to the court, then to the king, and only then a request to hand the refugees over without hearing them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relays that the envoys told the bishops the refugees were foolish youths who had left their people's religion for a new one, and asked them to advise the king to surrender them since their own kin knew them best. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that the king, though the bishops agreed, became angry, refused to give up people who had chosen his protection without hearing them, and said he would return them only if their account proved the envoys right. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He notes that Meccans already knew Abyssinia as a trading destination, and he praises the king as a sovereign who thought for himself and was not led by his court. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Lesson: firm creed, flexible action

- He says the emigrants agreed to tell the king only what they had been taught and commanded by their Prophet, whatever followed, and he reads this as the fruit of a prophetic upbringing that never compromised on the content of the message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He holds that flexibility belongs to action and politics, such as treaties, truces and negotiation, and cites the Hudaybiyya treaty, an offer of part of Medina's dates to the besiegers of the Trench, accepting Abu Talib's protection, the Abyssinian refuge and the Prophet's entering the protection of Mut'im ibn Adi. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that Mut'im died an unbeliever but that the Prophet later said, regarding the prisoners of Badr, that had Mut'im lived and asked, he would have released them to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He criticises, in his own context, movements whose training is only political manoeuvre without a firm and explicit body of belief. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Jafar as leader in Abyssinia

- He relates that the king summoned his bishops with their scrolls and asked the refugees what religion had separated them from their people without joining his or any known faith, and that Ja'far ibn Abi Talib answered for them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He holds that Ja'far shows the value of leaders and of complementary roles beside the Prophet, comparing it with Abu Bakr's role in early preaching, and relays a view that Ja'far went at the Prophet's order to lead or represent the group, perhaps because as Abu Talib's son he had family protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He says life in Abyssinia was hard because new revelation and rulings did not reach them, citing a report from Ibn Mas'ud that on returning from Abyssinia the greeting given to the Prophet in prayer was no longer answered since a ruling about silence in prayer had come down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He suggests, as reflection only, that Ja'far's presence comforted the emigrants as a likeness of the Prophet in looks and character and eased the Prophet's own parting from them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that on Ja'far's arrival the Prophet said he could not tell which gave more joy, Khaybar's conquest or Ja'far's coming, and he notes that Ja'far was martyred at Mu'ta soon after, in a battle where three commanders were named in succession, after some fourteen or fifteen years abroad by his count. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Jafar's address to the Negus and the reading from Surat Maryam

- He relates that the Negus asked which religion they followed, and he reads the answer as a model of speech adapted to the listener: Ja'far described the pagan ignorance of his people, the coming of a Messenger whose lineage, truthfulness and honesty they knew, the commands to worship one God and to keep trust, kinship and good neighbourliness, the persecution they suffered, and their choice of the Negus as refuge. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He holds that clear communication means conveying what is in the mind in the manner the listener grasps, not ornate rhetoric, and says reformers, preachers, educators and politicians should learn this from Ja'far. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that the Negus asked whether Ja'far had anything of what the Prophet brought, and that Ja'far recited the opening of Surat Maryam, which the speaker thinks he chose because the Negus knew Zakariyya, Yahya, Maryam and Isa, and that he probably did not reach the part about Isa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that the Negus and his bishops wept until their beards and scrolls were wet, and that the king said this and what Musa brought came from one lamp and refused to hand the group over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### Amr's second plot about Isa and the Negus's verdict

- He relates that Amr ibn al-As resolved to return next day with something to uproot the refugees, that Abdullah ibn Abi Rabia, also an unbeliever, begged him not to because they were kin, and that Amr planned to tell the king they call Isa a servant of God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He says this touched what the Abyssinian Christians held sacred and was the heaviest trial the emigrants had met, so they again agreed to say only what God said and their Prophet brought. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that Ja'far told the king that Isa is the servant and messenger of God, his spirit and his word cast to Maryam the virgin, and that the Negus drew a line on the ground with a stick and said Isa was no more than that, to the grumbling of the bishops. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He holds that the emigrants did not know beforehand that the Negus held this belief, and that the common view among the Christians of that time was different from his. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that the king declared them safe in his land with a penalty for anyone who harmed them, returned the envoys' gifts and said God took no bribe when restoring his kingdom, so the trial became a greater security for the emigrants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}

### The rival claimant, al-Zubayr's swim and the return in stages

- He relates that a rival for the Abyssinian throne later marched on the Negus with the Nile between them, and that the emigrants feared that a ruler ignorant of their case would replace the Negus. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that al-Zubayr, among the youngest of the group, volunteered to scout, swam across the Nile holding an inflated skin on his chest, watched the fighting, and came back running to announce that the Negus had won, to great joy among the emigrants. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He holds that al-Zubayr was gifted at special missions, recalling his scouting at the Trench, his charge at the Yarmuk, and a feat at a fortress in the Egyptian conquest, and he says a mentor should discover such traits early. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
- He relates that Umm Salama came back to the Prophet while he was still in Mecca, and says the return from Abyssinia came in stages: many came to Mecca early and later fought at Badr, while those with Ja'far returned around Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep20}
