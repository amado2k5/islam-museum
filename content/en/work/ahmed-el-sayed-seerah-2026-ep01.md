## Summary

Opening lecture of a long series on the Prophet's biography that follows Sirat Ibn Hisham. He explains why he chose that book, its strengths and the work of its commentators, which sira books to combine, why the sira matters, and five aims for studying it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Opening and plan of the series

- He announces the start of a long series on the Prophet's biography, after a long delay caused by other open series and lessons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He says this is his third series on the sira: the first was aimed at the rising generation, the second at reformers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He describes the new series as extended, detailed and comprehensive, covering events, jurisprudential points, scholarly benefits and reform and spiritual lessons, where the earlier one stressed reform lessons. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Choice of Sirat Ibn Hisham as the base text

- He says a comprehensive series needs a detailed reference rather than a digest, so he adopts Sirat Ibn Hisham, whose title is Sirat Rasul Allah, and will mostly use that title for the series. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He holds that the book's rank is higher than many students credit it with. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Three founding works with no equal successors

- He names three early foundational books that, in his view, later authors in their fields did not match in level or method: al-Shafi'i's Risala for legal theory, Ibn Hisham's recension of Ibn Ishaq's sira, and al-Tabari's Qur'an commentary. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He holds that most later writers on legal theory followed a different pattern and different topics from al-Shafi'i. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He says al-Tabari paid great attention to the setting of verses in the Prophet's life, even linking verses about earlier prophets to the Prophet's own situation, and cites his comment on the verse about Joseph reaching maturity as an example; he adds that he knows no other exegete who did this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He qualifies these negative judgments as limited to his own reading. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Strengths of Sirat Ibn Hisham: verses, language, poetry

- He counts as a first strength that the book cites the verses revealed on each event and explains them, and gives the Badr section as an example, where the whole of the chapter of the spoils is reviewed after the battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates, from the book, that Ubada ibn al-Samit said the spoils verse came down about the Badr veterans' dispute over booty, which was then redistributed equally. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He says the book also explains the setting of verses about the march to Badr, the rain on the eve of the battle and the sleep that calmed the fighters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He counts as a second strength its attention to rare words in the narrations and hadiths. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He counts poetry as a third feature: no sira book collects as many verses from Quraysh, the Arabs and the Companions, and the poems alone would retell the sira. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He adds that early scholars criticised Ibn Ishaq for many unattested or forged poems, and that Ibn Hisham pruned some and omitted verses insulting the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Ibn Hisham's recension of Ibn Ishaq and the chains of transmission

- He says Ibn Hisham transmitted Ibn Ishaq's sira through Ziyad ibn Abd Allah al-Bakka'i, shortening, adding, explaining rare words and sometimes criticising Ibn Ishaq, and that scholars approved this work. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He holds that the material rests on chains of transmission: Ibn Ishaq lived in the second Islamic century, died around its middle, is said to have seen some Companions, and narrated from Successors such as al-Zuhri and from Medinese families. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He argues that reports Ibn Ishaq takes from the elders of Ansar families, such as through Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, should not be dismissed for anonymous narrators, since those families or their fathers witnessed the events and passed them down in households. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He compares this to a Palestinian family handing down the 1948 expulsion from grandfather to grandson. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Other chain-based sira sources: Ibn Sa'd, al-Waqidi, Ibn Kathir, Zad al-Ma'ad

- He names al-Waqidi's work and Ibn Sa'd's al-Tabaqat al-Kubra as chain-based sira books, saying Ibn Sa'd is more strictly chain-based than Ibn Ishaq and that the Tabaqat has weak editions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He says Ibn Kathir's al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya follows Ibn Ishaq with his chains and adds material from the hadith collections, including Ahmad's. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He says Zad al-Ma'ad is not a chain-based book but draws from chain-based ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Ibn Ishaq's prose, with the mockers and the conversion of Umar as examples

- He praises Ibn Ishaq's eloquent, well-linked prose and says reading it improves one's command of Arabic. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq describes the conversion of Umar ibn al-Khattab in a single phrase picturing him as a formidable man, and that through Umar and Hamza the Muslims gained strength against Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates a report from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud that the Muslims could not pray at the Kaaba until Umar converted, and that this came after the emigration to Abyssinia. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq lists five leading mockers of the Prophet by clan, naming al-Aswad ibn al-Muttalib, al-Aswad ibn Abd Yaghuth, al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, al-As ibn Wa'il and al-Harith ibn al-Tulatila, and links them to the verse promising protection from the mockers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates, as another example, the passage on God's will to strengthen His religion that leads into the Prophet's meeting with a group of Khazraj men at al-Aqaba, introduced by Asim ibn Umar's chain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Genealogy: Ibn Sa'd's opening and Ibn Ishaq's attention to lineage

- He says he will start the series at the mission rather than at the book's beginning, passing over the birth, Quraysh genealogy and earlier events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He describes the opening of Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat, which treats the prophets in the Prophet's lineage, the ancestors and the mothers of the Prophet, beginning with Amina bint Wahb. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He holds that Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Ishaq are unmatched in recording wives, children and clans of the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq names the first emigrants to Abyssinia by clan and lineage: Uthman ibn Affan with his wife Ruqayya, Abu Hudhayfa ibn Utba, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Mus'ab ibn Umayr, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Abu Salama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Al-Suhayli's commentary al-Rawd al-Unuf

- He says scholars did not only abridge Ibn Hisham but commented on him, and that the best known commentary is al-Suhayli's al-Rawd al-Unuf, about ten volumes against four in the edition he uses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates al-Suhayli's preface, in which the author describes explaining rare words, grammar, genealogy and legal points, apologises for falling short, and cites a proverb about not abandoning a lesser gain when the greater is missed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He remarks that al-Suhayli, like al-Tabari, ends by confessing his work is incomplete. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Method of the series

- He says the lessons will read the text with commentary, skip chapters that rest on weak or unimportant reports, and stop at verses, hadiths, chains and hadith method as needed, consulting other works for fuller treatment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He says he hopes to launch an accompanying educational programme on the sira in about six weeks, calling it a wish, not a promise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Importance of the sira: Ibn Hazm, Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn al-Qayyim

- He relates that Ibn Hazm held the Prophet's biography, for anyone who reflects on it, would suffice as proof of his prophethood even without any miracle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates that Ibn Taymiyya, in al-Jawab al-Sahih, counted the Prophet's life, character, law, community and the saintly wonders of his followers as signs of his prophethood, and he recommends that book for proofs of prophecy aimed at Christians. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He relates that Ibn Taymiyya's argument is that knowing the Prophet leads to belief, while pride can make some, like many Quraysh, deny him anyway; he notes the Prophet's early appeal to his reputation for truthfulness at al-Safa. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He recommends Zad al-Ma'ad, saying Ibn al-Qayyim's opening chapter on divine choice of persons, places and times, and his passage on humanity's need for the messengers, are among the finest writing on the subject. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He notes that Ibn al-Qayyim wrote Zad al-Ma'ad while travelling and without books, then compares scholars who apologise for their works with those who praise them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Which books to combine and later sira works

- He holds that readers need not read the great many sira books, since later narrative books mostly repeat Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He recommends taking Sirat Ibn Hisham as the base and adding the distinct strengths of other works: Zad al-Ma'ad, al-Rawd al-Unuf for language, Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Kathir for reports. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He names Abd al-Salam Harun's abridgement of Ibn Hisham as a starting point for beginners. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He praises Badhl al-Quwwa fi Hawadith Sini al-Nubuwwa by Muhammad Hashim al-Sindi, who died in 1174 AH, for arranging events year by year in the Mecca and Medina periods, with raids, expeditions and other events, and notes its date for the Mecca years counted from the Prophet's fortieth-first year. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He also names al-Sira al-Nabawiyya min khilal al-Kutub al-Sitta by Husam Abd Allah Hamsho for gathering hadiths from the six collections with comments. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}

### Five aims of studying the sira

- He counts the aims of studying the sira as increasing certainty and faith in the Prophet, increasing knowledge of the religion and of his conduct, acting and following him, and deepening understanding of the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He gives as an example of faith-strengthening the Tabuk episode where the food multiplied, and the cave scene where the Prophet reassured Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He holds that love alone is not enough and that knowledge of the Prophet's practice is needed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He argues that studying the sira helps understand the Qur'an, since verses came down on events such as Badr, Uhud, the Trench and the migration, and the Meccan chapters answer the polemic of the polytheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He says Ibn Ishaq helps by ordering the Meccan period, which is hard to date, into phases of mockery, torture and the emigration to Abyssinia and Dar al-Arqam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
- He notes that the sira and the Prophet's detailed practice in hadith are two lines of books and that following him requires both. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep1}
