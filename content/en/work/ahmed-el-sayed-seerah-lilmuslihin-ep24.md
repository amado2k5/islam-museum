## Summary

The lecturer continues from the Battle of the Trench to the siege of Banu Qurayza: the angel Jibril's summons, the terror cast into the besieged, their surrender and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh's judgment, and Sa'd's prayer and death. He then treats the marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh as the practical end of adoption, and closes with the dispute over praying the afternoon prayer on the road, which he reads as a model of accepted disagreement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Banu Qurayza after the Trench

- He says that the siege of Banu Qurayza followed the Trench directly, and that the same sura, al-Ahzab, mentions it at the end of its verses on the Trench, which he takes as proof that it came right after. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that the Jews, seeing Medina besieged by a large host and the Muslims stretched along the trench, judged it a good chance to strike from behind and shake the Muslim ranks. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says they betrayed the covenant and advanced towards the strongholds where the women and boys had been placed, and that this deepened the distress of the Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says that after about twenty-five days the polytheists were defeated, their pots overturned and tents torn up by a strong wind, and the Prophet went home, bathed and laid down his armour. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Jibril's summons and the order about the afternoon prayer

- He relates that Jibril came to the Prophet at his door, asked whether he had laid down his arms, and said the angels had not, then pointed towards the homes of Banu Qurayza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that the Prophet's instruction to the Companions to pray the afternoon prayer only at Banu Qurayza was a reflection of this urgent summons, meaning that they must set out at once. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that the Companions split, some praying on the road and some praying only on arrival, and he says each side explained the instruction differently. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Jibril's bond with the Prophet and his forms

- He holds that the Prophet's bond with Jibril was remarkable and deeply moved him, and says he would like to give one session to it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates, from a well-known report, that the Prophet was most generous in Ramadan when Jibril met him, and he reads this as evidence of the effect of these meetings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that Jibril is reported to have come in the form of Dihya al-Kalbi, a Companion, repeatedly, and that the Companions saw only that form. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says the Prophet saw Jibril in his true form only twice, once on the horizon in Mecca after the first revelation and once at the Night Journey and Ascension. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### The march and the siege of the strongholds

- He says that the homes of Banu Qurayza now lie within built-up Medina, a few kilometres south of the Quba Mosque. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says the Prophet went with about three thousand fighters and besieged them at their strongholds. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says Banu Qurayza relied on strongholds, and gives those of Khaybar as the clearest surviving example. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Terror cast into the besieged and their surrender

- He says that one of the main tasks of the angels in this campaign was to cast terror into the hearts of the Jews, which he counts as a gift of victory to the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that the same support is mentioned for Badr and Uhud, and that sura al-Ahzab mentions it for Banu Qurayza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says they held out for less than a month and then surrendered, hoping that, as with earlier Jewish groups, the Prophet would hand the verdict to one of their Ansar allies who might be lenient. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that the Prophet gave the judgment to Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, who was wounded and ill from an arrow at the Trench. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Sa'd ibn Mu'adh's judgment

- He relates that Sa'd's people carried him and tried to persuade him to be lenient because of their old alliance, and that in a report Sa'd answered that he would let no censure move him in matters of God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that when Sa'd drew near, the Prophet told those around him to stand for their leader, as a mark of honour. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says Sa'd judged that their fighters be killed and their children taken captive, and that the Prophet said he had judged by the judgment of God, with a version saying from above seven heavens. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that trenches were dug in Medina, the fighters were brought and executed, and he describes this as ending the plotting and treachery of Banu Qurayza in the city. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He notes that Banu Qurayza had betrayed once before and been pardoned, and holds that this second betrayal left no room for pardon. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Purposes of the episode and the new phase

- He holds that the episode deepened the fear in the polytheists' hearts, and that one purpose was to give the Prophet awe and strength so that others would be deterred by what befell the earlier ones. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that Islam when respected and defended deters those who are not moved by argument alone, and he cites Ibn Taymiyya on religion standing on a guiding book and a supporting sword. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that the Trench was a dividing line in the biography and that the fall of Banu Qurayza closed the earlier phase and opened a new one, in which the Muslims march against others rather than being marched against. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Sa'd's prayer and death

- He relates that after the judgment a tent was set up for Sa'd in the mosque so that his wound could be tended. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that Sa'd prayed that if any war with Quraysh remained he be kept alive for it, but if God had ended the fighting, the wound should open and his death come through it, and he reads this as showing that people knew a new phase had begun. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that blood then ran into the neighbouring tent of Banu Ghifar, that his wound had burst, and that he died as a martyr from the arrow of the Trench. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He cites a sound report that the throne of God shook at Sa'd's death, and a report in al-Bukhari that the Prophet said Sa'd's handkerchiefs in paradise were softer than silk garments shown to the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Lesson from Sa'd: late arrival and sincerity

- He says Sa'd had been a polytheist and lived only about six years as a Muslim, and compares his brief life in the faith to the sorcerers of Pharaoh, who he says held to certainty and sacrifice in a single stand. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He lists Sa'd's stands: at Badr, when the Prophet consulted the Ansar on fighting a full army and Sa'd answered well and pleased him, his leadership of the Aws, and his zeal for the Prophet in the incident of the slander. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that no one should say they came too late, since sincerity with God can reach what others did not, and he cites Ibn al-Qayyim on migration of the heart to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh and the end of adoption

- He says that right after the Trench and the affair of Banu Qurayza the Prophet married Zaynab bint Jahsh, who had been married to Zayd ibn Haritha, his adopted son. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that sura al-Ahzab, in its first page, mentions this marriage, which links it closely in time to the events, and that the sura first abolishes adoption in principle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that the marriage was the practical abolition of a long-standing Arab custom, and he says it was exceptional, concluded without a guardian because God married them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He relates that Zaynab used to boast to the other wives of the Prophet that their families had married them while God had married her from above the heavens. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says the marriage involved circumstances that may have caused the Prophet some embarrassment and a divine rebuke, and cites Aisha that had he concealed any verse, he would have concealed this one. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### The dispute over praying on the road

- He stresses that the dispute among the Companions was about the time of a central duty, the prayer, and not about a minor detail such as hand position or finger movement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says that some took the instruction literally, and names Ibn Hazm the literalist as one who said he would not have prayed except at the destination. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He says others read the instruction together with the fixed times of prayer and its purpose of haste, and prayed on the road, and that Ibn Kathir calls their position the sounder. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that what matters is that the Prophet blamed neither group. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

### Accepted and rejected disagreement

- He holds that this is the clearest event in the biography for establishing accepted disagreement in the community, since the Prophet did not decide between the two groups. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that room for such disagreement is intended by the law, so unity is not to be sought by removing it but by building awareness of how to treat disagreement and the one who disagrees. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He distinguishes two circles, accepted disagreement, to be handled calmly and left to scholars and their followers, and rejected disagreement, which he says should be refuted and distanced. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}
- He holds that treating accepted disagreement as rejected is a blameworthy division and cancels the limits of the law. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep24}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.
