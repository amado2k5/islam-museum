## Summary

The thirteenth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers treats the last Meccan events before the migration: the Prophet's presenting himself to the Arab tribes at the markets and the pilgrimage season, the six men of the Khazraj, the twelve of the following year and the seventy-odd of the second pledge of al-Aqaba, drawing on the accounts of Jabir and Ka'b ibn Malik. The speaker discusses how the pledge gave the Ansar their name, their later loyalty at Badr and Uhud, and the lessons he draws for those who work for reform today. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Opening: the last Meccan session and the Prophet's patience

- He says that this is the thirteenth session, that the previous one covered al-Ta'if and the Night Journey, and that this should be the last on Mecca before the migration, which the next one will take up. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that after ten years of patience and different approaches, soft words, encouragement and warning, the Prophet looked for a place outside Mecca that could protect the call and serve as a base for it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He recalls the story of Utba ibn Rabi'a listening to the Prophet recite a chapter and asking him to stop, as an example of the many ways the Prophet tried with Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Presenting himself at the great Arab markets

- He says that before the tenth year the Prophet already went to the large Arab markets, Ukaz, Majanna and Dhu al-Majaz, and met people in the pilgrimage season to call them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates a report from Jabir that the Prophet stood before the people at the season and asked for a man to carry him to his people because Quraysh had prevented him from delivering his Lord's message, and he says al-Tirmidhi recorded it and judged it strange but sound. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He holds that the verb used for presenting himself carries a deep meaning: the Prophet laid out what weighed on his heart and sought someone to shelter him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He stresses the contrast between the most honoured of creation and his situation, unable even to enter Mecca after al-Ta'if except under the protection of Mut'im ibn Adi, and now going to tribes such as Banu Hanifa and Banu Amir ibn Sa'sa'a and meeting refusal each time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Two purposes: calling people and seeking shelter; Rabi'a ibn Abbad and Abu Lahab

- He relates the account of Rabi'a ibn Abbad, who accepted Islam after being a pagan, that he saw the Prophet at the market of Dhu al-Majaz calling people to the declaration of faith while a squint-eyed man behind him called him a liar, and that people said this was his uncle Abu Lahab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that the account is in the Musnad of Ahmad, and that Rabi'a answered a doubt about his youth by saying he was old enough to understand. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He holds that these reports show two contexts for the Prophet's going to people outside Mecca, calling to the faith and searching for shelter, and that at the markets the call was the first aim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He notes that some markets were far from Mecca, so the Prophet travelled long distances to reach crowds, and concludes that his Meccan call was not limited to Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Ibn Ishaq on the tribes that refused

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that after returning from al-Ta'if the Prophet presented himself in the season to Banu Kalb, Banu Hanifa and Banu Amir, and that Banu Hanifa gave the worst reply of any Arab tribe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that Ibn Ishaq describes the Prophet as going to every Arab of name and standing who came to Mecca, and that none responded because God had kept this honour in store for the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Why God did not tell the Prophet who would shelter him

- He holds that God knew the tribes would refuse and that the Ansar would be the ones, yet did not tell the Prophet, so that he would take the means, search, tire and try. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He cites verses and the report of the angel of the mountains, as he understands them, to show that God saw and heard what the Prophet endured. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He draws the lesson that those who work for reform must exert every possible means and not wait for revelation to give the details, after which God blesses the steps he wishes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### The first meeting: six men of the Khazraj

- He says that the Ansar first came in the tenth or eleventh year of the mission, a small meeting with six men, all from the Khazraj, to whom the Prophet presented Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that the Aws and Khazraj descend from the Azd of Yemen, who scattered across Arabia after the flood and the dam, and that some settled at Yathrib and later became the Ansar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that the Prophet met them by chance, asked where they were from, learned they were allies of the Jews, and sat with them to speak. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that they recognised him as the prophet the Jews had spoken of, whom the Jews threatened to join against the Arab polytheists, and decided to be first to follow him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that they told the Prophet that their people were deeply divided after the war of Bu'ath, which had destroyed their leaders, and hoped God would unite them through him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that the six returned to Medina after the season, and that nothing yet showed that the Prophet would move to them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### The second year: twelve men and the count of the pledges

- He says that the next year twelve Ansar came, some of the Khazraj and some of the Aws, which he takes to mean that the first six had spread Islam until it reached the Aws. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that scholars count the pledges differently: he takes the six as the first pledge of al-Aqaba, the twelve as the second and the seventy or eighty as the third, while the twelve are best known as the pledge of al-Aqaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that the Prophet took their pledge on not associating anything with God, not stealing, not committing adultery and similar terms, and says some of these terms may belong to the later pledge. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that this reassured the Prophet that these people had what others lacked and that matters were promising. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The seventy at al-Aqaba in Jabir's account

- He relates from Jabir, in the Musnad of Ahmad, that the Prophet spent ten years following people at their camps and the seasons asking who would shelter and help him in return for paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that word spread each year, so that visitors were warned by their people against the youth of Quraysh, and the Prophet walked among them while they pointed at him with mockery. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that God then sent men from Yathrib who believed, taught the Qur'an to their families, and spread Islam until every Ansar household had Muslims. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that they asked how long the Prophet would be driven about the mountains of Mecca, and seventy men travelled and agreed to meet him at the pass of al-Aqaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Ka'b ibn Malik's account of the night at al-Aqaba

- He says that he counts the second pledge of al-Aqaba among the greatest events of the biography, that Islam before it and after it were different things, and that Ka'b ibn Malik, who missed Badr and stayed behind at Tabuk, said he would not trade this pledge for Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates from Ka'b that the Medinan pilgrims travelled with al-Bara ibn Ma'rur, their leader, did not know the Prophet by sight, and were directed by a Meccan to the man sitting with al-Abbas. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Haram was invited to Islam by the group on the way, accepted, took part in the pledge and became a chief. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that after a third of the night they slipped out quietly from among their polytheist companions to the pass, seventy men and two women, Nusayba bint Ka'b and Asma bint Amr, and he points to the women's presence at such an occasion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Al-Abbas speaks, the terms of protection and the twelve chiefs

- He relates that al-Abbas, still on his people's religion, attended to make sure of the matter for his nephew, and spoke first, telling the Khazraj that Muhammad was protected by his clan and urging them to be certain before taking him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that the Prophet then called to God, encouraged them toward Islam and asked them to protect him as they protect their women and children. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that al-Bara ibn Ma'rur took his hand and promised protection, saying they were people of war and arms inherited from their fathers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that Abu al-Haytham ibn al-Tayyihan interrupted to ask whether, once they cut their ties with others, the Prophet would return to his people if God gave him victory, and that the Prophet smiled and said he would stay with them and be one of them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that the Prophet asked them to bring out twelve chiefs, nine from the Khazraj and three from the Aws, and he sees in this a lesson on choosing the representatives of people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### The terms in Jabir's account and As'ad ibn Zurara's warning

- He relates from Jabir that the pledge included hearing and obeying in energy and laziness, spending in hardship and ease, enjoining good and forbidding wrong, speaking for God without fear of blame, and protecting the Prophet as they protect themselves and their families, in return for paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that As'ad ibn Zurara, one of the youngest, took the Prophet's hand and warned the men that taking him in meant breaking with all the Arabs and losing their best men, and urged them to be honest about any fear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that they answered that they would never abandon the pledge and then pledged to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### The weight of a pledge in that society

- He holds that the Prophet made the matter one of binding covenants rather than a smooth acceptance, and that people then knew the value of a word and a pact. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He recalls the protection given to Abu Bakr by Ibn al-Dughunna and to the Prophet by Mut'im ibn Adi after al-Ta'if as examples of how serious a promise of protection was. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### How the pledge gave the Ansar their name

- He holds that great events changed personal identity in the Prophet's time, so that the first label of a person became what he had done, Emigrant or Helper, ahead of his clan. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that before this the people were known as Quraysh, Aws and Khazraj, and inside Quraysh by clans such as Banu Hashim, Banu Adi and Banu Makhzum. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that because of the pledge the Ansar earned their name, and he relates a report in the Sahih in which a man of the Ansar said that God named them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He cites verses that mention the Emigrants and the Ansar, and the Prophet's later address to the Ansar after the distribution of spoils, as he remembers it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### Lessons: the great days of Islam and sacrifice today

- He holds that Islam has great days in which a believer may be granted a share, and that a Muslim should look for their occasions so as to be among their people, as in the report about the people of Badr and the story of Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a that he cites. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He holds that the present age is a hard stage of Islam with changed concepts, distortion of fundamentals, doubts and persecution, and that devoting life, time, money and thought to the religion's cause is now among the most demanding paths. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He cites a verse that distinguishes those who spent and fought before the conquest from those after it, to argue that the same deed differs in rank by the time of hardship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that the sacrifice needed today is mostly of time and worldly gain, for deep constructive work on concepts and on restoring trust in revelation as a reference. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### The Ansar keep the pledge: Uhud, Badr and Anas ibn al-Nadr

- He relates, from Sahih Muslim, that at Uhud when the ranks broke and the Prophet withdrew to the ravine with few men, the Ansar came forward one after another to defend him and were killed, until he said of his companions that they had not been fair to the fallen. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that before Badr, near the valley of Dhafiran, the Prophet consulted his companions and, after the Emigrants spoke, turned to the Ansar, because he feared their pledge covered only the defence of him in Medina, and they answered in a way that reassured him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that a verse about men who were true to their covenant was revealed about Anas ibn al-Nadr, who missed Badr, vowed to show God what he would do, and at Uhud pressed forward past Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and fought until he was killed, so mangled that he was not recognised. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He concludes that the Ansar fulfilled their covenant and that it was not mere words. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}

### After the pledge: Mus'ab ibn Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum in Medina

- He says that after the Ansar returned to Medina the Prophet sent Mus'ab ibn Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum, who stayed until the Prophet arrived and had a great part in teaching the people religion and the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He relates that Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, a chief of the Aws, accepted Islam at the hands of Mus'ab, and that his tribe accepted with him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
- He says that the next session will treat the Prophet's preparation for the migration, the first migrations of the believers and the Hijra itself. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep13}
