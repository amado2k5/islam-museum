## Summary

Al-Muhalla bi-l-Athar fi Sharh al-Mujalla bi-l-Ikhtisar ("The Adorned, with Reports, in Commentary on the Polished, in Brief") is a work of law by [[person/ibn-hazm|Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Hazm]] of al-Andalus, through which his [[group/zahiri-madhhab|Zahiri]] school is known. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.136} The museum's copy is the Dar Ibn Hazm edition (Beirut, 1437/2016), supervised by Khalid al-Rabbat, from some sixty-odd manuscript volumes. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.1} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.2} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.9} Its editors estimate the sayings and reports in it at some twenty thousand and more. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.10}

## Its place among Ibn Hazm's legal works

It is one of four books Ibn Hazm wrote on the lawful and the unlawful: the largest, al-Isal ila Fahm Kitab al-Khisal, which al-Dhahabi says ran to fifteen thousand leaves; then al-Khisal al-Jami'a li-Jumal Shara'i' al-Islam in two volumes; and the smallest, al-Mujalla, in one volume, short questions like a primer of Zahiri law. All are lost except al-Muhalla, from which al-Mujalla can be recovered. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.136} In his preface Ibn Hazm says his companions wished him to write a short commentary on the brief questions gathered in al-Mujalla, limited to the essentials of proof without excess; the editors quote Abu Abd al-Rahman ibn Aqil al-Zahiri's view that it is an independent work, not a literal abridgement of al-Isal. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.136} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.137}

## Its completion

Ibn Hazm wrote al-Muhalla from the start to question 2034 in this edition; his son Abu Rafi' al-Fadl completed it from his father's al-Isal. Ibn Khalil al-Abdari disliked Abu Rafi's completion because it did not take al-Mujalla as its base, and wrote al-Qadh al-Mu'alla fi Ikmal al-Muhalla, published in volume 22 of this edition. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.137}

## Method

Ibn Hazm divided it into large books and each book into questions, opening with a book setting out his creed and some questions of legal theory, then purity, prayer and the rest. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.139} His way is to state the question from al-Mujalla, introduced by "Ali said" or "Abu Muhammad said", then his proof from the Book and the Sunna, then the Companions and Followers who agreed, then the opponents, especially the Hanafis and Malikis, whose views he answers one by one; the editors note that he kept to this at the start and later relaxed it. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.139} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.140} His sources of law are four: the text of the Qur'an, the words of the Prophet soundly transmitted by trustworthy narrators or mass transmission, the consensus of the community's scholars, and an inference from these admitting only one meaning — an application of what he laid down in [[work/al-ihkam-fi-usul-al-ahkam|al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam]]. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.141} In hadith he accepts single reports as yielding both knowledge and practice and rejects disconnected and weak reports as proof; the editors quote Ibn Abd al-Hadi's and Ibn Hajar's criticism of his haste in weakening hadiths and in calling unknown narrators he did not recognise. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.151} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.152} Most of his hadiths and reports he relates with his own chains through his Andalusian teachers. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.150}

## Its standing

The editors quote al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam: "I have seen nothing in the books of Islam on knowledge like Ibn Hazm's al-Muhalla and Shaykh Muwaffaq al-Din's al-Mughni"; al-Dhahabi, that it is in eight volumes of the utmost thoroughness; and Muhammad al-Muntasir al-Kattani, that one could extract from it a volume on Ibn Hazm's law, another on the legal rulings of the Qur'an, another on those of hadith, and others on the law of the Companions and Followers. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.4} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.138} They see its importance confirmed by the Companion and Follower reports it preserves from sources many of which are lost, and hold that anomalous views in some questions do not lessen its standing. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.138} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.139}

## Scholarly attention

Works completing it include Abu Rafi's completion and Ibn Khalil's al-Qadh al-Mu'alla; refutations include Abd al-Haqq al-Ansari's al-Radd ala al-Muhalla, Ibn Zarqun's al-Mu'alla fi al-Radd ala al-Muhalla wa-l-Mujalla, Ibn al-Qattan's al-Radd ala al-Muhalla and al-Sayf al-Mujalla ala al-Muhalla, printed in India; abridgements include Abu Hayyan al-Andalusi's al-Nur al-Ajla, al-Dhahabi's al-Mustahla and one attributed to Muhyi al-Din ibn Arabi. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.155} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.156}

## Printings and manuscripts

The first printing was by Matba'at al-Nahda in Egypt in 1352 AH, in eleven volumes, overseen by Muhammad Munir al-Dimashqi; Ahmad Muhammad Shakir edited and annotated its first part, into volume 6, after which the rest was printed with less care, though the whole became known as Shakir's edition; then came the Matba'at al-Imam printing with notes by Muhammad Khalil Harras, and the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya printing edited by Muhammad Abd al-Ghaffar al-Bundari, which the Dar Ibn Hazm editors criticise. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.161} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.163} They note that the manuscripts of al-Muhalla in the Egyptian National Library are catalogued under "Hanbali law", and reject the claim, based on this, that Ibn Hazm was a Hanbali; one of the volumes bears an endowment by Sultan al-Mu'ayyad Abu al-Nasr Shaykh. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.164} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.171}
