## Summary
*Fath al-Qadir al-Jami' bayna Fannay al-Riwaya wa'l-Diraya min 'Ilm al-Tafsir* ("The Victory of the All-Powerful, Uniting the Two Arts of Transmission and Understanding in the Science of Interpretation") is a complete commentary on the Qur'an from al-Fatiha to al-Nas by [[person/al-shawkani|Muhammad ibn Ali al-Shawkani]], who died in Sana'a in 1250 AH. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.1} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11} Its title states its aim: to unite the method of the transmitters with that of the reasoners in tafsir. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.10} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11}

## The author's purpose, as his preface explains it
Al-Shawkani opens by saying that the noblest of sciences is tafsir "when it follows the recognised way in what it takes and what it gives, unmixed with any interpretation by opinion", citing al-Tirmidhi's hadith on the superiority of God's speech over all other speech. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.10} He then divides the commentators into two groups: one confined itself to transmission alone; the other turned its attention wholly to what the Arabic language and the instrumental sciences require, paying no heed to transmission, or citing it without establishing its basis. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.10} Both, he says, hit the mark and wrote at length, yet each left out something without which the work is incomplete; what is firmly established from the Prophet in tafsir must be followed, but it is little, and so he resolved to combine the two. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.10} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11} See [[concept/tafsir-bil-mathur|tafsir by transmitted report]] and [[concept/tafsir-bil-raay|tafsir by opinion]].

## His method
Al-Shawkani says that he weighs conflicting interpretations wherever he can, takes "the fullest share" in explaining the lexical, grammatical and rhetorical meaning, and is careful to give what is established in tafsir from the Prophet, the Companions, the Successors, their followers or recognised imams. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11} He states openly that he sometimes cites a report with a weak chain when something in the context strengthens it or it agrees with the Arabic sense, and sometimes cites a hadith attributed to its narrator without stating the condition of its chain because he found it so in the sources he quoted, such as the commentaries of Ibn Jarir, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti; he thinks they may have passed it on without examining the chain, and invites anyone who has those sources to examine the chains. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11}

He says that al-Suyuti's [[work/al-durr-al-manthur|al-Durr al-Manthur]] contains most of what is in the early commentaries — reports traced to the Prophet and sayings of the Companions and later generations — and that his own book contains all of it that is needed, abridging repetitions, with additions from other commentaries and from his own judgements of authenticity, sound-but-lesser grading, weakness, correction, harmonisation or preference. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11} He invites the reader to compare the commentaries of the transmitters, then those of the reasoners, and then to look at his own book "after both". {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11}

The editor of the Dar al-Wafa' edition summarises, after Dr Muhammad Hasan al-Ghumari, the order in which al-Shawkani treats the verses: whether the sura is Meccan or Medinan, its merits, the disjointed letters, language, occasions of revelation and grammar, the overall meaning, and finally the transmitted reports. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.49} Among its distinctive features the editor names its criticism of the Mu'tazili school and of some Zaydi views — though al-Shawkani was himself one of the Zaydis — and its fairness to many views of the Salafi school. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.49}

## His sources
In his preface al-Shawkani names the commentaries of Ibn Jarir, [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]], [[work/tafsir-ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] and al-Suyuti as sources he quotes, and the Dar al-Ma'rifa edition adds a section headed "His references" with notes on some of the authors he relied on, among them al-Nahhas, [[work/tafsir-ibn-atiyya|Ibn Atiyya]], author of *al-Wajiz* in tafsir, and al-Qurtubi, author of the great commentary. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.8} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.11} On the names of al-Fatiha he quotes [[work/al-kashshaf|al-Kashshaf]], and on Meccan and Medinan suras he often quotes al-Qurtubi. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.12} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.552}

## Some of its discussions
- **Where al-Fatiha was revealed:** he lists the reports used by those who said it was Meccan and by those who said it was Medinan, then the view that reconciles them by saying it came down twice; see [[question/where-was-al-fatiha-revealed|Where was Surat al-Fatiha revealed?]]. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.12}
- **Surat al-Tawba:** he quotes al-Qurtubi that it is Medinan by agreement and al-Bara' that it was the last sura revealed complete, then gives the scholars' views on why it lacks the basmala, including the report from Uthman about joining it to al-Anfal; see [[question/why-no-basmala-in-surat-al-tawba|Why does Surat al-Tawba not open with the basmala?]]. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.552} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.553}

## Manuscripts and editions
The Dar al-Wafa' edition states that the original is in the library of the Great Mosque of Sana'a in six large volumes, no. 79 tafsir, under the title Matla' al-Badrayn wa-Majma' al-Bahrayn, and that the work printed as Fath al-Qadir is that same manuscript, not another book. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.43} At the end of the Dar al-Ma'rifa edition a note records that the book "was completed by audition to its author" on Monday morning 5 Rabi' I 1241 AH, written by Yahya ibn Ali al-Shawkani. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.1672} The same edition is prefaced by the chain of its transmission from Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Yahya Zabara al-Hasani back to the judge Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Shawkani (d. 1281 AH), from his father the author. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.10}

The museum used two editions: the one-volume Dar al-Ma'rifa (Beirut) printing prepared by Yusuf al-Ghush, 4th edition 1428 AH / 2007; and volume 1 of the Dar al-Wafa' edition by Dr Abd al-Rahman Umayra, who says his chief concern, after consulting the manuscripts and printings, was referencing the hadith and reports. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.2} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.1} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-wafa-v1 p.62}
