## Summary
Nukhbat al-Fikar fi Mustalah Ahl al-Athar is a concise treatise on hadith terminology by [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani]] (773–852 AH), the base text of his commentary [[work/nuzhat-al-nazar|Nuzhat al-Nazar]]. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 36}

## Why he wrote it
After listing the works on hadith terminology from al-Ramahurmuzi to [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] and those written on [[work/muqaddimat-ibn-al-salah|his book]], Ibn Hajar says a friend asked him to sum up what mattered, "so I summed it up in a few slender leaves, which I called Nukhbat al-Fikar fi Mustalah Ahl al-Athar, in an order I devised and on a path I took, together with the stray gems and extra benefits I added to it". {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 33-36} The editor, [[person/nur-al-din-itr|Nur al-Din 'Itr]], observes that the aim was not merely the brevity that "slender leaves" suggests, but a new arrangement of the discipline and a method of its own. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 18}

## Its arrangement
The treatise proceeds by "probing and division": it first divides reports by their chains into those with unlimited chains ([[concept/al-hadith-al-mutawatir|mutawatir]]), those with a limited number above two ([[concept/al-hadith-al-mashhur|mashhur]]), those with two ([[concept/al-hadith-al-aziz|'aziz]]) and those with one ([[concept/al-hadith-al-gharib|gharib]]), then divides solitary reports into accepted and rejected and branches each. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 19} {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 36-47} The base text does not spell out the conditions of mass transmission; Ibn Hajar explains that the mutawatir is not a subject of the science of chains. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 41}

## Commentary and editions
The author himself explained it, at the same friend's request, in Nuzhat al-Nazar, merging the base text into the commentary. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 36} In the Maktabat al-Bushra printing (Karachi 2011) the base text is marked in a distinct colour within the commentary and is not repeated at the end, unlike 'Itr's edition, which gave it separately for memorisation. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 24}
