## Summary
*Al-'Ubudiyya* ("Servanthood") is an answer [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] wrote to a question about [[passage/quran-2-21|al-Baqara 21]], which commands people to worship their Lord: what is worship and what are its branches? Does the whole of religion fall within it? What is the reality of servanthood? Is it the highest station in this world and the next, or is there a station above it? {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 149} It stands in volume 10 of [[work/majmu-al-fatawa|Majmu' al-Fatawa]] after *Amrad al-Qulub wa-Shifa'uha*. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 772}

## The definition of worship
The answer opens with a definition that became well known: worship is "a comprehensive name for everything God loves and is pleased with, of inner and outer words and deeds". {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 149} He counts within it prayer, zakat, fasting and pilgrimage; truthful speech, returning trusts, kindness to parents, keeping ties of kinship and keeping covenants; commanding right and forbidding wrong, and jihad; kindness to the neighbour, the orphan, the poor, the wayfarer, the slave and animals; supplication, remembrance and recitation; and then the works of the heart: love of God and His Messenger, awe of Him and turning to Him, making religion sincerely His, patience with His judgement, gratitude for His blessings, contentment with His decree, reliance on Him, hope for His mercy and fear of His punishment. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 149} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 150} He makes worship the end beloved to God for which He created creation, with which He sent all the messengers, as Noah, Hud, Salih and Shu'ayb said to their peoples, by which He described His angels, His prophets and the elect of His creation, and for refusing which He blamed the arrogant. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 150} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 151}

## What is God's alone and what is God's and His Messenger's
Ibn Taymiyya distinguishes what is given to God and His Messenger together, such as obedience, pleasing and giving, from what belongs to God alone, such as worship and what goes with it – reliance and fear – and being "sufficient" (hasb). He calls it a gross error to read [[passage/quran-8-64|al-Anfal 64]] as making the believers, together with God, sufficient for the Prophet. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 154}

## The two senses of "servant"
The answer sets out that "servant" ('abd) first means the one made subject (mu'abbad), whom God has humbled, governed and directed; in this sense all creatures are God's servants, the righteous and the wicked, believers and unbelievers, for He is their Lord and Owner and they do not escape His will and power. The difference is that the people of faith knew this and acknowledged it, while knowledge combined with arrogant refusal of the truth becomes a torment to its possessor. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 154} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 155} The second sense of "servant" is the worshipper ('abid), who worships God and none but Him: the particular, religious servanthood around which the answer turns. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 157}

## The creature's perfection lies in servanthood
He holds that the perfection of a creature lies in realising its servanthood to God; the more a servant realises it, the greater his perfection and the higher his rank; and whoever imagines that a creature can leave servanthood in any respect, or that leaving it is more perfect, is in his view among the most ignorant and misguided of creatures. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 176} He counts reliance and seeking help as part of worship, singled out for mention only because they are the help for all its other kinds. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 176}

## Freedom and servitude of the heart
He cites the hadith "Wretched is the slave of the dirham, wretched is the slave of the dinar" to show that one whose heart clings to wealth becomes its slave. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 180} He holds that the captivity of the heart is greater than that of the body: a Muslim captured by an unbeliever or wrongfully enslaved by a wicked man is not harmed if he does what obligations he can, whereas one whose heart is enslaved to other than God is harmed even if outwardly he is king over people. He sums it up: "Freedom is the freedom of the heart, and servitude is the servitude of the heart." {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 186}

## Intimate friendship, love and annihilation
According to the volume's table of contents the answer goes on to people's differing degrees in worship, faith and love; to arrogance as contrary to servanthood; to intimate friendship (khulla) and love as the realisation of servanthood; and to servanthood not being mere humility without love. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 772} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 773} It then sets out the three kinds of [[concept/fana-sufi|annihilation (fana')]]: annihilation from willing anything but God, annihilation from witnessing anything else, and annihilation from the existence of anything else. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 218} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 219} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 223}

## Its place
The answer joins the chapter of tawhid with the chapter of the spiritual path: worship for him covers both the outward laws and the works of the heart, and no creature rises above servanthood. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 149} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 176}
