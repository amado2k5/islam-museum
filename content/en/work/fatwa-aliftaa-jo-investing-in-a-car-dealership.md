## Summary

A question on giving money to a brother who owns a car dealership to trade with in return for a share of the profit, which Jordan's General Iftaa Department classified as mudaraba. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3803}

## The question

The questioner said his brother has a car showroom selling for cash and on instalments, and that he gives him money to put to work there in return for a percentage of the profit; he asked whether this is forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3803}

## The answer

The Department answered that this is a form of mudaraba, in which the owner of capital gives money to someone who trades with it, the profit being shared at an agreed ratio, quoting the definition from Ibn Hajar al-Haytami's Tuhfat al-Muhtaj. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3803} It required that each party's share be a proportion of the profit actually made: a fixed sum for either party, or a percentage of the capital, is not allowed, because that amounts to guaranteeing the capital and a fixed return. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3803} It concluded that sharing the realised profit at the agreed ratio is permitted, while tying the return to the amount paid in with the capital guaranteed is forbidden riba. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3803}
