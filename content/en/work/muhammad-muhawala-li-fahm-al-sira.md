## Summary

*Muhammad: Muhawala li-Fahm al-Sira al-Nabawiyya* ("Muhammad: An Attempt to Understand the Prophet's Biography") is a book by the Egyptian writer Mostafa Mahmoud; the copy used here is the fifth edition from Dar al-Ma'arif, Cairo, legal deposit number 1985/4792 {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 3} {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 98}. What follows sets out the author's views, attributed to him.

## The chapters

By its table of contents the book has five chapters: Muhammad; Not greatness but prophethood; A radiant, captivating spirit; A march like a hurricane; Muhammad, the maker of men {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 95}.

## The thesis: a new jahiliyya

The author opens by saying that in the twentieth century time has returned "to its first state" and that the jahiliyya has again wrapped people round, a jahiliyya he calls "denser and coarser" than the first {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 9}. He refers to [[passage/quran-2-213|al-Baqara, verse 213]] when speaking of mankind as one community {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 9}.

## Against "a new kind of sira book"

In this age, he writes, "a new kind of sira book has appeared" that strips the Prophet of the unseen; some of it made him "something like Guevara" and Islam something like a social revolution {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 10}, or spoke of him as one speaks of Abraham Lincoln {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 12}. He calls this a shortsighted understanding, because "the essence of prophethood is not reform or building" {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 12} but "the mysterious link with God and His hidden unseen", an in-between (barzakh) state between nature and what lies beyond it {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 13}.

## Prophet, saint and social reformer

He distinguishes three figures. The prophet receives from his Lord "the word, the law and the charge" and is protected from error; the saint has only "a moment of transparency", fleeting, with no protection from error, no charge and no mission to convey; the social reformer is a person of personal effort like us, who errs and is right and never leaves the realm of the senses {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 11} {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 12}. He cites [[passage/quran-18-110|al-Kahf, verse 110]] for the Prophet's humanity together with revelation {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 11}, and [[passage/quran-2-3|al-Baqara]] and [[passage/quran-4-136|al-Nisa', verse 136]] on belief in the unseen {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 12}.

## His proof of prophethood

Genius, he argues, is excelling in a single quality, while the meeting of every perfection in one person is "a miracle, not genius"; he calls this "my proof of Muhammad's prophethood" and repeats: "we are not, then, before Abraham Lincoln, nor before Guevara" {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 25}.

## The author as the edition presents him

The edition ends with a list of fifty-four of the author's books, which records that his novel *Rajul taht al-sifr* ("A Man Below Zero") won the State Prize for 1970 and that his complete works were published in Beirut in 1972 {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 98}. See [[person/mostafa-mahmoud|Mostafa Mahmoud]] and [[work/al-quran-muhawala-li-fahm-asri|The Qur'an: An Attempt at a Modern Understanding]].

## For young visitors

Mostafa Mahmoud wrote this book to say that the Prophet Muhammad was not just a clever man or a reforming leader, but a prophet who received revelation from God, which in his view is what sets him apart from other great people {cite:mm-muhammad-maarif-ed5 p. 12}.
