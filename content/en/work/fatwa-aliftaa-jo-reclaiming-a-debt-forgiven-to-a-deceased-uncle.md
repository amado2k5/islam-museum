## Summary

A question about reclaiming a debt forgiven to a deceased uncle, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that once a creditor releases his debtor from a debt by any wording that conveys it, and the conditions of release are met, he may not later go back and claim the debt, since what has been dropped does not return. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3651}

## The question

The heirs asked about a man who was owed 15,000 dinars by his uncle; when the uncle died, he announced publicly at the condolence gathering, before witnesses, that he forgave the whole debt. They accepted the forgiveness and divided the estate, but five years later he began demanding the money. Must they collect it for him? {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3651}

## The answer

The Department answered that if a creditor releases his debtor from a debt by any wording that conveys this, whether forgiving it or releasing it, he has no right to go back and claim the debt he has dropped. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3651} It said that a valid release requires the person releasing to know the amount of the debt in detail, to be legally responsible and competent to make gifts, and not to be joking or acting under coercion; if these conditions are met, the release is valid. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3651} It relied on the legal maxim that what has been dropped does not return, quoting Durar al-Hukkam fi Sharh Majallat al-Ahkam (1/54) that whoever drops a right he is entitled to drop loses it for good, so if someone forgives a debt and later regrets it, he may not claim it from the debtor, whose liability has been discharged. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3651} It concluded that if the matter is as described in the question, the creditor has no right to go back on the debts he forgave his uncle. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3651}
