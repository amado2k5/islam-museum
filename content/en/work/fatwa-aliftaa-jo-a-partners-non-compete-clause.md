## Summary

A question about a partner stipulating that the other partner may not set up a competing business during or after the partnership, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the condition is valid and binding during the partnership but void and without effect after it ends, while a former partner still may not harm the company or take its clients. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3982}

## The question

The questioner asked about the ruling on one partner stipulating that the other may not compete by setting up a business similar to the company's, during the partnership or after the partnership contract ends. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3982}

## The answer

The Department answered that among the established rules is that the contract is the law of the contracting parties, and that a partner stipulating a recognised condition serving a real purpose, such as not setting up a similar business during the partnership, is a valid condition that must be kept, citing a hadith reported by al-Tirmidhi that Muslims are bound by their conditions except a condition that forbids the permitted or permits the forbidden. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3982} It said this condition is valid because it secures a benefit for one party and protects him from harm. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3982} It held a condition barring competition after the partnership contract has been dissolved to be void and without effect, because it restricts the partner's right without just cause, citing the agreed-upon hadith that any condition not in the Book of God is void. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3982} It noted that after the partnership ends a partner still may not harm his former company or take its clients, relying on the hadith reported by Ahmad that there is to be no harm and no reciprocal harm. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3982}

## About this record

Jordan's General Iftaa Department published this fatwa on aliftaa.jo as no. 3982, dated 3 July 2025. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3982} The museum records this text as a historical document attributed to the body or scholar that issued it; it issues no ruling of its own.
