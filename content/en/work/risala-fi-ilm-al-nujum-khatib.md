## Summary
"Risala fi ilm al-nujum" ("Treatise on the Science of the Stars") by [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib al-Baghdadi]] (d. 463 AH), the hadith scholar and historian of Baghdad, is one of the sources closest in time to [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]]'s [[work/al-haya-al-saniyya-suyuti|al-Hay'a al-saniyya]]. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 34} Heinen deliberately renders ilm al-nujum loosely, since a clear-cut distinction between astronomy and astrology is foreign to the text. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 34}

## Manuscript
The treatise survives in a single manuscript in the Süleymaniye Library, Istanbul (Atıf Efendi 190); to Heinen's knowledge it had never been published or translated, though several authors had described and quoted it, not always correctly. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 34}

## Contents
From the outset al-Khatib distinguishes an acceptable branch of the science, rooted in the Qur'an, the Sunna and the sayings of Companions, Successors and later scholars, from a branch to be rejected. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 35} The acceptable branch includes star names, their appearances, risings, settings and courses, guidance by them, the Bedouins' movements by their seasons, knowledge of rain by the rain-stars, finding the qibla, and knowing prayer times and the hours of the night. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 35} Heinen suggests it may have been al-Suyuti's chief formal inspiration, though he quotes it only once. {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 34} {cite:heinen-islamic-cosmology p. 35}
