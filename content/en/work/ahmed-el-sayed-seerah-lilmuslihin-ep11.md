## Summary

The eleventh session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers returns to Mecca: Quraysh's plot to kill the Prophet, Abu Talib's withdrawal with Banu Hashim to the quarter, the three-year social and economic siege and its cancellation, then the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadija in the Year of Sorrow. The speaker explains the Khaybar share for kin, recounts Abu Talib's deathbed and draws lessons on God's right, intercession and bad company, then dwells on Khadija's standing. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Where the series stands and the new trial

- He says that the series is still in the Meccan period, around the seventh year of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that the two previous sessions dealt with the migration to Abyssinia and with Umm Salama's account of what passed between the Negus and the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that this session returns to Mecca and treats a new trial of the Prophet's call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Quraysh plot to kill the Prophet and Abu Talib withdraws to the quarter

- He relates, following al-Zuhri, that after every other measure had failed Quraysh turned its full weight toward killing the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that Abu Talib, who had been defending the Prophet, learned of this and decided to retire to the quarter of Banu Hashim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He places that quarter near the Masa, on the side of the mountain when one leaves the walkway going forward. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that once the clan was inside and Quraysh knew it could not reach the Prophet, it chose to press them from every side so that he would be forced out and killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that this action is known as the siege of the quarter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### The nature and terms of the siege

- He says that the siege was not an army surrounding the quarter to stop all entry and exit, but a social and economic pressure meant to deny the Prophet and his clan the ease of earlier years. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that the Prophet and the Hashimites who defended him had been trading and going to the markets, and Quraysh now wanted to tighten the grip on his sheltering circle so that they would hand him over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that the Prophet described the pact as one sworn on disbelief, and he ties this to a report from a pilgrimage season about camping at a place of Banu Kinana where Quraysh had sworn that oath. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that the terms barred marriage with Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, barred selling to them or buying from them, and had the Meccans buy up arriving caravan goods first so the clan could not obtain them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that conditions grew severe, to what could be called a famine, with social and psychological hardship as well, while the Prophet was consoled by the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Who was in the quarter, its length and its end

- He says that those defending the Prophet in the quarter were not only Muslims but also polytheist kinsmen from Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, and he notes that the second clan is al-Muttalib, not Abd al-Muttalib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that biographers give the duration as three years, which does not mean no one could ever come or go but that the unjust terms stood that long. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that the pact document was hung inside the Kaaba to secure it, since they honoured the Kaaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that after three years a group of Quraysh notables, among the least hostile and likened to the kind of man Umm Salama had called the more God-fearing of two envoys, objected that it was wrong to live comfortably while their cousins suffered. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that they agreed to cancel the unjust pact, did so, and the siege ended, with the Prophet and those with him leaving the quarter, around the tenth year of the mission. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### The deaths of Abu Talib and Khadija and the Year of Sorrow

- He says that Abu Talib died in the same year the Prophet left the quarter, and cites Ibn Ishaq for the phrase that Abu Talib had been tender toward his nephew. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that Khadija died soon after, and that Abu Talib had been the outer support of the Prophet while Khadija was the inner support. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that biographers called this the Year of Sorrow, but the Prophet did not withdraw to his house or abandon the call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that Quraysh grew bolder against him after Abu Talib's death, especially, than they had dared before. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He mentions the physical abuse covered earlier, namely the camel-offal incident and the strangling by Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, from a report in al-Bukhari, and says some scholars hold that such physical harm came after Abu Talib's death, though no explicit evidence says so and it is a deduction. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The Khaybar share for kin and the genealogy of Banu Hashim

- He says that in the seventh year after the migration, at the distribution of Khaybar's spoils, the Prophet gave a share of the spoils to Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib and not to Banu Abd Shams or Banu Nawfal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He lays out the line of the Prophet as Muhammad son of Abdullah, son of Abd al-Muttalib, son of Hashim, son of Abd Manaf, and names Hashim's brothers al-Muttalib, Abd Shams and Nawfal. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that Banu Umayya all belong to Banu Abd Shams, that Mut'im ibn Adi, who gave the Prophet protection, and his son Jubayr belong to Banu Nawfal, and that Ubayda ibn al-Harith, who fought in single combat at Badr, belongs to Banu al-Muttalib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that Uthman, of Banu Abd Shams, and Jubayr ibn Mut'im, of Banu Nawfal, came to the Prophet and asked why he favoured Banu al-Muttalib when they stood in the same closeness to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that the Prophet answered that Banu al-Muttalib and Banu Hashim are one thing, because since pre-Islamic times they had been bound by closeness and alliance, entered the siege and defended him, whereas the other two clans were with the besiegers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that this shows how the siege shaped a tie beyond bloodline, and that the share of the kin in the Qur'anic verse on spoils goes to Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, which is the view of al-Shafi'i. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Abu Talib's long ode of complaint and pledge

- He says that Abu Talib has a famous long ode rhyming in lam, which Ibn Kathir judged weightier than the mu'allaqat and which, he says, could only be by Abu Talib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that in it the poet complains, grieves and threatens, and sets out his pledges never to hand the Prophet over. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that the ode calls down swift retribution on Abd Shams and Nawfal, which he takes as supporting the point that those two clans stood against the besieged. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### The death of Abu Talib

- He says that Abu Talib spent his life from the open call until his death defending the Prophet and bearing harm and hard words, yet did not enter his religion. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from Sahih al-Bukhari, that when death came the Prophet sat by his head, with Abu Jahl and Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya present, and urged him to say the declaration of faith so that he could plead for him with God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that nothing mattered more to the Prophet at that moment. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that the two men asked Abu Talib whether he would turn from the creed of Abd al-Muttalib, and that this exchange was repeated. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that Abu Talib said he would have said it to please the Prophet were it not for fear that Quraysh would say he spoke it from fear of death, and that he died refusing, on the creed of Abd al-Muttalib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that the Prophet said he would seek forgiveness for him unless forbidden, and that verses then came forbidding forgiveness for polytheists even if kin and saying that the Prophet cannot guide whom he loves, for God guides whom He wills. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Lessons: faith, not sentiment, decides the matter

- He says that some people dislike this account in the two Sahihs, and that he names the Shia as the best known of them, claiming Abu Talib is in Paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He holds that Paradise is in God's hand and not in anyone's, and that the account does not please Muslims; nothing in the biography would please them more than Abu Talib having become Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He holds that the matter is one of religion: God accepts no deed from one who has not submitted to Him and affirmed that there is no god but Him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from Sahih Muslim, that Aisha asked whether the kindness of Ibn Jud'an, a Qurayshi noble who kept kin ties in pre-Islamic times, would benefit him, and that the Prophet answered that he never asked for forgiveness on the Day of Judgement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that the Prophet was also asked whether pre-Islamic charity counts if one becomes Muslim, and answered that one embraces Islam on the strength of past good. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He draws the lesson that religion has no favours, patronage or intermediaries. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### What the Prophet's help did for Abu Talib

- He holds that had anyone been able to rescue another from God's punishment, the Prophet would have done so for Abu Talib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from Sahih al-Bukhari, that the Prophet benefited Abu Talib only by lightening his punishment, so that he is in a shallow part of the Fire and would otherwise be in its lowest depth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates a further report that Abu Talib has the mildest punishment of the people of the Fire, and says that even that is enough to make one flee from it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that only a Muslim soul enters Paradise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that the declaration of faith is the greatest blessing, which many do not value, and that whoever neglects it and does not thank God for it is a loser. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Intercession and the right of God

- He cites a verse about the grateful and a verse about those who claim a favour by embracing Islam, and says some scholars take the grateful as those who value the blessing of faith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He holds that the question of Abu Talib is one of creed bound up with God's right, which is not set aside for lineage or closeness to the righteous. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that once a person brings the essential condition, the intercession of those whom God permits may benefit him on the Day of Judgement. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from the Sahih collections, that some people of the declaration who did not keep its right are sent to the Fire, that believers then intercede for those they know, and that God brings them out. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that when the intercessors have finished God will bring out of the Fire everyone who said the declaration, leaving only those who stay there forever. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Both sides of the matter: God's right and the Prophet's right

- He holds that the case shows that the first and greatest right in the religion belongs to God, and that the messengers, though the noblest of creatures, are only bearers of what God sends. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that, on the other side, it shows the Prophet's standing and the merit of supporting him, since Abu Talib, though a polytheist who neglected God's right, had his punishment lightened for his support of the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He draws that, for God's right, Abu Talib did not enter Paradise nor leave the Fire, while for the Prophet's right his punishment became the lightest of all. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He argues that one who spends a life supporting the Prophet and his Sunnah while affirming the declaration of faith should hope for a great reward from God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### The danger of bad company

- He says that the story of Abu Talib alone shows the danger of bad companions, since Abu Jahl and Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya pressed him at that moment against putting God's right first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He adds a Qur'anic passage about the wrongdoer who wishes he had not taken a certain man as friend, and a report likening a bad companion to a bellows operator. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says that young people being trained as future reformers should not compromise on companionship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}

### Khadija: memory, standing and last words of the lecture

- He says that after Khadija's death the Prophet kept remembering her worth, including the nights he came home having heard abuse and accusation from the polytheists and she comforted him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that Aisha, who married him after Khadija's death, said she felt jealousy toward no one as she did toward Khadija, because of how often he recalled her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that Aisha once said something dismissive of Khadija as an old woman of Quraysh, and says Khadija had a role no other wife had. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari, that when Hala bint Khuwaylid, Khadija's sister, asked to enter, the Prophet was reminded of Khadija and was startled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates that the Prophet used to send charity to Khadija's close friends in her memory. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He notes that scholars differ over whether Khadija or Aisha was the better, but says Khadija had a distinction no other wife of his reached fully. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He relates, from al-Bukhari and Muslim, that Gabriel told the Prophet Khadija was coming with food and drink, that she was to be greeted with peace from her Lord and from him, and given news of a house of hollow pearl in Paradise free of noise and toil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
- He says the Prophet lost, with her death, the inner support, as he had lost the outer protection in Abu Talib, and that the next session will treat what followed and what he then sought. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep11}
