## Summary

A question about sports that involve blows to the head, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that whatever is proven harmful is proven forbidden, so such sports are forbidden when they harm the person struck, unless the precautions needed to prevent injury, such as helmets and protective clothing, are taken, in which case there is no blame in them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3799}

## The question

The questioner asked about the ruling on sports in which blows are struck to the head. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3799}

## The answer

The Department answered that Islam encourages Muslims to practise sport, especially activities of high value in giving the body fitness, skill and health, and forbids whatever harms the body, such as blows to the face and head, whose danger, and the concussion or death that may follow, has been established. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3799} It cited the hadith in [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] in which the Prophet forbade striking the face, saying that whoever fights should avoid the face, and the reasoning that [[person/badr-al-din-al-ayni|Badr al-Din al-Ayni]] quotes in [[work/umdat-al-qari|Umdat al-Qari]] (13/116) from [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]]: the face is delicate, gathers a person's beauty, and is the seat of most perception through its organs, so a blow to it risks disabling or disfiguring all or part of it, and a blemish there is gross because it is exposed and visible. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3799} It ruled that it is a principle of sharia that whatever is proven harmful is proven forbidden, blows to the face and head being among such things, so these sports are forbidden in sharia if they cause harm or injury to the person struck, unless the measures needed to protect the players from injury are taken, such as wearing a helmet and clothing that protects against blows that would harm any part of the body, and similar precautions, in which case there is no blame in practising them. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3799}
