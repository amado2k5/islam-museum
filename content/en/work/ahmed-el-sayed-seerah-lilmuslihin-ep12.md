## Summary

The twelfth session of the series on the Prophetic biography for reformers covers what followed the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadija: the Prophet's journey to al-Ta'if and its rejection, why he counted that day the hardest of his life, the angel of the mountains and his hope for the descendants of his enemies, the jinn who listened to the Qur'an, and his return to Mecca under the protection of Mut'im ibn Adi. The speaker draws lessons on wisdom in dealing with non-Muslims, then gives a short account of the Night Journey and the Ascension, Quraysh's reaction and the place of prayer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### After Abu Talib and Khadija: protection lost and harm increased

- He says that the previous session treated the death of Abu Talib and its lessons, and that an earlier one treated Khadija and her support for the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that the main protection Abu Talib provided ended with his death, though al-Abbas tried to give some cover afterwards, as shown by what he said to the Ansar at the pledge of al-Aqaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that al-Abbas did not carry the weight in Quraysh that Abu Talib had. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that Quraysh grew bolder after Abu Talib died, and that some scholars place the strangling of the Prophet and the camel entrails thrown on him after that death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the Prophet, under this pressure, looked for another land that could hold his call, and turned to al-Ta'if. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Offering himself to the leaders of al-Ta'if

- He says that the Prophet described his approach to the sons of Abd Yalil ibn Abd Kulal with a phrase meaning he offered himself, and that this shows he spent everything he had. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that these men were among the great lords of al-Ta'if, and that one commentary view names an Abd Yalil as one of the great men of the two towns meant in a verse. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He explains that the Prophet's aim was to ask whether those who refused him could at least shelter and protect him so that he could deliver his Lord's message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the Prophet reported in Sahih al-Bukhari that they did not answer what he wanted. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### Aisha's question: the hardest day

- He says that people might name Uhud as the hardest day, for the wounds to the Prophet's face, the broken tooth and the deaths of Hamza, Mus'ab ibn Umayr and many believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that others might name the day of the camel entrails, or the Year of Sorrow with the deaths of Khadija and Abu Talib. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari that Aisha asked the Prophet whether any day had been harder for him than Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that the Prophet answered that the hardest thing he met from his people was the day of al-Aqaba, when he offered himself to Ibn Abd Yalil and was not answered. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He identifies that day with the visit to al-Ta'if, where the Prophet sought shelter from the nobles, was refused, and they set their fools against him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### Why that day was the hardest: context and verbal harm

- He holds that one understands the day only by joining two things: the stage of the mission it fell in, and what the Prophet felt inside. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the Prophet was then in about the tenth year, after years of denial, mockery and abuse of his Companions in Mecca, whereas Uhud came after the victory at Badr, the death of Abu Jahl, a place of refuge in Medina and a war of alternating fortunes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that verbal harm hurts a noble and dignified person more than physical beating, and cites a man in al-Ta'if who asked whether God had found no one else to send. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the Qur'an mentions the mockery and the accusations of madness and sorcery against earlier messengers far more often than it mentions beating or torture, though those occurred. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He concludes that the Prophet's own words mark that day as the hardest of his life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### The visit to al-Ta'if and its refusal

- He says that the Prophet went up to al-Ta'if on foot, a hard climb to a city on a high mountain, though no report dwells on the road. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that it is said Zayd ibn Haritha was with him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the books of the sira give more detail than al-Bukhari, including a report that he recited Surat al-Tariq among other things and spoke with them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that one man asked whether God had found no one else, and another said he would never speak to him, since if he was truthful he was too great to answer and if he lied he was not worth answering. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates from the sira that the Prophet asked them to keep the matter secret, and that some scholars explain this as his wish that the news not reach Mecca and make Quraysh bolder, since the two cities were closely linked. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that they nonetheless incited their fools and boys, who threw stones at him until he bled. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### Qarn al-Tha'alib and the angel of the mountains

- He relates from al-Bukhari that the Prophet left al-Ta'if grieved and came to himself only at Qarn al-Tha'alib, which most scholars identify with Qarn al-Manazil, the pilgrim station. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that a cloud shaded him, that Jibril appeared and told him God had heard his people's words, and that the angel of the mountains greeted him and offered to carry out whatever he commanded. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He explains that the angel offered to close the two mountains on the people of Mecca, not of al-Ta'if, because everything in al-Ta'if had come from Mecca's leaders, which he takes as showing how much context matters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that most scholars take the two mountains to be those beside the sanctuary, Abu Qubays and Qu'ayqi'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### The Prophet's hope for their descendants

- He relates that the Prophet declined the offer and said he hoped God would bring from their loins people who worship God alone. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that this shows a man looking to the future, who saw that some of those unbelievers would pass to faith, and that such moments are evidence of prophethood meant to strengthen believers, not to answer atheists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He cites Ikrima, son of Abu Jahl, as a leading horseman of Islam who fell in the conquests after the Prophet's death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He cites Umm Kulthum bint Uqba, daughter of the man who laid the camel entrails on the Prophet, as one of the good female Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He adds that many leaders of Quraysh themselves accepted Islam later, that Mecca entered Islam in full, and that it was among the few regions that did not abandon the faith after the Prophet's death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### The jinn who listened: timing and mission to the jinn

- He relates that on the way back to Mecca the Prophet was praying and God turned a group of jinn to listen to the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the report is in the Qur'an and in the two Sahihs, but that scholars differ on when it happened. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that some place it on this return from al-Ta'if, while al-Bukhari has the Prophet going out to the market of Ukaz with a group of Companions, and another occasion at Mecca is linked with Abdullah ibn Mas'ud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that the Prophet was sent to humankind and to the jinn alike, and that both are accountable. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Wadi Nakhla, the verses on the jinn and the link with Moses

- He says that the event took place at Nakhla, and that there are two parallel valleys between Mecca and al-Ta'if, Nakhla al-Yamaniya in the south and Nakhla al-Shamiyya in the north. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the Yamaniya valley saw several events of the sira, including the jinn listening and the sending of a Companion to cut down the idol al-Uzza. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that God recorded the jinn episode in more than one place, in a whole sura named after them and in Surat al-Ahqaf. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He counts the jinn's remark about a book revealed after Moses as the third link between the Prophet and Moses, after Waraqa ibn Nawfal and the Negus. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that the relation between the Prophet and Moses is a notable theme of the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### Return to Mecca under Mut'im ibn Adi's protection

- He says that after Abu Talib's death the Prophet feared that entering Mecca would bring severe harm, so he looked for someone to grant him protection. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates from the sira that he sent word to several men who did not answer, until Mut'im ibn Adi, father of Jubayr ibn Mut'im, ordered his sons to bear arms and gave him protection while still an unbeliever. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the Prophet kept this favour in mind, and relates from a sound report that after Badr he said that had Mut'im been alive and asked him for the captives he would have released them to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He notes that Mut'im died an unbeliever shortly after the Hijra and before Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He draws a lesson for reformers, that the Prophet never flattered anyone in matters of religion and called even his supporting uncle a man of the Fire, yet he was prudent in politics and in guarding rights. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that the Prophet treated people differently, knowing that some opponents harboured no hatred of the faith but clung to their fathers' religion and some honoured tribal pacts, and that a reformer should not make every person an enemy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He adds that the Prophet also hired a non-Muslim guide for the Hijra because he knew the road best. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### Degrees of dealing with non-Muslims: treaties, allies and clarity of creed

- He cites the treaty of Hudaybiyya, where Khuza'a, then unbelievers, joined the Prophet's side and Bakr joined Quraysh, and says that the Prophet marched on Mecca because Bakr attacked Khuza'a. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds from this that the Prophet kept distinctions between categories of people and did not treat all non-Muslims alike. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that some people take only this side and forget the clarity of creed discussed earlier, and that the Prophet joined both, with a clear creed and a clear verdict on those who reject it, together with wide room for how to treat them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that God distinguished these categories in Surat al-Mumtahana, between those who did not fight Muslims over religion, towards whom kindness and fairness are allowed, and those who fought and expelled them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that later events show the Prophet making alliances and neutralising some opponents so as not to multiply enemies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that a reformer should convey the faith with wisdom, and that a movement should not expect to win the whole world in a few years when the Prophet spent thirteen years in Mecca without much response. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### The Night Journey to Jerusalem and the place of al-Aqsa

- He says that the last event of this phase is the Night Journey and the Ascension, which many sira scholars place after the return from al-Ta'if, and that many scholars see it as a consolation God gave his Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that he will treat it only briefly because it deserves a long study. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that God, as creator of the laws of nature, can suspend them, and that the journey was done by God's power and not by the Prophet's human ability. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that being carried to Jerusalem before the ascent shows the value of al-Aqsa Mosque and of Jerusalem in Islam, a standing that no one may belittle whatever the circumstances. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates a remark of Ibn Ashur on Surat al-Isra that the word for farthest is a comparative that points to two mosques, one nearer than the other, and so alludes in advance to the Prophet's Mosque, which did not yet exist, so that al-Aqsa is the farthest in relation to Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### The Ascension and the five prayers

- He relates that after the journey the Prophet was taken up to heaven, met earlier prophets, and that among what concerned believers was the setting of the five daily prayers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that prayer had been required before, though not in this form, and that many scholars hold it was two prayers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He lists among the details the meeting with Moses, the Frequented House, the rivers, and the choice of milk as a sign of the natural disposition against wine, and tells listeners to read the full account in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the Prophet came back with this experience and spoke to Quraysh about what he had seen of Jerusalem, which they knew from their journeys. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### Quraysh's reaction and questions about Jerusalem

- He relates from the sira, and thinks also from the Musnad of Ahmad, that Abu Jahl met the Prophet, asked him about the night and then called Quraysh together to hear him, after which they mocked and denied him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that they then asked him for details of Jerusalem's buildings. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates from al-Bukhari, in a report from Jabir, that when Quraysh denied him God made Jerusalem appear before him so that he described its signs while looking at it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates from what he thinks is Sahih Muslim that the Prophet felt a distress he had not felt before because he could not recall every detail of a night so full of events, until God made the place appear to him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

### Apostasy, Abu Bakr's belief and the centrality of prayer

- He relates a report in al-Hakim's Mustadrak that some Muslims abandoned the faith because of this event, and holds that belief in the unseen must come before doubt about what revelation establishes. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He relates that Ibn Abbas, in al-Bukhari, took the vision mentioned in Surat al-Isra to be a vision of the eye and not a dream. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that Abu Bakr became known as al-Siddiq at this event, answering when told what his friend claimed that if he said it then it was true. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that a believer who accepts revelation coming from heaven each day should not find a single night's ascent hard to believe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He says that the event consoled the Prophet and holds lessons for believers, unbelievers and doubters alike. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He holds that its greatest lesson is the centrality of prayer, which was prescribed in that exalted setting and is the central bodily act of worship, though other pillars of the faith also exist. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}
- He notes that he has abridged a long subject and that listeners should read the account in the two Sahihs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep12}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.
