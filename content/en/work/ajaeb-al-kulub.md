## Summary
A short daily Ramadan series by Omar Abdelkafy titled "Wonders of the Hearts" (Ajaeb al-Qulub), on the diseases and the character of the heart, beginning with envy and what is linked to it, in episodes of about seven minutes. This set has twenty-nine episodes from the Assabile archive, and everything in it is the sheikh's speech as it stands in the machine transcript; what is not traced to a chain or is garbled in the transcript is marked as needing a source.

## Episode 1: introduction to the series and the Muslim's heart on the first day of Ramadan
- Omar Abdelkafy opens a daily Ramadan series titled "Wonders of the Hearts" and says he will meet the viewers every day at the same time. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:01:00}
- He says a human being is a handful of earth and a breath of God's spirit, and the more that breath shines with the light of the Book and the Sunna, the less the darkness of the earthen body, so the heart rises through the ranks of good. This is his explanation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:01:30}
- He notes that the heart is called qalb because it turns (taqallub) between a moment of openness and a moment of tightness, and between approach and withdrawal. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:02:30}
- He describes the days of Ramadan as travel across time and an inner emigration from the heart to God, in which the servant leaves what he was before the month. This is his picture. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:02:30}
- He relates a prophetic saying by its sense that hearts have an approach and a withdrawal: when the heart approaches, one multiplies voluntary prayers, and when it withdraws, one keeps to the obligatory, which is the minimum that separates a Muslim from a non-Muslim. (needs_source: the report has no chain and no narrator, and its wording is garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:03:30}
- In the introduction he calls on every Muslim to weigh himself at the start and end of Ramadan, and holds that a fast that ends in weight gain has not reached its aim. This is his opinion. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:04:30}
- He says statistics show that some Arab and Muslim peoples consume in Ramadan what would last many months outside it, and counts that a calamity. (needs_source: the figure and the statistic are his without a source, and the reading is garbled on the number of months) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:05:00}
- He says the pre-dawn meal is a blessing and not a store of food, since the stomach digests what is in it after two hours, and advises the fasting person to stop eating while still having an appetite. This is his opinion. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:06:00}
- He mentions that the Prophet used to break his fast on a few dates, then a sip of water, then stand to pray. (needs_source: the report has no chain and no reference to a book) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:06:00}
- He advises that a Muslim should not eat in Ramadan several times what he eats in other months. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep01 00:06:30}

## Episode 2: envy as a disease that rages through the heart
- He says the heart that turns to God with sincerity, reliance, truthfulness and certainty knows only contentment with what God has provided, while other hearts are struck by a deadly disease, envy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:00:30}
- He counts greed and avarice among the worst things in the heart, because their owner looks at what others hold. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:01:00}
- He reports from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet said that if the son of Adam had two valleys of wealth he would seek a third, that only earth fills his belly, and that God relents toward whoever repents, and says it is agreed upon. (needs_source: the reference to the two Sahihs is his and was not checked, and some wording is garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:01:30}
- He says the envier lives in grief and distress, and recites verses whose gist is that the envier offends against God in what He has done because he is not content with what was given, and that the envier dies having gained of the world only what was written for him. (needs_source: the verses are not attributed to any poet and are not quoted) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:02:00}
- He says envy devours good deeds as fire devours wood. (needs_source: said here in the course of his explanation, and ascribed to the Prophet in episode 4 without a chain) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:02:00}
- He makes envy close to arrogance and the father of hatred and ill will, stirring strife and spite, and sees the envier as rejecting God's apportionment from eternity, so that he cannot sleep when God favours someone else. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:03:00}
- He relates by its sense that the Prophet told whoever sees in his brother something he likes to pray a blessing for him, and that the angel then says to him, "and you the same". (needs_source: the report is by sense and without a chain, and its reading is garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:03:00}
- He says the harm of envy falls on the envier before the envied, because he objects to God's decree, and he voices that objection in questions about a high-achieving colleague, an obedient wife and the like. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:04:00}
- He likens the envier to a fire that consumes itself when it finds nothing else, and says envy is an act of the heart that can turn into harmful action. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:04:00}
- He recalls that the son of Adam, when he envied his brother, killed him, and that the brothers of Joseph, when they envied him, threw him into the well. See [[passage/quran-5-27|al-Ma'ida, verse 27]] and [[passage/quran-12-15|Yusuf, verse 15]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:04:30}
- He says the envier never learns to thank for a blessing and always compares himself with those who have more, so his envy never ends. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:05:00}
- He promises that the next episode will be on the cure for envy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep02 00:05:30}

## Episode 3: the two kinds of envy and its stages
- He recalls that the previous episode was about envy and says a Muslim who finds this disease in himself must look for its cure. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:00:30}
- He distinguishes blameworthy, forbidden envy, which is wishing the blessing to leave the envied person, from permissible envy, which scholars call ghibta, wishing for the like of what another has without wishing his blessing to go. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:01:00}
- He cites a prophetic saying that there is no envy except in two cases: a man God taught the Qur'an who stands with it through the hours of night and day, so his neighbour says "would that I had been given the like", and a man God gave wealth who spends it rightly, so another says the same, and he ascribes it to al-Bukhari. (needs_source: the ascription to al-Bukhari is his and was not checked) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:01:30}
- He says God tied character to the law and to faith, so whoever is given good character finds no way for envy into his heart, because God apportioned character and provision in eternity. This is his explanation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:02:30}
- He says the first thing in envy is ill opinion of God and distrust of what He apportioned, and that one cure is for people to compete in good and each to love good for himself and his kin. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:03:30}
- He sees envy in three stages: first, wishing to be like so-and-so and belittling the owner of the blessing; second, wishing the blessing to leave others, where the danger lies and whose cure is training the self in good character; third, the hardest, striving personally to remove it, for instance by informing against the person to his manager or superior, and then one loses this world and the next. This is his division. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:05:00}
- He says he has never seen an envier who left a good mark on life, that this trait must vanish from societies, and promises the next episode on the cure. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep03 00:05:30}

## Episode 4: the causes of envy and its cure
- He cites a prophetic saying by its sense that forbids envying one another, hating one another and spying, and bids people be servants of God and brothers. (needs_source: the report is by sense and without a chain or ascription, and its reading is garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:00:30}
- He cites a saying he ascribes to the Prophet that envy eats good deeds as fire eats wood, and another about three things none escapes: ill opinion, omen-taking and envy. (needs_source: both reports are without a chain, and the second is garbled in reading) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:01:00}
- He says one cause of envy is people's distance from the truth of religion, so a person becomes a slave to his wishes and desires and seeks what others have, and another is ill opinion of God and lack of reliance on Him. This is his reasoning. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:02:00}
- He counts among the causes the deceptive show of the world, so a person wants the post another holds and sees himself more deserving, and he says the envier always looks at the empty half of the glass. This is his illustration. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:03:00}
- He makes part of the Qur'anic cure God's call to people to compete for what is with Him, not in gathering money, and mentions a saying that the world weighs nothing with God. (needs_source: the wording of the saying is garbled and it has no chain) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:04:00}
- He illustrates the Salaf's competing for the next life by saying that Umar gave half his property and Abu Bakr all of his. (needs_source: the incident is without a chain and without detail in his words) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:04:30}
- He advises that when a person sees a blessing with another he should ask God instead of thinking ill of His wisdom, should purify tawhid in his heart and know that envy only adds to him rancour and sickness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:05:30}
- He advises doing good to whoever one thinks envies one, especially a relative, citing God's command to repel with what is better. See [[passage/quran-41-34|Fussilat, verse 34]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:06:00}
- He advises that a Muslim seek refuge with the two Mu'awwidhat and teach his children to recite them before sleep, blowing into the palms three times and wiping the face and body. See [[passage/quran-113-1|al-Falaq, verse 1]] and [[passage/quran-114-1|al-Nas, verse 1]]. (needs_source: the manner described is without a reference to a report) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy ajaeb-al-kulub/ep04 00:06:30}
