## Summary

The fifth session turns from the first revelation to the secret stage of the call. He reflects on the opening verses of revelation as a call to knowledge of God, then describes who first embraced Islam: the young, the freed and enslaved, women and a few men of standing. He draws four lessons for reformers and ends with a passage of Ibn Ishaq on Abu Bakr's role in bringing early converts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Opening: Ibn Ishaq's closing passage recalled and the world-changing weight of revelation

- He says this is the fifth session, that the first three covered the time before revelation and the fourth the start of revelation, and that he will pick out central lines of Ibn Ishaq between sessions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He describes Ibn Ishaq as an early author of the maghazi and sira in the second Muslim century, a pupil of the successor al-Zuhri, and repeats the passage that closed the last session about revelation following on the Prophet and the heavy burdens of prophethood. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds that the descent of revelation is the event that changed the world, including some cosmic matters, and relates that the jinn, seeing meteors increase, searched until they found the Prophet leading dawn prayer with his companions at Wadi Nakhla and understood the cause. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### The Prophet's name repeated across the earth and the end of revelation

- He points out that the name of Muhammad is proclaimed every day in every region through the call to prayer and the declaration of faith, and that Muslims prostrate in the Prophetic manner everywhere. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that commentators read a verse about God raising the Prophet's mention in this light, with the call to prayer and the declaration of faith as the chief example. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He traces all this back to the cave of Hira and holds that this was the last revelation to come to the earth and the last prophet God sent. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### The first five verses revealed: knowledge of God at the start

- He says that the first revealed verses are the opening of surat al-Alaq and should be pondered, as they open the whole stage of revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates a point some commentators make: the divine attribute of creator fits the start because a created being passes from death to life, hinting that this revelation brings people from death to life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds that the five verses speak only of God, of His perfection and knowledge, and of the human being as weak and ignorant, made from a clot and taught by God what he did not know. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He draws from these two facts, human weakness and human ignorance before God's teaching, that the fitting response is humility and submission, and notes that the verses that follow speak of the human being rebelling when he thinks himself self-sufficient and of the return to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the passage also signals that what is revealed is knowledge, since the command to read is a command to read the Qur'an, and that the Qur'an elsewhere calls what came to the Prophet knowledge, in verses in surat al-Baqara, al-Ra'd and Ta Ha. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He concludes that the Muslim should regard the Qur'an as a basic source of knowledge, not only a means of earning reward by reciting it, and that the noblest knowledge is knowledge of God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### After Khadija and Waraqa: the command to warn and the secret stage of the call

- He recalls that in the previous session he covered the Prophet's return to Khadija, Waraqa ibn Nawfal's promise of support and his link between the Prophet and Musa, and the pause in revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says scholars named the first stage of prophethood the secret call, that the early command to warn was not yet the command to proclaim openly, and that the order to warn the nearest kin came later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that God sent down the Qur'an with its explanation, and holds that a student of the Qur'an should treat the Prophet's guidance as a measure and compass for understanding it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that the Prophet began warning those close to him whom he trusted, taking care with privacy and trust so that word would not reach the idolaters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds that this was a founding lesson in taking practical means: bearers of the faith must not rely on its divine origin to neglect means, and the first practical course was concealment of the call from the general public of Quraysh. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### The merit of the first to embrace Islam

- He holds that to come forward when there are no others coming is very hard, unlike joining in a time of general acceptance, and that this gave the earliest believers a standing that could not be matched later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says God points to this idea in verses about the foremost and in the verse that ranks those who spent and fought before the conquest above those who did so after, and that hadiths also favour the first to believe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Who accepted first, and the circle around Abu Bakr

- He counts Khadija as the very first to believe, then Abu Bakr, Zayd ibn Haritha and Ali ibn Abi Talib as roughly the first four. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that a wider circle came to Islam through Abu Bakr, among them Talha, al-Zubayr, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Uthman, and he also names Ammar, Yasir, Sumayya, Suhayb, al-Miqdad and Abdallah ibn Masud among the early believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He notes that three of the four rightly guided caliphs, Abu Bakr, Uthman and Ali, were among the first, while Umar accepted slightly later. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He observes that those who went on to carry Islam, guard it and manage its affairs after the Prophet were largely among the earliest believers, though others later joined them in this work. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### First observation: the youth of many early believers

- He observes that many of the early believers were very young, and he gives ages from reports he mentions loosely: Ali about ten, al-Zubayr about thirteen to sixteen, and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Ammar also young. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says Ali's place among the first is not a filler in a list, since sources note him specifically as the first of the boys to accept. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the same pattern continued after the Hijra, naming Zayd ibn Thabit, the scribe of revelation, who was very young when he accepted, and Abdallah ibn Umar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that al-Bara said he and Ibn Umar were judged too young at Badr, being under fifteen and so kept from fighting, and that they fought later at Uhud or the Trench. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He adds that Anas ibn Malik began serving the Prophet as a boy of about ten and served him for ten years, that Usama ibn Zayd was around fifteen or sixteen when the Prophet died, and that Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr, born at the start of the Hijra, was about eleven at the Prophet's death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that when the noble woman of the Makhzum tribe was found stealing, people sought someone to intercede with the Prophet and chose Usama, whom he puts at perhaps fourteen then. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Second observation: freedmen, enslaved and weak believers, and their persecution

- He holds that many early believers were mawali and weak people, and relates that Heraclius asked Abu Sufyan whether the nobles or the weak followed the Prophet and, hearing that the weak did, said such people are the followers of messengers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He ties this to the Qur'an's report of Nuh's people saying that only the lowest followed him, and to what Shu'ayb's people said of him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He names Ammar, Yasir, Sumayya, Suhayb, Bilal and Abdallah ibn Masud, with Khabbab, as early believers who were mawali or enslaved, and says their status was itself a reason for their persecution because their masters tormented them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says Sumayya was killed by her master Abu Jahl, and that Abu Bakr bought Bilal from his master Umayya ibn Khalaf, whom Bilal later saw at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Third observation: women among the first, and the elite who stayed away

- He says that the first to believe was a woman and the first to die for the faith was a woman, which he reads as a sign of women's place in carrying the message. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the early believers also included some of standing and honour who were not among the first or second rank of Quraysh leadership, and he lists that rank as Abu Jahl, Utba, Shayba, Umayya, Suhayl and their like. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds that the Qur'an always presents this leading group as the denying, oppressing party, and that the Prophet's early experience was therefore no departure from what happened to earlier prophets. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Parallels in the Qur'an: youths, the people of the cave, Musa's followers, the wife of Pharaoh

- He says that shared patterns in the lives of prophets recur in the Qur'an, and points to the youths of the cave in surat al-Kahf who opposed a majority and powerful polytheism and found no way to keep their faith except to withdraw into a cave. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds that their withdrawal is the praised and preserved picture, and that a bearer of the faith may meet moments when honour lies in hiding or enduring loss, as happened to later scholars and righteous people and as the Prophet hid in a cave three days, though he does not call for copying that form. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that some commentators read the descendants of Musa's people who believed in him as the youth, and that the Qur'an again and again names the notables as those who deny and mock, while the weak and young respond. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the fourth parallel is women who bear burdens and defy falsehood, for which God gave the wife of Pharaoh as an example, and that Sumayya's trial and martyrdom recall her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Lesson for reformers: no group is to be dismissed, whatever its age, sex or rank

- He holds that the session's central point is that youth, low social class and being a woman were no barrier to joining the first believers, and neither was high standing, as Abu Bakr and Uthman, who were noble, did not suffer the long persecution that Bilal, Khabbab, Ammar, Yasir and Suhayb did. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says Hamza and Umar came after, and draws for reformers the point that no section of society should be belittled, whether children, women, the poor or the honourable. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds that the highest class and the leaders, as in the Qur'an and in Quraysh, usually come in only when victory is clear. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds it a grave mistake to treat the rising generation as mere children, or to confine women to the home, since Islam addresses women directly whether or not they are married and their role in serving the faith does not conflict with their other duties. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Umm Haram and the sea expedition, as an example of women's role

- He holds that many of the Companions among the women were married, and the Prophet did not limit them to doing nothing, and some went out with him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates the report of Umm Haram, in whose house the Prophet woke from a dream of people of his community crossing the sea as kings on thrones; she asked that God place her among them, and he said she was among them, and in a second dream said she was among the first. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that years later she sailed with her husband Ubada ibn al-Samit and the Muslim army in the time of Mu'awiya and died on that expedition. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the lesson is that a woman's role should not be sidelined, and likewise that lower social classes are not beyond hope, as both groups hold good and bad just as nobles do. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Fourth lesson: Abu Bakr's initiative while the Prophet was present

- He holds that the fourth meaning is hastening to carry responsibility, and that Abu Bakr is the chief model, working from his first days of Islam to deliver the faith and support it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says that in the secret stage service meant calling individuals by name rather than proclaiming openly, and that through Abu Bakr some of the early leaders of the Muslims entered Islam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He draws the point that a reformer should not belittle his own early role because someone higher or more learned exists, since Abu Bakr took a central role with the Prophet himself present. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He holds that the presence of a greater person does not make one's own role marginal or merely supplementary, and that one has duties by which to draw near to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Recap of the four lessons for reformers

- He sums up four central lessons for reformers from the early stage: first, putting knowledge of God at the base, taken from the first verses of revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the second is taking means even while God watches over the bearer of the faith, taken from the secret call. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the third is the variety of groups addressed by the call, taken from the early believers: boys, mawali, women and nobles, none to be despised. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He says the fourth is bearing responsibility for the call even when someone better or more learned exists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}

### Ibn Ishaq on Abu Bakr and the first eight

- He closes with a passage of Ibn Ishaq saying that once Abu Bakr accepted Islam and made it known, he called others to God. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that Ibn Ishaq describes Abu Bakr as a man liked by his people, easy to be with, the best-informed genealogist of Quraysh, and a trader of good character whom men visited for his knowledge, trade and good company. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that Abu Bakr called to Islam those of his people he trusted, and that, as Ibn Ishaq says he heard, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Uthman ibn Affan, Talha ibn Ubaydullah, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf accepted through him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
- He relates that Abu Bakr brought them to the Prophet, who presented Islam, recited the Qur'an to them and told them its right, and they believed, becoming with him the eight who were first, which the speaker calls a great honour for Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep5}
