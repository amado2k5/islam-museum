## Work and author

Al-Taysir fi al-mudawat wa al-tadbir is [[person/ibn-zuhr|Abu Marwan Abd al-Malik ibn Zuhr]]'s practical medical work, not his father Abu al-Ala's or his son's. It treats diseases of the organs, therapeutics and regimen; the study describes progression from head and other body regions to general conditions. This entry does not imply a complete museum reading: its actual evidence is a selected translated study and Kaya's article {cite:abdel-halim-ibn-zuhr-surgery-2005 p.1334} {cite:tdv-ibn-zuhr-kaya work1}.

## Reported experiment

The work contains the [[invention/ibn-zuhr-goat-tracheotomy-demonstration|reported animal-recovery investigation after tracheal injury]]. Abdel-Halim's translation describes recovery and long survival, referencing149of Khuri's1983edition through reference42. That is a transmitted reference, not a primary page independently read. The excerpt establishes no complete series size, subsequent human case or universal human safety {cite:abdel-halim-ibn-zuhr-surgery-2005 pp.1335,1338}.

The study also discusses examination of sheep lungs for another clinical question, and warnings against specialist intervention without practical knowledge and training. The accounts are not merged into one experiment, nor are their dimensions or operative details reproduced as treatment instruction. Evidence type and the distinction between observation and general inference matter here {cite:abdel-halim-ibn-zuhr-surgery-2005 p.1336}.

## Disputed composition story

Abdel-Halim says Ibn Rushd requested a work on particulars to complement his Generalities and celebrates the relationship between the books. Mahmut Kaya rejects the common account, saying the request was to copy the work, not initiate composition; he also records dedication to Abd al-Mumin. Both readings remain attributed, not settled merely because the works were printed together {cite:abdel-halim-ibn-zuhr-surgery-2005 p.1334} {cite:tdv-ibn-zuhr-kaya work1}.

## Transmission and editions

The work reached Hebrew and Latin. The study counts eight Latin printings between1490and1554; Kaya ten between1490and1574. The different ranges and counts are disclosed, not collapsed into the larger figure.
