## Summary

Al-Hidaya Sharh Bidayat al-Mubtadi is a Hanafi law book by Imam [[person/burhan-al-din-al-marghinani|Burhan al-Din Ali ibn Abi Bakr al-Marghinani]] (511-593 AH), which its editor Sa'id Bakdash describes as one of the best-known, most used and most relied-on books of the [[group/hanafi-madhhab|Hanafis]]. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.5} It is a commentary on the author's own base text [[work/bidayat-al-mubtadi|Bidayat al-Mubtadi]], and so in fact a commentary on [[work/mukhtasar-al-quduri|al-Quduri's Mukhtasar]] and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan's [[work/al-jami-al-saghir-fi-al-fiqh|al-Jami' al-Saghir]], which that text combines. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.8}

## Why it was written

The editor quotes the preface of al-Hidaya: al-Marghinani had promised at the start of the Bidaya a commentary to be called [[work/kifayat-al-muntahi|Kifayat al-Muntahi]]; he began it, and when nearly finished found some prolixity in it and feared the book would be neglected for that, so he turned to another commentary, al-Hidaya, joining "the best of the transmitted rulings and the core of understanding" and leaving out what was superfluous. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.7} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.8} He reports from al-Laknawi that al-Marghinani began it at noon on a Wednesday in Dhu al-Qa'da 573 AH, aged about sixty-two, and found in a manuscript of Taj al-Shari'a's Nihayat al-Kifaya that the author wrote at the end of the Book of Waqf that it was completed in Dhu al-Hijja 575 AH. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.60} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.61}

## Method and terms

Al-Marghinani did not state his method in the preface of al-Hidaya, nor his sources for the additions, for the evidence of the school's masters or for the views of Malik and al-Shafi'i, nor the basis of his preferences; the commentators traced these and recorded them as the "habits and terms" of the author of al-Hidaya; among those who collected them were al-Itqani in Ghayat al-Bayan, al-Laknawi in the introduction to his gloss, and al-Hamzawi in the treatise Misbah al-Diraya fi Istilah al-Hidaya. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.58} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.59} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.60} Among them: he says "he said" at the start of every question from al-Quduri, al-Jami' al-Saghir or the Bidaya; he puts al-Quduri's questions first and those of al-Jami' al-Saghir at the end of chapters, and quotes al-Jami' al-Saghir's wording where it differs from al-Quduri's. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.61} When he says a hadith "is understood" in some sense he means the hadith scholars' reading; when he says "we understand it" he means his own. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.61} In more than twenty places he refers readers wanting more detail to Kifayat al-Muntahi. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.32}

## Its sources

The editor says al-Marghinani named few sources, and lists among them: the Muwatta'; Abu Yusuf's books (al-Imla', al-Jawami', al-Nawadir); Muhammad ibn al-Hasan's books (al-Asl, al-Jami' al-Kabir, al-Jami' al-Saghir, the two Siyar, al-Ziyadat and Ziyadat al-Ziyadat); al-Khassaf's Adab al-Qadi; the Sunan of Abu Dawud; statements from al-Tahawi, Abu Mansur al-Maturidi, al-Karkhi and al-Jassas; and al-Hakim al-Shahid's al-Muntaqa and al-Kafi. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.98} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.99}

## Standing and commentaries

The editor quotes scholars' praise of it, among them the author of al-Wiqaya, Taj al-Shari'a and al-Sighnaqi in the introduction to al-Nihaya. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.36} {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.37} He says he gathered 120 works built on al-Hidaya, commentaries, glosses, abridgements and hadith studies, of which only about ten commentaries are printed; of the books tracing its hadiths, al-Zayla'i's Nasb al-Raya, Ibn Hajar's al-Diraya and Munyat al-Alma'i are printed, and among commentators al-Saruji in al-Ghaya, al-Ayni in al-Binaya and [[person/ibn-al-humam|Ibn al-Humam]] in [[work/fath-al-qadir|Fath al-Qadir]] attended to its hadiths. {cite:hidaya-bakdash-v1 p.101} Among its commentaries in the museum: [[work/al-inaya-sharh-al-hidaya|al-Inaya]].
