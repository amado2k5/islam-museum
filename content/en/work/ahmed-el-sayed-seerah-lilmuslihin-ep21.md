## Summary

Ahmed Elsayed resumes the series after the Ramadan sessions and draws out the lessons of the Battle of Uhud, relying mostly on the chapter Ibn al-Qayyim devotes to its wisdoms in Zad al-Ma'ad. He then follows the aftermath, the steadfastness at Hamra al-Asad, and the sacrifices of the Companions who protected the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### How Uhud stayed in memory

- He says that the series resumes after twenty sessions given nightly in the previous Ramadan, and that the last session stopped at the Battle of Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He holds that Uhud was fixed deep in the community's memory: he relates that Aisha chose it as the hardest day when she asked the Prophet about his worst day, and that the Prophet visited the martyrs of Uhud near the end of his life. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Khabbab, offered food in later prosperity, recalled the poverty of the early emigrants and the death of Mus'ab ibn Umayr, whose burial cloth could not cover both head and feet; he places a similar moment of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf beside it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Umar, while caliph, withheld a fine garment from a relative of the Prophet and gave it to Umm Salit, remembering that she had carried water skins for the army at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Why practical experience deepens understanding

- He says that the Qur'an preserved the memory of Uhud through one of the longest passages revealed about a single event, in Surah Al Imran. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He holds that understanding of religion comes by two routes, study with scholars and lived practice, and that truths seen in action, such as the cost of disobedience by the archers or the good outcome of patience, take firmer root than those only learned. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that al-Tabari, commenting on the verse about a group going out to gain understanding, explains that those who set out gain it by witnessing God's support and the defeat of unbelief, and then warn their people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He states his first lesson: many truths of religion become settled in the soul only when seen on the ground. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Ibn al-Qayyim's chapter on the wisdoms of Uhud

- He recommends Zad al-Ma'ad by Ibn al-Qayyim as a work for students who already have some grounding, and not necessarily to be read in full. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He says it is exceptional among works on the Prophet's life because it treats both the events and the Prophet's guidance on many practical matters. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that after narrating Uhud, Ibn al-Qayyim adds a chapter on its wisdoms, which he says he will quote from, covering under a quarter of it. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Disobedience and dispute, taught first at Badr

- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's first wisdom: the Companions learned the bad outcome of disobedience, failure and dispute. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He holds that the theory was given before Badr in Surah al-Anfal, through commands to stand firm, obey and avoid quarrelling, and that Uhud supplied the practical confirmation when some archers disobeyed and the Prophet nearly died. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Alternating fortunes and the exposure of the hypocrites

- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's point that God's way with prophets and followers is for them to win at times and be defeated at times, so that the sincere are told apart from those who follow only success. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He explains that after Badr some people of the Aws and Khazraj who had held back entered Islam outwardly, out of fear and having expected the Muslims to be destroyed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He says that Uhud exposed the hypocrites, who spoke openly, so the believers recognised an enemy living among them and prepared against them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Ibn al-Qayyim ties this to the verse in Al Imran saying God would not leave the believers mixed with others until the good was separated from the bad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He holds that this is a standing divine pattern: when Muslims turn seriously to their religion, trials come that separate the true from the false. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Worship in ease and hardship, and humility before victory

- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's wisdom that trials draw out the servitude of God's friends in good times and bad, in what they like and dislike. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Ibn al-Qayyim says constant victory would make souls arrogant, as constant wealth would, so God directs his servants through ease and hardship. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that defeat brings humility and so makes people deserving of help, citing the contrast between Badr, won when the Muslims were weak, and Hunayn, where numbers made them proud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Martyrdom and those denied it

- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's view that martyrdom is among the highest ranks of God's friends, second only to that of the truthful, and that it is reached only through events that expose people to enemies. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He notes that the Al Imran verses on Uhud say God wishes to take martyrs from the believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's remark that the hypocrites who withdrew before the battle were denied martyrdom, which he calls the reverse of worldly reasoning, where those loved are kept safe. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Wisdoms seen from several sides, and the destruction of enemies

- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's point that when God means to destroy enemies, he sets up the causes, chiefly their rebellion and their excess in harming his friends, which also cleanses the believers of their faults. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He observes that the lessons come from several angles on one scene: some concern the believers, through the cost of disobedience, discernment and martyrdom, and some concern the hypocrites and the unbelievers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Kindness in the failings: latent flaws brought out

- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's claim that the Companions' failings came from natural dispositions and were not hypocrisy. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He counts two failings at Uhud: the archers leaving their post out of desire for spoils, and flight through fear when the lines broke. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He explains that such flaws were latent and the Companions may not have known they had them, and that the trial brought them from potential to actual so that they could repent and guard against them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Ibn al-Qayyim closes the passage with the second half of a verse line of al-Mutanabbi, meaning that bodies sometimes become healthy through illness. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Wrong thoughts about God

- He says that Ibn al-Qayyim, commenting on the Al Imran verse about a group thinking wrongly of God at Uhud, wrote at length on kinds of bad assumptions that arise in trials. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Ibn al-Qayyim holds almost everyone harbours some complaint against fate and that a person should ask forgiveness for it and suspect his own self rather than his Lord. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He adds that the believer is not free of such thoughts but resists them, and that Surah al-Fath also speaks of the hypocrites' wrong thoughts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He cautions that the advice to think ill of oneself should be taken with balance and not as a rule of constant self-contempt. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Testing and purifying hearts; deeds as soldiers

- He relates Ibn al-Qayyim's point that the trial tests what is in hearts, showing faith or hypocrisy, and that it also purifies the believers' hearts of the effects of habit, desire and heedlessness, as a bitter medicine treats a disease. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Ibn al-Qayyim describes a person's deeds as soldiers either for him or against him, so that those who fled at Uhud were undone by troops of their own deeds sent by the devil. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Ibn al-Qayyim notes God forgave them, since the flight came from neither hypocrisy nor doubt, and that God also told them the hardship came from themselves. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### Seeing the larger good: from Uhud to the Trench

- He holds that people in hardship see only the narrow loss, whereas a wider view shows wisdom in many misfortunes, and he lists the losses at Uhud: the Prophet's wounds, the deaths of Hamza and Mus'ab ibn Umayr, and the unbelievers' pride after the battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He says that the Quraysh leader called out at the end that the day was set against Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He argues that the steadfastness of the Muslims at the Battle of the Trench was mainly due to what they learned at Uhud, relating that they recognised the siege as the trial their verse had foretold. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Hamra al-Asad and the Companions' reply

- He relates that right after Uhud word reached the Prophet that the Quraysh army, camped at Hamra al-Asad on its way home, considered returning to finish off Medina. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He says that Hamra al-Asad lies south of Medina and is now almost joined to it, though he places it about twenty kilometres away in earlier times. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that the Prophet called out only those who had been at Uhud, and that Jabir ibn Abdullah, who had stayed behind to care for his sisters, was allowed to join since his father Abdullah had been killed at Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that the Qur'an describes the Companions' reply to the warning as increased faith, and that Aisha told Urwa his father al-Zubayr and her father Abu Bakr were among those meant. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He holds that the details of this account of Aisha's speech are uncertain in the recording. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}

### The Companions' sacrifices at Uhud

- He recalls the seven Ansar who shielded the Prophet one after another, and the stands of Talha, al-Zubayr and Abu Talha. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Qays ibn Abi Hazim, a Successor, saw Talha's hand paralysed for life from the blow he took protecting the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He relates that Abu Talha shot arrows while shielding the Prophet and told him not to expose himself, and that Anas ibn al-Nadr rushed forward saying he could smell paradise near Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
- He holds that those days are the Companions' greatest record of honour, and that this is why their standing should be respected. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-lilmuslihin/ep21}
