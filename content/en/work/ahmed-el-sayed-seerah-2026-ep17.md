## Summary

After a short biography of the early Companion Hatib ibn Amr, the speaker explains how Qur'anic verses relate to events of the Prophet's life. He sorts the relationship into explicit and implicit links, names the sources that connect verses to events (books of occasions of revelation, the hadith collections, Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham, and al-Tabari's tafsir), then gives many Meccan examples centred on the Quraysh leaders who opposed the Prophet and on the persecuted early believers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Plan of the session

- He says this is the seventeenth session of the series and that it will focus on the link between Qur'anic verses and the Prophet's biography. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that the commentary on the chapter of the Cave is paused until the next session, and that this session draws on Ibn Hisham and on reports about occasions of revelation, mainly from al-Tabari's tafsir. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Biography of the early Companion Hatib ibn Amr

- He presents Hatib ibn Amr, a Badr veteran who is little known, and distinguishes him from two other early Companions named Hatib, Hatib ibn al-Harith and Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that Hatib was a brother of Suhayl ibn Amr and of Salit ibn Amr, and that he accepted Islam before the Prophet entered the house of al-Arqam. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He says Hatib made both emigrations to Abyssinia, and that al-Zuhri is reported as asserting he was the first to go there. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that the sources disagree over whether his brother Salit was at Badr, with Musa ibn Uqba alone listing him and Ibn Sa'd doubting it, while all agree that Hatib himself was at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that al-Baladhuri rejects a report that Hatib was the last to return from Abyssinia with Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, since Hatib was at Badr, and that Ibn Hajar mentions him in the marriage of the Prophet to Sawda bint Zam'a. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He cites the modern author Sulayman al-Awda as noting that al-Dhahabi gave him no entry in Siyar A'lam al-Nubala while Ibn al-Athir treated him twice in Usd al-Ghaba, and that his death date and place are unknown. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He adds that Suhayl ibn Amr, who stood against the Prophet at the Treaty of Hudaybiyya, later accepted Islam and was among those who steadied the Meccans in the Ridda wars under Abu Bakr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Two kinds of link between verses and events

- He holds that learning the Qur'an through the biography, and the biography through the Qur'an, is among the most important subjects, and that reducing the biography to event data narrows a vast subject. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He says the Qur'an came down in pieces tied to events, and that verses relate to events in two ways: an explicit link stated in the text, as with the chapter of the Spoils and Badr, and an implicit link that the reader does not notice. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He holds that explicit links are mostly in Medinan verses connected to battles, while implicit links are the main question for Meccan verses, with the Night Journey and the chapter on Abu Lahab as clear Meccan exceptions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### The implicit branches and why the link matters

- He divides the implicit link into verses with explicit reports tying them to an event and verses with no report at all, whose connection to an event comes from the exegetes' own inference. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He holds that knowing the occasion gives a deeper grasp of the biography and of God's way of steadying believers and restraining aggressors, and he describes the Prophet's struggle in Mecca as a struggle by means of the Qur'an. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He sets the scope of the session to Meccan verses only, since the series is still in the Meccan phase and since most explicit event links lie in Medinan verses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Sources that link verses to events

- He counts two main sources for the reported link: books on occasions of revelation, and the books of sira and hadith, including the tafsir chapters of the hadith collections such as that of al-Bukhari. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He holds that the biographies of Ibn Ishaq, in the recension of Ibn Hisham, are an important but overlooked source tying verses to events. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He holds that for inferred links the most important source is the tafsir of al-Tabari, who explains verses with an eye on the Prophet's circumstances. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Al-Tabari's inferred links: Joseph and Noah verses

- He relates that al-Tabari reads a verse in the chapter of Joseph, about God rewarding the doers of good, as addressed to the Prophet: as God delivered and established Joseph, so He will deliver Muhammad from the polytheists of his people. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that al-Tabari reads a verse in the chapter of the Spider about Noah staying among his people as consolation for the Prophet and his Companions against Quraysh harm, though the verse names neither. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He holds that such links come only from a commentator who keeps the biography in mind, and that few later exegetes followed al-Tabari in this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Reliability of Successors' reports and the Companions' concern

- He says he will not examine chains of transmission for each example, since many of the reports are mursal from Successors such as Mujahid and Qatada, whom he regards as broadly reliable or at least widely usable on occasions of revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that Mujahid presented the Qur'an to Ibn Abbas and asked him about every verse. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He says the Companions taught the Successors the occasions of revelation, that they used this knowledge against the Kharijites, and he relates that Uthman answered the Kharijites that the verses were not about them and that Ibn Mas'ud claimed to know where and about whom each verse came down. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Ammar ibn Yasir, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith and the Persian tales

- He relates, from al-Tabari, a report from Abd Allah ibn Ubayd ibn Umayr that the verses on the testing of believers came down about Ammar ibn Yasir when he was tortured for his faith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates reports from Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi that the verses on the one who asked for an impending punishment came down in Mecca about al-Nadr ibn al-Harith. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that al-Nadr would follow the Prophet's gatherings telling tales of Persian heroes and kings, claiming to be a better storyteller, and that several verses on tales of the ancients and on the slanderer came down in this connection; he reports Ibn Hisham glossing the slanderer as liar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He says al-Nadr was executed after Badr, with Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, by order of the Prophet. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Badr as the fulfilment of Meccan warnings

- He holds that Badr should be understood through three lenses: the biography, the Qur'an and the divine laws of history. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that al-Tabari and others read some Meccan verses of general wording as pointing to Badr, for instance the verse that every report has its settled time. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates the disagreement in al-Tabari over a verse on the dead man brought to life, taken by some as Umar and Abu Jahl and by others as Ammar ibn Yasir and Abu Jahl. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Verses tied to Abu Bakr and the freed slaves

- He relates that most exegetes link the closing verses of the chapter of the Night to Abu Bakr, who used his wealth to free enslaved and tortured believers in early Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Ibn Abi Shayba, on a sound chain through Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, that those freed included weak women such as Zinnira, al-Nahdiyya and her daughter. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that al-Tabari reports the same from Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, and from a report on Sa'id naming Bilal and Amir ibn Fuhayra among those freed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### The poor believers and the Quraysh leaders

- He relates from al-Tabari, via Mujahid, that the verses where the arrogant ask why they do not see men they counted as wicked refer to Abu Jahl and al-Walid ibn al-Mughira speaking of Suhayb, Ammar and Khabbab. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Ibn Mas'ud, in al-Tabari, that Quraysh nobles passed the Prophet seated with Suhayb, Ammar, Bilal and Khabbab and asked him to send them away so the nobles might follow him, and that the verse in the chapter of the Cattle forbidding him to expel them came down in reply. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that al-Tabari ties a similar verse in the chapter of the Cave to the same circumstances, with Mujahid naming Bilal and Ibn Mas'ud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Ibn al-Zib'ara's dispute over Jesus and the idols

- He relates from al-Baghawi that Ibn Abbas and most exegetes held that verses in the chapters of the Ornaments and the Prophets came down over the dispute of Abd Allah ibn al-Zib'ara with the Prophet about Jesus. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that Ibn al-Zib'ara argued that if everything worshipped besides God goes to the fire, then Jesus and the angels, who are also worshipped, would too, and that his people greeted this with cheering and noise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that the exegetes read the verse on those for whom the best was decreed as a clarification that the righteous who are worshipped against their will are not included, citing al-Shinqiti for the sense of the noise. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Abu Jahl and the verses of warning

- He relates from al-Tabari, through Qatada, that verses in the chapters of the Smoke and of the Resurrection came down about Abu Jahl, to whom the Prophet is said to have taken hold and spoken a phrase of warning, Abu Jahl replying with a boast about his standing in Mecca. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Qatada, in a further report, that at Badr Abu Jahl declared that God would no longer be worshipped after that day and was killed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that exegetes also tied a verse on the walk of the arrogant to Abu Jahl, with some saying instead it describes the Banu Makhzum. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from al-Tabari that Abu Jahl rejected the Prophet by saying that he did not doubt the man but doubted what he brought, and that this was linked to a verse in the chapter of the Cattle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He holds that the Qur'an names only Abu Lahab, and that the Successors and exegetes knew who each verse concerned because they knew what the opponents had done. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Verses on Abu Talib, the Mala' and the tree of Zaqqum

- He says that there are also authentic reports traced to the Prophet linking Meccan verses to events, and cites the verses tied to Abu Talib, treated in an earlier session. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from al-Tabari that the verses in the chapter of Sad about the elders who walked out urging steadfastness to their gods concern a group of Quraysh including Abu Jahl, al-As ibn Wa'il and al-Aswad. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Qatada and Ibn Abbas, in al-Tabari, that when the tree of Zaqqum was mentioned Abu Jahl mocked it, called for dates and butter and said this was Zaqqum, and that verses on the tree and on the warnings that only increased their rebellion came down in answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### The nineteen guardians and the will verse

- He relates from al-Tabari, via Ibn Abbas, that when the verses naming nineteen keepers of the fire came down, Abu Jahl told Quraysh that ten of them could overpower each keeper, and that the verse saying the keepers are angels answered him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates the same report as saying that the Prophet was then told to take Abu Jahl by the hand in the valley of Mecca and speak the phrase of warning, and that Abu Jahl was humiliated at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that al-Tabari reports Abu Jahl saying the matter was up to them whether to stay upright, and that a verse in the chapter of the Folding Up answered this. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He notes that Quraysh also called the Prophet Mudhammam instead of Muhammad and that Abu Jahl called him son of Abu Kabsha. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### A third inferred example: the chapter of the Forgiver

- He relates that al-Tabari reads verses in the chapter of the Forgiver on Moses' book and on patience as parallel to the Prophet's case: Pharaoh and his people denied Moses as Quraysh denied Muhammad, and God's promise of victory to the Prophet is certain. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He says the link between the Prophet and Moses runs through the Meccan verses and the biography, and recalls the account that Waraqa ibn Nawfal likened the angel to the one sent to Moses and that Moses figured in the Night Journey and the prayers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}

### Ibn Ishaq on al-Akhnas, Ubayy ibn Khalaf, Uqba, Umayya and al-As

- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that al-Akhnas ibn Shariq, an ally of the Banu Zuhra who listened to the Prophet and answered back, was the subject of verses describing a petty swearer and slanderer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Ubayy ibn Khalaf forbade his friend Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt from sitting with the Prophet and made him spit on the Prophet's face, and that a verse on the wrongdoer biting his hands came down about the two. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that Ubayy once crumbled a decayed bone and blew it toward the Prophet asking whether God would raise it, and that verses in the chapter of Ya Sin came down in answer. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Umayya ibn Khalaf used to sneer at the Prophet and that the chapter of the Slanderer came down about him, with Ibn Hisham's gloss of the two Arabic terms and poetry cited by him from Hassan ibn Thabit and Ru'ba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates from Ibn Ishaq that al-As ibn Wa'il owed money to the smith Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, mocked him that he would pay in the Garden, and that verses in the chapter of Maryam came down about him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He says Uqba was also the one who threw the camel's entrails on the Prophet while he prayed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that Quraysh nobles said of the new believers that had it been good, they would not have been ahead of them, and that Abu al-Zinad is reported as saying a verse came down about Zinnira, a woman of weak sight, and others like her. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
- He relates that a verse in the chapter of the Scatterers, bidding the Prophet turn away, alarmed his Companions until the following verse reassured them, in a report from Mujahid on Ali. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep17}
