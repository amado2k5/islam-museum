## Summary

The lecturer finishes the topic of the early converts. He discusses who the Qur'anic early forerunners were according to scholars, then walks through Ibn Sa'd's grouping of the emigrant Badr veterans by Quraysh clan, and profiles less famous men from that list using Ibn Sa'd's details on lineage, migration to Abyssinia, hosts in Medina, pairing in brotherhood, battles and death. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Recap and who the early forerunners were

- He recalls that the previous session covered the opening verses of one early surah, the manner in which revelation came, the obligation of prayer and Ibn Ishaq's account of it, and the first converts. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He says the word forerunners as a label is partly scholarly usage, while the phrase about the first Emigrants and Helpers comes from revelation. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that scholars differ over who is meant: those who prayed toward both directions, which he links to being Muslim before Badr; those who accepted Islam before Hudaybiyya; those who were present at Badr; and, for the Helpers, those at the pledge of al-Aqaba. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He cites al-Tabari as preferring the reading in which the Helpers are part of the forerunners, with a further group who followed them. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He holds that whichever view is taken, the forerunners have a special rank and that within them there are degrees of merit, one reason being how isolated a believer was when he believed. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

This is the speaker's view; the museum reports it as his and does not adopt it.

### Why the series stresses names, and Ibn Sa'd as the guide

- He says a stated aim of the series is to learn the names, lineages and key episodes of the early Companions, many of whom are unknown even to students of knowledge because they appear only in biographical and seerah books. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He explains that he chose Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat because it records for nearly each Emigrant where he stayed in Medina, who was paired with him in brotherhood, his wives, children and precise lineage. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Ibn Sa'd devotes one volume to the Badr veterans, divided into Emigrants and Helpers, with women placed in a separate section at the end of the work. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He holds that Badr veterans equal forerunners on one view, and that those who prayed to both directions is a nearby view. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Quraysh clans as the frame of the seerah lists

- He says Ibn Sa'd arranges the Emigrants by Quraysh clan, and that Ibn Ishaq also lists the migrants to Abyssinia by clan, so knowing clans is basic to detailed seerah study. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He explains that under each clan Ibn Sa'd names the men, then allies and freed clients, and he distinguishes an ally, a tribe or group joined by pact, from a freed client. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that about eighty-five Emigrant Badr veterans are listed, and that men who converted late, such as Jubayr ibn Mut'im, Abu Sufyan and Mu'awiya, are left out because they were not among the forerunners. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Banu Hashim, Banu al-Muttalib and Banu Nawfal

- He says Ibn Sa'd opens the Badr list with the Prophet himself, followed from Banu Hashim by Hamza and Ali, with Zayd ibn Haritha and a few freed men listed among allies and clients. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that from Banu al-Muttalib the list names Ubayda ibn al-Harith, al-Tufayl, al-Husayn and Mistah ibn Uthatha. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates the incident after Khaybar when Uthman and Jubayr asked why Banu al-Muttalib received a share and not them, and the Prophet answered that Hashim and al-Muttalib are one body, a point he says he will explain later with the boycott in the valley and Abu Talib's poem. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates from al-Bukhari that Gabriel asked the Prophet how the Badr veterans were ranked, and that the angels present at Badr share that rank. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He lists the allies of Banu Nawfal as Utba ibn Ghazwan and Khabbab, the latter freed by Utba, and describes Utba as the founder of Basra under Umar and the speaker of a well known sermon in Sahih Muslim about his early poverty. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Banu Abd Shams, Asad, Abd al-Dar and their allies

- He says the Abd Shams section has sixteen names, only about two of them truly from the clan, the rest allies and freed men, among them Uthman ibn Affan and Abu Hudhayfa ibn Utba with his freed man Salim. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He holds that the verse about two disputing parties is reported to concern the Badr duel between Ali, Hamza and Ubayda on one side and Utba, Shayba and al-Walid on the other, relating a report from Ali. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that allies of Abd Shams from Banu Ghanm ibn Dudan include Abd Allah ibn Jahsh, Ukasha ibn Mihsan, Shuja ibn Wahb, Uqba ibn Wahb, Rabi'a ibn Aktham and Muhriz ibn Nadla. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He notes that Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a is an ally of al-Zubayr of Banu Asad, with Sa'd as Hatib's own freed man. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He mentions Mus'ab ibn Umayr, Suwaybit ibn Sa'd and Tulayb ibn Umayr from Banu Abd al-Dar, and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Sa'd's brother Umayr from Banu Zuhra, noting Umayr died at Badr aged sixteen. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He says the allies of Banu Zuhra include Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, al-Miqdad, Khabbab ibn al-Aratt and Dhu al-Shimalayn, and that a monument at the Badr cemetery lists the fourteen martyrs. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Banu Taym, Makhzum, Adi and the remaining clans

- He lists Abu Bakr and Talha from Banu Taym, with Abu Bakr's freed men such as Bilal and Amir ibn Fuhayra and the ally Suhayb. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He names from Banu Makhzum Abu Salama, al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam and Shammas ibn Uthman, with Ammar ibn Yasir among the freed men of the clan, linking this to why Abu Jahl killed Sumayya. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He names from Banu Adi Umar, Sa'id ibn Zayd and Zayd ibn al-Khattab, and says Abd Allah ibn Umar counts as an emigrant but missed Badr as too young. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Umar reduced his own son's stipend, saying he had been taken along by his parents rather than migrating on his own, as an example of a ruler not favouring his family. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He adds allies and freed men of Adi such as Amir ibn Rabi'a, the sons of al-Bukayr, Waqid ibn Abd Allah, Khawli and Mahja', then Khunays ibn Hudhafa from Banu Sahm, the sons of Maz'un from Banu Jumah, several names from Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy, and Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah from Banu Fihr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He says Zubayr named his sons after martyrs while Talha named his after prophets. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Dhu al-Shimalayn, Mas'ud ibn al-Rabi' and Mahja'

- He relates Ibn Sa'd's entry for Dhu al-Shimalayn: his lineage, his nickname because he used both hands, his father's pact with Banu Zuhra, his stay with Sa'd ibn Khaythama in Medina, his pairing in brotherhood with Yazid ibn al-Harith, and that both were killed at Badr, he being in his thirties. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He says Ibn Sa'd stresses the hosts in Medina such as Sa'd ibn Khaythama and Kulthum ibn al-Hidm, and holds that some Helpers reached a higher rank of support by opening their houses. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates the entry for Mas'ud ibn al-Rabi' of al-Qara, an ally of Banu Zuhra, who became Muslim before the Prophet entered the house of al-Arqam, was paired with Ubayd ibn al-Tayyihan, and whose presence at Badr Ibn Sa'd says he could not confirm. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Mahja', freed man of Umar and reportedly of Yemeni origin, is reported as the first Muslim killed at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Suwaybit ibn Sa'd and Tulayb ibn Umayr

- He relates that Suwaybit ibn Sa'd was among the migrants to Abyssinia, stayed in Medina with Abd Allah ibn Salama al-Ajlani, was paired in brotherhood with a man of the Helpers and fought at Badr and Uhud. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Tulayb ibn Umayr, son of the Prophet's paternal aunt Arwa, became Muslim in the house of al-Arqam, and that his mother Arwa then supported the Prophet and urged her son to help him. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He says Ibn Sa'd cites Musa ibn Uqba, Ibn Ishaq, Abu Mashar and al-Waqidi as agreeing that Tulayb went to Abyssinia in the second migration, while only al-Waqidi has him at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Tulayb was killed at Ajnadayn in year thirteen aged about thirty-five, and he works out that Tulayb must have converted in his early teens, comparing him to the youth of Ali. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Abd Allah ibn Makhrama, Hatib ibn Amr and Abd Allah ibn Suhayl

- He relates that Abd Allah ibn Makhrama of Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy migrated to Abyssinia, according to al-Waqidi twice, stayed in Medina with Kulthum ibn al-Hidm, was paired with Farwa ibn Amr, fought at Badr, Uhud and the Trench, and died at al-Yamama in year twelve. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He says the lineage details and the differences between Ibn Ishaq, Musa ibn Uqba and al-Waqidi on who migrated are the kind of precision Ibn Sa'd offers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Hatib ibn Amr became Muslim before the house of al-Arqam, went to Abyssinia, was named as the first to arrive there, lodged in Medina with Rifa'a ibn Abd al-Mundhir and fought at Badr. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Abd Allah ibn Suhayl, son of Suhayl ibn Amr, returned from Abyssinia, was held and pressured by his father, went out with the idolaters to Badr, then crossed to the Muslims before the fighting and fought as a Muslim, and was killed at the battle of al-Yamama. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Suhayl later, when consoled by Abu Bakr, spoke of a report that a martyr intercedes for seventy of his family. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Allies of Abd Shams: the Ghanm ibn Dudan group

- He relates that Abu Sinan ibn Mihsan, brother of Ukasha, died in year five while the Prophet besieged Banu Qurayza and was buried in their quarter, and that Ibn Sa'd calls a report of his oath of allegiance at Hudaybiyya a mistake. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that it was his son Sinan ibn Abi Sinan who pledged at Hudaybiyya and, per the report, was the first to pledge, dying in year thirty-two. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Shuja ibn Wahb was slim and tall, migrated to Abyssinia in the second migration, was paired with Aws ibn Khawli, led an expedition of twenty-four men against Hawazin, carried the Prophet's letter to al-Harith ibn Abi Shimr al-Ghassani, and was killed at al-Yamama in year twelve. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Uqba ibn Wahb fought at Badr and later battles, and that Rabi'a ibn Aktham fought at Badr, Uhud, the Trench and Hudaybiyya and was killed at Khaybar in year seven. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Muhriz ibn Nadla was paired with Umara ibn Hazm, fought at Badr, Uhud and the Trench, had a dream of the heavens opening that Abu Bakr read as martyrdom, and was killed at the raid of Dhu Qarad in year six. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Khunays ibn Hudhafa, Shammas ibn Uthman and al-Sa'ib ibn Uthman

- He relates that Khunays ibn Hudhafa of Banu Sahm became Muslim before the house of al-Arqam, migrated to Abyssinia, was the earlier husband of Hafsa bint Umar, stayed in Medina with Rifa'a ibn Abd al-Mundhir, was paired with Abu Abs ibn Jabr, and died about twenty-five months after the migration, buried at al-Baqi beside Uthman ibn Maz'un. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Shammas ibn Uthman of Makhzum was named for his radiant look, had Utba and Shayba's sister as mother, stayed with Mubashshir ibn Abd al-Mundhir, was paired with Hanzala ibn Abi Amir and was killed at Uhud, the pair dying in the same battle. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that al-Sa'ib ibn Uthman ibn Maz'un migrated to Abyssinia, was paired with Haritha ibn Suraqa, fought at Badr and later battles, was struck by an arrow at al-Yamama and died of it, and he counts him among the youth of the Companions. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}

### Mu'attib ibn Awf, Khawli ibn Abi Khawli and the sons of Amr

- He relates that Mu'attib ibn Awf, an ally of Banu Makhzum, migrated to Abyssinia, stayed in Medina with Mubashshir ibn Abd al-Mundhir, was paired with Tha'laba ibn Hatib, fought at Badr and later battles and died in year fifty-seven aged seventy-eight, and he reasons that he converted as a youth. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Khawli ibn Abi Khawli, ally of the father of Umar, is said to have fought at Badr, with sources differing on whether his father, son or brother came with him, and he says early historians took care to settle such lists. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He relates that Malik, Mudlij and Thaqaf, sons of Amr, all fought at Badr, Malik being killed at al-Yamama, Mudlij dying in year fifty under Mu'awiya and Thaqaf being killed at Khaybar. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
- He corrects a misreading he made while reading aloud about Thaqaf's death, saying the sixteen men in the passage refers to the sixteen of Abd Shams listed there and not to Thaqaf's killers. {cite:podcast-ahmed-el-sayed seerah-2026/ep9}
