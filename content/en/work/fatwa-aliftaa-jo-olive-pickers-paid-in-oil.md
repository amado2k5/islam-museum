## Summary

A question on farm owners who hand their olives to pickers in return for a set number of tins of oil for the owner, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that this is invalid because of uncertainty, but valid if the yield is divided by proportion. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3840}

## The question

The questioner said some olive-farm owners tell a worker at harvest: pick my farm and give me three tins of oil after pressing, and asked about the ruling. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3840}

## The answer

The Department explained that harvesting (jidhadh) is a contract in which the worker picks the olives in season without tending the trees; the Shafi'i principle is that a wage must be fixed and known in advance, not a share of the yield, while the Hanbalis allow a wage as a share of the yield provided it is a proportion such as a half or a third. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3840} It said the owner's stipulating three tins of oil after pressing is invalid because of uncertainty and ignorance, since the oil may fall short of the number required. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3840} It quoted [[person/al-buhuti-mansur-ibn-yunus|al-Buhuti]]'s Daqa'iq Uli al-Nuha that handing over property to someone who works it for a common share of its growth is valid, as with trees in sharecropping, and that the forbidden 'miller's measure' refers to a fixed quantity of the output, which leaves the worker's benefit unknown. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3840} It concluded that agreeing on picking and pressing for proportional shares, such as a half or a third, is permitted, while agreeing on two or three tins of oil is not. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3840}
