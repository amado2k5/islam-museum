## Summary

A question from an employee left with surplus medicine dispensed under health insurance, in which [[group/general-iftaa-department-jordan|Jordan's General Iftaa Department]] answered that the rules for dispensing medicine must be kept and the prescription not altered, but that if medicine was dispensed lawfully for a real illness without deception, it may be sold, given away or exchanged once received. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3878}

## The question

The questioner said he works for a private company that provides staff health insurance, and that he is sometimes left with medicine from a prescription, or medicines he did not use; he asked whether he may exchange them at the pharmacy for other medicines or non-medical items of the same value. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3878}

## The answer

The Department answered that health insurance is a contract between the beneficiary and the insurer, through which care is provided to him and to those named in the contract, and that the rules regulating the dispensing of medicines must be kept. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3878} It said the principle is that medicine is dispensed only to the beneficiary according to the doctor's prescription, and he may not alter the prescription even if he does not need the medicine. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3878} It concluded that if the treatment was dispensed lawfully and as the treating doctor prescribed, without deception, and the illness genuinely required it, there is no objection to disposing of it once received, by sale, gift or exchange, since ownership passes to the beneficiary once it is dispensed in line with the insurance contract's terms. {cite:aliftaa-jo fatwa 3878}
