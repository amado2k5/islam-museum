## Summary

*The Long Divergence: How Islamic Law Held Back the Middle East* is an English book by the economist Timur Kuran, published by Princeton University Press in Princeton and Oxford in 2011 in 405 pages; Abdul Azim Islahi reviewed it in the Journal of King Abdulaziz University: Islamic Economics in 2012. {cite:openlibrary-the-long-divergence-2011} {cite:mpra-review-the-long-divergence} According to its contents, it begins with the puzzle of the Middle East's economic retardation, then treats commercial life under Islamic rule, the inheritance system, the absence of the corporation in Islamic law, credit markets without banks, the ascent of the Middle East's religious minorities and the capitulations, and closes with the question of whether Islam inhibited economic development. {cite:archive-org-the-long-divergence-2011}
