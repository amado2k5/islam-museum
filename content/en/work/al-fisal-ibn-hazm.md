## Summary
Al-Fisal fi al-Milal wa-l-Ahwa' wa-l-Nihal is a work on religions, sects and theological questions by Imam Abu Muhammad [[person/ibn-hazm|Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Hazm]], the Zahiri of al-Andalus (d. 456 AH). {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-salam-v1 p. 1} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 8} The author of his life in the Maktabat al-Salam edition calls it among the most valuable of books and the most comprehensive in examining religions, prophecies, revealed books, the opinions of philosophers, and in answering deniers of God and adherents of religions other than Islam. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-salam-v1 p. 9}

## The author's aim and method
Ibn Hazm opens by saying that many who wrote on how people divided in their religions and doctrines either wrote at length and used fallacies and wrangling, or abridged and left out the strongest objections of the doctrines' holders, thus wronging their opponents, and that most wrote so obscurely as to be hard to understand. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 8} He says his aim is to present proofs derived from premises of sense perception, or traced back to the senses closely or remotely, accepting only what those proofs establish, and to write clearly without obscurity. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 9}

## The degrees of the groups opposed to Islam
Ibn Hazm ranks the main groups opposed to Islam as six, in order of distance: deniers of reality, whom the theologians call sophists; those who hold the world eternal with no maker or governor; those who hold it eternal with an eternal governor; those who hold more than one eternal governor; those who hold one creator but deny all prophecy; and those who accept one creator and prophecy but deny some prophets. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 9} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 10} He notes other views compounded from these, such as transmigration of souls and the continuous succession of prophets in every age. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 10}

## On the Jews
Ibn Hazm notes that Jews and Christians who deny the Trinity agree with Muslims in affirming divine unity and prophecy, and that Sabians and Magians agree in affirming some prophets. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 184} He says the Jews divided into five groups, as he describes them: the [[group/samaritans|Samaritans]], the Sadducees, the Ananites (followers of Anan), the Rabbanites - the majority, who follow the rabbis' teachings - and the Isawiyya, followers of Abu Isa al-Isfahani. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 184} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 185} He says he met many learned Jews inclined to the Isawiyya's view, and read a history of theirs compiled by an Aaronide named Yusuf ibn Harun. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 185} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 186} He then divides the Jews on abrogation into those who deny it is possible and those who allow it but say it never happened, and answers them that their own laws are commands for set times, such as work being permitted on Friday and forbidden on the Sabbath. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 186} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 187}

## The sects of the Muslims as Ibn Hazm classifies them
Ibn Hazm says the groups who affirm Islam are five: Ahl al-Sunna, the Mu'tazila, the Murji'a, the Shi'a and the Khawarij; that Ahl al-Sunna differ mostly in legal rulings and a few points of belief, while among the other four some differ from Ahl al-Sunna widely and some narrowly. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 595} In his own classification:
- **The [[group/murjia|Murji'a]]**: closest to Ahl al-Sunna, for him, are those who follow Abu Hanifa in holding that faith is assent by tongue and heart together and that works are its laws; furthest are the followers of Jahm ibn Safwan, with whom he places al-Ash'ari and Muhammad ibn Karram on the basis of the definitions of faith he attributes to them. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 595} This is Ibn Hazm's own classification and terminology, built on the definition of faith.
- **The [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazila]]**: closest, for him, are the followers of al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Najjar and Bishr al-Marisi, then of Dirar ibn Amr; furthest the followers of Abu al-Hudhayl. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 595}
- **The [[group/shia|Shi'a]]**: closest, for him, are those attached to al-Hasan ibn Salih ibn Hayy; furthest the [[group/imamiyya|Imamiyya]]. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 595} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 596}
- **The [[group/khawarij|Khawarij]]**: closest, for him, are the followers of Abd Allah ibn Yazid al-[[group/ibadiyya|Ibadi]]; furthest the [[group/azariqa|Azariqa]]. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 596}
He names each group's core topic: for the Murji'a, faith, unbelief and the threat; for the Mu'tazila, divine unity and the attributes, then predestination and the intermediate position; for the Shi'a, the imamate and the ranking of the Companions; for the Khawarij, faith and unbelief, promise and imamate. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 596} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 597}

## Theological questions
Ibn Hazm then treats divine unity and the denial of anthropomorphism, knowledge and life, the face, hand and eye, the vision of God, the Qur'an and its inimitability, predestination, capacity and guidance, the sinlessness of prophets, promise and threat, intercession, the balance, the pool and the punishment of the grave, the resurrection, then the "scandalous views" of the Shi'a, Khawarij, Mu'tazila and Murji'a, then jinn, natures, the prophethood of women, dreams, the name and the named, and the "states", as the table of contents of the digital text shows. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 5} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 6} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-kotobarabia p. 7}

## His books and ordeal as the edition tells it
The author of his life in the Maktabat al-Salam edition says Ibn Hazm wrote on logic using legal examples, that his son Abu Rafi' reported his works came to some four hundred volumes of nearly eighty thousand leaves, and that his opponents managed to have some of his books burned in Seville. {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-salam-v1 p. 8} {cite:fisal-ibn-hazm-salam-v1 p. 10}
