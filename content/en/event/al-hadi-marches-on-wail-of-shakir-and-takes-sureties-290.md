## Overview
A clan of [[group/shakir-hamdan-tribe|Shakir]] called [[group/wailat-shakir|Wa'ila]] had made trouble on the road to Najran while [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] was away at Sana'a, and he said he did not think he should disperse these armies until he had passed through Wa'ila and set it right {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.244}. He set out that day until he spent the night at a place called Wasit, the night Safar began, and in the morning went on until he reached a place called Kitaf in the land of Wa'ila, which was the place of the disorderers {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.244}.

## Detail
The army plundered what money and other things they found there, cut their vines and laid it waste; he then went on to another place called al-Mutla' and did as he had done at Kitaf, and Wa'ila came to him in hearing and obedience and asked safety, which he gave; they stood surety for one another, he took a number of the "disorderers" (as printed) and returned to [[place/saada|Sa'da]], where he stayed some time {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.244} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.245}. The places Wasit, Kitaf and al-Mutla' are as the printed text and the page image read them; the book gives no coordinates, and does not say what the trouble was. This is the narrator's account of acts of war, with no judgement of the museum. See [[event/al-hadi-marches-on-ibn-abbad-and-razes-the-houses-and-vines-of-allaf-290]].
