## Summary
The detachment of al-Qarada was the last and most successful Muslim fighting patrol before the battle of Uhud; it took place in Jumada al-Akhira 3 AH (November 624 CE) under [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] with a hundred riders to intercept a great Quraysh trade caravan bound for al-Sham. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 245}

## Quraysh's trade crisis and the Iraq route
The Prophet's treaties with the coastal tribes (Juhayna, Banu Damra and Banu Mudlij) had closed the traditional coastal trade route to Quraysh, so al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Muttalib proposed to [[person/safwan-ibn-umayya|Safwan ibn Umayya]] that they take the long Iraq route running through Najd east of [[place/medina|Medina]]; they hired [[person/furat-ibn-hayyan|Furat ibn Hayyan al-Ijli]], a guide expert in the route. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 246} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 2/50}

## Medina's intelligence and the surprise
Word of the caravan and its itinerary leaked to Medina through [[person/salit-ibn-al-numan|Salit ibn al-Nu'man]], so the Prophet at once fitted out a hundred riders under Zayd ibn Haritha, who pressed the march until he surprised the caravan as it halted at the water of [[place/al-qarada|al-Qarada]] in the land of Najd. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 246}

Safwan and the caravan's guard fled leaving the goods without a fight; the Muslims seized the whole caravan and captured its guide Furat ibn Hayyan. It carried vessels and silver valued near a hundred thousand dirhams; the Prophet took the fifth and divided the rest among the detachment's men, and Furat ibn Hayyan became Muslim in Medina. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 246}
