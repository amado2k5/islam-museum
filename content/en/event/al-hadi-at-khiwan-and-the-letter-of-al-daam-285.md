## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad relates from his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] that a man of the Yemeni rulers who had taken power, [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am ibn Ibrahim]], wrote to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] asking him to appoint him over the land he held. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.91} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.92} Al-Hadi said: "No, by God, not for one hour, except on what I command him: to rule by the Book of God and the sunna of His Messenger"; if he agreed, ruled by their rule and followed what they follow, he would have from them what is due from God's wealth and be raised in rank if he repented and turned, obeying God and them in what they command of enjoining good, forbidding wrong and taking the right from whoever owes it, near or far, noble or lowly; otherwise not, and many letters passed between them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.92} The text names neither the land nor the outcome of the correspondence.

## Entering Khiwan
Al-Hadi went out with a great crowd and reached [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]] on Wednesday with two days left of the month, which by the context is Jumada I, and entered it "in the finest and easiest way"; all its people met him, who until then had not come together because of the strife, trials, ruin and exile among them, "and God joined their hearts and gathered their affairs through al-Hadi", so they mixed and entered with him; he reconciled them and made them swear to one another to end the feud and stand with him on the truth, and they heard and obeyed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.92} That is the Zaydi narrator's interpretation of the event, which the museum attributes to him.

## The stay
He stayed at Khiwan, while the clans of [[group/banu-hamdan|Hamdan]] kept coming to him, pledging and swearing to hear and obey, for the rest of Jumada I, Jumada II, Rajab and twenty-three days of Sha'ban. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.92} He then left Khiwan on a Thursday with some days left of the month for "Batnat Hujur", the account continuing on p. 93. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.92}
