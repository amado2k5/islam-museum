## Summary
In 780 Ibn al-Mulaqqin — a companion of Barquq — wrote in his own hand a promised sum to Amir Baraka while seeking the judgeship; Barquq denied the arrangement, produced the letter and, furious, confined him in the stable bound for the money, until Akmal al-Din al-Babarti and Siraj al-Din al-Bulqini won his release. He never entered the judicial deputyship again {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 dhayl p. 123}.



## The exchange — what each side said
- **The recorded report**: Ibn al-Mulaqqin was tried in 780 AH — he was companion to Sultan Barquq, and wrote in his own hand to the amir Baraka a sum toward a judicial office; Barquq denied it, the letter in his own hand was produced before him, and he grew angry, sentencing him to the stable and obliging him the money, until he was released through the efforts of Akmal al-Din al-Babarti and Siraj al-Din al-Bulqini. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 dhayl p. 123}
- **Barquq (denial then anger)**: denied the charge at first; when his own letter to Baraka was set before him he grew angry — the public denial fails before the written hand, and turned into a sentence of confinement and a fine.
- **Ibn al-Mulaqqin (his stance as it appears)**: no word of his is transmitted in the confrontation; his recorded stance is endurance of the stable and payment of the obligation — silence before the incriminating hand.
- **The interceders (mediation)**: Akmal al-Din al-Babarti and Siraj al-Din al-Bulqini worked for his release — the worth of intercession in the sultan's council.
- **What is not preserved**: the report gives the event, not the argument's wording — what Ibn al-Mulaqqin said in his defence is not transmitted in the Durar, a single narration here needing a second witness. {cite:durar-kamina-jil-1993 dhayl p. 123}
