## Overview
Al-Suyuti reports that [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]] gave the oath of succession to his son Muhammad in 175 AH, titled him al-Amin, when he was five years old, at the urging of his mother [[person/zubayda-bint-jafar|Zubayda]]. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.463-464}

## The three sons
After him he gave the oath to his son Abd Allah in 182 AH, titled him al-Ma'mun, and appointed him over Khurasan. Then to his son al-Qasim in 186 AH, titled him al-Mu'tamin, and appointed him over al-Jazira and the frontiers. He hung a copy of the oath in the Ancient House. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.463-464}

## The views of historians
Al-Dhahabi said: this was the first weakness to befall the Islamic state with regard to the imamate. Some wise men said: he cast their arrows among them, and the danger of that harms the subjects. It is also said that al-Rashid turned the caliphate away from his son al-Mu'tasim because he was unlettered, and God brought it to him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.463-464}

Al-Amin: [[person/muhammad-al-amin|Muhammad al-Amin]]. Al-Ma'mun: [[person/abd-allah-al-mamun|Abd Allah al-Ma'mun]].
