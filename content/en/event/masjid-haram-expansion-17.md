## Summary
In 17 AH [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|'Umar ibn al-Khattab]] performed 'umra and rebuilt and enlarged the Masjid al-Haram in [[place/mecca|Mecca]], demolishing the houses of those who refused to sell and depositing their prices in the bayt al-mal; he stayed twenty nights in Mecca and ordered the boundary markers of the haram renewed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 360}

## Details
Recording the events of year seventeen, Ibn al-Athir relates that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab performed 'umra, built the Masjid al-Haram and enlarged it, and remained in Mecca for twenty nights {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 360}. To make room for the enlargement he demolished the houses of people who had refused to sell, placing the prices of their houses in the public treasury until they later took them; his 'umra fell in the month of Rajab {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 360}. During his absence he left Zayd ibn Thabit in charge of [[place/medina|Medina]] {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 360}. He also ordered the renewal of the ansab — the markers of the haram boundary which may not be crossed in a state of prohibition — entrusting the task to Makhrama ibn Nawfal and al-Azhar ibn 'Abd 'Awf {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 360}. These works formed part of 'Umar's care for the sanctuary and its rites during his caliphate, within a year that also saw the founding of Kufa and Basra and the conquests of al-Jazira and Fars {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 360}.
