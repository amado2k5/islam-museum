## Overview
The "chapter on the humility of Yahya ibn al-Husayn" opens with Ali ibn Muhammad's report from his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] that part of [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s humility was giving up pride and arrogance in his gathering and elsewhere, in his food and drink. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.53} The praise is the narrator's, and the museum does not state it as a finding.

## Greetings and visiting the sick
When he left his house for prayer or any other purpose he greeted everyone he passed, noble or lowly, poor or rich, slave or child; the narrator says the tradition came down the same of his grandfather [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]], and that he saw him visit the sick, even a servant of one of his companions. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.53} Hearing a man say to him "may I be your ransom, master", he said: do not say this again, the Master is God and I am a lowly servant; a man present cited a verse of Al Imran ([[passage/quran-3-39|Qur'an 3:39]]), and he answered yes, but I do not like to be addressed so. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.53}

## The call in the mosque
After the Friday prayer a man he had told stood up and called in the mosque: where are the poor, where the needy, where the travellers, where is anyone with a need, is there a petitioner to be given or one seeking a need to be met? A stranger stood and said "O son of the Messenger of God, I am naked"; al-Hadi stood with him a long time in the mosque until he had said all he wished, then ordered him clothing and ample provision. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.53} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.54}

## The two old women
After the afternoon prayer a woman cried out "O son of the Messenger of God"; he stopped, an old woman came near and took hold of his garment, a servant scolded her, and he said leave her; she complained of being wronged while he stood until she had finished, and he then called [[person/muhammad-ibn-sulayman-al-kufi|Abu Jafar Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Kufi]] and told him to go with her and secure her right, and the opponent was brought and the matter settled. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.54} On a night, near his house, another old woman cried that she was wronged; a servant motioned her away and al-Hadi said "leave her, glory to God, you are nothing but a tyrant!", and sent al-Kufi to see her done justice; then he told the boy at the door: did I not tell you to bring me every weak person, are you Muslims? {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.55}

## Note
Al-Hadi calls al-Kufi "Abu Jafar", so the kunya is shared in the book and does not by itself settle who the narrator of the earlier sayings is. A few damaged words are read from context.
