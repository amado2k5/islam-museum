## Overview
Khuza'a's expulsion of Jurhum from Mecca in the mid-second century CE, after they wronged newcomers and made free with the Ka'ba's treasure — their blocking of Zamzam and their departure to Yemen.

## The story
[[group/jurhum|Jurhum]]'s state in Mecca soured and their affairs straitened; they wronged those who came to it and made free with the Ka'ba's wealth, which enraged the Adnanis {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. When [[group/khuzaa|Khuza'a]] settled at Marr al-Zahran and saw the Adnanis' estrangement from Jurhum, they seized on it: with help from subtribes of Adnan — Banu Bakr ibn Abd Manaf ibn Kinana — they warred on Jurhum until they expelled them from Mecca and took over its rule, in the mid-second century CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

As Jurhum faced exile they blocked the well of Zamzam, effaced its site and buried things in it; Ibn Ishaq says: Amr ibn al-Harith ibn Mudad al-Jurhumi went off with the two gazelles of the Ka'ba and the stone of the Black Corner and buried them in the well of Zamzam {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}. Al-Mubarakpuri estimates Jurhum's residence in Mecca at twenty-one centuries and their rule over it at some twenty centuries {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab}.

## For young visitors
Jurhum ruled Mecca for an age; when they mistreated the pilgrims, Khuza'a fought them with their allies until they drove them out — and the departing people buried treasures in the well of Zamzam and blocked it.
