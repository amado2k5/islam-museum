## Overview
Al-Baladhuri, in "Futuh al-Buldan", reports from some scholars of Syria and from Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Salam that the people of Cyprus created an incident during the governorship of [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-salih|Abd al-Malik ibn Salih]] over the frontiers. He wanted to break their treaty, and wrote to the jurists of his time to ask their opinion. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The jurists consulted
Those named are [[person/layth-ibn-sad|al-Layth ibn Sa'd]], [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik ibn Anas]], [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Sufyan ibn Uyayna]], Musa ibn A'yan, [[person/ismail-ibn-ayyash|Isma'il ibn Ayyash]], Yahya ibn Hamza, [[person/abu-ishaq-al-fazari|Abu Ishaq al-Fazari]] and Mukhallad ibn al-Husayn. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Their answers
- Al-Layth ibn Sa'd accuses them of deceiving the Muslims and conspiring with the Romans, and holds that the covenant should be cast off toward them and that they be given a year to choose between staying as protected subjects, joining the land of the Romans, or continuing the war. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
- Malik ibn Anas holds that their safe conduct is old and was confirmed by the governors, and that the covenant should not be broken hastily until proof is established against them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
- Sufyan ibn Uyayna knows of no people who broke a covenant with God and were not then made lawful to kill, except the people of Mecca. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
- Musa ibn A'yan holds to keep faith with them on their condition, and reports from al-Awza'i that a protected subject who guides the polytheists to the weak points of the Muslims leaves his protection, and that the people of a treaty are notified on equal terms. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
- Isma'il ibn Ayyash sees them as subdued and overcome by the Romans, so it is right for the Muslims to protect them and keep them to their covenant. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
- Yahya ibn Hamza compares their case to that of Arabsus in the days of Umar, and says they are a people of ransom, not a people of protection. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
- Abu Ishaq al-Fazari and Mukhallad ibn al-Husayn see nothing like the case of Cyprus except Arabsus. They report from al-Awza'i that Cyprus was reconciled for fourteen thousand dinars, seven for the Muslims and seven for the Romans, and that a treaty is not broken except by an order in which their treachery is known. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The answers are given here as al-Baladhuri relates them, and some concern the rules of the [[concept/dhimma|protected status]]. The museum does not judge between these opinions. What is not covered here: the governor's final decision, which needs an additional source.
