## Summary
In 621 AH Muzaffar al-Din Kukburi ibn Zayn al-Din, lord of Erbil, besieged the city of Mosul, investing it on 13 Jumada al-Akhira, then withdrew after ten days. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 441}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that the lord of Erbil had agreed with Shihab al-Din Ghazi of Akhlat and al-Mu'azzam Isa of Damascus to march on al-Malik al-Ashraf's lands; the lord of Damascus turned back because his brother, the lord of Egypt, wrote threatening to come and besiege Damascus if he left it, while Ghazi was besieged in Akhlat and lost it; the lord of Erbil gathered his army, marched on Mosul and besieged it, expecting al-Ashraf to abandon Akhlat when he heard. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 441} Mosul's lord Badr al-Din Lu'lu' had secured its affairs — troops posted on the walls, siege engines displayed, supplies brought out — and what emboldened the lord of Erbil was that most of its garrison had gone with al-Ashraf to Akhlat leaving few troops inside, while prices were severe everywhere, at three makkuks per dinar in Mosul; he stayed before it ten days then departed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 441}
