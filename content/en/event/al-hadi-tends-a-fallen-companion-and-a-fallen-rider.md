## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] says he saw [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] go out into the open country one day when one of his companions, [[person/muhammad-ibn-abbas-al-sanani|Muhammad ibn Abbas al-San'ani]], was struck by an illness and lay fallen on the ground. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.55} Al-Hadi dismounted to him, wiped his face with his hand and recited over him, then had a servant bring a donkey and the man rode to [[place/saada|Sa'da]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.55} At al-Hadi's house the man sat before him; he asked about his state, had a boy bring pomegranates, peeled them with his own hand, took out the seeds and passed them to the man, who ate; and he said, "I see you walking on the ground and it weighs on me, but be glad, you are in great good"; the man rose, recovered from his illness. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.55}

## A rider thrown from his horse
He relates seeing him ride out one day when a companion fell from his horse and hit his nose on the ground, and he treated him with his hand and recited over him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.55} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.56} Then he called a servant of his and said: bring every weak person to me, and do not burn me and yourself in the Fire, for I have taken the matter off my neck and put it on yours. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.56} This is a report from a Zaydi book; the treatment by recitation and touch is the narrator's description, and the museum draws no medical conclusion from it and does not assert the cure.
