## Summary
Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak, the scholar of Marw and one of the imams of hadith, asceticism and frontier campaigning, died at Hit on the Euphrates while returning from a campaign, in Ramadan 181 AH, aged sixty-three in the best-known accounts. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 775} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 568} The books report that a man sat at his head prompting him to say the declaration of faith, and that he asked the man not to repeat the prompt unless he spoke some other words afterwards, so that it would be his last utterance. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478}

## Context
Al-Dhahabi reports from Abdan ibn Uthman that Ibn al-Mubarak first travelled to Iraq in 141 AH and died at Hit and Anat in Ramadan 181 AH. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 246} Ibn Khallikan says he had been on campaign and, on his return from it, turned aside to Hit and died there. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 264} Hit is a town on the Euphrates above al-Anbar, and Anat a town between al-Raqqa and Hit overlooking the river. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 241} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 246} Ahmad ibn Hanbal is quoted as saying that he went to hear from Ibn al-Mubarak but missed him, since he had come to Baghdad and then left for the frontier. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478}

## His last words
Ahmad ibn Abd Allah al-Ijli reports from his father that when Ibn al-Mubarak was dying a man began prompting him, 'Say: there is no god but God,' and pressed him with it. He answered: 'You do not do it well, and I fear you will trouble a Muslim after me. If you prompt me and I say it, and then I utter no other words, leave me; if I do speak afterwards, prompt me again so that it is my last speech.' {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 246}
Sifat al-Safwa gives the report on the authority of al-Hasan ibn al-Rabi', who was present, and names the prompter Nusayr: he said, 'Abu Abd al-Rahman, say there is no god but God,' and Ibn al-Mubarak replied, 'Nusayr, you see how hard speech is for me. When you hear me say it, do not repeat it to me until you hear that I have spoken after it'; the author adds that they liked this to be the last words of a servant. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 774} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 775} The pages I read do not say who Nusayr was, nor that he was a freedman.
Al-Hasan ibn al-Rabi' also reports that Ibn al-Mubarak told him before he died that he was sixty-three. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478}

## Death and burial
Al-Hasan ibn al-Rabi', quoted by al-Dhahabi from al-Fasawi, says he witnessed the death: Ibn al-Mubarak died at dawn on the tenth of Ramadan 181 and they buried him at Hit. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478} Sifat al-Safwa says he died at Hit on his way back from the campaign on the thirteenth of Ramadan 181, aged sixty-three. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 775} Ibn Kathir records the death at Hit in Ramadan of that year at sixty-three and quotes Ibn Abd al-Barr that the scholars agreed on his acceptance and leadership. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 568}
The books add that when news of his death reached al-Rashid he said, 'The master of the scholars died today'; another version says he said the formula of return ('to God we belong') and told al-Fadl to let people come to give condolences. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2477} Both are given as 'it is said', not with a chain.

## The grave
The editor of Tarikh al-Islam notes, on the place of death, that Hit is a town on the Euphrates above al-Anbar and 'the grave of Ibn al-Mubarak is there'. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 241} Al-Dhahabi quotes verses attributed to 'some of the learned' (to the vizier Ibn al-Maghribi in Tarikh al-Islam) in which the speaker passes the grave one morning and is admonished by it, though it speaks no word. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 248} In the pages I read there is no description of the grave or any building over it.

## Where the reports differ
The year is 181 in most books; Ibn Khallikan adds that 182 is also said. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 264} The day differs: the tenth of Ramadan in al-Hasan ibn al-Rabi's account, the thirteenth in Sifat al-Safwa. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2478} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 775} I found nothing in these books about a will concerning money or an estate, nor about his washing, shroud or funeral prayer.
