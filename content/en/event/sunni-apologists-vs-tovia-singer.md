## Summary
Digital encounters between Sunni speakers and Orthodox rabbis such as Tovia Singer — the Muslim–Jewish debate's return to the network. {cite:youtube-sunni-jewish-debate}

## Themes
The Talmud and Oral Torah, Masoretic textual integrity, the scope of prophecy, and Deuteronomy 18:18 — the axes of two strict monotheisms, unlike the Trinity-centred Christian debates.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **The Sunni interlocutors (Hijab, Rashid, Ali Dawah and others)**: carried Deuteronomy 18:18 — 'a prophet like you from among their brothers' — to the nations at large, not Israel alone, demanding a measure that admits a prophet from outside the line; and they queried the Masoretic text's transmission soundness.
- **Singer**: 'their brothers' in Deuteronomy's context is Israel itself — the near context's comparison concerns Israelites, and the promised prophet stands inside the covenant; and he defended the Oral Torah as parallel revelation preserving text and interpretation.
- **The Sunni interlocutors**: the Oral Torah is itself a late-redacted text — they put the question of an unwritten chain against the Qur'anic reading of scripture.
- **Singer**: late redaction does not erase ancient tradition — the difference is the date of writing, not of origin — and the Masoretic text is the most exact in letter.
- **The verdict**: a continuing textual-transmission exchange across platforms — Deuteronomy 18:18 and the Oral Torah question are its file's two headings. {cite:youtube-tovia-singer-debates}
