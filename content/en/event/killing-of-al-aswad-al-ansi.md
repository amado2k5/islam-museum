## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates that al-Aswad al-Ansi claimed prophethood in Yemen, took control of [[place/sanaa|Sanaa]], and was then killed there by magic at the hands of a group of its people. This account is part of the events of the [[event/ridda-wars|Ridda wars]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The claimant and Sanaa
Al-Baladhuri says that al-Aswad ibn Ka'b al-Ansi practised soothsaying and claimed prophethood, and a group from the Ans and others followed him. He called himself "the Compassionate of Yemen". The accounts differ about the reason for the title. He says that the Prophet sent [[person/jarir-ibn-abd-allah-al-bajali|Jarir ibn Abd Allah al-Bajali]] to call him to Islam, and he did not answer; some transmitters deny that this mission took place. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

He then entered Sanaa and took control of it, and expelled the Prophet's agent there. He harmed the "sons", the Persian families whom Khosrow had sent to Yemen. He married the wife of their king, Badhan. The transmitters differ on the name of the agent who was expelled. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Planning the killing
The Prophet sent [[person/qays-ibn-hubayra|Qays ibn Hubayra]] to win over the sons, and sent [[person/farwa-ibn-musayk|Farwa ibn Musayk]] with him. When they reached Yemen, news of the Prophet's death reached them. Qays showed his agreement with al-Aswad until he entered Sanaa, then he won over [[person/fayruz-al-daylami|Fayruz al-Daylami]] from the sons, and they agreed to kill him. The wife of al-Aswad, who disliked him, informed the plotters and showed them an entrance to his house. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The killing
They entered on him before dawn while he was asleep and drunk, and Qays slaughtered him. Then he climbed the city wall and shouted the takbir; when al-Aswad's followers gathered, he threw down his head to them and they dispersed. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Differing accounts
Al-Baladhuri reports from some transmitters that Fayruz was the killer, and that Qays only cut off the head. He mentions that Umar said "this lion killed him", meaning Fayruz. Some scholars say that the killing took place five days before the Prophet's death, and that the news reached Abu Bakr ten nights into his caliphate. These details need an additional source. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Afterwards Qays was accused of killing Dadhawayh. Abu Bakr summoned him and made him swear fifty oaths that he had not killed him, and then released him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
