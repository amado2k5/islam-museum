## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates on several chains that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] wished to take the poll tax from the Christians of the [[group/banu-taghlib|Banu Taghlib]], and they fled, and some of them crossed the Euphrates heading for Byzantine land. [[person/zura-ibn-al-numan|Al-Nu'man ibn Zur'a]] interceded for them, so Umar returned them and made peace with them on doubling the alms taken from the Muslims. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.249-252}

## The conditions
They accepted double the alms and said it was not like the poll tax of the foreigners. He stipulated that they should not make a boy Christian and not force him into their religion. Umar sent [[person/ziyad-ibn-hudayr|Ziyad ibn Hudayr]] to take half a tenth of their property. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.249-252}

## Differing views
Al-Baladhuri relates from Dawud ibn Kardus that they had no protection because they had baptised their children. He quotes [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] saying that they broke the covenant by making their children Christian. He says that al-Zuhri held that there is no alms on the livestock of the People of the Book except the Christians of the Banu Taghlib. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.249-252}

He says that Sufyan al-Thawri, al-Awza'i, Malik, Ibn Abi Dhi'b, [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] and Abu Yusuf held that double what is taken from a Muslim should be taken. He details that the people of Iraq and the Hijaz differed over the livestock of a child or an imbecile. They agreed that what is taken is spent on the same objects as the land tax, because it is a substitute for the poll tax. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.249-252}

The museum passes no judgement on the question. The date is approximate, around 20 AH, and the conversion is derived. See [[concept/jizya|the poll tax]] and [[concept/dhimma|protection]].
