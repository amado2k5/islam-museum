## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] ordered his men to go back to [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]]; the head of the army went on, but a party from its rear turned and joined the fight and did not hear his cry until they had entered a narrow place; they were Tabari men with no archer or swordsman among them, and the men closed on them with arrows and stones from every side {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.236} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.237}. Al-Hadi went back to turn them from the fight, stood in the faces of the men for his companions until his army had passed the rough road, then charged their horse and pierced one of them; then he found the way closed against him, charged again and threw a man with his spear; his horse was struck by a stone at the base of the ear and fell with him, and a man hit him from near by with a stone on the head so that he fell in a faint, and the men all rushed him and wounded him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.237}.

## Detail
The narrator says that his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] turned, overtook a horseman who meant to spear al-Hadi and threw him, and killed another who was striking al-Hadi, and that men of [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|the Tabaris]] stood fighting in front of him until all were "martyred", in the narrator's word {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.237}. Ali ibn Muhammad reports hearing [[person/abu-maymun-ahmad-ibn-muhammad-ibn-bahlul-al-sanani|Abu Maymun Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Bahlul al-San'ani]], one of the learned men of Sana'a, say that the people do not know the full worth of Abu al-Qasim on that day, and that nothing revived Islam but Abu al-Qasim; this is praise by the man quoted, not a judgement of the museum {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.237}. A cry went through the army that al-Hadi was killed; some companions turned back, brought him his horse and mounted him, and the people went on, with Abu al-Qasim behind his father and horsemen fighting on the right and left {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.237} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.238}. Al-Hadi then halted, called men he knew who stood with him, turned on the men and drove them off, and ordered the foot to go on; when blood from the wounds of his head covered his face and eyes he drew aside and told his son to stand with the horse until his face was washed, then rode on as the people passed; Abu al-Qasim turned with horsemen on the men and killed two riders, and the narrator counts many Tabaris and men of Sana'a among those who were killed that day {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.238}. Abu al-Qasim here is al-Hadi's son, probably al-Murtada Muhammad, not certain. See also [[event/the-night-raid-on-zabwa-and-the-death-of-abu-al-qasim-al-jafari-289]].
