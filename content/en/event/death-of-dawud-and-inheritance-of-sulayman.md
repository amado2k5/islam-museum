## Summary
Ibn Kathir, in his Stories of the Prophets, reports that [[person/dawud|Dawud]] (David) died suddenly after the angel of death came to him in his house, and that his son [[person/sulayman|Sulayman]] (Solomon) saw to him after his death and took over after him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 605} The verse of al-Naml ([[passage/quran-27-16|Qur'an 27:16]]) says that Sulayman inherited from Dawud; Ibn Kathir says this means he inherited prophethood and kingship, and that inheritance of wealth is not what is meant. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 609}

## Dawud's age and the length of his reign
Ibn Kathir mentions a hadith reported by Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi, which al-Tirmidhi declared sound, in which God gave Dawud forty years out of Adam's life, so that Dawud's life came to a hundred years. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 604} He also quotes Ibn Jarir that the People of the Book claimed an age of seventy-seven; Ibn Kathir rejects this as a mistake, but says that their report that his reign lasted forty years may be accepted because he has nothing that contradicts it. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 604}

## How death came to him
Ahmad reports in his Musnad from Abu Hurayra, with a chain Ibn Kathir calls good, that Dawud was very jealous of his household's privacy, and whenever he went out he locked the doors so that nobody could enter until he returned. One day, after he had gone out, his wife saw a man standing in the middle of the locked house. When Dawud came back he asked the man who he was, and the man said he was the one who does not fear kings and is not kept out by door-keepers. Dawud said: then you are the angel of death, welcome to God's command; and he stayed until his soul was taken. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 604} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 605} The Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya gives the same report. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 365}

The reports differ on the day. From al-Suddi, on the authority of Ibn Abbas and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, he died suddenly on a Saturday; from al-Hasan, he died at a hundred years old, suddenly, on a Wednesday. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 605} Ibn Kathir also quotes Abu al-Sakan al-Hajari that Ibrahim, Dawud and Sulayman all died suddenly. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 605} Another report, given only "from some of them", says the angel of death came as Dawud was coming down from his prayer chamber and told him that the years, months, traces and provisions were exhausted; he fell in prostration on one of the steps and was taken while prostrating. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 605}

## Washing, shrouding and the funeral
The same hadith of Ahmad continues that after Dawud had been washed and shrouded and everything was finished, the sun rose over him. Sulayman told the birds to shade him, and they did until the earth went dark; then he told them to fold one wing. Abu Hurayra says that the Prophet began to show how the birds did it and closed his hand, and that the long-winged falcons predominated in the shading that day. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 605}

In a report that Ishaq ibn Bishr gives from Wahb ibn Munabbih, the people attended his funeral on a summer day, forty thousand monks in hooded cloaks followed the bier besides everyone else, and the Israelites had not mourned anyone since Musa and Harun as they mourned Dawud. When the heat troubled them they called on Sulayman; he told the birds to shade them, they pressed together until the wind was stopped and the people nearly suffocated, so he told them to shade from the side of the sun and leave the side of the wind open. This was the first thing the people saw of Sulayman's kingship. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 606} The editor of the Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya notes at that point that Ishaq ibn Bishr was criticised by a number of hadith authorities. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 365}

Ibn Kathir also gives a hadith from Abu al-Darda that God took Dawud from among his companions before they had been tried or had changed; he calls it strange, with doubt over its attribution to the Prophet, and notes that one transmitter, al-Wadin ibn Ata, was weak in hadith. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 606}

## What Sulayman inherited
Ibn Kathir explains the verse of al-Naml as Sulayman inheriting from his father in prophethood and kingship, not in wealth, because Dawud had other sons and Sulayman would not have been singled out for wealth over them, and because it is established in the sound collections that the Prophet said that prophets are not inherited from and that what they leave is charity. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 609} Al-Qurtubi reports from al-Kalbi that Dawud had nineteen sons, and that Sulayman alone inherited his prophethood and kingship; had it been inheritance of wealth, all the sons would have shared equally. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5106} He quotes Ibn al-Arabi that if wealth were meant it would have been divided by the number of heirs, so God singled out Sulayman for wisdom and prophethood and added a kingdom such as no one after him would have. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5106}

Al-Qurtubi also quotes Ibn Atiyya that Dawud was a king of the Israelites and that Sulayman inherited his kingdom and his station of prophethood in the sense that these passed to him after his father's death, so it was called inheritance figuratively, like the saying that scholars are the heirs of the prophets. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5106} Al-Qurtubi himself prefers the first view, saying that the general wording "we, the company of prophets, are not inherited from" allows no exception without evidence. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5106} The wider question is set out in [[concept/prophets-leave-no-inheritance|the record on "The prophets leave no inheritance"]].

## Bayt al-Maqdis after Dawud
Ibn al-Athir says that Dawud began building the mosque at the place of the rock after the plague was lifted from the people, that he died before finishing it and charged Sulayman with completing it, and that Sulayman buried his father and then carried out his orders against the commander who had killed his brother. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 178} Ibn al-Athir then gives another account, that it was Sulayman who began the building and Dawud had only wished to build it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 178} Al-Tabari says that Sulayman began building Bayt al-Maqdis in the fourth year of his reign, "as it is reported". {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 603}

## What these books do not give
The books on which this record is built do not address where Dawud was buried. Everything above is reported from Companions, Successors and those who took from the People of the Book, and Ibn Kathir points out weakness in some of it, so this record attributes it to its narrators and states none of it in the museum's own voice. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 606}
