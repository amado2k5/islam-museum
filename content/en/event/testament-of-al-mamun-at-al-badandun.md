## Summary
The caliph [[person/abd-allah-al-mamun|Abd Allah al-Ma'mun]] made his testament while ill at al-Badandun in Byzantine territory, calling witnesses in the presence of his son [[person/al-abbas-ibn-al-mamun|al-Abbas]], the judges, commanders, viziers and scribes, and left the caliphate to his brother [[person/al-mutasim|al-Mu'tasim]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 677} Al-Dhahabi gives extracts from the text: a declaration of God's unity, an affirmation of the resurrection, an instruction about his body and counsel to his brother. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 26} He died a few days later and was carried to [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]] and buried there. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 240} This record completes the [[event/death-of-mamun|record of al-Ma'mun's death]]; the museum also holds a draft card for the [[building/tomb-of-al-mamun|tomb of al-Ma'mun]] whose sources are not yet checked.

## Context: the illness at al-Badandun
Al-Dhahabi says al-Ma'mun marched on campaign into Byzantine land, and when he reached al-Badandun and his illness grew heavy he bequeathed the caliphate to his brother al-Mu'tasim; in the campaign he had taken fourteen fortresses, then camped at the spring of al-Badandun, stayed there and fell ill. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 239} Quoting al-Mas'udi, he says the coolness and clarity of the spring pleased him, and when he saw a silver-like fish caught by an attendant, it thrashed and splashed his chest and clothes; a shivering took hold of him and he was covered with blankets. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 239} As his illness worsened he sent for his son al-Abbas to come, and letters had already gone out to the provinces as from Abd Allah al-Ma'mun and his brother Abu Ishaq, the caliph after him; it is said this was at his order, and it is said it was written during a faint that came over him. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 25}

## The testament and its content
Al-Dhahabi relates that the testament began by calling witness that God alone has no partner, that He is Creator and all else created, that the Qur'an cannot be other than a thing with a likeness, whereas God has none, that the resurrection is true and that he is a sinner who hopes and fears. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 25} He instructed about his body: when I die, lay me out toward the qibla, let the nearest of you in kinship pray over me and let him say five takbirs. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 26} The editor of al-Dhahabi's text refers in a note to the full text in al-Tabari and in Ibn al-Athir. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 26} In his admonition he says that the glory of the caliphate did not avail him when God's command came, that its only use is that God multiply his good deeds through it, and he wished he had not been human, indeed that he had been nothing. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 26}

## Counsel to al-Mu'tasim
Al-Ma'mun addressed his brother: Abu Ishaq, come near, take warning from what you see, follow your brother's way regarding the Qur'an, work in the caliphate like one who seeks God and fears His punishment, and do not neglect the subjects, for the realm is by them. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 26} He took from him God's covenant that he would uphold God's right over His servants and prefer obedience to disobedience. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 26} He commended Abd Allah ibn Tahir, to be kept in his post, and Ahmad ibn Abi Du'ad, not to be parted from, forbade him to take a vizier after him and warned him of Yahya ibn Aktham. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 26} He commended his cousins of the line of Ali ibn Abi Talib, to treat them well, overlook those who did wrong and give them gifts, and Ibn Kathir adds to keep up their gifts every year. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 677}

## What is said about the doctrine of the Qur'an's createdness
Ibn Kathir reports that al-Ma'mun charged al-Mu'tasim to hold what his brother held about the Qur'an and to call people to it, and that he died on it without repenting, {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 677} and al-Dhahabi says he held firm to the question in the year 218 and put the scholars to trial. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 239} Sunni scholars rejected this doctrine: al-Dhahabi relates that when those held for the trial were brought, Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Sajjada, Muhammad ibn Nuh and al-Qawariri first declined to answer and were put in fetters; Sajjada and then al-Qawariri answered, and Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Muhammad ibn Nuh were sent to Tarsus. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 25} In his biography of al-Ma'mun Ibn Kathir quotes lines by a Sunni poet answering those who say the Qur'an is created. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 676} Al-Dhahabi relates that Ahmad ibn Hanbal said he had prayed that God not bring him together with al-Ma'mun, and he did not see him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 940}

## The last hours and death
Al-Dhahabi relates that he asked the meaning of the name al-Badandun in Arabic and was told 'stretch your legs', which he took as an ill omen; he asked the name of the place and was told al-Raqqa, and his birth-chart had said he would die at al-Raqqa, so he knew and despaired, and said: O You whose dominion does not pass, have mercy on one whose dominion has passed. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 239} Al-Mu'tasim seated someone by him to prompt the declaration of faith, and Ibn Masawayh told him not to raise his voice; then al-Ma'mun raised his gaze to heaven with his eyes full of tears and said: O You who do not die, have mercy on one who dies, and passed away. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 240}

## The funeral and burial
He died on Thursday, twelve nights before the end of Rajab of the year 218, aged about forty-eight. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 240} His brother al-Mu'tasim prayed over him and he was buried at Tarsus in the house of Khaqan the servant. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 677} Al-Dhahabi says his son al-Abbas and his brother al-Mu'tasim carried him to Tarsus. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 18 p. 240} Another report says he died four stages outside Tarsus and was carried to it, and another that he was moved to Adhana in Ramadan and buried there. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 677}

## Where the reports differ
The day of his death is given as Thursday, at noon or after the afternoon prayer, or Tuesday, or Wednesday eight days before the end of Rajab. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 677} Al-Dhahabi describes al-Badandun as a river of the Byzantine land. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 940} The pages read give no detail of his washing and shrouding beyond his instruction to lay him out and say five takbirs.

## Sources and documentation
The record rests on al-Dhahabi and Ibn Kathir. The full text of the testament is in al-Tabari and is not among the pages read, so what appears here is the extract as al-Dhahabi and Ibn Kathir give it.
