## Summary
From his youth Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj journeyed to the Hijaz, Iraq, Syria and Egypt in the quest for hadith, entering Baghdad more than once so that its people transmitted from him {cite:wafayat-ayan v p. 195}. His masters were many: foremost Yahya ibn Yahya al-Naysaburi (d. 224-6), Abd Allah ibn Maslama al-Qa'nabi (d. 220-1) and Qutayba ibn Sa'id (d. 240), all major pupils of Malik ibn Anas; also Sa'id ibn Mansur (d. 227), Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 241), Ishaq ibn Rahwayh (d. 237-8) and Muhammad ibn Bashshar Bundar (d. 252), and in the Hijaz Isma'il ibn Abi Uways (d. 226-7) and Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Ansari {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 690-691}.
