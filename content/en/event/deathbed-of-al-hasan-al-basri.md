## Summary
[[person/al-hasan-al-basri|Al-Hasan al-Basri]] died at Basra in 110 AH. Ibn Sa'd reports that Isma'il ibn 'Ulayya placed it in Rajab and that a hundred days separated him from [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Muhammad ibn Sirin]], whom he preceded. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 177} The biographical dictionaries preserve reports of his illness, his last testimony, the books he ordered burned, his washing, and a funeral that drew the whole city; this record sets them out under their narrators.

## His illness and last words
Ibn Sa'd reports from Thabit that he visited al-Hasan in his illness while his son made his words understood, and that he kept repeating the formula of return. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 174} Salam ibn Miskin says they visited him when sick and he glanced at them and said: if only the son of Adam would take from his health for the day of his sickness. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 174} Al-Dhahabi quotes Yunus: when death came to him he kept saying the formula of return, and his son said, "Father, you have grieved us; have you seen something?" He answered that it was his own soul, and he had never been afflicted with the like. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1462}

## His testimony at death
Abu Tariq al-Sa'di says he saw al-Hasan dictate his will at the point of death: he told a scribe to write his testimony that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's Messenger, and that whoever bears it truthfully at death enters Paradise. The report adds that this is related of Mu'adh ibn Jabal, who bequeathed the same at his death, and is related from the Prophet. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 174} The will as transmitted is therefore a written profession of faith; this report assigns no division of property.

## His books
Ibn Sa'd reports from Sahl ibn Husayn, who asked Abd Allah ibn al-Hasan for his father's books. Abd Allah replied that when al-Hasan grew heavy he said, "Gather them for me." They did so without knowing his purpose; then he had the servant fire the oven and ordered them burned, except one sheet, which was sent to Sahl. Abd Allah repeated the account to him in person. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 175} The report gives no reason.

## Washing, prayer and burial
Ibn Sa'd relates from Hammad ibn Zayd that al-Hasan died on the night of Friday; [[person/ayyub-al-sakhtiyani|Ayyub]] and [[person/humayd-al-tawil|Humayd al-Tawil]] washed him, and he was carried out when the people had dispersed; the narrator adds that his father took him along. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 177} In Ibn Khallikan, Humayd al-Tawil says al-Hasan died on Thursday evening, that on Friday morning they finished his affairs, and that they carried him after the Friday prayer and buried him. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 3 p. 161} Al-Dhahabi adds that the prayer over him was said right after the Friday prayer at Basra. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1462}

## The funeral and the afternoon prayer
Ibn Khallikan says everyone followed the bier and was absorbed in it, so that the afternoon prayer was not held in the congregational mosque, and that he knew of no earlier day in Islam when that had been neglected. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 3 p. 161} Al-Dhahabi calls the funeral a great public occasion: the people escorted it and crowded round until the afternoon prayer was not held in the mosque. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1462} Ibn Khallikan also relates that al-Hasan fainted in his master's presence and, reviving, said they had woken him from gardens, springs and a noble station. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 3 p. 162} Al-Dhahabi gives this one in the hedged form "it is said". {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1462}

## The effect on Ibn Sirin
Ibn Sa'd relates that a man told Ibn Sirin he had dreamed a bird took the finest pebble in the mosque; Ibn Sirin said that if the dream was true al-Hasan had died, and before long he did. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 174} Hisham ibn Hassan says they were with Ibn Sirin on Thursday evening when a man came in after the afternoon prayer and said al-Hasan had died; Muhammad asked God's mercy for him, his colour changed, and he said nothing until sunset. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1462} Ibn Khallikan says Ibn Sirin did not attend the funeral because of something between them, and died a hundred days later. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 3 p. 162}

## The grave
The sources read for this record do not name where al-Hasan was buried. The museum holds a preliminary card for the [[building/mausoleum-of-hasan-al-basri|Mausoleum of al-Hasan al-Basri]] at Basra whose sources have not yet been checked; for that reason this record attaches the grave to no particular spot.

## Where the reports differ
The time of death is "the night of Friday" in Ibn Sa'd {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 9 p. 177} and "Thursday evening" in Humayd al-Tawil's account as Ibn Khallikan gives it {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 3 p. 161}; Ibn Khallikan places it at the start of Rajab. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 3 p. 161} As to his age, al-Dhahabi quotes Abd Allah ibn al-Hasan that his father lived about eighty-eight years. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1462}
