## Summary
After Ali received the pledge of allegiance to the caliphate, al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah advised keeping Mu'awiyah in Syria temporarily, but Ali refused, saying he would not employ Mu'awiyah for two days {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.94}.

## The Counsel
al-'Aqqad quotes al-Mughirah as telling Ali that today's judgment secures tomorrow's outcome, and advising him to confirm Mu'awiyah in his post until the army's allegiance to him stabilized, after which he could be replaced or left in place {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.94}. Ibn Abbas cautioned that Mu'awiyah and his companions were people of worldly ambition, and that if dismissed, the people of Syria would say the matter was taken without consultation, causing both Syria and Iraq to turn against him {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.94}.

## The Refusal
Ali refused to compromise on his religion or yield in his duty {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.94}. Umar had appointed Mu'awiyah over Syria and Uthman had confirmed him, so Ali used to reply to those who argued that Mu'awiyah was one of Umar's governors: Umar was more feared by Mu'awiyah than his own servant, whereas after Umar he fears no one {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.95}.

## al-'Aqqad's Assessment
al-'Aqqad holds that Mu'awiyah did not act in Syria as a governor content with his post, but as the founder of a dynasty being built for his sons, gathering notables around him, buying supporters, and preparing for a long stay {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.96}. Thus, confirming Mu'awiyah would not have been safer for the community, and in his view Ali was in the right {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.96}. This is the author's judgment, presented here as an interpretation rather than a ruling by the museum.

## What Remains Unsettled
The book does not offer proof that confirming him would have averted the conflict, nor that dismissing him would have ended it. This remains an open question, and the museum presents both views as the opinion of the advisors and the opinion of the author, without favoring either.
