## Summary
In his Stories of the Prophets, Ibn Kathir reports that when death approached [[person/adam|Adam]] he entrusted his son Shith with a charge, taught him the hours of the night and day and the worship fitting each hour, and told him that the Flood would come after that. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 72} Linked to this charge are the reports of Adam's death, his preparation and burial, and the disagreement over where he lies. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73}

## Adam's charge to Shith
Ibn Kathir relays from Ibn Ishaq that Adam entrusted his son Shith at the end of his life, and that the name Shith means 'gift of God', given because he was born after Habil was killed. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 72} Al-Tabari gives the same report from Ibn Ishaq in the hedged form 'as they relate, and God knows best': Adam wrote his testament, Shith became his father's executor and succeeded to the leadership, and God sent down fifty scrolls on him, according to what is related from the Prophet. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 159} Ibn Kathir cites a hadith from Abu Dharr about the revealed scrolls, and the editor's footnote says that Ibn Hibban's chain includes a narrator whom Abu Hatim and others called a liar. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 72} Ibn Ishaq adds, in Ibn Kathir's transmission, that the lineages of Adam's descendants all lead back to Shith while Adam's other sons died out; Ibn Kathir comments only 'and God knows best'. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 72}

## His death and preparation
Ibn Kathir says Adam died on a Friday, that the angels came with perfume and a shroud from Paradise and from God, and that they consoled his son Shith. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 72} He quotes Ibn Ishaq that the sun and moon were eclipsed for seven days and nights. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 72} He then relays from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, in the version of Abdallah ibn Ahmad, that when death came to Adam he craved the fruit of Paradise and his sons went to look for it; angels met them carrying shrouds, perfume, axes and spades and told them to go back, for their father had passed away. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73} When Hawwa saw the angels she clung to Adam, and he said: keep away from me, for what befell me came through you; leave me with the angels of my Lord. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73} The report says the angels took his soul, washed, shrouded and perfumed him, dug a niche grave, prayed over him, laid him in it and covered him, and then said: this is your custom, children of Adam. The text calls its chain sound as far as Ubayy. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73}

Al-Tabari has a close report: when his time came God sent Adam his perfume and shroud from Paradise; after he died they washed him with lote leaves and water, an odd number of times, shrouded him in an odd number of garments, dug the niche grave and buried him, and said: this is the custom of Adam's children after him. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 168}

## The prayer over him
Ibn Kathir cites Ibn Asakir, from a hadith narrated from Ibn Abbas as the Prophet's words, that the angels pronounced four takbirs over Adam, then Abu Bakr four over Fatima, Umar four over Abu Bakr and Suhayb four over Umar; Ibn Asakir adds that another narrator transmitted it from Maymun as coming from Ibn Umar. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73} In al-Tabari, from Ibn Abbas, Shith asked Jibril to pray over Adam and Jibril told him to step forward and pray over his father; he pronounced thirty takbirs, five for the prayer and twenty-five as a mark of Adam's excellence. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 168}

## The grave and the differing reports
Ibn Kathir says they differed over where he was buried: the best-known report is that he lies by the mountain on which he was sent down, in India; some say Mount Abu Qubays at Mecca; and some say that Nuh carried him and Hawwa in a coffin at the time of the Flood and buried them at Bayt al-Maqdis, which Ibn Jarir relates. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73} He also quotes from Ibn Asakir a report that his head is by Ibrahim's mosque and his feet by the Rock at Bayt al-Maqdis, and that Hawwa died a year after him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73}

Al-Tabari records the same disagreement: Ibn Ishaq said what is given above, while others said he was buried at Mecca in a cave on Abu Qubays. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 168} He then gives a report from Ibn Abbas that Adam was buried at Bayt al-Maqdis after Nuh left the ark, and another that he died on the mountain where he had been sent down, that Hawwa lived a year after him and was buried with him in the cave, and that the two stayed there until the Flood, when Nuh took them out, carried them in a coffin in the ark and returned them to their place when the water withdrew. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 169} A later passage adds that Shith lived at Mecca making the pilgrimage and the lesser pilgrimage until he died, that when he fell ill he entrusted his son Anush, and that he was buried with his parents in the cave on Abu Qubays, again with the word 'it is said'. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 170}

## Note on the nature of these reports
These reports rest on Ibn Ishaq, Ubayy ibn Ka'b, Ibn Abbas and those after them; none gives a prophetic statement about the place of the grave, which is why Ibn Kathir and al-Tabari present them in hedged forms and close with 'God knows best'. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 72} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 159} Neither source decides between the places, so India, Mecca and Bayt al-Maqdis stand side by side, each assigned to those who reported it and none preferred.
