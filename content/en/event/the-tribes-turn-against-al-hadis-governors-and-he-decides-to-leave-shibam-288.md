## Overview
The clans learned that Ali ibn Sulayman had left Sana'a and turned from every place, drove out the governors of [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] from their lands and took the animals and goods they had; the narrator explains this by the people's wickedness and turning from God, their refusal to let the religion stand and their not giving up drinking wine, and he adds the words of [[passage/quran-85-8|Qur'an 85:8]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.221}. With al-Hadi at Shibam were many women of the Prophet's household, the narrator's term for women of his family; he weighed staying at Shibam and fighting, then considered the women, who were weak and had no means or way out, and knew that war goes back and forth, sometimes for him and sometimes against him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.221}. The narrator says he knew this people did not fear God or feel shame before Muhammad, and had done to his son what they had done when al-Husayn was killed and his women were carried to Yazid; so he did not allow himself to stay at Shibam for fear for the Prophet's household and knowing the wickedness of the people of Yemen, and judged that taking the women out veiled was better in religion and in this world than risking them in a land in turmoil whose people could not be trusted {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.221}. He resolved to leave out of fear for his household, or fear that none of his troops would stay with him, and knew that they inclined to falsehood {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.221}. On the Friday he ordered the camels loaded with his furniture and arms, the litters made ready, the women set in them veiled and hidden, and the baggage train sent ahead with some of his companions from the army {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.221} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.222}.

## Detail
All the reasons are the narrator's account of al-Hadi's mind and of the people of Yemen, attributed to him, with no verdict from the museum; the words of abuse and the cursing after the people's name are not repeated, and the comparison with what befell the family of al-Husayn is the narrator's own. The verse is not typed; it is linked. The household of the Prophet is the narrator's term for the Alid women with al-Hadi, and their number is not given. The Friday is not tied to a tabular date. It follows [[event/the-rising-of-ibn-mahfuz-at-sanaa-and-ali-ibn-sulayman-leaves-for-madar-288]].
