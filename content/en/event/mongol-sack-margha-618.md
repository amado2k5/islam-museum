## Overview
In Safar 618 AH the Mongols marched from the direction of Georgia into Azerbaijan, besieged Maragha, took it by force on the fourth of Safar, and put its people to the sword. Ibn al-Athir relates this in "al-Kamil". {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 411-412}

## Before the siege
The lord of Tabriz bought them off with money, clothes and animals, so they turned away from it toward Maragha. It had no lord to defend it, as its lady was a woman living in the citadel of Ruwindiz. They set up catapults against it and advanced. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 411-412}

## The assault
Ibn al-Athir says that it was their custom to put Muslim captives in front of them when they advanced. After some days they took the city, and killed of its people what "goes beyond counting", and plundered and burned. They ordered the captives to call out through the lanes that the Mongols had gone, and whoever came out of hiding was taken and killed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 411-412}

Ibn al-Athir reports two stories from its people: a Mongol man entered an alley where a hundred men were, and killed them one by one; and a woman among them killed a group while they thought she was a man. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 411-412}

## After Maragha
The Mongols moved toward Irbil, and news reached Mosul, and some of its people intended to leave. [[person/badr-al-din-lulu|Badr al-Din Lu'lu]], lord of Mosul, was told that the land feared him and that he had sent reinforcements. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 411-412}

## Date
Fourth of Safar 618 AH, with a Gregorian equivalent (around 13 March 1221 CE) that is derived. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 411}
