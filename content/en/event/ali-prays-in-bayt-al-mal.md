## Summary
Al-Sallabi recounts that when Ali was informed that the Muslim public treasury was full of gold and silver, he ordered all of it distributed to the people of Kufa, and then prayed two units of prayer in the treasury after it had been sprinkled with water {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 206}.

## The Report
Ibn al-Nabbah came to Ali and said, "O Commander of the Faithful, the Muslim public treasury is filled with yellow and white [gold and silver]," whereupon Ali said, "God is greatest," and then rose, leaning on Ibn al-Nabbah, until he stood at the treasury and recited verses disparaging gold and silver {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 206}. He then ordered a proclamation made to the people, and gave away "everything in the Muslim public treasury" until neither "a dinar nor a dirham" remained in it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 206}.

## Praying Inside It
Ali ordered the treasury sprinkled with water and then prayed two units of prayer in it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 206}. The book mentions another report by Abu Nu'aym from Mujammi al-Taymi that Ali used to sweep the treasury, pray in it, and treat it as a mosque "hoping that it would testify for him on the Day of Resurrection" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 206}.

## The Book's Reading
Al-Sallabi sees in the incident an eloquent example of rising above the fleeting goods of this world: Ali does not look upon wealth with admiration, but puts it in its place and makes the magnification of God greater than everything else {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 206-207}. He interprets treating the treasury as a mosque as a symbol of the supremacy of the Hereafter over this world, complementing his conduct in spending wealth in its lawful avenues {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 207}.
