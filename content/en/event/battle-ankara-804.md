## Overview
In 804 AH, 1402 CE, the armies of the Ottoman sultan [[person/bayazid-i-yildirim|Bayazid I]], called Yildirim, the Thunderbolt, and of [[person/timur-lang|Timur]] met near [[place/ankara|Ankara]], on the plain of Cubuk to the north of the town. Timur won, and Bayazid was taken prisoner. The Encyclopaedia of Islam, whose articles on Bayazid, Timur and Ankara this page follows for the setting, the date and the aftermath, records that Bayazid died in captivity in 805/1403 and that the hastily founded Ottoman empire collapsed. The [[polity/ottoman-empire|Ottoman state]] then passed through an interregnum of rival princes, 804-16/1402-13. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 534} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1179} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1000}

A second account, al-Sallabi's book on the Ottoman state, gives the course of the day itself and estimates Bayazid's forces at about a hundred and twenty thousand; Timur is said to have advanced at the head of large forces on 20 July 1402. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 69-70}

## Between Timur and Bayazid
By the end of the eighth century AH the two rulers were neighbours in eastern Anatolia. The article on Timur says that his involvement in the politics of the Arab, Kurdish and Turkmen dynasties of that region had brought him into conflict with the [[polity/mamluk-sultanate|Mamluks]] and the Ottomans. Since 1395 he had been considering war against the Mamluks, and at that time he was still friendly with Bayazid. Relations changed in 800/1398, with the deaths of the Mamluk sultan Barquq and of Qadi Burhan al-Din, the ruler of [[place/sivas|Sivas]]; the confusion that followed drew in both men, and their relations rapidly worsened. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 536}

The article on Bayazid describes the same drift from his side. From 801/1399 Timur claimed suzerainty over all the rulers of Anatolia, while Bayazid claimed to be the heir of the Seljuks there. Timur hesitated before attacking the sultan of the ghazis. Each gave shelter to the other's enemies: Timur to the Anatolian rulers whom Bayazid had driven out, Bayazid to [[person/qara-yusuf|Qara Yusuf]] and [[person/ahmad-jalayir|Ahmad Jalayir]], and the article says this exasperated Timur. He took and sacked Sivas in August 802/1400. Bayazid answered in 803/1401 by seizing the amir of [[place/erzincan|Erzincan]], a protege of Timur named Mutahharten. Earlier, in the year after his victory over the Karamanid ruler in 800/1397, Bayazid had set aside an alliance with Egypt against Timur and annexed Albistan, Malatya, Behisni, Kahta and Divrigi. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1178} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1179}

Timur campaigned in Syria in the autumn and winter of 803/1400-1, after the Mamluks had murdered his ambassadors and given refuge to his enemies. In the spring of 804/1402 he set out against the Ottomans. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 536}

## The battle
The two armies met on the Cubuk plain north of Ankara. The Encyclopaedia of Islam gives the day as 19 Dhu l-Hijja 804, equated with 20 July 1402, in its article on Timur, and its article on Ankara also names 20 July. Its article on Bayazid instead gives 27 Dhu l-Hijja 804, equated with 28 July 1402. This page records both and chooses neither. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 536} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 534} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1179}

Al-Sallabi's account of the fighting adds that it was a summer of intense heat and that many of Bayazid's soldiers died of thirst for lack of water. The Tatar soldiers in Bayazid's army, and the soldiers of the Asian principalities he had recently conquered, fled and joined Timur, and the Mongols won. Bayazid was taken captive and remained in chains until he died the following year. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 69-70}

## Captivity and the end of Bayazid
According to the article on Timur, he took Bayazid captive but treated him well. The article on Bayazid gives the date of his death in captivity as 13 Shaban 805, which it equates with 8 March 1403. Among the others taken were two of his sons: the article on Mehmed I says that Mustafa was captured by Timur and carried to [[place/samarqand|Samarkand]], and that [[person/musa-ibn-bayezid|Musa]] was captured together with his father. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 536} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1179} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1001}

## Anatolia after the battle
Timur's armies moved through the Ottoman lands collecting tribute from the cities, but he left the Ottoman dynasty in place. The article on Bayazid says the Anatolian princes all regained their territories in 804/1402, while the Ottoman princes divided the rest of the country among themselves and recognised Timur as their suzerain. It adds that the Ottomans took the offensive again only under Mehmed II. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 536} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1179}

The article on Mehmed I describes the interregnum, 804-16/1402-13, that followed. Bayazid's sons Suleyman and later Musa held Rumeli from [[place/edirne|Edirne]], while Mehmed ruled Anatolia from [[place/tokat|Tokat]], [[place/amasya|Amasya]] and [[place/bursa|Bursa]]; Mehmed had been sent in 801/1399 as governor of the province of Rum, which included Amasya, Tokat, Sivas and Ankara. During Timur's siege of [[place/izmir|Izmir]], from 6 Jumada I 805, or 2 December 1402, until 10 Jumada II, or 6 January 1403, Suleyman received from him a patent granting rule over the lands beyond the Bosphorus. Mehmed, summoned to Timur in person at Kutahya, could not or would not come, yet he accepted Timur's suzerainty and struck a silver coin bearing his name. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1000} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1001}

In the fighting among the brothers, Ankara lay within the territory of Mehmed Celebi. The article on the town notes that he had to defend it against his brother Isa in 1404 and against the amir Suleyman in 1406; the article on Mehmed I adds that in the year beginning 21 July 1403 he lost Bursa and Ankara to Suleyman. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 534} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 1001}

## Reception in the West
Al-Sallabi writes that the Christian states of the West rejoiced at Timur's victory, and that the kings of England, France and Castile and the emperor at [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]] sent messages of congratulation; he adds that Europe believed it had been delivered for ever from the Ottoman danger. After the battle Timur took Iznik and Bursa and relieved Izmir of the knights of Rhodes, which the author presents as an attempt to justify his conduct before Muslim opinion. These are the author's judgements and are reported here as his. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 69-70}

The Encyclopaedia of Islam sets this in a wider frame: common enmity to the Ottomans drew Timur and the Christian powers together, and in his later years he exchanged embassies with the Palaiologan rulers of Constantinople, the Venetians, Henry IV of England, the Genoese of Galata and Charles VI of France. After Timur's death his son Shah Rukh held his domain and kept a formal suzerainty over the Ottomans. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 537}

## What the sources assess
The article on Bayazid judges that he was responsible for founding the first centralised Ottoman empire, built on the kul system and on administrative methods perfected by earlier Muslim-Turkish states in the Middle East, and notes that popular tradition criticised him as an innovator in finance, administration and manners. These are the author's assessments, not the museum's. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1179}
