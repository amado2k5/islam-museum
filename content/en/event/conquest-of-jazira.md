## Overview
al-Baladhuri relates on the authority of Maymun ibn Mihran that all of al-Jazira was a conquest of [[person/iyyad-ibn-ghanm|Iyad ibn Ghanm]] after the death of Abu Ubayda, and that Umar appointed him over it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.236}

## Accounts of the appointment
Sulayman ibn Ata said that Abu Ubayda sent Iyad to al-Jazira and he died there, so Umar afterwards appointed him over it. al-Waqidi, whom al-Baladhuri's sources held to be the most reliable in his account, holds that Abu Ubayda died in the plague of Amwas in 18 AH and appointed Iyad as his successor. Then the letter of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] came to him appointing him over Homs, Qinnasrin and al-Jazira. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.236}

## The march
Iyad marched to [[place/jazira|al-Jazira]] on Thursday, the middle of Sha'ban 18 AH, with five thousand men. His vanguard was under [[person/maysara-ibn-masruq|Maysara ibn Masruq al-Absi]], his right wing under [[person/said-ibn-amir-al-jumahi|Sa'id ibn Amir al-Jumahi]], and his left under [[person/safwan-ibn-al-muattal|Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami]]. It is also said that [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] was on the left. It is also said that Khalid remained in Homs until he died there in 21 AH, and some say he died in Medina. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.237}

The conquest is part of the [[event/conquest-of-syria|conquest of Syria]]. The date is derived from the account.

Al-Baladhuri reports from Maymun ibn Mihran that the whole Jazira was among Iyad ibn Ghanm's conquests after Abu Ubayda's death, and that Umar appointed him to it.

## For Explorers
Al-Baladhuri reports from Maymun ibn Mihran that the whole Jazira was among Iyad ibn Ghanm's conquests after Abu Ubayda's death and that Umar appointed him to it. Al-Waqidi — the firmest authority they heard — holds that Abu Ubayda died in the plague of Amwas in 18 AH having named Iyad successor, and Umar's letter came appointing him to Homs, Qinnasrin and the Jazira. Iyad marched to the Jazira on Thursday, mid-Sha'ban 18 AH, in five thousand: Maysara ibn Masruq on the vanguard, Sa'id ibn Amir al-Jumahi on the right, Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal on the left — or Khalid ibn al-Walid on the left in one telling. The conquest is part of the opening of Syria.

## For Scholars
Al-Baladhuri reports from Maymun ibn Mihran that the whole Jazira was among Iyad ibn Ghanm's conquests after Abu Ubayda's death, and that Umar appointed him to it. Sulayman ibn Ata says Abu Ubayda sent Iyad to the Jazira and died while he was there, Umar appointing him to it after; al-Waqidi — the firmest authority they heard on his affair, by al-Baladhuri's judgment — holds that Abu Ubayda died in the plague of Amwas in 18 AH having named Iyad successor, and Umar's letter came appointing him to Homs, Qinnasrin and the Jazira. Iyad marched to the Jazira on Thursday, mid-Sha'ban 18 AH, in five thousand: Maysara ibn Masruq al-Absi on the vanguard, Sa'id ibn Amir al-Jumahi on the right wing, Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami on the left — or Khalid ibn al-Walid on the left in one telling; and Khalid is said to have stayed in Homs until his death there in 21 AH, or to have died in Medina. The conquest is part of the opening of Syria, and the date is derived from the account.
