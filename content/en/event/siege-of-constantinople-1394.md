## Overview
A long blockade imposed by the Ottoman sultan Bayezid I on Constantinople from about 796/1393 until 1402, when he was forced to lift it to face Timur. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 68, 89-90}

## Bayezid's terms to the emperor
Even before the battle of Nicopolis, Bayezid was able to impose on the emperor the appointment of a qadi in Constantinople to rule on Muslim affairs; he then blockaded the Byzantine capital, and the emperor accepted an Islamic court, the building of a mosque, the allocation of seven hundred houses inside the city to the Muslim community, and the cession of half the Galata quarter, where an Ottoman garrison of six thousand soldiers was stationed; the tribute imposed on the Byzantine state was increased. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 67}

## The tight blockade
After the victory at Nicopolis, Bayezid asked Emperor Manuel to surrender Constantinople; the emperor appealed to Europe in vain. Bayezid marched at the head of his armies and drew a tight blockade around the Byzantine capital, pressing it until it was on the verge of falling, while Europe expected its fall from one day to the next. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 68}

## The siege lifted
Bayezid's forces had besieged the city strongly in 796/1393 and he negotiated with the emperor for its peaceful surrender, but the arrival of the Mongol armies led by Timur inside Ottoman lands forced him to withdraw his troops and lift the siege to face them. The battle of Ankara followed, in which Bayezid was captured and then died in captivity in 1402, and thinking about the conquest of Constantinople was suspended for a time. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 89-90}
