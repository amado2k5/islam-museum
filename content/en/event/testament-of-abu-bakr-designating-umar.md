## Summary
When illness grew heavy on Abu Bakr al-Siddiq he called his senior Companions and asked them about Umar ibn al-Khattab, then dictated to Uthman ibn Affan a written covenant naming Umar as his successor and ordered that it be read to the people. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908} Both accounts say the covenant was put before the people, who accepted it and pledged allegiance before Abu Bakr died. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908}

## Setting
Aisha relates that his illness began when he bathed on a cold day; he fevered for fifteen days and did not come out to prayer, and he told Umar to lead the people. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 258} For the background of Abu Bakr's own selection see [[event/saqifa|the Saqifa]].

## The consultation
Ibn al-Athir reports that Abu Bakr called [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]] and said: tell me about Umar. Abd al-Rahman answered that Umar was better than Abu Bakr's own view of him, except that there was harshness in him. Abu Bakr replied that this was because Umar saw him as gentle; if the matter came to Umar he would give up much of what he was like. He had watched him: when he himself was angry with a man, Umar showed him approval of that man, and when he was soft, Umar showed him firmness. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 266} He then called [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]], who said that Umar's inner self was better than his outward self and that there was none like him among them; Abu Bakr asked both men not to repeat what he had said to them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267}

In Ibn Sa'd's report, which Ibn al-Athir quotes in Usd al-Ghaba, Abu Bakr also consulted Sa'id ibn Zayd, Usayd ibn Hudayr and others of the Emigrants and Ansar; Usayd said that Umar's hidden character was better than his open one and that no one was stronger for the task. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908}

## The objection and the answer
Someone came in to object to appointing Umar because of his harshness. In al-Kamil this is [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha ibn Ubayd Allah]]; Abu Bakr said, "Sit me up," rejected being frightened with God, and said that when he met his Lord he would say he had appointed the best of His people over them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} In Ibn Sa'd's report the speaker is an unnamed Companion, and Abu Bakr says that whoever takes provision from their affair by wrongdoing is lost, and that he will tell his Lord he appointed the best of His people. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 260} Aisha transmits that he said: I have set over them the best of them. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 260}

## Writing the covenant
Ibn Sa'd and Ibn al-Athir agree that Abu Bakr called Uthman to write. In Ibn al-Athir, he dictated the opening, "In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful; this is what Abu Bakr ibn Abi Quhafa has covenanted to the Muslims; now then", and then fainted; Uthman wrote on his own: "Now then, I have appointed Umar ibn al-Khattab over you and have not fallen short of good for you." When Abu Bakr came to, he said, "Read it to me." Uthman read; Abu Bakr said the takbir and said he supposed Uthman had feared that people would disagree if he died in his faint. Uthman said yes, and Abu Bakr prayed that God reward him well on behalf of Islam and its people. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267}

Ibn Sa'd's version gives a longer opening: this is Abu Bakr's covenant at the last of his time in the world, departing it, and the first of his time in the next, entering it, where the disbeliever believes, the sinner is certain and the liar tells the truth. It then announces Umar's appointment and tells the people to listen and obey; Abu Bakr says he has not fallen short in sincere counsel to God, His Messenger, His religion, himself and them; if Umar is just that is his opinion and knowledge of him, and if he changes, each person has what he earned; he meant good and does not know the unseen. It ends with a Qur'anic sentence about the fate awaiting wrongdoers, and the greeting. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908} Al-Muhibb al-Tabari gives the same text from Ibn Sa'd. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 260} For the verse see [[passage/quran-26-227|Qur'an 26:227]].

## Reading it to the people
In al-Kamil, Abu Bakr ordered the document read; the people gathered, and he sent it with a freedman of his, accompanied by Umar. Umar told the people to be silent and listen to the Messenger's successor, for he had not fallen short in counsel; they quietened, and when the document was read they listened and obeyed. Abu Bakr had looked out to the people and asked whether they accepted the man he had appointed, since he had not appointed a kinsman; they said, "We hear and obey." {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267}

In Ibn Sa'd's report, Uthman came out with the sealed document, accompanied by Umar and Usayd ibn Sa'ya al-Qurazi, and asked the people, "Do you pledge to the one named in this document?" They said yes; some said, "We know who he is, he is Umar"; Ibn Sa'd says the speaker was Ali. They all acknowledged it and pledged. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908} Abu Bakr then called Umar privately and counselled him, as told in [[event/last-counsel-of-abu-bakr-to-umar|the next record]]; then he raised his hands and prayed that he had intended only the people's good, had feared sedition for them, and had set over them the best, strongest and most concerned for their guidance; he asked God to be his successor over them. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908}

## Where the reports differ
The reports differ in visible ways. In al-Kamil the objector is Talha, while in Ibn Sa'd it is an unnamed Companion. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908} In Ibn al-Athir only the opening was dictated by Abu Bakr and Uthman completed it after he fainted, whereas Ibn Sa'd's report gives the whole text as dictated. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908}

A report from Yasar, also in Usd al-Ghaba, says that when Abu Bakr grew heavy he looked at the people from a window and said, "I have made a covenant; will you accept it?" They said, "We accept, Caliph of the Messenger of God," and Ali said, "We shall accept only if it is Umar ibn al-Khattab." {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 909} This report attributes to Ali a statement about accepting the covenant and differs from Ibn Sa'd's account of a sealed document being read. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 909} In another report from Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Abu Bakr says in his illness that he had appointed the best of them in his own judgment and that each of them had swelled with pride wanting the matter for himself. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 908}

## What followed
Abu Bakr died soon after; when he was buried Umar went up and addressed the people. See [[event/death-of-abu-bakr|the death of Abu Bakr]] and [[event/deathbed-and-burial-of-abu-bakr|his last hours and burial]]. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 268}

## Sources and documentation
This record rests on Ibn al-Athir's al-Kamil for the consultation, the covenant and its reading, on Usd al-Ghaba for Ibn Sa'd's report, and on al-Muhibb al-Tabari's al-Riyad al-Nadira.
