## Overview
After returning to [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]], [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] stayed until Thursday, seven days into Ramadan, when word came that [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am ibn Ibrahim]] had left his land with a great army for "al-Bawn"; al-Hadi thought it a ruse and that he meant [[place/athafit|Athafit]], which had been in al-Da'am's hand and then in al-Hadi's. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.95} He had appointed over Athafit one of the best of the Muslims, learned in God's unity and justice, a scrupulous jurist called Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan of Bahrain, kunya Abu Umar. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.95} This is the name of the man al-Hadi put over Barat (p. 85), where he is "of Najran", so it is not settled whether they are one person. Al-Hadi left Khiwan on the night of Friday with seven nights left of Ramadan and was at Athafit by morning. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.95}

## The head of al-Naqil
He moved on to a place called "Rasar Bakil" and spent the night; next morning he set out for Bayt Dhu'd, and on the road word came that al-Da'am meant to climb "al-Naqil", which in Yemeni speech is the mountain pass, as the editor's footnote has it from the Mu'jam al-buldan, so al-Hadi went up to the head of al-Naqil. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.95} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.96} Looking down he saw al-Da'am and his army and had the banners unfurled; seeing this al-Da'am went back to a village of al-Bawn called "Hamda", and al-Hadi returned to [[place/bayt-dhud-yemen|Bayt Dhu'd]], set sentries on al-Naqil and sent a rider to [[person/sasaa-ibn-jafar-al-hamdani|Sa'sa'a ibn Ja'far]] with the news. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.96}

## Najd al-Dayr
He then went down to "Dahyan" and sent for Sa'sa'a, who brought a group from al-Bawn, al-Hadi thinking al-Da'am still at Hamda; a cry came from the head of al-Naqil that al-Da'am had come on Bayt Dhu'd; he rode there and was told al-Da'am had climbed the pass of Hamda, and went to meet him at a place called "Najd al-Dayr" and found al-Da'am and his men there. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.96} He had his army drawn up: the Ma'mariyyin, Khawlan and a Hamdani group called "al-Mahra" on the right, Banu Rabi'a and Banu Sarim in the centre, and the people of Bayt Dhu'd and al-Bawn on the left; al-Da'am drew up his army in right, left and centre too. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.96}

## Prayer between the lines and the offer of single combat
Al-Hadi then dismounted between the lines, put off his weapons, purified himself and prayed short; when one of his companions said al-Da'am's army was near, he answered that in a little while all of them would ask security, and al-Da'am with them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.96} After the prayer he sent to one of al-Da'am's men: "Go to your master and say: let not the Arabs fight among themselves; come out to me, and let the people stand back until I fight you; if you kill me you are rid of me and may do what you like, and if I kill you the people of Islam are rid of you." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.96} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.97} Al-Da'am disliked it and returned the messenger with fair words and said he did not want war, and messengers kept passing. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.97}

## A short fight and the settlement
Al-Hadi's right wing came near al-Da'am's left, they exchanged words and fought until there were dead: a man of Hamdan of al-Mahra, not named, and from al-Da'am's side a man said to be brave who had spoken ill of al-Hadi before he was killed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.97} When word came, al-Hadi sent to his right wing to stop and they did; messengers went on until peace was made; al-Da'am came to al-Hadi and swore to hear and obey and the two armies mixed; al-Hadi went back to Bayt Dhu'd and al-Da'am to Hamda; al-Hadi stayed three days while messengers went on the basis of the settlement, and on Thursday with a day left of Ramadan al-Da'am left for "Warur", a great fortress in the mountains of Sanaa in the land of Hamdan according to the editor's note, the matter between them standing on the settlement. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.97} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.98} The report is in praise of its Zaydi narrator's imam, and the museum attributes it to him.
