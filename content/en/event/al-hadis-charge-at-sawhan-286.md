## Overview
Then [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] went on until he made for [[place/sawhan-najran|Sawhan]], where the best of the people and their men were, and the people fought until a man of his companions called [[person/ibn-al-miqdam-killed-at-sawhan-286|Ibn al-Miqdam]] was killed and carried to a place the text calls "Rahat" to be buried; the fighting grew hard and [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|the Tabaris]] entered the pass. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.161} Al-Hadi stood behind the Tabaris urging the people to advance. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.161}

## The rush of Banu al-Harith
The camps of [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] came out of the village and Minas, went between the palms and trees to a place called "Mihdar", crossed the wadi and kept under the palms alongside until close to Sawhan, with the believers unaware of them, then fell on them in one rush while the people of the fort came out with them; al-Hadi's companions gave way hard and turned their backs. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.161}

## Al-Hadi's stand
Al-Hadi followed on, turning his horse in the faces of the enemy, now toward their right wing, now at their centre, now toward their left, with no one left beside him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.161} A Kufan called [[person/abu-isa-al-kufi-companion-of-al-hadi|Abu Isa al-Kufi]] had grown heavy and slow, and al-Hadi called out to him that the enemy was behind him; he cried back that he was worn out, so al-Hadi charged the Harithi and speared and killed him; he charged into their midst, speared another man and killed him, and his spear broke. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.161} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.162} Then he saw [[person/ibn-humayd-of-banu-al-harith-286|Ibn Humayd]], who had sworn to Banu al-Harith that he would go for al-Hadi if he saw him; al-Hadi called out to him recalling his oaths, Ibn Humayd and his men turned and fled, and al-Hadi chased them nearly to the fortress gate. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.162} Ibn Humayd went into an enclosure at the gate and shouted to his men to break the wall, which they did, and his horse leapt him through and he went away beaten; al-Hadi struck a man who had been shooting for Ibn Humayd near the pass gate. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.162}

## The people return
[[person/muhammad-ibn-said-ibn-yusuf|Muhammad ibn Sa'id]], at the back, cried out that al-Hadi was fighting the enemy alone and had checked them, and that the people should go back for God's sake; some of the army returned and followed al-Hadi and stood with him, killed three Harithis in their charge, and Banu al-Harith fled into the fortress while al-Hadi's army drew off to one side. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.162}

## Al-Hadi and Ibn Ghitrif
[[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-malik-ibn-ghitrif-al-wadii|Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Ghitrif al-Wadi'i]] asked al-Hadi how many he had killed, and he said two with the spear before it broke and one good blow when Ibn Humayd got away. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.162} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.163} He ordered the army back to camp, and that evening word came that the man struck had died, and that Ibn Humayd had told his men to keep it quiet lest people see the blow and none fight al-Hadi, so he was buried that hour in the stony plain, and al-Hadi went back to the fortress and stayed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.163} The account is Zaydi and the detail of the blow and the burial is the narrator's report, which the museum neither confirms nor denies.
