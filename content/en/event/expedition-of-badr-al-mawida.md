As [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] and the polytheists withdrew on the day of Uhud, he called out: your tryst is Badr next year. When Sha'ban of the fourth year of the Hijra came, the Prophet went out with fifteen hundred of his Companions and ten horses, [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] carrying his banner and Abdullah ibn Rawaha left in charge of Medina, until he reached Badr and stayed there awaiting the polytheist army. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Abu Sufyan marched out with two thousand of the people of Mecca and fifty horses, reaching Majanna by al-Zahran; then God cast terror into his heart — he pleaded the year's drought and ordered his people to turn back, so the people of Mecca mockingly called them "the army of sawiq". {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Muslims stayed at Badr eight days, selling and buying at its annual market season; they earned two dirhams on each dirham and returned to Medina with their awe fixed in men's hearts and their mastery in the Hijaz confirmed. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
