## Overview
It is said that Abu Dawud composed the Sunan and presented it to the Imam Ahmad, who admired and approved it {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 359}; as Ahmad died in 241 the presentation falls before that year.

## From five hundred thousand to four thousand eight hundred
Ibn Dasa heard him say: I wrote down five hundred thousand traditions from the Messenger of God and selected from them what I placed in the Sunan — four thousand eight hundred traditions — naming the sound ones and those resembling or approaching them {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 360}. He was the first to attach detailed notes on their value to the traditions themselves, paving the way for the more systematic criticism of his pupil al-Tirmidhi {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 133}.
