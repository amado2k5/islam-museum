## Summary
In Jumada al-Akhira the Muslims took Ascalon and Tiberias by storm, the conquest led by Fakhr al-Din ibn al-Shaykh {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}.

## Details
Under the events of 645 AH Ibn al-'Imad records that in Jumada al-Akhira the Muslims took the two towns of Ascalon and Tiberias 'anwatan — by force, through assault — and that the conquest was achieved by Fakhr al-Din ibn al-Shaykh, one of the foremost military commanders of Egypt and Syria in that period {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}. The capture formed part of the Muslim recovery of coastal and inland positions then in Frankish hands: Ascalon was a key coastal fortress on the road to Egypt, while Tiberias on the Sea of Galilee commanded the Galilee and the Damascus route {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}. The source states explicitly that the towns were taken by storm rather than by treaty, indicating the severity of the engagement and that neither town surrendered peacefully {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 230}.
