## Summary
In Dhu al-Hijja of 574 AH a severe war broke out in [[place/mecca|Mecca]] between Tashtakin, amir of the hajj caravan, and Mukthir ibn 'Isa, amir of Mecca. The caliph had ordered Mukthir's removal and the installation of his brother Dawud; Mukthir fled and Dawud assumed the emirate. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 77}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir explains that the cause was a fortress Mukthir had built on Jabal Abi Qubays. When the pilgrims moved on from 'Arafat they did not spend the night at Muzdalifa but passed through it, nor did they stop at the Jamarat — some stoned while on the move — and they camped at al-Abtah, where a group of Meccans came out and fought them, killing a number on both sides; the cry went up "raiders upon Mecca!" and the Meccans charged the pilgrims {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 77}. Mukthir fled to the fortress he had built on Jabal Abi Qubays, was besieged there, then abandoned it and left Mecca, while his brother Dawud took over the emirate {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 77}. Many pilgrims were plundered, much wealth was seized from the resident merchants, and many houses were burned {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 77}. Among the strange incidents, a blue-eyed man hurled a flask of naphtha at a house belonging to orphans and burned it with all it held; as he took a second flask to strike elsewhere, a thrown stone shattered it and he burned, lingering three days in agony before dying {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 77}.
