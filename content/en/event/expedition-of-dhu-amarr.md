## Summary
The expedition of Dhu Amarr (also called the expedition of Ghatafan) was the largest military muster the Prophet led before the battle of Uhud; it took place in Muharram 3 AH (July 624 CE) with four hundred and fifty fighters of the [[group/muhajirun|Muhajirun]] and the [[group/ansar|Ansar]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 241}

## The army marches out
[[place/medina|Medina]]'s intelligence learned that a great gathering of Banu Tha'laba and Muharib of [[group/ghatafan|Ghatafan]] had assembled, meaning to raid Medina's outskirts under [[person/dathur-ibn-al-harith|Du'thur ibn al-Harith al-Muharibi]] (or, it is said, Ghawrath). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 241} {cite:zad-al-maad 2/90}

The Prophet left [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]] in charge of Medina and marched swiftly to Najd; when the Bedouin heard of his advance they fled scattering into the mountaintops and ravines. The Prophet halted at the water of [[place/dhu-amarr|Dhu Amarr]] and stayed all of Safar or near it, to overawe the enemy and display Muslim strength. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 241} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 2/46}

## The incident of the sword
Rain caught the Prophet and his companions and his clothes were soaked; he drew off alone under a tree and spread his clothes to dry. Du'thur descended on him with a drawn sword, stood over his head and said: who will keep you from me today? The Prophet said, calm and firm: "God" — and the sword fell from Du'thur's hand. The Prophet took it and said: "who will keep you from me?" He said: be the best of takers — and he pardoned him. Du'thur became Muslim and returned to his people calling them to Islam. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 242} {cite:zad-al-maad 2/90}
