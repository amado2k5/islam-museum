## Overview
[[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] called for the oath of allegiance to his son [[person/yazid-ibn-muawiya|Yazid]] as heir apparent. al-Suyuti says that the people of Syria gave their allegiance in 50 AH in one report, and in 56 AH in another, and that he was the first to designate a successor for his son while he was alive. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.326}

## Medina
Mu'awiya wrote to Marwan in Medina to take the oath. Marwan gave a sermon and said that the Commander of the Faithful had decided to appoint Yazid as successor on the model of Abu Bakr and Umar. [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-abi-bakra|Abd al-Rahman]], son of Abu Bakra, said, according to al-Suyuti: "Rather, the custom of Khosrow and Caesar; Abu Bakr and Umar did not place it among their own children." {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.326}

## Mu'awiya's pilgrimage
Mu'awiya made the pilgrimage in 51 AH and took the oath for his son. al-Suyuti relates that [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]] answered him that the caliphs before him had sons, and they did not see in them what he saw in his son. Ibn Abi Bakr replied that the matter should be returned to consultation. Mu'awiya told Ibn al-Zubayr that he was a wily fox, and Ibn al-Zubayr answered that he should withdraw and let them pledge to his son. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.326-327}

Mu'awiya went up the pulpit and declared that they had heard and obeyed and pledged, while the people said that they had not pledged. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.326-327}

The Gregorian date is derived. The disagreement between the years 50 and 56 stands in the source, and the museum does not favour either.
