Once the conquest was settled in Mecca, in the last days of Ramadan of the eighth year of the Hijra the Prophet sent out his detachments to demolish the great strongholds of idolatry — the greatest idols of the Arabs around Mecca — proclaiming pure monotheism. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He sent [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] with thirty horsemen to Batn Nakhla, who demolished al-Uzza — the greatest idol of Quraysh and Kinana — cut down her trees and killed her keepers; and he sent [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] with horsemen to Rahat, who demolished the idol Suwa', held sacred by the tribe of Hudhayl. {cite:zad-al-maad}

And he sent [[person/sad-ibn-zayd-al-ashhali|Sa'd ibn Zayd al-Ashhali]] with twenty horsemen to al-Mushallal at Qudayd, who demolished Manat, the oldest of the Arabs' idols, held sacred by the Aws, the Khazraj and Ghassan. Thus God cleansed the Arabian Peninsula of the landmarks of idolatry, and the banner of monotheism flew unfurled. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
