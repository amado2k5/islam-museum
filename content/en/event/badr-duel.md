## Summary
The opening duel of the battle of [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], on 17 Ramadan 2 AH: three nobles of Quraysh against three Muslims. It ended with the death of the three Qurashis and a mortal wound to one of the Muslims {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.216-217}.

## What happened
The first death to ignite the battle was al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Asad al-Makhzumi, who had sworn to drink from the Muslims' cistern or to destroy it. [[person/hamza|Hamza]] killed him at the cistern {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.216}. Then three of Quraysh's finest horsemen, from one family, came out seeking a duel: Utba and Shayba, the sons of Rabi'a, and al-Walid ibn Utba {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.216}.

Three young men of the Ansar went out to them first: 'Awf and Mu'awwidh, the sons of al-Harith, and Abdullah ibn Rawaha. The Quraysh asked who they were, and when they said they were a company of the Ansar, they answered: "Noble equals, but we have no need of you." They called out: "O Muhammad, bring out to us our equals from our own people." So the Prophet ordered [[person/ubayda-ibn-al-harith|Ubayda ibn al-Harith]], Hamza and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] to rise {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.216}.

## The duels
In Ibn Ishaq's account, Ubayda, who was the eldest of the men, duelled Utba; Hamza duelled Shayba; and Ali duelled al-Walid. In the account of Ahmad and Abu Dawud, Ubayda duelled al-Walid, Ali duelled Shayba, and Hamza duelled Utba {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.216-217} {cite:mishkat-al-masabih}.

Hamza and Ali gave their opponents no time before killing them. As for Ubayda, he and his opponent exchanged two blows, each of which crippled the other. Then his two companions turned on Utba and killed him, and carried Ubayda off with his leg cut off. Ubayda died at [[place/al-safra|al-Safra]] four or five days later {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.217}.

Ali used to swear that the verse about the two opponents who dispute concerning their Lord (al-Hajj 19) was revealed about these duellists {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.217}. The end of the duel was a bad start for the idolaters: they had lost three of their finest horsemen and leaders in a single stroke {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.217}.
