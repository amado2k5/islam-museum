## Overview
The taking of Alexandria opens [[event/french-campaign-egypt-1213|the French campaign in Egypt]]. [[person/abd-al-rahman-al-jabarti|Al-Jabarti]] opens the events of 1213 AH in [[work/ajaib-al-athar|Aja'ib al-athar]] by calling it "the first of the years of the great battles and grave events", then tells what happened at the port as the news reached Cairo in couriers' letters. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.33}

## The English warning
On Sunday 10 Muharram letters reached Cairo from the port: on Thursday the 8th ten English ships had appeared, then fifteen more, and ten men landed and met the town's leaders, chief among whom was al-Sayyid Muhammad Kurayyim, "the one who decided every matter". They said they had come in search of the French, who had sailed with a great fleet for an unknown destination and might fall on the port. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.33} Muhammad Kurayyim did not believe them and took it for a trick; they asked for water and provisions at a price and were refused, being told: "This is the Sultan's country; neither the French nor anyone else has any way into it." They sailed away. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.33}

Al-Jabarti says the news caused "much talk" in Cairo, until people calmed down on hearing the ships had gone, and that the amirs "paid no attention to any of this", trusting their strength and claiming that if all the Franks came they could not stand against them. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.33}

## The landing and the capture
On Wednesday 20 Muharram letters came from the port, Rosetta and Damanhur: the French ships had arrived on Monday the 18th and sent for the consul and some townsmen, whom they detained. By night some of their ships moved to al-Ajami and landed troops and weapons, "and in the morning the people of the port knew nothing until they were around the town like swarming locusts". {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.33} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.34}

The townsmen, the Bedouin who had joined them and the governor (kashif) of al-Buhayra went out but could not hold; the kashif and the Bedouin fled, and the townsmen fell back to fight with muskets from their houses and walls, until they realised they were lost "because the towers were empty of guns and powder", and asked for safe-conduct, which was granted. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.34}

## The first French measures
The French proclaimed safety in the town and raised their flags over it. They summoned the notables, ordered them to collect the weapons and to wear on their chests the "jukar" (cockade), which al-Jabarti describes as three round pieces of broadcloth or silk, black, red and white, laid one on another so that the three colours show. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.34} At Alexandria they also printed a proclamation and sent copies to the country: [[work/bonaparte-proclamation-1213|Bonaparte's proclamation to the people of Egypt]]. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.35}

## The response in Cairo
When the news arrived people were alarmed and most thought of flight. Ibrahim Bey, Murad Bey, the other amirs, the ulama and the qadi met at Qasr al-Ayni and agreed to write to Istanbul and to send Murad Bey out against the French; they spent five days preparing, "confiscating from people and taking most of what they needed without paying". {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.34} Murad Bey ordered an iron chain a hundred and thirty cubits long to be stretched across the river mouth to stop the French ships, "thinking that the Franks could not fight them on land"; the opposite happened, for the French marched from Alexandria along the west bank "without delay". {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.34} That march led to [[event/battle-of-the-pyramids-1213|the battle of Imbaba]].
