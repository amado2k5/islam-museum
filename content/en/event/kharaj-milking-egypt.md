## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates from Yazid ibn Abi Habib that [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] collected the land tax and poll tax of Egypt, two million, and that [[person/abdullah-ibn-sad-ibn-abi-sarh|Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh]] collected four million. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.303}

## The proverb
[[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] said to Amr that the milch camels of Egypt had yielded their milk since he left. Amr answered that this was because they had starved their young. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.303}

## Umar's policy
Al-Baladhuri says that Umar ibn al-Khattab used to write down the wealth of his governors when he appointed them, and then share with them what had grown, and sometimes take it from them. He wrote to Amr that goods, slaves, vessels and animals had come to light which were not there when he took over Egypt. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.307}

The event took place in [[place/al-fustat|Fustat]], dated about 25 AH in the caliphate of Uthman.

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Ibn Abd al-Hakam gives Umar's letter to Amr complaining of the slow kharaj, saying Egypt was a vast land the Pharaohs had cultivated yet it did not yield half its former revenue, and Amr's long reply offering that Umar take back his post. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 213} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 214} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 215} In another version Amr said the landholders had asked him to wait until their harvest came in, and he judged gentleness better than forcing them to sell what they could not do without. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 215}

## The figures
Al-Layth reports that Amr collected twelve million; others say al-Muqawqis had collected twenty million the year before, which prompted Umar's letter. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 215} Al-Layth also reports that Abd Allah ibn Sa'd, appointed by Uthman, collected fourteen million, and that Uthman told Amr the milch camel had given more than before, to which Amr replied: "You have harmed her young." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 216}
