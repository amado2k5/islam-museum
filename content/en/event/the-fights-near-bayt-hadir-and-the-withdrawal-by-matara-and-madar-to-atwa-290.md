## Overview
Then [[person/ibrahim-ibn-khalaf-ibn-tarif|Ibn Khalaf]] brought out part of his army and camped it near al-Hadi's camp; al-Hadi sent out an army of the Khawlanis who were with him, they camped facing the men, and they fought with many wounds and drove them up to their fort {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.246} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.247}. They stopped two days and then returned, going out in the morning to the Khawlanis' place, and fought hard, with wounds and dead; al-Hadi reinforced them with Hamdani men, two of al-Hadi's men were hit and a number of the others, and they were driven back until they clung to their fort {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.247}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi stayed some days until things grew hard on his army and the people of the place pressed them, then left with his men by night and spent the night at a place called Matara, where he stayed two days; he went on to [[place/madar-yemen|Madar]] and gave leave to those with him from Khawlan, Hamdan and the people of Najran, and stayed at Madar with a small army. When [[group/banu-tarif|Al Tarif]] learned how few were with him they marched out in large armies and halted near him, while [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]] held to their place and did not move; the people advised al-Hadi to move to a place called [[place/atwa-yemen|Atwa]], and he halted there and stayed some days {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.247}. He sent criers among Hamdan and no one came, and each time his messengers reached a Hamdani village its people rose to Al Tarif; the narrator says that Hamdan united with them against him out of greed, which is the narrator's judgement and not the museum's {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.247}. Before this is [[event/al-hadi-waits-at-bayt-dhud-and-falj-and-moves-by-raydah-and-madar-to-sabal-290|al-Hadi's wait and his move to Sabal]]; after it, [[event/al-tarif-attack-al-hadi-at-atwa-on-1-rajab-and-the-hamdani-wing-gives-way-290|the attack of Al Tarif at Atwa at the start of Rajab]]. The book does not name the fort or the place where the fighting took place; it follows the report of Bayt Hadir, which the museum takes as its setting. The text adds in square brackets, from the other copy, the words "he halted there" at Madar.
