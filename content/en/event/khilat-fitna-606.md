## Summary
In 606 AH the people of Akhlat rose against al-Malik al-Awhad Najm al-Din ibn al-Adil's troops after he marched to Malazgirt; he returned, besieged Akhlat, took it and killed many of its people. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 342}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that once al-Awhad's rule over Akhlat and its districts was complete, he marched to Malazgirt to settle its affairs; when he left, the people of Akhlat rose against the troops there, expelled them and besieged the citadel where al-Awhad's men were, shouting the slogan of Shah-Arman — though he was dead — meaning to restore rule to his companions and mamluks. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 342} The news reached al-Awhad, who returned reinforced by an army from the Jazira and besieged Akhlat; its people fell into disagreement and some inclined to him out of envy of others, so he took the city, killed many of its people, captured a group of notables and sent them to Mayyafariqin, killing more of them daily until few survived; the people of Akhlat were humbled after this blow and the word of the fityan, in whose hands authority had lain, was scattered. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 342}
