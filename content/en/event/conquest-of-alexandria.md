## Overview
al-Baladhuri relates two accounts of Alexandria. In the first, [[person/al-muqawqis|al-Muqawqis]] made peace with [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]], but the king of the Byzantines overturned that, and Amr conquered the city by force. In the second, Amr marched to it in 21 AH, besieged it for three months, and then took it by the sword. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The first account
On the chain of Ibn Lahi'a from Yazid ibn Abi Habib: al-Muqawqis made peace with Amr on condition that those Byzantines who wished could leave and those who wished could stay, and that the Copts be assessed two dinars. This reached [[person/heraclius|the king of the Byzantines]], who was enraged and sent armies. They closed the gate of Alexandria and gave Amr notice of war. al-Muqawqis asked Amr three things: that he should not offer the Byzantines what he had offered him, that he should not break faith with the Copts, and that he should be buried in a church he named. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Then Amr took Alexandria by force and wrote of the conquest to Umar. According to Yazid ibn Abi Habib, all of it was a treaty. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The other account
Amr marched in 21 AH, and he appointed [[person/kharija-ibn-hudhafa-al-adawi|Kharija ibn Hudhafa]] over Egypt. He met those of the Byzantines and Copts who had gathered for him at al-Karyun and routed them. Then he besieged the city for three months, took it by the sword, spared its people and made them dhimmis. He sent the conquest and the fifth to Umar with [[person/muawiya-ibn-khudayj|Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

It is said that al-Muqawqis made peace with him for thirteen thousand dinars and two dinars for each adult male of the Copts. Amr appointed [[person/abdullah-ibn-hudhafa-al-sahmi|Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa]] over Alexandria with a garrison of Muslims. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
The Muslims camped between Hulwa and Qasr Faris, with Coptic leaders supplying them with food and fodder. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 110} In a report of Muhammad ibn Yahya al-Iskandarani, Amr stayed at Hulwa for two months, then moved to al-Maqs, where horsemen came out against him from the Buhayra side under cover of the fortress, and twelve Muslims were killed that day at the Church of Gold. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 111}

## Duration and date
In the report of Yahya ibn Ayyub and Khalid ibn Humayd, the Muslims besieged Alexandria for nine months after Heraclius's death and five before it, and it fell on a Friday at the new moon of al-Muharram in the year 20. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 117} Zayd ibn Aslam reports that Umar wrote to Amr complaining of the delay and ordered a single concerted charge on Friday after the prayer, after which it was taken. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 116} It is also said that [[person/maslama-ibn-mukhallad-al-ansari|Maslama ibn Mukhallad]] advised Amr to give command of the fighting to [[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]], who took the city that same day; in the author's father's version Amr said that the end would be set right only by those who set right the beginning, meaning the Ansar. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 116} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 117} From the start of the Alexandria campaign to its fall, twenty-two Muslims were killed. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 119}

## "The second conquest"
Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports from Ibn Lahi'a a second conquest: Amr left a thousand men in Alexandria, the Romans returned by sea and killed them, and Amr came back and took the city, writing to Umar that it was taken by force without treaty or covenant. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 118} He adds that a gatekeeper called Ibn Bassama opened the gate in return for a safe-conduct, and that Ibn Bassama still had descendants in Alexandria in the author's day. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 118} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 119}

## The messenger and the city
Amr sent [[person/muawiya-ibn-khudayj|Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj]] to Umar with the news, without a letter, telling him: "Are you not an Arab who can tell what you saw?" When he told Umar, Umar fell prostrate. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 119} Amr is reported to have written that he found there four thousand villas with four thousand baths, forty thousand Jews paying jizya and four hundred royal places of amusement, and that twelve thousand greengrocers were found in the city. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 120} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 121} Most of the men wanted it divided, but Umar wrote that it was not to be divided and that its land tax should be fay' for the Muslims. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 122}
