## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah sent to Banu Abd al-Madan to tell them of Ibn Humayd's approach and to recall the covenant al-Hadi had made with them before leaving for Sa'da. When he asked for their counsel they answered that the strife of Banu al-Harith was lighter for them than the strife of al-Hadi, and swore that their own lives would come before his. He accepted their oath.

## Detail
The book says that before leaving for Sa'da, al-Hadi had gathered Banu Abd al-Madan, told them of Abu Ja'far's position among them and of his kindness to them, left him with them, and charged them: if you start a disturbance, or obey someone who does, or if a soldier camps in your village, I will make it lawful for myself to act, and you will have made it a land of war. They swore to this. When Muhammad now sent to them about Ibn Humayd's approach and about what he and Banu al-Harith had agreed, and asked their counsel, he reminded them of the covenant and of their own pledges. He said that if they meant to stand with him against this man, well; but if they said 'we are with you' and then, when the fighting was joined, said they feared ruin for him and for themselves and asked him to leave the village, that would not serve him. Their reply, as the book gives it, was that the strife of Banu al-Harith was lighter for them than the strife of al-Hadi. They swore that their own lives would come before his and their women before his, and that when the fighting came he should keep near them until they had shown their stand; if they fell before him, he would then be entitled to look to his own life. He accepted this from them.
