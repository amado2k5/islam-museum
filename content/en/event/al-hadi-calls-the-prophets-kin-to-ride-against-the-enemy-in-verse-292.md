## Overview
A poem in the kamil metre under the heading "and also of his", begun at the foot of p. 310 with swords drawn and claiming Muhammad as their kin, in which al-Hadi asks the Prophet's family and clan when they will rise and how long they will keep to the beds of their wives, urges them to gird up, and pictures an armed host with its horsemen and horses; the footnote says a line and what follows is missing from manuscript S. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}

## Detail
At the foot of p. 310, under the heading "and also of his", lines in the kamil metre begin by saying the swords are drawn "and claim Muhammad as their own" until they reach the marks of the valleys and "we make the cutting swords judge among them until we set up the sway of the road" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}. On p. 311 he says "we are the trusted ones, sons of the Prophet Muhammad" and asks [[group/ahl-al-bayt|the Prophet's family]] when their rising will be and how long they will cling to the beds of their wives, urging "the Prophet's clan" to gird up and prepare as the noble do {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.311}. He asks when their horses will go out toward the enemy with a dusty army, many in neighing and armed, with the noble youths in it, "the armoured before the clan of Muhammad", death their habit, mail gleaming above them like lightning in the dark, the white-faced horses carrying them through the dust, and they dash on their enemy with polished swords and lances {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.311}. In the close he urges them to fold up the hem of rest for the fight and asks how long "the people of folly" will ride their backs and necks, a phrase that tags a group with a contemptuous epithet the museum does not repeat {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.311}. The footnotes say this line and what follows to the end of the poem are missing from manuscript S, and that "al-qustalayn" is the dust {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.311}. The book names no enemy here and says only that the call is to "the Prophet's family" and "clan", which is the poet's word. Earlier, on pp. 300-301, stand [[event/al-hadi-to-his-cousins-of-the-prophets-family-on-wakefulness-and-their-division-292|his lines to his cousins of the Prophet's family]], with a close appeal. It follows [[event/al-hadi-names-his-hospitality-to-the-guest-and-the-neighbour-in-verse-292|the lines on hospitality and neighbours]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-answers-a-letter-of-reproach-and-the-claim-of-ingratitude-in-verse-292|his answer to a letter of reproach]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
