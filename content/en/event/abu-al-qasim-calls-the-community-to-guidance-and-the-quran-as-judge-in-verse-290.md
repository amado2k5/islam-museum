## Overview
On p. 268 Abu al-Qasim says to the community that the watering place and the return from it have been fouled for them, that what they did is the doing of one who changed a religion and betrayed, and he calls them to return to guidance, to follow the truth with light and sight, to let the Qur'an judge between them and to leave the tales of the night talk; he says that the word of God is a better cure for them and that the sword has its judgement on us, and he urges them to follow what Yahya said, by which they are saved, warning them of the blaze with a clear warning {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.268}. On p. 269 he says that the swords and the brown lances have lost him, that he has exchanged sleep for wakefulness, that he will raise a high cloud of dust with horses and with cutting blades and that he will pass round to their enemies the cup of destruction {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.269}.

## Detail
This piece addresses a wider public after the address to the covenant-breakers in [[event/abu-al-qasim-addresses-the-covenant-breakers-and-the-sky-of-tyranny-in-verse-290|the two previous pieces]]; the "Yahya" the lines send them to is read as his father al-Hadi, which the context gives and the line does not state. The notes record differences of manuscript S, among them a dropped line. The book ascribes the verse to Abu al-Qasim and the museum has not checked the attribution; the call, the threat and the appeal to the Qur'an are the poet's, with no verdict in the museum's voice. The museum does not quote the lines nor the words of abuse.
