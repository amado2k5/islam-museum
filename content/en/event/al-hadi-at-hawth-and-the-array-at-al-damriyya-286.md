## Overview
"[[person/muhammad-ibn-ali-al-tabari|Muhammad ibn Ali al-Tabari]] told me: the people went out to consult about fighting the group and how to proceed until their crier helped them." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} The account is Zaydi and continues [[event/al-hadi-and-the-wrongdoers-of-banu-mamar-at-al-ahsa-and-the-first-days-at-hawth-286]]. The date is given to the month, since the book names days of the week and no day of the month, and it may run into early Sha'ban.

## A man among the people
Al-Tabari says one of them said: "People, will you fight your cousins? Do not, clan, and do not be the cause of your companions' ruin," and they said: "We have oaths to al-Hadi on our necks," and he said: "Do you prefer al-Hadi's oaths to your cousins?" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} The narrator also heard from one of his companions whom he trusts that word had reached him of a saying among them: "What are you doing, leaving your clans and standing with a man for none but God?" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} Al-Tabari says the first man was breaking the people from fighting and holding them back, "and he did not rise from his seat before his companions brought him a litter, and he was carried in it in severe pain, in the state of death". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} The manuscript margin reads "a karama". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} The museum gives the report as the narrator's own and links it to nothing else.

## Tuesday and Wednesday
On Tuesday al-Hadi sent a party to call them to the truth, when the criers' cries had come from Banu Rabi'a, and they told the messengers they feared meeting him on one side of the camp, the wording being unclear in the printed text; al-Hadi disliked that, and on Wednesday he sent the messengers again, calling them to God, and they refused to answer. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135}

## Thursday: the array
Al-Hadi went out on Thursday beyond Hawth and gathered the people; [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]] has it from [[person/muhammad-ibn-sulayman-al-kufi|Muhammad ibn Sulayman]] from some of their companions. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} The Khawlanis said: "Son of the Messenger of God, we fear the rain on our bows and our weapons," and he said: "March, for no rain will fall on you today." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136} He went on until he came out of Hawth and saw the group near a pond called "al-Damriyya", and ordered the people to array. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136}
He put [[person/husayn-ibn-al-hasan-al-alawi|al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan al-Alawi]] with Banu Ubayd of [[group/wadia-tribe-yemen|Wadi'a]] and the people of [[place/athafit|Athafit]] on the left; [[person/ismail-ibn-al-muslim-al-hamdani|Ismail ibn al-Muslim]] with the Khawlanis and al-Mahra on the right; Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Tarif al-Wadi'i, of the Wadi'a of Najran, with Banu Rabi'a and Banu Sarim in the centre; and al-Hadi went before them among [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|the Tabari emigrants]] and others, in front of the centre. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136} The narrator says God kept the rain from them that day. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136}

## The messenger and al-Ghubayyib
Before the lines met a messenger came from the group saying they would "all come to him and give what is due from them"; al-Hadi told him: "Go and bring them", and told the people to take the road by their units to a place called "al-Ghubayyib", where the editor refers to Sifat Jazirat al-Arab, p. 139. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136}

## Ismail's proposal and al-Hadi's refusal
Ismail ibn al-Muslim said to al-Hadi: "Take us to these rebels' vines and let us cut them and pull down their houses," and he answered: "I shall not go to their places while they are here, until I call them to God; if they come, well, and if not I shall fight them." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136} Al-Hadi went on until near them; when they saw he meant to fight and saw the size of his army, "they raced to a rough place on the road and stayed in it", and al-Hadi told the people to hold back, sent a party to call them, and when the prayer time came told the people to pray. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136} The account runs on to the next page.

## Notes
This batch ends with the people at prayer, so the book's account of how the meeting ended is not yet read. The text reads "qiyasina", which the editor explains as the plural of "qaws", bows. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.136} The man who urged them not to fight is not named. The name "al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan al-Alawi" is as printed, and is not matched here with the governor of Saqayn.
