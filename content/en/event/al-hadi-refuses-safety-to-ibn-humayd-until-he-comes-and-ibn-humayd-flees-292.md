## Overview
The narrator says Ibn Humayd had not come to al-Hadi; "then in days left of the month of Ramadan it reached him that Ibn Humayd was meeting bedouin of Yam and Banu al-Harith and gathering them to disorder", so he went out to the place he was told of and did not find him, drew near it and ordered its palms cut, and many palms were cut {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.294}.

## Detail
Then Banu al-Harith gathered to him and asked him to stop cutting palms for the rest of that day until they could meet Ibn Humayd and see what he had, and they brought a body of their companions to him; he agreed and went back to the village of al-Hajar {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.294}. Banu al-Harith went to Ibn Humayd and asked him to go with them to al-Hadi, or else he would be ruined and his wealth wiped out; he told them he feared him and was ashamed before him and that these sons of his would go with them to ask safety from him, so they all went in to al-Hadi and asked safety for Ibn Humayd {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.294}. He said: "I will not give him safety unless he steps on my carpet; as for these palms I give them to you, and I am seeking his person and his camels, and if I get any of that there is no blame on you from me" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.294}. When that reached Ibn Humayd he left at once for the desert, fearing for himself {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.295}. The account presents al-Hadi as one who forgives the town and seeks one named man, in the voice of his supporters in a Zaydi book; the museum describes it without judgement. The clause "or else he would ruin him" can be read as Banu al-Harith speaking to Ibn Humayd or as al-Hadi speaking of him; the context favours the first and the museum does not insist. It follows [[event/al-hadi-enters-al-hajar-on-monday-17-shaban-292|the entry into al-Hajar]]. For the man see [[person/ibn-humayd-of-banu-al-harith-286|Ibn Humayd]].
