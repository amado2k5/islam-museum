## Summary
Al-Tha'alibi sojourned at least twice at Jurjan, staying with members of the city's leading family, and dedicated a series of works to its amir Qabus ibn Wushmgir — among them al-Mubhij, a collection of rhymed-prose sentences; Qabus was himself a noted stylist, and al-Tha'alibi had probably met him during the amir's long exile at Nishapur before he regained his throne in 388 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 450-451}.
