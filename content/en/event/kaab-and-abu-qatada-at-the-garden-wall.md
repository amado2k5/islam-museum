## Summary
The Muslims' estrangement from Ka'b wore on; he climbed the wall of his cousin Abu Qatada's garden and greeted him, and Abu Qatada did not return the greeting — then Ka'b adjured him three times, and at the third he said only: "God and His Messenger know best." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/178}

## The account
Ka'b said: when the Muslims' estrangement wore long upon me I walked until I climbed the wall of Abu Qatada's garden — my cousin and the dearest of people to me — and greeted him; by God, he did not return my greeting. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/178}

## The adjuration
I said: Abu Qatada, I adjure you by God — do you know that I love God and His Messenger? He was silent. I adjured him again; he was silent. I adjured him again, and he said: "God and His Messenger know best." My eyes brimmed and I sprang up, climbed back over the wall and went off to the market. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/178}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- Ibn Ishaq names him only "Abu Qatada", Ka'b's cousin; he has not been identified with any other biography in this batch.
