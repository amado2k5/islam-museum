## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad relates from his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]], who speaks here in the first person, that on Tuesday with seven days left of Rabi' II "Abu Muhammad", who by the context of pp. 87 and 88 is [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]], marched to "Afara", Abu al-Dughaysh's village where he was camped, and the narrator went there by a second road. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.87} The account is Zaydi, in an eyewitness voice, and is given as it stands.

## The ambush
[[person/abu-al-dughaysh-al-shihabi|Abu al-Dughaysh]] had laid an ambush, which came out on the narrator and his companions, who had the men of [[place/wasaha-yemen|Wasaha]] with him and no other horseman; his companions fled and three of them were killed; he stood against the people with twenty on foot; his companions went to Abdullah ibn al-Husayn and none returned; he sent the remainder one by one asking for reinforcements and none came, so he stayed with eleven men; two hundred charged them, hit his horse with an arrow, it fell, they hamstrung it with their swords and killed three more of his companions. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.87} One of his men shot one of them dead, and God cast awe into their hearts, "so they dispersed from us". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.87}

## The rout and the burning of the village
War broke out between Abu al-Dughaysh and Abdullah ibn al-Husayn, God gave Abdullah victory, Abu al-Dughaysh fled and Abdullah's army entered Afara; those who had been fighting the narrator surrounded him until he and his men were in the middle of them with no way out, when one of his men saw fire and smoke in Afara and Abu Muhammad's men burning it; the army near them turned, saw the fire and smoke, and fled. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.87} The fighting lasted from mid-morning to noon; the narrator fought on foot after his horse was hit, with nothing but a garment he had taken from one of his companions and wrapped on his arm for protection, until God gave victory over them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.87} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.88} Abu Muhammad killed a number of them and they were driven out of their village. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.88}

## After the battle
Some of the army raided without Abu Muhammad's knowledge and took a little from the village; [[person/yusuf-ibn-muhammad-al-hasani|Yusuf ibn Muhammad al-Hasani]] was sent and stopped the people from plunder; seeing this, the villagers all asked security and were given it; they said the army had plundered goods of theirs, he looked into it and found no proof, and when their alms were collected a quarter was divided among their poor, then he ordered them half a sixth more in compensation for what they claimed and asked them to hold the army's harm blameless. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.88}

## Meeting Abdullah ibn al-Husayn
The narrator says he came to Abdullah ibn al-Husayn with only eight companions; Abdullah told a servant to bring him a horse, and the narrator did not ride it; seeing that, Abdullah came and said "Cousin, what is the matter?"; he told him what had happened and Abdullah apologised and swore he knew nothing of their plight and no messenger had come; he accepted this and believed him given the evil of those he had sent to that place and their fear of returning, then mounted the horse, and Abu Muhammad went back to Wasaha and he with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.88} The word "cousin" points to a kinship between the narrator and Abdullah ibn al-Husayn that the text does not spell out.
