## Summary
In Jumada al-Akhira of 623 AH Muzaffar al-Din Kukburi, lord of Erbil, marched into the Mosul districts heading for the city, then turned back without crossing to it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 462}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that the march sprang from a pact between him, Jalal al-Din ibn Khwarazmshah, al-Malik al-Mu'azzam of Damascus, the lord of Amid and Nasir al-Din of Mardin to take and divide al-Malik al-Ashraf's lands; Muzaffar al-Din moved quickly toward Mosul, while Jalal al-Din set out from Tiflis for Akhlat but, on hearing that his Kirman deputy Baraq Hajib had rebelled, dropped Akhlat — though his troops plundered and ravaged parts of it — and hastened to Kirman, so the whole plan collapsed except Muzaffar al-Din's march. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 462} He camped on the bank of the Zab but could not cross toward the city; Badr al-Din had written from Mosul to al-Ashraf, then at Raqqa, begging aid, and al-Ashraf marched via Harran to Dunaysir, ravaging and plundering the land of Mardin; the Mu'azzam of Damascus meanwhile moved on Homs and Hama and sent word to Muzaffar al-Din to leave Mosul, so he withdrew and each party went home; the districts of Mosul and Mardin were devastated by this movement, already stricken by dearth and the flight of most of their people, so ruin increased. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 462}
