## Overview
In 381 AH the caliph [[person/al-tai-billah|al-Ta'i]] was seized. Al-Suyuti says the reason was that he had imprisoned a man of the intimate circle of [[person/baha-al-dawla|Baha' al-Dawla]]. Baha' al-Dawla came while al-Ta'i was sitting in the portico with a sword girded on, kissed the ground and sat on the chair. His companions advanced and pulled al-Ta'i from his couch, and the Daylam crowded upon him and wrapped him in a cloak. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.631-632}

## The deposition
Baha' al-Dawla wrote a document deposing al-Ta'i himself and stating that he had handed over the matter to [[person/al-qadir-billah|al-Qadir billah]], and the notables and nobles bore witness to it. It was on the nineteenth of Sha'ban. Al-Qadir was sent for to attend, and was in al-Bataiha. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.631-632}

## After the deposition
Al-Ta'i remained honoured in the house of al-Qadir until he died on the night of the Feast of Breaking the Fast in 393 AH. Al-Qadir prayed over him, the notables followed his bier, and al-Sharif al-Radi composed an elegy for him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.631-632}

## Al-Suyuti's judgement
Al-Suyuti relates that the awe of the caliphate fell in the days of al-Ta'i, to the point that the poets satirised him, and he describes him as harshly deviant toward the family of Abu Talib. This is al-Suyuti's description. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.631-632}
