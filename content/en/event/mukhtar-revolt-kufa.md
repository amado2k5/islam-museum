## Overview
In 65 AH the Shi'a stirred in Kufa, as the book of Bakr on the Umayyad state relates. The movement was led by [[person/al-mukhtar-ibn-abi-ubayd|al-Mukhtar ibn Abi Ubayd al-Thaqafi]]. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.206}

## Kufa
The book says that Ibn al-Zubayr sent al-Mukhtar to [[place/kufa|Kufa]]. He settled in one of its quarters, showed grief for the Talibiyyun, and urged vengeance for them, and the Shi'a inclined to him. He removed Abd Allah ibn Mut'i from the governor's palace and took control of Kufa. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.194}

## Al-Khazir
In 66 AH [[person/ibrahim-ibn-al-ashtar|Ibrahim ibn al-Ashtar]] was in command of the cavalry of Iraq on behalf of al-Mukhtar. He met Ubayd Allah ibn Ziyad at al-Khazir and killed him. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.209}

The book gives a report that al-Mukhtar's head was brought before Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.220}

## In al-Dinawari
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports that the Hamra', Kufans of Persian descent, numbered about twenty thousand, and that Hamdan were the largest group to answer al-Mukhtar. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 288} Al-Mukhtar came out with seven thousand horse and appointed governors over Mosul, Adharbayjan, Hamadhan, Isfahan, Rayy and other provinces. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 290} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 292} The revenue of those lands came to him for eighteen months. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 299} Mus'ab then besieged him in the palace for forty days; two brothers of Banu Hanifa killed him, and some six thousand of his men in the palace surrendered to Mus'ab's judgement after two months and were executed, two thousand Arabs and four thousand non-Arabs. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 307} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 308} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 309}
