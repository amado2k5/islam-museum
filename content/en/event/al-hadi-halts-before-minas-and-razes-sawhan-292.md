## Overview
"When it was Thursday" al-Hadi gathered his troops and marched until he was near Minas and halted the troops to one side of it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.290}; he did not like to enter it because Ibn Bustam was writing to him, and stood an hour facing it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}.

## Detail
Then he went on to Sawhan, ruined and demolished it, hoped the people would come out to him, and they did not {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}. He then went back with his army to al-Hisn, where he stayed on Friday and Saturday {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}. The account gives Ibn Bustam's correspondence as the reason al-Hadi left Minas alone; what lay between Ibn Bustam and Banu al-Harith is in [[event/ibn-bustam-writes-to-both-sides-and-banu-al-harith-resolve-to-march-on-wadia-292|the first event of this batch]]. The Thursday is read as that of the week of the Sunday fourth of Rajab from the order of the text, but the book gives no date, so the precision is the month. Sawhan is a place near Najran met earlier in [[event/al-hadis-charge-at-sawhan-286|the fight at Sawhan in 286]]; the book gives no reason for the razing beyond the hope that people would come out. The account is from al-Hadi's supporters in a Zaydi book and no word of abuse is repeated.
