## Overview
When the men knew that [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] was about to charge them and would not leave them, they feared destruction for themselves and some cried out for safety; al-Hadi halted his army, and a number of their commanders and men came until they dismounted, greeted him and asked him to turn back from them that night {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.234} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235}. They all spent the night, and some of his army meant to kill them; he disliked it for them, drew his sword on his companions and said, as the narrator gives it, whoever commits an act I shall strike his head, and ordered them back to [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235}. Some of the men went with him and the others went to their camp, to be there for the night and to come back to him the next day; they did not come and broke the pledge, the war resumed, and al-Hadi stayed at Sana'a {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235}.

## Detail
The editor took from another manuscript the reading and returning in place of and morning-goers in the clause about the next day {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235}. The museum reports the asking for safety and al-Hadi's answer as given and passes no judgement on the men's breaking of it; the account is the narrator's, from al-Hadi's side. It follows [[event/the-army-of-eid-al-adha-is-deceived-and-the-battle-at-the-maydan-288]] and leads to [[event/the-sorties-of-17-dhu-al-hijja-and-the-night-fight-at-sanaa-288]].
