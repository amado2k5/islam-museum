## Overview
Al-Hadi's letter to Banu al-Harith, begun in [[event/banu-al-harith-swear-against-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-and-al-hadi-writes-to-them-292]], continues on p. 276. He says he has no doubt that much abandonment is with them because of their dislike of truth and faith and their following of Satan, that whoever opposes the truth is the loser, and that God willed to change their favour and remove the good with which He had begun, though nothing they had done earned what they were given {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.276}.

## Detail
The letter reads their story as a pattern. They had lived in blessings and, once rich, disbelief in the favour and disobedience appeared among them; it cites [[passage/quran-96-6|al-Alaq 96:6]] and [[passage/quran-96-7|al-Alaq 96:7]] on the one who turns rebellious when he sees himself self-sufficient, names those who were in the same state, denied their Lord's favour and lost it, and sets before them the secure and tranquil town that denied God's favours, along the lines of [[passage/quran-16-112|an-Nahl 16:112]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.276}. It then says their news has reached him, that they were ever people of scheming and betrayal against God's friends, that whoever schemes against them schemes only against himself, and that their co-religionists before them tried the same and it turned against them; it begins to cite [[passage/quran-35-43|Fatir 35:43]], which is completed on p. 277 {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.276}. The footnotes record that "and among you" is added from manuscript S and that S reads "your might" where the text has another noun. The words are al-Hadi's own as copied in a Zaydi book; the museum neither adopts them as its judgement nor repeats the words of abuse.
