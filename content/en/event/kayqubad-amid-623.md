## Summary
In Sha'ban of 623 AH Ala al-Din Kayqubad, king of the land of Rum, marched into the lands of the lord of Amid and took several of his forts, among them the formidable Kakhta after routing his forces. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 465}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir gives the cause: the lord of Amid had agreed with Jalal al-Din Khwarazmshah, al-Malik al-Mu'azzam of Damascus and others against al-Ashraf; seeing this, al-Ashraf wrote to his ally Kayqubad, king of Rum, asking him to attack the lord of Amid's country; the king marched to Malatya — his own city — halted there, and sent armies into the lord of Amid's wilaya, which captured Hisn Mansur, Hisn Shimkazad and others. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 465} The lord of Amid then wrote to al-Ashraf and returned to his side; al-Ashraf told Kayqubad to restore what he had taken, but the king refused, saying he was not al-Ashraf's deputy to be ordered about; al-Ashraf then went to Damascus and ordered his Jaziran troops to aid the lord of Amid if the king persisted; the lord of Amid marched with his army and reinforced troops to Kayqubad's army besieging Kakhta fortress, they met in Shawwal, the lord of Amid and his allies were routed heavily with many wounded and taken, and Kayqubad's army took Kakhta, one of the strongest fortresses. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 466}
