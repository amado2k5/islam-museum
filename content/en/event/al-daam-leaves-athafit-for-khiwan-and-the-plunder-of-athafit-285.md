## Overview
The book says some people of [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]] had been writing to [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] asking him to come to it; when he left [[place/athafit|Athafit]] he went to Khiwan and settled there. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108} The account is Zaydi, in the voice of the imam's followers; its sayings are attributed to their narrators and the museum gives no verdict on them.

## The plundering of Athafit
At the morning prayer the news reached [[group/banu-sarim-hamdan|Banu Sarim]] and their companions, who were near Athafit, that al-Da'am had left it; the foolish, the slave and the boy among them raided, entered Athafit and plundered goods that had been left in it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108} The people of Athafit had removed most of their goods for fear of Banu Sarim and [[group/banu-rabia-hamdan|Banu Rabi'a]], because they had not supported them on the day [[person/abu-umar-abd-al-aziz-ibn-marwan-al-bahrani|Abu Umar]] was killed, and the two peoples bore a grudge against them for it, so they plundered the household goods they found. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108} The narrator says he heard some of them say they had thought what they took was lawful for them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108}

## Al-Hadi's grief
When [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] heard that al-Da'am had left he did not believe it and thought it false, and spent his day checking until people of Athafit came and told him of the departure and of the plunder, which weighed on him heavily and filled him with deep grief. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108} He told [[person/muhammad-ibn-said-ibn-yusuf|Muhammad ibn Sa'id]] that he had resolved to withdraw, to speak to no one and let no one come in, and that but for his fear of harm to Islam he would not have stayed in Yemen and would have gone home. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108} These are al-Hadi's words as the narrator gives them.

## The narrator's witness and the two companions' counsel
Ali ibn Muhammad reports Muhammad ibn Sulayman saying that in all his time with al-Hadi he never saw him so grieved as then: he spoke swallowing his sobs, tears choked him, and he kept repeating the words of return to God and saying that religion was departing. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108} The narrator and Muhammad ibn Sa'id kept coaxing him, saying that if he did this Islam would perish; he answered that he would stay there until God gave him believing people who would hear of him and come, and he would stand with them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.108} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.109} They said gentleness was all that would serve them, and that if he saw fit he should send for them, speak to them and ask them to repent, for they were ignorant bedouins who may not have thought it forbidden; he said this was an opinion to follow. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.109} The sequel is in [[event/al-hadi-has-the-loot-returned-and-the-gathering-on-the-day-of-arafa-285]].
