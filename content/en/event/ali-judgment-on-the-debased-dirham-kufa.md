## Summary
Al-Sallabi recounts a judgment by Ali in the market of Kufa between two men: a sheep seller who stipulated that his buyer not give him a defective dirham, yet then gave him a defective dirham himself, and when the buyer returned the dirham to him, the seller slapped the buyer. Ali sentenced the slapper to punishment after giving the one who was slapped the choice between qisas and pardon {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}.

## Facts
A man bought a sheep on condition that the seller give him dirhams that were neither defective nor clipped, but he gave him a defective dirham; the buyer returned it, and the seller slapped him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}. When Ali asked the buyer about the dirham, he said, "He spoke the truth, Commander of the Faithful" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}, so Ali said, "Give him what he stipulated," then said to the slapper, "Sit," and said to the one who had been slapped, "Take qisas," whereupon the one who had been slapped asked, "Or pardon, Commander of the Faithful?" and Ali said, "That is up to you" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}.

## Punishment
When the man passed on—that is, turned to leave—Ali said, "O assembly of Muslims, seize him." He was hoisted onto a man's back just as schoolboys in a kuttab are carried, and Ali struck him fifteen lashes with the dirra, saying, "This is an exemplary punishment for your violating his dignity" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}. In another report, he said, "This is the right of the ruler" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}.

## The Book's Reading
Al-Sallabi regards this report as a lofty example of humility, as Ali goes out to the market to inspect people's affairs in person, and he considers that people's sense that their governors are with them suffices to reassure those who have rights and to deter anyone who infringes upon the rights of people and the right of God {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}. He comments that Islam does not attach importance to outward form, but rather to objectives: preserving security and spreading peace among the believers {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book attributes the report in its footnote to Tarikh al-Tabari; it should not be relied upon alone in judging the incident without consulting the original source.
- The rule of ta'zir is derived from this report, but the book does not specify the type of punishment or its limits; this should be verified from a legal reference.
- The distinction between "qisas" and "ta'zir" in the incident requires review; the book describes it as the right of the ruler.
