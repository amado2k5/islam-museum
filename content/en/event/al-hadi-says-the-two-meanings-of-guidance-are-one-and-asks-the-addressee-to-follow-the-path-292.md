## Overview
A poem in the kamil metre under the heading "and also of his" on p. 325, in which al-Hadi tells an unnamed addressee that the two meanings are one thing so he should seek right guidance, that nothing equals the face of truth, that he should shun the desire of the braggart, and that he is the friend, brother of the friend, and should leave the path of the crooked and corrupt; the lines run on into p. 326. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}

## Detail
At the top of p. 325, under the heading "and also of his" (kamil), he says "the two meanings, may you be guided, are one thing, so seek, may you be led aright, the meanings of success" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}. He says "nothing equals the face of truth, so seek it, and shun, may you be safe, the desire of the braggart"; the footnote says al-fajfaj is one who talks much and boasts of what he lacks {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}. He asks him to aim at what he wants exactly and to roll up his aim to its farthest end, and says "if you seek from us an Arabic goal, it will be done when carried on the path" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}; the footnote says manuscript S reads "need" for "goal". Then he says "you are the friend, brother of the friend, and of generosity, so leave the path of the corrupt, crooked", the footnote glossing "crooked" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}. He says that when the hard parts of matters are sought they are tried and become like the dark of night {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}. The book does not name the addressee, and nothing shows whether he is al-Da'am or another, so no link is made; and it does not date the poem. The lines run on into the lines of p. 326, which are for the next batch. It comes after [[event/al-hadi-names-the-wrongs-of-his-opponents-and-what-he-did-for-the-people-in-verse-292|what the poet says of his opponents and the subjects]]. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
