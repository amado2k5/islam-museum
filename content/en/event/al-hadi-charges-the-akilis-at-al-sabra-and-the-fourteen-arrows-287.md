## Overview
A party of Akilis were on a mountain of theirs called [[place/al-sabra-allaf-yemen|al-Sabra]]; they had wounded many of the men, and were about a hundred and fifty riders whom [[person/ahmad-ibn-abbad-al-akili|Ibn Abbad]] had picked and placed there. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.192} When [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] saw them he charged them with seven riders of his companions, among them Ali ibn Muhammad and Yahya ibn Mahamil al-Sulami. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.192} When they saw him they shot arrows at him and hit him with fourteen arrows, in the narrator's account, and he drove them from the place they held, and his foot charged them and climbed the mountain and routed them in the worst rout, and night came between him and them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.192}

## Detail
Al-Hadi withdrew and ordered the heads of the Akilis who had been killed cut off, and they were, and he went back to Sa'da and stayed there some days. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.192} A note in the margin of the original has it that he ordered the heads of the dead cut off and carried. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.192} The number of arrows, his survival and the report are the narrator's, a follower of the imam, and the museum asserts neither the count nor the escape; the cutting off of heads is likewise the narrator's report and the museum gives no judgement. Ali ibn Muhammad is probably the narrator, and Yahya ibn Mahamil al-Sulami is named only.
