## Overview
"When it was Sunday" al-Hadi set out for the village of al-Hajar in Najran and, as he neared the place, ordered his army: the Wadi'is on the right, the Shakiris and al-Ahlaf on the left, the Khawlanis in the centre {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}. He had set the Tabaris and a number of others apart on their own and told them to stay out of the fighting and not approach it, placed his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] with them and his brother [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abu Muhammad]] aside with the horse together with Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi advanced with the army until each body fought from its own side, and he himself drew near the lane gate on the left; those at the gate fled, then a man called out without being told to Abu al-Qasim and his men to answer al-Hadi, and they came on unformed, the Tabaris going forward step by step until they went in with the others at the lane gate {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}. The gate had four twisting walls, one behind another, which turned the lances back from the Tabaris, and there were loopholes in the walls from which people shot without being seen, so arrows struck among them; the Tabaris entered by the side of the village and routed them until the rout reached the far end of the village {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}. Al-Hadi then went in on the Tabaris and drew them out of their place under many arrows and stones; four of his men fell and seven or eight of Banu al-Harith were killed, with many wounded {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291}. He then withdrew with his army, carried his dead and buried them at al-Hisn, and stayed there some days {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.291} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.292}. The text says the man who called to the Tabaris had not been ordered to, and does not name him; the word "raabiya", used of the Tabaris set apart, is read as a group standing by itself, an understanding from the context and not an editor's gloss. The numbers are from al-Hadi's supporters in a Zaydi book and the museum does not confirm them. On the groups see [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|the Tabaris]] and [[place/al-hajar-najran|al-Hajar]]; the story goes on at [[event/the-camp-at-qarqar-the-cutting-of-palms-and-the-razing-of-markets-292|the camp at Qarqar]].
