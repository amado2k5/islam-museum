## Overview
After the account of the fight at Najran the narrator gives a poem by [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] that he says he composed on that day. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.170} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.171} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.172} It opens with the phantom of a beloved visiting, walking proudly in her ornaments, then the poet turns his concern to war: his mail, his weapons and his lances; he describes riders, horses, the army and the Mashrafi swords, says his people amuse themselves with the thrust of spears and that war is their wine, and describes the heat of the fight, the squadrons piling on each other and the enemy host breaking up. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.170} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.171}

## Its themes
The poet says he hopes, by God's favour in giving him victory, for a lasting Paradise; he takes pride in being of the stock of Hashim and of the family of Muhammad, who inherit the Prophet's station and show every community of Ahmad the ways of right; and he praises [[group/banu-hamdan|Hamdan]] as helpers of his house, naming with them Sa'd and Khawlan. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.171} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.172} The museum does not reproduce the lines, which are praise of himself and his people by his own voice; only the sense is given.

## The poem of Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Hamdani
The narrator next gives a poem by [[person/ahmad-ibn-muhammad-al-hamdani-poet-286|Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Hamdani]] in which he excuses his people: he says they were called and counselled to honour, the Lord's pleasure, security, an end of evil and of injustice, through a just imam of the Messenger's line who speaks the truth, and were on another path, so that their counsel showed its result against them; he describes his people as men of forbearance, pardon and patience, among them the guide Yahya, which is al-Hadi's own name, fearsome in the charge, who helped the truth and did what its obligations required of them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.171} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.172}

## Notes
After the poems the book says that when Banu al-Harith fled to the Mountain of al-Ukhdud they clung to it and threw away arms and clothes. The order of lines in the printed text is disturbed where the sense breaks off, so the boundaries of each poem are given only as the printing shows. The account is told from the imam's partisans' side. The context is in [[event/al-hadi-routs-banu-al-harith-and-the-forty-horsemen-286]].
