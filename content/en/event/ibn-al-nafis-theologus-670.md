## Summary
At Cairo Ibn al-Nafis composed al-Risala al-Kamiliyya fi al-sira al-nabawiyya — the Theologus Autodidactus — an intellectual tour de force in which a solitary man called Kamil on a desert island deduces by abstract reasoning that the events of the Prophet's life and of the community's history were the best that could possibly have happened {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 898}.

## In the text
The treatise takes in the Mongol incursions of the author's own lifetime and the physical appearance of the Muslim ruler — no doubt sultan Baybars — and ends with a naturalistic account of the Last Things; since it portrays the living Baybars (658–676 AH) it is dated approximately within his reign {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 898}.
