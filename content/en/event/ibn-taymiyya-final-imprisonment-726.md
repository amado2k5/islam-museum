## Overview
On 16 Sha'ban 726/18 July 1326 Ibn Taymiyya was arrested without further trial, and a sultanic decree depriving him of the right to issue fatwas was read out in the Umayyad Mosque, over his epistle on visits to tombs (ziyarat al-qubur) in which he condemned the cult of the saints. His disciples were arrested with him and soon released — all but Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya. He remained a prisoner in the citadel more than two years, writing and issuing fatwas; works of this period include Ma'arij al-wusul, Raf' al-malam, and the Radd 'ala al-Ikhna'i. On 9 Jumada II 728/21 April 1328, on a complaint by al-Ikhna'i, the sultan ordered his paper, ink and pens taken from him. Five months later he died in the citadel, on 20 Dhu al-Qa'da 728/26 September 1328, and was buried in the cemetery of the Sufiyya, where his tomb is still honoured {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 985}.
