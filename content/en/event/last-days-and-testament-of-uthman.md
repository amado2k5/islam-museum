## Summary
This record covers the last days of [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]] in his besieged house, his refusal to let his defenders fight, the dream reported on the night of his death, the document found in his chest, and his burial by night at Hashsh Kawkab beside al-Baqi'. The siege and the killing themselves are told in [[event/siege-of-uthman-35|the siege of Uthman]] and [[event/murder-of-uthman|the murder of Uthman]] and are not repeated here. He was killed on Friday, eighteen nights into Dhu al-Hijja 35 AH, on the well-known account. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188}

## Holding back his defenders
Ibn Kathir says the siege lasted from the end of Dhu al-Qa'da to Friday the eighteenth of Dhu al-Hijja. The day before his death Uthman told those in the house, Emigrants and Ansar, about seven hundred, among them Abd Allah ibn Umar, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, al-Hasan, al-Husayn, Marwan and Abu Hurayra: "I charge anyone who owes me a debt of loyalty to hold back his hand and go home." And he told his slaves that anyone who sheathed his sword was free. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 180} It is reported that the last to leave after he had charged them was al-Hasan ibn Ali, and that the commander of the defence of the house was Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 180} Ibn Kathir says that he ordered the people and charged them not to fight for him, and that but for his firm instruction they would have defended him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 182}

## The dream on the night of his death
The dream is told in several forms. From Ibn Umar, through Nafi': Uthman got up in the morning telling people he had seen the Prophet in his sleep, who said, "Uthman, break your fast with us"; he woke fasting and was killed that day. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 180} From Kathir ibn al-Salt: Uthman saw the Prophet with Abu Bakr and Umar, who told him, "Go back, for tomorrow you will break your fast with me," and Uthman said the sun would not set that day except that he would be of the people of the next world. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 180} In another report from Ibn al-Salt, Uthman dozed on the day he was killed and woke saying he had seen the Prophet say to him, "You will witness the Friday prayer with us." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 180} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 181}

In a third report Ibn al-Salt went in to Uthman while he was under siege and Uthman said he saw himself as killed that day; he had stayed awake that night, and towards dawn dozed and saw the Prophet with Abu Bakr and Umar; the Prophet said, "Uthman, join us, do not keep us waiting, for we are waiting for you." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 181} From Abd Allah ibn Salam: he came to greet him under siege and Uthman said he had seen the Prophet that night, who asked whether they had besieged and thirsted him, and let down a bucket of water from which he drank until quenched; the Prophet said that if he wished he would be given victory over them and if he wished he would break his fast with him, and he chose to break his fast with him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 181} His wife relates that he dozed and on waking said the people were going to kill him, for he had seen the Prophet, Abu Bakr and Umar telling him he would break his fast with them that night; Na'ila bint al-Furafisa says he fasted that day and when it came time to break his fast he asked for fresh water and they refused. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 181}

In the report of Abu Ya'la, Uthman freed twenty slaves that day and called for trousers and put them on, though he had not worn them in the age of ignorance or in Islam, then called for a codex and spread it open before him, and was killed with it before him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 182} Ibn Kathir explains the trousers by his great modesty: he did not want his nakedness to be exposed if he were killed. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 182}

## The will and what was found in the chest
Hisham ibn Urwa reports from his father that Uthman made al-Zubayr his executor. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 182} Al-Asma'i relates from al-Ala' ibn al-Fadl, from his father, that when Uthman was killed they searched his treasury and found a locked chest; they opened it and found a box with a sheet saying it was Uthman's will: that he bears witness there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger, that Paradise is true and the Fire is true, that God raises those in the graves for a day about which there is no doubt, and that on this he lives, on this he dies and on this he will be raised. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 182} Al-Muhibb al-Tabari gives the same account and adds that on its back were found lines of poetry on contentment of the soul and patience in hardship. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 516}

## Burial
Ibn Ishaq, quoted by al-Muhibb al-Tabari, says he was killed on Wednesday after the afternoon prayer and buried on Saturday before noon; others say he was killed on Friday; Abu Uthman al-Nahdi says in the middle of the days of tashriq. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 514} Ibn Kathir lists the reports about how long he lay unburied: Ibn Jarir says three days, one says two nights, and one says he was buried that night between sunset and the night prayer for fear of the Kharijites. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188}

In al-Waqidi's account, given by al-Muhibb al-Tabari, he was buried on the night of Saturday in a place called Hashsh Kawkab and his grave was concealed; a hashsh is a garden, and Uthman had bought it and added it to al-Baqi', and he was the first buried there. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 514} Malik says Uthman used to pass the spot of his grave in Hashsh Kawkab and say a righteous man would be buried there. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188} Al-Kamil says the garden is outside al-Baqi'. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 546}

A few Companions took him out; Ibn Kathir names Hakim ibn Hizam, Huwaytib ibn Abd al-Uzza, Abu al-Jahm ibn Hudhayfa, Jubayr ibn Mut'im, Zayd ibn Thabit, Ka'b ibn Malik, Talha, al-Zubayr and Ali, with his two wives Na'ila and Umm al-Banin; they carried him on a door. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188} In a report given by al-Muhibb al-Tabari, when he was killed he was left three days on a refuse heap; at night twelve men came, among them Huwaytib, Hakim and Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, and carried him; when they reached the graveyard some men of Banu Mazin stopped them, and they carried him to Hashsh Kawkab. His daughter [[person/aisha-bint-uthman-ibn-affan|Aisha]] was with them with a lamp, and when they took him out she cried out; al-Zubayr threatened her if she did not be quiet. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 515}

## Washing and prayer
Ibn Kathir prefers that he was washed and shrouded, and says some claimed he was neither washed nor shrouded, adding that the first is correct. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 189} Al-Hasan says he saw Uthman buried in his clothes with his blood, and another report says he was not washed. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 515} Al-Kamil says it is said he was not washed and was shrouded in his clothes. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 546}

On who prayed over him: [[person/jubayr-ibn-mutim|Jubayr ibn Mut'im]]; or al-Zubayr; or [[person/hakim-ibn-hizam|Hakim ibn Hizam]]; or Marwan ibn al-Hakam; or al-Miswar ibn Makhrama. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 189} Some Ansar came to stop the prayer and were then left alone for fear of strife. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 546} In the report of Urwa they wanted to pray over him and were stopped, and Abu Jahm ibn Hudhayfa said, "Leave him; the Messenger of God has already prayed over him." {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 515} Some Kharijites opposed it and wanted to stone his bier and throw him off, intending that he be buried in the Jews' graveyard at Dayr Sal', until Ali sent to forbid them. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 189}

## The grave and afterwards
Beside him were buried two of his slaves killed in the house, Subayh and Najih, though it is also said the Kharijites did not allow them to be buried. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 189} Banu Umayya built a large dome over his grave, which Ibn Kathir says still stood in his day. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188} In the days of Mu'awiya's rule the wall between his grave and al-Baqi' was raised and people were told to bury their dead around it until it joined the Muslims' graveyards. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 189} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 546} See [[place/baqi-al-gharqad|al-Baqi' al-Gharqad]].

## What he left
Ibn Kathir reports from al-Waqidi that his treasurer held on the day he was killed thirty million and five hundred thousand dirhams and a hundred thousand dinars, which were plundered and lost; he left a thousand camels at al-Rabadha and charitable endowments: the well of Aris, Khaybar and Wadi al-Qura, and the well of Ruma that he bought in the Prophet's lifetime and made a public water. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 189}

## Where the reports differ
- Day of the killing: Friday, eighteen nights into Dhu al-Hijja on the well-known account; also said to be in the days of tashriq, Wednesday after the afternoon prayer, or the day of sacrifice. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 514}
- Length of the siege: forty days on the well-known account, some forty days and more, twenty-two nights according to al-Sha'bi. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188}
- The dream: "break your fast with us", "go back, you will break your fast with me tomorrow", "you will witness the Friday prayer with us" and "join us". {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 180} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 181}
- Washing and prayer: washed and shrouded on the sounder account for Ibn Kathir, not washed in al-Hasan's report; the one who prayed is Jubayr or another. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 189} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 515}
- His age: over eighty-two, or eighty-four; other statements are lower and higher. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 188}
