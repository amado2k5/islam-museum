## Summary
What followed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]] in deciding the fate of some seventy Qurashi prisoners: the counsel of ransom versus execution, then ransom, the execution of two prisoners, and the Qur'anic comment on the decision {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 228-231}.

## Distribution and the charge
The Prophet divided the prisoners among his Companions and charged them to treat them well; the book relates that the Companions gave their captives bread and contented themselves with dates {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 229}.

## The counsel
The Prophet consulted Abu Bakr and Umar: Abu Bakr advised taking ransom to strengthen the Muslims, hoping God might guide them; Umar advised striking necks. The Prophet preferred Abu Bakr's view and took the ransom {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 230} {cite:tarikh-umar-ibn-al-jawzi}.

## The terms of ransom
The ransom ranged from a thousand to four thousand dirhams; whoever had no ransom was released on condition of teaching ten Medinan boys to write {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 230}. Al-Muttalib ibn Hantab, Sayfi ibn Abi Rifa'a and Abu Azza al-Jumahi were freed without ransom {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 230}. Abu al-As ibn al-Rabi' was released against a ransom Zaynab sent — a necklace that had been Khadija's — on condition she be released, and she emigrated {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 230}. The Prophet rejected a proposal to break out [[person/suhayl-ibn-amr|Suhayl ibn Amr]]'s front teeth, guarding against mutilation {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 230}. The captive Amr ibn Abi Sufyan was exchanged for Sa'd ibn al-Nu'man, held in Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 230}.

## The two executions
[[person/al-nadr-ibn-al-harith|Al-Nadr ibn al-Harith]] — bearer of the polytheists' standard in the battle — was executed at [[place/al-safra|al-Safra]] by Ali's hand {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 228}. [[person/uqba-ibn-abi-muayt|Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt]] was executed at Irq al-Zibya by the hand of Asim ibn Thabit, or it is said by Ali's {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 228} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud}.

## The Qur'anic comment
On the consultation over taking ransom came the rebuke "it is not for a prophet to have captives until he has made slaughter in the land" (al-Anfal 67–68); then the verse of Sura Muhammad 4 came ruling for release or ransom. The book relates from Ibn al-Jawzi Umar's remark that the verse agreed with him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 230-231} {cite:tarikh-umar-ibn-al-jawzi}.
