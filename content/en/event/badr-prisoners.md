## Summary
What followed [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]]: the consideration of the fate of about seventy captives from Quraysh. It covers the consultation between ransom and execution, then the ransom, the execution of two captives, and the Qur'anic comment on the decision {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.228-231}.

## Distribution and instruction
The Prophet distributed the captives among his companions and enjoined kindness towards them. The book relates that the Companions would give their captives bread and make do with dates themselves {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.229}.

## Consultation
The Prophet consulted Abu Bakr and 'Umar. Abu Bakr advised taking ransom, so that the Muslims would be strengthened, and perhaps God would guide the captives. 'Umar advised striking off their necks. The Prophet preferred Abu Bakr's view and took the ransom {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.230} {cite:tarikh-umar-ibn-al-jawzi}.

## Terms of ransom
The ransom ranged between a thousand and four thousand dirhams. Whoever had no ransom was released on condition that he teach ten of Medina's boys writing {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.230}. Al-Muttalib ibn Hantab, Sayfi ibn Abi Rifa'a and Abu 'Izza al-Jumahi were granted their freedom without ransom {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.230}. Abu al-'As ibn al-Rabi' was released in return for a ransom sent by Zaynab, a necklace that had been with Khadija, on condition that she be released; and she emigrated {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.230}. The Prophet rejected a suggestion to remove the two front teeth of [[person/suhayl-ibn-amr|Suhayl ibn Amr]], to avoid mutilation {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.230}. 'Amr ibn Abi Sufyan, a captive, was exchanged for Sa'd ibn al-Nu'man, who was held in Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.230}.

## Execution of two captives
[[person/al-nadr-ibn-al-harith|Al-Nadr ibn al-Harith]], the bearer of the idolaters' banner in the battle, was executed at [[place/al-safra|al-Safra]] by Ali's hand {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.228}. [[person/uqba-ibn-abi-muayt|Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt]] was executed at 'Irq al-Zabya by the hand of 'Asim ibn Thabit, and it is said by Ali's {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.228} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud}.

## The Qur'anic comment
A rebuke came down in the consultation over accepting ransom, concerning the taking of captives before the enemy had been thoroughly subdued (al-Anfal 67–68). Then came the verse of Muhammad (47:4), which decides between release by grace and release by ransom. The book relays from Ibn al-Jawzi that 'Umar said the verse agreed with him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp.230-231} {cite:tarikh-umar-ibn-al-jawzi}.

## Ransom and the rebuke (The Sealed Nectar, p. 230)
The rebuke in al-Anfal came for taking ransom before subduing the enemy. The ransom was between four thousand and one thousand dirhams, and whoever could not afford ransom taught ten boys of Medina to read ([[event/badr-aftermath|the aftermath of Badr]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.230}.
