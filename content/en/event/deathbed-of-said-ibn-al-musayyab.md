## Summary
[[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]] died at Medina in 94 AH, in the caliphate of al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik; the year was called the year of the jurists because so many of them died in it. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 143} The best-known reports of his last days are his instructions for his funeral and his words about the dinars he left; Ibn Sa'd gives them in his Tabaqat.

## In his illness
Ibn Sa'd reports from Abd al-Rahman ibn Harmala that he saw Sa'id in his illness praying lying on his back, nodding his head toward his chest and raising nothing to his head, and that Sa'id held that a sick person who cannot sit should pray by nodding. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 140} In another report Ibn Harmala entered when he was gravely ill, praying the noon prayer lying down and nodding, and heard him recite the sura of the sun. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 141}

## His instructions for his funeral
Ibn Sa'd relates that at a funeral, when a man said "ask forgiveness for her," Sa'id said: what does their chanter say? He forbade his family to have a chanter chant with him, or to say "Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab has died," or to walk with him carrying a censer; if he was good, what is with God is better than their perfume. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 141} From Yahya ibn Sa'id he relates Sa'id's words: "I charged my family, when death came to me, with three things: that no chanter and no fire follow me, and that they hurry me; if I have good with my Lord it is better than what you have." {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 141} From Abu Hazim he relates what Sa'id said in his last illness: when I die, do not pitch a tent over my grave, do not carry me on a red velvet cloth, do not follow me with fire, and do not announce me to anyone. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 141} Zur'a ibn Abd al-Rahman says he was present the day Sa'id died and heard him say: I call you to witness against my son Muhammad that he must announce me to no one; four men carrying me to my Lord are enough for me, and no wailing woman is to follow me saying of me what is not in me. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 142} Al-Dhahabi repeats some of these reports from Ibn Sa'd. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1829}

## The direction of prayer
Ibn Sa'd gives several versions in which Sa'id fell unconscious and his bed was turned toward the qibla; on reviving he asked who had ordered it, whether it was Nafi' ibn Jubayr, and said that if he was not on the qibla and the faith, turning his bed would not help him. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 142} In a report from Nafi' ibn Jubayr, Nafi' told Sa'id's son Muhammad to turn the bed toward the qibla and Sa'id said: do not; I was born on it, I will die on it, and I will be raised on it if God wills. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 142}

## What he left
Ibn Sa'd reports from Yahya ibn Sa'id that when death came to Sa'id he left some dinars and said: "O God, You know I left them only to protect my standing and my religion." {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 142} The report does not give the sum. A separate report says Sa'id had some thirty-odd thousand of his allowance in the public treasury and was invited to collect it but refused, saying he needed none of it until God judged between him and the house of Marwan. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 129}

## The grave
Ibn Sa'd quotes Abd al-Hakim ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Furwa: "I was present the day Sa'id died and saw his grave sprinkled with water." {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 143} The report names no cemetery and says nothing of who washed him or prayed over him.

## Where the reports differ
His age differs: in Ali ibn Zayd's account he was born four years after Umar became caliph and died at eighty-four {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 120}; in Talha ibn Muhammad's account from his father he was born two years before Umar's death and died at seventy-two {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 120}; in Abd al-Hakim's he died at seventy-five. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-khanji-2001 vol. 7 p. 143} The reports agree that he forbade the chanter, the fire and the announcing of his death, and differ in wording.
