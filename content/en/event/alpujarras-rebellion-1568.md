## Overview
The Rebellion of the Alpujarras was the rising of the Muslims who remained in the kingdom of [[place/granada|Granada]] after its fall. It broke out in the Alpujarras mountains of southern al-Andalus in December 1568 under a sultan they swore allegiance to and named Muhammad ibn Umayya, continued under Ibn Abbu, and ended with Ibn Abbu's killing in 1571 and the surrender of the last districts in 1573, as Tareq al-Suwaidan relates. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:17:30}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives three episodes (ten to twelve) of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]] to the revolt of the Alpujarras. He says that most Muslims do not know who the Moriscos were or what the revolt of the Alpujarras was, and that his aim is not only to tell the story but to draw out its lessons and meanings. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:01:00} He asks viewers to write down the lessons rather than the stories, and promises to sum up, at the end of the series, six main causes of victory and six main obstacles to it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:03:30}

### The plan and the spark (episode ten)
Al-Suwaidan relates that the Muslims who remained in al-Andalus drew up a secret plan, agreed on a ruler they called Sultan Muhammad ibn Umayya, and swore allegiance to him to restore Muslim rule in al-Andalus; they wrote to the Saadian state in Morocco and to the Ottomans in Algiers, set a date for the rising and divided their forces. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:02:00} He says a first attempt to proclaim the revolt failed because of betrayal and was postponed, and that the second was set for 24 December 1568. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:07:30}

He tells that on 23 December 1568 a party of guards of the Granada court set out for a village in the Alpujarras with fifty soldiers armed with muskets, and that Spanish soldiers passing through Andalusi Muslim villages habitually attacked the inhabitants with plunder, killing and rape. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:04:30} When they halted near Cádiar, the town of Muhammad ibn Umayya, and began to attack its people, a band of the "exiles", the men who had refused the peace and been driven out, fell on them, wiped them out and took their weapons; that was the spark that proclaimed the revolt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:05:00}

In al-Suwaidan's view this band broke a plan that had been distributed and timed, and so threw all of it into disorder; keeping to the plan came first, even though they were stirred by what had been done to their families. He counts patience and endurance, and careful planning kept to, among the causes of victory, and says that had the plan been carried out they might have taken Granada itself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:06:00}

He relates that the Spanish began arresting Muslims, so Sultan Muhammad ibn Umayya left Granada secretly on the night of 23 December for the Alpujarras. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:06:00} That same night a man who worked in the Alhambra told a priest that some Muslims had asked him for the measurements of the palace chains; the military command was told, and patrols and guards were increased. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:06:30} Then heavy snow fell on the mountains and cut the road to Granada, so the rebels who were to come from outside the city dressed as Ottomans, so that the Spanish would think it an Ottoman attack and lose heart, could not arrive; al-Suwaidan says sound planning keeps alternatives in reserve. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:07:30} Under the agreement the governor of Algiers, [[person/kilic-ali-pasha|Uluj Ali]], was to send forces and supplies to land near [[place/almeria|Almería]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:07:30}

He says the force that did arrive, led by Faraj ibn Faraj, numbered only 180 men, delayed because he had not reckoned with the snow; the people of the Albaicín inside Granada refused to join once they saw the plan had not gone ahead, so after desperate attempts Faraj went back to the Alpujarras, and all the fighters gathered there. Al-Suwaidan counts this as the revolt's first setback, caused by the zeal of a group of young men that aborted a rising whose conditions for success were in place. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:09:30}

### The second oath and the freeing of the Alpujarras
Al-Suwaidan says a revolt needs land to set out from, and that outside support helps it greatly. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:11:00} He relates that Muhammad ibn Umayya went to the valley of Lecrín (Wadi al-Iqlim), where his oath was renewed on 29 December 1568 as king of al-Andalus and head of the revolt; he named his uncle head of the army and Faraj ibn Faraj chief minister. Al-Suwaidan approves this, saying armed struggle alone is not enough and needs political work alongside it, like a "shadow government". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:12:00} He sent delegations to the districts near the Alpujarras, twelve of them, comparable to today's provinces, and all swore allegiance to him as sultan of the Muslims of al-Andalus. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:12:30}

He says that before 1569 began the fighters held all the Alpujarras and the valley around them and had driven out or destroyed the Castilian garrisons and the Christian missions, so that for the first time in decades the Muslims had land of their own to rule inside al-Andalus. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:13:00}

### The quarrel with Faraj ibn Faraj
Al-Suwaidan relates that from the start Faraj ibn Faraj carried out operations on his own, because he thought negotiation with the Spanish necessary, while Muhammad ibn Umayya refused to negotiate; Faraj insisted, withdrew from the leadership, gathered his supporters, and fought and negotiated at the same time without coordinating with the general command. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:14:00} The sultan issued a decision removing him and appointed his own uncle Ibn Jahwar chief minister in his place. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:14:30} Al-Suwaidan calls this a second setback that passed without a large split, and says Faraj went on fighting without office, which shows his sincerity, for "not everyone who negotiated was a traitor"; in his view Faraj's idea of combining struggle with demands was not wrong, his mistake was to split the rebels' ranks. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:15:30}

### Return to identity, and reprisals
Al-Suwaidan says Ibn Umayya did not stop at armed action: he called fighters and villagers back to their Islamic roots, so they began to drop the Christian names and titles forced on them and take back Muslim names, to keep the five prayers and revive the mosques. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:16:00} He relates that some rebels overreached and killed priests, judges and government officials, and he calls this a mistake, since in his view Muslims are commanded not to touch monks, women or non-combatants, those who took part in the Inquisition aside. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:16:30} He says this raised a wave of feeling in Spain that swelled the number of volunteers for the Spanish army, and that Muhammad ibn Umayya worked to stop the fighters from taking revenge and killing the innocent. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:17:00} Ibn Umayya wrote to the Muslim world for help, sending his brother Abdallah ibn Umayya to Algiers and Fernando al-Habaqi to Morocco. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:17:30}

### Massacres and the first battles (episode eleven)
Al-Suwaidan relates that the Spanish let soldiers and Christian settlers loose to take revenge on unarmed Muslims in Granada, Almería and elsewhere, with the killing of children, rape and displacement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:03:00} Then two Spanish armies, one from the east and one from the south, marched on the Alpujarras; the sultan was beaten in the first battle and had to withdraw. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:04:30}

He says that on the night of 18 January 1569 the Muslims found in one town were gathered in a prison inside a church, 2,400 children and women and 300 old men, about 2,700 people, and were all killed in a massacre that lasted until morning and opened a new record of massacres in this war. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:05:30} In his view the aim was to spread terror so that people would break and surrender, and he says any victory needs sacrifice, patience and steadfastness, for which a people must prepare itself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:06:30}

He relates that the Spanish commander thought the end of the revolt was near and pursued the sultan himself over the mountain tops; spies reported that Ibn Umayya was spending the night in a house in one of the towns, and the army attacked it, but Ibn Umayya was on his guard and slipped away. Al-Suwaidan points to the part betrayals played in such moments. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:07:30}

He says a Spanish army of five thousand men and three hundred horse, whose men Spanish historians described, as he reports, as thieves and killers concerned only with plunder, fought the fighters in its largest battle with them, and before this force the Muslims withdrew to Andarax. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:08:30} A Muslim unit under Ibn Maknun got away and settled near Almería; the other army turned on it for fear Almería would fall, and forced it to retreat despite its stubborn defence of the fort, in which women and children also fought; several thousand Muslims were killed, and about two thousand women and children were taken and sold as slaves. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:09:30} Al-Suwaidan thinks it was a mistake to meet a regular army head-on: whoever lacks a regular army's means fights a guerrilla war. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:09:00}

### The letter of Sultan Selim II
Al-Suwaidan says that by then only some Moroccan and Algerian volunteers and a little money and equipment from Algiers had reached the rebels. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:10:00} He then reads parts of a reply from the Ottoman sultan [[person/selim-ii-uthmani|Selim II]] to the people of al-Andalus, which he places in Mühimme register no. 14, dated 24 Shawwal 977 AH, which he equates with 20 January 1569. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:10:30} The letter, as he reads it, says the Spanish had taken the Muslims' weapons, forbidden them to speak Arabic and assaulted their women; that reports had reached the sultan that they had twenty thousand Muslim men and a hundred thousand able to bear arms; and that they had received weapons from Algiers and inflicted losses on the Spanish. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:11:30} The sultan then explains that the Christians of Cyprus, close to his lands, had broken their covenants and cut the roads of merchants, travellers and pilgrims. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:12:30} Al-Suwaidan concludes that the sultan had to choose between Cyprus and the people of al-Andalus, chose to settle Cyprus and put al-Andalus off, and that world conditions did not favour the revolt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:14:00} He pauses on a phrase in the letter that seeks help through the Prophet and the Companions, and says that in his view this is not permissible, since reliance is on God alone. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:13:30}

### The revolt spreads; the battle of Berja
Al-Suwaidan relates that the Spanish feared the small Muslim state in the Alpujarras and called on Europe, receiving troops and support from Italy, Germany, the Pope and France, while the Muslims' supplies came mainly from Algiers and a little from Morocco. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:15:00} He says the Muslims, though few, were united and set on their aim, planning and fighting, and that these are the factors of victory. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:15:30} He says Ibn Umayya kept the initiative by avoiding open battles, and the fighters spread their units around Almería, Guadix, the Vega of Granada, the Alpujarras and the Ronda district; within weeks they freed hundreds of forts, villages and towns across the old kingdom of Granada, though the main city did not fall to them, and their number rose to thirty thousand, five thousand of them volunteers from Algiers, Morocco and the Ottoman Turks. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:17:00}

He then relates that in 1569 Ibn Umayya marched in person on Berja at the head of several thousand fighters, about four hundred of them Moroccan volunteers, with an Ottoman as battle commander, and there the first great battle of this phase was fought; afterwards Ibn Umayya had to withdraw to Cádiar, having inflicted heavy losses on the Spanish army; both sides claimed victory, and 1,500 fighters were killed. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:18:00} Al-Suwaidan counts this as another strategic mistake, since keeping to guerrilla war would have been better. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:18:00}

He says that in July one of the leaders of the struggle died, Muhammad ibn Jahwar the Younger, uncle of Muhammad ibn Umayya, who he says began the revolt and proposed Ibn Umayya. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:18:30} Don Juan of Austria, the king's brother, was in Granada leading the Spanish military council, whose members disagreed because the revolt was, he says, bigger than they had reckoned. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep11 00:19:00}

### The killing of Ibn Umayya and the leadership of Ibn Abbu (episode twelve)
Al-Suwaidan says the Muslims changed their sultan's name from the Christian Fernando to Muhammad ibn Umayya, and that the revolt spread until it held nearly all of southern al-Andalus except the main cities. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:02:30} He relates that the Spanish called on Christians everywhere and pressed until a decision was issued to deport the Muslims, or those of Muslim origin, from Granada to the north. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:02:30} In the massacre of the Granada prison a hundred and fifty of the great leaders of al-Andalus held there were killed, while Ibn Umayya's father and brother were left alive to put pressure on him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:04:30}

Then a letter attributed to the sultan was found, addressed to one of his commanders, asking him to stop fighting because he wanted to negotiate with the Spanish for the release of his father and brother. Al-Suwaidan says the historians state it was forged, and that Ibn Umayya swore solemn oaths he had not sent it, but they did not believe him, imprisoned him, and he was then killed on this charge. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:06:00} In his view fear of betrayal and infiltration makes everyone suspect even his brother, one of the ills of revolts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:06:00}

The leaders of the revolt chose Ibn Abbu as sultan of al-Andalus, and he took the name Abdallah Muhammad ibn Abbu. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:06:30} Some commanders, Ibn Maknun among them, were dismayed at Ibn Umayya's killing, left the struggle and chose to emigrate to the Maghrib. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:06:30} He relates that Ibn Abbu reorganised the army and brought arms and ammunition from the ports of Morocco and Algiers until his army reached ten thousand fighters; he besieged the town of Órgiva and its castle at the end of October 1569 and freed it, and the Muslims rallied round him. Al-Suwaidan describes him as a sincere man who united the people; his rule reached Ronda and his raids on the Vega of Granada multiplied. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:07:30}

### Galera and the turning of the scales
Al-Suwaidan says that at the same time the Ottomans and Spain were fighting on the Tunisian and Algerian coasts, and that on 19 January 1570 the Ottoman governor-general of Algiers, Ali Pasha ([[person/kilic-ali-pasha|Uluj Ali]]), freed the city of Tunis from the Spanish. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:08:00} The king of Spain feared the Ottomans would help the revolt, so he raised three armies, the largest under his brother Don Juan of Austria; they took the initiative from the fighters, the Spanish tightened their hold on the sea until supplies dwindled, and the king resolved to settle the matter for fear that the Muslims still neutral in Valencia and Murcia would join. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:09:30}

He relates that Don Juan's army left Granada on 29 December 1569 with twelve thousand soldiers for the fortress of Galera, defended by about three thousand fighters, among them a unit of Ottoman volunteers, with Muslim women taking part in its defence. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:10:00} The army dug a trench round the town, used artillery and laid a long siege, losing hundreds dead and wounded, until resistance collapsed on 10 February 1570; the army entered the town, Don Juan ordered all captives killed, men, women and children, 1,400 of them were killed in his presence, and the town was laid waste. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:10:30} The army then turned east against a Muslim force of six thousand under al-Habaqi; in a fierce battle the fighters defeated Don Juan's army and nearly killed the king's brother himself, so the war swung back and forth. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:11:00}

### The end
Al-Suwaidan relates that the Spanish gathered their own forces and European forces to be rid of the revolt and imposed a complete blockade that cut off supplies; nothing came from the Muslim states except what was smuggled, and the Muslim forces began to shrink. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:13:00} At the request of some commanders, Ibn Abbu sent al-Habaqi to negotiate with the Spanish a compromise that would secure Islamic identity, the Arabic language and worship in return for peace; al-Habaqi conceded more than he had been empowered to, in matters touching the giving up of mosques, and Ibn Abbu and those with him rejected the agreement because they had not authorised it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:13:30} Al-Habaqi, angered, gathered his men and attacked his brothers; they overcame him, and he was tried and executed for treason. Al-Suwaidan calls his conduct reckless even if he was sincere. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:14:00}

He says that when the Spanish saw Ibn Abbu would not give up the struggle or concede except on his own terms, they launched heavy attacks before which the resistance collapsed, and the coasts of Morocco and Algeria filled with refugees fleeing with their faith and their lives. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:14:30} On 28 October 1570 King [[person/philip-ii-spain|Philip II]] announced a decision to banish all the Andalusis of the kingdom of Granada, converts included, to the various regions of Castile and León. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:15:00}

He relates that Ibn Abbu carried on the resistance in the highest and roughest mountains, fighting from caves with only four hundred fighters left, and the Spanish tempted one of his men, named al-Shanish, with pardon for himself and his family and the return of his captive wife and two daughters, on condition that he kill Ibn Abbu or bring him in alive. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:16:00} On 13 March 1571 al-Shanish and six followers attacked Ibn Abbu in the cave where he was hiding, and he fought until he was killed. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:16:30} Al-Suwaidan says the Spanish carried his body to Granada and brought it into the city in a great procession followed by columns of Andalusi captives, then carried out the death sentence on the dead man, cut off his head, tore his body apart and burned it in the largest square of Granada, and set the head in an iron cage above the city facing the Alpujarras, where it hung for thirty years. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:17:30} He says the revolt did not end completely until 1573, with the surrender of all the districts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:17:30}

### The lessons he draws
Al-Suwaidan contrasts the fate of the Muslims of al-Andalus, where he says hardly any Muslims are found today except immigrants or recent converts, with the survival of the Copts in Egypt and of Jews in Muslim lands, and puts what happened down to what he calls the Muslims' own main mistakes that obstructed victory. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep12 00:18:00} He links the revolt to armed resistance in Palestine today and says the purpose is to learn how revolts succeed and how they fail: the more they keep to the plan and to principles, the more they succeed; the more individual action, division and independent decisions there are, the more they collapse. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep10 00:17:30}
