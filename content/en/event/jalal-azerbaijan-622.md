## Summary
In 622 AH a band of Georgians set out from Tiflis for Azerbaijan; Muslim troops caught them in a mountain pass and cut them down. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 447}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that a company of Georgians marched from Tiflis making for Azerbaijan and the lands held by Uzbak, and camped behind a mountain pass negotiable only by a mounted horseman; they settled there feeling safe from the Muslims — holding them in contempt and trusting in the inaccessibility of the spot, believing there was no way to reach them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 447} A party of the Islamic armies mounted, made for the Georgians, reached that pass and crossed it at great risk; the Georgians were unaware until the Muslims were upon them, put them to the sword and killed them as they pleased, the survivors fleeing routed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 447} This was among the victories Ibn al-Athir records for the Muslims over the Georgians that year, before the greater set battle between them and Jalal al-Din in Sha'ban. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 447}
