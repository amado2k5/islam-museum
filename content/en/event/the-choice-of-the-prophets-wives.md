## Summary
The Prophet's wives once asked for an easier life amid the austerity of the Prophet's household; the verse of choice came down in Sura al-Ahzab — and they all chose God and His Messenger, not one of them inclining to the world {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 478}.

## The account
The Prophet lived an austerity no one could bear; Anas said he never saw a fine loaf until he met God, and A'isha said they would watch for three crescents across two months without a fire being lit in his houses — their living was the "two blacks", dates and water {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 478; sahih-al-bukhari}. Amid this austerity nothing requiring reproach issued from his wives except once, as human nature requires, so God sent down the verse of choice, [[passage/quran-33-28|al-Ahzab 28]] and [[passage/quran-33-29|al-Ahzab 29]]: "if you desire the life of this world and its adornment, come — I will provide for you and release you in a fair release; but if you desire God and His Messenger and the Abode of the Hereafter, God has prepared for the good-doers among you a great reward" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 478; tanzil-quran}. Of their honour and nobility, they all preferred God and His Messenger and not one inclined to choose the world {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 478}. Nothing of what passes between co-wives occurred among them despite their number but a small thing — God rebuked them at the opening of Sura al-Tahrim and they did not repeat it {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 478}.

## For young visitors
God offered the Mothers of the Believers the choice between the world's adornment and remaining with the Prophet in his austere life — and every one of them chose God and His Messenger.
