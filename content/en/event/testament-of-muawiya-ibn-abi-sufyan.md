## Summary
[[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]] died in [[place/damascus|Damascus]] in Rajab of the year 60, on which, as Ibn Kathir says, there is no disagreement. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} The histories record two bequests from his last days: counsel to his son [[person/yazid-ibn-muawiya|Yazid]], which al-Waqidi transmits, and an instruction to be shrouded in a garment the Prophet had given him and to have placed in his mouth, nose and eyes some hair and nail parings he had kept from the Prophet. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} This record completes the [[event/death-of-muawiya|record of Mu'awiya's death]].

## The dying hours and what he said
Ibn Kathir reports from Ibn Sa'd that as he lay dying Mu'awiya bequeathed half his wealth to be returned to the treasury, apparently to cleanse what remained for his heirs, since Umar ibn al-Khattab used to share with his governors. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 511} They also mention that cold troubled him late in life and he had a garment made from the crops of birds, then it grew heavy on him and he said that he had held this abode forty years, twenty as governor and twenty as caliph, and this was his state and his destination. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 511} Ibn Sirin related that he laid one cheek on the ground, then turned the other, weeping, and asked God to place him among those He chose to forgive, recalling the verse on God's forgiving what is less than associating others with Him, [[passage/quran-4-48|Qur'an 4:48]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 511} Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr related that when he grew heavy and people spoke of his death he had his eyes lined and his head oiled and sat propped up so that people greeted him standing and saw no sign of illness. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 511} Abu Amr ibn al-Ala said that when he was asked whether he would not leave a testament he answered: O God, excuse the stumble and pardon the slip. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3890}

## His counsel to Yazid
Al-Waqidi relates, through Marwan ibn Abi Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla, that Mu'awiya told Yazid as he counselled him on his deathbed that he had smoothed the way for him, and that if it proved good he would be the happier for it and otherwise unhappy; he urged him to be gentle with people, to overlook what hurt him in what he heard, not to be carried off by anger, and to be gentle with men of standing so that they saw no weakness in him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 600} He told him to consult the old and experienced and not to act on his own view, to be present at prayer, to know that the people of Medina and Mecca were his root and kin, to guard the standing of the people of Syria, who were his supporters and soldiers, to write to the provinces promising kindness, and not to listen to a slanderer. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 600} In another report he commended to Yazid his friend Abd Allah ibn Ja'far, and after Mu'awiya's death Yazid doubled his allowance. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 600} In a short version cited by al-Dhahabi he said: fear God, I have made the matter smooth for you, be gentle with people and beware of arrogance toward men of standing. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3890}

## The shroud, the hair and the nail parings
It is reported by more than one route that he charged that he be shrouded in the Prophet's garment that the Prophet had given him, which he had kept for this day, and that the hair and nail parings he held be placed in his mouth, nose, eyes and ears. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} In al-Dhahabi's reports he said: I used to help the Messenger of God perform ablution, and he took off his shirt and gave it to me, and I patched it and kept his nail parings in a vial; when I die put the shirt on my skin and grind the parings into my eyes. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 7 p. 316} In a report from Ibn Abbas he mentioned that he had called for an arrow-blade on al-Safa and taken some of the Prophet's hair, and told them to stuff his mouth and nostrils with it. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3890} In another he told his son that what he feared most was something he had done concerning him, and said he had gathered the Prophet's nail parings and some hair to fill his mouth and nose when he died. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3890}

## The prayer and burial
The reports differ on who prayed over him: Ibn Ishaq and al-Shafi'i say it was his son Yazid, while others say Yazid was away at Hawwarin and al-Dahhak ibn Qays prayed over him after the noon prayer in the mosque of Damascus. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} Al-Dahhak had addressed the people with Mu'awiya's shrouds in his hands and sent a courier to Yazid urging him to come. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} They differ on his grave: some say the governor's residence, the Green Palace, others the cemeteries of Bab al-Saghir, which is the view of most. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} Abu Mushir has it between Bab al-Jabiya and Bab al-Saghir. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3890} Ibn Kathir relates that when Yazid arrived he went in by Bab al-Saghir, dismounted at the grave and prayed over his father after the burial. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} Mu'awiya was seventy-eight, or according to the better-known report more than eighty. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512}

## Where the reports differ
The day of death in Rajab is given as the night of Thursday at mid-month, or eight days before the end, or four days in, or the first. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} The wording of the bequest of the relics also varies: in some he asks that his mouth and nose be filled with the hair, in some that the parings be ground into his eyes, in some that the shirt be put on his skin. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3890} Al-Dhahabi says in his biography that Mu'awiya was among the best of kings, whose justice outweighed his injustice, and that he wishes he had not named his son to succeed him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3890}

## Sources and documentation
The record draws on al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, Siyar A'lam al-Nubala and al-Dhahabi's Tarikh al-Islam. The volumes of al-Tabari for the year 60 were not among the pages read, so Mu'awiya's counsel to Yazid is given from al-Waqidi through Ibn Kathir's chain.
