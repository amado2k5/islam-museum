## Overview
The narrator writes that it had reached him about [[person/ibrahim-ibn-khalaf-ibn-tarif|Ibrahim ibn Khalaf]] that he entered a town called Jaysan, gave it up to plunder for the troops with him, and that many of its women were taken, some carried to Mecca and sold, and that he allowed his companions licence; he says the same could be told of each man of [[group/banu-tarif|Al Tarif]] but it would make the book long and the people of Yemen know more of them than he can set down. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.205} Of the [[group/al-jafatim-sanaa|Jafatim]] he reports hearing from some people of Sana'a that their men took what they pleased in the markets and seized the property of the people by force, none daring to speak to them. These are the allegations of an adherent of the imam against his opponents, reported at second hand, and the museum records them as such and gives no finding. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.205}

## Detail
The narrator goes on that when [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] saw how things had been, and that it would not help him with his Lord, he thought it over, and, helped by God in the narrator's telling, wrote to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] at Sa'da that he wished to free himself of the matter and hand it over to him because al-Hadi was more entitled to it than he; al-Hadi's march was the result. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.205} When al-Hadi reached [[place/hadaqan-sanaa-yemen|Hadaqan]], Abu al-Atahiya ordered all the Jafatim to go to a place called al-Sirr, and some of Al Tarif with them including [[person/abdullah-ibn-jarrah-of-al-tarif|Abdullah ibn Jarrah]], to lie in ambush for al-Hadi, telling them he was marching to fight him and that they must not leave their post until his decision reached them. They went on that understanding and believed what he said; the narrator's reading is that the man was managing and putting in order his own affair between himself and God. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.205} The name Jaysan is read as printed and not tied to a site.
