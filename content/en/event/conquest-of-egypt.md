## Overview
al-Tabari mentions the conquest of Egypt in the events of 20 AH, with disagreement over its date: Ibn Ishaq gives 20 AH and Sayf gives 16 AH. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.4 p.102} {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.4 p.104} In the index of the volume the heading "the account of the conquest of Egypt and Alexandria" appears. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.4 p.578}

## The march in al-Baladhuri
[[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] was besieging Caesarea. When people withdrew from Yarmuk, he left his son in charge there and went to Egypt with three thousand five hundred men; it is also said that Umar had written to him to set out. He halted at [[place/al-arish|al-Arish]], then at [[place/al-farama|al-Farama]], where he fought its people, and then camped at the Gardens of Rayhan. The Muslims named the city [[place/al-fustat|Fustat]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Zubayr and the fort
[[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] joined Amr with ten thousand men, and it is said twelve, among them [[person/kharija-ibn-hudhafa-al-adawi|Kharija ibn Hudhafa]] and [[person/umayr-ibn-wahb|Umayr ibn Wahb]]. al-Zubayr came with a ladder, climbed it and cried "Allahu akbar", and the Muslims followed him and took the fort by force. Umar confirmed its people as dhimmis. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## By force or by treaty
The manner of the conquest is disputed. [[person/abdullah-ibn-amr-ibn-al-as|Abd Allah ibn Amr]] reported that his father took it by force and then its king made peace with him, on condition that each adult male pay a dinar, except the poor, with conditions about food and clothing. In another chain, "We conquered Egypt without a treaty." Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz wrote that it was taken by force without a treaty. Urwa ibn al-Zubayr said that Amr took it by treaty and agreement, and Uqba ibn Amir al-Juhani testified that its people had a covenant and a pact. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
[[person/ibn-abd-al-hakam|Ibn Abd al-Hakam]] reports that Umar came to al-Jabiya in the year 18, and [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr]] took him aside to ask leave to march on Egypt, calling it the richest of lands and the least able to fight; Umar feared for the Muslims but then gave leave. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 80} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 81} Amr marched with four thousand men, all of Akk, or, some say, three thousand five hundred; a report from Yazid ibn Abi Habib adds that a third of them were of Ghafiq. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 81}

Umar wrote to him to turn back if the letter reached him before he entered Egypt. Amr did not open it until he camped at a village between Rafah and al-Arish, asked about it, was told it belonged to Egypt, and then read it and went on. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 81} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 82} Ibn Abd al-Hakam gives other versions too: that Amr set out from Palestine without leave, and that Uthman ibn Affan warned Umar of Amr's rashness and love of command, so that Umar regretted his permission. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 82} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 83}

The first place fought over was [[place/al-farama|al-Farama]], where the Romans fought hard for about a month; then Amr went on to al-Qawasir, then [[place/bilbays|Bilbays]], where they fought him for about a month, then [[place/umm-dunayn|Umm Dunayn]], after which he wrote to Umar for reinforcements and settled before [[event/siege-of-babylon-fortress-640|the fortress of Babylon]]. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 85} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 86} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 87} [[person/abdullah-ibn-sad-ibn-abi-sarh|Abd Allah ibn Sa'd]] held Amr's right wing from his departure from Caesarea until the end of the campaign. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 85} Then came [[event/treaty-of-babylon|the peace]], and the battles of [[event/battle-of-kom-sharik|Kom Sharik]] and [[event/battle-of-al-kiryawn|al-Kiryawn]] on the road to Alexandria. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 103} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 107} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 109}
