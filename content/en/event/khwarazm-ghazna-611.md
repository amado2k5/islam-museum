## Summary
In 611 AH, in Sha'ban, Ala al-Din Muhammad Khwarazmshah took the city of Ghazna and its dependencies after its amir Qutlugh Tekin surrendered it to him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 366}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that once Khwarazmshah had taken most of Khurasan and Bamiyan, he wrote to Taj al-Din, lord of Ghazna, asking him to pronounce the khutba and strike coinage in his name and send him one elephant in exchange for peace and keeping Ghazna; Taj al-Din consulted his amirs, and the chief of them, Qutlugh Tekin — a mamluk of Shihab al-Din al-Ghuri and the real authority in al-Diz's state — advised compliance, so the khutba and sikka were made for Khwarazmshah. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 366} Then Qutlugh Tekin invited Khwarazmshah to come and receive Ghazna; he marched quickly, the city and citadel were surrendered, and on entering he killed the Ghurid troops there, especially the Turks, so al-Diz fled to Lahore. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 366} Khwarazmshah then summoned Qutlugh Tekin and asked about his standing with al-Diz to establish a charge against him; when Qutlugh explained that he himself had governed Ghazna, Khwarazmshah said: if you do not honor your companion and benefactor, how would you treat me or a son of mine left with you? He seized him, took immense wealth carried on thirty pack-animals, rounded up four hundred mamluks, then killed him, leaving his own son Jalal al-Din at Ghazna with troops; some say Ghazna was taken in 613. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 366}
