## Overview
After the battle [[person/abu-al-dughaysh-al-shihabi|Abu al-Dughaysh]] withdrew from his village and sent to [[person/yusuf-ibn-muhammad-al-hasani|Yusuf ibn Muhammad al-Hasani]] asking to meet him and obtain security; Yusuf obtained it and took his heavy oaths to be faithful and enter the truth, then brought him to [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]], who confirmed Yusuf's security, had him swear heavy oaths to hear and obey and enter the truth, and let him go home. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.88} Abdullah ibn al-Husayn stayed at [[place/wasaha-yemen|Wasaha]] until Tuesday with two days left of Rabi' II, setting the people's affairs right. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.88} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.89} This is Zaydi narration continuing from the previous pages.

## The return to Sa'da
Then a letter from [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] ordering him back reached him; he went back on Tuesday to [[place/saada|Sa'da]], arriving on Thursday, the first day of Jumada I, and told the narrator to stay behind at Wasaha. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.89}

## The fallen mosque
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] relates that Abu al-Dughaysh gathered a large force and came by night close to Wasaha meaning to attack at dawn, and spent the night near them without their knowing; some of his men spent the night in a mosque outside the village, a night of much wind and rain, "and part of the mosque fell on some of Abu al-Dughaysh's men, and God killed several of them and broke up their band, and they turned back at once disappointed". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.89} They did not learn of it until morning, when they went to the mosque and found it had fallen on the men, took them out and they were buried. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.89} These are the narrator's words in explaining the event, which the museum gives as his without asserting its cause. Al-Hadi stayed at Sa'da for the rest of Rabi' II and ten days of Jumada I. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.89}
