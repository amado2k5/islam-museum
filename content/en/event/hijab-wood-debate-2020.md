## Summary
The long 2020 debate between Muhammad Hijab and David Wood on the reliability of the Qur'an and the Gospels. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}

## The line of the debate
Mutual historical criticism of transmission, manuscripts and narrative, in an open-length digital format.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Hijab**: the Qur'an is historically reliable by continuous communal transmission — his pivot question: what chain of transmission would satisfy the critic? — and he attacked the instability of the Gospel text among its earliest witnesses.
- **Wood**: defended the Gospels' historical reliability — manuscript earliness and patristic testimony — and argued the Qur'anic text itself is open to compositional and transmission criticism.
- **Hijab (rebuttal)**: worked the limits of the historical method: no external observer can attest revelation itself, so the question is acceptance of transmission; he put the practical-memorisation argument for the Qur'an.
- **Wood (rebuttal)**: met method with method — applied historical criteria to the later Qur'anic materials and argued his accounts' coherence.
- **The verdict**: the digital generation's long-form debate — dissected clip by clip online, it became the reference for the historical-textual argument on the internet. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}
