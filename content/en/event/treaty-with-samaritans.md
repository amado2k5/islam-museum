## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates that [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] made peace with the [[group/samaritans|Samaritans]] in Jordan and Palestine, on condition that they be informants and guides for the Muslims, in return for the [[concept/jizya|poll tax]] per head, with their land exempted. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.215-216}

## How their affair developed
The Muslims raided them once and took captives, until they returned to what they had been, and they were left alone; their affair ran until the author's time on their old peace. In the days of Yazid ibn Mu'awiya a land tax was imposed on their land and five dinars on each man's head. Al-Baladhuri describes the Samaritans as Jews of two kinds, the Dustan and the Kushan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.215-216}

At the beginning of al-Rashid's caliphate a devastating plague struck Palestine, and their land was ruined; the sultan appointed someone to cultivate it, and it became estates of the caliphate. In the year 246 some of the land tax was lifted from them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.215-216}

The date is approximate, around 15 AH, and the conversion is derived. Among the concepts of this peace is [[concept/dhimma|protection]].
