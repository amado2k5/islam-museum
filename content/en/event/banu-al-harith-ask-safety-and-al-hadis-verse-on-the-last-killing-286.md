## Overview
[[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] sent [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] men from among his partisans to ask safety for them, and he gave it, and they entered the village and traded. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.187} [[person/abdullah-ibn-bustam|Ibn Bustam]] had fled to the land of [[group/shakir-hamdan-tribe|Shakir]] and stayed there; some of al-Hadi's partisans asked safety for him but al-Hadi disliked it of them, and Ibn Bustam stayed in Shakir's land for a time, abased and humbled in the narrator's words, and wrote to [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] asking him to write to al-Hadi to give him safety. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.187}

## Detail
Al-Hadi composed verse on the last killing. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.187} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.188} In it he says that he rose for God's right, striking and thrusting, that his justice showed in all the towns, that he forgave the one who erred and made his excuse plain so that his pardon corrupted them, that they resented nothing in him but that he called them to what was sent down in the surahs, that God's light is shining and His command plain, that of the people who thought otherwise some had their skulls split in the village of al-Hajar and some flee in humiliation, and he tells one who did not attend that his reward is due for his readiness and his heart's gladness, and ends with good news of God's help. The verse also reproaches a man who showed no thanks for kindness done him, whom the museum does not name. These are the imam's own lines, with his judgement of his opponents; their sense is given and no line quoted. It is set at [[place/al-hajar-najran|al-Hajar]].
