## Overview
Al-Suyuti relates that the caliph [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] received ten hadith transmitters, among them Faraj ibn Fadala and Ghiyath ibn Ibrahim. Al-Mahdi was fond of pigeons. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.445}

Ghiyath entered and related to him from Abu Hurayra, traced to the Prophet: "There is no racing except on hoof or blade", and he added "or wing". Al-Mahdi ordered ten thousand dirhams given to him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.445}

When he rose, al-Mahdi said: "I testify that your nape is the nape of a liar, and you only earned this." Then he ordered the pigeons slaughtered. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.445}

The judgement on the addition here is al-Mahdi's, as al-Suyuti relates it. The date is approximate and derived.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] tells the story while speaking of forgers who sought favour with rulers: the caliph [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] loved pigeons, and Ghiyath ibn Ibrahim came in while he was playing with them, and was told to narrate something to the Commander of the Faithful. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:14:00} He gave a chain for a hadith that there is no prize except in arrow-head, hoof or camel's foot, and added "or wing". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:14:30} Al-Heweny says the authentic hadith has three only, the arrow-head for archery, the camel's foot for camels and the hoof for horses, all used in training for jihad, and that when the liar added a fourth he dropped the word "three" from its opening so as not to be found out. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:16:00}

He relates that al-Mahdi gave him ten thousand dirhams, and when he turned to leave said he bore witness that this was the nape of a liar against the Messenger of God, then ordered the pigeons slaughtered. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:16:30} Al-Heweny comments that he is surprised at al-Mahdi, since the liar deserved punishment more than the innocent pigeons, and he went off with the ten thousand dirhams; this is his opinion. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:17:00} He says al-Mahdi never admitted him again, and that rulers then honoured the Book and the Sunna, so hadith was "the marketable commodity". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:17:30} The ten thousand dirhams and the slaughter of the pigeons are also in al-Suyuti's account, as given above on this page. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.445}
