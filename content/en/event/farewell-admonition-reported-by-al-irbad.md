## Summary
[[person/al-irbad-ibn-sariya|Al-Irbad ibn Sariya]] was a Companion who reported that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] gave them an admonition so moving that they took it for the admonition of someone saying farewell, and asked him for a counsel. The report is among the best known of its kind and is the twenty-eighth hadith in the Jami' al-Ulum wa-l-Hikam of [[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]]: it commands fear of God and hearing and obeying, warns of disagreement after him, and urges holding to his sunna and the sunna of the rightly guided successors. This page sets out the report, its narrators and Ibn Rajab's commentary as they appear in his book. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 581}

## The text
Al-Irbad says the Prophet gave them an admonition at which hearts trembled and eyes streamed, so they said: it seems the admonition of one saying farewell, so counsel us. He said: "I counsel you to fear God, to hear and obey, even if a slave is set over you", and told them that whoever of them lived after him would see much disagreement, so "hold to my sunna and the sunna of the rightly guided successors; bite on it with your molars; beware of newly invented matters, for every innovation is a misguidance". {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 581} Ibn Rajab says that Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi transmitted it and that al-Tirmidhi called it hasan sahih; the editor's note gives its place as no. 4607 in Abu Dawud's Sunan and no. 2676 in al-Tirmidhi's Jami'. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 581}

## Chain and the narrators' differences
Ahmad, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah transmit it through Thawr ibn Yazid from Khalid ibn Ma'dan from Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr al-Sulami; Ahmad in one version, and Abu Dawud, add Hujr ibn Hujr al-Kala'i, both from al-Irbad. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 581} Abu Nu'aym calls it a good hadith from the sound hadith of the Syrians, and al-Hakim claimed that al-Bukhari and Muslim left it out because they thought Thawr alone related it from Khalid; Ibn Rajab answers that it is not on their conditions, since neither took anything from Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr al-Sulami or from Hujr al-Kala'i. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 581} He adds that the chain varies on Khalid ibn Ma'dan, and that the version of Ahmad and Ibn Majah through Damra ibn Habib from Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr al-Sulami has an added sentence, "I have left you on the white path, its night like its day; none strays from it after me but one who is lost", and another at the end, "the believer is like a docile camel, led wherever it is led"; a group of the hadith masters rejected that last addition as interpolated, not part of the hadith. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 582} He also says that Ibn Majah's report through Yahya ibn Abi al-Mutta', which states that he heard from al-Irbad, was rejected by the hadith masters of Syria, who said he did not hear from him and the report is a mistake. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 582}

## When was the admonition given?
Ibn Rajab notes that in the versions of Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi it was after the dawn prayer, and that the Prophet often admonished his Companions outside the regular sermons but did not do so continually, rather from time to time. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 583} He reads the Companions' words "the admonition of one saying farewell" as showing that he went further in it than in others. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 586} He then says that in that sermon he may have hinted at farewell, as in the Farewell Pilgrimage sermon, and mentions the Ghadir Khumm address in Muslim, the prayer on the martyrs of Uhud followed by the ascent of the minbar like one taking leave, and a report from Abdallah ibn Amr whose chain the editor weakens; he concludes: "Perhaps the sermon that al-Irbad refers to was one of these sermons, or like them." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 587} Ibn Rajab therefore does not place this admonition among the days of the Prophet's life; he only judges it close in meaning to the sermons of farewell. See [[event/farewell-sermon-at-arafat|The Farewell Sermon at Arafat]].

## Ibn Rajab's commentary
**Fear of God, hearing and obeying.** Ibn Rajab says these two words gather the happiness of this world and the next; fear of God is God's own counsel to the first and last, and by hearing and obeying those in authority people's affairs are ordered. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 587} He gives al-Hasan's saying that rulers handle five matters: the Friday prayer, the congregation, the festival, the frontiers and the penalties. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 588} He notes that the Prophet counselled these two things also in the Farewell Pilgrimage sermon, in the reports of Umm al-Husayn and Abu Umama in Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 588}{cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 589} On the Abyssinian slave he says reports on this are many, citing Anas in al-Bukhari and Abu Dharr in Muslim, and then mentions the view that the Abyssinian slave is given as an example. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 589}{cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 590}

**Disagreement and the sunna.** He says the words "he will see much disagreement" announce what happened in the community, the many disagreements over the principles and the branches of religion, and he sets beside it the hadith of the community's division into seventy-odd groups. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 590}{cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 591} For him the sunna is the path followed, and so covers what the Prophet and his successors held in belief, deed and word; the early generations used the word sunna only for all of that, and he reports a similar sense from al-Hasan, al-Awza'i and al-Fudayl ibn Iyad, while many later scholars limit the word to beliefs. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 591} He reads the placing of this saying after the command to hear and obey as a sign that there is no obedience to those in authority except in obeying God, and cites "obedience is only in what is right", and Mu'adh's question about rulers who do not follow his sunna and the answer, "There is no obedience to one who does not obey God." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 591}

**The rightly guided successors.** He says that the command to follow the sunna of the rightly guided successors after the command to hear and obey is evidence that their sunna is to be followed as his sunna is, unlike other rulers; he names Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali, relying on the hadith of Safina that the succession after him lasts thirty years and on Ahmad's use of it as an argument. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 591}{cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 592} He adds that many imams have stated that [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] was also a rightly guided caliph. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 592} On the agreement of the four he says scholars differ on whether it is consensus or an argument when other Companions disagree, and that Ahmad has two reports about it. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 593} See [[concept/sunna|Sunna]], [[concept/khilafa-rashida|The rightly guided caliphate]] and [[concept/bidah|Innovation]].

## Where the reports differ
- Al-Tirmidhi says the hadith is hasan sahih and Abu Nu'aym calls it good, but some additions are disputed, among them "the believer is like a docile camel", which a group of hadith masters rejected. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 582}
- This page gives no date for the admonition, since Ibn Rajab names no day for it. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 587}
- What Ibn Rajab says about the rightly guided successors, about Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and about the authority of the four's agreement are the statements of scholars, attributed to them, and not a judgement of the museum.
