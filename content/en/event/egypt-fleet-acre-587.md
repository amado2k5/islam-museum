## Summary
During the Frankish siege of Acre the Muslims burned the besiegers' great wooden siege towers that had brought the city to the brink of falling, and fighting took place between the fleets at sea. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 191}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that during their stay before Acre the Franks built three very tall wooden towers, each five storeys filled with fighters, covered with hides, vinegar, clay and substances that prevented fire, and pushed them against the city from three sides until they overlooked the wall, fought the defenders and began filling in the moat, so the city was on the point of being taken by force. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 191} The people of Acre sent a swimmer through the sea to tell Salah al-Din of their plight; he rode out with his armies and fought the Franks on every side in a great continuous battle for eight days, while the Muslims had despaired because nothing could bring the towers down. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 192} The cause of their burning was a man of Damascus devoted to collecting fire-launching devices and drugs that strengthen flame, who was inside Acre; he came to the amir Qaraqush, the city's commander, and had the mangonel crews throw his pots — first unfired ones until they lodged in the tower, then a pot full of fire which set it ablaze, followed by more until flames spread through it and it burned with all its five storeys' men. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 192}
