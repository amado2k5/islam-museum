## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad says: on the morning after [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s arrival at [[place/asal-khawlan|Asal]] my father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] was ordered to call out the [[group/khawlan-tribe|Khawlan]], and a large force gathered to him, and we marched in it until we met al-Hadi at Asal and found he had pitched his tents. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.128} The account is Zaydi, in the imam's followers' voice; its heading is "the account of al-Hadi's meeting with al-Da'am at Madhab and his reconciling Khawlan and Banu Salman"; the "sign" in it is the narrator's saying, and the manuscript margin heads it "the honour of the shade of the cloud". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.129}

## Consulting the guardians of the slain
We greeted him, were told to dismount, and Khawlan were told to dismount, and they stayed that day. At night he sent for the guardians of the slain, one of whom was al-Husayn ibn Abi al-Abbas, whose father was among the great chiefs of Khawlan, and the other a man called Ali ibn Sayf of Yarsam, whose clan were among the notables. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.128} Al-Hadi said to the Futaymis and the Yarsamis: "What do you say of this matter? We have promised al-Da'am to meet him with Banu Salman, so advise what we should meet him with." They all said: "Son of the Messenger of God, our blood and our wealth are yours; God has put them in your hands and given them to you." He thanked them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.128}

## Going out to Madhab
In the morning he went to meet al-Da'am, and ordered my father Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah to halt Khawlan at Naqil al-Ajala, which overlooks the place of meeting, fearing that Khawlan and Hamdan would meet and strife break out between them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.128} He took a group of the notables of Khawlan with the guardians of the slain and went to meet al-Da'am on a hot day of much simoom, until he met him at a place called [[place/madhab-yemen|Madhab]], a stretch of open land with no shade and no trees. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.128}

## The cloud
Ali ibn Muhammad says: that day my father and I saw one of the signs of God, which He gave to the son of His prophet; we saw a cloud God had brought into being as He wished until it came to rest over the head of al-Hadi ila al-Haqq and all his companions and shaded them on that fiercely hot day; by God it never ceased to shade him until he went back, and the sky was clear with no other cloud in it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.128} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.129} He says the people marvelled at what they saw of the great signs, proofs and wonders with which God marks out whom He wills of His believing servants. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.129} This is one narrator's own eyewitness report; the museum carries it as told, neither affirming nor denying it.

## The peace and the departure
Yahya ibn al-Husayn made peace between the Khawlanis and the Hamdanis, and went on to [[place/saada|Sa'da]], and al-Da'am sent his son [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-daam|Muhammad]] with him with twenty-six horsemen, while al-Da'am went back to his own land. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.129} The manuscript margin reads "his making peace between the Khawlanis and the Hamdanis in the killing". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.129}

## Notes
The text does not state what the guardians accepted for the blood, or the terms of the peace, beyond their putting the matter in his hands. The date is no finer than Jumada II.
