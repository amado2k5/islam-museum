## Summary
Al-Diz took the city of Lahore after fleeing Ghazna and defeating its lord Qabaja, then marched toward Delhi, was defeated at Samana and killed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 367}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that when al-Diz fled Ghazna to Lahore, its lord Nasir al-Din Qabaja — also a mamluk of Shihab al-Din al-Ghuri — met him with about fifteen thousand horsemen while only about fifteen hundred remained with al-Diz; in the battle al-Diz's wings were routed and his elephants taken, leaving him two, whose drivers ordered them to seize Qabaja's standard and kettledrum, which they did; al-Diz bared his head and cried in Persian words meaning "either kingship or death," whereupon Qabaja and his army fled and al-Diz took Lahore. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 367} He then marched into India to take Delhi and the other lands held by the Muslims; their lord Shams al-Din Iltutmish, a mamluk of Qutb al-Din Aybak, marched against him with all his forces, met him at Samana, and al-Diz was defeated, captured and killed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 367} Ibn al-Athir describes al-Diz as a ruler of praiseworthy conduct, much given to justice and kindness toward his subjects, especially merchants and strangers. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 367}
