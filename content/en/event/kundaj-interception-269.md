## Overview
In 269 AH the caliph [[person/al-mutamid|al-Mu'tamid]] became greatly afraid of his brother [[person/abu-ahmad-al-muwaffaq|al-Muwaffaq]], and wrote to his deputy in Egypt, [[person/ahmad-ibn-tulun|Ahmad ibn Tulun]]. Ibn Tulun set out and came to Damascus. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 566-567}

## The interception
Al-Mu'tamid left Samarra for leisure and headed for Damascus. Al-Muwaffaq wrote to [[person/ishaq-ibn-kundaj|Ishaq ibn Kundaj]] to turn him back. Ishaq met him between Mosul and al-Hadītha and brought him back to Samarra. He was lodged in the house of Ahmad ibn al-Khasib and was barred from the palace of the caliphate, and five hundred men were assigned to guard him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 566-567}

## Afterwards
Al-Muwaffaq sent Ishaq a robe of honour and money, and gave him the title "Lord of the Two Swords", and gave Sa'id ibn Makhlad the title "Lord of the Two Vizierates". Al-Mu'tamid was left without authority to loose or bind. Al-Suyuti says that he was the first caliph to be subdued, restrained and put under guard. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 566-567}
