## Overview
"Al-Hadi advanced until he halted at Tartar, and the Hakami's envoys met him there with sheep, cattle, food and fodder that he had ordered as hospitality for al-Hadi" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.296}.

## Detail
The Hakami "had taken from the people of the land"; when al-Hadi heard this "he sent to the people of the village and returned to them what had been taken", and told them, as the narrator gives it, that it was not lawful for them to take this, that they would not do it and did not allow it; he sent the Hakami's envoys back to him and they told him what al-Hadi had done, "and he marvelled at it and the people of the land marvelled, for they did not know the right nor al-Hadi's way", so they desired his nearness, loved him and came to him from every side, and letters passed between al-Hadi and the Hakami {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.296}. The marvel, desire and love are the Zaydi narrator's report and the museum does not judge it. The page follows [[event/al-hadi-goes-out-to-tihama-and-camps-at-al-sharis-and-al-uyayn-292|al-Hadi's going out to Tihama]] and is followed by [[event/the-hakami-regrets-and-camps-near-al-hadi-with-three-thousand-292|the Hakami's regret and his camp near al-Hadi]]. The book does not locate Tartar or the Hakami's district; the Tartar of Azerbaijan on the graph is a different place with no established link. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
