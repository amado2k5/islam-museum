## Summary
The caliph [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] died in [[place/damascus|Damascus]] in mid-Shawwal of the year 86; his son and heir [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid]] prayed over him and he was buried at the small Bab al-Jabiya. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 79} Al-Dhahabi relates his counsel to his sons and his words to his heir. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 143} This record completes the [[event/death-of-abd-al-malik|record of his death]].

## His words in his last illness
Ibn Kathir relates that as he lay dying he ordered the gates of his palace opened, and heard a launderer washing clothes in the valley and asked about him; told he was a launderer, he said: would that I were a launderer living by the work of my hands and had never held the caliphate. When Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab heard this he said: praise be to God who makes them, when they die, flee to us and not we to them. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 79} Abu Mushir said he was asked in his last illness how he found himself and answered that he found himself as God had said, and recited a verse saying that people come to their Lord one by one as they were created, [[passage/quran-6-94|Qur'an 6:94]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 79} It is related that he said: lift me, and they lifted him until he smelled the air, and he said: O world, how sweet you are; your long is short and your much is small. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 79} According to al-Dhahabi, when al-Walid came in and wept, his father said: what is this, are you moaning like a slave girl? {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 143}

## His counsel to al-Walid
Al-Dhahabi relates that when Abd al-Malik saw al-Walid weeping he told him: when I die, gird yourself, wrap your waist-cloth, put on a leopard's skin and set your sword on your shoulder; whoever shows what is in his mind, strike his neck, and whoever is silent will die of his own sickness. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 143} After the death of his brother Abd al-Aziz, Abd al-Malik had sworn the oath of succession for his two sons, al-Walid and then Sulayman. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 135}

## His counsel to his sons
Ibn Kathir reports from some that as death came he called his sons and counselled them. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 79} Al-Dhahabi relates that he enjoined on them fear of God and forbade division and dispute, saying: look to Maslama and take your lead from his opinion, for he is your shield and your fang; be dutiful sons of one mother, be free men in war and beacons of kindness, for war does not bring death before its time, while kindness leaves its reward and its remembrance; be sweet in bitterness and gentle in hardship. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 143} In the report of al-Mada'ini, when he knew he would die he called his freedman Abu Ilaqa and said: by God, I wish that from the day I was born until today I had been a porter. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 143}

## What he left, and his daughter
In the same report he had only one daughter, Fatima, to whom he had given the earrings of Maria and the unique pearl, and he said: O God, I leave nothing that matters more to me than her, so guard her; Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz married her. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 143} Ibn Kathir relates that he left nineteen children, male and female, and that his caliphate lasted twenty-one years, nine of them shared with Ibn al-Zubayr and thirteen years, three months and a half alone. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 80} Ibn Kathir names among his children al-Walid, Sulayman, Marwan, A'isha, Yazid, Hisham, Abu Bakr, Fatima, Abd Allah, Maslama, al-Mundhir, Anbasa, Muhammad, Sa'd al-Khayr and al-Hajjaj. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 80}

## Prayer and burial
Ibn Kathir relates that he died in Damascus on a Friday, or Wednesday, or Thursday, in mid-Shawwal of the year 86; his son al-Walid, who was to succeed him, prayed over him, and he was buried at the small Bab al-Jabiya. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 79} He gives his age as sixty according to Abu Ma'shar, which al-Waqidi held sound, sixty-three according to al-Mada'ini, and some say fifty-eight. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 80}

## Where the reports differ
Ibn Kathir notes that Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan recited at his own death the same verses that are put in Abd al-Malik's mouth, in which the speaker wishes he had never held kingship. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 79} Al-Dhahabi gives the report with the hedge 'it is said' that al-Walid, when he came in, recited some lines. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 9 p. 143} The day of death differs, and so does the number of his daughters: Ibn Kathir names Fatima and A'isha, while al-Dhahabi says he had only Fatima. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 80}

## Sources and documentation
The record rests on Ibn Kathir and al-Dhahabi. The volumes of al-Tabari for the year 86 were not among the pages read.
