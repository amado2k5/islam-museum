## Overview
Al-Suyuti says that in 583 AH [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din]] took many of the Syrian lands held by the Franks, the greatest of them [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]]. Its stay in Frankish hands had lasted ninety-one years. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.692-693}

## After the recovery
The sultan removed the monuments the Franks had made and tore down the churches they had built, and in place of one church built a madrasa for the Shafi'is. He did not demolish the Holy Sepulchre, following the example of Umar, who did not demolish it when he took Jerusalem. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.692-693}

Al-Suyuti relates that Ibn Barjan said in his commentary on the surah of al-Rum that Jerusalem would remain in the hands of the Romans until 583 AH, and then would become a Muslim house. Abu Shama describes this as a strange claim, and says that Ibn Barjan had died long before. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.692-693}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The speaker's view (opinion): the Palestine question is an Islamic question, the land of Palestine is an Islamic endowment, and Umar ibn al-Khattab endowed Jerusalem in particular (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:12:00}.

The speaker's view (opinion): the state of Palestine is shameful, and he names two moments in Jerusalem's history: the Islamic conquest, and the time of the Crusaders {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:03:30}.

He says al-Aqsa remained under Crusader occupation for ninety-two years (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:09:30}, and says elsewhere that Jerusalem was held by wrongdoers for nearly a century before it was returned (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:22:00}.

He describes the judge's sermon on the day Jerusalem returned after ninety-two years of occupation (needs_source). The judge began with the Opening chapter and recited it to the end {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:14:00}, then prayed for the just king Salah al-Din {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:13:00}.

He recounts that the judge prayed for Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali, and said of Umar that he was the first to remove the Crusader rites from the House (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:20:30}.

He recounts the judge's prayer for Salah al-Din: that God cleanse al-Aqsa of the polytheists, and that his command and prohibition take effect in the East and the West {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:44:00}.

He recounts that the judge said the army had been honoured to free al-Aqsa, and that no one can harm the one whom God has helped to victory {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:32:00}. The speaker's view (opinion): the victory was God's pleasure, not the Muslims' doing alone {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:21:30}.

He says the Companions taught that Jerusalem would be occupied twice, and that the present tense in the verse signifies continuity (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:25:30}. He reads the first of the two warnings as Umar's entry into Jerusalem without battle {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:23:00}.

The speaker's view (opinion): al-Aqsa returns only through pure monotheism {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 01:08:00}.

He recounts that the Companions asked Bilal to call the adhan at al-Aqsa, and that when he did, he moved everyone present to tears {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:19:30}.
