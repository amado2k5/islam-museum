## Summary
As the Prophet's call grew stronger and the pilgrimage season drew near, Quraysh gathered around [[person/al-walid-ibn-al-mughira|al-Walid ibn al-Mughira]] to agree on one thing to say to the Arabs about the Prophet. He told them to settle on a single opinion about him, and they concluded that he was a sorcerer. They sat along the people's roads warning them against him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.81} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.82} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/271}.

## The council
The book says that Quraysh feared the Arabs arriving for the season would be affected by the spread of his call, so they gathered at al-Walid's. They suggested calling him a soothsayer, but al-Walid replied that he was not a soothsayer. They suggested a madman, and he replied that he was not mad; then a poet, and then a sorcerer. He rejected each of them with what he knew of soothsaying, madness, poetry and sorcery. Then he said: "His speech has a sweetness; its root is like a date-palm and its branches bear fruit" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.81} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.82}. They asked: "What shall we say, then?" He said: "The nearest thing to say about him is that he is a sorcerer, who has brought speech that divides a man from his father, his brother, his wife and his clan" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.82}.

## Revelation concerning al-Walid
The book states that verses of Sura al-Muddathir were revealed concerning al-Walid. They describe his reflection and his assessment, his frowning and turning away, and his arrogance, and his judgement of the message as sorcery {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.82}.

## Notes for researchers
- The book says al-Walid hastened to think and then gave his opinion. The book presents this detail from other narrations; the account given here is its main narration.
- The book does not date the season in which the warning took place.

## For young visitors
Quraysh feared that the Arabs would believe what the Prophet said. They gathered around al-Walid ibn al-Mughira and tried to find a description of him that people would dislike.
