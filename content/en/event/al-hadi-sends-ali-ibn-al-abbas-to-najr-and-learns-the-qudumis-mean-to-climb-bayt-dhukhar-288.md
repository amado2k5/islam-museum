## Overview
When [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] learned that the two sons of Abu al-Khayr had gone to the land of [[place/qudum-yemen|Qudum]], what had gathered to them and that [[person/sasaa-ibn-jafar-al-hamdani|Sa'sa'a ibn Ja'far]] had risen in al-Bawn, he sent for those of Al Tarif who were at Shibam, and he sent [[person/ali-ibn-al-abbas-governor-of-the-mountain-288|Ali ibn al-Abbas al-Alawi]] with a force to a place in al-Bawn called Najr, where he stayed; al-Hadi stayed at Shibam and wrote to his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] to come to him at Shibam {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.217}. On Friday, eleven nights gone of Jumada II, al-Hadi heard that the Qudumis and the people of al-Masani' had agreed and decided to climb the mountain of [[place/bayt-dhukhar|Bayt Dhukhar]] to fight him from it at Shibam {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.217}. He sent to [[person/ali-ibn-sulayman-ibn-al-qasim|Ali ibn Sulayman]] to send him a body of armed men of Sana'a, and to [[person/ubaydallah-ibn-muhammad-al-hasani|Ubaydallah ibn Muhammad al-Hasani]], who was governor at Zahr, where Abu al-Ghasham and some of Al Ya'fur were jailed, to raise the call in the district and send his men to Shibam {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.217}. Ali ibn Sulayman ordered the people of Sana'a to go out to al-Hadi, and a great crowd went until they reached Shibam; Ubaydallah ibn Muhammad sent the men of the district of Zahr to the imam as well {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.217}.

## Detail
The text says Ali ibn al-Abbas al-Alawi here and Ali ibn Abi al-Abbas on p. 222; the museum links him to [[person/ali-ibn-al-abbas-governor-of-the-mountain-288]] by name and patronym as a probable identity, not a certain one. What was sent to those of Al Tarif at Shibam is not clear from the sentence and is not guessed. The sending clause has the kunya Abu al-Qasim before Ubaydallah ibn Muhammad's name without a clear place in the grammar; the page also calls him Abdullah ibn Muhammad once, and the museum uses Ubaydallah, which the next pages and a footnote agree on. The Friday named does not fall on the eleventh in the tabular calendar, a familiar difference in this book, so the date is given to the month. Najr and al-Masani' are written as printed and have no records.
