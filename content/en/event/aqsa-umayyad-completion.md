## Overview
The Umayyad mosque of [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]] rose on the pattern of the great hypostyle mosques; it was built from scratch or completed in the caliphate of [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]] (86–96/705–715), after [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik]] had built upon the earlier foundation. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 734} {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim p. 143-144}

## What happened
The Umayyad mosque was a hypostyle building: many aisles perpendicular to the qibla wall, a wider central nave on the very axis of the Dome of the Rock, and a great deal of reused material from earlier structures. Architectural historians judge it either built anew or completed under al-Walid I. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 734} It served as Jerusalem's congregational mosque and as the roofed part of the whole Haram, which counted as the city's mosque; al-Muqaddasi, writing centuries later, says describers deemed it fairer than the Damascus mosque until the Abbasid-era earthquakes struck it. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim p. 143-144}
