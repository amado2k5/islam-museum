## Summary
The first Islamic debate in the court of a Christian king: the Meccan embassy demands the return of the emigrants from Abyssinia, [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far]] rises and sets out Islam's case, recites Surat Maryam, and [[person/al-najashi|the Negus]] rules for the emigrants. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/337-339}

## Day one — the summons and the address
**The Negus** gathered the Muslims and asked: 'what is this religion in which you left your people, entering neither mine nor any nation's?' **Ja'far ibn Abi Talib** stood and said: 'O king, we were a people of ignorance: we worshipped idols, ate carrion, committed outrages, severed kinship, wronged neighbours — the strong among us devouring the weak. We were so until God sent us a messenger from among us whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness and chastity we knew. He called us to God to worship and unify Him, and to cast aside the stones and idols we and our fathers worshipped besides Him; he commanded us to speak truth, deliver trusts, keep kinship, be good neighbours, and refrain from forbidden things and bloodshed; he forbade us outrages, false speech, devouring the orphan's wealth and slandering chaste women; he commanded us to worship God associating nothing with Him, and ordered prayer, alms and fasting. So we believed him, trusted him, and followed him in what he brought: we worshipped God alone, associating nothing with Him, made unlawful what he forbade and lawful what he permitted. Our people then attacked us, tortured us and tempted us from our religion to return us to the worship of idols instead of God and to make lawful the foul things we used to make lawful. When they overcame us, wronged us, beset us and barred us from our religion, we came to your country, chose you over all others, desired your protection and hoped not to be wronged in your presence, O king' {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/338-339}.

## The recitation — the answer by the text
The Negus said: 'do you have with you anything of what he brought from God?' Ja'far said: yes — and recited to him the opening of Surat Maryam. The Negus wept until his beard was wet, and his bishops wept until their books were wet when they heard what he recited; then the Negus said: 'this and what Jesus brought come from one lamp. Go — by God, I will never hand them over to them, and I mean it' {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/339}.

## Day two — Amr's stratagem and Ja'far's reply
The next day **Amr ibn al-As** returned to the Negus and said: 'O king, they say a monstrous thing about Jesus son of Mary — send for them and ask them about him.' He sent for them and asked. The Muslims said among themselves: what shall we say about Jesus? They answered: by God, we will say what our Prophet said and brought, come what may. When they entered, he asked: 'what do you say of Jesus son of Mary?' **Ja'far** said: 'we say of him what our Prophet brought — he is God's servant, His messenger, His spirit and His word cast into the untouched virgin Mary.' **The Negus** struck the ground with his hand, picked up a twig and said: 'Jesus son of Mary does not exceed what you said by this twig.' His bishops snorted around him when he said it, and he said: 'snort as you will, by God. Go — you are safe in my land; whoever insults you shall be fined. Not for a mountain of gold would I wish to have wronged one man of you. Return their gifts — we have no need of them. By God, God did not take a bribe from me when He restored my kingdom, that I should take a bribe in it; and God did not obey the people concerning me, that I should obey them concerning Him' {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/339}.

## The embassy returns empty
The envoys left the Negus repudiated, their gifts returned, and the Muslims remained in his protection — the best of shelters with the safest of neighbours {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/339}. It was a complete disputation: question and answer, a recited text, and a verdict between two cases.
