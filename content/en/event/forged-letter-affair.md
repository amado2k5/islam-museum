## Summary
On the way back for the people of Egypt from Medina after the reconciliation with the delegations, they were met by a rider on a camel claiming to be Uthman's messenger to his governor in Egypt, carrying a letter with Uthman's seal ordering the execution of a group of the dissidents. The Egyptians did not believe him, and al-Sallabi considers the letter forged {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}.

## The Incident
After Uthman reconciled with the delegations individually and they departed for their homes, the rebels saw that their plan had failed, so they devised something else {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}. The book states that a rider on a camel intercepted the people of Egypt on the road; when questioned, he said he was the messenger of the Commander of the Faithful to his governor, and offered that they bring two Muslim men to swear by God that he had neither written, dictated, nor known of it, yet they did not believe him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}.

## Reasons for Judging It Forged
Al-Sallabi lists several points: that the people of Iraq returned to their land to the east and the people of Egypt to the west, yet they all turned back at the exact same time, which makes no sense unless the forgers had arranged the matter; and that they hired another rider to inform the Iraqis that the Egyptians had discovered a letter {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}. He adds that the letter was not the first of its kind, as the mischief-makers had forged letters in the names of the Mothers of the Believers, Ali, Talha, and al-Zubayr {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 165}.

## Denial by the Companions
Aisha denies that she wrote anything to the people, and it is denied that Ali wrote to the delegates inviting them to Medina {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 165}. Al-Sallabi quotes Ibn Kathir as saying that what was attributed to the Companions of letters calling for their coming is "a lie against the Companions," and that the forged letters were written in their names {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 165}.

## Views on the Incident
The book presents the Sunni view, which is that the letter was forged, attributing this to al-Sallabi and Ibn Kathir {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 165-166}. No modern academic study on the authenticity of the letter appeared in the pages read (needs_source). The book transmits from al-Tabari that the messenger swore he had not written the letter, and mentions this report without making it a conclusive proof {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}.
