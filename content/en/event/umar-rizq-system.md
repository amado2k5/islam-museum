## Overview
"Futuh al-Buldan" relates that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] made monthly rations of food for the people, and the book follows the fate of this system in the later reigns. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The trial
Umar ordered a jarib of food ground, baked and eaten with oil; thirty men ate their lunch from it, and then he did the same at supper. He said: "Two jaribs suffice a man each month." So he gave a man, a woman and a slave two jaribs a month. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## From the pulpit
The book quotes [[person/abu-al-darda|Abu al-Darda']]'s saying that Umar instituted rightly guided sunnas, among them the debt-payments and the equal shares. Umar said from the pulpit that he imposed on every Muslim soul each month a mudd, a measure of oil and a measure of vinegar, and that this includes the slave. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Inheritance and the newborn
Uthman and those after him upheld the rules of dependants and made them inheritable. When [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] asked about this, he rejected inheritance. Sulayman ibn Habib warned him that people would follow his ending of inheritance and not of the whole allotment, and he left them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Umar used to impose a stipend for a child when it was born, and in the report of Ibn Umar it was not imposed until it was weaned. [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] imposed it for the weaned child. [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik]] cut this off except for whom he wished. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
