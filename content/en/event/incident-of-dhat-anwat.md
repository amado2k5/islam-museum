## Summary
An incident on the army's road from Mecca to [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]] in Shawwal 8 AH: the Muslims passed a great green lote-tree called Dhat Anwat, on which the polytheists used to hang their weapons, at which they would tarry in devotion, and to which they would sacrifice {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 415}.

## "Make us a Dhat Anwat"
Some of the army — many of them Meccans new to Islam — said to the Prophet: make us a Dhat Anwat as they have a Dhat Anwat {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 415}. He rebuked their words and said: "God is greatest — you have said, by Him in whose hand Muhammad's soul is, as the people of Moses said: make us a god as they have gods. He said: you are a people who act in ignorance," and told them this was the way of things — that they would tread the ways of those before them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 415; jami-al-tirmidhi}.

## For young visitors
Some of the new Muslims saw a great tree the polytheists venerated, hanging their swords on it, and asked for a tree like it for themselves. The Prophet taught them that monotheism needs neither idols nor sacred trees, and that their request resembled the people of Moses asking for an idol — so they learned a lesson in the oneness of worship.
