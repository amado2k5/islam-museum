## The exchange — what each side said
- **Al-Khatib (the author)**: the Khutut is a neutral-seeming exposition of Twelver principles as the school's own modern authorities state them — taqiyya, the imamate, the school's stance on the Companions and on the corruption question — citing each item to its Shia source then annotating it with the Sunni position.
- **The Shia material (as the book documents it)**: texts of the school's modern authorities on its doctrinal unity — positions on the Qur'an, the Companions and taqiyya — as published amid the rapprochement debates.
- **Al-Khatib's annotation**: the ordered Sunni reply — taqiyya is for Sunnis a dispensation of necessity, not a principle of faith; the Companions are upright transmitters, not material of disparagement; and textual corruption is a claim answered by the Qur'an's transmitted text.
- **The Shia counter-answer**: the book was charged with selection and misrepresentation — Shia authorities replying that the Khutut assembles statements cut from their context.
- **The value**: a small booklet that became the most translated Sunni polemical text on the question — the concise Sunni position on the reply to rapprochement. {cite:mahajjah-al-murajaat-critique}
