## Summary
Quraysh gathered at the valley of Khayf of Banu Kinana, in Wadi al-Muhassab, and pledged against [[group/banu-hashim|Banu Hashim]] and [[group/banu-al-muttalib|Banu al-Muttalib]] that they would not marry them, sell to them, buy from them, sit with them, mix with them, enter their houses or speak to them, until they handed over [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] to be killed. They wrote this agreement in a document and hung it inside the Ka'ba {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.109} {cite:zad-al-maad 2/46}.

## The siege
Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, believers and unbelievers alike, withdrew, except [[person/abu-lahab|Abu Lahab]], who stayed with Quraysh. They were confined in [[place/shib-abi-talib|the valley of Abu Talib]] on the night of the new moon of Muharram in the seventh year of the mission {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.109}. Supplies were cut off from them. The polytheists bought up every food item that entered Mecca, and the Hashimites could go out to buy only in the sacred months. The people of Mecca raised their prices until the Hashimites could no longer afford them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.110}.

## Those who brought in food in secret
[[person/hakim-ibn-hizam|Hakim ibn Hizam]] was carrying wheat to his aunt [[person/khadija|Khadija]] when [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]] confronted him. Abu al-Bakhtari intervened and let him carry it through {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.110}. Abu Talib would order the Prophet, when people slept, to sleep in his own bed. Then he would have one of his sons or brothers sleep in the bed until he saw who meant to assassinate him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.110}.

## Notes for researchers
- The author puts the length of the siege at three years, a period on which the sources he uses agree {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.115}.

## For young visitors
Quraysh decided to cut off all dealings with the whole household of the Prophet: they would not marry into it or sell it anything. They confined Banu Hashim in a narrow valley for three years, until they ate leaves.

## For Sprouts
Quraysh agreed to cut off trade and marriage with the Banu Hashim, so the clan withdrew into a valley in the hills, the Shi'b Abi Talib, for about three years.

## For Explorers
Quraysh agreed to boycott the Banu Hashim and the Banu al-Muttalib: no one would sell to them or marry into their families. The clan gathered in the valley of Abu Talib, a valley between the hills, and lived there for about three years in great hardship, until the agreement they had written was torn up.

## For Scholars
The general boycott agreed by Quraysh against the Banu Hashim and the Banu al-Muttalib, and the clan's withdrawal into the Shi'b of Abu Talib, a valley between the hills, for about three years. No one would sell to them or marry them, and the hardship fell hardest on the children and the old. The sources say the document was later torn up, and that some people of Mecca rejected the injustice and tore it. The entry gives the boycott as the sira books tell it and passes no judgement on either side.

## In Abla al-Kahlawi's programme 'In the Love of al-Mustafa'
[[person/abla-al-kahlawi|Abla al-Kahlawi]] calls the boycott 'the strongest and harshest weapon' Quraysh used, in her programme [[work/fi-hub-al-mustapha|'In the Love of al-Mustafa']], saying they turned to it after trying every kind of harm, once Umar ibn al-Khattab and Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib had entered Islam and it had begun to spread among the tribes. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:07:00} She relates that Quraysh met and wrote down and sealed an agreement to boycott the Banu Abd al-Muttalib entirely, not marrying with them, selling to them, feeding them or speaking with them, and shut them in the valley (shi'b) for three years without food or drink, so that they ate the leaves of trees and even leather, many children died and others fell ill. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:08:00}

The gravest consequence of the siege, she says, was the illness of Khadija and of Abu Talib; the Prophet did not soften or change until Quraysh felt ashamed of what they had done, being Arabs with codes of honour, and lifted the siege. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:08:30} The siege left its mark on Khadija, who died after it of a grave illness, and Abu Talib died straight after her, so that year was called the Year of Sorrow. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep09 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep13 00:02:00}
