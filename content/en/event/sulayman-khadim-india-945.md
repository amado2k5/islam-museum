## Summary
The great Ottoman expedition to India under [[person/sulayman-pasha-khadim|Sulayman Pasha al-Khadim]], governor of Egypt, in 945/1538 under Sulayman the Magnificent {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 191, 196}.

## The firman
Al-Salabi records Sulayman's compact with the Indian rulers of Calicut and Cambay against Portugal, and his decree to Sulayman Pasha: "Upon receiving our orders, prepare your baggage and provisions, fit out a fleet at Suez for jihad in God's path; once it is armed and victualled and a sufficient army gathered, sail for India, take those parts and hold them — for if you cut the road and beset the ways leading to Mecca, you will have averted the evil the Portuguese wrought and struck their banner from the sea" {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 196}.

## The course
Sulayman al-Khadim reached Jeddah in seven days, turned to Kamaran, took Aden and set a six-hundred-man garrison, then pressed on to besiege Diu; unable to storm it he withdrew having lost some four hundred men, though he took a forward castle with eighty Portuguese prisoners — and but for fresh Portuguese reinforcements, the book says, every castle would have surrendered {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 196-197}.
