## Summary
In 256/870 Bayakbak, holder of Egypt's apanage, was killed {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 666} — al-Mas'udi says he fell into al-Muhtadi's hands and was beheaded, or was killed in the battle of the Samarra bridge {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 559-560}. Egypt was then granted as an apanage to Yarjukh, who had married one of his daughters to Ibn Tulun; he confirmed Ahmad as vice-governor and added Alexandria, Barqa and the frontier districts, hitherto outside his government — after a four-year struggle with the intendant of finance Ibn al-Mudabbir that ended in the latter's removal {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 300}.
