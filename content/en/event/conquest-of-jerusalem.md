## Overview
al-Tabari relates that Umar ibn al-Khattab came to al-Jabiya, and the people of Iliya went out to him seeking safe-conduct. They made peace with him for the jizya and opened the city to him. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.3 p.607}

## The Jewish man
A Jew who used to ask him about the Antichrist advised him not to return until God had opened Iliya. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.3 p.607}

## The date
The index of the third volume of al-Tabari places the account in 15 AH, with its pages running from 607 to 613. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.3 p.632}

The details of the treaty need an additional source.

## In the Futuh al-Sham's telling
The [[work/futuh-al-sham-waqidi|Futuh al-Sham]] ascribed to al-Waqidi gives a long account of the conquest of Jerusalem (1/219-236): Abu Ubayda's commanders besiege the city, its people say they find in their books that only the companion of the Muslims' prophet named Umar will take it and that they know his description, and Abu Ubayda writes to [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] accordingly. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.225} {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.226} Umar consults the Companions, Ali advises him to go, and he sets out on a red camel. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.227} The book records judgments he gave among the Bedouin on the way, {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.228} his sermon at al-Jabiya and Bilal's call to prayer that brought the people to tears, {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.230} and how he put on white clothes and mounted an ambling Byzantine pack-horse, then dismounted in haste and returned to his patched cloak for fear of vanity. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.231}

When Umar camped where Abu Ubayda was, the patriarch (al-batrak) asked that Umar stand alone facing the wall so that he might verify his description; Umar rode out on his camel in his patched cloak with only Abu Ubayda, and on seeing him the patriarch said: this is the one whose description we find in our books, and told the people of the city to go down to him; they came out asking for covenant and protection and accepting the jizya. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.232} {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.233} In this account Umar entered on a Monday and stayed until Friday, marked out a prayer niche on the eastern side, the site of his mosque, led his companions in the Friday prayer, and stayed ten days in all. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.233} It includes the conversion of [[person/kab-al-ahbar|Ka'b al-Ahbar]] at Umar's hands after the dawn prayer at the Rock. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.234} {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.235} Umar then wrote a covenant for the people of Jerusalem, confirmed them on the jizya, went to al-Jabiya where he set up the registers, and divided Syria between Abu Ubayda (from Hawran to Aleppo) and Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan (Palestine and the coast). {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v1 p.235}

This account gives no year for the conquest, and the book's ascription to al-Waqidi is open to question (see [[question/attribution-of-futuh-al-sham-waqidi|the attribution of Futuh al-Sham]]).
