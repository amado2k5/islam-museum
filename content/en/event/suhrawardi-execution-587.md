## Summary
In 587 AH the philosopher Shihab al-Din Yahya al-Suhrawardi was killed in Aleppo. He had come there and his name had spread. The king al-Zahir Ghazi, son of the sultan Salah al-Din, held a gathering for him, where his merit showed and his learning dazzled. Al-Zahir became attached to him and favoured him {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 770}.

## The scholars' accusation
The scholars came to see heresy and moral laxity in him. They issued a legal ruling that he was an unbeliever and sent it to Salah al-Din. They warned him that Suhrawardi might corrupt his son's belief, and Salah al-Din sent word to his son to kill him without further discussion {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 770}.

## Death by starvation
He was offered a choice, and he chose to die of hunger, because he was used to fasting. He was denied food until he died. He had lived thirty-six years. The sword-bearer al-Amidi said: "I saw him, with much knowledge and little sense." Ibn Khallikan reports that he was strangled in the citadel of Aleppo on 5 Rajab 587 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 770; wafayat-ayan}.
