## Summary
This card gathers what the biographical dictionaries say about the last illness of [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] in Medina: his weeping and what he said when asked why, his prayer when Marwan ibn al-Hakam visited him, his instructions about his funeral, and his burial in al-Baqi'. Historians differ on the year of his death between 57 and 59. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075}

## Background
According to al-Dhahabi, Abu Hurayra entered Islam at the start of year 7, the year of Khaybar. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4067} Al-Dhahabi quotes al-Waqidi that he lived at Dhu al-Hulayfa and owned a house in Medina which he gave in charity to his freedmen, who sold it. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4067} His musnad, in al-Dhahabi's count, holds 5,374 reports. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4076} Ibn Hajar notes that he said twelve thousand glorifications a day, remarking that he did so in proportion to his sins. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 1792}

## His weeping in illness
Al-Dhahabi relates, through Ibn al-Mubarak from Wuhayb ibn al-Ward from Salm ibn Bashir, that Abu Hurayra wept in his illness. Asked why, he said he was not weeping over their world but over the distance of his journey and the scantiness of his provisions, and because he had come to a climb whose far side led to Garden or Fire, not knowing which would take him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075} Sifat al-Safwa gives the same report and adds, from Ibn Shawdhab, that when death came he wept and cited the distance of the desert, the thin provisions and a steep ascent whose descent leads to Garden or Fire. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 250} Ibn Hajar mentions al-Baghawi's report that he wept at death and explained it by scarcity of provision and the harshness of the desert. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 1793}

## Marwan's visit and his prayer
Malik relates from al-Maqburi that Marwan went in to Abu Hurayra during his complaint and said: God heal you, Abu Hurayra. Abu Hurayra answered: O God, I love to meet You, so love to meet me. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075} The report adds that Marwan had not yet reached the market when Abu Hurayra died, as Ibn Hajar gives it from Ibn Abi al-Dunya. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075} {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 1793} Marwan sometimes appointed him deputy over Medina, al-Dhahabi notes in his biography. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4073}

## His instructions for the funeral
Ibn Hajar cites Ahmad and al-Nasa'i, with an isnad he calls sound, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Mihran: when death came to Abu Hurayra he said, do not pitch a tent over me, do not follow me with a censer, and hurry with me. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 1792} He gives another report from Abu Salama: when I die do not wail over me, do not follow me with a censer, and hurry with me. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 1793} The two agree on three points: no tent over the grave or bier, no incense carried with the procession, and speed; the second adds a ban on wailing.

## Prayer and burial
Al-Dhahabi says the governor al-Walid ibn Utba prayed over him, that Ibn Umar and Abu Sa'id escorted the bier, and that he was buried in al-Baqi'. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075} He adds that al-Walid wrote to Mu'awiya with the news; Mu'awiya replied that he should see whom Abu Hurayra had left, give them ten thousand dirhams and treat their neighbourhood well, because he had been among those who stood by Uthman and was with him in the house. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075}

## What he left
The sources read do not state the size of his estate. What they record is the house at Dhu al-Hulayfa that al-Waqidi says he gave in charity to his freedmen {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4067} and Mu'awiya's gift of ten thousand dirhams to his family after his death. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075}

## Where the reports differ
Three years are given for his death. Al-Dhahabi quotes Ibn Uyayna from Hisham ibn Urwa that A'isha and Abu Hurayra died in 57, followed by Yahya ibn Bukayr, Ibn al-Madini, Khalifa, al-Mada'ini and al-Fallas; Abu Mash'ar, Damra and others say 58; al-Waqidi, Ibn Ishaq and Abu Ubayd say 59, at the age of seventy-eight. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075} Ibn Hajar judges Hisham ibn Urwa's date the reliable one. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 1793} Ibn Abd al-Barr records that al-Haytham ibn Adi said 58. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 864} As for the place, he died in Medina, or, it is said, at al-Aqiq. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 275} He is also reported to have prayed, O God, do not let me reach the year sixty. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075} Who prayed over him is likewise reported as al-Walid ibn Utba, in al-Dhahabi's text introduced with the hedge 'it is said'. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 4075}

## Sources and documentation
The card rests on al-Dhahabi's Siyar a'lam al-nubala', Ibn al-Jawzi's Sifat al-safwa in al-Tartusi's abridgement, Ibn Hajar's al-Isaba and Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Isti'ab. Page numbers are scan pages as read. The texts of Ahmad and al-Nasa'i themselves were not found in the books read, so the instruction is given as Ibn Hajar reports it from them.
