## Summary
After the collapse of the removal expedition, Ibn Tulun occupied Syria in 264/878 under the pretext of holy war and the defence of the frontier against Byzantium {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 301}, extending his authority over Hims, Hama, Aleppo, Kinnasrin and the Diyar Mudar and naming the amir Lu'lu' as his representative over them {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 426}. He soon had to return to Egypt to face the revolt of his son al-'Abbas {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 301}.
