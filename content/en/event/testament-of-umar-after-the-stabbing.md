## Summary
After Abu Lu'lu'a stabbed him in the dawn prayer on Wednesday, four nights before the end of Dhu al-Hijja 23 AH, [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] lived some days in which he gave instructions about his debt, about being buried with his two companions, and about the groups to be looked after: the first Emigrants, the Ansar, the people of the garrison towns, the Bedouin and the protected peoples; and he made the succession a consultation among six. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 403} {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 404} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 138} For the stabbing see [[event/death-of-umar|the death of Umar ibn al-Khattab]], for the consultation [[event/shura-council-of-uthman|the shura in the caliphate of Uthman]], and for his death and burial [[event/deathbed-and-burial-of-umar|his last hours and burial]].

## After the stabbing
The report of al-Bukhari, which Ibn Hajar explains under number 3700, says that when Umar was struck he asked Ibn Abbas to find out who had killed him; Ibn Abbas said it was the slave of al-Mughira, and Umar said, "Praise be to God, who did not make my death at the hand of a man who claims Islam." He was carried to his house; they brought him a date drink and he drank it and it came out of his belly; they brought milk and it came out of his wound, and they knew he would die. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 403} In the report of Abu Ishaq that Ibn Hajar cites, the doctor told him: make your will, for I think you will die today or tomorrow. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 409}

People came and praised him. A young man said: rejoice in the good news God has given you, of your company with the Messenger of God, your early place in Islam, then your rule in which you were just, then martyrdom. Umar said he wished it could be even, neither for him nor against him. When the young man turned away Umar saw his lower garment touching the ground, called him back and told him to lift his clothes, for it was better for his garment and more God-fearing. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 403} In al-Muhibb al-Tabari's report it is Ibn Abbas who praised him, and Umar answered that if he had the earth full of gold he would give it to ransom himself from the terror of the Resurrection. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 409} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 410}

## The charge about his debt
He told his son [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Abd Allah ibn Umar]]: see what I owe in debt. They counted it and found it to be eighty-six thousand or thereabouts. He told him that if the property of the family of Umar sufficed, he should pay it from their wealth; if not, ask among Banu Adi ibn Ka'b, and if that did not suffice, among Quraysh, and not go beyond them to others. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 403} In Jabir's report, which Ibn Hajar mentions, Umar made Ibn Umar swear not to wash his head until he had sold properties of the family of Umar for eighty thousand and put it in the Muslims' treasury; when Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf asked what it was for, Umar said he had spent it on pilgrimages and on calamities that had befallen him. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 411} Ibn Hajar adds that in Ibn Zabala's Akhbar al-Madina the debt was twenty-six thousand, and Iyad is sure of that, though he himself relies on the first figure; and that Nafi', Ibn Umar's freedman, denied there was any debt because one of the heirs had sold his inheritance for a hundred thousand. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 411} {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 412}

## The request to be buried
He sent his son to Aisha to give her his greeting without the title Commander of the Faithful, for he was no longer commander of the believers, and to ask permission to be buried with his two companions. She gave permission, saying she had wanted the place for herself but would prefer him to herself that day. Umar said nothing mattered more to him; when I am dead, carry me, greet her again, and say that Umar asks permission; if she permits, bring me in, and if she refuses, take me back to the Muslims' graves. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 403} {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 404} The burial is told in [[event/deathbed-and-burial-of-umar|the next record]].

## What he told the next caliph
They said, "Make a will, Commander of the Faithful, appoint a successor." He said he knew none more deserving of the matter than the group of men with whom the Messenger of God had been pleased when he died, and named Ali, Uthman, al-Zubayr, Talha, Sa'd and Abd al-Rahman. He said Abd Allah ibn Umar would be present with them but have no share in the matter; if the leadership came to Sa'd, well, and if not, whichever of them was made commander should use his help, for Umar had not removed him from weakness or dishonesty. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 404}

He then charged the caliph after him: to know the right of the first Emigrants and guard their honour; to deal well with the Ansar, who were first to the abode and faith, accepting from their good-doers and forgiving their wrong-doers; to treat the people of the garrison towns well, for they are Islam's support, collectors of the revenue and the enemy's rage, and to take from them only their surplus with their consent; to treat the Bedouin well, for they are the root of the Arabs and the substance of Islam, taking from the edges of their property and returning it to their poor; and to keep the covenant of God and His Messenger with those under protection, to fulfil their agreement, fight behind them and not burden them beyond their capacity. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 404} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 408} For the verse on the Ansar see [[passage/quran-59-9|Qur'an 59:9]].

## His other charges
In the report of Ma'dan ibn Abi Talha, given by al-Muhibb al-Tabari, Umar had preached on the Friday before, saying he had dreamed of a cock that pecked him three times and took it to mean his time had come, and that some people were telling him to appoint a successor; God would not lose His religion, and if something overtook him the caliphate was to be consultation among the six. After the stabbing people came in to him, Emigrants, then Ansar, then the people of Medina, Syria and Iraq, his wound bound with a black cloak. They said, "Counsel us." He said: I counsel you to hold to the Book of God, for you will not go astray while you follow it; to look after the Emigrants; the Ansar; the Bedouin, who are your root and your substance and your brothers; and the people under protection, who are your Prophet's charge and your families' provision. Then he said, "Get up and leave me." {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 415} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 416}

It is reported that Umar looked at Ali and said: fear God, and if you take charge of anything of the people's affairs do not put Banu Hashim over the necks of the Muslims; he looked at Uthman and said the same of Banu Umayya or Banu Abi Mu'ayt; he told Sa'd and al-Zubayr to fear God, and told Abd al-Rahman not to put his relatives over the people. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 417} He told Ibn Umar: when I die close my eyes and be moderate with my shroud, be moderate with my grave, let no woman go out with me, do not praise me for what is not in me, and hurry in the walking. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 417} He swore to his daughter [[person/hafsa|Hafsa]] that she should not lament him after she left that sitting, for an angel loathes a dead man who is lamented with what is not in him. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 417} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 418}

He told Suhayb to lead the people in prayer for three days until the consultation ended, and that the members of the council should assemble with men set over them; he set fifty men over them, with Abu Talha and al-Miqdad at their head. Ibn Kathir says he did not name Sa'id ibn Zayd in the council because he was his cousin and he feared he would be favoured. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 145} In an exchange about not appointing a successor he told Ibn Umar that if he appointed one, one better than he had done so, and if he left it, one better than he had left it, and Ibn Umar understood he would not name one. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 414}

## Where the reports differ
- How many were stabbed: thirteen men, of whom seven died, in the report of al-Bukhari; twelve in the report of Abu Ishaq; in al-Bidaya six died. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 403} {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 407} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 137}
- When he was struck: in prayer after the takbir in al-Bukhari; before the prayer began in the report of Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 403} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 412}
- The debt: eighty-six thousand in al-Bukhari; twenty-six thousand in Ibn Zabala and Iyad; and Nafi' denies a debt. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 411} {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 412}
- Who said "appoint a successor": Ibn Hajar notes that in the Book of Rulings it is Abd Allah ibn Umar. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 413}
