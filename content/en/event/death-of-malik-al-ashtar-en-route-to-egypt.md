## Summary
Ali chose Malik al-Ashtar al-Nakha'i as governor of Egypt after Qays ibn Sa'd left its governorship, but al-Ashtar died on the way before entering Egypt {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 334}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}. It was reported that he was given a poisoned drink of honey, and the book leans toward the view that Muawiya incited his killing {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}.

## Appointment of al-Ashtar
The book states that Ali appointed al-Ashtar over Egypt after suspicions had settled around Qays ibn Sa'd; Ali met al-Ashtar before his departure and said to him, "There is no one for it but you" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}. The book says that al-Ashtar set out for Egypt with a group of his companions {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}.

## Death
Al-Ashtar reached the shores of the Red Sea and died before entering Egypt {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}. It was said that he was given a poisoned drink of honey, and some among the tax-collectors were accused of poisoning him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}.

## The Book's Position
The book holds, following Dr. Yahya al-Yahya, that Muawiya incited the killing of al-Ashtar, while presenting the report with the phrasing of uncertainty "it was said," without establishing it conclusively {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not specify the date of death, and the estimated year here is derived from the sequence of governors in the book.
- The report of poison is narrated with phrasing indicating weakness and should not be transmitted as established fact.
