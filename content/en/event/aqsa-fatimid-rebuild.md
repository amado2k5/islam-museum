## Overview
After the devastating earthquake of 1033, the Fatimid caliph [[person/al-zahir-fatimid|al-Zahir]] had [[building/al-aqsa-mosque|al-Aqsa]] rebuilt between 1034 and 1036, and the work was completed under [[person/al-mustansir-fatimid|al-Mustansir]] in 1065. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 734-735}

## What happened
The earthquake struck the whole city hard and is counted among the causes of a major reorganisation of Jerusalem. The new mosque was smaller than its predecessor — seven aisles instead of fifteen, probably without side doors — but famous for the brilliance of its mosaics and the triumphal arch carrying a royal inscription in gold, the first documented appearance of the Night Journey verse on the building. Nasir-i Khusraw described it in 1047, shortly before its completion, and most of the central part of today's mosque dates from that Fatimid rebuilding. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 734-735}
