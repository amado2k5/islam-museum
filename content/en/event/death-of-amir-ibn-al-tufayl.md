## Overview
[[person/amir-ibn-al-tufayl|Amir ibn al-Tufayl]] died on his way back from [[event/assassination-attempt-of-amir-and-arbad|the delegation of Banu Amir]], of a disease that struck his neck, in the house of a woman of [[group/banu-salul|Banu Salul]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/214}

## The account
Ibn Ishaq relates that when the Prophet turned Amir away from him, he said: "O God, spare me Amir ibn al-Tufayl." Then the delegation set out on the way home, and when they were on part of the road, God sent upon Amir a plague in his neck, and he died in the house of a woman of Banu Salul. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/214} Before his death he kept saying: "O Banu Amir, a swelling like the swelling of a young camel, in the house of a woman of Salul!" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/214} Ibn Hisham notes that his words are also transmitted as: "a swelling like the swelling of camels, and death in the house of a woman of Salul." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/214} Then his companions buried his body and went on to the land of Banu Amir. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/214}

## What the editors explain
The editors of the 1936 edition explain "ghuddah" as a disease that strikes a camel and kills it, something like the angina that strikes a person, and "bakr" as a young camel. They say that Amir regretted that he did not die killed, as brave men regret it, and regretted dying in the house of a woman of Salul, for which the Arabs described them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/214}

## Marks of the report
- Ibn Ishaq links the death to the Prophet's prayer against Amir; this is the narrator's link, transmitted as part of the traditional narrative. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/214}
- In al-Bukhari, Amir died on his horse, according to what al-Mubarakfuri's book transmits. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.453}
