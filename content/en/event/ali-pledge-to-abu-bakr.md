## Summary
Al-Sallabi states that [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] pledged allegiance to [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] at the very outset, on the day of the death of [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]], and that [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] pledged allegiance with him, based on a report with a sound chain of transmission from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, and that reports of a long delay are unsound {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.122}.

## The Sound Report
The book transmits the report of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri concerning [[event/saqifa|the pledge of al-Saqifa]]: When Abu Bakr sat on the pulpit, he looked at the faces of the people and did not see Ali, so he asked about him; he was brought, and Abu Bakr said to him, "Cousin of the Messenger of God and his son-in-law, did you wish to split the unity of the Muslims?" He replied, "No reproach, O successor of the Messenger of God," and pledged allegiance to him, and then Abu Bakr did the same with al-Zubayr {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.122}. The book considers this report among the soundest transmitted on the subject, and mentions that Muslim said about it to his teacher Ibn Khuzayma that it "is worth a sacrificial camel," to which Ibn Khuzayma replied that it "is worth a purse of money" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.122}.

## Reports of Delay
The book notes that many reports claim Ali and al-Zubayr delayed their pledge of allegiance, and considers most of them unsound. Among them is a report in al-Tabari narrated by Sayf ibn Umar, which the book describes as weak {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.123}. It cites a dialogue between Amr ibn Hurayth and Sa'id ibn Zayd stating that the pledge of allegiance took place on the day of the death and that the Emigrants followed Abu Bakr {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.123}.

## Ali's Own Statement
The book cites Ali's statement to Ibn al-Kawwa and Qays ibn Abbad: "Had I held any covenant from the Prophet regarding this, I would not have left the brother of Banu Taym," then says that the Prophet did not die suddenly, that Abu Bakr led the people in prayer during the Prophet's illness, and that Ali said he "rendered to Abu Bakr his due," went on campaigns with him, and carried out legal penalties in his presence {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.123}.

## Renewal of the Pledge
Ibn Kathir holds, as cited by the book, that Ali renewed his pledge of allegiance six months after the first pledge, following the death of Fatima, and the book considers this renewal to be based on sound reports {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.124}.

## Perspectives
**Sunnis (the book's account):** Ali pledged allegiance at the outset, his recognition of Abu Bakr's precedence is established, and the widespread reports of delay are rejected {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.122-124}.

**The Imami Shi'a View (as transmitted by the book):** Reports relate that Ali held back and did not consent to the pledge of allegiance; the book describes these as weak and does not accept them {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.122-123}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book mentions neither a day nor a month; the monthly date is derived from the day of death and is an estimate.
- The book calls the Imami reports "al-Rawafid"; this is the book's own term, and its label is not adopted in the museum's voice.
