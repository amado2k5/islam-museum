## Overview
Al-Hadi wrote to them at the foot of his letter some lines of verse {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.278}. As the book gives it on pp. 279-280, the poem opens with a warning: take your guard from me, for I am sending you armies of God, and God prevails {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.279}. It is ascribed to al-Hadi in this Zaydi book; the museum gives its sense and does not copy its lines.

## Detail
The poet pictures the armies of Muhammad's party moving on the rebels, filling the earth with their squadrons, on lean horses led by chiefs with thin keen swords and blue-headed lances, riders at whose fear hair turns white in hard war; the only thing holding them back, he says, is leaving their land and the gathering of the squadrons around him for the march {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.279}. He says they will find him clear-sighted in jihad against them and that they already have experience of him, and reflects that nothing is like war, whose fire a heedless man lights and then meets disasters, strong in kindling and weak when the consequences press, seeking peace where there is none {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.279}. He ends that whoever sets to fight him fights a lion defending its cubs, with a fearsome spring and claws, a horseman whose meeting is death and whose opponent fails {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.279} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.280}. The notes of p. 279 give manuscript S's readings ("armies", "they march against the rebels", "seeking help", "the desirable", "what"). The OCR scrambles the order of the lines, so the printed order as read from the page image is followed. The words of abuse in the lines are not repeated.
