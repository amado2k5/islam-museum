## Overview
Al-Baladhuri reports that Baghdad was an old place, and that [[person/al-mansur|al-Mansur]] made it a garrison city and built in it a city he began in 145 AH, and named it the City of Peace. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.414}

## The building
When news reached him of the rising of Muhammad and Ibrahim, the sons of Abd Allah ibn al-Hasan, he returned to Kufa, then moved the treasuries, the storehouses and the registers from Kufa to Baghdad in 146 AH. He completed the building of the wall of his city and the old wall of Baghdad in 147 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.414}

He bought the land of the city from the owners of villages in Badurya, Qatarbal, the Buq River and the Bayn River, and granted it to his family, his commanders, his troops and his scribes. He made the market-place at al-Karkh, built two mosques and the new bridge over the Sarat. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.414}

## Al-Rusafa
He built al-Rusafa for al-Mahdi on the eastern side, which was called the camp of al-Mahdi. Al-Mahdi lodged in it in 151 AH when he returned from Rayy. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.414}

## Al-Suyuti's account
According to al-Suyuti, al-Mansur began building Baghdad in the year 140 AH and finished it in the year 149 AH, which differs from what was said before. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424-425}

## For Sprouts
Caliph Abu Jafar al-Mansur built the round city of Baghdad on the Tigris River, making it a thriving center of peace and learning.

## For Explorers
The Abbasid Caliph Abu Jafar al-Mansur founded the city of Baghdad in 145 AH, naming it the City of Peace (Madinat al-Salam). Master builders designed it in a remarkable circular plan surrounded by walls and monumental gates, and it quickly grew into a grand capital, trading crossroads, and vibrant home of scholars and libraries.

## For Scholars
The founding of Madinat al-Salam (the City of Peace, Baghdad) by the second Abbasid Caliph, Abu Jafar al-Mansur, who initiated its construction in 145 AH (762 CE) on the western bank of the Tigris River as the new imperial capital. Renowned for its ingenious circular city plan with concentric walls and four directional gates facing the empire's key provinces, it placed the caliphal palace and congregational mosque at its core. Baghdad rapidly evolved into the cosmopolitan metropolis of Afro-Eurasia, housing the House of Wisdom, hospitals, grand academies, and transcontinental commerce, epitomizing the classic zenith of Islamic civilization.

## In al-Dinawari
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports that the caliphate was secured for Abu Ja'far al-Mansur in the year 138, that Baghdad was then a village with a monthly market, and that he built his city and named it the City of Peace, with his palace and the congregational mosque at its centre, giving the people of each district of Khurasan their own quarter, and dug a canal eight farsakhs long from the Euphrates to bring it supplies. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 383} He dates the building to the year 139, unlike those who place it in 145. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 383}
