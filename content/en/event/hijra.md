## Overview
The Hijra is the migration of the Prophet [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] from [[place/mecca|Mecca]] to [[place/medina|Medina]] in 622 CE, the fourteenth year of the mission {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.163-164} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 577}. His companions had already begun to emigrate to Medina before him {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3905}. The Prophet left his house on the night of 27 Safar in the fourteenth year of prophethood, corresponding to 12/13 September 622 CE {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.163-164}. He went out of Mecca with [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and Abu Bakr's freedman [[person/amir-ibn-fuhayra|Amir ibn Fuhayra]]; their guide was [[person/abdullah-ibn-urayqit|Abdullah ibn Urayqit]], who was not a Muslim {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 577}. He hid in [[place/ghar-thawr|the cave of Thawr]] for three nights, and then travelled from the cave to Medina {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.161} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.174}.

He reached [[place/quba|Quba]] on Monday 8 Rabi' al-Awwal, corresponding to 23 September 622 CE, and entered Medina on the Friday {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.170} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.172}. [[person/al-masudi|Al-Mas'udi]] dates his entry into Medina to Monday, when twelve nights of Rabi' al-Awwal had passed, and says that he stayed at Quba until Thursday and set out on Friday {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 577}. [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] remained in Mecca three days after him, until he had handed over what he had been told to hand over, and then joined him {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 577}. The Prophet lived in Medina for ten years {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 577}. The [[concept/hijri-calendar|Hijri calendar]] is counted from the Hijra: in the chapter on dating in Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahl ibn Sa'd says that they did not count from the Prophet's mission or from his death, but only from his arrival in Medina {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3934}.

## What happened
After years of hardship in Mecca, a delegation from Medina (which was then called Yathrib) pledged to protect the Prophet. His companions began to leave Mecca for the oasis in small groups. The Prophet left with [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and reached the outskirts of Medina in autumn 622 CE. [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] joined him a short time later. {cite:ibn-ishaq-guillaume}

The traditional date for arrival at Quba is 12 Rabi' al-Awwal of the first year AH. Historians debate the exact sequence of events, so the dates here are marked as traditional. {cite:watt-1956-medina}

## Significance
The community that formed in Medina became the first Islamic political entity. About fifteen years later, the caliph Umar made the start of the Hijri calendar the first of Muharram of that year, which corresponds to 16 July 622 CE in the Julian calendar.

## For young visitors
The Hijra is the journey with which a new calendar began. When someone says "year 1447 AH," they are counting the years from it.


## Stages in detail (The Sealed Nectar pp.161–174)
The account of the Hijra in this chapter divides into two stages: [[event/hijra-night-escape|the Prophet's departure from his house and his hiding in the Cave of Thawr]], and then [[event/hijra-journey-to-medina|the journey of the Hijra from the cave to Medina and his entry into it]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.161} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.174}

## For Sprouts
In the year 622, the Prophet Muhammad travelled from Mecca to Medina. His friend Abu Bakr travelled with him. The journey through the desert was long. He reached Medina in the autumn, and the people were happy to see him. The Hijri calendar begins with this journey, and it is called the Hijra. When someone says "the year 1447 AH", they are counting the years from it.

## For Explorers
In 622 the Prophet Muhammad and his companions had lived through hard years in Mecca. A group of people came from a town called Yathrib and promised to protect the Prophet and welcome his companions. The Muslims began to travel from Mecca to Yathrib in small groups. Then the Prophet set out with his friend Abu Bakr. They stayed in a cave on a mountain for three nights, and Asma, Abu Bakr's daughter, brought them food. After a long journey through the desert they reached Quba, at the edge of the town. The Prophet then entered the town and stayed in the house of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, and Ali ibn Abi Talib joined him a short time later. The town got a new name, Medina, and became the first community of the Muslims. About fifteen years later the caliph Umar made the Muslim calendar begin from the year of this journey, which is why we say "the Hijri year".

## For Scholars
The Hijra is the move of the Prophet Muhammad and his companions from Mecca to Yathrib, which came to be known as Medina, in 622 CE. Years of harm in Mecca came before it, then the two pledges of Aqaba, in which a delegation from Yathrib undertook to protect the Prophet, and his companions began to leave for the town in small groups. The sira books relate that the Prophet left with Abu Bakr by night, that the two stayed three nights in the cave of Thawr, where Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr brought them news and Asma bint Abi Bakr brought them food, and that they then travelled to Medina with a guide named Abdullah ibn Urayqit. The Prophet reached Quba on the outskirts of Medina; the traditional date is 12 Rabi' al-Awwal of the first year. He then entered the town and stayed with Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, and Ali ibn Abi Talib joined him a short time later. Historians debate the exact sequence of days, so the museum marks these dates as traditional. The Hijra was a turning point: the community that formed in Medina became the first Islamic political entity. About fifteen years later the caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab made the start of the Islamic calendar the first of Muharram of the year of the Hijra, which corresponds to 16 July 622 in the Julian calendar; this is why it is called the Hijri calendar.
