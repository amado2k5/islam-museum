## Overview
The battle of Karbala took place in 61 AH, and [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn ibn Ali]] was killed in it on the day of Ashura, the tenth of Muharram, according to al-Mas'udi. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 60-61} The sources give several accounts of its details, which are set out here under the names of their transmitters. The place: [[place/karbala|Karbala]].

## According to al-Tabari
al-Tabari, in the account for 61 AH, reports that al-Husayn prayed with his companions and with al-Hurr ibn Yazid and his army, and then addressed them. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.5 p.402}

## According to al-Suyuti
al-Suyuti reports that Ubayd Allah ibn Ziyad sent four thousand men under Umar ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, and that the people of Kufa abandoned al-Husayn. When the weapons pressed upon him, he offered to surrender, to return, or to go to Yazid, but they refused except to kill him. Sixteen men of his household were killed with him. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 341-342}

There are several reports of his killer: Sinan ibn Anas al-Nakha'i; some say a man of Madhhij; and some say Shimr ibn Dhi al-Jawshan. al-Suyuti says that Khawli ibn Yazid al-Asbahi cut off his head and brought it to Ibn Ziyad. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 341-342}

## According to al-Mas'udi
al-Mas'udi relates that the people of Kufa wrote to al-Husayn, and that Ibn Abbas advised him to go to Yemen and not to take his women and children out with him. He says that thirty-three stab wounds and thirty-four blows were found on al-Husayn, and that the people of al-'Adiriyya of Banu Asad buried him a day later. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 53-55} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 60-61}

The two sources differ on his age: fifty-five years, and it is also said nine. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 60-61}

This battle is the subject of differing positions among the various traditions. What is not set out here, namely the academic account and other perspectives, needs an additional source.

## In Khalifa ibn Khayyat
[[person/khalifa-ibn-khayyat|Khalifa ibn Khayyat]] says al-Husayn was killed on Wednesday the tenth of al-Muharram, the day of Ashura, 61. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 234} He attributes the killing to Shimr ibn Dhi al-Jawshan, with Umar ibn Sa'd commanding the army, and reports from Ibn al-Hanafiyya that seventeen of al-Husayn's family were killed with him. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 235}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"

[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] closes his series on "the revolt of al-Husayn" in the programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]] with the Battle of Karbala {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:12:00}. What follows is his account, attributed to him.

### Warnings and Ibn Ziyad's measures
He says the Umayyads learned that [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn]] had left Mecca, and [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]] wrote to [[person/ubayd-allah-ibn-ziyad|Ibn Ziyad]] reminding him that the one coming was al-Husayn, son of Fatima the Prophet's daughter, and warning him against any act the common people would never forget {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:09:30}; al-Suwaidan reads this as Marwan knowing that Umayyad rule would not stand if al-Husayn were killed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:09:30}. He says the governor of Medina wrote to Ibn Ziyad in the same vein, and that [[person/yazid-ibn-muawiya|Yazid]] wrote to warn him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:12:00}. He says Ibn Ziyad wanted both to prevent a revolt in Kufa and not to harm al-Husayn {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:14:00}; he handed out stipends to loyal fighters to secure them, and set forces under al-Husayn ibn Tamim to guard the roads out of Kufa toward Mecca, Syria and Basra, so that no one came in or went out {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:14:30}.

### Al-Hurr ibn Yazid
He relates that when news reached al-Husayn of the killing of Muslim ibn Aqil and Hani ibn Urwa, he gathered those with him, his family, his supporters, the sixty Kufan elders and those who had joined on the way, told them the news and gave leave to anyone who wished to go; they scattered, and only his household and those closest to him remained {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:06:00}. Some who stayed begged him to turn back since he had no helper in Kufa, but the sons of Aqil, Muslim's brothers, sprang up and swore not to leave until they had their revenge or tasted what Muslim had tasted, and he went on {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:07:00}. Al-Suwaidan remarks that there was no clear plan once entry to Kufa had been barred {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:07:30}. He reached Sharaf and told his young men to take on plenty of water; then at midday the vanguard of Ibn Ziyad's cavalry met him under [[person/al-hurr-ibn-yazid|al-Hurr ibn Yazid]], a thousand horsemen {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:08:00}. Al-Hurr asked him to turn back or let himself be taken to Kufa and handed to Ibn Ziyad, and al-Husayn refused {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:08:30}. Al-Suwaidan describes al-Hurr as a sensible man: he quietly suggested that al-Husayn take a road leading neither to Kufa nor back to Medina, so al-Husayn went by way of [[place/udhayb|al-Udhayb]] and [[place/al-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]], north toward the Syria road, with al-Hurr keeping pace {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:09:30}. When prayer time came al-Hurr's men prayed behind al-Husayn, until al-Husayn and his party reached [[place/karbala|Karbala]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:10:30}.

### Umar ibn Sa'd and the three offers
He says the main army came under [[person/umar-ibn-sad|Umar ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]], four thousand fighters, originally bound for [[place/rayy|Rayy]] to fight the Daylam, and Ibn Ziyad ordered it to turn toward al-Husayn {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:12:30}. Umar objected that he was going to fight on the frontier, Ibn Ziyad threatened him severely, and he went under pressure, his aim being not to fight but to bring al-Husayn and his party to Ibn Ziyad {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:13:00}. Al-Husayn refused to go to Ibn Ziyad, saying he had come only at the request of the people of Kufa, here were their letters, and if they did not want him he would go where he pleased {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:13:30}. When Ibn Ziyad insisted, al-Husayn offered three choices: to let him return to Medina, to let him go to Syria and come to terms with Yazid directly, or to let him go to one of the Muslim frontiers and fight there as one of the soldiers {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:14:00}. Al-Suwaidan calls these offers fair and reasonable {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:14:30}. Umar ibn Sa'd wrote to Ibn Ziyad that the deadlock was resolved and peace was near, and Ibn Ziyad nearly agreed to send al-Husayn to Yazid, but Shimr ibn Dhi al-Jawshan, sitting in his council, objected and advised that al-Husayn be required to submit to Ibn Ziyad's judgement, and Ibn Ziyad took his advice {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:16:00}. Al-Husayn refused to submit to Ibn Ziyad's judgement and told his companions they were released from obeying him, and all of them insisted on staying with him and fighting to the death {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:16:30}.

### The day of Ashura
He says al-Husayn drew up his companions on the morning of Friday, 10 Muharram 61 AH, with 32 horsemen and 40 foot soldiers, and al-Suwaidan holds that he was defending himself, not attacking, having offered a political way out of the crisis {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:16:30}. He gave the banner to [[person/al-abbas-ibn-ali|al-Abbas ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib]], put the tents with the women and children behind him, and had wood and reeds piled behind them and set alight, for fear the army would wheel round and come at them from behind {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:17:00}. Al-Hurr then asked Umar ibn Sa'd what he meant to do, and when Umar said he was under orders, al-Hurr left his army, joined al-Husayn and was killed with him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:17:30}. The battle began with single combat, and Umar's army met fierce resistance from al-Husayn's companions; al-Suwaidan calls it a battle unequal both in numbers and in morale {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:18:30}. He says al-Husayn did not fight at first; as his companions were killed one after another the army drew near him, each man afraid to be the one who killed the Prophet's grandson and pushing it onto another, hoping he would surrender, but he did not yield and fought them with rare courage {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:20:00}. Then Shimr shouted at the troops to kill him and went at him himself; al-Suwaidan says it was Shimr who killed al-Husayn and cut off his head, and names [[person/khawli-ibn-yazid-al-asbahi|Khawli ibn Yazid al-Asbahi]] as the one who carried the head to Ibn Ziyad {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:20:30}. He dates the killing to 10 Muharram, the day of Ashura, 61 AH {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:20:30}.

### The dead and the women
Al-Suwaidan says 72 men of al-Husayn's companions and household were killed with him, and 88 of Umar ibn Sa'd's men {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:21:30}, and that eighteen of the Prophet's household were among the dead {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:22:00}. He relates that after the battle Umar ibn Sa'd ordered that no one approach al-Husayn's women and children or do them any harm, and holds that the reports of the women being molested are not sound, arguing that [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]], [[person/ibn-al-athir-izz-al-din|Ibn al-Athir]] and [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] do not report anyone entering upon them {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep23 00:22:00}. This is his reading; the other accounts set out on this page differ on the number of the dead and on the name of the killer.
