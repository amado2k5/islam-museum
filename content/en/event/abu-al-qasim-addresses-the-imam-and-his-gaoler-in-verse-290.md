## Overview
On p. 256 a piece begins in which Abu al-Qasim says "tell the imam" and describes his father in the terms of praise the narrator gives, then says that he is on their covenant and has not changed, that he did not shrink at the fetter or weaken under the heavy iron or call his companion to haste, that his aim surpassed the mountains and that patience is his nature {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.256} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.257}. In a piece after it on p. 257 he addresses his gaoler, whom the verse calls a base slave, asking whether he thinks he will show abasement or yield to talk, and says that being killed for God is to him like pure honey and sweeter than nectar, and he takes pride in being the son of the Messenger of God's line and in his descent from Ali {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.257}.

## Detail
The epithets the poet gives the imam are the poet's and not the museum's judgement; they are praise in a Zaydi book. The margin of the manuscript glosses the word "al-zam'" as a thing like thunder that takes a man from bewilderment and fear. The words by which the gaoler is described are the poet's and the museum does not repeat them. Before this are [[event/abu-al-qasim-ibn-al-hadi-composes-verses-in-prison-290|the earlier pieces of the same section]]. The museum does not reproduce the lines nor the words of insult in them and gives only their sense as the book has it; the book ascribes the verse to Abu al-Qasim and the museum has not checked the attribution. The praise and the epithets in the lines are the poet's, given as the book gives them, with no verdict in the museum's voice.
