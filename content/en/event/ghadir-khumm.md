## Overview
Ghadir Khumm was a halt on the road back from the [[event/farewell-pilgrimage|Farewell Pilgrimage]], at which the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] spoke about [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]]. It took place at [[place/ghadir-khumm|Ghadir Khumm]] between Mecca and Medina. {cite:sahih-muslim 2408}

## Muslim's report
Muslim relates from Zayd ibn Arqam that the Prophet preached at a water called Khumm between Mecca and Medina. Among what he said was that he was leaving among the people "two weighty things", the first being the Book of God, then his household, and that he reminds them of God concerning his household. Zayd identified the household as those forbidden the alms after him. {cite:sahih-muslim 2408}

## What did he mean by "whoever I am his patron"?
- The people of the Sunna read his words "whoever I am his patron, Ali is his patron" as a declaration of Ali's rank and virtue, not as a text on the caliphate.
- The academic view: historians note that the event is related in both Sunni and Shi'i sources, and that the dispute concerns its meaning, not whether it happened. {cite:watt-1956-medina}
- The Twelver Shi'a read the event as the designation of Ali as caliph and first imam.

The museum does not adjudicate between these readings. What is not covered here: the sources of the Sunni and Shi'i positions and other details of the report, which need an additional source.
