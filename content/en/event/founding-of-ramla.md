## Overview
Al-Baladhuri reports that [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]] appointed his brother [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman]] over the troops of Palestine, and he lodged at Lydda, then founded and laid out the city of [[place/ramla|Ramla]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.195}

## The building
The first thing he built was his palace and the House of the Dyers, and he made a cistern in it. Then he laid out the mosque and built it, but he became caliph before he completed it. [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] completed it with a reduction in the plan, and said that the people of Ramla would be content with this amount. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.195}

Sulayman allowed the people to build, and a canal called Barda and wells were dug for them. The expense was managed by a Christian scribe from Lydda called al-Batriq ibn al-Naka. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## After Sulayman
Ramla did not exist before Sulayman; its site was sand. The Umayyads continued to spend on its wells and its canal, and then the Abbasids did. Al-Mu'tasim recorded that expense in a register by which it continued to flow. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The date
The date falls in the governorship of al-Walid (86–96 AH) approximately, and the conversion is derived. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.195}
