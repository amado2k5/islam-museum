## Overview
Men of Ahlaf told al-Hadi what Wada'a had done and that Yam had set out for revenge. Al-Hadi sent to the Wada'a who were with him, told them what had happened, and ordered them to return to their places. The narrator states that the affair was a move by Ibn Bistam, who paid Wada'a large sums to stir up strife among the Hamdan and keep al-Hadi busy away from the war with Banu al-Harith.

## Detail
The book says that men of Ahlaf came to al-Hadi and told him what Wada'a had done to Yam and that Yam and Ahlaf were setting out for revenge against Wada'a. Al-Hadi sent to the Wada'a who were with him, told them the news, and ordered them back to their places; he then spent the night. The narrator says this was Ibn Bistam's doing: Ibn Bistam had given the Wada'a large sums in order to stir up strife among the Hamdan and to keep al-Hadi from the war with Banu al-Harith. The attribution is the narrator's own and is recorded as such.
