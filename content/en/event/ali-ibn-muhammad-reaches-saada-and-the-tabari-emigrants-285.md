## Overview
[[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]], the narrator, says his emigration to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] was in Dhu al-Hijja of 285 AH; he reached [[place/saada|Sa'da]] and found his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] there as al-Hadi's governor. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.115} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116} The account is Zaydi, in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions of opponents and of God's aid to al-Hadi are the narrator's and the museum gives no verdict.

## How his father came to govern Sa'da
When the war with al-Da'am had come about, [[place/wasaha-yemen|Wasaha]] turned against Ali's father and [[person/abu-al-dughaysh-al-shihabi|Abu al-Dughaysh]] raised his banner to fight him; he wrote to al-Hadi, who was then at the al-Darb of Banu Rabi'a, telling him of his enemy's rise and the few who supported him, and al-Hadi wrote ordering him to go to Sa'da and stay there, since he had sent his brother [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] to the Hijaz on some business. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116} He set out in hiding, on foot, until he passed his cousin [[person/husayn-ibn-al-hasan-governor-of-saqayn|Husayn ibn al-Hasan]] of the line of al-Abbas ibn Ali, then governor at [[place/saqayn-yemen|Saqayn]]; Husayn took him by the hand and the two went out in fear until they reached Sa'da and stayed some days, after which Abdullah ibn al-Husayn left for the Hijaz and left him there. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116} The editor's footnote refers to Sifat Jazirat al-Arab, p. 69, for Saqayn. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116}

## The unrest and the Tabari emigrants
The narrator arrived with the pilgrims and found the town unsettled by al-Hadi's war on al-Da'am; the townspeople had hoped a commander of "the black-clad", the Abbasid side, would reach them that year, and their expectation was disappointed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116} God supported al-Hadi, in the narrator's telling, with helpers who emigrated to him from the [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|Tabaris]]; they reached Sa'da, the pilgrims heard of them, and they were close to fifty men. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116} Al-Hadi was glad of them and al-Da'am was grieved; they stayed some days at Sa'da and then went out to al-Hadi at Khiwan, arriving in the best state. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116}

## The effect, and the narrator stays
It became plain to the people of Yemen, the narrator says, that al-Hadi's affair was advancing and that of his opponents receding; their intentions were renewed and they wished to stand with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116} The narrator stayed at Sa'da with his father, by al-Hadi's order. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.116} See [[event/al-hadi-sends-abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-against-abu-al-dughaysh-285]] and [[event/al-hadi-sets-out-against-al-daam-and-holds-council-285]].
