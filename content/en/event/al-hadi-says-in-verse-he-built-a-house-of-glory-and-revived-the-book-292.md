## Overview
"I rose and was not found wanting, and I spoke counsels, stores of knowledge, if a hearer takes them in" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.302}.

## Detail
He then supposes a hearer saying in his heart that the preacher's words are lost, and answers that the hand's sufficiency in its striving depends on the fingers helping it with deeds. He says "I built you a house of glory whose height is a little below the Pleiades", through which they gained honour, boasts, fame and glory, and that "I raised the Book of God after its death", forbade what the texts of the Book forbid so every house of Muhammad grew tall by his deed, their partisans stand high in every argument, their affair is gathered in the family of Ahmad and their faces shine with the light of their deeds {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.302}. At the head of p. 303 it is because "they revived a Book and a Sunna" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.303}. This is the poet's claim for himself in a Zaydi book; the museum sets no other claim against it and does not accept it as a verdict. It follows [[event/al-hadi-calls-the-family-of-ahmad-to-unite-like-a-torrent-292|the call to the family of Ahmad to unite]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-answers-the-blame-over-giving-and-says-his-land-is-poor-292|his answer to their blame over giving]]. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
