## Overview
A poem in the sari' metre under the heading "and also of his" built on the repeated question "have you a wish for": for the unspoiled nobility that does not wear out, for the station of those who guarded God's sanctuary at Badr, for the resolve of one with intent, for the rising of a man of power, for Paradise, for the Merciful, for repentance before the soul's turning, and for a matter that gives safety from the terror of the resurrection. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}

## Detail
From p. 308 into p. 309, after the heading "and also of his", comes a poem in the sari' metre each of whose lines is a question beginning "have you a wish for": the unspoiled nobility, bright, that does not wear out with time; the station of those who guarded God's sanctuary at [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]]; the resolve of one with intent that is firmly made, clear of doubt; and the rising of a man of power that raises him rank upon rank {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}. Then Paradise, as a need, since it is the best of what is stored, and the Merciful, as a desire, since His command runs over every command {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}. On p. 309 he addresses "the busy one" and asks whether he has a wish for repentance before the soul's turning in the chest, for the return of a repentant who will keep the heat of fire and coals from him, and for a matter which, if he seeks it, brings safety from the terror of the resurrection and the gathering {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.309}. It is a verse exhortation in the form of questions; the book gives it with no named addressee and no occasion, and no fighting or opponent appears in its lines. Badr is named as the example of those who guarded God's sanctuary, an allusion the Zaydi book ascribes to the poet. It follows [[event/al-hadi-offers-his-judgment-or-war-and-names-his-descent-in-verse-292|his verses offering judgment or war]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-sends-word-to-banu-kab-qushayr-and-banu-qurra-in-verse-292|his verse message to Banu Ka'b, Qushayr and Banu Qurra]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
