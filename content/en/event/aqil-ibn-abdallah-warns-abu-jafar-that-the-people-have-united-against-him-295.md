## Overview
After Banu al-Harith gathered in their camp on the Friday night and settled on the ambush, Aqil ibn Abdallah of Najran went to Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah and warned him that the people had united against him. Abu Ja'far answered that he would not leave while he still found a supporter among them, and that he hoped God would give him victory over them even if they all turned against him.

## Detail
Ali ibn Muhammad, who is Abu Ja'far's son and the narrator of the book, reports from Aqil ibn Abdallah that Aqil told Abu Ja'far the people had united against him and that he should save himself, since disgrace is easier to bear than destruction. Abu Ja'far replied that disgrace was by God and by al-Hadi, and that he would not leave until he saw all the people united against him, as long as he could find a supporter among them, so that they could not later say he left them while they still obeyed him. The book then returns to Banu al-Harith, who gathered in their camp on the Friday night, took counsel, and settled that twenty men from each branch would go out and lie in ambush on the side of the village. They did so.
