## Overview
In the setting of the chapter on humility, [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] says he heard [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] preaching to a group and then say: but for fear of spoiling the affair of this community I would give my whole day to them, except for the times of ablution and answering nature's call, nobody but I would lead them in all their prayers, and I would wear the lowest dress. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.54} He said he put on good clothes and began with himself; at times he wished to sit among them, but thinking of the outcome he saw that if he did, people would turn against him, take his place lightly and lose their awe of him, "and if awe went, Islam would be spoiled". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.54} A note in the margin of the original manuscript glosses this as inspiring awe in God's enemies by dress and otherwise; it is a copyist's comment, not the imam's words. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.54}

## The night of the old woman and his words on the outcome
After the story of the old woman al-Hadi says: O God, You know that but for fear of Islam's corruption no one but I would lead them in prayer and I would spend my day with them, going round their markets and setting right their affairs; but I fear that if I am too much among them I shall be small in their eyes, and when they make light of the truth I would have to begin again what I had mended, "because most people in this age do not reason". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.55} This is the imam's verdict on his contemporaries as the narrator gives it, and the museum does not endorse it.

## Food and dress
Meat worth two dirhams was bought for him each day, the dirhams being small ones, a third of a full-weight dirham; and the narrator saw him cut a brocade-lined coat and say: were I among believers I would not wear this, I want only coarse cloth, but if I did people would take my place lightly; I have tested their affairs and find they obey only one wearing such a garment, "and it is as if thorns were on my skin from wearing it". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.56} The margin of the manuscript notes this as "his inspiring awe by dress".
