## Overview
Al-Baladhuri says that Sisar was named because it lies in a hollow between the heads of thirty hillocks, so it was said "thirty heads", and it used to be called Sisar Sakhdaniyya, meaning thirty heads and a hundred springs. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.434}

## The building
Sisar and what surrounded it were pastures for the herds of the Kurds, and there were meadows for the beasts of [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]]. Over it were his client [[person/sulayman-ibn-qayrat|Sulayman ibn Qayrat]] and his partner [[person/salam-al-tayfuri|Salam al-Tayfuri]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

When the vagabonds multiplied in the mountain and made this region a refuge, the two partners wrote to al-Mahdi. He sent an army, and ordered them to build a city where beasts and sheep could take shelter. They built it, fortified it, and settled people in it, and districts from Dinawar and Azerbaijan were added to it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## After al-Mahdi
In the caliphate of [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]] the vagabonds multiplied, so he ordered it repaired and fortified, and placed a thousand men in it. Then he sent [[person/murra-ibn-ali-al-rudhayni|Murra ibn Ali al-Rudhayni]], and it continued to pay its tax by a fixed contract. In the caliphate of al-Ma'mun it was taken out of the hands of his son Asim and returned to the estates of the caliphate. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
