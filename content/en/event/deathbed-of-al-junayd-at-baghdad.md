## Summary
Abu Bakr al-Atawi relates that he was with al-Junayd when he was dying: al-Junayd finished the Qur'an, began Surat al-Baqara, recited seventy verses and died. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008} He was buried in Baghdad in the Shuniziyya cemetery beside al-Sari al-Saqati, and Ibn al-Munadi says the crowd that prayed over him was estimated at about sixty thousand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008}

## Context
Al-Junayd ibn Muhammad was one of the leading Sufi masters of Baghdad; the books differ on the year of his death, between 297 and 298 AH. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 3 p. 148} {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 134} After reporting the accounts, al-Dhahabi says that whoever dated it to 297 was mistaken. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008} Al-Dhahabi quotes Ismail ibn Nujayd as saying that there were three with no fourth: al-Junayd in Baghdad, Abu Uthman in Nishapur and Abu Abd Allah ibn al-Jalla' in Syria. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 25 p. 122}

## His last hours
Al-Atawi says, as the Siyar has it: 'I was with al-Junayd when he was dying; he completed the Qur'an, then began al-Baqara, recited seventy verses and died.' {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008} Al-Dhahabi gives the same report in Tarikh al-Islam. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 25 p. 122} Ibn Khallikan says that at his death he had finished the Qur'an, then started al-Baqara, read seventy verses and died. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 134}
I did not find in the pages I read the text of any prayer or testament he spoke in these hours; what is transmitted is his recitation, and a dream al-Khuldi reports after his death: asked what God had done with him, he answered that the allusions, the phrases, the sciences and the traces had all gone, and nothing had benefited him except the few prayer-cycles he used to pray in the last part of the night. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 25 p. 122} This is a reported dream, not a firm report.

## Funeral and burial
Ibn al-Munadi said they estimated those who prayed over him on the day of his funeral at about sixty thousand, and that people kept coming to his grave every day for about a month. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008} He was buried beside al-Sari al-Saqati. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008} Ibn Khallikan adds that he was buried on Saturday at the Shuniziyya beside al-Sari al-Saqati. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 134}

## The grave
Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, in his entry on al-Sari, says he was buried in the Shuniziyya cemetery, that his grave is visible and well known, and that beside it is the grave of al-Junayd. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib scan vol. 1 p. 197} A note in Tarikh al-Islam says the Shuniziyya is a known place in Baghdad with a famous cemetery where masters of the Sufi path are buried, among them al-Sari al-Saqati and al-Junayd. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 29 p. 327}

## Where the reports differ
The books differ on the year of death, 297 or 298, and al-Dhahabi prefers the second. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 134} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1008} Ibn Khallikan gives Saturday as the day of burial. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 134} In these books I found no bequest of property and no description of his washing or shroud.
