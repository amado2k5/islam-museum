## Summary
[[place/dinawar|Dinawar]], al-Saymara and what lay beyond them were taken in 21 AH in the wake of [[event/battle-of-nahawand|Nahavand]], when Abu Musa al-Ash'ari turned back from Nahavand and settled over Dinawar until its people accepted the jizya and kharaj — part of the Muslims' sweep through the Persian lands authorised by [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|'Umar ibn al-Khattab]]. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 399} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 314}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri relates that Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, who had marched in person to Nahavand as reinforcement for al-Nu'man ibn Muqarrin, returned and moved on Dinawar, where he stayed five days; fighting lasted one day, after which the people acknowledged the jizya and kharaj and asked for safety over their lives, property and children, which he granted, leaving an agent there with cavalry {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 314}. He then went on to Masabadhan, whose people did not fight, while the people of Sirawan made peace on the same terms as Dinawar; and he sent 'Abdallah ibn Qays al-Ash'ari and al-Sa'ib ibn al-Aqra' al-Thaqafi to al-Saymara, the city of Mihrajanqadhaq, which they took by treaty on the sparing of blood and the leaving of captives {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 314}. This formed part of the sweep for which the Kufans had asked 'Umar's leave after Nahavand, when commanders were dispatched from Basra and Kufa {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 399}. Later, in Mu'awiya's caliphate, Dinawar was assigned to the people of Kufa when they grew numerous, so that Nahavand was called Mah al-Basra and Dinawar Mah al-Kufa {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 313}.
