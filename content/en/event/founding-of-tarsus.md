## Overview
Al-Baladhuri, from al-Waqidi, reports that [[person/al-hasan-ibn-qahtaba|al-Hasan ibn Qahtaba]] raided the land of the Romans in 162 AH, and informed [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] of the value of building and fortifying [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.231-233}

## Exploration
Al-Hasan distinguished himself in that raid, so that they called him "the devil". When he left the land of the Romans, he looked at the city and it was in ruins, and he estimated the number of those who would live in it at a hundred thousand. Al-Mahdi ordered him to begin with the city of al-Hadath, and advised that Tarsus be built. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The building
In 171 AH word reached [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]] that the Romans had resolved to go out against Tarsus, so he sent the summer raid under [[person/harthama-ibn-ayan|Harthama ibn Ayan]] and ordered him to build and fortify it. The matter was carried out by the hand of [[person/faraj-ibn-sulaym|Faraj ibn Sulaym]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Faraj brought the first levy from the people of Khurasan, three thousand men, then a second, two thousand men from Massisa and Antioch. He surveyed the land between river and river and it came to four thousand plots, each twenty cubits by the same. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The two levies settled in Tarsus in the middle of Rabi al-Akhir 172 AH, and the building of the mosque was completed. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.233}

The conversion to the Gregorian calendar is derived.
