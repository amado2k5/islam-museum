## Summary
In Dhu al-Qa'da 420 AH the caliphal court arrested the preacher of the Baratha mosque because of extremism in his sermon. In his place Abu Mansur ibn Tammam gave a sermon closer to the middle course, and those present pelted him with clods of earth until his shoulder was broken. A decree from [[person/al-qadir-billah|al-Qadir]] went out in anger, likening the mosque to the mosque of harm, and the Friday prayer was suspended there for weeks before it resumed with a sermon written in an official hand. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.383-387}

## The sermon and the stoning
The substitute preacher gave the Friday sermon on the nineteenth of Dhu al-Qa'da. He lessened the mention of Ali ibn Abi Talib and closed with the words, "O God, forgive the Muslims, and whoever claims that Ali is his mawla." The common people rose up and threw clods of earth at him; his shoulder and teeth were broken, and blood ran down his face. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.384-385}

## The caliph's decree
The decree from al-Qadir came in harsh terms. It described the mosque as a gathering of "unbelievers and heretics" and said it was much like the mosque of harm. The house of the former preacher was attacked by about thirty men. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.385}

## The weeks without Friday prayer
The Friday prayer was suspended for weeks, until the day of 'Id al-Nahr came, when the muezzin garbled the takbir and panic spread among the worshippers. Then the elders of al-Karkh and [[person/al-sharif-al-murtada|al-Sharif al-Murtada]] interceded, and the preaching was resumed on the first of Muharram from a written text. The preacher was excused from striking the pulpit with a sword and from mentioning "his mawla." {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.385-387}
