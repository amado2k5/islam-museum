## Summary
[[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] used to consult [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] on legal cases; sometimes Ali would correct his ruling, and sometimes he would advise him. al-Sallabi cites examples involving fixed penalties and marriage, and notes that Umar said: "I seek refuge in God from a dilemma for which Abu al-Hasan is not present" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.142}.

## The Insane Woman
A woman who had committed adultery was brought to Umar and he ordered her stoned, but Ali sent her back and said: "The pen is lifted from three: the sleeper until he wakes, the child until he comes of age, and the afflicted until he regains reason"—this woman is afflicted among such-and-such clan, and perhaps he approached her while she was in that state. So Umar did not stone her {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.142}. The book notes that the report has a weakness in its chain of transmission according to some hadith scholars {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.142}.

## The Pregnant Woman
A pregnant woman who had confessed to immorality was brought to Umar and he ordered her stoned, so Ali sent her back; Umar asked him, "Did I not order her stoned?" He replied, "Yes." Ali said, "Perhaps you rebuked her or frightened her?" and then asked, "What authority do you have over what is in her womb?" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.143}. He narrated that the Prophet said: "There is no fixed penalty upon one who confesses after undergoing ordeal," so Umar released her and said: "Women are incapable of giving birth to the like of Ali ibn Abi Talib; were it not for Ali, Umar would have perished" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.143}. The book comments by quoting Ibn Taymiyyah that if the leader did not know of the woman's pregnancy, Ali's informing him was part of his informing him about people's circumstances {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.143-144}.

## Ignorant Practices and the Sunnah
A woman married during her waiting period, so Umar separated the couple and placed her marriage gift in the public treasury. When this reached Ali, he said: if they were ignorant of the Sunnah, she is entitled to the dower for what was made lawful of her, and they are separated; once her waiting period ends, he may be a suitor. Umar then addressed the people: "Return ignorant practices to the Sunnah," and adopted Ali's view {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.144}.

## The Woman with the Egg White
A woman claimed that a young man had made advances toward her, and there was a trace of semen on her garment. Umar was about to punish him, when Ali called for hot water and poured it over the garment, causing the white substance to coagulate; he then tasted it, recognized the flavor of egg, and rebuked the woman, whereupon she confessed {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.144}. The book concludes from the incident that methods of proof in adjudication included circumstantial indicators and discernment, and that Umar used to consult senior Companions {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.144}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not date these cases; the timeframe is derived from Umar's caliphate.
- Most of the reports in this chapter of the book are drawn from works of jurisprudence and Hadith cited by the book and require independent verification.
