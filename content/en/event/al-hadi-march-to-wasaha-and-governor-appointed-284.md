## Overview
The book says that after [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] returned from Najran to [[place/saada|Sa'da]] he set out on Tuesday the eighth of Shawwal for a town called [[place/wasaha-yemen|Wasaha]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.80} It is a Zaydi account, and the "awe" is the narrator's word for the imam.

## The place and the story of Al Ya'fur
The editor's footnote says Wasaha is also written "Washaha" and lies near Harad. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.80} It is in rough, harsh mountains that no ruler had entered but a sultan of [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]]; Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Yarsami and others the narrator trusts told him that thousands gathered with that sultan and a man called [[person/al-bahri-of-banu-bahr|al-Bahri]] of Banu Bahr and his clan met them and killed them, fewer than a hundred escaping, because the land is rugged, with great mountains and hard to approach. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.80}

## Entry and stay
Al-Hadi entered it "with the awe God set in him", and no one met him except with hearing and obedience; he entered the town, its people pledged to him, he gathered those who had seized control of parts of it and stayed eight days, and those near it obeyed him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.81} He then left for Sa'da, with al-Bahri, who had killed Al Ya'fur's army, going before him, and Abu al-Dughaysh al-Shihabi and the two sons of Rida, who were the men who used to levy the town, and he reached Sa'da with them, leaving "Abu Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah al-Alawi" in Wasaha. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.81} This is evidently the narrator [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]], since pp. 81 and 85 give "Abu Muhammad" and "Abu Jafar" for one governor of Wasaha. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.81} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.85}

## The return
Al-Hadi reached Sa'da on Saturday and stayed there for the rest of Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram and twenty-two days of Safar. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.81}
