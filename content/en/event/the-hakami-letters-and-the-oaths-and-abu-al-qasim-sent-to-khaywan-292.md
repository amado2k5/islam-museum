## Overview
After al-Hadi settled at Sa'da, letters reached him from [[person/ibrahim-ibn-ali-al-hakami|Ibrahim ibn Ali]] and [[person/al-ghatrif-al-hakami|al-Ghatrif]], the two Hakamis, asking him to come to their country, for they would hand over to him what they held of money, troops and the district, and they gave him their pledges {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.295}. The margin of the original says: "letters of the Hakamis to al-Hadi" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.295}.

## Detail
He would not agree, knowing the fickleness of the people of Yemen and their small loyalty, and he stayed so until they sent a body of their trusted men to bind the argument on him and asked him to send some of his own trusted men to take their oath to what they had given of themselves {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.295}. He sent a number of his trusted men, who took their oaths and came back and told him how the people stood; when what they had given of themselves was certain to him he sent criers among Khawlan and a large force gathered to him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.295}. He sent his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] to [[place/khiwan|Khaywan]] with orders to stay there, and when his letters came from Zabid he was to march "in the war against the Qarmatians" to Sana'a with Hamdan, to learn their bedouin and gather what horsemen he could; Abu al-Qasim went and reached Khaywan {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.295}. The account makes al-Hadi's wariness of the Yemenis the reason for his first refusal and the oath the reason he accepted; that is the judgement of the imam's supporter in a Zaydi book, not the museum's judgement of the people of Yemen. The "Qaramita" are a group with [[group/qaramita|their own record]], and the text does not spell out their affair here. The rest of the account of al-Hadi and Tihama comes after this page in the next batch, from the top of p. 296. The event is joined to [[event/al-hadi-leaves-najran-for-saada-in-dhu-al-qada-292|al-Hadi's going to Sa'da]].
