## Summary
In 235 AH Itakh al-Khazari, al-Mutawakkil's Turkish commander, was killed on his return from Mecca to Iraq, after leading the pilgrimage as governor of Mecca, Medina and the festival season. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.9 p.168}

## Details
Al-Tabari relates that Itakh had been a Khazar slave of Sallam al-Abrash the cook; al-Mu'tasim bought him in 199 AH, and as he had vigor and courage al-Mu'tasim, then al-Wathiq after him, raised him until many of the caliphate's functions were gathered into his hands. Al-Mu'tasim made him co-governor of Samarra, and whoever al-Mu'tasim or al-Wathiq wished killed was killed at Itakh's hands; Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik al-Zayyat and the sons of al-Ma'mun were imprisoned by him. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.9 p.168}

When al-Mutawakkil acceded, Itakh retained his rank over the army, the Maghariba, the Turks, the mawali, the postal service, the chamberlains and the caliphal palace. He led the pilgrimage in 234 AH as governor of Mecca, Medina and the season, with the funeral eulogy pronounced for him from the pulpits. As he turned back from Mecca toward Iraq, al-Mutawakkil sent Sa'id ibn Salih al-Hajib to meet him at Kufa or along his road, having already instructed his police chief in Baghdad, Ishaq ibn Ibrahim; Itakh was brought into Baghdad, seized, and killed in 235 AH. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.9 p.168}
