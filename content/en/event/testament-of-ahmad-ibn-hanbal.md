## Summary
This record gathers what is reported about the written will of [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]], his last days, his shroud and estate, and the hairs of the Prophet he asked to have placed on him. It complements [[event/death-of-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|the record of his death and funeral]], which covers the date of death, the prayer over him and the size of the crowds. He died on Friday, twelve nights into Rabi' al-Awwal 241 AH, in the accounts of his sons Salih and Abd Allah. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 964} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 965}

## How the will came about
Abu Bakr al-Marwazi relates that Abu Abd Allah woke him one night after fasting continuously and said, "Here I am being turned round by hunger; feed me something"; he brought him less than a loaf and he ate it. He would rise to relieve himself and rest, and sat down from weakness, so that al-Marwazi wet a cloth and laid it on his face to revive him; and he made his will while they were at al-Askar and called witnesses to it. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 964} The report gives no date for the dictation.

## The text of the will and its content
Salih relates that when his father's illness grew severe he said to him, "Read me the will," and he read it, and his father approved it as it stood. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 326} Ibn Asakir then gives its opening: in the name of God, this is what Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal bequeathed, that he testifies there is no god but God alone with no partner and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger, and then describes his sending in a Qur'anic phrase that can be consulted at [[passage/quran-9-33|Qur'an 9:33]]; he enjoined those of his family and kin who obeyed him to worship God among the worshippers, praise Him among the praisers and give sincere counsel to the community of Muslims, and he declared himself content with God as Lord, Islam as religion and Muhammad as Prophet. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 326} Al-Dhahabi says that Abd Allah ibn Ahmad transmitted this will and that it included, besides the testimony, a statement of debt: that he owed Fuwaran about fifty dinars, that she was to be believed in what she said, that it be paid from the income of the house, and that when it was paid the house passed to the children of Abd Allah and Salih, sons of Ahmad. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 950} The machine reading of that page is interleaved with another column, so only this clear part is relied on.

## His last days
Abd Allah relates that when his father's illness grew severe he groaned, and when asked about it said he had heard from Tawus that a sick man's groaning is a complaint to God; he did not groan again until he died. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 325} He says that the day before his death his father took from his pocket a small purse with about two silver dirhams and said: expiate for me a single oath's expiation, for I think I once broke an oath in my whole life. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 325} Salih adds that on Tuesday his father told him to look in his purse, where there was one dirham, and to send and buy dates and expiate an oath for him; a third of a dirham or so remained of the price of the dates, he told him, and he said, "Praise be to God," and, "Read me the will." {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 326} Salih also relates that when death came to his father he sat by him with a cloth to bind his jaws; Ahmad would sweat, revive, open his eyes and say with his hand "not yet, not yet" three times, and when asked said that Iblis stood in front of him biting his fingers and saying, "Ahmad, you have escaped me," and he answered, "Not until I die." {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 324} Salih says his mind remained clear until he died. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 964}

## The hairs of the Prophet
Al-Khallal relates from Isma ibn Isam from Hanbal that a son of al-Fadl ibn al-Rabi' gave Abu Abd Allah, while he was in prison, three hairs, saying they were of the Prophet's hair; at his death he ordered that one hair be placed on each eye and one on his tongue, and this was done. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 965} The report is given with its chain in the biographical praise literature; the museum attributes it to its narrator and does not verify the origin of the hairs.

## Washing and shroud
Salih relates that Ibn Tahir sent his chamberlain Muzaffar with two boys carrying cloths holding garments and perfume, saying the governor sent greetings and had done what the Commander of the Faithful would have done had he been present; Salih answered: tell the governor that the Commander of the Faithful excused Abu Abd Allah in his life from what he disliked, and I do not wish to follow him after death with what he disliked. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 965} The maid had spun a cloth of ten cubits valued at twenty-eight dirhams from which two shirts were to be cut; two wrappers were cut from it and a third taken from Fuwaran, so that he was wrapped in three; perfume was bought for him, his washing and shrouding were completed, and about a hundred of the Hashimites were present, kissing his forehead until he was lifted onto the bier. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 965} Elsewhere the report says the maid spun a large cloth and he said, "Do not cut it, leave it," and that became his shroud. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 964} The report does not say who washed him.

## What he left: his children and estate
Al-Dhahabi says Ahmad's eldest son Salih became judge of Isfahan and died there in 265 AH aged sixty-odd; his son Abd Allah, who transmitted his father's words, died in 290 AH aged seventy-seven; Sa'id was born to Ahmad fifty days before his death; and nothing has reached him of the circumstances of Hasan, Muhammad and Zaynab. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 964} The sources read mention no property beyond the house whose income was to pay his debt and the few small coins in his purse. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 950} {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 326}

## The grave
[[event/death-of-ahmad-ibn-hanbal|The record of his death]] covers the prayer over him and his burial after the afternoon prayer; the sources read here add nothing to fix the grave's site.

## Where the reports differ
Al-Marwazi's account and Abd Allah's differ in the occasion of the will: in the first he was at al-Askar, weak from fasting and hunger before the illness {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 964}, in the second Salih read it to him in his final illness and he approved it {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 vol. 5 p. 326}. Neither al-Dhahabi nor Ibn Asakir says whether it is one will spoken on both occasions. Al-Dhahabi also notes that Ibn Qani' erred in putting his death in Rabi' al-Akhir. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 965}
