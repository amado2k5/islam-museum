## Summary
At Siffin, Ali gained the upper hand over Amr ibn al-As when his private parts became exposed, so Ali turned away from him and did not kill him {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.35}.

## The Incident as Narrated by the Book
Al-Aqqad states that Amr ibn al-As was lying on the ground with his private parts exposed, careless of what protection came to hand to ward off death, so Ali turned his face away from him and did not wish to strike down a man who feared death to that degree {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.35}. He says that had Ali had the chance to finish off Amr, he would have known he was eliminating a seedbed of hostility and cunning, yet he paid no heed to that {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.35}.

## Place of the Incident in the Book
Al-Aqqad regards this chivalry as among the rarest examples of a warrior's chivalry in the heat of battle, and links it to Ali's freedom from rancor toward the most hostile of people to him and the most notorious for bearing malice against him {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.25}. He states that Amr, in his view, was more dangerous to Ali than an equipped army {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.25}.

## The Author's Reading
Al-Aqqad considers that a knight does not strike an exposed enemy who fears death to such a degree, because doing so does not befit his standing in the arena of conflict {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.35}. This is a literary reading of chivalry, not a report with a verified chain of transmission; the book does not mention a chain for the report, so in the museum the report remains attributed to the author.
