## Overview
At the beginning of 337/948 al-Mutanabbi established himself at the Hamdanid court in Aleppo as official poet of the amir Sayf al-Dawla, staying nine years. He was genuinely attached to this patron, in his eyes the ideal Arab chief — brave, magnanimous, generous — who in turn overwhelmed him with gifts and never treated him with arrogance. The poet accompanied his expeditions and sang his exploits against the Byzantines and the desert Bedouins; Eastern critics generally hold the poems of this period the summit of his art {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 770}.
