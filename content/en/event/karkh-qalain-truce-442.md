## Summary
In 442 AH a remarkable truce was concluded in Baghdad between the people of [[place/al-karkh|al-Karkh]] and the Sunnis, after a season of killing around the newly built gates. The two sides mixed and went out together to visit the two shrines. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.487-488}

## The scene
Ibn al-Nasawi was appointed to cross to the west bank to keep the city under control. The common people of al-Karkh, al-Qalayin, Bab al-Sha'ir and Bab al-Basra agreed on one position: if he crossed, they would burn their markets and withdraw from the city. The people of al-Karkh went to the gate of the al-Qalayin canal and prayed there, giving the call to prayer with the phrase "Hasten to the best of deeds"; the people of al-Qalayin answered with "Prayer is better than sleep." The two sides made peace, and the people of al-Karkh openly prayed for mercy on the Companions. In Dhu al-Hijja the two groups went out to visit the shrines of Ali and al-Husayn. The people of al-Qalayin raised a black banner bearing the name of the caliph, and the people of al-Karkh brought out gilded, multicoloured floats in the shape of mangonels. Some Turks and Sunnis who were not used to such visits also came out, and people scattered coins over them. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.487-488}
