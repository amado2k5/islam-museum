## Overview
"You resented us over the giving, so hear", and then he says that he gives of his life and his wealth "before you", stores no treasures and keeps nothing back when he gives {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.303}.

## Detail
He says he guards their lineages and wards off from them, that he does not pass the night at ease while his neighbour is poor and hungry, that he does not seek what is not lawful, that he lived among them for ages, that "by God's praise and the right I speak out", and that were the whole earth of God his own he would give it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.304}. Then: "cousins, I am in a mean land", of little produce, its harm continuous, with no wealth to cover a part of its needs, its inhabitant bare, hungry and famished; he asks them to ask the people about it, says they have forgotten his defending them, that if they do not repay him they should at least be kind, that the world turns with its people so they should not despair of him, that time has states that turn its people about, and that whoever is in one thing awaits its opposite, and ends "God's peace on you while the sun rises" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.304}. A footnote says that manuscript S reads "contends" for "awaits" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.304}. The book gives here no independent witness of the giving or of the land's poverty, so these remain the poet's words in a Zaydi book, which the museum neither endorses nor rejects. It follows [[event/al-hadi-says-in-verse-he-built-a-house-of-glory-and-revived-the-book-292|his saying that he built a house of glory]] and precedes [[event/al-hadi-warns-the-unjust-governors-in-verse-and-names-the-yemeni-regiments-292|his verses on the unjust governors]]. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
