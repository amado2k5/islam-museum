## Summary
In his final illness [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] left no single written testament that gathers everything. The books instead report short sayings scattered among different narrators, each of whom kept what he or she heard. This page brings the best known together, assigns each to its narrator and book, and leaves unspoken what the sources leave unspoken. For his last days see [[event/death-of-the-prophet|The death of the Prophet]], and for the request for writing materials see [[event/prophets-deathbed-writing-request|The Prophet's request for writing in his illness]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 263}

## Prayer and those in one's charge
Ibn Kathir reports from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]] that most of the Prophet's counsel when death came was "prayer, and what your right hands possess", until he was gurgling it and his tongue could scarcely bring it out; he notes that al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah and Ahmad transmit it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 263} [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama]] reports the same, and Ibn Kathir adds that al-Bayhaqi preferred the route of Humam from Qatada from Abu al-Khalil from Safina from her. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 264} In Ahmad's collection [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] says that the Prophet told him to bring a tablet on which to write what would keep his community from going astray; Ali feared the Prophet's life would pass first and said he would remember and retain it, and the Prophet said: "I counsel prayer, zakat and what your right hands possess." Ibn Kathir says Ahmad alone has it by this route. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 264} His counsel for the weak is treated on its own page, [[event/prophets-counsel-on-copts-women-and-those-in-bondage|The Prophet's counsel on the Copts, women and those in bondage]].

## The three counsels of the Thursday
[[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] reports that the pain grew heavy on the Prophet on the Thursday and he asked for a shoulder-bone to write a document after which they would never go astray; they fell into dispute, some asking what was wrong with him and to question him. He said: "Leave me, for what I am in is better than what you call me to", and he commanded three things: "Expel the polytheists from the Arabian Peninsula, and reward the delegations as I used to reward them." The narrator says he fell silent about the third, or that it was said and he forgot it. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 132} Al-Bayhaqi has the same report in the Dala'il, and the editor's note says the one who fell silent was Ibn Abbas and the one who forgot was Sa'id ibn Jubayr. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3309} Ibn Hajar explains that the reward was a gift, which in the Prophet's day was an uqiyya of silver, forty dirhams. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 135}

### What was the third?
The commentators differ. Ibn Hajar allows that the one who forgot was Sa'id ibn Jubayr, then adds that al-Isma'ili states it was Ibn Uyayna, and that al-Humaydi's Musnad has Sufyan reporting from Sulayman that he did not know whether Sa'id mentioned the third and he forgot it, or kept silent about it; Ibn Hajar judges that the stronger. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 135} He reports that al-Dawudi said it was the counsel about the Qur'an; that al-Muhallab said it was equipping the army of Usama, which Ibn Battal strengthened by Abu Bakr's word to the Companions, when they disagreed with him over sending it, that the Prophet had ordered it at his death; and that Iyad allowed it might be "do not make my grave an idol", since the Muwatta joins it with the expulsion of the Jews, or the "prayer and what your right hands possess" in Anas's report. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 135} Al-Bayhaqi adds, in the same entry, that Sufyan ibn Uyayna said people claimed that what he meant to write was the succession of Abu Bakr. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3309} No one of them is certain; all are interpretations. See [[event/expedition-of-usama|The expedition of Usama]].

### The Peninsula and the narrator's wording
In the note to the Dala'il, Abu Ubayd, quoting al-Asma'i, defines the Arabian Peninsula as running from the far end of Aden to the villages of Iraq in length and from Jidda to the borders of Syria in width, and he gives a second view from Abu Ubayda. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3309} In the Fath, Ya'qub ibn Muhammad asks al-Mughira ibn Abd al-Rahman about it and he answers: Mecca, Medina, al-Yamama and Yemen. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 197} In al-Jurjani's transmission of al-Bukhari the wording is "expel the Jews" instead of "the polytheists", and Ibn Hajar says the former is better established. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 313} See [[place/jazirat-al-arab|The Arabian Peninsula]].

## "Two religions must not remain in the land of the Arabs"
Ibn Kathir reports from the Muwatta, through Isma'il ibn Abi Hakim, that he heard [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] say that among the last things the Prophet said were: "God fight the Jews and Christians, who took the graves of their prophets as places of prayer; two religions shall not remain in the land of the Arabs." Ibn Kathir notes that Malik transmitted it as a mursal report from Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 263} See also [[passage/muwatta-31-2606|Muwatta 2606]] {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.470} and [[event/prophets-warning-about-graves-in-final-illness|The Prophet's warning about graves in his final illness]].

## Thinking well of God
Ibn Kathir reports, through al-Bayhaqi, that [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abdullah]] heard the Prophet say, three days before his death: "Think well of God." He gives Muslim's wording from Jabir, "None of you should die except thinking well of God", and sets beside it the hadith "I am as My servant thinks of Me" from the two Sahihs. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 263}

## What he told Fatima in confidence
[[person/aisha|Aisha]] reports in the two Sahihs that [[person/fatima|Fatima]] came walking, her walk no different from her father's; he welcomed her, seated her and spoke to her in private, and she wept; then he spoke again and she laughed. After his death Aisha asked her, and she said that he had told her Gabriel used to review the Qur'an with him once a year and had reviewed it twice that year, and that he thought it could only mean his term was near; he told her to fear God and be patient, and that he was an excellent forerunner for her, so she wept; then he told her she was the chief of the believing women, and she laughed. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 250} Ibn Hajar notes that in the other report, Urwa's, he told her she would be the first of his family to follow him, while in Masruq's report she is the chief of the women of Paradise; he judges that both were said, since Masruq's report has additions to Urwa's and Urwa is a reliable, exact narrator. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 135}

## Where the reports differ
- These counsels come from different witnesses, and Ibn Kathir fixes no particular day for each in these passages. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 263}
- The counsel on prayer and those in bondage comes from Anas, Umm Salama and Ali, and Ibn Kathir notes a difference over the chain of Umm Salama's report. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 264}
- The content of the third counsel of the Thursday is not certain; the three views are given above. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 135}
