## Overview
After that [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] ordered his brother and his son out with an army to Hadda and Safa', where they fell on the men unawares; the men learned of it, came out of all their camps and the fight closed, then they turned their backs and each went home {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.240}. Al-Hadi then stayed some days until a reinforcement from the Ja'fari reached [[person/abu-al-ushayra-ibn-al-ruwayya|Abu al-Ushayra ibn al-Ruwayya]], who marched with them and his own army until he entered the fort of Ziyad, killed men in it and razed it; he wrote to al-Hadi of his arrival there and asked him to send his brother [[person/rabi-ibn-al-ruwayya|Rabi' ibn al-Ruwayya]] with the army he had with him and to add horsemen of the Ja'fari who were at Sana'a {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.240}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi ordered Rabi' to go to his brother with those asked for, and when he reached him they went on, with the part of al-Hadi's army that had been at [[place/sabal-sanaa-yemen|Sabal]], until they settled beside [[place/ghayman-sanaa-yemen|Ghayman]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.240}. The name "the Ja'fari" is as the book gives it and does not say who he is. The marginal heading on p. 233 mentions the coming of Rabi' ibn al-Ruwayya with the Ja'fari aid; the pages read here appear to lead to it but do not settle it, and this is noted in the lane ledger. Nor does the book say whether the "Ibn al-Ruwayya" of later reports is Abu al-Ushayra or Rabi'.
