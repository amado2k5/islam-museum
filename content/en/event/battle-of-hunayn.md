## Summary
A major battle fought in Shawwal 8 AH (January 630 CE) in [[place/wadi-hunayn|Wadi Hunayn]], between Mecca and al-Ta'if. The Muslim army of twelve thousand fighters faced the combined tribes of [[group/hawazin|Hawazin]] and [[group/thaqif|Thaqif]] under [[person/malik-ibn-awf|Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/80-84}. The polytheists lay in ambush, and the Muslims' cavalry broke at first light. Then the Prophet and a few of his Companions stood firm, the Muslims rallied, and they won {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/85-89}.

## Muster and preparation
Malik ibn Awf gathered Hawazin and Thaqif and brought their fighters' property, women and children with them so that they would not flee {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/80-81}. The experienced horseman [[person/durayd-ibn-al-simma|Durayd ibn al-Simma]] objected at Awtas: "Does anything turn back one who has fled? If the day is yours, only a man with his sword will avail you; if it goes against you, you will be disgraced before your families" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/81}.

When the Prophet learned of their gathering, he sent [[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-hadrad|Abdullah ibn Abi Hadrad al-Aslami]] as a scout to bring him their news {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/83}. The Prophet borrowed a hundred coats of mail with their gear from [[person/safwan-ibn-umayya|Safwan ibn Umayya]] as a guaranteed loan. He set out with twelve thousand fighters and appointed Attab ibn Asid over Mecca {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/84}.

## The ambush and the Prophet's stand
The Muslims went down into Wadi Hunayn in the grey of dawn. The battalions of Hawazin surprised them with a volley of arrows from the gullies and narrow passes of the valley, and the horsemen and the people turned back in flight {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/85-86}.

The Prophet withdrew to the right on his white mule, with a small band that included [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]], Usama ibn Zayd and [[person/ayman-ibn-ubayd|Ayman ibn Ubayd]]. He called out: "I am the Prophet, no lie; I am the son of Abd al-Muttalib" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/87-88}. He ordered al-Abbas to call out in his loud voice: "O people of the acacia tree! O people of the chapter of al-Baqara!" and the Muslims hurried back {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/88-89}.

## Close combat and victory
The two armies closed and the fighting grew fierce. The Prophet said: "Now the oven is heated," then threw pebbles at the faces of the polytheists, saying: "May the faces be disfigured." Hawazin and Thaqif were routed {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/89}. Thaqif fled to al-Ta'if under [[person/qarib-ibn-al-aswad|Qarib ibn al-Aswad]], Hawazin withdrew to [[place/awtas|Awtas]], and the Muslims seized their property and captives {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/90-93}.

## Along the road and the captives
The Prophet left Mecca on Saturday 6 Shawwal 8 AH with twelve thousand: ten thousand who had taken Mecca and two thousand of its people. He borrowed a hundred coats of mail with their gear from Safwan ibn Umayya {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.276}. On the road the army passed a tree called Dhat Anwat, and the well-known [[event/incident-of-dhat-anwat|story of Dhat Anwat]] took place. [[person/anas-ibn-abi-marthad|Anas ibn Abi Marthad al-Ghanawi]] volunteered to keep watch that night {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.277}.

Some of the army, seeing how large it was, said: "We will not be defeated today for lack of numbers." This displeased the Prophet {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.277}. When the Muslims were first routed, Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, who was recently converted, said that their rout would not end before the sea. Jabala or Kalda ibn al-Junayd cried out: "Sorcery has been undone today" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.277}.

There were six thousand captives, among them [[person/al-shayma-bint-al-harith|al-Shayma bint al-Harith al-Sa'diyya]], the Prophet's foster-sister. She made herself known to him, he honoured her, spread out his cloak for her and sent her back to her people {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.278}. The spoils were held at al-Ji'rana: twenty-four thousand camels, more than forty thousand sheep and four thousand uqiyya of silver. Mas'ud ibn Amr al-Ghifari was put in charge of them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.278}.

## Significance and legislation
The battle set down important principles. It broke the last armed polytheist gathering in the Hijaz. In it the Prophet forbade killing women, children and the 'usafa' (hired labourers) {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/95-96}. Qur'anic guidance came down drawing the lesson of pride in numbers, in [[passage/quran-9-25|سورة التوبة 25–26]].

## For young visitors
The Muslims went out with a large army, and the enemy surprised them with an ambush in the valley early in the morning, so some of them fell back. But the Prophet stood firm and called to his Companions bravely; they came back and won. Afterwards he forgave many of those who had fought him.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The speaker says Hunayn was the battle in which the Muslims outnumbered the enemy and were nonetheless defeated. He gives the figures as about twelve thousand Muslims and four thousand of the enemy (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:24:30}.

He says the Muslims fought about twenty-eight expeditions in the Prophet's lifetime and were defeated only at the start of Hunayn (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:30:30}. The speaker's view (opinion): the tide turned when the Muslims understood "there is no god but God". This is his reading of the event, not a historical report {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:31:00}.

He recounts that the army fled from around the Prophet at Hunayn, and sets this beside the archers at Uhud who left the gap that Khalid ibn al-Walid then entered {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:45:30}.

He says Abu Sufyan had noble conduct before Islam, and that Islam added to it: on the day of Hunayn a word of his steadied the people {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:46:30}.

He returns to the sizes of the armies: about twelve thousand Muslims, of whom ten thousand were from Medina at the conquest of Mecca, against about four thousand of Hawazin (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 01:15:30}.
## For Sprouts
In year 8 of the Hijra the Muslims set out from Mecca with a large army, and in the valley of Hunayn between Mecca and Ta'if they met the armies of the tribes Hawazin and Thaqif. The enemy's ambush worked at first, but the Prophet stood firm and called the Muslims back, and they returned and won. From the spoils of this battle the Prophet gave gifts to many people to soften their hearts.

## For Explorers
The battle of Hunayn was fought in Shawwal of year 8 AH (January 630 CE) in the valley of Hunayn between Mecca and al-Ta'if. Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri gathered the tribes Hawazin and Thaqif and brought their wealth and women so they would fight to the end. The Muslims, twelve thousand strong, came down into the valley at dawn, and the tribes lay in ambush in the passes and shot at them with arrows; the Muslim cavalry broke in the early morning. The Prophet stood firm with a group of Companions and called the people to him, and the Muslims returned and fought until they won. The captives and spoils were gathered at al-Ji'rana, and the Prophet divided them and gave gifts to reconcile hearts; the sira recalls that a verse was revealed about this day.

## For Scholars
The battle of Hunayn was one of the major engagements after the conquest of Mecca, fought in Shawwal of year 8 AH (January 630 CE) in the valley of Hunayn between Mecca and al-Ta'if. Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri rallied Hawazin and Thaqif and mustered their property, women and children so the fight would be decisive; the sira books record his saying that they would not be defeated with their wealth and families at stake. The Muslim army numbered twelve thousand after two thousand Meccans joined, and sources relate that some were impressed by their own numbers. Descending into the valley before dawn, the Muslims were ambushed in its narrows; the cavalry collapsed under the arrows and the line broke. The Prophet stood firm with al-Abbas, Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith and a group of Companions, and al-Abbas called out to the men of the tree and of the pledge, and the Muslims rallied around him until the polytheists fled, leaving wealth and captives. Spoils and prisoners were gathered at al-Ji'rana; the Hawazin delegation accepted Islam and their kin were returned, and the Prophet gave shares of the spoils to reconcile hearts. The sira records that the Qur'an spoke of this day, and historians count Hunayn the last great battle between the Prophet and the great Hijazi tribes.
