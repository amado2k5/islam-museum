## Summary
A great battle fought in Shawwal 8 AH (January 630 CE) in [[place/wadi-hunayn|Wadi Hunayn]] between Mecca and al-Ta'if, in which the Muslim army — twelve thousand fighters — faced the hosts of [[group/hawazin|Hawazin]] and [[group/thaqif|Thaqif]] under [[person/malik-ibn-awf|Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/80-84}. The polytheists ambushed the Muslims and broke their cavalry early in the morning; then the Prophet stood firm with a few of his Companions, the Muslims wheeled back, and they won {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/85-89}.

## Muster and preparation
Malik ibn Awf mustered Hawazin and Thaqif and drove the fighters' wealth, women and children with them so they would not flee {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/80-81}. The veteran horseman [[person/durayd-ibn-al-simma|Durayd ibn al-Simma]] reproached him for it at Awtas, saying: "does anything turn back the routed? If the day is yours, only a man with his sword avails you; and if it is against you, you are disgraced before your family" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/81}.

The Prophet learned of their gathering and sent [[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-hadrad|Abdullah ibn Abi Hadrad al-Aslami]] as a scout to bring him word of them {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/83}. The Prophet borrowed a hundred coats of mail with their arms from [[person/safwan-ibn-umayya|Safwan ibn Umayya]] as a guaranteed loan, and marched out with twelve thousand fighters, leaving Attab ibn Asid in charge of Mecca {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/84}.

## The ambush and the Prophet's stand
The Muslims descended into Wadi Hunayn in the darkness of dawn, and Hawazin's battalions surprised them with arrows and a fierce volley from the valley's gorges and narrows; horses and men turned back in flight {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/85-86}.

The Prophet took the right side on his white mule with a small band — [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]], Usama ibn Zayd and [[person/ayman-ibn-ubayd|Ayman ibn Ubayd]] among them — calling out: "I am the Prophet, no lie; I am the son of Abd al-Muttalib" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/87-88}. He ordered al-Abbas to call in his powerful voice: "O Companions of the Tree! O companions of Sura al-Baqara!", and the Muslims came rushing back {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/88-89}.

## The melee and the victory
The two armies grappled and the fighting grew hot; the Prophet said: "now the oven blazes," then cast a handful of pebbles at the polytheists' faces saying: "may the faces be disfigured" — and Hawazin and Thaqif were routed {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/89}. Thaqif fled to al-Ta'if under [[person/qarib-ibn-al-aswad|Qarib ibn al-Aswad]], Hawazin took refuge at [[place/awtas|Awtas]], and the Muslims took their wealth and captives {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/90-93}.

## Reports of the road and the captives
The Prophet left Mecca on Saturday 6 Shawwal 8 AH with twelve thousand — ten thousand of those who had conquered Mecca and two thousand of its people — having borrowed a hundred coats of mail with their gear from Safwan ibn Umayya {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 414}. On the road the army passed the tree Dhat Anwat and the famous [[event/incident-of-dhat-anwat|incident of Dhat Anwat]] took place, and [[person/anas-ibn-abi-marthad|Anas ibn Abi Marthad al-Ghanawi]] volunteered for the watch that night {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 415}.

Some of the army, looking to its size, said: we cannot be beaten today for want of numbers — and that grieved the Prophet {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 415}. When the Muslims were first routed, Abu Sufyan ibn Harb — then new to Islam — said their defeat would not end before the sea, and Jabala or Kalda ibn al-Junayd cried: sorcery is void today {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 415}.

Among the captives were six thousand souls, including [[person/al-shayma-bint-al-harith|al-Shayma bint al-Harith al-Sa'diyya]], the Prophet's foster-sister; she made herself known to him, he honoured her, spread his cloak for her and sent her back to her people {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 417}. The spoils were held at al-Ji'rana — twenty-four thousand camels, more than forty thousand sheep and four thousand uqiyya of silver among them — with Mas'ud ibn Amr al-Ghifari placed in charge {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 417}.

## Significance and legislation
The battle settled important matters: it broke the last armed pagan gathering in the Hijaz, and in it the Prophet forbade killing women, children and wage-labourers {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/95-96}. Qur'anic guidance came down teaching the lesson of pride in numbers, in [[passage/quran-9-25|Sura al-Tawba 25–26]].

## For young visitors
The Muslims went out in a great army, and the enemy surprised them with an ambush in the valley early in the morning, so some fell back. But the Prophet stood firm, courageously calling his Companions; they returned, won, and he afterwards forgave many of those who had fought him.
