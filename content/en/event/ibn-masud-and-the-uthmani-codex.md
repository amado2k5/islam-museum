## Summary
When [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] ordered burned whatever leaf or codex was other than the Imam codex, a number of Companions had private codices containing explanatory additions or variant forms; among the best known is the codex of [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]]. The books of Qur'anic sciences speak of Ibn Mas'ud's position on this unification, sometimes in the form 'it is reported of him', and I did not read the original reports in Ibn Abi Dawud's book. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 388} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 239}

## What is related
Al-Sabt says it is reported of Ibn Mas'ud that he first objected to Uthman's codices and refused to burn his own, then returned to the community's fold when the merits of those codices became clear to him, and the community's agreement on them and the uniting of its word through them. He says the codices of Ibn Mas'ud, of Ubayy ibn Ka'b, of A'isha, of Ali and of Salim, freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, came to be washed with water or burned. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 239} These are al-Sabt's own words and summary, not the text of a report with its chain.

## Ali's position and others
Abu Bakr al-Anbari relays, as al-Sabt quotes, from Suwayd ibn Ghafala: I heard Ali ibn Abi Talib say: people, fear God and beware of excess about Uthman and your saying 'burner of codices'; by God, he burned them only with the agreement of a group of us, the Companions of the Messenger of God. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 240} In Ibn Abi Dawud's version, with a sound chain as al-Suyuti says, from Suwayd ibn Ghafala, Ali said: say nothing of Uthman but good, for by God he did what he did with the codices only with a group of us; then he said: what do you say of this recitation? It has reached me that some say my reading is better than yours, and this almost amounts to unbelief; we said: what do you think? He said: I think we should unite people on one codex so there is no division or disagreement. We said: well thought. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 390} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 391} Al-Suyuti adds a report, which a note calls weak for a vague narrator, that Ali said: had I been in charge I would have done with the codices what Uthman did. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 393}

## What scholars say of what was kept and dropped
Ibn al-Jazari says the community, protected from error, agreed on what the Uthmanic codices contained and left aside what departed from them in addition, omission and substitution of one word for another that had been allowed as a concession, because its being Qur'an was not established to them widely. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 17} Elsewhere a note by the editors of this edition of al-Itqan, on [[concept/masahif-al-sahaba|Ubayy's codex]], says that what some private codices held of supplication or explanation was not Qur'an, and that Ubayy and the whole community agreed to accept Uthman's codex. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 1465}

## What I leave out
I do not state here where Ibn Mas'ud was on the day or the text of what he said, or what became of his codex, since the sources I read do not say; what is told of him in them is in the form 'it is reported'. See also [[event/mushaf-ibn-masud-burned-398|the burning of a codex attributed to Ibn Mas'ud in 398 AH]] in Baghdad, a report nearly four centuries after the unification.
