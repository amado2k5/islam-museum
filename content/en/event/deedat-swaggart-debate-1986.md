## Summary
The 1986 Baton Rouge debate between Ahmed Deedat and Jimmy Swaggart on 'Is the Bible the Word of God?' — the most printed and transcribed debate of the modern era. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}

## Subject and arguments
Deedat challenged that the Bible in circulation is God's own revelation, arguing from variant versions and textual history; Swaggart defended the inspiration of the two Testaments and the doctrine of Christ. The full transcript and video spread so widely that the debate became the model method on which the next generation of Muslim debaters measured itself.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Deedat (opening case)**: the Bible in circulation has no unbroken divine chain: differing versions and contested passages (the longer ending of Mark, the woman taken in adultery), divergent manuscript families — so it is 'the word of God *in* it', not the word of God itself.
- **Swaggart**: defended the plenary inspiration of both Testaments — God preserved His word through the copyists, and the Bible testifies of itself.
- **Deedat (rebuttal)**: the textual differences run deeper than translation — content varies between manuscripts, breaking the continuous attestation a claim of revelation requires.
- **Swaggart (rebuttal)**: affirmed New Testament reliability and the weight of manuscript witnesses, answering the definitional argument: the true text survives in the totality of the evidence.
- **The public verdict**: no adjudicator; the printed booklet and recording spread so widely the debate itself became the contending text on both sides. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}
