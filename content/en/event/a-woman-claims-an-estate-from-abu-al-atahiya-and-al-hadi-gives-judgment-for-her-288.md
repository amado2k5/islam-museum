## Overview
While [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] was staying at [[place/samh-yemen|Samh]] a woman cried out at his door; he ordered her brought in and when she came in she said: Commander of the Faithful, do me justice against Abu al-Atahiya {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.212}. Al-Hadi sent for [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]], who came, and told him: do this woman justice; then he said to the woman: what do you claim against him? She said: he holds an estate of mine that his father seized. Abu al-Atahiya said to al-Hadi: apply to me and to her what is due, Commander of the Faithful. Al-Hadi asked the woman: have you witnesses? She said yes, went and brought witnesses, and they testified before al-Hadi for her to the estate; al-Hadi gave judgment for the woman to have the estate, ordered her to take possession of it, and she did {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.212}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi left Samh and put over it a man of the Futayimis called Zayd ibn Abi al-Abbas, charging him to fear God and to command the good and forbid the wrong, and went on until he halted at [[place/dhamar|Dhamar]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.212}. The narrator gives the case as a witness to al-Hadi's even-handedness even toward those who were with him in his cause; the museum does no more than set the case out as told, does not value the estate or describe the claim beyond what is written, and does not compare the judgment with any other.
