## Overview
When the horse of [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] reached [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] with [[person/jarrah-ibn-bishr|Jarrah ibn Bishr]], he went out for [[group/khirfan-arhab|Khirfan]] and [[group/al-subay-arhab|al-Subay']], calling them to hearing and obedience. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} The narrator says they were of Arhab and cousins of [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]], men he had used for raids and to strengthen himself in his war on al-Hadi. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} The account is Zaydi, in the voice of the imam's followers; its sayings are attributed to their narrators and the museum gives no verdict on them.

## The empty villages and the safe-conduct
When he reached al-Subay' and Khirfan he found the villages empty of people, their inhabitants having fled to the mountain tops; he sent them safe-conduct and some elders came down to him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} When Abu al-Atahiya's soldiers saw the villages empty, some entered houses holding straw and fodder belonging to the people and took some of the fodder. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112}

## The fodder returned
Al-Hadi heard of it, gathered them and said: return all that you took, and if any of you does it again, taking anything without my leave, I will cut off his hand. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112} They returned all they had taken to its place in the presence of the people's elders, who were pleased at al-Hadi's justice and went back to their companions to tell them of his justice, forgiveness and safe-conduct. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112} All of them came down to him; he gave them safety and had them swear to the right, to stand with him, to hear and to obey, and they swore, as did their governors; he then went back to the al-Darb of Banu Sarim and stayed some days. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112} The editor notes that a marginal note headed "a benefit" stands in the original manuscript at the word for "his governors". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112}

## The effect on al-Da'am
The news of the people of al-Subay' and Khirfan reached al-Da'am and angered him, since they had been his support; he saw al-Hadi's affairs advancing and his own receding, and, in the narrator's picture of his opponent, grieved over his earlier war and his errors and sins. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112}

## The march to Hawth
Al-Hadi resolved to camp against him at Khiwan and bring it to an end, sent the criers among the people, and they gathered to him in the days past of Safar; he then marched until he camped at a place called [[place/hawth-yemen|Hawth]], with a great army including Abu al-Atahiya's brother and his horse. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112} Al-Da'am heard of his arrival at Hawth and gathered the people of Khiwan; the narrator heard from someone that he asked Banu Salman and his companions what they said of this man who had drawn near and was set on war. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112} It reached the narrator that some told him the opinion was to fight, and others said not to fight but to deceive the man, he going out and they going out; the page breaks off in mid sentence there. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.112} See [[group/banu-salman-hamdan|Banu Salman]] and [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]].
