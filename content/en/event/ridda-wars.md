## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates in "Futuh al-Buldan" that groups of the Arabs withheld the alms after [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] became caliph, and some of them said that they perform the prayer but do not pay the zakat. Abu Bakr resolved to fight them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.131-133}

## The position on those who withheld alms
Al-Baladhuri says that Abu Bakr said he would fight them even if they withheld a cord (iqal), and in another account "a kid goat" ('anaq). For him the iqal was the alms of the year. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.131-133}

He quotes Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud that after the death of the Prophet the Muslims were on the brink of destruction, had it not been for God's grace through Abu Bakr, since the opinion of many of them had leaned toward abandoning the fight over the zakat. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.131-133}

In the report of Tariq ibn Shihab, Abu Bakr gave them the choice between "the war that clears" and "the humiliating peace", and the report mentions the terms of the peace. The report quotes Aisha on what came upon her father when the Arabs apostatised and hypocrisy appeared in Medina. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.131-133}

## The campaigns
Abu Bakr set out to al-Qassa and sent armies against the apostates. Kharija ibn Hisn and Mandhur ibn Zaban al-Fazariyyan held them back, and Talha ibn Ubayd Allah followed them. Then Abu Bakr appointed [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] over the people, and [[person/thabit-ibn-qays|Thabit ibn Qays]] over the Ansar. He began with Tulayha ibn Khuwaylid at Buzakha, then battles took place at al-Yamama, Bahrain, Oman, Yemen, Hadramawt, Mecca, Tihama and Iraq. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.131-133}

The details of the dates and places need an additional source, and the views of other parties on these wars have not yet been added.

## For Sprouts
After Abu Bakr took the caliphate, groups among the Arabs withheld the sadaqa — some saying they would keep the prayer but not pay the zakat — and Abu Bakr resolved to fight them and sent out the armies.

## For Explorers
Al-Baladhuri's Futuh al-Buldan tells that groups among the Arabs withheld the sadaqa after Abu Bakr took the caliphate, some saying they would keep the prayer but not pay the zakat, and Abu Bakr resolved to fight them — for even a hobbling cord, 'or a she-kid' in another telling, the cord meaning the year's sadaqa. Ibn Mas'ud is quoted that the Muslims were near ruin but for God's favor through Abu Bakr, many leaning toward not fighting over the zakat; and Abu Bakr offered the choice of 'open war' or 'shameful peace'. He marched to al-Qassa and sent the armies — Kharija ibn Hisn and Manzur ibn Zaban turned them back, Talha ibn Ubaydullah following — then gave the standard to Khalid ibn al-Walid over the people and Thabit ibn Qays over the Ansar, beginning with Tulayha at Buzakha, then Yamama, Bahrain, Oman, Yemen, Hadramawt, Mecca, Tihama and Iraq.

## For Scholars
Al-Baladhuri's account in Futuh al-Buldan tells that groups among the Arabs withheld the sadaqa after Abu Bakr took the caliphate, some saying they would keep the prayer but not pay the zakat, and Abu Bakr resolved to fight them. Al-Baladhuri records Abu Bakr saying he would fight them for even a hobbling cord — 'or a she-kid' in another telling — the cord meaning the year's sadaqa. He quotes Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that the Muslims after the Prophet's death were near ruin but for God's favor through Abu Bakr, as many of them leaned toward not fighting over the zakat. In Tariq ibn Shihab's telling Abu Bakr gave them the choice of 'open war' or 'shameful peace' and set out the peace terms; and the account relays Aisha's description of what befell her father when the Arabs apostatized and hypocrisy appeared in Medina. Abu Bakr marched to al-Qassa and sent the armies to the apostates — Kharija ibn Hisn and Manzur ibn Zaban al-Fazari turned them back with Talha ibn Ubaydullah following. Then Abu Bakr gave the standard to Khalid ibn al-Walid over the people and Thabit ibn Qays over the Ansar, beginning with Tulayha ibn Khuwaylid at Buzakha, then the events of Yamama, Bahrain, Oman, Yemen, Hadramawt, Mecca, Tihama and Iraq.
