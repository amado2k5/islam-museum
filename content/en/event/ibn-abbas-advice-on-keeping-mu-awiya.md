## Summary
Al-Sallabi presents reports attributing two pieces of advice to Ibn Abbas and al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba given to Ali after his pledge of allegiance regarding Uthman's governors and Mu'awiya, and then rejects them due to the weakness of their chains of transmission and the inconsistency of their texts {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 352}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}. These reports form the basis of the criticism that the book mentions in some contemporary studies {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}.

## The First Report: al-Waqidi
The book narrates, on the authority of al-Waqidi, that Ibn Abbas said Uthman had appointed him to lead the pilgrimage; when he arrived in Medina after Ali's pledge of allegiance, he found al-Mughira with him, so Ali kept him waiting until al-Mughira left, and then told him that al-Mughira had advised Ali to send letters of appointment to Ibn Amir, Mu'awiya, and Uthman's governors confirming them in their posts so they would pledge allegiance to him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 352}. The book mentions that Ali replied: "By God, even if it were only for an hour of the day, I would exercise my own judgment in it; I would never appoint these men, nor should the likes of them be appointed" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 352}.

In the report, al-Mughira returned and advised dismissing them, whereupon Ibn Abbas said: "As for the first time, he advised you sincerely; as for the last time, he deceived you" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 353}. The book mentions that Ali offered to appoint Ibn Abbas over Syria, but Ibn Abbas refused for fear of being killed because of Uthman, so Ali said: "By God, this shall never happen" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 353}.

## The Second and Third Reports
The second report conveys the same meaning with additions and discrepancies that raise doubt, and the book considers that it contains material contradicting historical fact, while the scan is garbled in part of it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 353}. As for the third report, which is from Abu Mikhnaf without a chain of transmission as the book notes, al-Mughira tells Ali to confirm Mu'awiya over Syria and to appoint Talha and al-Zubayr over Basra and Kufa; Ibn Abbas objected that Basra and Kufa were the main source of revenue, so Ali listened to Ibn Abbas and did not accept al-Mughira's advice {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 353}.

## The Book's Response
The book considers the reports "feeble" in terms of their chain of transmission, and holds that this is sufficient to dismiss them, then notes that they are inconsistent and rejected in terms of their text {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}. Among its points of criticism are:
- That its statement that Ibn Abbas arrived in Medina after Ali's pledge of allegiance contradicts reliable reports indicating that he arrived before the pledge was given {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}.
- That most of the governors mentioned had left their provinces and gone to Mecca, so how could letters be sent to them to confirm them in office? {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}
- That these governors were qualified for leadership, among them Abd Allah ibn Amir, under whose governorship the conquests of Basra reached Kabul, and Mu'awiya, who governed for twenty years {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}.
- That the report portrays al-Mughira as resorting to flattery and deceit at a critical time, which does not accord with his biography {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}.

The book considers the reports to be hypothetical, saying "if such-and-such happens, such-and-such will happen," rather than transmitting what actually occurred, and that the intervention of narrators with their own inclinations and biases affected them {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}.

## Dismissal Is Not Weakness
The book affirms that Ali was an independent jurist-leader entitled to dismiss whomever he saw fit to dismiss in the public interest, that the caliphs before him did the same with the governors of their predecessors {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 355}, and that appointing new governors was more conducive to securing the people's pledge of allegiance in the conquered lands {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 355}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The reports are transmitted in the book by reference to al-Waqidi and Abu Mikhnaf, and have not been verified from their original sources in this summary.
- The stated date is approximate and derived from the account of the pledge of allegiance; the book does not establish the exact date of the conversation.
- The scan on page 353 is garbled in some sentences, and for this reason the wording of the third report is not relied upon except for its general meaning.

## The Book's Position and Contemporary Debate
Al-Sallabi rejects the reports and considers relying on them to impugn the senior Companions of the consultative council to be an error built on a weak foundation {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 354}. He notes that contemporary readings of Ali's dismissal of the governors range between those who view it as firmness in upholding the truth and those who view it as a lack of political experience {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 355}.
