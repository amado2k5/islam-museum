## Summary
In the tenth year of the Hijra, before the Farewell Pilgrimage, [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] sent [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] to [[place/yemen|Yemen]]. The books of hadith and sira report it from Mu'adh himself: the Prophet went out with him giving counsel, walking while Mu'adh rode, and then told him that he might not meet him after that year. Other reports add instructions on the call, on alms and on conduct. The sending itself and the envoys of the Himyarite kings are in [[event/mission-of-muadh-to-yemen|The sending of Mu'adh to Yemen]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 108}

## The mission and the counsel of ease
Ibn Kathir begins with al-Bukhari's report from Abu Burda that the Prophet sent [[person/abu-musa-al-ashari|Abu Musa]] and Mu'adh to Yemen, each over a district, and told them: "Make things easy and not hard; give good news and do not repel", and in one version "agree with one another and do not differ". {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 108} Al-Bayhaqi gives the same report and adds that each of them, when travelling through his land and near his companion, would renew his acquaintance and greet him. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 3 p. 415}

## Going out with him
Ibn Kathir reports from Ahmad, from Mu'adh, that when the Prophet sent him to Yemen he went out with him counselling him, Mu'adh riding and the Prophet walking beneath his mount. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109} Al-Salihi gives the same report in his chapter on the etiquette of farewell, says that Ahmad and Abu Ya'la transmitted it with trustworthy men, and adds from Ibn Asakir that the Prophet walked with him a mile while Mu'adh rode, having told him to do so. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 3456} For al-Salihi the report is an example of the Prophet walking with the commanders of his expeditions while they rode.

## "You may not meet me after this year of mine"
When he had finished he said: "O Mu'adh, you may not meet me after this year of mine, and perhaps you will pass by this mosque of mine and my grave", and Mu'adh wept from distress at parting from the Prophet. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109} In another report from Ahmad quoted by Ibn Kathir he forbade him to weep and said it was from the devil, the narrator being unsure of the exact wording. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109} In the version Ibn Kathir gives, the Prophet then turned his face toward Medina and said: "The closest of people to me are the God-fearing, whoever and wherever they are." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109} Ibn Rajab adds that al-Tabarani has more in this report, that the Prophet's household think themselves closest to him and it is not so, his friends being the God-fearing. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 752} In a further report from Ahmad quoted by Ibn Kathir he said: "Perhaps you will pass by my grave and my mosque; I have sent you to a people of tender hearts who fight for the truth twice", and told him to camp between the two tribes of al-Saksak and al-Sakun. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109}

## His instructions
Ibn Kathir reports from Ibn Abbas in al-Bukhari that the Prophet said to Mu'adh on sending him: you will come to a people of the Book, so first call them to testify that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is the Messenger of God; if they obey, tell them God has made five prayers obligatory on them each day and night; if they obey, tell them God has made alms obligatory on them, taken from their rich and given back to their poor; if they obey, beware of the best of their property, and fear the supplication of the wronged, for there is no veil between it and God. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 108}{cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109} Ahmad reports from Mu'adh that he told him to take a dinar, or its equivalent in Ma'afir cloth, from every adult; from every forty cows a mature one and from every thirty a yearling; and a tenth from what rain waters and half a tenth from what is watered by wheels. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 110}

## Other counsels Mu'adh reports
Ibn Kathir quotes Ahmad that Mu'adh said the Messenger of God counselled him with ten words, among them not to associate anything with God even if killed and burned; not to disobey his parents even if they told him to leave his property and family; never to omit an obligatory prayer on purpose; not to drink wine; to beware of disobedience and of fleeing from the battle line; to stand firm if a plague struck among his people; to spend on his family from his means; not to lift his staff from them in discipline; and to love them for God's sake. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 110} He also reports that the Prophet said to him when sending him to Yemen: "Beware of luxury, for God's servants are not those who live in luxury", and that he said: "Follow a bad deed with a good one and it will erase it, and treat people with good character", and, when Mu'adh asked him to counsel him, "Fear God wherever you are." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 110} Ibn Rajab relates that Shu'ba, when about to travel, would ask al-Hakam if he had a need, and al-Hakam would answer: I counsel you with what the Prophet counselled Mu'adh. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 385} These last reports do not say they were spoken at the farewell itself.

## Did he meet him again?
Ibn Kathir reads in the Prophet's words an allusion that Mu'adh would not meet him again, says that this is what happened, and then notes that the Prophet died eighty-one days after the day of the greater pilgrimage. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109} Al-Bayhaqi says that Abu Musa's report shows he returned to Mecca for the Farewell Pilgrimage, but as for Mu'adh the likelier view is that he did not return until the Prophet died. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 3 p. 416} Ibn Kathir also gives, in the same place, a report in Ahmad through Abu Zabyan from a man of the Ansar from Mu'adh, in which he came from Yemen and asked whether they should prostrate to the Prophet since he had seen men prostrating to one another; he says a narrator of that kind cannot be relied on, and others relate the story as having occurred when Mu'adh came from Syria. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109} It is understood from his words that he does not rely on the report that has Mu'adh coming from Yemen. For Mu'adh's own death see [[event/deathbed-of-muadh-ibn-jabal|The deathbed of Mu'adh ibn Jabal]].

## Where the reports differ
- The reports given here do not say that the ten counsels and the others were spoken at the farewell; what belongs to the farewell is the walking beside the mount and the words "you may not meet me". {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109}
- Ibn Kathir gives from Ahmad more than one report of the farewell: one in which Mu'adh weeps and is told not to, another with "the closest of people to me are the God-fearing", and a third describing the people of Yemen. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 109}
- Whether Mu'adh returned from Yemen before the Prophet died is disputed: al-Bayhaqi says the likelier view is that he did not, and a report of Abu Zabyan says he did, which Ibn Kathir weakens. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 3 p. 416}
