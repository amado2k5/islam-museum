## Overview
After Gabriel informed the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] of the plot of Quraysh and gave him leave to go out, the Prophet left his house on the night of the twenty-seventh of Safar, the fourteenth year of the mission. He threw dust on the heads of those surrounding it, then went to the house of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], and they went out through a small door in his house until they reached the [[place/ghar-thawr|Cave of Thawr]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.161} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.163} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.164}

## The night in the house
The Prophet asked Abu Bakr to leave him so that he could be alone with the matter. Abu Bakr said: "They are only your family." The Prophet said: "I have been given leave to go out." Abu Bakr asked for companionship, and he said yes. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.161} The polytheists placed watchmen at his door from the darkness of the night, so the Prophet ordered Ali to sleep in his bed and wrap himself in his green Hadrami cloak. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.163} In the morning a man told them they had thrown dust on their heads for nothing, and they looked and found Ali on the bed. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.163}

## The cave
They reached the cave after taking a road that runs against the known road to Medina, south toward Yemen, and then climbed the rough mountain of Thawr, with Abu Bakr carrying him at the end of the way. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.164} When they reached the cave, Abu Bakr went in first to clear it; he stopped a hole with his waistcloth, and then the Prophet ordered him to enter. The Prophet slept in his lap, Abu Bakr was bitten in his foot and did not move so as not to wake the Prophet, and the Prophet spat on it and the pain went away. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.164}

## Three nights
The two remained in the cave for three nights. Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr spent the nights with them, heard what Quraysh were plotting, and told them when darkness fell. Amir ibn Fuhayra, Abu Bakr's client, pastured a flock of sheep for them, so they slept with its milk until he came back with his flock before dawn. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.165} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/553-554}

## The pursuit
Quraysh went wild when they learned the Prophet had gone out. They struck Ali and imprisoned him, then went to the house of Abu Bakr and asked Asma, Abu Bakr's daughter, about her father; [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]] slapped her so hard that her earring fell from her. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.165} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/487} Quraysh set a reward of a hundred camels for whoever brought back the two men, alive or dead, and placed armed watch on the roads. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.165}

## At the mouth of the cave
The pursuers reached the mouth of the cave. Abu Bakr said to the Prophet that if one of them lowered his gaze he would see them. The Prophet said: "Be quiet. What do you think of two whom God makes the third?" The pursuers turned back, and there were only a few steps left between them and the Prophet. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.166} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/558}

## Notes for researchers
- The book holds that Abu Bakr's fear was not for himself but because if the Prophet were killed the community would be lost, which it relates from al-Najdi. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.166}
- The book prefers that the night of departure was the twenty-seventh of Safar, an order based on counting the years from Muharram. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.164}

## For young visitors
At night the Prophet left his house after Ali had slept in his bed, and hid with Abu Bakr in a cave on a mountain. For three full days Abu Bakr's son brought them news of Quraysh, and the polytheists searched for them and did not find them.

## In Abla al-Kahlawi's programme 'In the Love of al-Mustafa'
[[person/abla-al-kahlawi|Abla al-Kahlawi]] relates in her programme [[work/fi-hub-al-mustapha|'In the Love of al-Mustafa']] that after Quraysh had tried the weapons of humiliation and torture and then of the boycott, they agreed to take a strong young man from every tribe to strike him with a single blow, so that his blood would be divided among the tribes, and they came and surrounded his house. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:05:00} He went out from among them without their seeing him, she says, for sleep overcame them, while Ali insisted on sleeping in his place covered with his cloak, and slept with a certainty he had taken from 'the master of certainty'. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:06:00} When Ali got up in the morning, she relates, they said it was not him they wanted but Muhammad, and went away. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:06:00} She says Ali stayed behind in Mecca to settle the Prophet's debts. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep15 00:06:30}
