## Summary
On the Thursday four days before his death, the Prophet asked that writing materials be brought so he could write a document after which they would not go astray; the household disputed when Umar said "God's Book suffices you", and the Prophet said: "leave me" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 466}.

## The account
The Prophet, his pain severe, said: bring me something to write you a document after which you will not go astray — and in the house were men, Umar among them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 466; sahih-al-bukhari}. Umar said: the pain has overcome him, and you have the Qur'an — God's Book suffices you. The household disputed and quarrelled, some saying bring it so he may write for you, others saying what Umar said; when the clamour grew loud the Prophet said: "leave me" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 466; sahih-al-bukhari}. That day the Prophet made three bequests: the removal of Jews, Christians and polytheists from the Arabian peninsula, the hospitality of delegations as he had hosted them — and the third the narrator forgot {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 466-467}. Despite the severity of his illness the Prophet kept leading the people in prayer until that day, and led them in the sunset prayer reciting al-Mursalat 'urfa in it {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 467; sahih-al-bukhari}.

## Perspectives
The Sunnis hold that Umar showed compassion for the Prophet's state and sufficed with God's Book, yet Ibn Abbas still counted the day a calamity for the document that was lost {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 466; sahih-al-bukhari}. Shi'i traditions read the event as a prevention of a text that was to be written, while historians treat the report as a later dispute whose text cannot be reconstructed {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 466}.

## For young visitors
In his illness the Prophet asked for a paper to write something for the people; those present disputed over whether to bring it or suffice with the Qur'an, so the Prophet asked them to leave him.
