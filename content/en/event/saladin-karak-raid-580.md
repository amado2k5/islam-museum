## Summary
In Rabi al-Akhir, Salah al-Din besieged al-Karak and set up siege engines. The Muslims took the outer town, but the fortress remained, with a moat between them about sixty cubits deep. He built sheds of timber and mudbrick under which men advanced to fill in the moat, while his catapults fired day and night. Those inside appealed to the Franks, who gathered and marched to their relief, so Salah al-Din turned onto their route to form his ranks against them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 127-128}
