## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad relates from his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] that when the date collection fell due at [[place/najran|Najran]], [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] gathered the notables of the land, of Banu al-Harith, Hamdan and Najran, and consulted them on their collection, saying: "We may take from you only dates for dates and grain for grain, we allow nothing else, and I do not overstep the practice of the Messenger of God." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70} The people answered that appraising the dates suited them better, being easier than taking fresh dates, soft dates and poor dates; they would set it on what was easy for them and set aside good and poor, and in their view dates are not like grain. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.71} A footnote explains rutab as palm dates ripe but not yet dried, rajiz as the term still used in Yemen for ripe soft sweet dates, and khushu as the poorest dates, citing al-Askari's Talkhis and the Mukhassas. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.71}

## The answer and the appraisers
Al-Hadi agreed because the ease for them was clear to him, in their handling of it and in feeding those under them, and said that had his grandfather the Messenger of God not appraised Medina, Khaybar and other places he would not have appraised it for them; a margin note of the manuscript records that the Prophet appraised Medina and Khaybar. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.71} He consulted them on who should appraise and they named some just and trustworthy men; he sent for them and made them swear to take only the due, to lay no burden on the weak nor indulge the strong, and to take nothing below thirty-three and a third farq, and ordered grapes appraised on the same basis. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.71}

## Selling the due
[[person/muhammad-ibn-sulayman-al-kufi|Al-Kufi]] heard a Najran man tell him that if a man owed something from the alms on his palms, selling it back to its owner would suit better; he answered that this is neither allowed nor lawful, but that what is owed is set aside and then sold to whoever wants it, as the Messenger of God did with the eaters of Khaybar. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.71} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.72} This is the Zaydi imam's position as his narrator reports it, shown without comparison.
