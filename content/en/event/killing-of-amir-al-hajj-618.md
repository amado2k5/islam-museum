## Overview
In 618 AH Aqbash, the amir of the Iraqi pilgrimage, was killed near Mecca in a confrontation with [[person/hasan-ibn-qatada|al-Hasan ibn Qatada]]. Then al-Hasan granted the pilgrims safe conduct, and they completed their pilgrimage. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.426}

## The account
Ibn al-Athir relates that Aqbash, a mamluk of the caliph [[person/al-nasir-billah|al-Nasir]], behaved well toward the pilgrims. Rajih ibn Qatada offered him money to help him take the rule of Mecca from his brother [[person/hasan-ibn-qatada|al-Hasan]]. The caravan camped at al-Zahir, and al-Hasan came out with a host of Arabs. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.426}

Aqbash advanced alone and climbed the mountain. Al-Hasan's men surrounded him, killed him and hung up his head, and the caliph's army was routed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.426}

## Safe conduct for the pilgrims
The soldiers surrounded the pilgrims to plunder them, and al-Hasan sent his turban as a safe conduct for them, and nothing of theirs was taken. They stayed ten days performing the pilgrimage and then returned safely to Iraq. Al-Hasan then sent envoys to apologise and ask for pardon, and they were granted it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.426}

The book also says that [[person/qatada-ibn-idris-al-alawi|Qatada ibn Idris]], the father of al-Hasan, died in that year. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir}
