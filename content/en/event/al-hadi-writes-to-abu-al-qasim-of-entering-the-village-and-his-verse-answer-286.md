## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] wrote to [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] telling him of his entry into the village and of his first killing of [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]]; Abu al-Qasim was glad of it and read it to all his supporters. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.180} He wrote al-Hadi the answer to his letter and sent verse with it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.180}

## The poem
In the lines the courier arrives with good news after al-Hadi has killed the enemy; the poet wishes he had been at the fight and offers his neck against the brown spears in al-Hadi's defence, says he moves about on his horse with his lance and in the melee with his Indian blade, seeks his Creator's pleasure in standing before the imam, brother of the noble, holds the shedding of the blood of those who broke their pledges a binding duty on him like an inheritance share, and says he was not at the day of the meeting only because his horse was far from them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.181} He adds that he has dealt without boast with a hard matter and is loyal to every believing servant. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.181} The editor's notes show the other manuscript reading some lines differently and omitting others.
