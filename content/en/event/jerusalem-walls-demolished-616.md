## Summary
At the start of 616 AH al-Malik al-Muʿazzam had the walls of Jerusalem demolished, out of fear and an inability to resist, lest the Franks take it. Work on tearing down the walls began on the first day of Muharram {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 65}.

The people cried out. Veiled women, girls, old men and young men went out to the Rock and to al-Aqsa. They cut their hair, fled leaving their property behind, and believed the Franks would come upon them at dawn. Some fled to Egypt, some to Karak, some to Damascus, and many died of hunger and thirst. The property they had left in Jerusalem was plundered. A qintar of oil was sold for ten dirhams and a rotl of copper for half a dirham {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 66}.

People blamed al-Muʿazzam, and poets composed verses criticising him. One poet wrote a verse that mocked him for the ruin of Jerusalem in Muharram and for employing Copts and Christians afterwards. The judge of Tur, Majd al-Din, composed a poem in which he said that if the city could be ransomed with lives, he would ransom it with his own life {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 66}.

Al-Muʿazzam feared the Franks might take the city again and use it as a base. But the demolition fell on its inhabitants before the Franks arrived. In the same year Damietta was handed over by its people to the Franks, and great terror spread through all of Syria {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 66}.

## Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's account
The account in Shadharat al-dhahab is taken as a whole from [[work/mirat-al-zaman|Mir'at al-zaman]] by [[person/sibt-ibn-al-jawzi|Sibt ibn al-Jawzi]], who opens the events of 616 AH with it and adds the reason for the demolition and the letters exchanged. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.236} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.65-66}

### The reason
Al-Mu'azzam had gone to his brother al-Kamil at Damietta when he heard that a party of Franks meant to make for Jerusalem. The amirs agreed to dismantle it, saying: "Syria is empty of troops; if the Franks took it, they would rule Syria." {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.236} In Jerusalem were his brother [[person/al-malik-al-aziz-banias|al-Aziz Uthman]] and Izz al-Din Aybak, master of the household. Al-Mu'azzam wrote ordering them to dismantle it; they hesitated and replied, "We will hold it." He wrote back that if the Franks took it they would kill everyone in it and rule over Damascus and the lands of Islam: "necessity has forced its dismantling". {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.236}

### The day of the demolition
They began on the wall on the first day of Muharram, and "a great outcry" arose in the city. The people went out to the Rock and [[building/al-aqsa-mosque|al-Aqsa]], cut their hair and tore their clothes, "so that the Rock and the mihrab of al-Aqsa were filled with hair", then fled, leaving their property behind. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.236} He records that secluded young women tore their clothes and tied the strips round their bare feet, that many died of hunger and thirst, and that a qintar of oil fell to ten dirhams and a ratl of copper to half a dirham. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.236} {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.237}

### Blame on al-Mu'azzam
Sibt ibn al-Jawzi, himself one of al-Mu'azzam's intimates, writes: "People blamed al-Mu'azzam." He quotes a verse by a scholar pairing the ruin of Jerusalem in Muharram with the licensing of wine in Rajab, and comments: "No one excused him." He also cites lines by Majd al-Din Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Hanafi, qadi of al-Tur, lamenting the remains of Jerusalem. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.237} Al-Mu'azzam himself justified the demolition by necessity and fear of a Frankish seizure, as in his letter to his brother. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.236}
