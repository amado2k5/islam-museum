## Summary
Two bequests mark the story of [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]], decades apart: his bequest of a third while ill in Mecca, the origin of the well-known rule on bequeathing a third, and his instruction at death in his house at al-Aqiq that he be shrouded in a woollen garment in which he had met the polytheists at Badr. After his death he was carried to Medina, where [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]] and then the wives of the Prophet prayed over him, and he was buried in al-Baqi'. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 143}

## The bequest of a third
Sa'd reports that the Prophet came to visit him in Mecca while he was ill and averse to dying in the land he had emigrated from, and said: may God have mercy on Ibn Afra'. Sa'd asked: may I bequeath all my wealth? He said no. Half? No. A third? The Prophet answered, in substance, that a third, and a third is much, and that leaving one's heirs well-off is better than leaving them in need, begging from people. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2742} See [[passage/bukhari-api-2742|Bukhari 2742]]. In the Muwatta's version he visited him in the year of the farewell pilgrimage during a severe illness. {cite:muwatta-malik 2219} In Abu Nu'aym's version he had only one daughter then, and the Prophet told him that perhaps God would raise him so that some people would benefit from him and others be harmed by him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 94}

## His last years
Al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar says that late in life Sa'd withdrew to a house he had built at the edge of Hamra' al-Asad. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 169} Ibn Abd al-Barr says that in the civil strife he stayed at home and told his family not to inform him of any news of the people until the community agreed on one leader. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 277} His son Amir said he was the last of the Emigrants to die. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 169}

## His testament at death
Al-Zuhri, through al-Layth from Aqil, relates that when Sa'd was dying he called for an old woollen garment of his and said: shroud me in it, for I met the polytheists in it at Badr, and I have only kept it for this. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 168} Ahmad gives the same account in his Zuhd. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 scan vol. 3 p. 236} Ibn al-Jawzi says he had bequeathed to be shrouded in it and was. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 143}

## What he left
His daughter Aisha bint Sa'd relates that he sent Marwan five thousand as his zakat and left, on the day he died, two hundred and fifty thousand dirhams. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 169} Among his children who narrated from him were Amir, Mus'ab, Muhammad, Ibrahim and Aisha. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 2 p. 475} At the time of the bequest of a third he had only one daughter, as noted above. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 94}

## Funeral, prayer and burial
Sa'd died in his house at al-Aqiq, ten miles from Medina, was carried to Medina on men's shoulders, buried in al-Baqi', and Marwan ibn al-Hakam prayed over him. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 277} Ibn al-Athir says al-Aqiq is seven miles away and that he was taken into the mosque, where Marwan and the wives of the Prophet prayed over him. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 2 p. 475} Sifat al-Safwa relates from [[person/aisha|Aisha]] that the wives of the Prophet sent word that his bier be brought past them in the mosque; it was stopped at their rooms and they prayed over him, and it left by the Gate of Funerals; when it reached them that people had objected, Aisha said that the Prophet had prayed over Suhayl ibn Bayda' only inside the mosque. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 143} [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama]] reports that when his bier was brought in to her she wept and said: the remnant of the Companions of the Messenger of God. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 169}

## The grave
He was buried in al-Baqi', that is [[place/baqi-al-gharqad|Baqi' al-Gharqad]]. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 277} Malik relays from more than one source that he died at al-Aqiq, was carried to Medina and buried there. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 143}

## Differing reports
The year of his death is 55 for al-Waqidi, al-Mada'ini and Abu Ubayda, 58 for Abu Nu'aym al-Fadl ibn Dukayn, and 54 for al-Zubayr, al-Hasan ibn Uthman and Amr ibn Ali al-Fallas. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 277} His age is given as over seventy, as seventy-four by al-Fallas, as eighty-two in Ibrahim ibn Sa'd's report, and as eighty-three by Ahmad ibn Hanbal in Mu'awiya's time after his last pilgrimage. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 169} The distance of al-Aqiq from Medina is ten miles in Ibn Abd al-Barr and seven in Ibn al-Athir. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 2 p. 475}
