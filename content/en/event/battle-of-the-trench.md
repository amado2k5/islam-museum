In Shawwal of the fifth year of the Hijra, twenty men of the chiefs of the Jews of Banu al-Nadir and Khaybar — foremost Huyayy ibn Akhtab and Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq — went out to Quraysh at Mecca and to Ghatafan at Najd, inciting them to raid Medina and root out Islam; and a vast army of ten thousand fighters assembled under [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan ibn Harb]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When the news reached the Messenger of God he consulted his Companions, and [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman al-Farisi]] advised him to dig a trench barring the polytheists from Medina on its exposed northern side; three thousand Muslims went out, the Prophet leading them, digging with their own hands in bitter cold and severe hunger, and the digging witnessed prophetic marvels in the splitting of rocks and the multiplying of food. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When the Confederates arrived they found the trench a barrier they had not known in their wars, and laid siege to Medina for a month; at the height of the distress Huyayy ibn Akhtab tempted [[person/kaab-ibn-asad|Ka'b ibn Asad]], chief of Banu Qurayza, into breaking his pact with the Muslims, so the Muslims were threatened from behind by a war within — a fearful scene the Qur'an describes in [[passage/quran-33-10|Sura al-Ahzab, verse 10]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Then God prepared the means of victory through the secret conversion of [[person/nuaym-ibn-masud|Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud al-Ashja'i]]; the Prophet ordered him to sow doubt, and he spread suspicion between Quraysh and Ghatafan and between them and Qurayza. Then God sent on them a bitter, violent wind on a cold dark night that uprooted their tents, overturned their pots and cast terror into their hearts, and they fled in retreat; [[person/hudhayfa-ibn-al-yaman|Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman]] slipped among them and returned to the Messenger of God with the news of their departure. {cite:sahih-muslim}

## For Sprouts
The Muslims dug a trench around Medina to protect it. God protected the people of the city, and a strong wind sent the armies away.

## For Explorers
Large armies came to attack Medina. Salman al-Farisi suggested digging a trench to guard the open side of the city. The Muslims were patient and worked together until the armies left, by God's leave, and a strong wind was a sign of God's help.

## For Scholars
In Shawwal of the fifth year after the Hijra, a coalition of Meccans and allied tribes marched on Medina. On Salman al-Farisi's advice the Muslims dug a trench on the northern side, and they stayed patient for nearly a month. The sources tell of the Banu Qurayza breaking their pact, of Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud's stratagem that divided the armies, and of a strong wind at night. The reports differ on the size of the armies, so each number needs a source. This entry gives the events of the trench as the sira books tell them, and shows how counsel and patience protected the city.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 5)
The Sheikh relates that the night at the Trench was so bitterly cold that two of the Companions wrapped themselves in a single garment, and that the Prophet said: who will cross the trench and bring me news of the enemy, without causing any incident? No one answered him (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:52:30}. He says the Prophet repeated the request, that the night was at the end of the month and the place was dark, and that the Companion crossed and Abu Sufyan passed in front of him at a very short distance (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:53:00}. He mentions that the Companion thought that Abu Sufyan was the head of the group and that killing him might end the matter, but remembered the Prophet's words not to start anything, so he held back his sword and stopped at the limits set by God (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:53:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The speaker names the Battle of the Confederates, also called the Trench, and mentions Ghatafan in its context. He holds (speaker's view, opinion) that the present day resembles it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:14:30}.

The speaker's view (opinion): Muslims today do not learn the language, the thinking or the view of others toward them. He says that at the Battle of the Confederates God sent a wind and hosts that they did not see, and the matter ended {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:15:00}.

He says the Qur'an describes that battle in an extraordinary way: the believers were shaken, hearts reached the throats, until God's help came {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:15:30}.

He recounts Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman's account of a freezing night, when the Prophet asked who would go to the enemy and bring back news without starting anything. Hudhayfa rose into the dark {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:16:00}. Hudhayfa says Abu Sufyan was close to him, within a spear's thrust, and he held back because he remembered the Prophet's charge not to start anything against them {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:17:00}.

The speaker's view (opinion): one man's deed is better than a thousand men's speeches, and the Battle of the Confederates is a model of the Muslims' victory through "there is no god but God" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 01:00:30}.
