## Overview
Ibn al-Dahhak besieges the Sanaa merchants in Saada and plans to march in early Rajab. Hassan ibn Uthman falls ill on the last Thursday of Jumada II 326 AH, the day he had promised to advance, and the attack is delayed.

## Detail
The book says Ibn al-Dahhak besieged the Sanaa merchants living in Saada, blocked the caravans from reaching them, and declared that he meant to shed their blood and take their women. The men prepared to march on Saada at the start of Rajab. Hassan ibn Uthman fell ill on the last Thursday of Jumada II of the same year, the day he had promised them the advance on Saada, and the people took note; the book's words are that God's decision prevailed. The threat is attributed to Ibn al-Dahhak in the book and is not repeated here as the museum's own judgement.
