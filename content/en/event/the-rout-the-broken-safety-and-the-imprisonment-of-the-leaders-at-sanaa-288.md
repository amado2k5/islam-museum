## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad says: the people were routed until they had left Sana'a, and [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] went out after them, driving them off, and a number of them were killed on the parade ground; then [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] caught up with him and asked him to go back to his house, and he went back, took off his arms and told his men to keep to his door {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.210}. The [[group/al-jafatim-sanaa|the Jafatim]] at once sent to Abu al-Atahiya asking for safety; he spoke to al-Hadi for them, and al-Hadi answered: do as you wish; Abu al-Atahiya sent word to go to their homes, and they all went into Sana'a {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.210}. The next day al-Hadi had a crier proclaim the pay of the army; some of the troops rode out and took their stipend but not all of them came, and ugly words from them reached al-Hadi; the safety that had been given them was broken, and they agreed to fight him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.210}.

## Detail
The following day al-Hadi sent for their chiefs and leaders and men of force and, in the narrator's word, corruption; when they came to his house he ordered them put in prison and irons, took their weapons and mounts and shared them among [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|the Tabaris]], and, as the narrator puts it, the town grew quiet and secure, people put on well-being and the sedition ceased {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.210}. The narrator's sentence on the breaking of the safety does not name who broke it, so the museum says only that the safety was broken and that they agreed to fight him, as the text goes on; the editor's footnote says manuscript S reads "the safety, and he gave them safety". The sentence on the killing on the parade ground also leaves its doer unclear in the printed text, so the museum says only that a number of them were killed. The imprisonment is an act reported as the book gives it, with no judgment and no comparison.
