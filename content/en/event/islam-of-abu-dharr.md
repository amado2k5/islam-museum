## Summary
Abu Dharr al-Ghifari came to Mecca wanting to meet the Prophet, and disliked asking about him lest Quraysh learn of his intent, so he spent nights without asking anyone. Ali saw him, recognized that he was a stranger, hosted him for three nights without asking him anything, and then asked the reason for his arrival; after confirming that he wanted to meet the Prophet, he guided him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.33-34}.

## Meeting the Prophet
Ali said to Abu Dharr: "Indeed it is true, and he is the Messenger of God; so in the morning follow me, and if I see anything I fear for you, I will stand as though pouring water, and if I walk on, follow me." Abu Dharr heard from the Prophet and embraced Islam, and the Prophet instructed him to return to his people and inform them until his command reached him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.34}.

## Publicly Declaring the Profession of Faith
Abu Dharr went out to the Mosque and called out the two testimonies of faith at the top of his voice; the people rose against him and threw him down, until al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib rescued him, warning them of the retaliation of Ghifar and the risk to their trade passing through their territory to Syria {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.34}.

## What the Book Draws from the Incident
The author sees lessons in the incident: patience in seeking information, caution before speaking, Ali and Abu Dharr agreeing on a signal to cover their movements if anyone watched them, and that the Companions possessed a strong sense of security awareness {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.34-35}. In a nearby passage, the book cites al-Dhahabi's ruling that the report's chain of transmission is weak and its text rejected, followed by Ahmad Shakir's ruling authenticating it, without the author deciding between the two rulings in this passage {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.33}. The book views the incident more from the angle of security and planning than from that of strict verification of every detail.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not mention a date for Abu Dharr's conversion to Islam; the recorded date is derived from the Meccan period and marked as approximate.
- The detail of the three nights and Ali's guidance is transmitted with a chain whose grading differs between the two rulings mentioned; the detail is treated as the author's narration rather than an independent verification.
