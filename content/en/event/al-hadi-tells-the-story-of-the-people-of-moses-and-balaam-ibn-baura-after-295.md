## Overview
Al-Hadi tells his companions the story of the people of Moses and of Balaam ibn Ba'ura, and they accept that their turning back was the fault of their own deeds.

## Detail
Al-Hadi went on with his companions to the people of Moses. He said that when they turned back on their heels, sorrow and anger came upon them, and that after they repented Moses set up his cloak as a sign that their repentance was accepted. He said that Moses ordered the trumpet blown at dawn on a Friday, because his army complained that they could not feel his movement, and God inspired him with brass horns and drums. After the repentance their feet stood firm and the enemy turned away in flight. He then named Balaam ibn Ba'ura, whose punishment was that his tongue hung out like a dog's for some days, and then death came upon him. He cited a verse of Surat al-A'raf, which this record does not reproduce. The companions said that they now understood that al-Hadi held that their turning back was the fault of their own deeds.
