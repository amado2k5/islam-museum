## Summary
[[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman al-Farisi]] died at al-Mada'in. The books of the Companions gather the accounts of his dying in three closely related reports: his weeping when his companions visited him, because the Prophet had charged them that each one's provision be like a rider's; his request for the pouch of musk to be sprinkled around his bed; and the little that was found in his house after his death. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 200}

## His weeping and the Prophet's charge
Amir ibn Abdallah reports that when death came to him they noticed some distress in him and asked: what troubles you, Abu Abdallah, when you have precedence in good and fought good campaigns and great conquests beside the Messenger of God? He said it grieved him that their beloved Muhammad, when he left them, had charged them that a believer's needs be met like a rider's provision. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 197} Anas reports entering and asking why he wept, and receiving the same answer. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 197} In al-Hasan's report in Ahmad's Zuhd he wept as he was dying and, asked how he could weep as a companion of the Messenger of God, said he was weeping neither in sorrow for the world nor desire for it. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 scan vol. 3 p. 41}

## Who visited him
Ibn al-Jawzi reports from Abu Sufyan, from his elders, that [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]] visited him and Salman wept; Sa'd said that the Messenger of God had died pleased with him and that he would meet him at the Pool. Salman replied that he wept neither in dread of death nor out of greed for the world, repeated the charge, and added, "and around me these 'asawid'" — though all that lay about him was a basin, a bowl or a water-jug. Sa'd asked him for a covenant to hold to after him, and he said: Sa'd, remember God in your concern when you are concerned, in your judgement when you judge, and in your giving when you divide. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 200} In Anas's report in al-Dhahabi, Sa'd and Ibn Mas'ud entered upon him at death and he wept. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 412}

## The musk
From al-Sha'bi: Salman received a pouch of musk at the conquest of Jalula' and deposited it with his wife; when death came to him he said, bring the musk; he dissolved it in water and said, sprinkle it around me, for visitors are coming to me now who are neither human nor jinn. She did so, and he stayed only a little before he was taken. In another version he said they find the scent but do not eat food. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 200} From Sa'id ibn Suwqa: they visited him when he was suffering a stomach illness and he asked his wife what she had done with the musk they had brought from Balanjar; she threw it in water, mixed it and sprinkled it around his bed; they left and returned to find him taken. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 200} His wife Buqayra relates that he called her in her upper room, which had four doors, and told her to open them since he had visitors and did not know by which door they would enter; he had her put musk in a bowl and sprinkle it round his bed, then go down and wait; when she looked in she found his soul taken, as though he were asleep on his bed. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 200}

## What he left
Ibn al-Jawzi reports from Anas that when he died they looked through his house and found only a saddle-cloth, a mat and some goods valued at about twenty dirhams. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 200} In Amir ibn Abdallah's report his property was gathered and valued at fifteen dinars, while Abu Nu'aym says the other narrators agree on a little over ten dirhams. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 197} From Ali ibn Budhayma: his goods were sold and fetched fourteen dirhams. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 197} From Thabit: he left only some twenty-odd dirhams. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 412} Ibn al-Jawzi cites Ibn Abi Dawud that he had three daughters, one at Isfahan and two in Egypt. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 201}

## Death and grave
Ibn al-Jawzi says he died at al-Mada'in in Uthman's caliphate, and that some say in the year 32. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 201} At [[place/al-madain|al-Mada'in]] he died, according to Abu Ubayda and Ibn Zanjawayh, in the year 36 before the Battle of the Camel, and according to Khalifa in 37. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 412} The pages I read give no report of his washing, funeral prayer or the site of his grave, so I do not assign him a particular tomb.

## Differing reports
His death is dated 32, 36 or 37, and by al-Waqidi to Uthman's caliphate in general. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 412} It is said he lived 250 years, and the most that is said is 350; al-Dhahabi in his Siyar prefers the first, {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 412} but elsewhere, as a note in Sifat al-Safwa records, says he does not accept or authenticate it. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 201} The value of his goods is variously given as fourteen dirhams, twenty dirhams or fifteen dinars. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 197}
