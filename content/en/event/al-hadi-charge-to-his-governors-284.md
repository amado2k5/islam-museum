## Overview
After [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] appointed governors in the districts to collect food, he wrote each a charge, and the book gives its text under the heading "Copy of the covenant that al-Hadi ila al-Haqq covenanted to his governors". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.44} The report is in a Zaydi book transmitted by the imam's followers from [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] and his son [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]]; the text is shown as its transmitters give it, not as the museum's ruling. The editor notes differences between manuscripts in his footnotes.

## Appointment and the governor's conduct
The charge opens by saying that al-Hadi has appointed so-and-so over the collections of a named village and over gathering what God made due on the tithes, entrusting him with God's trust and His Messenger's. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.44} On reaching the town he is to enter with calm, dignity and remembrance of God, have a house rented for him to live in, put no burden of his upkeep, small or large, on anyone of the town, and accept no gift from anyone; a gift made to an official because of his post belongs to the treasury of the Muslims, and the text says this ruling passed from [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.44} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.45} See [[concept/bayt-al-mal]].

## What he begins with
The first task laid on the governor is enjoining good and forbidding wrong and teaching people to perform prayer and complete bowing and prostration; for a bedouin or anyone in the town who does not know enough Qur'an to pray with, he is to teach what he can of the mufassal, and to teach as much as he is able of the principles of religion, the merit of jihad and those who strive, knowledge of the truth and its people, and loyalty to those God ordered loyalty to among the family of His Prophet. The last is the Zaydi doctrine of the book, attributed here to the imam, not a position of the museum. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.45} See [[concept/amr-bil-maruf]] and [[concept/jihad]].

## The measures of the tithe
Crops watered by running water or rain are taxed a full tenth; those watered by camels drawing buckets and by water-wheels a half-tenth; the same for what Yemenis call "al-athari" and Iraqis "al-a'dha'". The minimum is thirty-three and a third farq, which is five full awsaq; below that the owner keeps his crop and nothing is taken. The footnotes explain that the "sawani" are the camels that draw the buckets, that a farq is three sa's and a wasq sixty sa's, and that al-Khalil says a wasq is a camel's load. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.45} Barley is not added to wheat, so no zakat falls on a kind until that kind itself reaches the stated measure. If a man has partners or scattered plots, they are added together, and if the total his land produced, in one place or several, reaches five awsaq, its zakat is taken; if not, the governor has no claim on him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.45} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.46} See [[concept/ushr]], [[concept/zakat]] and [[concept/nisab]].

## Trust and accountability
The charge tells the governor to put intention and trust first in guarding what he collects, reminds him that God sees every act and repays every deed, and cites the verse of al-Zalzala ([[passage/quran-99-7|Qur'an 99:7-8]]); he may take from the collection what he needs for his own upkeep and for those who help him, "in the accepted manner". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.46}

## The poor and the quarter of the collection
He is to write down the names of the town's poor and needy, but not everyone in need, only those with no means of earning or of doing without, because listing all who need would harm those who have no means; he is to prefer "the people of the matraba", the destitute, and set aside those who have some means of livelihood until God gives more. When their number is fixed he sets apart a quarter of his town's collection for them and writes to al-Hadi with their number and all that God assigned them, so that al-Hadi can write his view and how to distribute it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.46} The charge ends by telling him that if a traveller passes and complains of need he should strengthen him and gather up his scattered affairs, and run all his business toward what brings him near to God. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.47} See [[concept/sadaqa]].

## Notes
- The village and governor are not named: the text is a model copy that reads "so-and-so son of so-and-so".
- The technical terms and measures are given as the book gives them and are not compared here with other schools; the school of the charge is Zaydi, and it is attributed to them.
