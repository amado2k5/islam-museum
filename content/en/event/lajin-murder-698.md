## The killing of Lajin and the return of al-Malik al-Nasir in 698 AH
Qabjaq al-Mansuri, the governor of Syria, Baktamur al-Silahdar and others grew uneasy at the conduct of Mankutimur, and feared that he would strike at them. News had reached them that the Tatar king had entered Islam, so they agreed to march to him from Homs {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 769}.

Before long the news came that Sultan Lajin and his deputy Mankutimur had been killed at the hands of Karaji al-Ashrafi and those who stood with him. Karaji fell upon him with six men while he was playing chess after the night prayer, and the Hanafi judge Husam al-Din witnessed seven swords coming down upon him {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 769}.

His deputy was then seized and slaughtered the next day. It was announced that al-Malik al-Nasir would reign, and he was brought from Karak. He appointed Salar as the deputy of the realm. Then Tughaji and Karaji, the two Ashrafis, were killed. Al-Malik al-Nasir rode out wearing the robe and investiture of the caliph, and al-Afram was appointed governor of Damascus in Jumada I {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 769-770}.
