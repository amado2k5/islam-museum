## Summary
[[person/aisha|Aisha]] died in Ramadan, on the night of Tuesday the seventeenth in the most widely given account, in the year 58 AH; the books differ on the year between 57 and 58. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461} {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 921} The books I read agree that she charged that she be buried by night in al-Baqi, and Ibn Kathir reports that she told her nephew not to bury her in her room beside the Prophet and his two companions. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300}

## The room that held the graves
Aisha lived in the room where the Prophet was buried, and where Abu Bakr and Umar were buried after him. Ibn Kathir relates the report of al-Qasim, who went in to her and asked her to uncover the three graves for him; she uncovered three graves, neither raised high nor flat to the ground, spread over with red pebbles from the courtyard. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300}

## Her testament about burial
Ibn Kathir relates from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father, from Aisha, that she charged [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr]] not to bury her with them but to bury her with her companions in al-Baqi, and she gave as her reason that she did not wish to be extolled through it, ever. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300} The wording in the book is terse; it is read as meaning that Aisha did not want any merit attributed to her for lying beside the Prophet's grave. Ibn Kathir gives the report while speaking of the rebuilding of the Prophet's Mosque under al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, when the room was brought inside it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300}

## Ibn Abbas's visit in her last illness
Ibn Kathir relates from Imam Ahmad, from Dhakwan, Aisha's doorkeeper, that [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Abdallah ibn Abbas]] came asking leave to enter, while her nephew Abdallah was at her head. The nephew told her that Ibn Abbas was asking leave and that she was dying; she said, leave me out of Ibn Abbas. He answered that Ibn Abbas was one of her righteous sons, who greeted her and bade her farewell, and she said: let him in if you wish. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461} When he had come in and sat down he said: good news. She asked what of; he said that between her and meeting Muhammad and the loved ones there was only the soul leaving the body; that she had been the most beloved of the Prophet's wives to him, and he loved only what is good; he reminded her that her necklace had fallen at al-Abwa so that the allowance for dry ablution was revealed on her account, and that her vindication was revealed from above the seven heavens, so that no mosque of God lacks its recitation by night and by day. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461} She replied: leave me, Ibn Abbas; by Him in whose hand is my soul, I wish I had been a thing forgotten and lost. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461}

Ibn Hajar sets out al-Bukhari's version of the same report, in which she was overcome by the agony of death, said she feared being praised, and, asked how she found herself, answered that she was well if she had been God-fearing; he adds that the one who spoke to her was her nephew Abdallah ibn Abd al-Rahman, and that her freedman Dhakwan was the one who asked leave for Ibn Abbas. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 483} Ibn Hajar also notes the route through Ahmad and Ibn Sad that Ibn Kathir used. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 483}

## Her death and burial
Ibn Kathir says she died in the year 58, or a year before or a year after, that the common view is Ramadan though Shawwal is also said, that the best-known night is that of Tuesday the seventeenth of Ramadan, and that she charged that she be buried in al-Baqi by night. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461} [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] led the prayer over her after the witr prayer. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461} Five went down into her grave: Abdallah and Urwa, sons of al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam by her sister Asma bint Abi Bakr; al-Qasim and Abdallah, sons of her brother Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr; and Abdallah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461} He adds that she was sixty-seven, since the Prophet died when she was eighteen. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461}

Ibn Abd al-Barr cites Khalifa ibn Khayyat that she died in 58, on the night of Tuesday when seventeen nights of Ramadan had passed, that she ordered burial by night, so she was buried after the witr prayer in al-Baqi, Abu Hurayra praying over her, and he ascribes this to Salih ibn al-Wajih, al-Zubayr and a group of the biographers and chroniclers. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 921} Before that he gives the view that she died in 57, on the authority of al-Madaini through Ibn Uyayna, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 921} Ibn al-Athir gives the same accounts and the names of the five. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 1551}

## Grave and resting place
These sources say the grave is in al-Baqi, the cemetery of Medina, and that she wished to be there with her companions. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 300} In the pages I read I found no description of where the grave lies within al-Baqi nor any report of a structure over it, so I say nothing on that.

## Where the reports differ
Historians differ on the year of her death between 57 and 58, and Ibn Kathir gives a view of a year before and a year after; the month differs between Ramadan and Shawwal. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 461} {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 921} Ibn Hajar adds a point on the chain of Ibn Abbas's report: some commentators claimed that al-Bukhari's version is a mursal report since Ibn Abi Mulayka neither attended the exchange nor heard it from Ibn Abbas, and Ibn Hajar answers that nothing prevents his having been present. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 3 p. 483}

## Sources and documentation
