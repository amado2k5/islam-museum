## Overview
On Friday [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] set out for [[place/athafit|Athafit]], prayed the noon prayer at its first time near it, then drew up the army for war. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.101} The account is Zaydi and in the voice of the imam's followers; the museum gives it as the narrators tell it, without a verdict.

## The ranks
He put Banu Sarim, Banu Ma'mar and Khawlan on the right, Banu Malik of Banu Rabi'a in the centre and Banu Ubayd of Banu Rabi'a on the left; [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] brought out his men and arrayed them, the two sides closed and fighting began. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.101} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102}

## Al-Kitd and the ramp
The ground was rough and horses could do nothing in it, but al-Da'am took a place called [[place/al-kitd-athafit-yemen|al-Kitd]], smooth on top and hard to descend from the Athafit side, and had a ramp built to come down from it to Athafit; al-Hadi did not know. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} Fighting joined between al-Hadi's foot on the left and al-Da'am's foot on the right, and while it went on al-Da'am brought his horse up the ramp to the top of al-Kitd. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102}

## The numbers
The narrator says al-Da'am had a great many horse, 230 horsemen, and two thousand foot, and al-Hadi thirty horsemen and seven hundred foot. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} Elsewhere on the page he reckons those who went up on al-Kitd at about eight hundred foot and fifty horse. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} These are the narrator's figures, from an account that praises al-Hadi, and are given as they were said.

## Abu al-Qasim's charge and the ambush
Abu al-Qasim ibn al-Hadi went out with four horsemen, charged, and with them kept driving al-Da'am's men back until they neared Athafit. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} Al-Da'am had hidden horse and foot near the village; seeing his men being chased he split his horse and foot, sent some to face Abu al-Qasim, who achieved nothing and added to their companions' disorder, and himself came out with a hundred horse and a thousand foot and took the main track, meaning to cut off those of al-Hadi's men on al-Kitd. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} The editor's footnote says he found "al-Kitd" in none of the geographical books at hand, that one manuscript reads "al-Kabd", and that he prefers the original because "kitd" is the place where the shoulders meet and high places are called by it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} See [[event/al-hadi-stands-in-the-mahajja-at-athafit-285]].

## The date
Friday 14 Shawwal 285 AH, since the march was on Thursday and the night and then the Friday followed, corresponds to 3 November 898 in the Julian calendar, derived from the tabular calendar.
