## Summary
This is one of the longest financial testaments reported of a Companion. [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr]] relates it from his father [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] in Sahih al-Bukhari, which Fath al-Bari prints in the chapter on the blessing of the fighter in his wealth, living and dead; {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 262} al-Dhahabi summarises it in his life of al-Zubayr and adds the figures of the estate and the names of those who bought his land. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 130}

## Background
Al-Dhahabi says al-Zubayr, on the day of the Camel, was met by Ali, who reminded him of something he had heard from the Messenger of God; he swore not to fight him and withdrew from the fighting. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 125} He relates from Amr ibn Jawan that after Talha was killed and the ranks broke al-Zubayr came to Safawan, where al-Na'ar al-Mujashi'i met him and offered him his protection, and mentions that Ibn Jurmuz killed him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 125} Al-Bukhari and others say he was killed in Rajab 36 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 127} Al-Dhahabi says Wadi al-Siba' is seven farsakhs from Basra. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 128} In Abd Allah's account the testament was given as his father stood in the Camel line before the fighting. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 262}

## The testament and what it said
Hisham ibn Urwa relates from his father from Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr: when al-Zubayr stood on the day of the Camel he called me and I stood by his side. He said: my son, only a wrongdoer or a wronged man will be killed today, and I think I shall be killed wronged today; and one of my greatest worries is my debt. Do you think our debt leaves anything of our property? My son, sell our property and pay my debt. He bequeathed a third, and a third of that third to his sons, meaning Abd Allah's sons, and said: if anything is left of our property after the debt, a third of it is for your children. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 262} Al-Dhahabi gives it as: he bequeathed a third, and a third of the third, to Abd Allah, and what remained after the debt was for his children. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 129} Ibn Hajar quotes al-Muhallab on the wording and that the third of the third is a third of what was bequeathed to his sons, and concedes the phrase needs explanation. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 265} Abd Allah continues: he kept charging me about his debt, saying: my son, if you cannot manage any of it, ask help of my Master. I did not understand until I said: Father, who is your Master? He said: God. Whenever I fell into difficulty over his debt I said: Master of al-Zubayr, pay it for him, and He paid. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 129} {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 91}

## How the debt was settled
Al-Dhahabi relates that al-Zubayr was killed leaving neither a dinar nor a dirham except land at al-Ghaba and houses in Medina, Basra, Kufa and Egypt; his debt arose because men used to bring him money to hold and he would say no, it is a loan, I fear for it being lost; he had never held command, collected revenue or taxes. Abd Allah reckoned his debt and found it two million two hundred thousand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 129} Hakim ibn Hizam met Abd Allah and asked how much his brother owed; Abd Allah hid it and said a hundred thousand; Hakim said: I do not think your property will cover it. And if it is two million two hundred thousand? I do not think you can bear that; if you fall short, ask my help. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 129} Al-Zubayr had bought al-Ghaba for a hundred and seventy thousand; Abd Allah sold it for one million six hundred thousand and called: whoever has a debt on al-Zubayr, come to us at al-Ghaba. Abd Allah ibn Ja'far, owed four hundred thousand, offered to forgo it; Abd Allah refused, and he asked for a plot, which was sold to him against the debt. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 130} Four and a half shares remained: al-Mundhir ibn al-Zubayr took a share for a hundred thousand, Amr ibn Uthman one, Ibn Rabi'a one, and Mu'awiya the share and a half for a hundred and fifty thousand; Ibn Ja'far sold his holding to Mu'awiya for six hundred thousand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 130}

## The division and what the estate came to
When Ibn al-Zubayr finished paying the debt, al-Zubayr's sons said: divide our inheritance among us. He said: no, by God, not until I have called at the pilgrimage for four years: whoever has a debt on al-Zubayr, let him come and we shall pay it. He called each year, and after four years he divided it. Al-Zubayr had four wives; after the third, each received one million one hundred thousand, and all his property came to fifty thousand thousand and two hundred thousand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 130} Al-Dhahabi also gives Ibn Uyayna from Hisham from his father: al-Zubayr's property was divided on forty thousand thousand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 129} In a report from Ibn Qutayba from Hisham, al-Zubayr left goods worth fifty thousand thousand dirhams and fifty thousand thousand in cash. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 128} The share of Atika bint Zayd from his estate reached eighty thousand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 130}

## Death and grave
Fath al-Bari says al-Zubayr turned away and Amr ibn Jurmuz killed him at Wadi al-Siba'. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 265} Al-Dhahabi quotes a line of poetry mourning the loss of whoever Wadi al-Siba' holds in its grave. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 127} His age is given as sixty-four by al-Waqidi and Ibn Numayr, or a little over fifty, which al-Dhahabi finds likelier. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 128} Al-Dhahabi relates that Ibn Jurmuz went to Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr seeking justice for al-Zubayr, and Mus'ab wrote to his brother, who replied: shall I kill Ibn Jurmuz for al-Zubayr? Not for the strap of his sandal. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 128} For the later tradition on the site of the grave see [[event/zubayr-tomb-basra-386|The discovery of al-Zubayr's grave at Basra, 386]].

## Where the reports differ
The size of the estate is given differently: fifty thousand thousand and two hundred thousand in one report, forty thousand thousand in Ibn Uyayna's, and fifty thousand thousand in goods plus the same in cash in Ibn Qutayba's. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 130} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 129} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 128} His age has three reports. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 128} Al-Muhallab reads the phrase 'a third of the third' one way and Ibn Hajar says it does not make the arrangement clear. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 265} The books read do not say who washed him or prayed over him.
