## Overview
Abraham settled his wife Hagar and her infant Ishmael in a barren valley at the site of the Sacred House; Zamzam burst forth, and Jurhum settled by her leave — the beginning of Mecca's settlement in the Sira narrative.

## The story
Abraham brought out Hagar and her child Ishmael and settled them in a barren valley beside [[building/kaaba|the Sacred House]], whose site was then a rise of land like a hillock that floods reached {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. He left them a bag of dates and a water-skin, then returned to Palestine {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

When the provisions and water ran out, the well of [[place/mecca|Zamzam]] burst forth by God's favour and became their sustenance and provision for a time {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. Then a Yemeni tribe, [[group/jurhum|Jurhum]], came and settled by Ishmael's mother's leave; Bukhari's account states plainly that they settled in Mecca even before Ishmael grew up, and that they used to pass through this valley before that {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## Aftermath
Abraham would journey to Mecca from time to time to look in on his descendants; the sources preserve not all the journeys but four of them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}. Ishmael learned Arabic from Jurhum, won their affection and pleased them, and they married him to a woman of theirs {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## For young visitors
Abraham left his wife and their infant son in an empty valley, so God made the well of Zamzam for them; the tribe of Jurhum came to live there and Ishmael grew up among them.
