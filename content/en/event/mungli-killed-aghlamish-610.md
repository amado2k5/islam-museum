## Summary
Mankli, lord of Hamadhan, Isfahan and Rayy, was defeated by the caliph's army and Uzbak ibn al-Bahlawan, then killed at the city of Sawa; the country passed to Aghlamish, mamluk of Uzbak's brother. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 364}

## Details
Under the events of 612 AH, Ibn al-Athir reports that after Mankli killed Aytaqmish he estranged his own master, the amir Uzbak ibn al-Bahlawan, lord of Azerbaijan; the caliph incited Uzbak against him promising support, and wrote to Jalal al-Din the Ismaili, lord of the Ismaili fortresses, ordering him to aid Uzbak, with the lands divided between them by agreement. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 364} The caliph equipped a large army led by his mamluk Muzaffar al-Din Sanqar, called Wajh al-Sab', and ordered Muzaffar al-Din Kukburi, lord of Erbil, to come as supreme commander; armies of Mosul, the Jazira and Aleppo joined and they marched on Hamadhan. Mankli took to the mountains; supplies ran short for the caliph's army, but he grew ambitious, came down, and they fought the fiercest of battles; defeated, he fled by night, his army scattered, and the victors took the lands — Jalal al-Din got his share and Uzbak took the rest, handing it to Aghlamish, his brother's mamluk. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 364} Mankli fled to Sawa, whose governor was his friend; admitted and lodged in the governor's house, his weapons were taken and the governor prepared to bind and send him to Aghlamish; Mankli begged to be killed instead, so he killed him and sent his head to Uzbak, who forwarded it to Baghdad on a celebrated day. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 365}
