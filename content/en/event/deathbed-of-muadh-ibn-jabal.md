## Summary
The biographical dictionaries report that [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] took charge of the people in the [[event/plague-of-amwas|plague of Amwas]] after [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] was struck and named him successor. He preached to them and prayed that his own household might have a share of the affliction; his family died, then he fell ill and died in the district of the Jordan. The books preserve his last words welcoming death and his counsel to those around him that knowledge should be sought from four Companions. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 81}

## Context
The plague struck Syria in the year 18 according to most reports; Abu Zur'a says it ran through the years 17 and 18 and that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] turned the army back from Sargh in the first. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 652} Sifat al-Safwa relates that when the epidemic swallowed the land people said it could only be the Flood, and Mu'adh stood and told them it was the mercy of their Lord, the prayer of their Prophet and the death of the righteous before them. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 181} Ibn Abd al-Barr reports that [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] urged people to scatter from it as from a fire, and that Mu'adh answered that he had heard the Prophet call it a mercy to this community. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 652} In Ibn Asakir's version it is [[person/shurahbil-ibn-hasana|Shurahbil ibn Hasana]] who answers Amr, and Mu'adh then prays on hearing of it. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 3 p. 307}

## His sermon and prayer
After Abu Ubayda was struck and named Mu'adh in his place, Mu'adh addressed the people: this affliction is a mercy for you, the prayer of your Prophet and the death of the righteous before you, and Mu'adh asks God to apportion to the house of Mu'adh its share. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 81} In Ibn Asakir's report he prays that the share of the house of Mu'adh be the fullest, recalling that the Prophet had told them they would reach Syria and be struck with swellings through which God would grant them martyrdom. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 3 p. 307}

## What befell his household
Usd al-Ghaba says two of his wives were struck and died, then his son [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-muadh|Abd al-Rahman]] was struck and died. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 4 p. 193} Ibn Asakir instead has two daughters dying, whom he buried in a single grave. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 3 p. 307} Sifat al-Safwa adds that Abd al-Rahman, the son he loved best, fell ill that evening; asked how he was, he answered with a verse from [[passage/quran-3-60|Qur'an 3:60]], and Mu'adh said that God willing he would find him among the patient; he kept the body through the night and buried it next day. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 198} Then Mu'adh himself was struck in the palm; he would look at it, turn the back of his hand, and say he would not exchange what was in it for anything of this world. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 81}

## His words on the deathbed
He would lose consciousness and, on waking, say: O God, choke me with Your choking, for by Your might You know that I love You. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 4 p. 193} Amr ibn Qays reports that as death came he asked again and again whether morning had come until he was told it had; he sought refuge from a night whose morning leads to the Fire, welcomed death as a dear visitor arriving in need, and said he had feared God and now hoped in Him; he had not loved the world and long life for digging canals or planting trees, but for the thirst of midday heat, the labour of the hours and crowding the scholars on the knee at the circles of remembrance. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 4 p. 193} The same wording appears in Ahmad's Kitab al-Zuhd. {cite:zuhd-ahmad-dkim-1983 scan vol. 3 p. 230} Usd al-Ghaba also cites al-Hasan that he wept and, asked how he could weep as a companion of the Messenger of God, said he wept neither in dread of death nor for a world left behind but because he did not know which of the two handfuls he belonged to. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 4 p. 193}

## His testament
Al-Dhahabi reports from Yazid ibn Umayra that when death came to Mu'adh they asked him for counsel, and he said three times: knowledge and faith keep their places, and whoever seeks them finds them; seek knowledge from four: Uwaymir Abu al-Darda', Salman, Ibn Mas'ud and Abdallah ibn Salam. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 3 p. 251} In Ibn Asakir a man called Umayra weeps beside him, saying he weeps for the knowledge he used to gain from Mu'adh; Mu'adh tells him not to weep, since Abraham lived when there was no scholar on earth and God gave him knowledge, and then names the same four. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 3 p. 307}

## What he left
The sources I read give no figure for Mu'adh's property. After the plague, however, Umar came to Syria and divided the inheritances of those who had died, since their affairs had confused the commanders. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 81} Ibn Abd al-Barr also quotes al-Waqidi that no child was ever born to him, {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 652} a statement at odds with the death of his son Abd al-Rahman before him.

## Death and grave
Ibn Abd al-Barr, citing al-Mada'ini, says he died in the district of the Jordan in the plague of Amwas in the year 18, aged thirty-eight. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 652} Ibn Kathir places his death east of the Ghawr of Baysan, {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 97} and al-Dhahabi says his grave is in the Ghawr. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 347} The pages I read do not report his washing, funeral prayer or burial.

## Differing reports
His age is given as thirty-eight by al-Mada'ini and Ibn Kathir, thirty-three or thirty-four by Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, and twenty-eight by Abu Hatim. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 652} The year of death is 18 in the best-known report, with 17 and 19 also given. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 97} Abu Zur'a reports Ahmad ibn Hanbal as saying the plague was in year 18 and that both Mu'adh and Abu Ubayda died in it. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 652}
