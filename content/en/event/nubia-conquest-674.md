## Summary
In 674 AH the sultan [[person/baybars|al-Zahir Baybars]] sent an army to Nubia and Dongola. They were victorious; the king of Nubia was captured and sent to the sultan, and a poll tax was placed on the people of Dongola. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.728}

## Earlier attempts
The source relates from al-Dhahabi that Nubia was first attacked in 31 AH, when Abdallah ibn Abi Sarh raided it with five thousand horsemen. He made a truce with its people and returned without conquering it. It was attacked again in the time of Hisham and in the time of al-Mansur; then Takin the Turk, Kafur al-Ikhshidi and Nasir al-Dawla ibn Hamdan attacked it, and then Turanshah, brother of Saladin, in 568 AH. It had not been conquered until this year. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.728}

Ibn Abd al-Zahir said of this: "This conquest was witnessed with the eye, not something known only from chains of transmission." {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.728}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
[[person/ibn-abd-al-hakam|Ibn Abd al-Hakam]] reports from Yazid ibn Abi Habib that Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh, Uthman's governor of Egypt in the year 31, fought the Nubians, and several Muslims, among them Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj, lost an eye that day, so that the Nubians were called “archers of the eyes”; Abd Allah made a truce with them, being unable to overcome them. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 253} The truce provided that neither side would raid the other, that the Nubians would deliver a number of slaves each year, and that the Muslims would send them wheat and lentils. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 253} Some elders of Egypt report the agreed figure as 360 head a year, or 400, of which 360 went to the Muslims' fay' and 40 to the governor of the land. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 254}
