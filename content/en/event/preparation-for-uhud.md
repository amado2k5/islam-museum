## Summary
After the harsh defeat [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]] suffered at the [[event/battle-of-badr|battle of Badr]], Mecca was seized by fierce rage and an overwhelming desire for revenge {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 248}. The shock of losing its champions and nobles was a strong spur to muster every resource for a battle meant to erase that tragedy's traces and restore Mecca's standing.

## Details
Quraysh's leaders — headed by [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan ibn Harb]], Safwan ibn Umayya, Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl and Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'a — agreed on a comprehensive war against the Muslims {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 248}. To secure the needed funding, Quraysh sold the thousand camels by which the earlier caravan had escaped, raising fifty thousand dinars {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 248}.

Quraysh did not rely on its own strength alone: it opened volunteering to neighbouring tribes — the Ahabish, Kinana and the people of Tihama. It used incitement: Safwan ibn Umayya induced the poet Abu Uzza — released at Badr without ransom — to stir the tribes against [[place/medina|Medina]], in return for undertaking his daughters' support and enriching him if he returned alive {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 248, p. 249}. Quraysh also employed the poet Musafi' ibn Abd Manaf al-Jumahi for a like task in kindling the tribes' spirit of vengeance {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 249}.

Quraysh's resolve was hardened by the heavy economic losses it suffered in Zayd ibn Haritha's raid, which made it hasten to complete its equipment for a decisive battle {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 249}.
