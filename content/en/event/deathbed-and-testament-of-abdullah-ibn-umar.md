## Summary
[[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Abdallah ibn Umar]] died at Mecca after a wound to his foot received during the pilgrimage, in the year 73 on the account of most of those Ibn Abd al-Barr cites, about three months after the killing of Ibn al-Zubayr, or six months in another view. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420} This record gathers what the books say of the wound, his instruction about burial, his words of regret as he died, and what is said of his freeing of slaves and giving.

## The wound and al-Hajjaj's visit
Ibn al-Jawzi relates from Atiyya al-Awfi, from a freedman of Ibn Umar, that a man from Syria struck him on the foot with a spearhead; al-Hajjaj came to visit him and said: if I knew who hurt you I would strike off his head; Abdallah said: you are the one who hurt me; how? He said: on the day you let weapons into God's sanctuary. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229} In Ayyub's report from Nafi a litter-peg struck between two of his fingers at the Jamra in the crush and he fell ill; al-Hajjaj visited and he shut his eyes, and when al-Hajjaj spoke to him he did not answer. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229} In another report from Nafi, the spearhead of one of al-Hajjaj's men struck his foot and the wound closed, then reopened when he returned. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229} Ibn Abd al-Barr gives another account, that al-Hajjaj ordered a man with a lance to press up against him as people left Arafa and run the lance over his foot in the stirrup, the lance being poisoned it is said, because Ibn Umar used to go forward to the places where the Prophet had stood and this vexed al-Hajjaj. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420} When al-Hajjaj came to visit he asked: who did this to you, Abu Abd al-Rahman? He said: what will you do to him? He said: may God kill me if I do not kill him; Ibn Umar said: I do not think you will; you are the one who ordered the man who pricked me with the lance. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420} In another version he told al-Hajjaj: you are the one who ordered weapons into the sanctuary; he lingered some days and died. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420}

## His instruction about burial
Ibn al-Jawzi says that as death came he charged that he not be buried in the sanctuary, but he was overruled and was buried in the sanctuary and al-Hajjaj prayed over him. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229} In a report from Nafi he could not manage it because of al-Hajjaj, so they buried him in the cemetery of the Emigrants at Fakhkh near Dhu Tuwa. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229} Ibn Abd al-Barr says he charged that he be buried outside the sanctuary, which could not be done on account of al-Hajjaj, and he was buried at Dhu Tuwa in the Emigrants' cemetery, with al-Hajjaj praying over him. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420} Al-Dhahabi relays from Abu Bakr ibn al-Barqi that he died at Mecca and was buried at Dhu Tuwa, or at Fakhkh, the Emigrants' cemetery, in the year 74. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2442} The places named, [[place/dhu-tuwa|Dhu Tuwa]] and [[place/fakhkh|Fakhkh]], are near Mecca.

## Words of regret
Al-Dhahabi relates from Said ibn Jubayr that when Ibn Umar was dying he said: I grieve over nothing of the world except three things: the thirst of the midday heat, the labour of the night, and that I did not fight the rebellious party that came upon us, meaning al-Hajjaj. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2442} Al-Isti'ab has close reports: that he said he found in himself nothing about the world except that he had not fought the rebellious party with Ali ibn Abi Talib, and in another: I grieve over nothing except my leaving the fight against the rebellious party with Ali. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 421} The reports therefore differ over which party is meant, those who came upon them at Mecca or those against whom Ali stood.

## His freeing of slaves and his giving
Ibn al-Jawzi relates from Nafi that when anything of his property pleased Ibn Umar greatly he gave it to his Lord, that his slaves knew this and one of them would sometimes gird up and keep to the mosque, and when Ibn Umar saw him so he freed him, and he said to those who reproached him: whoever deceives us by God, we are deceived for him. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 206} Nafi says Ibn Umar did not die until he had freed a thousand people or more. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 206} It is reported he sometimes gave thirty thousand in one sitting, and that twenty-two thousand dinars came to him and he did not rise until he had distributed them. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 206} In his illness he longed for fish and gave it to a beggar who came. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 206} In the pages I read I found no report of an endowment or of a detailed inheritance, nor of a bequest of money, so I say nothing on those.

## His age and the disagreement over his death
Ibn Abd al-Barr says Malik related that he reached eighty-six and gave legal opinions in Islam for sixty years. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420} Al-Dhahabi says Malik said eighty-seven, that Damra ibn Rabia, Abu Nuaym, al-Haytham ibn Adi, Abu Mushir and others said he died in 73, that Said ibn Ufayr and Khalifa said 74, and he judges that he died at the end of 73, reckoning eighty-five from Ibn Umar's saying that he was fourteen at Uhud. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2442} Sifat al-Safwa says he died at Mecca in 74, or 73, aged eighty-four. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229}

## Grave and resting place
The sources agree that his grave is near Mecca in the Emigrants' cemetery and differ over naming the place, Dhu Tuwa or Fakhkh, and over whether al-Hajjaj prayed over him. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229} {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420} They do not describe a standing grave.

## Where the reports differ
They differ on the cause of the wound: the crush and a litter, a man from Syria, or a man al-Hajjaj ordered. They differ on the meaning of the rebellious party, the year of death, the place of the grave, and whether the burial was in or outside the sanctuary. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 229} {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 420} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2442}
