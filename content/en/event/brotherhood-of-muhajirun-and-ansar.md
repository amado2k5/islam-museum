## Summary
The Prophet established brotherhood between the Muhajirun and the Ansar in the house of [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]]. There were ninety men, half from the Muhajirun and half from the Ansar, bound by mutual consolation (al-muwasah). After death they inherited from one another, to the exclusion of blood relatives, until the battle of Badr. Then inheritance by kinship was restored with the revelation of the verse on kinship {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.185} {cite:zad-al-maad 2/56}.

## What the brotherhood meant
Muhammad al-Ghazali said that the meaning of this brotherhood was that the partisanship of the Jahiliyya melted away, so that zeal was only for Islam, and distinctions of lineage, colour and homeland fell away {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.186} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p.140-141}. It was a binding pact tied to blood and wealth, not an empty word {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.186}.

## Examples
The Prophet made brothers of [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]] and [[person/sad-ibn-al-rabi|Sa'd ibn al-Rabi']]. Sa'd offered to divide his property with him and to divorce one of his two wives for him. Abd al-Rahman prayed God's blessing on him. When he later married, the Prophet asked him about his bridal gift, and he said: a nawa (a date-stone's weight) of gold {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.186} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/553}.

The Ansar asked the Prophet to divide the date palms between them and the Muhajirun. He said no. They said: then spare us the labour, and we will share the fruit with you. They said: we hear and we obey {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.186} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/312}.

## Notes for researchers
- Ibn al-Qayyim mentions that a second brotherhood among the Muhajirun themselves was said to have taken place, but the book prefers the first {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.185}.

## For young visitors
When the Muhajirun arrived in Medina they had no houses and no property. So the Prophet made each emigrant a brother to one of the Ansar, and the Ansari would give his brother from his wealth and his home.

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
In the ninth episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] makes brotherhood the distinctive mark of the society of Medina, and says that one of the Prophet's first acts there was the brotherhood pact between the Emigrants and the Helpers, which joined two distant branches, Adnanis of Quraysh from Mecca and Qahtanis of Medina, a unity Arabia had never known. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep09 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep09 00:05:30} He says the Prophet took care to pair a poor Emigrant with a rich Helper, and that the paired brothers at first inherited from one another until the verse giving precedence to blood relatives came down. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep09 00:06:00} He mentions a Helper who offered to divorce one of his two wives so that his Emigrant brother, who had no wife, might marry her, and that the Helpers asked the Prophet to divide the palm groves between them and their brothers. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep09 00:06:30} This is his telling, within his talk on mutual support.
