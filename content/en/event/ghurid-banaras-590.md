## Summary
In 590 AH Shihab al-Din al-Ghuri, king of Ghazna, met the Indian king of Banaras at the river Makhun; the Indian was routed and killed, and Shihab al-Din entered the Banaras country and plundered it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 231}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that Shihab al-Din had sent his mamluk Qutb al-Din raiding into India, where he killed, took captives and booty and returned; when the king of Banaras — the greatest king in India, whose realm stretched from the borders of China to the land of Malwa — heard of it he gathered his armies and marched seeking the lands of Islam, so Shihab al-Din marched from Ghazna with his troops and they met at Makhun, a great river; the Indian had seven hundred elephants and a vast host said to be a million men, among them Muslim amirs descended from the days of Sultan Mahmud ibn Sabuktigin. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 231} In the battle the unbelievers held firm through numbers and the Muslims through courage; the unbelievers broke, slaughter filled the field, only boys and slave-girls were taken while the men were killed, ninety elephants were captured, and the Indian king was killed, recognized only by his teeth whose roots had decayed and were bound with a band of gold. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 231} Shihab al-Din then entered the Banaras country, loaded its treasuries onto fourteen hundred camels, and returned to Ghazna with the captured elephants, among them a white one. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 231}
