## Overview
The battle known in European books as the Battle of the Pyramids is called by [[person/abd-al-rahman-al-jabarti|al-Jabarti]] the battle of Imbaba. It was fought on the Imbaba bank west of the Nile, opposite Bulaq, on Saturday 7 Safar 1213 AH; the Mamluk army broke before the French, who then entered [[place/cairo|Cairo]]. It is part of [[event/french-campaign-egypt-1213|the French campaign in Egypt]]. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.39} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.40}

## The first skirmish
News came on Sunday 1 Safar that the Egyptian army had met the French on Friday 29 Muharram, "and within an hour Murad Bey was routed". It was only a skirmish between the vanguards; Murad Bey's boat blew up with its ammunition and its master gunner Khalil al-Kirdili, and terror seized him, so that he fled leaving his baggage and guns. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.37}

## The preparations
It was agreed to build earthworks from Bulaq to Shubra, held by Ibrahim Bey and the pasha, while Murad Bey built earthworks on the Imbaba bank as far as Bashtil and filled his boats and galleons with troops and guns. The ulama met at al-Azhar every day to read al-Bukhari, and the Sufi orders held gatherings. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.37} A general call to arms went out and the people went out to the Bulaq bank; each craft guild pooled money and put up tents for itself, "and no one at that time held back anything he owned". Al-Sayyid Umar Efendi, the naqib al-ashraf, brought down from the Citadel a great banner that the people called the Prophet's banner. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.38} Powder and lead became dear, the countryside rose "with people killing one another", and the Bedouin raided the outskirts. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.38}

Al-Jabarti faults the amirs because none of them had the energy to send a spy or a scouting party before the French arrived; Ibrahim Bey and Murad Bey each stayed where he was, "and there was no citadel, no fortress, no stronghold". He calls this "bad management and neglect of the enemy", and describes the soldiery as "with hearts estranged, resolve dissolved and opinions divided". {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.39}

## The battle
The French reached al-Jisr al-Aswad on Friday 6 Safar and on Saturday morning were at Umm Dinar. At midday some of the troops on the west bank rode out towards Bashtil and met the French vanguard, who struck them "with their rapid-firing muskets"; Ayyub Bey the daftardar, Abd Allah, kashif of al-Jurf, and several of Muhammad Bey al-Alfi's kashifs were killed. They were followed by a French column of some six thousand under "Dizah" (Desaix); al-Jabarti says Bonaparte "did not witness the battle but arrived after the rout". {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.39}

On the east bank the common people raised a clamour — "O Lord! O Gracious One! O men of God!" — and the sensible told them to stop, saying that the Prophet and the Companions fought with the sword, not by raising their voices, but they would not listen. A body of amirs tried to cross to the west bank and jammed the ferry landings, for there were very few boats. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.39} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.40} The wind blew hard from the enemy's side, driving sand into the Egyptians' faces, which al-Jabarti counts "among the greatest causes of the defeat". {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.40}

The column then divided until it surrounded Murad Bey's force front and rear. The fighting lasted "about three-quarters of an hour"; then the western army broke. Many horsemen drowned, some were captured, the French took the earthworks, and Murad Bey fled to Giza and then south. Among those who threw themselves into the river were Sulayman Bey al-Agha, who survived, and his brother Ibrahim Bey al-Wali, who drowned. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.40}

## The flight from Cairo
When the western army broke, the French turned their guns on the east bank. Ibrahim Bey, the pasha and the amirs rode off towards al-Adiliyya, leaving tents and baggage, and the people poured back into the city "in utter fear and terror" before sunset. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.40} A rumour spread that the Franks had crossed to Bulaq and burned it; the cause was that the galleon crews had set fire to a galleon at the Imbaba anchorage, and Murad Bey had ordered his great galleon burned when it stuck in the mud, and people seeing the flames were sure the towns were burning. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.40} {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.41}

That night most of the people of Cairo left for Upper Egypt and the east; a lame donkey sold for many times its price, and women went out on foot with their children. Outside the gates Bedouin and peasants stripped them of goods and clothes, and al-Jabarti says there happened "what never happened in Egypt, nor have we heard of anything like part of it in the histories of those before us". {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.41}

## A note on the text
Al-Jabarti's own Muddat al-Faransis and Mazhar al-taqdis add material at this point, including a comparison of the Egyptian soldiery with the French, as the editor Shmuel Moreh shows in his notes. {cite:ajaib-al-athar-moreh-v3 p.39}
