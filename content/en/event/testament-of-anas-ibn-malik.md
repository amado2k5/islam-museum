## Summary
[[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]], the Prophet's servant, lived at Basra until he died there, and is counted among the last Companions to die in that city. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 101} The books preserve two instructions of his: that a hair of the Prophet be put under his tongue, and that Muhammad ibn Sirin wash him. To this are added what is reported of his death and his burial in his palace near Basra.

## The hair under the tongue
Al-Isaba relates that Ibn al-Sakan reported, through Safwan ibn Hubayra from his father, that Thabit al-Bunani said: Anas ibn Malik said to me: this is a hair from the hair of the Messenger of God, put it under my tongue. He said: I put it under his tongue and he was buried with it under his tongue. {cite:isaba scan vol. 1 p. 277} I did not find this report in the other books I read; Ibn Hajar gives it on Ibn al-Sakan's chain. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 101} Anas himself said, in a report Ibn Hajar gives on the same page, that none was left who had prayed to both qiblas but himself. {cite:isaba scan vol. 1 p. 277}

## The small staff
It is sometimes said that a small staff of his was buried with him, but I did not find a report of a staff in the pages I read of al-Isaba, al-Isti'ab, Hilyat al-Awliya, Sifat al-Safwa, al-Bidaya wal-Nihaya or the Siyar, so I say nothing on it.

## His charge about who should wash him
Abu Nuaym in the Hilya relates, through Ibrahim ibn Habib ibn al-Shahid from Hisham, that Anas ibn Malik charged that [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Muhammad ibn Sirin]] wash him; Ibn Sirin was in prison, and when this was put to him he said: I am a prisoner; they said: we asked the governor and he allowed you; he said: the governor did not imprison me, the one who has the right imprisoned me; the man with the right allowed him and he came out and washed him. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 2 p. 267} Ibn al-Jawzi gives it in Sifat al-Safwa from Hisham. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 629} Ibn Kathir gives it in his notice of Ibn Sirin, saying Ibn Sirin had been given a fine bearing, gravity and humility. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 293}

## Death and burial
Ibn Abd al-Barr, citing al-Hasan ibn Uthman, says Anas ibn Malik died in his palace at al-Tuff, two farsakhs from Basra, in 91, and was buried there. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 4 p. 491} He cites Abu al-Yaqzan that Qatan ibn Mudrik al-Kilabi prayed over him. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 4 p. 491} He says Khalifa gives 93 at the age of a hundred and three, and it is also said a hundred and ten. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 4 p. 491} Ibn Sad relays from Muhammad ibn Abdallah al-Ansari that he was a hundred and seven when he died. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 4 p. 491}

## What he left
Ibn Abd al-Barr says it is said that eighty children were born to Anas, seventy-eight sons and two daughters, one called Hafsa and the other known as Umm Amr. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 54} In the pages I read I found no report of the amount of his property or the division of his estate.

## Grave and resting place
Al-Isti'ab says he was buried at al-Tuff near Basra where his palace stood. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 4 p. 491} The books I read give no description of the grave.

## Where the reports differ
The reports differ on his year of death, 90 in Shuayb ibn al-Habhab's account, 91 in al-Waqidi's and 93 in Khalifa's, and on his age at death, a hundred and three, a hundred and seven or a hundred and ten. {cite:isaba scan vol. 1 p. 277} {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 4 p. 491} The report of the hair stands alone in my reading, on Ibn al-Sakan's chain in al-Isaba, while the report about Ibn Sirin is shared by three books.

## Sources and documentation
This record rests on al-Isaba, Hilyat al-Awliya, Sifat al-Safwa, al-Bidaya wal-Nihaya and al-Isti'ab. The page numbers given are scan pages.
