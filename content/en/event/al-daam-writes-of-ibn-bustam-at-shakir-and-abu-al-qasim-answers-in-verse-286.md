## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad said: Muhammad ibn Sulayman told me that when [[person/abdullah-ibn-bustam|Ibn Bustam]] reached [[group/shakir-hamdan-tribe|Shakir]] and called out there, [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] learned of it and wrote to [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] telling him the news of Ibn Bustam, of what [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] had agreed on, that they wanted [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]], and that he was alarmed at what he had heard of their gathering. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.182} Abu al-Qasim wrote him an answer in verse. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.182}

## The poem
In the lines Abu al-Qasim says that a foolish man will be frightened by deeds and threats, meaning Ibn Bustam, that al-Hadi will bring him to account, that women of his people will weep for him, that they have already tasted the imam's thrust and blow, that Ibn Bustam left meeting him in his fort and ran off in fear and lost his chiefship, and now rouses his people after the rout; he says the believers are with the imam on guidance and long to meet him, that they fear none of the worlds even if the jinn were gathered, and that they help God's religion by His command. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.182} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.183} The lines carry words of abuse for Ibn Bustam which the museum does not repeat, giving the sense in neutral words; the editor notes the readings of the other manuscript.
