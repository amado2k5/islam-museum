## Summary
Peshawar Nights: the attributed transcript of a ten-night debate in Peshawar, 1927, between Sultan al-Wa'izin Shirazi and Sunni scholars. {cite:al-islam-murajaat}

## Content and contest
Sessions on Shi'ism, the Ahl al-Bayt and the caliphs that the text claims were transcribed for the press; Sunnis treat it as a one-sided apologetic text rather than a neutral record.

## The exchange — what each side said (as the text reports)
- **Sultanu'l-Wa'izin Shirazi (the Shia debater)**: drove the ten nights with a schedule of questions — succession and Ghadir, the merits of the Ahl al-Bayt, recorded Companion conduct — binding himself to argue from printed Sunni books (the Sahihs, Musnads and chronicles), and demanding his opponents verify every citation at source.
- **Hafiz Muhammad Rashid and Shaykh Abdus Salam (the Sunni debaters, as the text reports them)**: objected to the reports through chain and interpretation — accepting some merits of the Ahl al-Bayt while denying their bearing on a designated imamate, and answering the Saqifa accounts with ijtihad and public interest; in the later nights Shaykh Abdus Salam withdraws from the sessions, per the text.
- **The argument's course (as the text describes)**: the nights move from the root of succession to hadith (the Thaqalayn and the authority of the Ahl al-Bayt) then to historical conduct, closing with the audience's avowal recorded for the Shia debater.
- **The Sunni caveat (the museum's note)**: the Persian text was compiled by Shirazi himself, and Sunni examiners hold its proceedings to the compiler's authorship rather than to independently kept minutes — though the author records reporters publishing nightly; the events are thus claimed, not established, for Sunni readers. {cite:wikipedia-peshawar-nights} {cite:mahajjah-al-murajaat-critique}
