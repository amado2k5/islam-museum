## Summary
When the rebels besieged Uthman in his house, Ali was asked to lead the people in prayer, but he refused, saying he would not lead them while the leader was besieged, and prayed alone {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.62}.

## The Stance
Al-Aqqad states that the people called out to Ali, "O Abu al-Hasan, step forward and lead the people in prayer," and he said he would not lead them while the leader was besieged, but would pray alone, and then withdrew to his home {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.62}. He left his two sons along with the sons of a group of Companions guarding the caliph's house, so that the rebels would know they were assaulting every person of standing in Islam if they reached the caliph with violence {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.62}.

## What Transpired Between Ali and Uthman
The book relates that Ali entered upon Uthman and told him he saw the crowd intending nothing less than killing him, so order him and they would fight {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.61}. Uthman implored him to abandon fighting and not shed blood on his behalf; Ali repeated his offer and Uthman repeated his answer, and then Ali went out to the mosque {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.62}. Thereafter, the rebels scaled the house and killed him {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.62}.

## The Author's Reading
Al-Aqqad asks whether Ali bore any blame and whether he was capable of saving Uthman; he answers that Ali was in the most difficult position, caught between the caliph and the rebels, pressed for reform by one side and accused by the other, and that Muawiya was more capable of protecting Uthman had he wished {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.63}. This is the author's judgment, not the museum's judgment.
