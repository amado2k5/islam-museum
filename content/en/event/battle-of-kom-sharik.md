## Overview
After the peace, [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] led the Muslims out of [[place/al-fustat|al-Fustat]] towards [[place/alexandria|Alexandria]], with Coptic leaders who repaired the roads and set up bridges and markets for him. The Romans had prepared, and many ships had reached them from Roman lands. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 107} Amr met no one until Tarnut, where he fought a light engagement with a Roman detachment and routed it; then he met the whole Roman force at Kom Sharik, where they fought for three days until the Romans broke. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 107}

## Other reports
Abd al-Malik ibn Maslama, from Ibn Lahi'a from Yazid ibn Abi Habib, reports that Amr sent [[person/sharik-ibn-sumayy|Sharik ibn Sumayy]] after the Romans, and he caught and routed them at the mound called Kom Sharik. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 108} Another report says Sharik led Amr's vanguard and was driven onto the mound and surrounded; he sent Malik ibn Na'ima al-Sadafi on his matchless chestnut horse, who charged down the mound and outran the Romans to tell Amr, and when Amr came on the Romans withdrew. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 108}

## The Chestnut's Lane
Ibn Abd al-Hakam adds that the chestnut horse died and its owner buried it, and the spot in Misr was named the Chestnut's Lane (Khawkhat al-Ashqar) after it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 108}
