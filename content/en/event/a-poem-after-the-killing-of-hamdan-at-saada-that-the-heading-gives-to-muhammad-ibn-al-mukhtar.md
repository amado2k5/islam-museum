## Overview
A poem in the wafir metre under the heading "and also of his" on p. 328; the heading adds that the poem is correctly by Muhammad ibn al-Mukhtar and was said after his killing of "Hamdan" at Sa'da, and the margin of manuscript S says it is not al-Hadi's but al-Muntasir Muhammad ibn al-Mukhtar's, said when he took vengeance for his father at Raydah; in it the poet asks Salma why she blames him and tells of his attack, his thrusting and striking, and the easing of his heart by the killing of those who killed the imam.

## Detail
After the lines before it a new heading stands on p. 328: "and also of his" (wafir), and the heading itself adds that this poem is "by Muhammad ibn al-Mukhtar, correctly" and was "after his killing of Hamdan at Sa'da, the killers of his father" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}; so the heading itself ascribes it to someone other than al-Hadi, and the museum passes this on as printed. The first footnote says the margin of the original holds a few sentences, mostly effaced, of which the editor could read a mention of al-Hadi as the poet's grandfather and a line about "my father al-Hadi" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}, and the footnote of S says the poem "is not his" but is by the Imam al-Muntasir li-Din Allah Muhammad ibn the Imam al-Mukhtar li-Din Allah al-Qasim ibn Ahmad al-Nasir ibn al-Hadi, said when he sought vengeance for the killing of his father and killed a great killing where his father was killed at Raydah, saying it in boast {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}. In the poem the poet asks "on what am I blamed, Salma?" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}, says blame turned him from the addressee, and calls himself a Hashimite lion and brave hero {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}; he says Salma knows his open attack on the evening his soul did not fear death {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}, that he thrust unafraid and struck necks, bellies and heads {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}, and that he cooled his rancour and eased his soul by the killing of those who killed the imam {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}. The second footnote says one line and the nine lines after it are missing from S {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.328}. The museum does not decide here who composed the lines; it passes on what is printed in the heading and footnotes, in which the poet calls al-Hadi his father and a footnote ascribes the lines to a descendant of al-Hadi, and the question of the person and the time is recorded in the ledger. The book gives no date, so none is set. It runs on in [[event/the-same-poem-boasts-of-the-forefathers-the-sons-of-al-zahra-and-the-faith-given-to-yemen-and-sham|its continuation on p. 329]]. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
