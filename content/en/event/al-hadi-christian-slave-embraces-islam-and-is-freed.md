## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad relates from his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] that a man brought [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] a slave of a Christian owner and said the slave had come to love Islam. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.57} Al-Hadi told the slave to go and wash himself and his clothes and come back; on his return he told him to come near and sit and say "I testify that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger, sent to all people", and the slave said it as told; the margin of the manuscript notes "teaching the Christian the testimony of Islam". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.57} He then told the Christian to go with the slave and settle the price after a day or two so that its value would not be lost to him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.57}

## The freeing
Afterwards a man came and said the slave had been sold on but refused to do any work; al-Hadi told one of his companions: "Buy him from the zakat money that is for slaves and free him, so that his patronage belongs to all the Muslims; this is a poor man and we are most entitled to relieve him." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.57} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.58} The original reads "from the quarter of the slaves", and a margin note records the benefit that slaves are bought from the slaves' share; the editor notes it as the view of his uncle [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-qasim-al-rassi|Muhammad ibn al-Qasim]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.58} See [[concept/zakat]]. The book attributes this position to the Zaydis, and the museum gives it as stated, without comparison with other schools.
