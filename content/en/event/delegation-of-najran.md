## Summary
Sixty riders of the Christians of [[place/najran|Najran]] came to Medina in 9 AH; when they refused Islam the Prophet called them to mutual imprecation (mubahala), so they asked for peace — and he made peace with them on two thousand garments a year, the protection of God and His Messenger, and freedom in their religion {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 450-451}.

## The delegation's account
Najran was a large country seven stages from Mecca toward Yemen, comprising seventy-three villages, its people on the Christian religion {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 450}. The delegation numbered sixty men, twenty-four of them nobles, led by three: al-Aqib Abd al-Masih, master of rule; al-Sayyid al-Ayham, master of cultural and political affairs; and the bishop [[person/abu-haritha-ibn-alqama|Abu Haritha ibn Alqama]], master of religious leadership {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 450}. They asked the Prophet what he said of Jesus, and the mubahala verses came down upon him: [[passage/quran-3-61|Al Imran 61]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 450; ibn-hisham-sira}. When they refused Islam he called them to mubahala and came forward embracing al-Hasan and al-Husayn, Fatima walking at his back {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 451; zad-al-maad}. Al-Aqib and al-Sayyid said to each other: do not do it — by God, if he is a prophet and we imprecate him, neither we nor our descendants after us will prosper — and they agreed to accept his judgment over their affair {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 451}. The Prophet accepted the jizya from them and made peace on two thousand garments, a thousand in Rajab and a thousand in Safar, an uqiyya with each garment; he gave them the protection of God and His Messenger and left them complete freedom in their religion, and sent Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, "the trusted man of this community", to collect the peace payment {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 451}. They relate that al-Sayyid and al-Aqib became Muslim after returning to Najran {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 451}.

## For young visitors
A great delegation of the Christians of Najran came to ask the Prophet about Jesus; when they disagreed, he invited them to a solemn mutual prayer, and they chose peace instead — so the Prophet covenanted with them for the jizya and guaranteed their religion and their houses of worship.
