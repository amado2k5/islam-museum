## Summary
In 448 AH a severe famine and heavy plague struck Egypt. Iraq was full of strife, fear and plunder by the followers of Tughril Beg, by Bedouin, and by al-Basasiri {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 278}.

## Prices
Ibn al-Jawzi wrote in al-Shudhur: famine and plague fell on people, the air grew thin, flies multiplied, and hunger became so great that people ate carrion. A measure of greens seed reached seven dinars, and a quince or a pomegranate a dinar, and so did a cucumber. Famine and plague spread across all the lands {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 278}.

## The three thieves
A letter came from Egypt that three thieves had broken into a house, and in the morning they were found dead: one at the door of the house, the second at the top of the stairs, and the third on the bundle of clothes {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 278}.
