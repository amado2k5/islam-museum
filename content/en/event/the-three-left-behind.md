## Summary
The Prophet entered Medina after Tabuk, a band of hypocrites having stayed behind per Ibn Ishaq — and three Muslims stayed behind "in neither doubt nor hypocrisy": Ka'b ibn Malik, Marara ibn al-Rabi' and Hilal ibn Umayya. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/175}

## The account
Ibn Ishaq said: the Prophet said to his companions "speak to none of these three", and the hypocrites who stayed behind came swearing oaths and making excuses, and he pardoned them — "and the Muslims shunned speech with those three". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/175} The story's detail is Ka'b ibn Malik's own account, on Ibn Ishaq's chain from al-Zuhri, in [[work/kaab-ibn-malik-account-of-tabuk|Ka'b's account]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/175}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- Describing the others who stayed behind as hypocrites is Ibn Ishaq's phrasing; none of them is named here.
- "And neither God nor His Messenger excused them" (p. 175): the verb's vowelling on the page is not settled, and the phrase attaches to the excusers in context; to be reviewed.
- "Eighty-odd men", the number of the excusers, is on Ka'b's tongue (p. 177).
