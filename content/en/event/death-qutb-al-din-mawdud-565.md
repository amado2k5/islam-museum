## Summary
In Dhu al-Hijja of 565 AH, Qutb al-Din Mawdud ibn Zangi ibn Aqsunqur, lord of Mosul, died, and his son Sayf al-Din Ghazi succeeded him in place of the elder brother. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 24}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that his illness was an acute fever; when it worsened he bequeathed the kingdom to his elder son Imad al-Din Zangi, then changed it to his other son Sayf al-Din Ghazi. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 24} The reason the elder was passed over was that the steward and chief officer of his state was a servant of his named Fakhr al-Din Abd al-Masih, who disliked Imad al-Din because he was devoted to his uncle Nur al-Din — having long resided with him and married his daughter — while Nur al-Din hated Abd al-Masih; so Fakhr al-Din agreed with Khatun, daughter of Husam al-Din Timurtash ibn Ilghazi, to divert the kingdom to the other son. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 24}
