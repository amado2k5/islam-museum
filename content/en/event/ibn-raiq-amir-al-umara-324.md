## Overview
In 324 AH [[person/muhammad-ibn-raiq|Muhammad ibn Ra'iq]], amir of Wasit and its districts, took control of the state, and the office of the amirate of the amirs arose. Al-Suyuti relates this. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 605}

## Al-Suyuti's account
He ruled the lands, and the office of the vizierate and the registers were made void; he and his scribe took everything. Money was carried to him and the treasuries were emptied. [[person/al-radi-billah|Al-Radi]] remained with him as a figure, having of the caliphate only the name. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 605}

In 325 AH the lands came to be divided between those who seized them and governors who sent no money, like the kings of the factions. Only Baghdad and al-Sawad remained in al-Radi's hands. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 605}

## Ibn al-Athir's account
According to Ibn al-Athir, Ibn Ra'iq was in Wasit and also held Basra. After the killing of Mardawij the Turks wrote to him and asked for pardon; he summoned them and they went to him, and Bajkam came with them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 228}
