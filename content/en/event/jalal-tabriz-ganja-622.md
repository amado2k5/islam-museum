## Summary
In 622 AH Jalal al-Din inflicted a great defeat on the Georgians near their own lands, then returned to Tabriz, took the city of Ganja and married Uzbak's wife. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 450}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that the Georgians assembled more than seventy thousand fighters; Jalal al-Din marched at them, took their city of Dwin, then advanced on them; they fought a most grievous battle, the Georgians broke, and he ordered them killed on every road — confirmed reports put their dead at twenty thousand, some said more — and many of their nobles were captured, including Shalwa, while Iwani, their commander, fled and was pursued until he sheltered in a fortress which Jalal al-Din had besieged; he then spread his troops through the Georgian lands plundering, killing and ruining. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 450} Had nothing called him from Tabriz he would have taken the country without effort; the cause of his return was that the deputy he left there, his vizier Sharaf al-Din, reported that the chief men of Tabriz — its ra'is, Shams al-Din al-Tughra'i and other commanders — had conspired to resist him and hand the city back to Uzbak; Jalal al-Din hid the news, hastened to the Georgians and defeated them, left his army in their lands, and returned to Tabriz, where he took Ganja and married Uzbak's wife, daughter of Sultan Tughril. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 450}
