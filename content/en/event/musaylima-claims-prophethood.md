## Overview
When the [[event/delegation-of-banu-hanifa|delegation of the Banu Hanifa]] reached [[place/al-yamama|al-Yamama]], [[person/musaylima|Musaylima]] apostatised and claimed prophethood, saying that he had been given a share in the affair with Muhammad. He began to produce rhymed sayings for them, and the [[group/banu-hanifa|Hanifa]] united behind him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223}

## The account
Ibn Ishaq relates from a shaykh of the Banu Hanifa of al-Yamama that the delegation left the Prophet and brought Musaylima what he had been given. When they reached al-Yamama he apostatised, claimed prophethood and deceived them, saying: I have been given a share in the affair with him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223} He said to his delegation: did he not tell you when you mentioned me to him: "Indeed he is not in your place"? That was only because he knew that I had been given a share in the affair with him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223} Then he began producing rhymed sayings for them, saying among other things, to rival the Qur'an, words about the pregnant woman and the soul that moves between membrane and womb; he made wine and fornication lawful for them and removed the prayer from them, while still testifying that the Prophet was a prophet. The Hanifa united behind him on this. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223} Ibn Ishaq concludes: "God knows which of these was the case." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223}

## Notes from the editors
"Al-usayb" is a palm frond; "al-sifaq" is the thin part of the belly; "asfaqu 'ala dhalika" means they agreed on it; "mudahat" means resemblance. In one copy "al-sajiʿat" appears in place of "al-asajia". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: a report from some scholars of Medina in Ibn Ishaq**: Musaylima attended the Prophet's council covered in cloth and spoke with him and asked him, and the Prophet said to him: if you asked me for this frond, I would not give it to you. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/222} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223}
- **Sunni: a report from a shaykh of the Banu Hanifa in Ibn Ishaq**: Musaylima was left behind among the baggage and did not attend; the Prophet gave him the same as the others and said: "he is not in your place", meaning to guard the property of his companions. Ibn Ishaq does not prefer either account: "God knows which of these was the case." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223}
- **Sunni: al-Mubarakfuri**: He favours the view that Musaylima was proud and aspired to command, and did not attend with the rest of the delegation; the Prophet first tried to win him over with kindness, and when he found nothing he saw evil in him. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.452}
- **Researchers**: Modern studies read Musaylima as a tribal religious leader through whom al-Yamama tried to keep its independence from the new centre in Medina. A specific academic reference for this summary has not yet been added (needs_source). {cite:tabari-donner-1993}
:::

## What followed
Musaylima wrote to the Prophet at the end of year ten asking for half the land, and the Prophet answered him that the land belongs to God, who gives it as an inheritance to whom He pleases of His servants. He was killed in the [[event/battle-of-yamama|Battle of al-Yamama]] in the caliphate of Abu Bakr. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/247} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.452}

## Marks of the report
- "The liar" and "the enemy of God" in the text are the words of Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham, not the museum's words. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/222} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223}
- Ibn Ishaq says in the same place that Musaylima's rhymed sayings were a rival of the Qur'an, which the museum does not reproduce. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/223}
