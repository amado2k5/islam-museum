## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad says: I asked my father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] about the gatherings of Yahya ibn al-Husayn, and he said: I saw [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Yahya ibn al-Husayn]] in his gathering disciplining himself by the manners of the Messenger of God. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121} He says he knew this because he had written down the Prophet's manners and saw that Yahya followed them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121} The account is Zaydi, in the imam's followers' voice, and its praise is the narrator's, not the museum's judgment.

## The tone of the gathering
His gathering, he said, was calm, dignity, admonitions, sorrow, asking forgiveness and debate in knowledge, with no idle talk, no quarrelling over indecency and no false word, because he pressed a speaker until the truth of what he said was clear; he drew the weak, the poor and the child close in his gathering and told others to do so and to be tender to them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121} The narrator saw a small boy brought, drawn near and seated before him, and Yahya stroked his head and ordered something for him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121}

## Talk with those who sat with him
He spoke with his companions and laughed with them and debated them on every branch of knowledge until they understood; if they had the answer, well, and if not he taught it to them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.122} He honoured the noble of each people and knew his rank, and the narrator says the tradition came from the Messenger of God to this effect. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.122} He was forbearing, dignified and calm, not angered by much speech or wearied by questions, and answered each asker with calm, forbearance and knowledge. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.122}

## Equal in what is right
The near and the far, the friend and the enemy were equal before him in what is right, he leaned to nobody by whim in a judgment and made no show for anyone in knowledge. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.122} When people asked him about branches of knowledge he answered in the gentlest speech, rebuking none, never raising his voice or being harsh, repeating the answer until it was understood, and neither dominating nor puffed up. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.122}

## His bearing in the gathering
He turned his gaze among his companions right and left so that all who were present understood what he said and he did not give anyone all of his talk; he moved little, did not lean back among them or slight them, kept a good silence when silent, and was neither a chatterer nor halting in answering. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.122} He did not rise from those beside him until they rose, and if a need arose he waited with them until they left; the narrator concluded that he had often needed to rise sooner and was prevented by generosity and courtesy, and says the tradition tells that the Prophet did not rise from his companions until they dispersed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.122} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.123}

## His care for people
He saw him often thinking of the good of the people of Islam, showing compassion and mercy, frequent in admonishing, bidding obedience, enjoining the right and forbidding the wrong, and debating whoever debated him fairly, never calling anyone's answer wrong but dealing gently with him, teaching him his argument and saying, "Look and be sure." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.123} He listened and turned to whoever spoke to him until the speaker finished, never cutting him off, and then answered without roughness, harshness or irritation. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.123} He urged people to mercy and ties of kinship and forbade wrongdoing, envy and severing relations, asked after their affairs and livelihoods and taught them the manners that adorn their worldly life and bring them near their Creator. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.123}

## Notes
All of this is one witness's portrait of his imam, in the register of esteem; his comparison with the Prophet's manners rests on what he says he had written of them, and the museum states it without comment. In the first line of the section the main manuscript has "rank" where "his gathering" is printed, and the editor corrects it from the second manuscript. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121}
