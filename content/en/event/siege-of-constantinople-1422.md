## Overview
Sultan Murad II began a siege of Constantinople in 825/1422; the siege was raised within the same year. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 648-649}

## The context
Murad II came to the throne after the death of his father Mehemmed I; as crown prince he had taken part in suppressing the revolt of Bedr al-Din of Simawna. At the start of his reign he faced the pretender known in Turkish history as Düzme Mustafa and his ally Junayd, both supported by the Byzantine emperor Manuel. Murad defeated Mustafa at the bridge of Ulubad, recovered Gallipoli, entered Edirne and killed the pretender, then in 825/1422 began the siege of Constantinople. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 648}

## The siege raised
The siege was lifted either through Byzantine gold reaching the graecophile vizier Ibrahim Pasha, or because a new pretender to the throne appeared in Iznik in the person of the sultan's younger brother Mustafa, who was later betrayed by his former supporter Ilyas Pasha and killed. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 648-649}
