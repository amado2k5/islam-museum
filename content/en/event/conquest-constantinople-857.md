## Overview
Sultan [[person/mehmed-ii|Mehmed the Conqueror]] took Constantinople on Wednesday, 20 Jumada al-Akhira 857 AH, after a siege that lasted fifty days, according to Ibn al-Imad. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

## Preparation and siege
In al-Sallabi's account, Mehmed came to the sultanate in 855 AH with his eyes already on the city. He built [[building/rumeli-hisari|the Rumeli Hisari fortress]] at the narrowest point of the Bosporus, assembled some 250,000 soldiers and more than four hundred ships, and brought in the Hungarian engineer [[person/urban-cannon-founder|Urban]] to make the great guns {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 87-92}. The armies reached the outskirts of the city on Thursday 26 Rabi I 857 AH, corresponding to 6 April 1453 {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 94}.

## The conquest
Ibn al-Imad says that the sultan brought ships overland and by sea, besieged the city with the utmost severity, and took it on the fifty-first day. He prayed the Friday prayer in Hagia Sophia. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.344-345}

## Ships crossing the land
When the ships could not force the chain across the Golden Horn, the Ottomans dragged more than seventy ships on planks greased with oil and fat from Beşiktaş to the Golden Horn, three miles in a single night, so that the people of the city woke on 22 April to the cries of "God is greatest" from the Ottomans, whose ships now controlled the inlet {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 98-99}.

## Inside the war council
al-Sallabi relates that when four ships sent by the Pope reached the city, the emirs and viziers gathered and told the sultan that there was no hope of conquest. He sent his vizier to the tent of [[person/aq-shams-al-din|Shaykh Aq Shams al-Din]], who answered that God would certainly grant the conquest. The war council decided to continue. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.113-114}

## After the conquest
Mehmed turned Hagia Sophia into a mosque, allowed the Christians to practise their rites and to choose their religious leaders, and they elected [[person/gennadios-scolarios|Gennadios]] as patriarch {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 111-112}.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] tells the story of the conquest across six episodes (24–29) of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]], and makes it the closing story of the series because it is a story of glory after the stories of collapse, one in which, for him, every cause of victory appears and every obstacle to it is overcome {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:12:30}. What follows is his account.

### The prophecy and the earlier attempts
Al-Suwaidan says the Prophet foretold the city's conquest in a hadith he calls authentic and well known, praising its commander and its army; that rulers sent armies against it from the first century on; and that the first attempt included Companions, with [[person/abu-ayyub-al-ansari|Abu Ayyub al-Ansari]] dying at its walls {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep24 00:16:00}. He cites a second hadith in which the Prophet was asked which of two cities would be conquered first, Constantinople or Rome, and answered that it would be the city of Heraclius, that is, Constantinople {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:04:30}. He counts eleven earlier Muslim sieges, all of which failed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:13:00}.

### Preparations
Mehmed built [[building/rumeli-hisari|the fortress of Rumeli Hisari]] on the European side at the narrowest point of the Bosporus, facing the fortress built under Sultan Bayezid on the Asian shore; the Byzantine emperor tried to dissuade him with offers of large sums, without success {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:14:00}. Al-Suwaidan gives its height as 82 metres and the distance between the two fortresses as 660 metres, so that together they controlled the passage of ships and cut off supplies to the city {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:15:00}.

He brought in [[person/urban-cannon-founder|a Hungarian engineer]] skilled in casting cannon, received him in person and gave him every financial, material and human resource; the engineer designed huge guns, chief among them the "Sultan's cannon", said to weigh hundreds of tons and to need hundreds of oxen to haul it, and the sultan himself oversaw their casting and testing {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:16:30}. Al-Suwaidan reads this as importing a useful foreign industry and then establishing it in Muslim lands {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:16:00}. Because a maritime city cannot be fully besieged without sea power, Mehmed also built up the fleet, constructing more than 400 new ships {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:17:30}.

He concluded treaties with the neighbouring lordship of Galata, east of the city across the Golden Horn, and with Venice and others, so as to face a single adversary; yet forces from those cities came to defend Constantinople once the assault began {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep25 00:18:30}. He levelled the road from [[place/edirne|Edirne]], where the arsenal was, and the giant cannon travelled to the outskirts of Constantinople in two months under the guard of part of the army {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:12:30}.

### On the Byzantine side
The emperor tried to turn the sultan aside with money and gifts and by bribing some of his advisers; seeing his resolve, he sought help from the states and cities of Europe, above all the Pope in Rome {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:06:00}. Al-Suwaidan explains that the Byzantine churches were Orthodox and did not follow the Pope, so the emperor showed himself ready to place them under Rome; a papal envoy preached in [[building/hagia-sophia|Hagia Sophia]] and proclaimed there the union of the two churches under the Pope's authority, which angered the Orthodox of the city so deeply that some said they would rather see the Turks than Latin hats {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:08:30}. Here he cites [[passage/quran-59-14|al-Hashr 14]] on hearts that are divided, and contrasts this division with the unity of the Muslims under one sultan at the time, the reverse of the situation at the fall of Baghdad {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:09:00}.

### The city and its defences
He describes the city as bounded by water on three sides, the Bosporus, the Sea of Marmara and the Golden Horn, and by a great wall on the fourth, and says the Byzantines protected the Golden Horn with a huge chain stretched between the two shores, raised against enemy ships and lowered for their own {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:10:00}.

### Arrival and siege
He says the army reached the outskirts of the city on Thursday 26 Rabi' I 857 AH, 6 April 1453, with 250,000 soldiers {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:13:00}. The sultan addressed them, his sentences passed back through the ranks by criers standing on a line of poles; he urged them to jihad, to seek victory or martyrdom, recited verses of Surat al-Anfal, reminded them of the hadiths foretelling the conquest and of the merit of the conquering army and its commander, and scholars were spread through the ranks, bearing arms and fighting {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:14:30}. He then divided the land army before the walls into three sections that sealed the siege, set reserve armies behind them, emplaced the cannon, above all the Sultan's cannon, posted observers on the nearby heights, and the fleet took the Princes' Islands in the Sea of Marmara {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:16:30}. The bombardment broke parts of the walls, and the defenders rebuilt them at once {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:17:30}.

He says five ships from Genoa under [[person/giustiniani|Giustiniani]], with 700 fighters, broke through the naval blockade and reached the city, and the emperor made him commander-in-chief of the defence; priests went round the streets and fortifications urging steadfastness, and Emperor [[person/constantine-xi-palaiologos|Constantine]] himself went repeatedly to Hagia Sophia {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep26 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:03:30}. Mehmed wrote to the emperor demanding the surrender of the city, pledging that no inhabitant would be touched in person, property or honour, and that whoever wished could stay in safety and whoever wished could leave in safety {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:06:00}.

### The Lycus breach and the sea battles
On 18 April the cannon opened a breach at the Lycus valley in the western part of the walls; the soldiers rushed it while trying to scale other stretches of wall, but the defenders under Giustiniani held them off, and with the breach so narrow and night falling Mehmed ordered a withdrawal {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:08:30}. The same day Ottoman ships failed to break the chain, and the sultan resolved to dismiss the fleet commander {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:09:30}. Two days later European ships got through to the Horn although the sultan watched the battle himself from the shore; he summoned his fleet commander, rebuked him harshly and accused him of cowardice, and the commander uncovered an eye wounded in the fighting. Mehmed saw that he had done his utmost, rewarded him but removed him, and appointed Hamza Pasha in his place {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:11:30}.

### Ships over land
Mehmed conceived of bringing the ships into the Golden Horn overland from their anchorage at Beşiktaş, keeping clear of Galata, across three miles of hills and valleys; he put the idea to his war council, who studied it and encouraged it, and al-Suwaidan draws from their response a lesson about nurturing a creative idea instead of mocking it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep27 00:16:30}. The ground was levelled in a few hours, timber planks greased with oil and fat were laid, and the ships, small and light compared with the Byzantine vessels, were dragged over them by oxen and pushed by men; in a single night more than forty ships were moved, with the sultan supervising in person all night long {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:07:00}. Al-Suwaidan's lesson is that Mehmed added creativity to reliance on God {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:08:00}.

The people of the city woke on the morning of 22 April to the Ottomans' cries of "God is greatest" inside the Golden Horn, and al-Suwaidan reports a Byzantine historian's amazement that Mehmed had turned land into sea and outdone Alexander of Macedon {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:09:00}. The defenders had to pull troops to the Golden Horn walls, the weakest of the defences, and the emperor's attempts to destroy this fleet failed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:10:30}.

### Psychological war, tunnels and the tower
Mehmed set cannon on the heights behind Galata, and one shot sank a merchant ship; the bombardment by land and sea went on day and night to exhaust the besieged {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:13:00}. At a council called by the emperor one commander proposed a counter-attack to open a way to the outside world, but the meeting was cut short by news of a fierce Ottoman assault at the Lycus valley, and Constantine led the defence in person until the Ottomans withdrew {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:14:00}. The Ottomans then dug tunnels towards the inside of the city; the defenders dug counter-tunnels and poured fire and burning naphtha on the diggers, many of whom died, and the attempt failed; the Ottomans dug again elsewhere until terror spread through the city {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:17:00}. Next they built a mobile wooden tower on wheels, three storeys high, which al-Suwaidan says was taller than the walls, over 80 metres, covered with armour and wet hides, with archers on its top floor; they pushed it against the wall at the Gate of Romanus and some men climbed onto the wall, but the defenders set it on fire {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep28 00:19:30}.

### The last letters
Sure that the city was about to fall, Mehmed wrote to the emperor inviting him to surrender it without bloodshed, guaranteeing safe departure for him, his family, his followers and any inhabitants who wished, and leaving the people free to stay or leave {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:01:30}. The emperor consulted his commanders, most of whom favoured fighting on, and replied that he would pay tribute but would defend the city to his last breath, either keeping his throne or being buried beneath its walls {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:02:30}. Mehmed answered that soon he would have there either a throne or a grave {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:03:00}.

### The general assault
The bombardment grew so heavy that the Sultan's cannon burst from overuse, killing its crew and, first among them, the Hungarian engineer who had made it; the gunners kept firing, cooling the guns with olive oil {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:04:00}. On Sunday 18 Jumada I, 27 May 1453, the sultan and the army turned to supplication, prayer and the purification of souls {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:05:00}.

The general assault began at one in the morning on Tuesday, by land, by sea and with the cannon, to a carefully prepared plan, concentrated at the Lycus valley under Mehmed's own command, in successive waves: a first wave was exhausted and withdrawn; a second set up hundreds of ladders, which the defenders overturned; after two hours a third, fresh wave caught the exhausted defenders by surprise, while a naval attack went in at the same time {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:10:00}. Then a body of [[group/janissaries|Janissaries]] led by the sultan himself advanced, thirty of them climbed the wall, and some of them, their leader among them, were killed, but they opened the way and raised the Ottoman flags on part of the walls {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:12:00}. At that moment Giustiniani was badly wounded, left the battle and took a ship towards Europe, and the emperor took command of the defenders himself {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:12:30}. When he saw the Ottoman flags on the northern towers he took off his robes so as not to be recognised and dismounted, and rather than flee he fought until he was killed; the defenders lost heart and the Ottomans entered the city from every side {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:15:00}.

### The entry and Hagia Sophia
Al-Suwaidan says pockets of resistance remained and cost the lives of a number of Muslims, most of the inhabitants took refuge in the churches, and Mehmed was in the centre of the city by noon on Tuesday 20 Jumada I 857 AH, 29 May 1453 {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:16:30}. Mehmed went to Hagia Sophia, where crowds had gathered in fear with priests and monks; he granted them safety and told them to go back to their homes, and al-Suwaidan says no one who had been taken was killed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:18:00}. He prayed the afternoon prayer in Hagia Sophia; al-Suwaidan relates that he pressed the Christians until they sold him the church, which he bought and turned into a mosque, until Atatürk later turned it into a museum, and that Mehmed held the first Friday prayer there and preached {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:18:30}. He closes by saying the conquest came about because the causes of victory were complete and its obstacles overcome {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep29 00:18:30}.

## The city and its earlier Muslim campaigns in the Encyclopaedia of Islam
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, begins its article on Kustantiniyya with the city's names. Constantine the Great raised it to be the capital of the Eastern Empire on 11 May 330 and it took his name; the Arabs called it Kustantiniyya, in poetry sometimes Kustantina, and also knew the older name Byzantion. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 570} The Turkish name Istanbul comes from a Greek phrase meaning "to the city", while Kustantiniyya stayed the official name on Ottoman coins and decrees. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 570} The Istanbul article adds that a contemporary Armenian source credits the conqueror with the punning name Islam-bol, which appears in documents of the ninth/fifteenth century. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 237}

### The hadith as the Ottoman historians cite it
The article says the Prophet is reported to have foretold the conquest of Constantinople by the believers. Ottoman historians cite a hadith blessing the prince and the army to whom the conquest would be granted, giving al-Suyuti's *al-Jami' al-saghir* as the authority; the article remarks that older references are lacking. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 570}

### The Umayyad expeditions
The article records that the [[polity/umayyad-caliphate|Umayyads]] took up the enterprise energetically. Following the chronicler Theophanes, a fleet was fitted out at Tripoli in the year of the world 6146, beginning September 653, under [[person/busr-ibn-abi-artat|Busr b. Abi Artat]]; it defeated the Greek fleet at Phoenix on the Lycian coast but did not reach the city, while [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] invaded Byzantine territory by land at the same time. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 570} The article also mentions the campaign of [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-khalid|'Abd al-Rahman b. Khalid]] as far as Pergamon, dated 44 AH, and that Arabic sources have Busr reaching Constantinople. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 570} [[person/fadala-ibn-ubayd|Fadala b. 'Ubayd]] then advanced as far as Chalcedon and [[person/yazid-ibn-muawiya|Yazid]], son of Mu'awiya, was sent after him. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 570}

In 672 a strong fleet anchored under the walls on the European shore of the Sea of Marmara. The Arabs attacked from April to September, wintered at Cyzicus and renewed the attack the next spring, until they withdrew after what the article calls seven years of fighting; Greek fire destroyed much of the fleet and many ships were wrecked on the way home. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} The article stresses that the chronology of this blockade is uncertain: the land army seems to have arrived in 47/667 and the fleet to have left in 53/673, and the Arab historians give several different years. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} This siege became famous in the Arab world because the Ansari [[person/abu-ayyub-al-ansari|Abu Ayyub Khalid b. Zayd]] fell in it and was buried before the walls; the article compares the finding of his tomb during Mehmed II's final siege with the Crusaders' discovery of the holy lance at Antioch. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} It notes that Ibn Qutayba is the first to mention the grave, and that according to several Arabic authors the Byzantines respected it and prayed there for rain in times of drought. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571}

After a truce of over forty years, [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman b. 'Abd al-Malik]] came to the throne in 97/715-16. A hadith was then current that a caliph bearing a prophet's name would conquer Constantinople, and Sulayman took it to refer to himself. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} His brother [[person/maslama-ibn-abd-al-malik|Maslama]] led an army equipped with siege artillery through Asia Minor, crossed the Dardanelles at Abydos and surrounded the city; the fleet anchored partly by the walls on the Marmara coast and partly in the Bosporus, and the Golden Horn was barred by a chain. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} The siege began on 25 August 716 and lasted a year; Maslama withdrew because of Bulgar attacks and the scarcity of provisions. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} See also the museum's record of the [[event/siege-of-constantinople-717|siege of 717]]. Later Arab writers remembered Maslama's Well and mosque at Abydos, and credited him with making the building of a house for Arab prisoners a condition of the peace and with founding the first mosque in Constantinople. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} The article adds that the Ottoman traveller Evliya Celebi made two sieges out of this one campaign and embellished them with incredible stories. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571}

The only other occasion on which an Arab army came within sight of the city was in 165/782, when [[person/harun-al-rashid|Harun]], son of the caliph al-Mahdi, marched unopposed through Asia Minor and camped at Chrysopolis, now Scutari; the empress Irene, regent for her son, made peace and agreed to pay tribute. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571}

### Arab descriptions and Muslims in the city
Arab accounts of Constantinople date from the third/ninth century. They treated the Dardanelles, the Sea of Marmora and the Bosporus as a single channel between the Mediterranean and the Black Sea, mentioned the great chain that kept out Arab ships, and described the double walls, the Golden Gate, [[building/hagia-sophia|Hagia Sophia]], the Hippodrome with its obelisk, the four bronze horses and the great bronze equestrian statue. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} Ibn Hawqal and al-Muqaddasi pay particular attention to the Praetorium, where captured Arabs were kept under mild custody; the most detailed account is that of [[person/ibn-al-wardi|Ibn al-Wardi]] in the eighth/fourteenth century, and [[person/ibn-battuta-muhammad|Ibn Battuta]] described the monastic life of his day from his own observation. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} Besides prisoners, Muslim merchants and envoys stayed in Byzantium, Mamluk sultans occasionally banished troublesome persons there, and Seljuk sultans and pretenders such as Kilij Arslan II, Kaykhusraw I and Kaykawus II spent long periods in the capital. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571}

No definite trace of the Arab sieges or of the mosque attributed to Maslama has been found. The article says the mosque was destroyed in a popular rising in 1200 and pillaged by the Crusaders in 1203, and that according to Ibn al-Athir it was restored in 441/1049-50 by Constantine Monomachos at the request of the Seljuk [[person/tughril-beg|Tughril Beg]]; a report in al-Maqrizi has Michael VIII Palaeologus building a mosque about 660/1261-2, which the Mamluk sultan Baybars furnished splendidly. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 572} For the period after 1453 the Kustantiniyya article refers readers to the article on Istanbul. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 572}

## The day of the conquest and its results in the Encyclopaedia of Islam
The Istanbul article says that when [[person/mehmed-ii|Mehmed II]] proclaimed the assault and promised his troops a three-day sack, he announced that the stones and land of the city and its appurtenances were his, and that other goods, prisoners and foodstuffs were the troops' booty. The result, in its words, was that the city was emptied of its former inhabitants and its Byzantine character radically changed. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 237} The troops entered through the breach in the walls at dawn on 20 Jumada I 857/29 May 1453 and fought their way towards Hagia Sophia; some defenders held out, the Cretan sailors in the towers of Alexius until after midday, and the fighting ended towards the middle of the afternoon. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 237} Almost all survivors were taken prisoner. The sultan, wishing to prevent further destruction of the city he meant to make his capital, proclaimed that afternoon that fighting was to cease. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 237} The article notes that one source records a three-day sack, but judges that from 30 May there was no serious plundering or enslavement. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 237}

On 30 May Mehmed made his ceremonial entry, toured the city, declared Hagia Sophia the Great Mosque and announced that Istanbul would be his capital. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 237} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} His first concern was repopulation. On the third day after the conquest he proclaimed an amān: any fugitive who returned within a set time could freely reoccupy his home and practise his religion, and the Greeks were invited to elect a Patriarch; [[person/gennadios-scolarios|George Scholarius]] was appointed on 6 January 1454. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238}

Before returning to [[place/edirne|Edirne]] on 13 Jumada II/21 June, the sultan appointed a military prefect for the city with a garrison of 1,500 [[group/janissaries|Janissaries]], appointed Khidr-beg Celebi as qadi, and ordered the walls repaired, a citadel built by the Golden Gate, now [[building/yedikule|Yedikule]], and a palace built at the Forum Tauri, later the [[building/eski-saray-istanbul|Eski Saray]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} He settled the fifth of the prisoners that fell to him along the Golden Horn, gave them houses and freed them from taxes for a time. The article says he had considered making the Megadux Lucas Notaras city prefect but was dissuaded by his viziers, and that Notaras and the other Byzantine notables were executed. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} Prisoners who had paid or promised ransom were allowed to settle. The amān did not cover the Venetians: the bailo Girolamo Minotto and his son were executed, twenty-nine other Venetian nobles were ransomed, and their sons were drafted into the corps of Adjemi oghlans. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238}

The most effective means of repopulation, the article says, was the sürgün, compulsory resettlement of Muslim, Christian and Jewish families from Rumeli and Anatolia. Sources give figures of 5,000 families by September, or 4,000 from Anatolia and 4,000 from Rumeli. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} Finding the pace slow because of opposition, the sultan moved to [[place/bursa|Bursa]] in the autumn of 1453 and ordered severe measures; in autumn 1455 he found the walls repaired but learned that Muslim settlers had left, and ordered new deportations. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} A fragment of a survey from Muharram 860/December 1455 lists 918 houses in 22 districts, of which 291 were empty or ruinous; of 26 monasteries only one was still held by Greeks, and of 42 churches only two. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} The article takes this as showing that the Greeks were few, and as confirming the tradition that some embraced Islam. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238}

Hagia Sophia was the first Great Mosque of the new capital, and the first stages of its growth appear in the endowments attached to it, including converted Byzantine buildings; Mehmed's own mosque, the [[building/fatih-mosque-istanbul|Fatih]], was completed only in 875/1471. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} In 861/1457 he made over to Hagia Sophia's endowments the Byzantine buildings still standing, and gave it revenue-producing establishments including the covered market, baths and about 2,000 shops. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 238} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 239} The article adds that the tomb of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari was built immediately after the conquest, giving the Eyup district its sacred character, and that saints' lodges gave names to many quarters. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 239} It records occasional pressure to close churches and synagogues on the ground that the city had been taken by force, and that the Shaykh al-Islam himself looked for grounds to protect the [[concept/dhimma|dhimmi]] status of non-Muslims. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 239}

## For Sprouts
Sultan Mehmed II opened the city of Constantinople with wisdom and courage, protecting its inhabitants and building schools.

## For Explorers
In 857 AH (1453 CE), the young Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II opened Constantinople following a strategic siege. Mehmed devised an ingenious plan transporting warships overland on greased wooden logs into the Golden Horn. Upon entry, he granted a decree of protection to the citizens, transforming Istanbul into an imperial capital of learning and diversity.

## For Scholars
The conquest of Constantinople (Istanbul) on 20 Jumada al-Ula 857 AH (29 May 1453 CE) by the Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II ('the Conqueror') following a sophisticated fifty-three-day siege. The conquest marked the definitive end of the Byzantine Empire and represented a monumental paradigm shift in global history, ushering in the early modern era. Renowned for tactical innovations including the overland transport of fleet vessels over greased logs and super-sized cannon siegecraft, Mehmed subsequently issued guarantees of personal and religious liberty to the city's inhabitants, transforming Istanbul into the cosmopolitan imperial capital.

## The conquest in al-Albani's Silsilat al-Ahadith al-Sahiha
In [[work/silsilat-al-ahadith-al-sahiha|Silsilat al-Ahadith al-Sahiha]] (no. 4), [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|al-Albani]] gives the report of Abu Qabil from 'Abd Allah b. 'Amr: the Prophet was asked which of two cities would be conquered first, Constantinople or Rumiya, and said, "The city of Heraclius will be conquered first." {cite:silsila-sahiha-maarif-v1 p. 33} He quotes 'Abd al-Ghani al-Maqdisi calling its chain good and al-Hakim declaring it sound with al-Dhahabi's agreement, and endorses them. {cite:silsila-sahiha-maarif-v1 p. 33} He holds that the first conquest came at the hands of the Ottoman Mehmed the Conqueror more than eight hundred years after the prophecy, that the conquest of Rumiya — Rome — will also come, and that in his view it requires the return of the Rightly Guided Caliphate. {cite:silsila-sahiha-maarif-v1 p. 33}
