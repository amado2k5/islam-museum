## Summary
When the tribes apostatized and [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] went out to Dhu al-Qassa in person to fight the apostates, [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] advised him not to expose himself to danger and to return to Medina {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.124-125}.

## Ali's Advice
The book narrates, from Ibn Umar, that Ali said to Abu Bakr, citing what the Prophet had said on the day of Uhud, "Do not grieve us with your loss, and return to Medina; for by God, if we are bereaved of you, Islam will never again have order," and so he returned {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.125}.

## Consultation on Zakat and Apostasy
Abu Bakr asked Ali for his opinion, and Ali said, "If you leave aside anything that the Messenger of God used to take from them, you will be acting contrary to the practice of the Messenger," whereupon Abu Bakr said, "If you say that, I will surely fight them even if they withhold a hobbling cord from me" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.125}.

## The Book's Analysis
Al-Sallabi considers that had Ali disliked Abu Bakr, his going out to war in person would have been an opportunity to get rid of him, yet he advised him and pledged allegiance to him according to what he knew, which demonstrated his sincerity {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.124-125}. The book indicates that [[event/ali-pledge-to-abu-bakr|Ali's pledge of allegiance to Abu Bakr]] preceded this incident.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book traces the incident to Ibn Umar's report in Ibn Kathir's al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya; the chronological sequence within the year 11 AH is not readable in the book.
- The book cites the dialogue attributed to Ali in support of its position, and contrary readings have not yet been presented.
