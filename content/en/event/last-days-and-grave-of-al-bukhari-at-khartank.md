## Summary
Al-Bukhari lodged at the village of Khartank, two farsakhs from Samarkand, with relatives, fell ill there and died on the night of the Festival of Breaking the Fast in 256 AH, at sixty-two years less thirteen days, and was buried at Khartank. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3343} His host Ghalib ibn Jibril reports that he gave instructions to be shrouded in three white garments with neither shirt nor turban. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} The museum treats his death in [[event/death-al-bukhari-256|Death of Imam al-Bukhari]] and his departure from Bukhara in [[event/bukhari-expulsion-khartank-256|his expulsion from Bukhara]]; this card gathers his last days and what concerns his shroud and grave.

## Context
Ibn Adi reports from Abd al-Quddus ibn Abd al-Jabbar al-Samarqandi that al-Bukhari came to Khartank, a village two farsakhs from Samarkand, where he had relatives, and lodged with them. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 22 p. 272} As the [[event/bukhari-expulsion-khartank-256|account of his leaving]] has it, he had left Bukhara after a dispute with its governor. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342}

## His prayer
Abd al-Quddus said: I heard him one night, after he had finished the night prayer, pray that the earth had become narrow for him despite its breadth and ask God to take him; before the month was out he died. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 22 p. 272}

## His illness and his instruction about the shroud
Muhammad ibn Abi Hatim reports from Abu Mansur Ghalib ibn Jibril, with whom al-Bukhari stayed, that he lodged with them some days and fell ill, the illness worsening until a messenger was sent to Samarkand to bring him out. When the messenger arrived he made ready to ride, put on his boots and turban, and walked about twenty steps with Ghalib holding his arm and another man leading him to the mount; then he said, 'Let me go, I have grown weak.' He made some supplications, lay down and died. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 22 p. 272} A great quantity of sweat ran from him that did not stop until they wrapped him in his garments. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342}
Among what he said and charged them with was to shroud him in three white garments, with no shirt and no turban, and they did so. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 22 p. 272} I found no text mentioning property, inheritance or debt.

## Burial and the grave
The same narrator says that when they buried him a fragrance came from the soil of his grave, better than musk, lasting days; then long white columns appeared in the sky over the grave and people came and marvelled. People kept carrying away soil from the grave until it was exposed and could not be kept safe by guards, so they set a wooden lattice over it, and some of his opponents went out to the grave and showed repentance and regret. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3343} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 22 p. 273} These are reports Muhammad ibn Abi Hatim gives on Ghalib's authority; they are marvels with no corroboration in any other source I read.
Muhammad ibn Abi Hatim adds that Ghalib did not outlive him long and asked to be buried beside him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3343} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 22 p. 273}
Abu al-Fath Nasr ibn al-Hasan al-Samarqandi reports that Samarkand once suffered drought and people prayed for rain repeatedly without result; then the judge and the people went out to al-Bukhari's grave at Khartank and prayed for rain there, and God sent heavy rain, so that for about seven days people could not reach Samarkand; see [[event/istisqa-qabr-al-bukhari-464|the rain prayer at al-Bukhari's grave]]. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3343} The grave is today connected with the [[building/imam-bukhari-memorial|Imam al-Bukhari Memorial]] at [[place/khartank|Khartank]], in the district of [[place/samarqand|Samarkand]].

## Where the reports differ
The narrators differ on how and when he died. For Ghalib ibn Jibril he died as he prepared to ride from Khartank to Samarkand; for Mahib ibn Sulaym he died on the festival night of 256 AH, alone in a house, and they found him dead in the morning; for al-Hasan ibn al-Husayn al-Bazzaz he died on the night of Saturday, the festival night, at the time of the evening prayer, and was buried on the festival day after the noon prayer. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3343} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 22 p. 273} Al-Dhahabi does not choose among them; he gives them all.
Among the reported dreams, Abd al-Wahid ibn Adam al-Tawawisi saw the Prophet standing in a place with his Companions and asked why he stood; he answered that he was waiting for Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari, and when news of the death reached him he found it was the same hour. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3342} This is a reported dream.
