## Summary
In the year 608 AH a severe earthquake struck Egypt, destroying many houses in Cairo and killing large numbers of people beneath the rubble {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 40}.

## Details
Ibn al-'Imad al-Hanbali records under the events of 608 that a great earthquake hit Egypt, razing many houses in Cairo, while many people perished under the ruins; he gives no figure for the dead nor the extent of the damage beyond Cairo {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 40}. In the same year Qatada al-Husayni, the amir of Mecca, attacked the Iraqi pilgrim caravan at Mina: the pilgrims scattered, a number were killed, and property said to be worth a million dinars was lost, without the attackers being punished, as he reports from the 'Ibar {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 40}. The year 608 thus combined a major disturbance at the pilgrimage with a natural disaster in Egypt, both noted by the chroniclers among the year's opening events.
