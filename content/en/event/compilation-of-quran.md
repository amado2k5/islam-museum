## Overview
The story of the compilation of the Qur'an in the caliphate of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] is related on the authority of [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]], and its origin is in the Sahih of al-Bukhari. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

## The report
Abu Bakr sent for Zayd after the killing of the people of al-Yamama, and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] was with him. Umar said that the killing had raged among the reciters, and he feared that much of the Qur'an would be lost, and he saw that it should be gathered. Abu Bakr hesitated because the Prophet had not done it, and Umar said that it was good, and kept urging him until God opened his heart to it. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

Then Abu Bakr told Zayd that he was a young man of sense who could not be accused and who had written down the revelation, and asked him to trace the Qur'an and gather it. Zayd in turn hesitated, until God opened his heart. He gathered it from scraps, shoulder-blades, palm-stalks and the breasts of men. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

Zayd found two verses from the end of Sura al-Tawba with [[person/khuzayma-ibn-thabit|Khuzayma ibn Thabit]], which he did not find with anyone else. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

The full text in al-Bukhari is in the chapter on the virtues of the Qur'an, on the authority of Ibn Shihab from Ubayd ibn al-Sabbaq from Zayd. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

## Uthman's copy, in al-Tabari's report

Al-Tabari gives the account of the compilation with a chain running through Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri and Kharija ibn Zayd to Zayd ibn Thabit, and it goes on to the copy made under Uthman. After Abu Bakr's death the single sheet was kept with Hafsa, the Prophet's wife {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 60}. Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman came back from a campaign at Marj Armenia and told Uthman that the people of Syria recited by the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b and the people of Iraq by that of Ibn Mas'ud, and each side charged the other with error {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 60}. Uthman had Zayd write a copy with others, with the points they agreed on written down and the points they disagreed on referred back to him; the text names Aban ibn Sa'id ibn al-As among them {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 60}. Al-Tabari's reasons for the single ahruf are given in [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa]] {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 59}.
