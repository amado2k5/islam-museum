## Overview
The story of the compilation of the Qur'an in the caliphate of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] is related on the authority of [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]], and its origin is in the Sahih of al-Bukhari. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

## The report
Abu Bakr sent for Zayd after the killing of the people of al-Yamama, and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] was with him. Umar said that the killing had raged among the reciters, and he feared that much of the Qur'an would be lost, and he saw that it should be gathered. Abu Bakr hesitated because the Prophet had not done it, and Umar said that it was good, and kept urging him until God opened his heart to it. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

Then Abu Bakr told Zayd that he was a young man of sense who could not be accused and who had written down the revelation, and asked him to trace the Qur'an and gather it. Zayd in turn hesitated, until God opened his heart. He gathered it from scraps, shoulder-blades, palm-stalks and the breasts of men. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

Zayd found two verses from the end of Sura al-Tawba with [[person/khuzayma-ibn-thabit|Khuzayma ibn Thabit]], which he did not find with anyone else. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

The full text in al-Bukhari is in the chapter on the virtues of the Qur'an, on the authority of Ibn Shihab from Ubayd ibn al-Sabbaq from Zayd. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4986}

## Uthman's copy, in al-Tabari's report

Al-Tabari gives the account of the compilation with a chain running through Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri and Kharija ibn Zayd to Zayd ibn Thabit, and it goes on to the copy made under Uthman. After Abu Bakr's death the single sheet was kept with Hafsa, the Prophet's wife {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 60}. Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman came back from a campaign at Marj Armenia and told Uthman that the people of Syria recited by the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b and the people of Iraq by that of Ibn Mas'ud, and each side charged the other with error {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 60}. Uthman had Zayd write a copy with others, with the points they agreed on written down and the points they disagreed on referred back to him; the text names Aban ibn Sa'id ibn al-As among them {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 60}. Al-Tabari's reasons for the single ahruf are given in [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa]] {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 59}.

## How Zayd verified: the two witnesses
Ibn Abi Dawud relays that Umar said: whoever received anything of the Qur'an from the Messenger of God, let him bring it; they used to write it on leaves, tablets and palm stems, and he would accept nothing from anyone until two witnesses testified. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 383} He also relays, through Hisham ibn Urwa from his father, that Abu Bakr told Umar and Zayd: sit at the door of the mosque, and whoever brings you two witnesses to something of God's Book, write it. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 383} Ibn Hajar says the two witnesses were probably memory and writing; al-Sakhawi that they testify the writing was written in the Messenger of God's presence or that it is among the forms in which the Qur'an came down; and Abu Shama that their aim was that nothing be written except from the very thing that was written in the Prophet's presence, not from memory alone. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 384} Al-Layth ibn Sa'd says the end of Surat Bara'a was found only with Abu Khuzayma ibn Thabit, and he said: write it, for the Messenger of God made his testimony count as that of two men. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 384}

## Where the leaves stayed
The leaves stayed with Abu Bakr until God took him, then with Umar for his life, then with Hafsa bint Umar. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 379} Ibn Hajar is relayed as preferring that what was on hide and palm stems came before it was collected under Abu Bakr, and then it was collected into leaves. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 387} Books of Qur'anic sciences also report that Ali collected the Qur'an after the Prophet's death; Ibn Hajar takes this to mean memorizing it in his chest, and describes what was reported through Abd Khayr from Ali, that Abu Bakr has the greatest reward in the codices and was the first to gather God's Book, as the soundest and reliable. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 380} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 381}

## The difference between the two collections
Al-Suyuti quotes Ibn al-Tin that the difference between Abu Bakr's collection and Uthman's is that Abu Bakr's was out of fear that something of the Qur'an might go with the death of its bearers, since it was not gathered in one place, so he collected it in leaves with the verses of each sura ordered as the Prophet had shown them; whereas Uthman's was when disagreement multiplied over the forms of recitation, as they read in their own dialects with the breadth of the dialects, which led some to call others mistaken. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 391} Al-Harith al-Muhasibi says what is commonly said among people, that Uthman was the collector of the Qur'an, is not so: Uthman only had people read in one form by a choice made between him and those of the Muhajirun and Ansar present, and the first to collect the whole was al-Siddiq. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 392} Al-Hakim is quoted that the third collection was the ordering of suras in Uthman's time. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 387} See [[event/uthman-standardizes-mushaf|Uthman's standardization of the mushaf]], [[concept/adawat-al-kitaba-al-ula|the first writing materials]] and [[concept/kuttab-al-wahy|the scribes of the revelation]].
