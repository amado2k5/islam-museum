## Overview
This is the well-known encounter between the amir al-Hakam ibn Hisham and the people of the suburb of Cordoba. Al-Maqqari relates it in "Nafh al-Tib", and dates the second strife to 202 AH citing Ibn Idhari. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 375}

## What al-Maqqari reports
The people of learning and piety in Cordoba rose against al-Hakam, among them [[person/yahya-ibn-yahya-al-laythi|Yahya ibn Yahya al-Laythi]], the student of Malik and one of the transmitters of the Muwatta from him, and Talut the jurist. They deposed him and gave their allegiance to one of his relatives. They were in the western suburb of [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]]. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 375}

Al-Hakam fought them and defeated them, demolished their houses and mosques, and they fled to Fez and Alexandria. There they rose again, and Abd Allah ibn Tahir marched against them and defeated them, and ferried them to the island of Crete, where they stayed until the Franks took it. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 375}
