## Summary
Al-Sallabi recounts the story of a young man's complaint to Ali: his father had traveled with companions who returned without him, and his wealth disappeared with him. Ali investigated the matter using the police, separated the suspects, and one of them confessed, whereupon they were made to pay back the wealth {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}.

## Facts
A young man complained to Ali ibn Abi Talib about a group who had set out on a journey with his father and returned without his father returning; when he asked them, they said, "He died," and when he asked about his wealth, they said, "He left nothing," though he had a great deal of wealth with him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}. They brought their dispute before Shurayh, who made them swear an oath and let them go {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}.

## Investigation
Ali summoned the police, assigned two men to each suspect, and instructed them not to allow anyone to come near another or speak to him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}. He summoned a scribe and interrogated each one separately: When did the man set out with you? At which stopping place did you camp? How was your journey? From what illness did he die? What happened to his wealth? Who washed and buried him? Who prayed over him? And where was he buried? {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}. When their statements contradicted one another, the suspects thought their companion had confessed against them; Ali ordered the first man brought forward and said to him, "O enemy of God, I have seen your obstinacy and your lying from what I heard from your companions, and nothing will save you from punishment except the truth," and then ordered him sent to prison {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}.

## Outcome
Ali then summoned the suspect in prison and told him that his companions had confessed, so he confessed just as they had; Ali made them pay the wealth and exacted retaliation from them for the slain man {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}. Al-Sallabi comments that the story contains many lessons in the arts of criminal investigation {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 272}, and notes that prisoners were provided with what sustained them in food, condiment, and clothing {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 272}.
