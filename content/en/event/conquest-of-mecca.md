On the tenth of the blessed month of Ramadan in the eighth year of the Hijra, the Messenger of God left Medina with ten thousand Companions, bound for Mecca after Quraysh had broken the peace of al-Hudaybiyya by aiding Banu Bakr against Khuza'a, the Muslims' allies, at al-Watir. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When the army halted at Marr al-Zahran, [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] came out scouting for news; al-Abbas met him and brought him to the Messenger of God, and Abu Sufyan became Muslim. The Prophet made his house a place of safety for the people of Mecca, in honour of him and to win his people's hearts. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

At Dhi Tuwa the Prophet divided his army into four banners: [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] from the lower part of Mecca, [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] from Kada, [[person/abu-ubaydah-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] in the belly of the valley, and [[person/qays-ibn-sad-ibn-ubada|Qays ibn Sa'd ibn Ubada]] with the banner of the Ansar. The Muslims entered Mecca in peace except for a small skirmish at al-Khandama, where Khalid met a band led by [[person/ikrima-ibn-abi-jahl|Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl]] and [[person/safwan-ibn-umayya|Safwan ibn Umayya]] and defeated them; twelve polytheists were killed. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Messenger of God entered Mecca with his head bowed over his mount until his brow nearly touched the middle of the saddle, in humility to God; then he circled the Ancient House while three hundred and sixty idols stood around the Ka'ba, stabbing at them with a bow in his hand saying: "truth has come and falsehood has passed away — falsehood is ever bound to pass." Then he addressed Quraysh at the door of the Ka'ba and proclaimed his historic general pardon: "O company of Quraysh, what do you think I will do with you?" They said: good — a noble brother and a noble brother's son. He said: "no reproach is on you this day; go, you are free." {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## For Sprouts
The Prophet entered Mecca as a conqueror, humble before God. He gave the people of Mecca safety and said to them: Go, for you are free.

## For Explorers
In Ramadan of the eighth year after the Hijra the Prophet entered Mecca with ten thousand Muslims, and he came in humble before God. He cleansed the Kaaba of idols, and told the people of Mecca: Go, for you are free. It was a day of mercy and forgiveness.

## For Scholars
In Ramadan of the eighth year after the Hijra, after Banu Bakr and Quraysh broke the truce of al-Hudaybiyya, the Prophet marched with ten thousand fighters and entered Mecca with his head bowed in humility before God. He divided the army into four columns, cleansed the Kaaba of idols, and proclaimed a general pardon for the people of Mecca with the words: Go, for you are free. In the Sahih the Prophet is reported to have declared the sanctity of the city on that day. The reports give different numbers of idols and different lengths of stay, so each number needs a source. This entry presents the conquest as a day of pardon, as the sources transmit it.

## Al-Sallabi's analysis: the results of the conquest
In [[work/al-sira-al-nabawiyya-sallabi|his Sira]], [[person/ali-muhammad-al-sallabi|Ali Muhammad al-Sallabi]] lists the results of the conquest of Mecca as he sees them {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.778-779}: Mecca came under Muslim control and the way opened to Hunayn and al-Ta'if; a great power arose in Arabia that no tribal coalition could withstand; and there were social, political and religious effects — [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] was left in Mecca to lead the prayer and teach the people, Attab ibn Asid was appointed its governor, and the Arabs were convinced of Islam and entered it in crowds {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.779}. Among the legal rulings drawn from the conquest he gives, relying on al-Umari and Rizq Allah, the shortening of prayer during the nineteen days the Prophet stayed in Mecca, the prohibition of temporary marriage (*mut'a*) after the conquest, the rule "the child belongs to the marriage bed" in the case of Zam'a's slave-woman's son, and the bar on bequeathing more than a third in the case of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.778}. He notes that the Prophet camped at al-Hajun, where Quraysh had sworn to boycott Banu Hashim, and told Usama: "Has Aqil left us any houses or dwellings?" {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.778}.
