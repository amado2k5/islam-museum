## Summary
For the conquest of [[place/mecca|Mecca]] (January 630 CE) the Prophet [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] left [[place/medina|Medina]] on the tenth of Ramadan, 8 AH, with ten thousand Companions {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.380}. Its cause was that [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]] broke the [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|peace of al-Hudaybiyya]] by helping [[group/banu-bakr|Banu Bakr]] with weapons and men against [[group/khuzaa|Khuza'a]], the Prophet's allies, at al-Watir {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.377} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.378}. When the army camped at [[place/marr-al-zahran|Marr al-Zahran]], [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] came out seeking news; [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]] met him and brought him to the Prophet, he accepted Islam, and the Prophet declared safe whoever entered Abu Sufyan's house {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.381} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.382}.

The Prophet went on to [[place/dhu-tuwa|Dhu Tuwa]], his head bowed in humility before God, the hair of his beard almost touching the middle of the saddle, and there divided his army: [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] to enter from lower Mecca, [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]] from upper Mecca and [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda]] along the valley floor {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.384}; the banner of the Ansar had been handed to [[person/qays-ibn-sad|Qays ibn Sa'd ibn Ubada]] or, it is said, to al-Zubayr {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.383}. The Prophet and his army entered Mecca, each column by its road, and at [[place/al-khandama|al-Khandama]] Khalid skirmished with men of Quraysh gathered with [[person/ikrima-ibn-abi-jahl|Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl]] and [[person/safwan-ibn-umayya|Safwan ibn Umayya]]; twelve polytheists were killed and the rest fled {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.384} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.385}. The Prophet then circled the House and with his bow struck at the three hundred and sixty idols around the [[building/kaaba|Ka'ba]], reciting from the Qur'an {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.385}. At the door of the Ka'ba he addressed Quraysh and pardoned them, saying: "Go, for you are free" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.385} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.386}. Al-Mubarakpuri counts it the conquest that ended idolatry as a power in Arabia {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.391}.

## For Sprouts
The Prophet entered Mecca as a conqueror, humble before God. He gave the people of Mecca safety and said to them: Go, for you are free.

## For Explorers
In Ramadan of the eighth year after the Hijra the Prophet entered Mecca with ten thousand Muslims, and he came in humble before God. He cleansed the Kaaba of idols, and told the people of Mecca: Go, for you are free. It was a day of mercy and forgiveness.

## For Scholars
In Ramadan of the eighth year after the Hijra, after Banu Bakr and Quraysh broke the truce of al-Hudaybiyya, the Prophet marched with ten thousand fighters and entered Mecca with his head bowed in humility before God. He divided the army into four columns, cleansed the Kaaba of idols, and proclaimed a general pardon for the people of Mecca with the words: Go, for you are free. In the Sahih the Prophet is reported to have declared the sanctity of the city on that day. The reports give different numbers of idols and different lengths of stay, so each number needs a source. This entry presents the conquest as a day of pardon, as the sources transmit it.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh relates that the Prophet entered Mecca as a conqueror in humility, his beard touching the pommel of his saddle, reciting 'truth has come and falsehood has perished', then asked Quraysh: what do you think I will do with you? They said: good, a noble brother and son of a noble brother; and he said what Yusuf said to his brothers: no blame on you today, go, you are free; he says two thousand embraced Islam that day because of his mercy (needs_source: the number as the Sheikh gives it). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep11 00:08:00}

## Al-Sallabi's analysis: the results of the conquest
In [[work/al-sira-al-nabawiyya-sallabi|his Sira]], [[person/ali-muhammad-al-sallabi|Ali Muhammad al-Sallabi]] lists the results of the conquest of Mecca as he sees them {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.778-779}: Mecca came under Muslim control and the way opened to Hunayn and al-Ta'if; a great power arose in Arabia that no tribal coalition could withstand; and there were social, political and religious effects — [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] was left in Mecca to lead the prayer and teach the people, Attab ibn Asid was appointed its governor, and the Arabs were convinced of Islam and entered it in crowds {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.779}. Among the legal rulings drawn from the conquest he gives, relying on al-Umari and Rizq Allah, the shortening of prayer during the nineteen days the Prophet stayed in Mecca, the prohibition of temporary marriage (*mut'a*) after the conquest, the rule "the child belongs to the marriage bed" in the case of Zam'a's slave-woman's son, and the bar on bequeathing more than a third in the case of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.778}. He notes that the Prophet camped at al-Hajun, where Quraysh had sworn to boycott Banu Hashim, and told Usama: "Has Aqil left us any houses or dwellings?" {cite:sira-nabawiyya-sallabi p.778}.
