## Summary
After the Battle of the Camel, Ali wrote to Muawiya calling on him to enter into the pledge of allegiance, and Muawiya replied that he would not pledge allegiance until the killers of Uthman were handed over to him {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.78}.

## Ali's Letter
Al-Aqqad states that Ali wrote to Muawiya saying that his pledge of allegiance in Medina was binding upon him in Syria, because those who had pledged allegiance to Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman had pledged allegiance to him, so neither one present had the right to choose nor one absent the right to reject it {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.77}. He wrote that shura belongs to the Emigrants and the Helpers, and that Talha and al-Zubayr had pledged allegiance to him and then broke their pledge so he fought them, and he asked Muawiya to enter into what the Muslims had entered into {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.77}.

## Muawiya's Reply
Muawiya replied that Ali's pledge of allegiance would have been just like the pledge of the three caliphs had he been innocent of Uthman's blood, but he had incited against Uthman's blood and forsaken the Helpers, and that the people of Syria refused anything but fighting him until the killers were handed over to them {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.78}. He rejected Ali's treating the Hijaz as judges over the people, because truth had departed from them to the people of Syria {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.78}.

## The Author's Reading
Al-Aqqad considers that the letters opened one door after another, so whenever one door was closed another remained open behind it, because handing over the killers was not enough for Ali, and Ali's innocence was not enough for Muawiya, since the issue returned to shura and renewing the pledge of allegiance {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.78}. He considers that arguments had become futile when each side said something other than what was in its heart {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.78}.
