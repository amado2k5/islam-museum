## Summary
Mu'awiya did not pledge allegiance to Ali after the pledge in Medina; he demanded retribution for the killers of Uthman and said he would pledge allegiance to Ali once retribution was exacted from the killers {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 188}. Al-Sallabi states that Mu'awiya did not dispute the caliphate itself with Ali {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 188}.

## The Report of Abu Muslim al-Khawlani
The book narrates that Abu Muslim al-Khawlani and a group came to Mu'awiya and asked him: "Are you disputing with Ali, or are you his equal?" He replied: "No, by God; I surely know that he is better than I am and more deserving of authority than I am, but do you not know that Uthman was killed wrongfully, and I am his cousin and the claimant for his blood?" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 187}. He told them: Go to him and say to him: let him hand over Uthman's killers to us for retribution, and then I will be the first of the people of Syria to pledge allegiance to him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 188}.

## Citations from Ibn Hajar and Ibn Taymiyya
The book cites Ibn Hajar al-Haytami that the strife erupted "because Mu'awiya and those with him asked Ali to hand over Uthman's killers to them, as he was his cousin, and Ali refused" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 188}. It also cites Ibn Taymiyya that Mu'awiya neither claimed the caliphate nor fought Ali on the grounds that he was not caliph {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 188}.

## The Book's Assessment
Al-Sallabi concludes that "the dispute was over the blood, not over the leadership," and considers the consensus on Ali's rightful claim to be evidence that Mu'awiya did not contest his caliphate {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 188}. The book regards the reports that portray the conflict as a struggle for the caliphate as rejected, without naming their proponents on these pages (needs_source).
