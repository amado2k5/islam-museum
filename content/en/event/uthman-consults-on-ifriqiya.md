## Summary
Uthman consulted the senior Companions regarding a campaign into Ifriqiya after the governor of Egypt, Abd Allah ibn Sa'd, informed him that Muslims were raiding its borders. He met privately with each of them in the mosque, including Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, and al-Abbas, then consulted Sa'id ibn Zayd, who disliked it, citing Umar's view; no one else opposed him, so he called the people to the campaign {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 161}.

## The Book's Report
Al-Sallabi transmits this report from Riyad al-Nufus by al-Maliki, noting that al-Miswar ibn Makhrama said to Uthman, "Raid them," whereupon Uthman said he would gather the elders and consult them, and whatever they agreed upon he would do {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 161}. He then mentions that Uthman said to Sa'id ibn Zayd: "By God, we do not fear them, and they would be content to remain in their places" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 161}.

## After the Consultation
The book mentions that some of the Companions went out with the army, among them Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and Abu Dharr al-Ghifari {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 162}. The book presents this scene in the context of Uthman's practice of consulting the Companions before making a decision {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 161}.
