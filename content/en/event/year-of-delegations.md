## Overview
Ibn Hisham, on the authority of Abu Ubayda, names the ninth year of the Hijra "the year of the delegations". When the Prophet had conquered Mecca and finished with Tabuk, and Thaqif had accepted Islam and given allegiance, "the delegations of the Arabs came to him from every direction." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/205}

This page gathers the delegations that Ibn Hisham mentions in the fourth part (pp. 205–246), and refers each delegation to its own page where it is found, and leaves what has not yet been read in detail as a marker of the work still to do.

## Why the Arabs entered Islam in groups
Ibn Ishaq explains the speed with which the Arabs entered Islam by saying that the Arabs "were waiting for the outcome of this clan of Quraysh", for Quraysh were the leaders and guides of the people, the people of the Sacred House, the pure descendants of Isma'il, and the commanders of the Arabs; and Quraysh were the ones who had set themselves against the Prophet's war and opposition. So when Mecca was taken and Quraysh submitted to him, the Arabs knew they had no strength to fight him or to oppose him, and they entered the religion of God in groups, "as the Mighty and Majestic said". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/205} He points by this to the surah [[passage/quran-110-2|110:2]] and gives its verses in the text; see [[passage/quran-110|the surah of Victory]].

This is Ibn Ishaq's explanation from within the narrative; the modern researchers' readings have not yet been added here.

## The delegations in Ibn Hisham
The following list is in the order and places of Ibn Hisham (the numbers are the pages of the printed fourth part):

- **Banu Tamim** and the surah of al-Hujurat (pp. 206–212): Utarid ibn Hajib among the notables of the Banu Tamim, including [[person/al-aqra-ibn-habis|al-Aqra ibn Habis]], [[person/al-zibriqan-ibn-badr|al-Zibriqan ibn Badr]], Amr ibn al-Ahtam and al-Hubhab, then Nu'aym ibn Yazid, Qays ibn al-Harith and [[person/qays-ibn-asim|Qays ibn Asim]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/206} In their story there is their shouting at the Messenger, the speech of Utarid and the reply of [[person/thabit-ibn-qays|Thabit ibn Qays]], the poems of al-Zibriqan and Hassan, their acceptance of Islam and the Prophet's permission to them (pp. 207–212). In this section falls the opening of [[passage/quran-49-1|the surah of al-Hujurat]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/206}
- **Amir ibn al-Tufayl and Arbad ibn Qays** (pp. 212–215): Amir's scheme for treachery against the Messenger, his death by the Prophet's supplication against him, Arbad's death by lightning, what was revealed about them, and [[person/labid-ibn-rabia|Labid]]'s elegy for Arbad (see [[person/amir-ibn-al-tufayl|Amir]] and [[person/arbad-ibn-qays|Arbad]]).
- **Dimam ibn Thalaba**, as delegate of the Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr (pp. 215–220): his questions to the Messenger, then his acceptance of Islam and his call to his people (see [[person/dimam-ibn-thalaba|Dimam]]).
- **Al-Jarud among the Abd al-Qays** (pp. 220–222): the Messenger's guarantee of his religion and his acceptance of Islam, and his stance against his people in the apostasy; see [[event/delegation-of-abd-al-qays|the delegation of Abd al-Qays]].
- **The delegation of the Banu Hanifa** and Musaylima with them (pp. 222–223): what the Messenger did for Musaylima, then his apostasy and claim to prophethood; see [[event/delegation-of-banu-hanifa|the delegation of the Banu Hanifa]] and [[person/musaylima|Musaylima]].
- **Zayd al-Khayl in the delegation of Tayy** (p. 224): his acceptance of Islam and death; see [[person/zayd-al-khayl|Zayd al-Khayl]]. Then **the story of [[person/adi-ibn-hatim|Adi ibn Hatim]]** (pp. 225–227): his flight to Syria to escape the Messenger, the capture and release of Hatim's daughter, her advice to him to accept Islam, and his coming and acceptance of Islam.
- **Farwa ibn Musayk al-Muradi** (pp. 228–229): the day of al-Radm between Murad and Hamdan, and his coming and acceptance of Islam.
- **Amr ibn Ma'dikarib** with some people of the Banu Zubayd (pp. 230–231): his apostasy and his poem about it; see [[person/amr-ibn-madikarib|Amr ibn Ma'dikarib]].
- **Al-Ash'ath ibn Qays in the delegation of Kinda** (p. 232): their coming and acceptance of Islam, and al-Ash'ath's ascription to "the eater of the bitter" (Akil al-Murar); see [[person/al-ashath-ibn-qays|al-Ash'ath]].
- **Surad ibn Abd Allah al-Azdi** (pp. 233–234): his acceptance of Islam, his fighting with the people of Jurash, the Messenger's telling the envoys of Jurash what had happened to their people, and their acceptance of Islam.
- **The envoy of the kings of Himyar** with their letter and the Messenger's letter to them (p. 235), then **the dispatch of Mu'adh to Yemen** and his instruction (pp. 236–237); see [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]].
- **Farwa ibn Amr al-Judhami** (pp. 237–238): his acceptance of Islam, the Byzantines' imprisonment of him, his poem in prison and his killing; see [[person/farwa-ibn-amr-al-judhami|Farwa]].
- **The Banu al-Harith ibn Ka'b** at the hands of Khalid ibn al-Walid (pp. 239–242): Khalid's call and their acceptance of Islam, Khalid's letter and the Messenger's answer, their coming, and the dispatch of Amr ibn Hazm with his charge to them.
- **Rifa'a ibn Zayd al-Judhami** (p. 243): his acceptance of Islam and his carrying of the Messenger's letter to his people.
- **The delegation of Hamdan** (pp. 243–245): their names, the words "Ibn Namat" before the Messenger, and the Messenger's letter to them forbidding certain things; see [[event/delegation-of-hamdan|the delegation of Hamdan]].
- **The delegation of Thaqif** comes earlier in the order (pp. 182–199); see [[event/delegation-of-thaqif|the delegation of Thaqif]], and [[event/delegation-of-najran|the delegation of Najran]] in its place.

After the delegations in the book come the mention of the two liars, Musaylima and al-Aswad al-Ansi, the leaders and agents who rebelled over the alms, and then the Farewell Pilgrimage (pp. 246–247); see [[event/farewell-pilgrimage|the Farewell Pilgrimage]].

## What remains
This page relies on the index of the fourth part to locate the delegations, and on Ibn Ishaq's opening paragraph (p. 205) and the start of the delegation of Tamim (p. 206). The details of each delegation await the reading of its pages (pp. 206–246), and each delegation will have its own event or join an existing one.

## Significance
In Ibn Ishaq's account, this year is the one in which the Arabs entered Islam in groups, after their fear of confronting Quraysh had gone following the conquest.

## For young visitors
After the conquest of Mecca many delegations came to Medina from Arab tribes, each carrying its news and its questions, and the Prophet welcomed them and wrote to them. This year was called "the year of the delegations."

## Notes for researchers
- The date: "the ninth year" on the authority of Abu Ubayda, as Ibn Hisham says (p. 205).
- The places of the delegations are taken from the index of the printed fourth part, and some headings may need correction against the page images.
- Ibn Hisham does not mention Thaqif within this year, but before it in the section on al-Ta'if; the place here is only for reference.

## For Sprouts
In the ninth year after the Hijra the Arab tribes came to Medina from every side to pledge Islam to the Prophet, so much that historians call that year the Year of Delegations. Each delegation asked the Prophet its questions and carried Islam's teachings back to its people.

## For Explorers
The Year of Delegations is the ninth year after the Hijra (630–631 CE): after the conquest of Mecca, the expedition to Tabuk and the conversion of Thaqif, 'the delegations of the Arabs struck toward him from every side' as Ibn Hisham transmits from Abu Ubayda. Dozens of tribal delegations came to Medina — Tamim, Hawazin, Ghatafan, Bakr ibn Wa'il, Abd al-Qays, Thaqif and the peoples of Yemen and Bahrayn — each asking about Islam, pledging, and carrying the Prophet's instructions home, some taking teachers with them. Ibn Hisham devotes the fourth part of his sira to narrating these delegations one by one; some left lasting traces, like the Banu Abd al-Qays who came twice. In history the Year of Delegations marks the peninsula's political turn after the fall of Mecca.

## For Scholars
The Year of Delegations (9 AH / 630–631 CE) is the name Ibn Hisham chose, from Abu Ubayda, for the ninth year: after the conquest of Mecca, the Tabuk expedition and Thaqif's conversion, 'the delegations of the Arabs struck toward him from every side' — every tribe sending envoys or coming itself to Medina: Tamim, Hawazin, Asad, Ghatafan, Sulaym, Bakr ibn Wa'il, Taghlib, Abd al-Qays, Thaqif, Kinda, Hamdan, the peoples of Bahrayn, Yemen and Hadramawt, Judham, Akk and the Ash'aris, plus famous individuals like Abu Dharr, Dimam ibn Tha'laba and al-Tufayl ibn Amr. Ibn Hisham's fourth part (pp. 205–246) narrates these delegations one by one, some keeping political or religious consequence — like the two Abd al-Qays delegations of consecutive years instructed in prayer, alms, fasting and Qur'an recitation, or the Thaqif delegation that embraced Islam, built its mosque and destroyed al-Lat. Historians read in it the moment Islam gained dominion over the peninsula and the era of the besieged, autonomous tribe ended: individual and tribal pledge to the Prophet became the peninsula's new order of security.
