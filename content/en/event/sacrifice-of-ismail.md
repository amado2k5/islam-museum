## Summary
[[person/ibrahim|Abraham]]'s vision that he was slaughtering his son [[person/ismail|Ishmael]], their compliance with the command, and the ransom with a great sacrifice: this is the story the Qur'an recounts in Sura al-Saffat. Al-Rahiq al-Makhtum regards it as the first of Abraham's recorded journeys to Mecca. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19}

## The story
God states in the Qur'an that He showed Abraham in a dream that he was slaughtering Ishmael, and he carried out the command, as in [[passage/quran-37-103|al-Saffat 103]] through [[passage/quran-37-107|107]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19}

## Its place in Abraham's journeys
The author notes from the book of Genesis that Ishmael was thirteen years older than Isaac. He holds that the context of the story shows it happened before Isaac's birth, since the tidings of Isaac are given only after the whole story has been told. The story therefore includes at least one journey of Abraham to Mecca before Ishmael grew up. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19} {cite:sifr-al-takwin 17/25} See [[event/ibrahim-visits-ismail-in-mecca|Abraham's visits to Mecca]].

## What is not covered here
The old disagreement over which son was the one to be sacrificed (Ishmael or Isaac) is not presented by al-Rahiq at this point. It determines that the one sacrificed was Ishmael, and relies on the order of events in Genesis. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.19}

## For young visitors
Abraham saw in a dream that he was slaughtering his son. Both of them obeyed the command, and God ransomed the son with a great sacrifice.
