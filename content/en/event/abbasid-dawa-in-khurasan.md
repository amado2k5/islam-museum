## Overview
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports, introducing it with “they say”, that in the year Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik came to power, early in 101 AH, the partisans came to Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas at al-Humayma in Syria, and he told them to call people gently and in secret. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 332} He sent Maysara and Ibn Khunays to Iraq and Abu Ikrima and Hayyan to Khurasan, who went from district to district calling to him in the guise of merchants. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 333}

## The missionaries
[[person/bukayr-ibn-mahan|Bukayr ibn Mahan]] succeeded Maysara in Iraq and later appointed [[person/abu-salama-al-khallal|Abu Salama al-Khallal]]. Abu Ikrima and Hayyan were seized in Khurasan by Asad ibn Abd Allah, executed and crucified. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 334} The imam then sent five men, among them Sulayman ibn Kathir, Malik ibn al-Haytham and Musa ibn Ka'b, ordering them to bind every recruit by oath to secrecy; they travelled through Merv, Bukhara, Samarqand, Kish, Nasaf and other places until the cause was known throughout Khurasan. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 335} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 336}

## Abu Muslim
The leadership in Khurasan then passed to [[person/abu-muslim-al-khurasani|Abu Muslim]], who sent each companion to a region, travelling as merchants, and set them a fixed day for the rising. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 343} Al-Dinawari reports that the followers in Khurasan dyed their clothes black in mourning when they heard of Muhammad ibn Ali's death, the first being Hurayth, client of Khuza'a, and then [[person/qahtaba-ibn-shabib|Qahtaba ibn Shabib]]. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 339} On the appointed day they came from every district in black, with the watchword “O Mansur”, some hundred thousand men. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 360} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 361} The da'wa ended with the oath to Abu al-Abbas at Kufa in Rajab 132. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 370}
