## Summary
The Bishwa Ijtema, the "global congregation", is an annual gathering of Muslims at Tongi, on the bank of the River Turag on the outskirts of [[place/dhaka|Dhaka]], organised by the Bangladeshi branch of the Tablighi Jamaat. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} It lasts three days, during which participants pray and listen to talks by scholars from many countries, and it closes with the final supplication known as the Akheri Munajat. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} The name joins the Bengali *bishwa*, "world", to the Arabic *ijtima'*, "gathering". {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en}

## How it began
The first annual Tablighi Jamaat conference in the region was held in 1946 at [[building/kakrail-mosque|Kakrail Mosque]] near Ramna Park in Dhaka. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} After the movement formally began its work in East Bengal in 1954, it held gatherings at Haji Camp in Chittagong (1954), at Siddhirganj in Narayanganj (1958) and at the Ramna Race Course in Dhaka in 1960, 1962 and 1965. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} In 1967, because of the growing numbers, the government of East Pakistan allowed it to move to the field of Pagar village at Tongi on the Turag; from then it was known as the Bishwa Ijtema, and people came from many countries. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} Since 1972 it has been held on an open ground of 160 acres on the river's north-eastern bank, allotted by the Government of Bangladesh. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en}

## How it runs
It is usually held in January, sometimes in February, on a site of more than five square kilometres. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} Canopies are fixed on bamboo poles, and the ground is divided into sections (*khitta*) and pole numbers by which participants find their places, with a separate area for foreign guests. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} A phase usually opens on a Friday with a sermon after the Friday prayer and ends on the Sunday with the final supplication, which many attend who do not spend the three days, and in which the President, the Prime Minister and the Leader of the Opposition take part. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} The state helps with transport by air, rail and road, the armed forces with infrastructure, and the police and the Rapid Action Battalion with security, while volunteers provide meals and lodging free of charge. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} The final supplication had been given in Urdu; in 2018 it was given in Bengali, by the article's account for the first time since 1980. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en}

## Phases and attendance
Because of the crowds the gathering has been held since 2010 in two phases a week apart, each for people from designated districts, with foreign participants admitted to both; from 2015 the districts were divided into four groups. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} Attendance figures are press estimates and vary: about 2 million in 2001, about 5 million at its height as The Telegraph reported, about 2 million in 2018 and about 4 million in 2023. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} Reuters estimated between 20,000 and 50,000 foreign participants in 2007, from South Asia, Russia and Central Asia, Europe, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, North Africa and the United States. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en}

## Particular years
In 2008 the gathering was cut to a single day by rain and cold, and three participants died. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} In 2024 it was held in two phases, 2 to 4 and 9 to 11 February; the second drew participants from about 56 countries, and fourteen elderly participants died at the venue during the first. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} In December 2024 clashes broke out at the venue between two factions of the Tablighi Jamaat, one following Maulana Zubair Ahmed, imam of Kakrail Mosque, and the other Maulana Muhammad Saad Kandhlawi; eight people were killed and fifty injured, and police and soldiers cleared the ground. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en}

## Today
The gathering is still held each year at Tongi; the press reports the article draws on describe it as non-political and attended voluntarily. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en} It has Wikipedia articles in twelve languages, Bengali, Urdu and Chinese among them. {cite:wikipedia-bishwa-ijtema-en}
