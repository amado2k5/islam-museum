## Summary
The head of the hypocrites, [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy]], died after the Prophet's return from [[event/expedition-to-tabuk|Tabuk]] in 9 AH {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.439}.

## The prayer over him and Umar's stance
The Prophet asked forgiveness for him and prayed over him, although [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] had tried to stop him from doing so. Afterwards the Qur'an came down in agreement with Umar, forbidding prayer over such people and standing at their graves {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.439}.

## For young visitors
A man died who was reported to have said that he was a Muslim, but who harmed the Muslims in secret. The Prophet prayed over him, although Umar had advised him not to. Then God's ruling came that agreed with what Umar had said.

## Ibn Ishaq's account: Umar's words in full
Ibn Ishaq narrates from al-Zuhri, from Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah, from Ibn Abbas, from Umar ibn al-Khattab, that when Abdullah ibn Ubayy died, the Prophet was called to pray over him. He stood up to do so. When he stood before the body ready to pray, Umar moved until he stood in front of his chest and said: "O Messenger of God, will you pray over the enemy of God, Abdullah ibn Ubayy, who said such-and-such on such-and-such a day?" He began to count his days. The Prophet was smiling. When Umar had said much, the Prophet said: "Umar, step back from me. I was given a choice and I chose. I was told that it makes no difference whether you ask forgiveness for them or not. If I knew that by going beyond seventy God would forgive him, I would go beyond it" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/196}. Then the Prophet prayed over him, walked with the funeral and stood at the grave until the burial was complete {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/197}. Shortly afterwards, the verses [[passage/quran-9-84|verse 84 of al-Tawba]] and [[passage/quran-9-80|verse 80]] came down, and the Prophet never prayed over a hypocrite after that until God took him {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/197}.

## Notes on the report
- The report comes from Ibn Ishaq with a continuous chain to Ibn Abbas {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/196}.
- It is read alongside [[event/abu-bakr-leads-the-prayers|Abu Bakr's leading of the prayers]] during the Prophet's illness. Both revolve around prayer and what it implies for the congregation {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/197}.
