## Overview
These reports, from al-Baladhuri's "Futuh al-Buldan", tell the history of the governor's house (Dar al-Imara) in [[place/basra|Basra]], from its first building until it was placed in the qibla of the mosque in the caliphate of al-Rashid, so that the governors of Basra no longer had a governor's house. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The first building
- Utba built the house apart from the mosque, in an open space called al-Dahna', in which were the prison and the diwan.
- Then Abu Musa rebuilt it with mudbrick and clay.
- Ziyad turned it into the qibla of the mosque. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## al-Hajjaj
When [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj]] came, he wanted to remove Ziyad's name from it, and he resolved to rebuild it. It was said to him that he only wished to fix Ziyad's name in it, so he demolished it and left it. People built their houses around it of its clay and mudbrick and doors, so Basra had no governor's house. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The rebuilding
- In the governorship of Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik, [[person/salih-ibn-abd-al-rahman|Salih ibn Abd al-Rahman]] ordered it rebuilt, and he rebuilt it in brick and gypsum on its old foundation and raised its height.
- [[person/adi-ibn-artat|Adi ibn Artat]] wanted to build upper rooms over it, and [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] wrote to him rebuking him, and he refrained.
- Sulayman ibn Ali built, on the walls of the rooms he had raised, a building of clay, then moved to al-Mirbad and settled there.
- When al-Rashid became caliph, the house was included in the qibla of the mosque. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Note
The material transmitted here carries no specific dates, and it needs an additional source for dating.
