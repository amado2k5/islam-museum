## Overview
Ali ibn al-Fadl entered Sanaa at night with five thousand of his men, and by morning on Ashura they held Ghumdan and the great mosque. They plundered goods and furniture for fifteen days.

## Detail
The Qarmatians marched through the night until they held Mount Naqm, where they stayed three days without descending. Then, on the night of Saturday, Ali ibn al-Fadl marched with five thousand of his fighters and men on foot and entered Sanaa at night by the Shihabiyyin lane, which Mahdhab al-Shihabi let them in by. By morning they held Ghumdan and the great mosque, which was Ashura. The people of Sanaa fled with their women and children and left their homes and goods. As'ad fought them until after the afternoon prayer on Saturday, and then left Sanaa. The Qarmatians then plundered Sanaa, took all the goods and furniture, and dug out what was hidden underground. They stayed fifteen days and held back from killing, except for a few men. The date is Ashura 293 AH, which the tabular calendar places in about mid-November 905 CE (a Monday in the civil-epoch tabulation, a Sunday in the astronomical-epoch one); the text itself places the entry on the night of Saturday, and the record keeps the printed day and flags the difference.
