## Summary
In 623 AH the Frankish prince of Antioch gathered a great host and marched against the Armenians of al-Durub, and a hard war raged between them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 469}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir explains the cause: the Armenian Ibn Layun, lord of al-Durub, had died leaving no son, only a daughter, whom the Armenians made their ruler; then, seeing that kingship could not rest on a woman, they married her to the prince's son, who moved to their country and held the kingdom for about a year; they then repented, fearing the Franks would take over their land, rose against the prince's son, seized him and imprisoned him, and refused his father's demand to release and reinstate him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 469} The prince asked the Pope, king of the Franks in Greater Rome, for leave to attack their lands; the Pope forbade it, saying they were of his faith, but the prince disobeyed and wrote to Ala al-Din Kayqubad, king of Konya and Malatya, agreeing to invade Ibn Layun's lands together; the Davi and the Hospitallers objected, citing the Pope's prohibition, yet he entered the Armenian borders — hard passes and rugged mountains — and achieved little, while Kayqubad came in from his own, easier side, plundered and burned the country, besieged several forts taking four, and was driven back by winter. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 469}
