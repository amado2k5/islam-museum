Then the affliction on the Muslims grew severe and their clans pressed hard upon them, so the Prophet counselled them to emigrate to Abyssinia once more — harsher than the first time, for Quraysh was alert to it and resolved to thwart it; but the Muslims were quicker and reached [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]] before they could be caught. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

This time eighty-three men emigrated — if [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar]] was among them, it is doubted — and eighteen or nineteen women. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

It galled the polytheists that they had found refuge, so they chose two hardy men — [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-rabia|Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'a]], before they became Muslim — and sent them with gifts to the Negus and his bishops to have the Muslims expelled. The Negus heard [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far ibn Abi Talib]] describe Islam, then Ja'far recited to him the opening of Sura Maryam, and he wept until his beard was wet, and his bishops wept; he told them that this and what Jesus brought come from a single lamp-niche. At another meeting they asked them what they said of Christ, and Ja'far said: we say of him what our Prophet brought us — he is God's servant, His messenger, His spirit and His word which He cast to Mary. The Negus took a stick from the ground and said: Jesus son of Mary did not exceed what you said by this stick — and he refused to hand them over and returned the gifts. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The sources do not state the time of the two delegations, but the book judges, on reflection, that they fell in the middle of the sixth year of the prophethood with a short interval between them. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## For Sprouts
When the Muslims' ordeal grew heavy the Prophet pointed them to a second migration to Abyssinia — harder than the first — and eighty-three men and about eighteen women reached the Negus.
