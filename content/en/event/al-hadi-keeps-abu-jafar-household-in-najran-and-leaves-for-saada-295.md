## Overview
Abu Ja'far asked to be relieved of the town, saying its people were bad. He then asked that his household be placed at the fort among Hamdan while he stayed with Banu al-Harith, and al-Hadi agreed. After Ibn Bistam was killed, al-Hadi sent to forbid the move, saying it would weaken his position, and told Abu Ja'far to keep his household in the house where they were. Al-Hadi then left for Sa'da and left with Muhammad twenty-three horsemen and fifty-five foot soldiers.

## Detail
The book says that Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah came to al-Hadi before Ibn Bistam was killed and asked to be relieved of the town, because its people were bad and grew only worse and more cursed. Al-Hadi said he did not want to lay on him a charge he disliked and told him to seek God's guidance in the matter. Abu Ja'far then asked that his household be placed at the fort among Hamdan, where they would be safe, while he stayed with Banu al-Harith to face them. Al-Hadi agreed that his household go to the fort, with himself and his son Ali ibn Muhammad moving between al-Hajr and the fort. After Ibn Bistam was killed, al-Hadi sent to Muhammad that moving the household to the fort would weaken his position and invite his enemy, and told him to leave them in the house where they had been, since Banu al-Harith, if they meant treachery, would aim at that house. The book then says that al-Hadi left for Sa'da on Saturday, five days into Dhu al-Hijja 295 as printed; this date conflicts with the sequence of events and is flagged for checking. Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah stayed in the village of Najran, with twenty-three horsemen and fifty-five foot soldiers of the army.
