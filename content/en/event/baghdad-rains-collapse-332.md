## Overview
In Jumada I 332 the rains multiplied until houses collapsed and many died under the rubble; Baghdad property values kept falling amid dearth — coarse bread reached three ratls a dirham and dates two ratls. Bathhouses closed, markets and mosques went idle until men were sought to live in houses for a wage to guard them; night raids by thieves armed with candles grew, and people kept night watch with cudgels {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 623}.
