## Summary
Between 422 and 425 AH a run of deadly calamities struck the lands of Islam: drought, epidemics and smallpox spread through Khurasan, al-Jabal, Iraq and Egypt. It was said that forty thousand were buried in Isfahan within a few days, and that seventy thousand died in Baghdad in Dhu al-Hijja 425; these are the figures the sources give. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.402, 411-412, 422}

## From calamities to smallpox
In 422 the rain was late, bodies dried out, and coughing and calamities multiplied until the sale of broth became brisk. In 423 the epidemic came and spread through India, Ghazna, Khurasan, Jurjan, Ray and al-Jabal, and four thousand boys died of smallpox in Mosul. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.402, 411-412}

## Baghdad and the caliph
In [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]] smallpox spread from April to October, and no house was spared. In 424 the caliph [[person/al-qaim-billah|al-Qaʾim]] himself caught smallpox when he entered the bathhouse, and he was helped out. Death increased in 425, especially among the women of al-Hawaniq. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.412, 414, 419}
