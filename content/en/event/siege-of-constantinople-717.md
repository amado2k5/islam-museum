## Overview
A naval and land expedition sent by Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik against Constantinople under [[person/maslama-ibn-abd-al-malik|Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik]]. The book states that the land force was about a hundred and twenty thousand and the naval about a thousand ships. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.47}

## Preparation
Preparation began in the days of al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, and Sulayman continued it after his death in 96 AH. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.47}

## The plan of march
The commanders disagreed about the plan. Musa ibn Nusayr advised taking the cities and fortresses first, without Constantinople. Maslama advised heading straight for it, and the caliph took his advice. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.48}

## The withdrawal
At Sulayman's death Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz ordered Maslama to return. The army withdrew after a truce with the Byzantines. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.49}

## The reasons for failure in the author's account
The book attributes the failure to what it describes as treachery and incitement, to the severity of the winter of 99 AH, and to Maslama's lack of military experience. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 pp.50-52}

The book does not give the Gregorian date, which needs an additional source.
