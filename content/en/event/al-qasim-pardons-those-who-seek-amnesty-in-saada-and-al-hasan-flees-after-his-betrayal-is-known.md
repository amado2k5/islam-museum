## Overview
People come to him seeking amnesty and he pardons them, and the strife subsides. Al-Hasan's betrayal of his brother becomes known, and he flees to Khiwan, corresponds with Ibn al-Dahhak, and seeks help from the rulers against his brother, but none gives him aid.

## Detail
The book says al-Qasim did not let his men seize people's goods or their markets. The enemy then broke, and al-Qasim stayed on his horse until morning, until people came to him seeking amnesty; he granted it and pardoned them, the strife subsided, and the people learned that al-Hasan had broken faith with his brother, so the town turned to al-Qasim. When al-Hasan learned what the tribe now knew of his betrayal, he left for a branch of Sa'd, then fled to Khiwan. He corresponded with Ibn al-Dahhak and sought help from the rulers against his brother, and none of them gave him anything.
