## Overview
Near [[place/athafit|Athafit]] is a place called [[place/bayt-dhud-yemen|Bayt Dhu'd]], then in the hand of [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am ibn Ibrahim]]; its people came to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] among those who came at Athafit, and appealed to him against al-Da'am and what was done to them in their land. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.94} The narrator says that al-Da'am had with him profligate troops who drank wine and committed immorality in the open, and that they reported that a soldier had taken a girl by force and killed her father, and told al-Da'am, who did not object. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.94} These are a Zaydi narrator's allegations about his opponent's troops, given as his and not affirmed by the museum.

## Al-Hadi's governor
When they told him, he sent with them a man of his cousins of the line of Umar ibn Ali; on his arrival all of al-Da'am's governors there left the town, and al-Hadi's appointee enjoined good, forbade wrong and put away indecencies. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.94} The narrator reports from a man he trusts that there were in al-Da'am's army at Bayt Dhu'd four hundred women who openly practised immorality, with soldiers taking their disputes over them to their rulers; all of that was put away, justice was established and all the townspeople stood with al-Hadi. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.94} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.95} The details the narrator attributes to the troops are not repeated, being hearsay from unnamed sources.

## Sa'sa'a ibn Ja'far
In this connection a man of Hamdan called [[person/sasaa-ibn-jafar-al-hamdani|Sa'sa'a ibn Ja'far]], who was in a strong palace at [[place/raydah-yemen|Raydah]], wrote to al-Hadi and sent him word of hearing and obedience; al-Hadi sent some Hamdanis to hold the land with him, part of which was in al-Da'am's hand, and they put out al-Da'am's agents, at which the people were glad and heard and obeyed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.95} He is the same man who appears in the account of Raydah in 288 AH, and the text does not say here what became of him afterwards.
