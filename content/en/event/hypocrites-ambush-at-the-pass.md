## Summary
Twelve men of the hypocrites tried to do away with the Prophet at a mountain pass on the way back from [[event/expedition-to-tabuk|Tabuk]] to Medina in 9 AH {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 436}.

## The account of the pass
The Prophet was crossing that pass with [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar]] leading his she-camel by the halter and [[person/hudhayfa-ibn-al-yaman|Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman]] driving it, while the people took the belly of the valley; they heard the men's movement behind them as they crept up muffled. Hudhayfa struck the faces of their mounts with a staff he had, and God terrified them so they fled quickly until they caught up with the company {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 436}.

The Prophet told Hudhayfa their names and what they had intended — for which Hudhayfa was called the keeper of the Messenger of God's secret; and in it is His word: "they purposed what they did not attain" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 436}.

## For young visitors
Some hypocrite men hid in a narrow mountain pass to frighten the Prophet's camel by night; his companion Hudhayfa defended him bravely and drove them off, and the Prophet rewarded him by entrusting him with his secret.
