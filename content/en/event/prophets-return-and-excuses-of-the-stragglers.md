## Summary
Those who stayed behind came to the Prophet after his return, swearing oaths and excusing themselves; he accepted their outward show and left their innermost thoughts to God — per Ka'b ibn Malik's account in Ibn Ishaq. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/175, 177}

## The account
Ka'b said: the Prophet entered Medina in the morning; his custom when arriving from a journey was to begin at the mosque, praying two rak'as, then sit for the people. Those who had stayed behind came swearing oaths and excusing themselves — eighty-odd men — and he accepted their outward words and oaths, asked forgiveness for them and left their inner selves to God. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/177}

## Ibn Ishaq's phrasing
In the main text Ibn Ishaq describes these as a band of hypocrites who came, swore and made excuses — "and the Messenger of God forgave them". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/175}

## The verses
At the end of Ka'b's account Ibn Ishaq conveys two verses of Sura al-Tawba on those who lied and excused themselves: [[passage/quran-9-95|al-Tawba 95]] and [[passage/quran-9-96|al-Tawba 96]], as Ka'b mentioned. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/181}

## Source notes and markers of doubt
- "Eighty-odd men": the number is Ka'b's words, not Ibn Ishaq's (p. 177).
- Describing the excusers as hypocrites is the narrator's phrasing, not the museum's judgement.
