## Overview
A poem in the kamil metre under the heading "and also of al-Hadi" on p. 323: it opens by asking that the heart be healed of sorrows, says the one addressed will not seek martyrdom until he wakes from drowsiness, kindles fires after they died down and girds his saddle on a dark, full-grown horse, then describes the horse at length in its speed and strength, and says its rider is one with a blood-wrong who has studied the Book and ranged through it seeking guidance until he was certain of what is for and against him and of God's obligations, and so called to jihad against the tyrant; the lines run on into pp. 324-325. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.323}

## Detail
On p. 323 the heading reads "and also of al-Hadi" (kamil) and the first line "heal the heart, the heart of one of kindness, perhaps you will be healed of sorrows", and goes on "know that you will not seek martyrdom until you wake from the lethargy of the drowsy" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.323}. He says the one addressed must kindle fires after they died down, remove the bewilderment of the bewildered, and gird his saddle "on a dark, full-grown horse, lean in the flanks, rising, of standing", then describes the horse as stout, firm-hearted and the first on the field; the footnote says manuscript S reads "dhi mamima" in one descriptive word {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.323}. On p. 324 the description goes on: the horses fall behind it when they try to catch it, onlookers marvel at it as it walks and eyes are dazzled by its running, its hooves throw up behind them what look like firm rocks, it complains of no strain and fears no sore hoof, and it runs over level ground and rough {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.323}. He calls it wide in the nostrils and flanks, short in three parts and long in seven, "the grief of enemies", and says it "runs with a man who has a blood-wrong to his avenger, one with help and insight, wakeful" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.324}. He says this rider "studied the Book and ranged through its regions seeking guidance in it and every exposition, until he was certain of what is for him and against him and of the obligations to the One, the Giver", that the verses of the Book and the Furqan spoke in clear Arabic from their Lord, and he called with the firmest of what he supposed, and made clear "the duties of guidance and jihad against the one who oversteps" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.324}. Here the poet turns to those he describes, and those lines come in [[event/al-hadi-names-the-wrongs-of-his-opponents-and-what-he-did-for-the-people-in-verse-292|the next entry]]. The one addressed at the start is not named; the poet describes the horse and rider as an ideal, not as an event, and the book gives neither time nor war. It follows [[event/al-daam-answers-al-hadi-in-verse-with-body-sons-wealth-and-the-clans-of-hamdan-292|al-Da'am's answer]] in the book. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
