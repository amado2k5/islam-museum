## Summary
In 623 AH Baraq Hajib, Jalal al-Din's deputy at Kirman, rebelled and sought the kingdom for himself; Jalal al-Din marched on him, he fortified himself in his strongholds, and was confirmed in his wilaya. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 463}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that in Jumada al-Akhira word reached Jalal al-Din that his Kirman deputy, a great amir named Baraq Hajib, had rebelled, hoping to take and rule the land for himself because Jalal al-Din was far away and busy with the Georgians and others, and that he had written to the Tatars telling them of Jalal al-Din's strength and urging them to come and take the lands from him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 463} On hearing this Jalal al-Din, who had been marching toward Akhlat, left it and sped to Kirman, sending ahead a messenger with robes of honor to reassure Baraq Hajib and bring him in; when the messenger arrived Baraq realized it was a ruse, took what he treasured, climbed into an impregnable fortress and placed trusted men in his strongholds, writing back that he was the servant and slave and had only emptied the land for his master out of fear; Jalal al-Din saw he could not take the fortresses without a long siege, so he halted near Isfahan, sent him the robe of honor and confirmed him in his wilaya. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 463}
