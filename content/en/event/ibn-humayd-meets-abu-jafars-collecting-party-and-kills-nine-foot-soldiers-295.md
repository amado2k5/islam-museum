## Overview
When the soldiers' pay fell due and no revenue had come in, Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Ba'dani advised that the remnants of an old settlement be collected from Banu al-Harith. A party of three horsemen and fifteen foot soldiers was sent. Ibn Humayd met them, killed nine of the foot soldiers, took three horses, and wounded a man named Salih ibn Abi al-Tayyib badly; the rest escaped.

## Detail
The book says that when the soldiers' allowances came due and they demanded them from Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah, he sent for Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Ba'dani, who was in charge of revenue at Najran and of the army's expenses, and consulted him. Hasan replied that no revenue was coming in these days, and that Banu al-Harith still owed the remnants of a settlement; he should send to them and collect what was due and pass it to the soldiers and the Bedouin. This exchange took place before the army, and the soldiers asked that a list be drawn up of those who still owed, and it was. When the party sent with the list, three horsemen and fifteen foot soldiers, reached Banu al-Harith, Ibn Humayd met them with some of his companions. Seeing that they were few and alone, his men fell on them. They killed nine of the foot soldiers, among them men of Hamdan, took the three horses and stripped their riders, and inflicted heavy wounds on a man named Salih ibn Abi al-Tayyib; the rest escaped.
