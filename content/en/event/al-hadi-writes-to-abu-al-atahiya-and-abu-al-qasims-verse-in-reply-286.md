## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] had also written to [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] telling him of what he did to [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] and the victory and help God gave him over them, and wrote to [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] to send on Abu al-Atahiya's letters; Abu al-Qasim wrote him a letter giving the news of al-Hadi's fight and spoke a poem about it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.181} According to the editor the text here is badly disturbed in the other manuscript. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.181}

## The poem
The lines say they do not fear their enemy nor complain of death in hard times, that their father is the Messenger of God whose station they hold and whose guidance to justice they follow, that whoever seizes their right will find the blade and the sword his share, that whoever keeps pacts will find the best he hopes for in generous gifts, that they are the lions of war whose throats meet the spears and who do not meet them in jest, and that their spears flutter with a hum like bees, woe to whoever they reach. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.181} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.182} Abu al-Qasim then sent al-Hadi's answer on to [[place/najran|Najran]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.182}

## Note
The museum reads "Abu al-Atahiya" here as [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr]], the holder of Sana'a, as probable and not certain; the name recurs in the book. The book does not say where the letters were sent. The poem before this is in [[event/al-hadi-writes-to-abu-al-qasim-of-entering-the-village-and-his-verse-answer-286]].
