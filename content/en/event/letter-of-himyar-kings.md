## Summary
The kings of Himyar wrote to the Prophet after his return from Tabuk, declaring their Islam and their renunciation of polytheism. He answered them with a letter setting out what is due to the believers and what is upon them, and sent a group led by Mu'adh ibn Jabal. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.449}

## The letter
The letter of the kings of Himyar reached the Prophet after his return from Tabuk. They were al-Harith ibn Abd Kulal, Nu'aym ibn Abd Kulal, and al-Nu'man ibn Qayl Dhu Ru'ayn, of Hamdan and Ma'afir. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.449} Their envoy to him was Malik ibn Murra al-Rahawi, whom they sent to announce their Islam and their renunciation of polytheism and its people. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.449} The Prophet wrote to them a letter setting out what is due to the believers and what is upon them, and granted those of them who were under treaty the protection of God and of His Messenger, once they paid what was due from them in jizya. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.449} He sent them men of his Companions, led by Mu'adh ibn Jabal. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.449}

## Ibn Ishaq's account
The letter of the kings of Himyar reached the Prophet on his return from Tabuk. Their envoy, who brought news of their Islam, was [[person/al-harith-ibn-abd-kulal|al-Harith ibn Abd Kulal]], [[person/nuaym-ibn-abd-kulal|Nu'aym ibn Abd Kulal]] and [[person/al-numan-qayl-dhi-ruayn|al-Nu'man Qayl Dhu Ru'ayn, of Ma'afir and Hamdan]]. [[person/zura-dhu-yazan|Zur'a Dhu Yazan]] sent to him [[person/malik-ibn-murra-al-rahawi|Malik ibn Murra al-Rahawi]] with their Islam and their renunciation of polytheism and its people. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/235} The Prophet then wrote them a [[work/letter-to-the-kings-of-himyar|letter]] mentioning the meeting of their envoy with him at Medina on his return from the land of Rum, and what is due from them of prayer, zakat, a fifth of the spoils and the obligatory alms. It stated that whoever remained on his Judaism or Christianity would not be turned from it, but would owe a dinar of jizya for every adult. He then commended to Zur'a Dhu Yazan his envoys and their leader, [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]]; see [[event/mission-of-muadh-to-yemen|the sending of Mu'adh to Yemen]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/235} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/236} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/237}

## For young visitors
The kings of Yemen sent the Prophet a letter announcing their entry into Islam. He wrote back to them and sent them a teacher to teach them their religion.
