## Summary
The kings of Himyar wrote to the Prophet after his return from Tabuk declaring their Islam and their renunciation of polytheism; he answered them with a letter setting out what the believers have and what is upon them, and sent a party under Mu'adh ibn Jabal {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 449}.

## The letter
A letter came from the kings of Himyar — al-Harith ibn Abd Kulal, Nu'aym ibn Abd Kulal and al-Nu'man ibn Qayl of Dhu Ru'ayn, Hamdan and Ma'afir — after the Prophet's return from Tabuk {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 449}. Their messenger to him was Malik ibn Murra al-Rahawi, whom they sent with their Islam and their parting from polytheism and its people {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 449}. The Prophet wrote them a letter setting out what the believers have and what is upon them, granting their covenanters God's dhimma and His Messenger's dhimma when they paid the jizya due from them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 449}. And he sent to them men of his Companions under Mu'adh ibn Jabal {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 449}.

## For young visitors
The kings of Yemen sent the Prophet a letter declaring their entry into Islam, so he wrote them a reply and sent them a teacher to teach them their religion.
