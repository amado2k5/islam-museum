[[person/muhammad|The Prophet]] was born on the morning of Monday the ninth of Rabi' al-Awwal in the first year of the Elephant affair, in the quarter of Banu Hashim at Mecca; Mahmud Pasha al-Falaki, the Egyptian astronomer, established that this corresponds to the twentieth or twenty-second of April, 571 CE. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

On the seventh day his grandfather [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]] took him to the Ka'ba, prayed and gave thanks, named him Muhammad — a name not known among the Arabs — and circumcised him by Arab custom, then returned him to his mother [[person/amina-bint-wahb|Amina]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Tradition tells that his mother saw at his birth a light that went out from her by which Syria's palaces were lit for her; that it is said fourteen balconies of Khosrow's palace collapsed, the Magians' fire went out and the lake of Sawa dried up — reports among the portents, recounted as heritage rather than established fact. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

His mother nursed him for some days, as was the way of the Arab nobles, then [[person/thuwayba|Thuwayba]], Abu Lahab's freedwoman, nursed him with the milk of her son Masruh; then he went to [[person/halima-al-sadiyya|Halima al-Sa'diyya]] of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr, where he grew up in the desert, eloquent — and his foster-mother, the least comfortable of her women when she took him, returned to find blessing and bounty had overshadowed her. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
