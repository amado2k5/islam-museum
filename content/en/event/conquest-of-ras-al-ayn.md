## Overview
al-Baladhuri relates, on the authority of al-Hajjaj ibn Abi Mani', that Ras al-Ayn, which is the spring of the rose (Ayn al-Warda), resisted [[person/iyyad-ibn-ghanm|Iyad ibn Ghanm]]. It was taken by [[person/umayr-ibn-sad|Umayr ibn Sa'd]], Umar's governor over al-Jazira, after its people had fought the Muslims very fiercely. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.242}

## The terms
The Muslims entered the city by force and then made peace with them. The land was handed over to them, and the jizya was placed on their heads, four dinars on each head. Their women and children were not taken captive. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.242}

## Other accounts
On the authority of al-Waqidi, Umar wrote to Iyad to send Umayr to Ayn al-Warda, and that it was taken by treaty. al-Haytham ibn Adi claimed that [[person/abu-musa-al-ashari|Abu Musa al-Ash'ari]] was the one who raided it, but al-Baladhuri holds that the more firmly established account is that Umayr took it by force, and says that no one other than al-Haytham has said otherwise. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.243}

The conquest is part of the [[event/conquest-of-jazira|conquest of al-Jazira]]. The date is approximate and derived.
