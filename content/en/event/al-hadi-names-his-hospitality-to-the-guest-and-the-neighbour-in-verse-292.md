## Overview
The rajaz continues on p. 310 with the poet saying he kindles the fire of war if it is not kindled and gives the seeker of good what he asks, then describing his generosity: he prefers his guest to his own bedding and honours him, no neighbour of his spends the night naked and hungry, he turns from his neighbour's faults, and he glories in his forefathers' merit and praises God. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}

## Detail
On p. 310 the rajaz begun on p. 309 goes on, the poet saying "I kindle the fire of war if it is not kindled" and giving the seeker of good what his hand holds {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}. He then describes his honouring of the guest: he is not the foolish beggar nor slow with hospitality; if his guest arrives by night and lies on the bare ground he prefers him to his own bedding and gives him what he himself would rest his head on, and he honours his guest and humbles his own son {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}. He says no old neighbour of his has spent the night naked and thirsty while he slept full, and that his neighbour becomes strong through him, that he keeps off from his neighbour both near and far kin, and if his cousin troubles him he does not strive against him, and if his neighbour seeks the yard of the household he turns from his faults until he is praised {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}. He says that by his forefathers' merit he goes in his cloak, following their steps in lofty glory, and ends with praise of God, "the Most High, the One, the Everlasting, the Unique, the Generous, the Eternal" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}. The two footnotes say that "hilbaja" is the fool than whom there is none more foolish and that "musrahad" is the one who gives bounty and feeds {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}. This is the poet's picture of himself as the Zaydi book gives it, with hospitality and neighbourliness in it, and the book does not tie it to any one event. It follows [[event/al-hadi-sends-word-to-banu-kab-qushayr-and-banu-qurra-in-verse-292|his verse message to Banu Ka'b, Qushayr and Banu Qurra]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-calls-the-prophets-kin-to-ride-against-the-enemy-in-verse-292|his call to the Prophet's kin to ride]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
