## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah asked al-Hadi how to pay the army quartered at Najran. Al-Hadi ordered that half the grape tax be taken early from all the people. Banu al-Harith refused and said that al-Hadi had made peace with them and would take nothing from them.

## Detail
The book says the army quartered at Najran needed lodging, and Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah wrote to al-Hadi. Al-Hadi ordered him and his agent to borrow half the grape tax in advance and spend it on the troops, to be taken from all the people, the Harithi, Hamdani and Najrani growers. Everyone hastened to comply except Banu al-Harith, who said that al-Hadi had made peace with them at the day of Qarqar, promising to take from them no tax, levy or aid. Muhammad reported this, and al-Hadi ordered that the tax be demanded from them with the greatest severity: whoever paid willingly, and whoever did not, was made to pay under compulsion.
