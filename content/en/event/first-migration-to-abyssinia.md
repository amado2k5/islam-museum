When the trial grew fierce on the Muslims and the Prophet learned that [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]], king of Abyssinia, was a just king in whose presence none was wronged, he ordered them to emigrate there, fleeing with their religion from tribulation; on that occasion Sura al-Zumar came down pointing to emigration: "and God's earth is wide". {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

In Rajab of the fifth year of the prophethood the first band went out: twelve men and four women, their leader [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]], and with him the lady [[person/ruqayya-bint-muhammad|Ruqayya, the Prophet's daughter]] — of whom he said they were the first household to emigrate in God's cause after Abraham and Lot. They slipped through the darkness of night to the port of Shu'ayba, where two merchant ships carried them off; Quraysh caught wind of them and went out in pursuit but did not overtake them. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

They stayed in Abyssinia in the best of sanctuary, until word reached them in Shawwal that Quraysh had become Muslim — on the strength of [[event/sajdat-al-najm|the prostration of Sura al-Najm]] — and they returned; when they drew near Mecca they learned the truth of the matter, so some of them went back to Abyssinia, while the rest entered Mecca only in hiding or under the protection of a man of Quraysh. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
