## Summary
In 625 AH Ala al-Din Kayqubad, king of the land of Rum, took Arzinkan and its citadel Kamakh after the death of its lord Mengubirt Bahram Shah. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 478}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir explains the cause: Bahram Shah, lord of Arzinkan, had held it for a reign exceeding sixty years and died having never left obedience to Qilij Arslan and his sons; on his death his son Ala al-Din Dawud Shah succeeded. Kayqubad wrote asking him for troops to accompany him to besiege Erzurum — Dawud Shah himself to march with the army — and he did so. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 478} When he arrived, Kayqubad seized him and took the city of Arzinkan from him; he had an impregnable fortress named Kamakh containing a treasure-house of Dawud Shah's, and the king of Rum sent troops to besiege it, but they could not come near it for its height and defensibility, so he threatened Dawud Shah, who wrote to his deputy ordering surrender, and the citadel was handed over to Kayqubad. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 478}
