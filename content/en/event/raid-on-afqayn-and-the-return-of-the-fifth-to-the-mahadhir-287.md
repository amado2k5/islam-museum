## Overview
Some days later it reached [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] that the animals of [[group/al-mahadhir-saada|the Mahadhir]] had been put out at a place called [[place/afqayn-yemen|Afqayn]] with a party of them, so he sent Ali ibn Muhammad and Muhammad ibn al-Qasim, Alawis of the line of al-Abbas ibn Ali, with a party of horse and foot, ordering them to raid Afqayn and take the animals there. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.193} They went by a road on which no one knew of them until they reached Afqayn, raided the place and took the camels, horses, slaves, sheep and donkeys there, and returned to al-Hadi with their spoils. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.193} Some of the troops wished to plunder the spoils and began to inspect the animals, and when Ali and Muhammad saw it they took them out of their hands. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.193}

## Detail
[[person/muhammad-ibn-al-qasim-al-alawi-raider-at-afqayn-287|Muhammad ibn al-Qasim]] caught a man of al-Mahra riding a mount from the spoils, struck him with a whip and took the camel from him, and the man came to al-Hadi and appealed against Muhammad; al-Hadi ordered Muhammad brought so that the man might strike him, after Muhammad admitted striking him, but when the man saw it he pardoned him and gave up his claim. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.193} When the spoil reached al-Hadi he ordered it divided by the shares of God Most High, in five, and took the fifth and divided the other four fifths among the party that took it, two shares to a horseman and one to a footman. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.193} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.194} When the Mahadhir saw what had befallen them they sought safety from him and complained of the loss of their goods, and he returned to them the fifth he had received; he returned it, by the narrator's words and the gloss of the original, to win them and reconcile their hearts. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.194} Another gloss reads that he returned to the emigrants the fifth of what he had taken from them; the museum does not settle the reading emigrants. This is the narrator's report and the museum gives no judgement.
