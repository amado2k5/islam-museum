## Overview
"And he said, in what the jihad includes for the people of Iraq and other governors of tyranny: tell the governors of tyranny from me the words of one who speaks true" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.304}.

## Detail
He says that if he is spared them a little, and his hasty death lets him forget, they will see him "in regiments, rubbing your noses, when the polished blades are present", from Yemen, of which, the poet says, there is a saying that the Messenger brought; upon them is every full flowing mail, and they see unbelief as something that must pass away {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.304}. The two footnotes say that manuscript S reads "and he also said" in the heading and "because you" for "your noses" in the line {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.304}. On p. 305 he then describes noble marked horses amid the dust, cutting blades notched from striking heads, thirsty lances, and a fight in which the fire is kindled, the wife is left, the dead are left in every quarter, dust rises, the people are mixed, mail is plunged in gore and blood flows, "and a brother knows not his brother save that the watchword is their sign" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.305}. This is a poetic threat that the Zaydi book ascribes to al-Hadi against those it calls governors of tyranny, and the book names no governors here; it is not to be read as a report of events that happened, and the museum does not judge it. It follows [[event/al-hadi-answers-the-blame-over-giving-and-says-his-land-is-poor-292|his verses to his cousins]] and is completed by [[event/al-hadi-describes-the-battle-and-the-justice-after-it-in-verse-292|his verses describing the fighting and the justice that follows]]. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
