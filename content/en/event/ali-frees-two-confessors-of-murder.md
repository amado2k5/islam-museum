## Summary
The book reports that two men confessed before Ali to killing a single man, so Ali released both of them and paid the victim's blood money from the public treasury {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 313}. The book cites this case in the chapter on homicide and legal retribution {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 313}.

## The Incident
A man was brought to Ali after being found in a ruin holding a blood-stained knife, with a slain man writhing in his blood before him. He said, "I killed him," so Ali ordered him executed. Then a man came hurrying and said that the first man had not committed the murder, and that he himself was the killer; thus the second man confessed as well {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 313}.

## The Accounts of the Two Confessors
The first man explained that he was a butcher who had gone out to his shop before dawn, slaughtered a cow, felt the urge to urinate, and entered a nearby ruin; when he came out, he found the slain man and stood looking with the knife in his hand, whereupon people came and seized him, so he confessed to what he had not done because no one would accept anything else from him. The second man explained that he had killed the man out of greed for his money, then heard the night watch, went out, and encountered the butcher in the situation he described; when the butcher was brought to be executed, he confessed the truth {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 313}.

## The Ruling
Ali asked al-Hasan for the ruling, and al-Hasan said: "If he took a life, he has saved a life," citing the Quranic verse on saving a life {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 313}. So Ali released them both and paid the victim's blood money from the public treasury {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 313}. al-Sallabi suggests that Ali may have done so after the victim's next of kin waived their right to legal retribution, which is an inference by the author rather than a text in the book {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 313}.
