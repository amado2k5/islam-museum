## Overview
God routed them, in the narrator's words, and the striking among them did not stop until they went out of the village by the way they had come; [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] and his companions followed until all were put out in a wretched state. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.169} [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] had picked about forty of their horsemen, heavily armed, and made them swear not to fight but to stand together aside until, when they saw al-Hadi and sighted him, they would charge him as one man; the report reached al-Hadi, who took no notice and gave it no thought. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.169} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.170}

## The charge
When the last of Banu al-Harith went out in flight the first rider to come out after them was al-Hadi; the people in the village came out, and there were the horse that had been made ready for him, standing apart in a group as he had been told; he made for them himself and charged, naming his lineage to them, and not one rider stood before him; he overtook one, speared him and threw him and his horse into an arak thicket. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.170} The men scattered in every direction; Hamdan, seeing them in flight, turned back and followed them, the people caught up, the sword was laid on and a great number were killed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.170}

## The narrator's estimate
The narrator says that on that day al-Hadi killed many with his own hand, a number neither he nor anyone else kept count of, that he broke three spears in the men and then struck with his sword until the hilt was filled with blood and his fingertips stuck to the hilt with it, and he cites for this a poem by al-Hadi, taken up in [[event/al-hadis-poem-after-the-fight-at-najran-and-ahmad-al-hamdanis-reply-286]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.170} It is a picture drawn by a narrator who favours his imam, and the museum neither confirms nor denies it.
