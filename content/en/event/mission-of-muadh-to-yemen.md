## Overview
The Prophet sent [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]], as commander over his messengers, to [[place/yemen|Yemen]] after [[event/letter-of-himyar-kings|the letter to the kings of Himyar]], and gave him instructions when he sent him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/236} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/237}

## The messengers
[[work/letter-to-the-kings-of-himyar|The Prophet's letter to Himyar]] names his messengers: Mu'adh ibn Jabal, Abd Allah ibn Zayd, Malik ibn Ubada, Uqba ibn Nimr, and [[person/malik-ibn-murra-al-rahawi|Malik ibn Murra]] and their companions, and that their commander was Mu'adh ibn Jabal, and that they were not to return except content. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/236}

## The instruction
Ibn Ishaq relates from Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr that he was told that when the Prophet sent Mu'adh he instructed him and gave him charge, then said: "Make things easy and do not make them hard; give good news and do not drive people away." He said: you will come upon a people of the Book who will ask you what the key to Paradise is; say: the testimony that there is no god but God, alone without partner. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/237}

## A woman of Yemen asks
When Mu'adh came to Yemen he did what the Prophet had ordered him. A woman from Yemen came to him and asked: what is the right of a husband over his wife? He said: woe to you, a woman cannot fulfil her husband's right, so exert yourself in fulfilling his right as much as you can. She swore that if he was a companion of the Messenger of God he knew what the husband's right over the wife is; he answered her with a severe comparison of the greatness of that right. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/237} This answer is Mu'adh's words as Ibn Ishaq transmits them and is not quoted here in its wording; the editors explain "tanthana'u" as "to flow". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/237}

## Marks of the report
- The chain of the instruction is Ibn Ishaq from Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr, who "was told". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/237}
- Mu'adh's well-known report on judging by the Book, then the Sunna, then independent reasoning is not in this place in Ibn Hisham; it is in Ibn Sa'd and elsewhere. {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}
