In the pilgrimage season of the twelfth year of the prophethood (July 621 CE), twelve men of Yathrib came and met the Prophet at al-Aqaba in Mina, and swore him the first Pledge of al-Aqaba on the terms of the women's pledge: to associate nothing with God, not to steal, not to fornicate, not to kill their children, not to bring slander and not to disobey him in what is right. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

The Prophet sent with them [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]] as envoy and Qur'an-teacher to teach them the Qur'an and instruct them in the religion; he stayed with [[person/asad-ibn-zurara|As'ad ibn Zurara]], and at their hands the Aws chiefs [[person/usayd-ibn-hudayr|Usayd ibn Hudayr]] and [[person/sad-ibn-muadh|Sa'd ibn Mu'adh]] became Muslim — and with their Islam all Banu Abd al-Ashhal became Muslim until no house in Yathrib lacked Muslims. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

In the next year's pilgrimage season (June 622 CE), seventy-three men and two women of Yathrib's Muslims attended; they gathered secretly at the ravine by al-Aqaba in the depth of night, and the Prophet's uncle [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib]] attended with him to secure a binding pact for his nephew — he then being still on his people's religion. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

[[person/al-bara-ibn-marur|al-Bara ibn Ma'rur]] rose and took the Prophet's hand, covenanting to support him and protect him from what they protected their wives and children from; [[person/al-abbas-ibn-ubada|al-Abbas ibn Ubada]] rose stressing the gravity of the pledge — war on the red and the black. The Prophet chose twelve leaders from them — nine of the Khazraj and three of the Aws — and the pledge was sealed that opened the door to the hijra and the founding of the abode of Islam. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
