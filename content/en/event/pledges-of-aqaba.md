In the pilgrimage season of the twelfth year of the prophethood (July 621 CE), twelve men of Yathrib came and met the Prophet at al-Aqaba in Mina, and swore him the first Pledge of al-Aqaba, on the terms of the women's pledge: that they would not associate anything with God, not steal, not commit fornication, not kill their children, not bring forth slander, and not disobey him in what is right. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

The Prophet sent [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]] with them as an envoy and as a teacher of the Qur'an, to teach them the Qur'an and to give them understanding of the religion. He stayed with [[person/asad-ibn-zurara|As'ad ibn Zurara]], and at the hands of both of them the chiefs of the Aws, [[person/usayd-ibn-hudayr|Usayd ibn Hudayr]] and [[person/sad-ibn-muadh|Sa'd ibn Mu'adh]], embraced Islam. With their Islam all of Banu Abd al-Ashhal embraced it, until no house in Yathrib was without Muslims. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

In the pilgrimage season of the following year (June 622 CE), seventy-three men and two women of the Muslims of Yathrib attended. They gathered secretly in the ravine by al-Aqaba in the depth of the night. The Prophet's uncle, [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib]], attended with him, to make sure of the pledge on behalf of his nephew, who was then still on his people's religion. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

[[person/al-bara-ibn-marur|al-Bara ibn Ma'rur]] stood up, took the Prophet's hand, and covenanted to support him and protect him from what they protected their women and children from. [[person/al-abbas-ibn-ubada|Al-Abbas ibn Ubada]] stood up to stress the gravity of the pledge: it meant war against the red and the black. The Prophet chose twelve leaders from among them, nine of the Khazraj and three of the Aws. The pledge was confirmed, and it opened the door to the hijra and the founding of the abode of Islam. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}


## Before the first Pledge of al-Aqaba (The Sealed Nectar pp. 135-136)
Before the pledge, the Prophet met six men of the Khazraj at the Aqaba of Mina during the pilgrimage season. They embraced Islam and promised to call their people to it ([[event/six-men-of-yathrib|six men of Yathrib]]). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.135} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.136}


## The two pledges in detail (The Sealed Nectar pp. 143-152)
The first pledge took place in the season of the twelfth year of the mission, and the second in the season of the thirteenth year. ## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), unsigned article on al-'Aqaba, explains that the word means a mountain road or a steep ascent, and that there are many places of that name. The best known lies between Mina and Mecca, where Muhammad, according to tradition, had secret meetings with men from Medina at the pilgrimages of 621 and 622. In 621 twelve men were present and gave the pledge known as the pledge of the women; in 622 seventy-three men and two women promised to defend Muhammad by arms, the pledge of war. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 336}

Some Western writers held that there was only one meeting at al-'Aqaba, since al-Tabari mentions only one, and that the wording of the women's pledge is taken from a later Qur'anic passage. The article judges that such delicate negotiations would need more than one meeting. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 336}

## For Sprouts
Shortly before the Hijra, men from Yathrib — the town that became Medina — heard the Prophet and believed in him. At a rocky pass called al-Aqaba they met him twice and promised to believe him and protect him in their town. Those promises were the start of a new home for the Muslims.

## For Explorers
At the pilgrimage season of 621 CE the Prophet met twelve men of Yathrib at al-Aqaba near Mina, and they pledged what is called the Pledge of the Women: not to associate anything with God, not to steal, not to commit immorality, and not to disobey him in what is right. Mus'ab ibn Umayr went back with them to teach Yathrib. At the next season, in 622 CE, seventy-five men and two women came and pledged to hear and obey and to protect him as they protected their own families, and the Prophet asked for twelve chiefs among them. Through these two pledges his message gained a political refuge in Yathrib and the Hijra began.

## For Scholars
Al-Aqaba, a narrow pass near Mina, saw the decisive political turn of the mission: two pledges between the Prophet and people of the Aws and Khazraj of Yathrib. In the pilgrimage season of the twelfth year (July 621 CE) twelve men pledged the so-called Pledge of the Women — no association with God, no theft, no adultery, no killing of children, no slander, no disobedience in right — and Mus'ab ibn Umayr returned with them as teacher, through whom Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Usayd ibn Hudayr came to Islam. The following year (622 CE) seventy-five men and two women — among them Nusayba bint Ka'b — pledged hearing and obeying in ease and hardship, spending on the right, and protection: 'we protect you as we protect ourselves and our families'. The Prophet asked for twelve chiefs, who were chosen, and his uncle al-Abbas asked what they pledged for. With this pledge the road of the Hijra opened; the companions emigrated, then the Prophet. Historians read in it the moment the call moved from a persecuted group to a community with a home.
