## Overview
A poem in the tawil metre under the heading "and also of his", an answer to a letter that came to him with reproach: al-Hadi recalls that the writer said that but for the favours and benefactions of the Messenger's family he would have turned their favours into denial, replies that only a heedless man says so and that the free man is guarded and patient, and urges him to hold fast, renew his seeking and leave those who believe slanders. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.311}

## Detail
At the foot of p. 311, under the heading "and also of his", tawil lines begin: "a letter came to us from you with reproach in it, though in the past you were not unjust", and say the letter came with a greeting on swift camels and carried a speech {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.311}. On p. 312 al-Hadi gives the writer's words, "but for the favour and benefactions of the Messenger's family, whom Hashim knows, I would have turned their favours into denial and hatred and would not be at one with them in the right", and answers that none says such a thing but "a distraught sleeper" far from piety and near to desire, and that one who accepts every slander he hears and believes every newcomer's tale has no safe religion {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.312}. He says he recalls what the writer said of training and what he cried out about him, calls it "a dreamer's muddled dreams", and says the free man is guarded, dignified, firm and bears patiently every assault, and that life is never clear for one who is otherwise {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.312}. He tells the addressee that he is as people have known him and names "the noble of Yahya ibn al-Husayn ibn Qasim", which is al-Hadi's own name and descent as the book gives it, though the construction is unclear and is left as printed {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.312}. He urges him, if he takes the road again and holds firm, not to weaken if he says he was outstripped, but to renew "the highest and the spoils", for many an outstripped man ran and went ahead, and bids him leave a matter in which he clung to deceit and believed "the people of slander" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.312}. A footnote records that manuscript S reads "and I am not" for "and there is not" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.312}. The book does not name the writer or the place of the letter or date it, so the addressee stays unknown. The saying "but for the favour" is ascribed by the poet to the letter's writer and the museum does not verify it. It follows [[event/al-hadi-calls-the-prophets-kin-to-ride-against-the-enemy-in-verse-292|his call to the Prophet's kin to ride]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-counsels-the-writer-to-repent-and-hold-to-the-sons-of-al-qasim-292|his counsel to the writer to repent and hold to the sons of al-Qasim]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
