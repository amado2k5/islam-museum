## Summary
During [[person/muhammad|the Prophet's]] final illness, he asked to be brought writing materials for a document "after which you will not go astray." Those present disagreed, some saying "The Book of God is sufficient for us"; the document was not written, and the Prophet gave three injunctions {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.117}. Al-Sallabi reads the incident within the framework of the Sunna, considering the disagreement to be an exercise of judgment for which no one was rebuked, and that writing the document was not obligatory at that time {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.118-119}.

## The Report
The book cites the report in the two Sahihs from [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]]: the Prophet entered while men were in the house and said, "Come, let me write for you a document after which you will not go astray," whereupon they disputed, so he said, "Leave me, for the state I am in is better than what you call me to," and he enjoined three things upon them: expelling the polytheists from the Arabian Peninsula, rewarding delegations in the manner he used to reward them, and he remained silent about the third—or the narrator forgot it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.117}. The book mentions Ibn Abbas's statement: "The greatest calamity of all was what prevented the Messenger of God from writing the document" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.117}.

## The Word "Ahajara" (Is He Speaking Deliriously?)
The book notes that the expression "ahajara" appeared in the reports in the plural—"they said: what is the matter with him, is he speaking deliriously?"—not attributed to Umar, and cites Ibn Hajar that the question was a rebuke directed at those who said "Do not write"; al-Qurtubi, as cited by the book, says that the Companions agreed on the impossibility of delirium in the Prophet {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.120}.

## Explanation for Leaving the Document Unwritten
The book states, citing Ibn Taymiyya, that writing it was not something God had made obligatory upon the Prophet at that time, that he saw fit to leave it to avert dispute, that Umar considered leaving it unwritten to be preferable and was a mujtahid who is rewarded, and that the Prophet did not abandon conveying God's message because of someone's opposition, having lived for several days afterward {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.118-121}.

## Perspectives
**Sunni View (as presented by the book):** Leaving the document unwritten was for a benefit the Prophet saw; the Companions' exercise of judgment in the matter was justified, and neither side was rebuked {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.117-119}.

**Imami Shi'i View (as transmitted by the book):** Holds that the Prophet intended the document to designate Ali's succession and was prevented from doing so; the book refutes this on the grounds that it would imply the Prophet fell short in conveying his Lord's command, and that it makes no conceivable sense after God had perfected the religion {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.118-119}.
