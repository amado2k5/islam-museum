## Overview
At this point the book ends [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s answer to Banu Rabi'a of Hamdan, who had asked to raid the villages of Arhab: he said he feared they would "kill those who had not killed them and plunder those who had not plundered them". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.106} The account is Zaydi, in the voice of the imam's followers; its sayings are attributed to their narrators and the museum gives no verdict on them.

## The angry man's words
Muhammad ibn Sulayman reports that he heard an angry man of theirs tell his companions that if they wanted to take vengeance for their blood they should build mosques with al-Hadi in that place and sit with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.106} The narrator explains the remark by saying that these people could tell one another apart only through killing one another, without discrimination or recognition. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.106} Al-Hadi turned them from all of it and said that if anyone started anything without his order he would begin by fighting him, unless they would not hear and obey, in which case he would look for a clan to support him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.106}

## Hearing and obedience
Zeal and clan feeling took hold of them and they said they would rather all die before him, and asked him to command them as he wished, being ready to hear and obey; al-Hadi thanked them and asked them to appoint a group to stay on the road to [[place/athafit|Athafit]] and watch it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.106}

## The Sanaa merchant
They stayed on that road and seized a man of Sanaa with a load that included leopard skins and other goods, and brought him to al-Hadi, saying that he was [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]]'s messenger to Ibn al-Ajami and that the load was a gift for him; al-Hadi asked the man, who said he was a merchant. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.106} Al-Hadi spoke sharply to them, saying they were cutting the road on people, and ordered them to return his goods, which they did. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.106} The text identifies Ibn al-Ajami only here, so no record of a person is linked.
