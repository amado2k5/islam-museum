## Summary
The 1981 Durban debate between Ahmed Deedat and Josh McDowell on 'Was Christ Crucified?'. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}

## The axis of the debate
Deedat argued from the Qur'anic text of al-Nisa 157 and from the narrative discrepancies in the crucifixion accounts; McDowell argued from the Gospel testimony and historical sources. Its text translation remains one of the most circulated debate texts.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Deedat**: the crucifixion is not established — [[passage/quran-4-157|al-Nisa 157]] denies their killing and crucifying him and says 'it was made to appear so to them', so Islam's baseline is denial of the event; he read the Gospel passion accounts for their internal mismatches between cross, death, burial and appearances.
- **McDowell**: defended the crucifixion as historical fact — Gospel testimony, early Christian witnesses, and the 'most reasonable historical explanation' argument.
- **Deedat (rebuttal)**: the accounts conflict in details they should not conflict on were they preserved revelation; he set out the Qur'anic reading of Christ's fate.
- **McDowell (rebuttal)**: defended the accounts' essential harmony and the non-biblical source warrants.
- **The verdict**: the question remained a question: the two scriptures say two different things, and the historical argument alone does not settle it. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}
