## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] tells the story in the first lesson of [[work/madrassat-al-hayat|Madrasat al-Hayat]], because Ibn al-Jawzi built a reflection on misunderstanding upon it: [[person/abu-abd-al-rahman-al-sulami-al-muqri|Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami]] told Hibban ibn Atiyya that the hadith of the people of Badr was what emboldened Ali to shed blood, and Ibn al-Jawzi counted this a misunderstanding. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:06:30} Al-Heweny says that al-Bukhari gives the exchange between al-Sulami and Hibban in two places, at the end of the Book of Jihad and Campaigns and in the Book of Calling Apostates to Repent, and the hadith itself in several places. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:13:00}

In his telling the Prophet sent Ali, al-Zubayr and Abu Marthad al-Ghanawi, while in Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Rafi''s version in Muslim al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad takes Abu Marthad's place; the place was Rawdat Khakh, and whoever read it "Hajj" in some versions of al-Bukhari made a scribal slip, most scholars reading "Khakh". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:16:30} He relates that the woman took the letter out of her braids; that it was from [[person/hatib-ibn-abi-baltaa|Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a]] to some of the polytheists, telling them of some of the Prophet's plans; and that Hatib said he had done it neither as apostasy nor out of approval of unbelief, but because he was attached to Quraysh without kin among them and wanted a favour with them to protect his family. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:21:30} Umar asked leave to strike his neck, and the Prophet said Hatib had told the truth and cited the hadith of the people of Badr. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:22:30}

Al-Heweny disagrees with Ibn al-Jawzi on the hadith's meaning. Ibn al-Jawzi makes the forgiveness cover past deeds, not what is to come; al-Heweny sees that reading itself as a misunderstanding, because the imperative refers to the future and because Hatib's story took place after Badr. In his view the meaning is that one promised Paradise is granted repentance when he sins and so does not die in sin, though he is not infallible. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:32:00} He adds that the wording "I will forgive you", in the future, comes in a mursal report that is not sound. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep01 00:46:00}
