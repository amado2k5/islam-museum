## Summary
In Safar 11 AH the Prophet fitted out a great army under [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama ibn Zayd]] to tread with cavalry the borders of al-Balqa' and al-Darum in Palestine; people spoke against his youth, the Prophet confirmed his command, and the expedition was carried out in Abu Bakr's caliphate {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 463}.

## The account of the expedition
The pride of the Roman state had driven it to kill those of its subjects who entered Islam, as it did to Farwa ibn Amr al-Judhami, its governor over Ma'an {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 463}. So the Prophet set about mustering a great army in Safar 11 AH, placing Usama ibn Zayd ibn Haritha in command and ordering him to tread with cavalry the borders of al-Balqa' and al-Darum in the land of Palestine — to overawe the Romans and restore confidence to the Arabs on the frontier {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 463; sahih-al-bukhari}. People spoke against the army's commander for his youth, and the Prophet said: if you fault his command, you were faulting his father's command before — and by God he was worthy of command and among the dearest of people to me, and this one is among the dearest of people to me after him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 463; sahih-al-bukhari}. The men rallied round Usama until they went out and halted at al-Jurf a parasang from Medina, but the alarming news of the Prophet's illness forced them to wait — so this became the first expedition carried out in Abu Bakr's caliphate {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 463; ibn-hisham-sira}.

## For young visitors
The Prophet prepared an army and set over it a young man named Usama; people wondered at his youth, but the Prophet affirmed he was fit to command, and the army marched out in Abu Bakr's time.
