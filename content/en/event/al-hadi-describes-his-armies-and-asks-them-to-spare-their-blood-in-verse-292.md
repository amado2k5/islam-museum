## Overview
A poem in the wafir metre that begins at the foot of p. 306, in which the poet describes his march on his opponents in victorious armies with swords, lances and armoured horses, says he gave them excuse and did not hasten and asked them to spare their blood, and otherwise counts their blood and their land lawful to him by right. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}

## Detail
At the foot of p. 306, under the heading "and also of his", a wafir poem begins whose opening line likens the poet's opponents to animals in the fighting, an expression the museum does not repeat {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}. On p. 307 he says they marched on them "in victorious armies" with cutting swords, brown lances on which death rode, and horses armoured for the right's revenge, each carrying a brave man in full mail {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.307}. He says he gave them excuse and did not hasten, offered them every good, and said: spare your blood for me, and if you do not I do not care; he will not hurry until their unbeliever shows himself, and then, "by right", their blood becomes lawful to him, with the ruin of the lowlands and highlands and the cutting of crops {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.307}. He says he stood against them rightly and that his deeds confirm his words, and that they had long been in corruption, deceit and craft and thought it hidden from him, so they tasted the worst of the consequence {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.307}. The editor notes that the word "musrikhi" in the line on the brave man in mail may mean helper or aider, that manuscript S reads two words differently, and that this line and the six after it are missing from S {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.307}. The book names no one the poet addresses and no place of fighting, so the lines are not a report of an event; the poem goes on at p. 308 with [[event/al-hadi-offers-his-judgment-or-war-and-names-his-descent-in-verse-292|the offer of his judgment or war and the naming of his descent]]. It follows [[event/al-hadi-addresses-the-man-of-firm-mind-and-the-saying-of-what-will-be-292|his lines to the man of firm mind]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
