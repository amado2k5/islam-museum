## Overview
The book says that when al-Hadi's agents left [[place/najran|Najran]] with money "like a thousand dinars" and reached a place called "al-Rakb" on the road, a man called [[person/hunaysh-al-wadii|Hunaysh]] of [[group/wadia-tribe-yemen|Wadi'a]] followed them with a group of his clan, whom he had gathered, took the money and killed a man who was with the agents. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.89} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.90} Hunaysh had many palms, vines and houses at Najran. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.90} This is a Zaydi narrative of a dispute between the imam and an opponent, and the opponent's description is the narrator's.

## The second march to Najran
When the news came [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] told the people to prepare and on Monday the twelfth of Jumada I set out with many troops; he reached Najran on Wednesday and camped at a village called "Shawkan", where Hunaysh lived and had his property. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.90} He sent men of his clan to call him to God and ask him to return the money and come back to the truth; al-Hadi held off from his property and did nothing to it so as to establish the proof against him, and sent many messengers, but Hunaysh refused. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.90}

## The cutting of the palms
Al-Hadi then ordered his palms and vines cut: four hundred palms less a few, and two vines, were cut and a house pulled down, and, as the narrator has it, al-Hadi prayed earnestly that God would relieve the Muslims of him, because Hunaysh had gathered a band of bedouin thieves and meant to do mischief in al-Hadi's territory and cut the road. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.90} It is a punitive act as the narrator relates it, which the museum attributes to the imam and does not endorse.

## Hunaysh's death, as the narrator tells it
The narrator says he was told that Hunaysh had promised a group he would lead them out to waylay people, and on the night he meant to go "God struck him with a stab in his hand"; he cried out from it for two days and died, and a son of his died after him, and the narrator counts this "a fine sign" for al-Hadi. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.90} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.91} He relates from his father that al-Hadi told him he had seen the Prophet, on the night before the morning he ordered Hunaysh's property cut, cursing Hunaysh. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.91} These are the reports of a Zaydi narrator, which the museum attributes to him without affirming or denying the sign or the dream, and without repeating the curse in its own voice.

## Ibn al-Ajami
The narrator also names an "Ibn al-Ajami", one of the people most eager that al-Hadi leave Yemen, stirring men and giving money so that they would oppose and fight him; he kept praying against him, "and God seized him", the manuscript's margin reading "a marvel for al-Hadi". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.91} The text gives neither his name nor his fate beyond that phrase. Al-Hadi then stayed some days after cutting Hunaysh's property and told the people to prepare for Yemen proper, since its people had written to him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.91}
