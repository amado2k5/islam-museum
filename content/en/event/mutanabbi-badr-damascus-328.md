## Overview
At the beginning of 328/939 al-Mutanabbi became court poet to the amir Badr al-Kharshani — the Badr ibn Ammar of the Diwan — governor of Damascus for Ibn Ra'iq, who had just taken possession of Syria. Of Arab stock, Badr seemed to the poet the long-awaited Maecenas, and the panegyrics addressed to him carry sincere admiration; but intrigues of jealous rivals ended the friendship in about a year and a half, and al-Mutanabbi, no longer feeling safe, hid in the Syrian desert until Badr's departure for Iraq freed him {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 770}.
