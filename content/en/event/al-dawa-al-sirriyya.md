## Summary
The first stage of [[concept/marahil-al-dawa|the stages of the da'wa]] in The Sealed Nectar's division: three years of secret, individual preaching. Mecca was the centre of the religion of the Arabs and home to the custodians of the Kaaba, so it was wise that its people not be startled by what would provoke them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.74} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75}. In this stage the [[group/early-muslims|foremost early Muslims]] embraced Islam, [[concept/al-salat-qabl-al-isra|the first prayer]] was enjoined, and news of it reached Quraysh, but they did not pay it attention {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75-77}.

## The first circle
The Prophet presented Islam first to those closest to him, his household and his friends. He called on everyone he saw as good among those he knew and who knew him, and a group known as the foremost believers answered him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 1/50}. At their head were his wife [[person/khadija|Khadija bint Khuwaylid]], his freedman [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] ibn Sharahil al-Kalbi, his cousin [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]], who was a child in his care, and his close friend [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]]. These embraced Islam on the first day of the call {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.75} {cite:rahma-lil-alamin 1/50}.

Then Abu Bakr became active in preaching. He was a sociable, beloved and easy-going man of good character and kindness, and men of his people came to him for his knowledge, his trade and his good company. He began to call on those he trusted, and through his call [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]], [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]], [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]] and [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha ibn Ubayd Allah]] embraced Islam. These eight were the first circle and the vanguard of Islam {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}.

## Among the earliest Muslims
Then came [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal ibn Rabah]] the Abyssinian; then [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda Amir ibn al-Jarrah]], the trustee of this community; [[person/abu-salama|Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad]] and [[person/al-arqam-ibn-abi-al-arqam|al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam]], both of the Makhzum; [[person/uthman-ibn-mazun|Uthman ibn Maz'un]] and his two brothers, [[person/qudama-ibn-mazun|Qudama]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-mazun|Abdullah]]; [[person/ubayda-ibn-al-harith|Ubayda ibn al-Harith]] ibn al-Muttalib; [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Sa'id ibn Zayd]] al-'Adawi and his wife [[person/fatima-bint-al-khattab|Fatima bint al-Khattab]], sister of 'Umar; [[person/khabbab-ibn-al-aratt|Khabbab ibn al-Aratt]]; [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Mas'ud]] of the Hudhayl; and many others {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1/530}. They came from all the branches of Quraysh. Ibn Hisham counted them as more than forty men, and the author notes that there is some reservation about including some of them among the foremost believers {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/245-262}. Then people entered Islam in groups, men and women, until the mention of Islam spread through Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/262}.

## Secret gathering and the revelation
These embraced Islam in secret. The Prophet would meet them and guide them to the religion in hiding, because the preaching was individual and secret {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}. The revelation had followed on after the opening verses of al-Muddaththir. The verses revealed in this period were short, with fine endings and calm cadences; they encouraged the purification of souls and made their defilement repugnant, and they described Paradise and Hell as if seen with the eye {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76}.

## Prayer
Among the first things revealed was the command to pray two rak'as at dawn and two in the evening, along with Gabriel's teaching of ablution. The Muslims would go off into the ravines and pray in secret. Its details are in [[concept/al-salat-qabl-al-isra|Prayer before the Night Journey]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.76} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.77} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul-al-najdi p.88} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/247}.

## The news reaches Quraysh
It appears to the author that news of the call reached Quraysh during this stage, but they paid it no attention {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.77}. He quotes Muhammad al-Ghazali that Quraysh perhaps reckoned Muhammad to be one of the religious men who spoke about divinity, such as [[person/umayya-ibn-abi-al-salt|Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt]], [[person/quss-ibn-saida|Qass ibn Sa'ida]] and [[person/zayd-ibn-amr|'Amr ibn Nufayl]]. But they became wary of his news spreading and began to watch his fate {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.77} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p.76}. Three years passed, in which a community of believers formed on brotherhood and mutual help. Then revelation came, charging the Prophet to proclaim the message openly to his people {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.77}.

## For young visitors
In the first three years, the Prophet called people to Islam in secret. The first to believe in him were his wife Khadija, his friend Abu Bakr, his young cousin Ali and his freedman Zayd. Then others believed, one after another, and they prayed far from people's eyes.

## For Sprouts
At first the Prophet called people to God secretly for three years, and a small group believed at home: Khadija, Ali, Abu Bakr, Zayd and Bilal. They met quietly in the house of al-Arqam to pray and learn, until God told them to declare the call openly.

## For Explorers
The secret call was the first three years of the Prophet's mission (about 610–613 CE): he called people privately among those he trusted of his family and intimates, and Khadija believed first, then Ali, Abu Bakr, Zayd ibn Haritha, Bilal and others. The group met secretly in the house of al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam by al-Safa, learning the Qur'an and praying away from Quraysh's eyes, until God ordered the open call in the verse 'declare what you are commanded', and the Prophet climbed al-Safa and called out to Quraysh. Historians count these years the building of the first Muslim nucleus.

## For Scholars
The secret call (the concealed call) is the first years of the prophetic mission, from the start of revelation around 610 CE until the command to proclaim around 613 CE. The sira books record that the Prophet called in secret his inner circle: Khadija the first Muslim, then Ali ibn Abi Talib, Abu Bakr and Zayd ibn Haritha, and with them entered some of the weak — Bilal, Ammar, Sumayya. Their council was the house of al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam by al-Safa, where they met to learn the Qur'an and pray hidden from Quraysh, and the sources preserve meetings the early ones described, like those of Talha ibn Ubaydullah, al-Zubayr and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf. The command to proclaim came in 'declare what you are commanded and turn away from the polytheists', and the Prophet called Quraysh from al-Safa clan by clan, beginning a new era with the start of harm. Concealed stories from that era are told, like the Islam of Abu Dharr and al-Zubayr, and early Meccan suras are dated to it. Historians read these years as the founding phase that built the community's nucleus before confrontation.
