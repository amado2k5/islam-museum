The book divides the call into a Meccan period of thirteen years and a Medinan period of ten; the Meccan period has three stages, the first being three years of secret preaching. Mecca was the Arabs' religious centre and held the keepers of the Kaaba, so it was wise for the call to begin in secret, lest its people be taken by surprise by what would provoke them. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The first to answer the Prophet when Islam was offered were those closest to him: [[person/khadija|Khadija bint Khuwaylid]], his freedman [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]], his cousin [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] — then a boy living in his care — and his intimate friend [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]]; these became Muslim on the very first day of the call. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Then Abu Bakr threw himself into the call, and at his invitation [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]], [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]], [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]] and [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha ibn Ubaydullah]] became Muslim — these eight were the first rank and the vanguard of Islam. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Among the first Muslims were also [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal ibn Rabah]], [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]], [[person/abu-salama|Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad]], [[person/al-arqam-ibn-abi-al-arqam|al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam]], [[person/uthman-ibn-mazun|Uthman ibn Maz'un]] and his two brothers, [[person/ubayda-ibn-al-harith|Ubayda ibn al-Harith]], [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Sa'id ibn Zayd]] and his wife [[person/fatima-bint-al-khattab|Fatima bint al-Khattab]], [[person/khabbab-ibn-al-aratt|Khabbab ibn al-Aratt]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Mas'ud]] — Ibn Hisham counts them at more than forty from every clan of Quraysh. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}

In this stage the command of prayer came down: Muqatil ibn Sulayman says that at the start of Islam the obligation was two rak'as at morning and two at nightfall, and scholars differed whether anything was made obligatory before the five prayers; it is also reported that Jibril taught the Prophet ablution among the first things revealed to him. Whenever the hour of prayer came the Muslims would go into the ravines and conceal their prayers from their people; [[person/abu-talib|Abu Talib]] once saw the Prophet and Ali praying, and when he learned the truth of the matter he ordered them to stand firm. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

News of the call drifted to Quraysh, who paid it no attention at first — perhaps they counted him one of the godly sages like Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt and Qass ibn Sa'ida — but they began to watch his fate and his preaching, until the revelation came charging him to proclaim to his people openly, after three years. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
