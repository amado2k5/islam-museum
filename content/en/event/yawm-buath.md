## Overview
The Day of Bu'ath was the last and greatest of the wars of the [[group/al-aws|Aws]] and the [[group/al-khazraj|Khazraj]] before Islam, about five years before the Hijra according to the most correct view of al-Samhudi. Other views say four, and more. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 227} {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 230}

## The earlier wars
Al-Samhudi says that wars had taken place between them before it, among them the war of Mutayammir, the war of Ka'b ibn Amr, the Day of al-Sarara, and the Day of the Cock; then he mentions the Day of Fari', the two Fijars, the war of Hudayr ibn al-Aslat and the war of Hatib ibn Qays. He quotes from al-Khattabi, from Ibn Ishaq, that the war continued for a hundred and twenty years until Islam. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 227}

## The alliance
They fought at Bu'ath, a place at the upper part of Qawra in the land of [[place/medina|Yathrib]]. The Aws had been defeated in the earlier wars, so they allied with [[group/banu-qurayza|Qurayza]] and [[group/banu-nadir|al-Nadir]], and some of their clans settled at Khaybar for about a year. Before this the Aws had sought an alliance with Quraysh in Mecca, which Quraysh broke by a stratagem, which Ibn Shabba attributes to al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 227-229}

## The fighting
Al-Samhudi says that the Khazraj took pride in the victory and seized the pledge of the Jews, and the war was completed. The two leaders on each side were killed: [[person/hudayr-al-kataib|Hudayr al-Kata'ib]] of the Aws, the father of [[person/usayd-ibn-hudayr|Usayd ibn Hudayr]], and [[person/amr-ibn-al-numan-al-bayadi|Amr ibn al-Nu'man al-Bayadi]] of the Khazraj. The report says that [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abd Allah ibn Ubayy]] warned Amr that he would be carried away dead, and he answered with disobedience, and he was brought in carried on four men. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 227-230}

The victory was at first for the Khazraj, then Hudayr steadied the Aws and they returned and won. Sa'd ibn Mu'adh was saved by the side of Amr ibn al-Jamuh. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 227-230}

## In the Sahih
Al-Bukhari relates from [[person/aisha|Aisha]] that the Day of Bu'ath was a day God had brought forward for His Messenger in their entry into Islam, when their chiefs had split and their great men were killed. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3777} Al-Samhudi gives the hadith in its chapter. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p. 227-230}
