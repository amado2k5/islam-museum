## Summary
Headship of medicine in Egypt came to rest with him: he was made Chief Physician of Egypt and personal physician of sultan Baybars {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi s.a. 687 p. 312} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 897}, within Baybars's reign of 658–676/1260–1277.

## Teaching and logic
He lectured on Shafi'i law at the Masruriyya madrasa, where the grammarian Abu Hayyan al-Gharnati was his disciple in logic {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 897}; al-Suwaydan has him move to Qalawun's hospital as Baybars's private physician {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-mubdioune/ep17 00:10:30}.
