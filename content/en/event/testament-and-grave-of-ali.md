## Summary
This record gathers what Sunni books say of the testament of [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] to his sons [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|al-Hasan]] and [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn]] after [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-muljam|Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam]] struck him, of his instruction about his killer, of his washing, al-Hasan's prayer over him and his burial, and of the differing statements about where his grave lies. It does not retell the assassination, which is in [[event/death-of-ali-ibn-abi-talib|the killing of Ali ibn Abi Talib]]. He was struck at dawn on Friday, seventeen nights into Ramadan 40 AH on the well-known account, lived through Friday and the night of Saturday, and died on the night of Sunday. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 322}

## His instruction about his killer
After the blow Ibn Muljam was stood before Ali, bound; Ali asked him, and he answered that he had sharpened his sword for forty mornings and asked God to kill with it the worst of His creatures. Ali told him he saw him killed by it, then said, "If I die, kill him; if I live, I know what I shall do with him." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 318} In al-Muhibb al-Tabari's report he said: hold him, and if I die kill him and do not mutilate him; if I do not die, the matter of pardon or retaliation is mine. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 677} In another version: hold the man; if I die kill him, and if I live wounds are retaliated. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 675} Jundub ibn Abd Allah asked whether they should pledge allegiance to al-Hasan if he died; Ali said, "I neither command you nor forbid you; you see better." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 318}

It is also reported that he gave al-Hasan and al-Husayn a long testament ending with a charge not to plunge into Muslim blood saying "the Commander of the Faithful has been killed," that none should be killed for him but his killer, and that if he died of that blow he should be struck once and not mutilated. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 679} From Hushaym, freedman of al-Fadl: Ali told al-Hasan and al-Husayn to keep the man, and if he died to kill him without mutilation; when he died al-Husayn and Muhammad cut the man and burned him, and al-Hasan forbade it. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 679}

In the report of Imam Ahmad that Ibn Kathir gives, Ali said, "Do to him what the Messenger of God wished to do to a man who sought to kill him: kill him, then burn him." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 319} Ibn Kathir relates that when Ali had died al-Hasan called for Ibn Muljam, who offered to be released to go to Mu'awiya, promising to return if he did not kill him; al-Hasan refused, brought him forward and killed him, and people wrapped him in mats and burned him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321} It is also said that Abd Allah ibn Ja'far first cut off his hands and feet. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321}

## The text and content of the testament
Ibn Kathir says he charged his two sons to fear God, to pray, to pay zakat, to restrain anger, to keep family ties, to be forbearing with the ignorant, to study the religion, to be sure before acting, to attend to the Qur'an, to be good neighbours, to command good and forbid evil and to avoid shameful acts; he commended to them their brother [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-hanafiyya|Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya]], that they honour him and decide nothing without him, and he wrote all this in his testament. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 318}

Ibn Kathir then gives its form: it begins with the two declarations of faith, then charges al-Hasan, all his children and whoever his letter reaches to fear God, hold to His rope and not divide, and recalls what he heard from the Prophet on the excellence of putting things right between people over most prayer and fasting. He goes on to charge them about kinship, orphans, neighbours, the Qur'an, prayer, the House of God, Ramadan, striving with wealth and person, zakat, the people under protection, the Prophet's Companions, the poor and needy and those whom their right hands own; to command good and forbid evil, to keep in touch and give to one another and avoid turning away, cutting off and splitting; and he ends by entrusting them to God and giving his greeting. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 318} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 319} After that he said nothing but that there is no god but God until he died. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 319} It is also said that his last words were a verse about being repaid for an atom's weight of good or evil; see [[passage/quran-99-7|Qur'an 99:7]] and [[passage/quran-99-8|99:8]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 318}

In a report of al-Hasan al-Basri, from al-Hasan ibn Ali, Ali had dreamed before the blow of the Prophet and complained to him of what he had met from his community. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 675}

## What he left
Al-Muhibb al-Tabari reports from al-Hasan's address when Ali was killed that his father left no gold or silver except seven hundred dirhams of his stipend that he was setting aside for a servant for his family. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 596} From Harun ibn Sa'id: he had some musk and ordered that he be embalmed with it, saying it was left over from the embalming material of the Messenger of God. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 678}

## Washing and prayer
His two sons al-Hasan and al-Husayn and Abd Allah ibn Ja'far washed him, and al-Hasan prayed over him with nine takbirs, according to Ibn Kathir. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 319} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 320} Ibn al-Jawzi says he was struck on Friday with thirteen nights remaining of Ramadan, or on the night of the twenty-first, that his sons and Abd Allah ibn Ja'far washed him, al-Hasan prayed over him and he was buried before dawn. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 133} In al-Muhibb al-Tabari, al-Hasan said four takbirs, and al-Khujandi says nine. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 678} He also relates that when news of his death reached Aisha she said, "Let the Arabs do what they will; there is no one left to forbid them." {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 678}

## The place of the grave
The statements about where he is buried are many; each is given here with the one who transmits it.

- In the Dar al-Imara in Kufa, buried by night for fear the Kharijites would dig him up; this is the well-known account for Ibn Kathir. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 320} See [[building/qasr-al-imara-kufa|the governor's palace at Kufa]].
- On the qibla side of the congregational mosque at Kufa, which Ibn Kathir attributes to al-Waqidi, saying the well-known account is the Dar al-Imara. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321} See [[building/great-mosque-of-kufa|the Great Mosque of Kufa]] and, for further reports, [[event/death-of-ali-ibn-abi-talib|the killing of Ali]].
- In the governor's palace, or in the Rahba of Kufa, or at the Najaf of al-Hira, says al-Muhibb al-Tabari; al-Khujandi says the soundest view among them is that he lies behind the mosque, other than the one people visit today. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 677}
- From Ja'far ibn Muhammad and Abu Ja'far al-Baqir: he was buried at Kufa by night and the place of his grave was hidden, near the palace. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321} From Abu Ja'far: the place of his grave is unknown. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 677}
- From al-Hasan ibn Ali, in Ibn Asakir as cited by Ibn Kathir: he buried him in a room of the houses of the family of Ja'da. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321}
- Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi relates from Abu Nu'aym al-Fadl ibn Dukayn that al-Hasan and al-Husayn moved him to Medina and buried him at al-Baqi' by the grave of Fatima. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321}
- Al-Khatib also relates that when they put him on a camel it strayed from them and was taken by Tayy, who thought it carried goods; seeing a dead man, unknown to them, they buried the chest and its contents, so no one knows where his grave is. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321} Ibn Kathir rejects this account as something people claimed without knowledge. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 320}
- Ibn Asakir, through Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr: when Khalid ibn Abd Allah dug the foundations of the house of his son Yazid they found an old man buried, white of head and beard as if buried the day before; he had him wrapped in cotton cloth and perfumed and left him in place, opposite the Warraqin gate on the qibla side of the mosque. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321}
- From Ibn al-Kalbi: those who attended his burial by night were al-Hasan, al-Husayn, Ibn al-Hanafiyya, Abd Allah ibn Ja'far and others; they buried him outside Kufa and hid his grave. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321}

As for [[place/najaf|Najaf]]: Ibn Kathir says that what many people believe, that his grave is at the shrine in Najaf, has no evidence or basis that he knows of, and he reports from al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, through Abu Nu'aym and a chain to Matar, that the grave honoured at Najaf is that of al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321} This is the statement of Ibn Kathir and those he cites and does not represent a position of the museum. For the shrine known today at Najaf see [[building/imam-ali-shrine|the shrine of Imam Ali]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321}

## Where the reports differ
- Day of the blow: dawn on Friday seventeen nights into Ramadan; also said thirteen, eleven or eighteen, or the night of the twenty-first, or in Rabi' al-Awwal. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 678} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 133} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321}
- His death: the night of Sunday, after Friday and Saturday. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 322}
- Takbirs at the prayer: four or nine. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 678}
- His age: sixty-three on the well-known account; also sixty-five or sixty-eight; fifty-eight from Ja'far al-Sadiq; sixty-five from Abu Ja'far. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 321} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 133}
- The grave: the statements above; the well-known one for Ibn Kathir is the Dar al-Imara. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 320}
