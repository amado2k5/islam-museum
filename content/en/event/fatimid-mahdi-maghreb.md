## Overview
In the days of al-Muqtadir the cause of [[person/ubayd-allah-al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] grew strong in the Maghrib, the caliphate was proclaimed for him, and the Maghrib left the command of the Abbasids. [[person/ziyadat-allah-iii|Ziyadat Allah ibn al-Aghlab]] fled to Egypt and then to Iraq. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.587-590}

## The account
The source of the caliphs says that al-Mahdi spread justice among the people, built al-Mahdiyya, and his kingdom grew great. He marched toward Egypt with forty thousand Berbers, but the Nile came between him and it, so he returned to Alexandria. His son al-Qa'im took most of Upper Egypt. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.587-590}

## Al-Mas'udi's account
According to al-Mas'udi, Abu Abd Allah al-Muhtasib, the propagandist among the Kutama and the Berbers, drove Ziyadat Allah out of Ifriqiya in 296 AH, and called for Ubayd Allah, the lord of the Maghrib. The Aghlabids had been the amirs of Ifriqiya since al-Rashid appointed Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab in 184 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.647-648}
