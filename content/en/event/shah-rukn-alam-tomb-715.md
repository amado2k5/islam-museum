## Overview
About 715/1315 the great tomb at Multan was raised, said to have been built by Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq for himself and later devoted to Shah Rukn-i Alam by Firuz Shah {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 604}.

## The form
The tomb differs from the city's other mausolea in that its lower storey is octagonal rather than square, with tapered round buttresses at the angles crowned by small domes around the main one; inside, sixteen concentric arches in recessed planes form squinches and clerestory windows and carry a thirty-two-sided cornice {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 604}.

## A Multan school
It is the mature form of a Multan school of brick architecture in three zones — a battered-walled base, a tall octagonal drum with an opening on each face, and a hemispherical dome — sheathed outside with turquoise and white ceramic panels alone {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 604}.
