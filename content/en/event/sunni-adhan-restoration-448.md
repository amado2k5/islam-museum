## Summary
In the year 448 AH, after Sultan Tughrilbak had settled in Baghdad, the call to prayer was given in the shrines and mosques of Karkh with the words «al-ṣalāt khayr min al-nawm» ("prayer is better than sleep"); the phrase «ḥayy ʿalā khayr al-ʿamal» ("hasten to the best of deeds") was removed; and the inscription «wa ʿalā khayr al-bashar» was chiselled off the doors of houses and alleys. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.514}

## The scene
Sunni reciters entered Karkh through the Basra Gate and recited poems praising the Companions. The chief of chiefs, Ibn al-Muslima, instructed Ibn al-Nasawi to kill Abu ʿAbd Allah ibn al-Jallab, shaykh of the cloth merchants at Bab al-Taq, for openly displaying what the source calls extremist rafḍ; he was killed, and his body was publicly displayed on the door of his shop. The Shiʿi theologian Abu Jaʿfar al-Tusi fled, and his house was looted. In Safar 449 his house was raided; his notebooks and the chair from which he lectured were seized and burned, together with three white majānīq that pilgrims carried on their visits to Kufa. Ibn al-Muslima then ordered black banners raised in Karkh, which alarmed its people; he strove to harm them, and it was only Amid al-Mulk al-Kunduri who warded them off. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.513-514,521}
