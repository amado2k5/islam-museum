## Overview
When evening came they marched on him to the qibla side of the village, and he went out to meet them with those who were with him, Ibn Bustam also with him, and they fought an hour {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.281}. Then Abu Ja'far charged them as one of five horsemen of his companions and they routed Banu al-Harith, who were forty horse {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.281}.

## Detail
He kept to the place where they had been until dusk and ordered the lane of the village sealed {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.281}. The book then says that Ibn Bustam, seeing Banu al-Harith and Yam united against Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah, feared for himself and for the kinsmen with him, because of the war Banu al-Harith had set upon him, and was wary that they would enter the village and kill him with Abu Ja'far; the sequel is in [[event/ibn-bustam-advises-the-madanis-and-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-leaves-for-minas-292]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.281}. The page also has Ibn Bustam fighting at Abu Ja'far's side in this fight, which the book does not comment on. The numbers, five horsemen and forty, are the narrator's, and the museum offers no other estimate.
