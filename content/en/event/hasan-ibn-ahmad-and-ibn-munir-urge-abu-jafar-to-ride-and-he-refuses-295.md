## Overview
When Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Hamdani and Abdallah ibn Munir al-Mazuwi saw the enemy approaching, they told Abu Ja'far that the enemy had surrounded him and that he had no strength against them, and that he should mount his horse and fight from it. Abu Ja'far refused to ride, saying it was not the time, and that they should come out and fight the enemy on foot with him until God judged between them.

## Detail
Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Hamdani and Abdallah ibn Munir al-Mazuwi said: when we saw the enemy approaching we told Abu Ja'far that the enemy had surrounded him and that he had no strength against them, and that he should mount his horse and fight from it. He replied that this was no time to ride; they should come out with him and fight the enemy to defend themselves and their women until God judged between them. They answered that fear had reached the people, so that it showed on their faces and changed their colours, and that they themselves felt some of that fear, but that when the enemy had arrived they saw in Abu Ja'far a plain gladness and cheer, and that he smiled at them and comforted them, as though they were the victors over their enemy. They themselves disliked to dismount from their animals and preferred to fight from them. Abu Ja'far had placed his women in the house where al-Hadi ila al-Haqq stayed when Ibn Humayd asked for safe-conduct. The narrator, Hasan, then sent for his own women to join those of Abu Ja'far, and the enemy pressed them. He and Ibn Munir fought the enemy and drove them back.
