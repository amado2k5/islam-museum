## Overview
In 583 AH, the year Jerusalem was conquered, a great number of pilgrims came from Syria. On the eve of Arafat a clash took place between the pilgrims of Syria and the pilgrims of Iraq near [[place/mecca|Mecca]], which ended in the death of [[person/ibn-al-muqaddam-shams-al-din|Shams al-Din Ibn al-Muqaddam]], amir of the Syrian pilgrims. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.162-163}

## The dispute
Ibn al-Muqaddam, the senior of the Salahi emirs, ordered his drums beaten, which signalled the order to depart. [[person/mujir-al-din-tashtakin|Mujir al-Din Tashtakin]], amir of the Iraqi pilgrims, sent to him forbidding him to depart before him. Ibn al-Muqaddam replied that Tashtakin had no say over him: he was the amir of the Syrian pilgrims and Tashtakin was the amir of the Iraqi pilgrims. He set out and did not stop. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.162-163}

## The clash
Tashtakin rode out with his companions, and a large group of the Iraqi pilgrims followed him. They attacked the Syrian pilgrims, killed a number of them, plundered their property and took women captive, then returned them. Ibn al-Muqaddam was wounded while he restrained his companions from fighting, out of respect for the sanctity of the place and the day. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.162-163}

Tashtakin took him to his tent to treat him, and he died that day at Mina and was buried in the cemetery of al-Mu'alla. Ibn al-Athir says that he was granted martyrdom, after striving and witnessing the conquest of Jerusalem. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.162-163}
