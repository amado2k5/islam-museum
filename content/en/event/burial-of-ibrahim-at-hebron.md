## Summary
In Stories of the Prophets and al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya, Ibn Kathir says that [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]] fell ill and died and was buried in the cave at Habrun in the land of Canaan beside his wife [[person/sara|Sara]], and that his sons [[person/ismail|Ismail]] and [[person/ishaq|Ishaq]] saw to his burial. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 178} The cave lies at [[place/al-khalil|al-Khalil]] (Hebron), where the [[building/al-haram-al-ibrahimi|Ibrahimi Sanctuary]] stands today; a footnote in the Stories of the Prophets calls Habrun the village in which the grave of al-Khalil lies. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 236}

## From Sara's cave to Ibrahim's burial place
Sara died before her husband in the village of Habrun in the land of Canaan, aged one hundred and twenty-seven according to the People of the Book as Ibn Kathir reports; Ibrahim grieved for her and mourned her. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 236} Ibrahim bought a cave from a man of the Banu Heth called Ephron ibn Sakhr for four hundred mithqals and buried Sara in it. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237} This account comes from the People of the Book, and Ibn Kathir relays it here without comment on its soundness. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237}

## His illness and death
Ibn Kathir says Ibn Asakir related, from several early authorities, many reports drawn from the People of the Book about the angel of death coming to Ibrahim, 'God knows best how sound they are'. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237} He then says it was said Ibrahim died suddenly, as did Dawud and Sulayman, whereas what the People of the Book and others report is otherwise: that he fell ill and died. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237} He gives his age as one hundred and seventy-five years; the printed line adds a further figure, 'and ninety years', which I could not reconcile with it, and there are reports indicating he lived two hundred, as Ibn al-Kalbi said. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237}

Ibn Kathir relays from Ibn Hibban's Sahih a report from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said Ibrahim circumcised himself with an adze at the age of one hundred and twenty and lived eighty years afterwards, which makes two hundred; he notes that Ibn Asakir transmitted it by another path as the words of Abu Hurayra himself. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 178}

## The burial and the place
Ibn Kathir says he was buried in the cave at Habrun beside his wife Sara, in the field of Ephron the Hittite, and that Ismail and Ishaq saw to his burial. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 178} Neither book gives details of his washing, shrouding or the prayer over him, so none are attributed to him here.

## Other places attributed to Ibrahim
An editor's footnote in al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya quotes the Mu'jam al-Buldan that Iraq has places called Kutha, among them Kutha Rabba, where there is a shrine of Ibrahim al-Khalil and, they say, his birthplace. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 178} This is another site tied to Ibrahim, not to his grave in the Levant, and Ibn Kathir's text itself does not set it against his statement that he was buried at Habrun. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 178}
