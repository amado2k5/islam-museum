## Summary
The Prophet and [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] hid in the [[place/ghar-thawr|cave of Thawr]] for three nights — Friday, Saturday and Sunday — while Quraysh searched the countryside and set a prize of a hundred camels on each of them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 165-166}.

## In the cave
Abu Bakr insisted on entering the cave before the Prophet in case anything in it might harm him; he swept it and blocked its holes with his waist-wrap, then was stung in the foot and did not move, fearing to wake the Prophet {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 164}.

[[person/abdullah-ibn-abi-bakr|Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr]] spent the nights with them and left before dawn to spend the day with Quraysh as though he had slept among them; nothing he heard of plots against the two but he memorised it and brought it to them by night. [[person/amir-ibn-fuhayra|Amir ibn Fuhayra]] pastured a gifted flock of sheep near them — the two would spend the night on their milk — and drove his sheep over Abdullah's tracks to erase them {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 165}.

## The hunt
Quraysh beat Ali, dragged him to the Ka'ba and held him for a time, and struck [[person/asma-bint-abi-bakr|Asma]] on the cheek until her earring flew off when she denied knowing her father's whereabouts; they posted a hundred camels for whoever returned either of the two men, dead or alive {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 165}. The pursuers reached the very mouth of the cave, and Abu Bakr said: Prophet of God, if one of them but looked down he would see us; he answered: "what do you think, Abu Bakr, of two the third of whom is God?" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 166} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. Then the heat of the hunt died down after three days and they prepared to leave {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 166}.
