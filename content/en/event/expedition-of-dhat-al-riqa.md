In the seventh year of the Hijra the Prophet went out with four hundred — or seven hundred — of his Companions to Najd to discipline parties of Banu Tha'laba and Muharib of Ghatafan; the expedition was named Dhat al-Riqa' ("she of the rags") for the rags the Companions bound round their feet, worn through by the rough stones until their nails dropped, as [[person/abu-musa-al-ashari|Abu Musa al-Ash'ari]] relates. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

When the two armies met, each side feared the other; no fighting took place, and instead the Prophet led the Muslims in the prayer of fear for the first time, in its Qur'anic form, in Najd. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

On the way back the army halted in a ravine and the Prophet posted [[person/abbad-ibn-bishr|Abbad ibn Bishr]] and [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar ibn Yasir]] on night watch; Abbad rose to pray and a polytheist man shot him with an arrow — he pulled it out and went on praying — then with a second and a third as he drew out the arrows and finished Sura al-Kahf; when he concluded he woke Ammar. Ammar said: glory to God — why did you not wake me the first time he shot? Abbad said: "I was in a sura I was reciting and did not want to break it off before finishing; and by God, were it not for losing a post the Messenger of God charged me to keep, cutting off my life would have been dearer to me than cutting it short." {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
