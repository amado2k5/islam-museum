## Summary
On the Day of Sacrifice of the year 9 AH pilgrimage, Ali ibn Abi Talib proclaimed at the Jamra the opening of Sura Bara'a: the repudiation of pacts, a four-month respite, and the end of polytheist pilgrimage and naked circumambulation {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 440}.

## The account
The Prophet sent Abu Bakr as leader of the year 9 AH pilgrimage to establish the rites for the Muslims; then the opening of Sura Bara'a came down repudiating the pacts, so he sent Ali to proclaim it on his behalf — in keeping with the Arabs' custom that pacts of blood and property be announced by the principal or his kin {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 440; zad-al-maad}. Ali met him at al-Arj or Dajnan; Abu Bakr asked: "commander or commanded?" He said: commanded — and they went on, and Abu Bakr held the pilgrimage for the people {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 440}. Ali stood at the Jamra on the Day of Sacrifice and proclaimed what the Prophet had commanded: every pact-holder was given his pact's repudiation, those with no pact were granted four months, and the pact of whoever had not shorted the Muslims remained to its term {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 440; sahih-al-bukhari}. Abu Bakr sent men calling out: after this year no polytheist shall make pilgrimage and none shall circumambulate the House naked {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 440; sahih-al-bukhari}. Al-Mubarakpuri describes this proclamation as effectively announcing the end of idolatry in the Arabian peninsula {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 440}.

## For young visitors
In the pilgrimage season of year nine after the hijra, Ali ibn Abi Talib announced at the Prophet's command that the time of idol-worship in the Arabian peninsula had ended, and that whoever held a pact would have it honoured to its term.
