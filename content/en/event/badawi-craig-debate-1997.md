## Summary
The 1997 debate between Jamal Badawi and William Lane Craig on 'The Concept of God in Islam and Christianity'. {cite:reasonablefaith-concept-of-god-debate}

## The line of the debate
Tawhid against Trinity: Badawi reduces the concept of persons to a violation of rational and scriptural unicity, Craig argues the Trinity's philosophical coherence. Recording and transcript are published and circulating.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Badawi**: Sunni Tawhid is simple, not composite — God is one in essence and attributes, and God's unity admits no division into persons; he pressed the rational difficulty (how is one three?) and the doctrine's later accretion onto Hebrew monotheism.
- **Craig**: defended the Trinity's philosophical coherence — a person is not a partial god but a full subsistence of the divine essence — and ran his standard cosmological case for a personal creator.
- **Badawi (rebuttal)**: sharpened the dilemma: if the persons are fully divine they are three gods; if partial, not God; and argued Mosaic and Qur'anic revelation alike stand on pure unicity.
- **Craig (rebuttal)**: held that the Trinity is revealed, not a product of reason alone, and that the philosophical case for a personal creator accommodates tri-unity.
- **The verdict**: the model academic debate — Craig's mathematically elegant theism against Badawi's Qur'anic simplicity. {cite:reasonablefaith-concept-of-god-debate}
