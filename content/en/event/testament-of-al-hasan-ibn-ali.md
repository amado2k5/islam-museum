## Summary
[[person/hasan-ibn-ali|Al-Hasan ibn Ali]] died in [[place/medina|Medina]] after a long illness, and the historians record a testament he gave his brother [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn]] as he lay dying: to ask that he be buried beside his grandfather the Prophet, in the room of [[person/aisha|Aisha]], provided no blood was shed over it, and otherwise to bury him in the Muslims' cemetery at al-Baqi. What he feared came close to happening, since [[person/marwan-ibn-al-hakam|Marwan ibn al-Hakam]] refused to allow a burial in the room and weapons were nearly drawn, until Abu Hurayra and others calmed the people, and al-Hasan was buried in [[place/al-baqi|al-Baqi]] beside his mother. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 7 p. 40} This record stands beside the [[event/death-of-hasan-ibn-ali|record of al-Hasan's death]], which draws on al-Suyuti's History of the Caliphs; here we dwell on the testament and the funeral as Ibn Kathir and al-Dhahabi relate them.

## The illness and the dying hours
Ibn Kathir reports from Ibn Sa'd that his wife Ja'da bint al-Ash'ath poisoned him, and that he suffered from it for about forty days, a basin being placed under him and another taken away. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 408} He notes that some reported that Yazid ibn Mu'awiya had incited her, and then says that in his own view this is not sound. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 408} Al-Dhahabi comments on the report of incitement that it is not authentic and asks who could have known of it. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 7 p. 40} When al-Husayn asked who had given him the poison, saying he would kill the man before burying him, al-Hasan refused to name him, saying that this world is only passing nights and that he would meet the man before God. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 408} He asked to be carried out into the courtyard to look at the heavens, and said that he reckoned his own soul to God's account. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 408}

## His testament to al-Husayn
Al-Dhahabi, quoting Ibn Abd al-Barr, relates that al-Hasan told al-Husayn as he was dying that their father had reached for the caliphate and God had turned it from him, that he did not think God would join prophethood and caliphate in them, and he warned him not to let the fools of Kufa lure him out. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1436} He added that he had asked Aisha to be buried in her room and she had said yes, but he did not know whether she said so out of modesty; he told al-Husayn to ask her after his death, expecting that the people would prevent it, and in that case to bury him at al-Baqi. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1436} In al-Dhahabi's own wording of the same report he says: if they prevent you, do not argue with them. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 7 p. 40} In the version of Abu Hazim he said: bury me beside my father, unless you fear bloodshed, in which case bury me in the Muslims' cemeteries. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435} Jabir ibn Abd Allah reported that al-Hasan had charged his brother to bury him with the Messenger of God, but if he feared fighting or evil over it, to bury him at al-Baqi. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 409} Al-Waqidi reports from Ibn Umar that he attended al-Hasan's death and told al-Husayn to fear God, not to stir up strife or shed blood, and to bury his brother beside his mother, since his brother had charged him with that. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435}

## The funeral and burial
When he died Medina rang with weeping. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435} In the report of al-Waqidi from al-Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya, Marwan sent word to Mu'awiya, al-Husayn went to the Prophet's grave and ordered it dug, the governor Sa'id ibn al-As stood aside, Marwan called out to the Banu Umayya, who put on their arms, and al-Husayn called on the Hilf al-Fudul, so that Hashim, Taym, Zuhra and Asad gathered under arms. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435} Abd Allah ibn Ja'far kept reminding him of his brother's charge. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435} In the report of Abu Awana from Abu Hazim, al-Husayn took up arms and gathered his clients, and Abu Hurayra adjured him by God and his brother's testament, saying the people would not leave him alone until blood was spilled. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435} Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Abu Hurayra, Jabir and Ibn Umar advised al-Husayn not to fight, and he buried his brother near his mother's grave at al-Baqi. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 409} Al-Husayn put Sa'id ibn al-As forward to pray over him, saying that it was the sunna. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 7 p. 40} Ibn Kathir relates that Abu Hurayra stood at the Prophet's mosque calling out that the beloved of the Messenger of God had died that day and the people should weep, that al-Baqi could not hold the crowd, and that men and women wept for seven days. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 409} According to Juwayriya, Marwan carried the bier, and when al-Husayn reproached him he said that he used to do so for one whose forbearance matched the mountains. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435}

## Where the reports differ
The narrators differ on the year of his death: al-Waqidi, Khalifa and Sa'id ibn Ufayr give 49, al-Mada'ini, al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar and Ibn al-Kalbi give 50, and al-Bukhari is quoted as saying 51. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435} Ibn Kathir gives his age as forty-seven as the sounder report. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 409} The reports also differ on the wording of the testament, whether he said not to argue with them or to bury him at al-Baqi, and on the detail of the confrontation between al-Husayn and Marwan. Most reports say that Aisha answered yes, with honour, and that Marwan then rejected it, {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1436} while another is reported from her to the effect that she would not accept a fourth burial there, arguing that the house was hers, given to her by the Messenger of God in his lifetime. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1435} Ibn Kathir only says that al-Husayn put on arms, the Umayyads armed themselves and the people feared strife, without the detail of the exchange of arrows. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 409}

## Sources and documentation
This record rests on three works read from the scans: Ibn Kathir's al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, al-Dhahabi's Siyar A'lam al-Nubala and his Tarikh al-Islam. The volumes of al-Tabari covering this period were not among the pages read. The details of what passed between al-Husayn and Marwan are carried by the chains of al-Waqidi and those who quote him, and are given here as reports attributed to their narrators, not as verdicts.
