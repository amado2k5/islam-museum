## Overview
[[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] killed [[person/amr-ibn-said-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn Sa'id ibn al-As]] (al-Ashdaq), whom Marwan ibn al-Hakam had made heir after his own son. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.359}

## What al-Suyuti reports
Al-Suyuti, quoting Ibn al-Kalbi, says that the killing of Amr was the first act of treachery in Islam. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.359}

He gives a verse in which the speaker urges his people not to abandon their judgement, since they have already tried treachery from the sons of Marwan. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.359}

The date is approximate: it falls in the reign of Abd al-Malik before 73 AH, and the conversion is derived. An additional source is needed.

## In al-Dinawari
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports that a written pact, witnessed by the notables of Syria, was made between Abd al-Malik and Amr ibn Sa'id, giving Amr a partner beside every governor and the succession after Abd al-Malik. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 286} He attributes to Abd al-Malik the saying to Rawh ibn Zinba': “Have two stallions ever been together in one herd without one killing the other?” Amr was then killed inside the palace, and his head was thrown out with purses of money, which his men took. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 286}
