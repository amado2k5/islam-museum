## Summary
[[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] was commander of the armies of Syria when the [[event/plague-of-amwas|plague of Amwas]] broke out. The books report that he preached to the people when the affliction grew fierce, then was struck himself, called those who were with him and gave them a counsel, told [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] to lead the people in prayer, and died. {cite:hayat-al-sahaba-kandahlawi-sabbani scan vol. 1 p. 515}

## Context
Ibn Kathir reports from Shahr ibn Hawshab, on the authority of a man who witnessed the plague, that when the pain spread Abu Ubayda stood among the people and said that it was a mercy to them, the prayer of their Prophet and the death of the righteous before them, and that Abu Ubayda asked God to apportion him his share; he was struck, died, and named Mu'adh ibn Jabal to lead the people. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 81} Al-Dhahabi reports from Urwa that Abu Ubayda and his family were at first spared; he prayed, "O God, Your share in the house of Abu Ubayda," and a pustule came out on his little finger; told it was nothing, he said he hoped God would bless it, since when He blesses the little it becomes much. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 100}

## The testament
Ibn Asakir, as relayed in Hayat al-Sahaba from Sa'id al-Maqburi, reports that when he was struck in the Jordan he called those of the Muslims who were present and said: I counsel you with a counsel which, if you accept it, will keep you well: keep up prayer, pay zakat, fast the month of Ramadan, give charity, perform hajj and umra, counsel one another, be sincere to your commanders and do not deceive them, and do not let the world destroy you. He reminded them that a man, even if he lived a thousand years, must come to the place of falling they were looking at; that God has written death for the children of Adam; and that the shrewdest of them is the most obedient to his Lord and the most active for the day of return. He ended with the greeting of peace and said: Mu'adh ibn Jabal, lead the people in prayer; and he died. {cite:hayat-al-sahaba-kandahlawi-sabbani scan vol. 1 p. 515}

## His naming of Mu'adh
The reports agree he left Mu'adh in charge: Ibn Kathir has him name Mu'adh when struck; {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 81} in al-Hakim's report, as given by Ibn Hajar, they said "Mu'adh, lead the people", he led, and then Abu Ubayda died. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 755} Al-Dhahabi adds that earlier, when Abu Ubayda went from al-Jabiya to Jerusalem to pray, he had left Mu'adh in charge. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 100}

## Mu'adh's address after his death
Ibn Hajar quotes al-Hakim that Mu'adh preached after the death and said they had been bereaved of a man than whom he had seen none among God's servants with less rancour, kinder in heart, farther from malice, more mindful of the outcome or more sincere to the common people, and he asked them to pray for mercy on him. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 755}

## What he left
I found no mention of money left. The well-known report about his belongings is that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] came to Syria and asked for his brother Abu Ubayda, who arrived on a she-camel bridled with a rope; when Umar went with him to his house he saw nothing in it but his sword, shield and saddle, and asked why he had not acquired some goods; Abu Ubayda replied that these would carry them to their midday rest. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 96} In a report from Ibn Umar, Umar found only a felt rug, a bowl and a waterskin, and Abu Ubayda brought a few crusts from a basket, at which Umar wept and said the world had changed every one of them except him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 96} Al-Dhahabi says his line died out. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 347} After the plague Umar divided in Syria the inheritances of those who had died, since their affairs had perplexed the commanders. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 81}

## Burial and grave
Ibn Abd al-Barr says he died aged fifty-eight in the plague of Amwas in the year 18 in the Jordan district of Syria, where his grave is; Mu'adh ibn Jabal prayed over him, and Mu'adh, Amr ibn al-As and al-Dahhak ibn Qays went down into his grave. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 828} Al-Dhahabi relays from Urwa ibn Ruwaym that death overtook him at Fahl and he died there, near Baysan. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 100} See also the museum's draft card for the [[building/abu-ubaidah-mosque-jordan-valley|Mosque of Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] in the Jordan Valley, whose sources are not yet checked.

## Differing reports
His age is fifty-eight according to al-Fallas and others; the year of death is 18 in the well-known report, while Ibn A'idh alone, from Abu Mushir, says he read in the book of Yazid ibn Ubayda that Abu Ubayda died in 17. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 100} Ibn Abd al-Barr gives al-Mada'ini's figure of twenty-six thousand dead in the plague. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 828} Abu al-Muwajjih al-Marwazi claimed that Abu Ubayda was among thirty-six thousand troops of whom only six thousand survived. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 347}
