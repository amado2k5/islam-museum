## Overview
The rest of the tawil poem: al-Hadi says he has laid worry and hardship on the addressee, urges him to be first to rise in the heart of the truth, to repent and seek forgiveness, to oppose those who oppose the family of Muhammad and befriend their friends, then praises the sons of al-Qasim as having revived Islam and brought out God's limits, and ends warning him against weak opinion. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.313}

## Detail
P. 313 opens with a line in which al-Hadi says he has laid worry, grief and hardship on the addressee, that a sleeping soul is spoiled when loaded, and that whoever is not first to rise in the heart of the truth will not gain this world or the next {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.313}. He urges him not to be shamed among people but to return to what adorns him, to hasten to what pleases his God and fear Him, to repent, seek forgiveness, seek His help and hold to "the guidance of the pillars", and to oppose their opponents and befriend their friend {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.313}. He then says they are a strong fortress, a provision and the pillars of Islam for every peaceable man, that they are the "family of Muhammad", trusted, people of religion, kindness, virtue and guidance, sons of chiefs, who restrain from what angers God and command the good when disputes grow {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.313}. He then says of "the sons of al-Qasim", the resolute man of virtue and piety, that they are "an imam of guidance who effaces the wrong of mutual wronging", that by them "Islam was raised up after its death" and God's Book became high in its pillars, that God's limits are carried out despite the hard-hearted unbeliever, that the way of truth became bright and clear, that God turned back through them the falsehood of wrongs and made His religion appear after obscurity {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.313}. He calls them stars of the sky whose deeds are followed, lions in the fray who strike with swords and lances like lions loosed, and advises him to desire the like of them and gain what he wishes and a share of success {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.313}. On p. 314 he closes by warning against "weak opinion", which leaves the heart with the sorrow of one who regrets {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.314}. The footnotes record that the first line of p. 313 and the seven lines after it are missing from manuscript S, that the original reads a word the editor corrected by adding a letter to keep the metre, and that "al-khadarim" means the many {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.313}. The book does not say who "the sons of al-Qasim" are here; it may mean the descendants of [[person/al-qasim-ibn-ibrahim-al-rassi|al-Qasim al-Rassi]], al-Hadi's grandfather, a possibility the text does not settle, so no record is tied to it. That one among them is an imam of guidance and that Islam was revived through them is the poet's saying as the Zaydi book reports it, and the museum does not judge it. It follows [[event/al-hadi-answers-a-letter-of-reproach-and-the-claim-of-ingratitude-in-verse-292|his answer to a letter of reproach]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-abandons-the-abodes-of-love-and-names-his-arms-in-verse-292|his poem on leaving the abodes of love and naming his arms]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
