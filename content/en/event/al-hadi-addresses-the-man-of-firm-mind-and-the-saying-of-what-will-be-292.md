## Overview
After the heading "and also of his" come short lines in which al-Hadi addresses the man of firm mind, brother of guidance, saying he has fidelity to his covenant and his religion and love of the Prophet and his family, by which he succeeded while others lost, and ending on a proverb: what cannot be will not be by any contrivance, and what is to be will be. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}

## Detail
P. 306 opens with a short poem that follows the end of al-Hadi's lines on the justice that comes after the fighting, under the heading "and also of his", that is, of al-Hadi as the book ascribes it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}. The poet addresses the "man of firm mind, brother of guidance" and says he has fidelity to his covenant and his religion and love of the Prophet and his family, "and by that he succeeded while another lost" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}. He then gives the saying of "the man of letters", which the poet calls a proverb for the sound-minded: what cannot be will never be by any contrivance, and what is to be will be {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}. The editor records that manuscript S reads "generosity" where the text has "guidance" in the first line {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}. The book names no addressee and gives no occasion, so it is a general verse exhortation whose place is set by what precedes it. It follows [[event/al-hadi-describes-the-battle-and-the-justice-after-it-in-verse-292|his verses on the justice after the fighting]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-describes-his-armies-and-asks-them-to-spare-their-blood-in-verse-292|his verses on his armies and the call to spare their blood]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
