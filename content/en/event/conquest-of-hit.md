## Overview
According to al-Baladhuri, after [[person/umayr-ibn-sad|Umayr ibn Sa'd]] took Ras al-Ayn, he took the Khabur as far as Circesium, whose people had broken the covenant. He made peace with them on terms like their first treaty. Then he took the forts of the Euphrates one by one on the same terms, and met little real fighting in any of them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.246}

## Hit and the forts
When Umayr reached Hit, he found that [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar ibn Yasir]], Umar's governor over Kufa, had sent an army under [[person/sad-ibn-amr-ibn-haram|Sa'd ibn Amr ibn Haram]]. The people of these forts had asked for safety, and he granted it, and he excepted half of the church of the people of Hit. Umayr then returned to al-Raqqa. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.246}

## Another account
al-Baladhuri quotes some scholars that the one who went toward Hit and what lay beyond it from Kufa was [[person/midlaj-ibn-amr|Midlaj ibn Amr al-Sulami]], and that he built al-Hadītha on the Euphrates. al-Baladhuri concludes the report with: "God knows best." {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.246}

The date is approximate and derived, around 20 AH after the conquest of Ras al-Ayn.
