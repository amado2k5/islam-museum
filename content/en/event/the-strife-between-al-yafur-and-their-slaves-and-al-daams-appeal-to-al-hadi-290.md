## Overview
Then [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] stayed at [[place/saada|Sa'da]] for a time, until strife arose between [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]] and their slaves, and the two sons of Ya'fur wrote to [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] asking him to write to al-Hadi and urge him to march, on condition that they hand over what they held and fight the slaves with him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.245}. Al-Da'am wrote to him on this, disliking the march and telling him how little the people kept faith; but when the sons of Ya'fur pressed al-Da'am with letters and messengers he went in person {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.245}.

## Detail
Al-Da'am met al-Hadi at Asil, two miles from Sa'da, and, as the narrator reports, said to him: matters have become level for you and I have made sure of the men for you, and nothing is left but to march; he set out with him from [[place/saada|Sa'da]] on Sunday, two days of Jumada I gone, and went on until he halted at Khiwan {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.245}. What follows is the stay at Khiwan and the leaning of Ibn al-Dahhak, and the text breaks off at the foot of p. 245. The sons of Ya'fur are not named by the book; the year 290 is inferred from the order of the narrative after Safar 290. See [[event/al-hadi-marches-on-wail-of-shakir-and-takes-sureties-290]]. Earlier, at [[event/al-hadi-leaves-sanaa-for-sada-with-no-funds-and-al-daam-declines-to-help-289]], al-Da'am had declined to help al-Hadi; the museum records the turn without giving a reason or judgement.
