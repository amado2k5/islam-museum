## Overview
A poem in the basit metre under the heading "and also of his" on p. 317: al-Hadi says the carefree sleep while the eye of the age is in toil and the rulers of wrong have covered it over, that people are heedless of what befell the family of the Messenger, and that he rose for God's religion seeking reward, seeing no avenger for God to help it; then he asks a traveller who folds up the earth from the plain of Raydah to the Hijaz on Mahri camels to carry to the good Banu Hasan the message of a sincere adviser, the friend whose misfortune they did not fear. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}

## Detail
Under the heading "and also of his" on p. 317 basit lines open: "the carefree sleeps, and the eye of the age is in toil, the rulers of wrong having covered it with veils" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. He says people are heedless of what befell the family of the Messenger, each free of grief, until he rose for God's religion seeking reward, and God gives every seeker of reward abundantly {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. He says he sees no avenger for God to help Him and none angered for God's religion, and asks how there can be rest when the marks of what the Messenger laid down have become like the face of a blade with no trace, and how a man of lineage guarded by good manners can accept an offer of humiliation {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. He then addresses a traveller who folds up the earth, girded, toward the Hijaz on Mahri camels, his journey starting from the plain of [[place/raydah-yemen|Raydah]], swift and firm of purpose, and asks him to carry to "the good Banu Hasan" a message "from an adviser to them of fluent speech", the friend whose misfortune was never feared and who was never charged with falling short among the Arabs, whose love and guard for them is absent in body but not in heart {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. The footnote says manuscript S reads "religion" where the text has "the age", that "al-ma'laka" means the message, and that S reads "and by their guarding" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. The book does not name the traveller or say which branch of the Hasanids "Banu Hasan" are, and gives neither the time nor the place of the poem beyond Raydah as the start of the journey; it stands after [[event/al-hadi-reflects-on-death-the-reckoning-and-the-stranger-in-the-world-292|the lines on death and the reckoning]] in the book, where he asks when he will be seen standing in God's cause. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
