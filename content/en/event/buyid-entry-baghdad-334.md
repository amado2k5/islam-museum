## Overview
In 334 AH Ahmad ibn Buyah entered Baghdad, met the caliph [[person/al-mustakfi-billah|al-Mustakfi]], and was given the title Amir al-Umara' (commander of commanders). {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.632-633}

## What happened
al-Mustakfi sent to Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Buyah food and precious gifts. The oath was taken from him and he swore it, and the caliph swore to him and his two brothers, and a document was written to that effect, witnessed by the witnesses. He put on the robes of honour and had the collar placed on him, and was made Amir al-Umara', and according to Ibn al-Jawzi he was the first of the Buyid rulers. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.632-633}

His elder brother Ali was given the title Imad al-Dawla, and his middle brother al-Hasan the title Rukn al-Dawla. Their titles and kunyas were ordered to be struck on the dinars and dirhams. The Daylam and the Turks were lodged in the people's houses, and the source says that Baghdad had not known this before that day. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.632-633}
