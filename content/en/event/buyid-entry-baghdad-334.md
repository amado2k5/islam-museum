## Overview
In 334 AH Ahmad ibn Buyah entered Baghdad, met the caliph [[person/al-mustakfi-billah|al-Mustakfi]], and was given the title Amir al-Umara' (commander of commanders). {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.632-633}

## What happened
al-Mustakfi sent to Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Buyah food and precious gifts. The oath was taken from him and he swore it, and the caliph swore to him and his two brothers, and a document was written to that effect, witnessed by the witnesses. He put on the robes of honour and had the collar placed on him, and was made Amir al-Umara', and according to Ibn al-Jawzi he was the first of the Buyid rulers. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.632-633}

His elder brother Ali was given the title Imad al-Dawla, and his middle brother al-Hasan the title Rukn al-Dawla. Their titles and kunyas were ordered to be struck on the dinars and dirhams. The Daylam and the Turks were lodged in the people's houses, and the source says that Baghdad had not known this before that day. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.632-633}

See also [[person/muizz-al-dawla|Mu'izz al-Dawla]] and [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]].

## In Miskawayh's Tajarib al-umam
[[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] tells the story in [[work/tajarib-al-umam|Tajarib al-umam]] in detail from its beginning. In Muharram 334 AH [[person/tuzun|Tuzun]] died in his house in Baghdad; by Miskawayh's count his command had lasted two years, four months and seventeen days. The news reached his secretary [[person/abu-jaafar-ibn-shirzad|Abu Ja'far ibn Shirzad]] at [[place/hit|Hit]], and the army agreed to give him the command. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.111}

Ibn Shirzad camped at Bab Harb at the start of Safar, and on Friday 2 Safar the whole army of Turks and Daylamites agreed on his leadership and swore to him, "and greeted him with sweet basil in the Persian manner". He asked [[person/al-mustakfi-billah|al-Mustakfi]] to swear an oath to him before the judges and the notaries, and then again before the chief Turks and Daylamites; the caliph found this hard, but did it. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.112} Miskawayh says Ibn Shirzad raised the soldiers' pay, which deepened the shortage, so he turned to confiscations and levied money on secretaries, tax agents and merchants, and that two informers known as "Harut and Marut" pointed out anyone who had stores of food or wealth, until the merchants fled Baghdad. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.112} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.113}

## The march of Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Buwayh
When news came that Yanal Kusa, police governor of [[place/wasit|Wasit]], had gone over to Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Buwayh, and that the amir had set out from [[place/al-ahwaz|al-Ahwaz]] for the capital, the Turks and Daylamites camped at the musalla. When the amir reached Bajisra, Ibn Shirzad and al-Mustakfi went into hiding; Ibn Shirzad's command had lasted "three months and twenty days". The Turks crossed to the west bank and left for [[place/mosul|Mosul]], and al-Mustakfi reappeared and went back to the caliphal palace. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.114}

[[person/al-muhallabi-al-wazir|Abu Muhammad al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Muhallabi]], the amir's man, arrived, met Ibn Shirzad in his hiding place and then met al-Mustakfi. The caliph showed his pleasure at the amir's coming and told him he had hidden from the Turks only so that their cause would fall apart and the amir would gain power without effort. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.114}

## The audience and the titles
On Saturday 11 Jumada II the amir Abu al-Husayn encamped at Bab al-Shammasiyya and came to al-Mustakfi. He stood before him a long time, gave him the oath of allegiance and swore the strongest oaths, and the oath included safety for the caliph's secretary Abu Ahmad al-Shirazi, his stewardess Alam, Ibn Abi Musa al-Hashimi, the judge Abu al-Sa'ib and the chamberlain Abu al-Abbas ibn Khaqan. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.114} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.115}

The amir then asked the caliph about Ibn Shirzad and sought leave to take him as his secretary; the caliph granted him safety and gave leave. The amir put on the robes and was named [[person/muizz-al-dawla|Mu'izz al-Dawla]]; his brother Abu al-Hasan Ali was named [[person/imad-al-dawla-daylami|Imad al-Dawla]] and his brother Abu Ali al-Hasan [[person/rukn-al-dawla|Rukn al-Dawla]]. Their titles and kunyas were to be struck on the dinars and dirhams, and he left for the house of Mu'nis. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.115} The Daylamites, the Jil and the Turks were quartered in people's houses, which caused great hardship, and Miskawayh says this "became a custom imposed on them to this day". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.115}

## Afterwards
Ibn Shirzad came out of hiding and managed the land tax and revenue collection for Mu'izz al-Dawla, and Abu Abd Allah al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn Muqla was arrested over a note he had sent asking for Ibn Shirzad's post. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.115} Eleven days after this audience al-Mustakfi was deposed, as told in [[event/mustakfi-blinding-334|the deposition of al-Mustakfi]]. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.116} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.117}

## The two accounts
Ibn al-Jawzi and Miskawayh agree on the substance: the allegiance and the oath, the three titles struck on the coinage, and the quartering of soldiers in people's houses. Miskawayh adds the day, the names of those included in the oath, and the story of Ibn Shirzad that came before. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.632-633} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.114} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.115}
