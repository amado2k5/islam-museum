## Summary
In 518 AH the Franks took Tyre by safe-conduct, and it remained in their hands until 690 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 537}.

## Details
Under the events of 518 Ibn al-'Imad records that the Franks took the coastal city of Tyre "bi'l-aman" — under a grant of safe-conduct rather than by storm — and that it stayed in their hands until 690, that is for over a hundred and seventy years {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 537}. Tyre was one of the greatest and strongest fortresses of the Syrian coast, and the Franks made it a bastion of their kingdom of Jerusalem, until it returned to Muslim hands in the last days of that state, as the author dates it {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 537}. His note that it "remained in their hands until 690" reflects the chronicler's habit of closing an account by naming the year in which the Frankish presence on that coast ended {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 537}.
