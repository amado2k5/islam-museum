## Summary
Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj entered Baghdad several times {cite:wafayat-ayan v p. 195}, hearing there from Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 241/855), from whom he transmitted copiously, and he compiled a book of his questions to him, the Masa'il Ahmad ibn Hanbal {cite:tazkirat-al-huffaz j.2 p. 527-528}. The Encyclopaedia of Islam counts Ahmad among the masters of Muslim whose works survive in print {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 690-691}.
