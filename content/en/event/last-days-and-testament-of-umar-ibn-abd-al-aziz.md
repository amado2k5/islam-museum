## Summary
The caliph [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] died at Dayr Sim'an in the district of Homs in Rajab of the year 101, after an illness of about twenty days; his caliphate had lasted two years, five months and some days. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 90} The histories keep words from his dying hours, his answer when told to leave something to his children, the purchase of the plot for his grave from the people of the monastery, and an account of what he left. He had succeeded [[person/sulayman-ibn-abd-al-malik|Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik]], who [[event/sulayman-names-umar-ii-99|named him to the caliphate]], with Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik to follow. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 91}

## The illness
Ibn Kathir reports that the cause of his death is said to have been consumption, or else that a freedman of his poisoned him in food or drink for a thousand dinars. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 226} When told he had been poisoned he said he had known it the day he was given the drink; he summoned the freedman, who said he had been given a thousand dinars, and Umar took the money and put it in the treasury and told him to go where no one would see him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 226} Told to seek a cure, he said that if his healing lay in touching his earlobe or smelling perfume he would not do it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 226} His illness was at Dayr Sim'an, one of the villages of Homs, and lasted about twenty days. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 227} In al-Tabari, Yazid ibn al-Muhallab escaped from prison when he heard Umar was ill, fearing Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 88}

## His words as he died
Ibn Kathir relates that he asked to be sat up and, when they sat him up, said three times: my God, I am the one You commanded and I fell short, and You forbade me and I disobeyed, but there is no god but God. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 227} Then he raised his head and stared hard, and when asked why he said he saw presences that were neither human nor jinn, and he was taken. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 227} In another report he told his household to leave him, and Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik and his sister Fatima sat at the door and heard him welcome faces that were neither human nor jinn and recite the verse about the last abode, [[passage/quran-28-83|Qur'an 28:83]]; then his voice fell quiet and when they went in they found him with his eyes closed and his body laid toward the qibla. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 227}

## His answer when told to leave something for his children
He was told that his sons, who numbered twelve, were poor, and asked whether he would not make some bequest to them. He answered with the verse that says God is his guardian and guards the righteous, [[passage/quran-7-196|Qur'an 7:196]], and said he would not give them anyone's right, since they were of two kinds: if righteous, God would guard them, and if not, he would not help them in their wrongdoing. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 226} In another report he said: shall I leave them what will help them disobey God and so share in what they do after my death? {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 226} Then he called them, bade them farewell, consoled them and said: go, God protect you. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 226} Ibn Kathir says some of his sons were later seen supplying eighty horses for God's cause, while some of Sulayman's sons, for all that their father left, were receiving from Umar's sons. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 226}

## What he left
Ibn Kathir notes that he returned all wrongs, even the bezel of a ring that al-Walid had given him, that by one report he returned his wife Fatima bint Abd al-Malik's dowry goods to the treasury, and that his income before the caliphate, forty thousand dinars a year, shrank to four hundred. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 225} When his son Abd al-Malik died during his caliphate he showed no grief, saying it was a matter God approved and so he would not dislike it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 225} In al-Dhahabi's account his sons included Abd al-Malik, who died before him, Yazid, Asbagh, al-Walid, Zabban, Adam and Ibrahim. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2915} Ibn Kathir reports that he wore hair sackcloth beneath his clothes and put an iron collar on his neck when he rose to pray at night, and when he died they opened the door he had sealed and found the collar and sackcloth. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 225}

## The grave
Al-Tabari relates from one of his sons that Umar ordered them to buy the place of his grave and they bought it from the monk. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 98} Al-Dhahabi gives the report of Abu Umayya, his servant, whom he sent with two dinars to the people of the monastery with the words: if you sell me the place of my grave, well; otherwise I shall move from you. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2915} When it was suggested that he go to Medina, where if God decreed death he would be buried in the fourth grave with the Prophet, Abu Bakr and Umar, he said that every torment short of fire was dearer to him than that God should know in his heart that he thought himself fit for that place. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 227} Al-Tabari quotes a poet's lament that speaks of his burial at Dayr Sim'an. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 99} Al-Dhahabi relates that Makhul once left the road to visit his grave, five miles from the camp, and prayed for him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2915}

## Washing and prayer
Raja' ibn Haywa relates that Umar had charged him to wash and shroud him and to look at his face when he loosed the knot of the shroud; he did so and found the face white as paper, and Umar had told him that the face of each caliph before him whom he had buried was black when uncovered. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 228} They differ on who prayed over him: Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik, Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik, or his son Abd al-Aziz. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 228} Ibn Kathir relates from Ibn Abi Shayba that when he was laid by his grave a strong wind blew and a leaf fell bearing a pardon from God for Umar from the fire, which was placed among his shrouds. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 227} He also relates, from Ibn Asakir, that a letter fell from the sky as they smoothed the earth over his grave granting him security from the fire, and says that this report is very strange. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 228}

## Where the reports differ
The reports differ on the day of his death: five nights before the end of Rajab according to Abu Ma'shar and Hisham from Abu Mikhnaf, ten nights before the end according to al-Waqidi. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 89} Al-Tabari also has Wednesday, and Hisham from Abu Mikhnaf says Friday, {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 90} while Ja'far al-Sadiq says Thursday. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2915} He was thirty-nine and some months, though some say forty. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 3 p. 90}

## Sources and documentation
The record rests on al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and al-Dhahabi. No letter from Umar to Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik at his death was found in the pages read, so none is given.
