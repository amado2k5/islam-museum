## Overview
A poem in the basit metre under the heading "and also of his" at the foot of p. 321 and on p. 322, in which al-Hadi addresses "O Da'am": he tells him to rise, for the chance of Yemen is within reach, recalls the virtues of his forebears and their stands with Ali at Siffin and Nahrawan, urges him to seize the matter while the spirit is in his body and to strive in aid of the religion, and says religion is not set right by allegiance to Ibn al-Ajami or to the two sons of Ali, even if tempted with a price, but by a sincere, fearing heart. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.321}

## Detail
At the foot of p. 321, under the heading "and also of his (basit)", al-Hadi addresses him: "rise, for the chance of Yemen is within our reach, and join the virtues of the first age" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.321}. He says the precedences, boldness and nobility were with "the pure guide, Abu Hasan", who in the line's context is [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], that on [[event/battle-of-siffin|the day of Siffin]] marked horsemen plunged into the deeps of death, that [[event/battle-of-nahrawan|the day of Nahrawan]] was theirs with the dust raised by swords and fortresses, and that their aid to the Commander of the Faithful Ali was "pure love and the reviving of the practices" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.321}. He asks the addressee to add honour to honour "among [[group/banu-hamdan|Hamdan]] and the clans of Yemen", says "in you is that praise of God that we know, for you are the lion of battle in peace and strife", and cries "seize the matter, O Da'am, rising to it, while the spirit of life is in the body" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.321}. He says he will be favoured with God "if you subdue the enemies of religion, you will not weaken", and that he stands aiding God's religion with effort against its enemies {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.321}. Here p. 321 also carries the line in which he asks him to follow what his elders before him took up "by the good way", and the text keeps the order of the lines as printed on the page {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}. On p. 322 he says "none sets God's religion right by helping it, nor by allegiance to him in secret and open, nor by allegiance to Ibn al-Ajami nor to the two sons of Ali, though you be tempted with a price", unless with a sincere, fearing heart that clings to God from every rancorous person {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}. He urges him to help and to prefer the Almighty, our Creator, and says he must reject their allegiance and leave their affection {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}. The name "Ibn al-Ajami" is given as printed and the book does not say here who he is or who the "two sons of Ali" are, and no act is ascribed to either; the same name appears earlier in the book and is not tied to this line. Al-Da'am answers with the poem that follows. The claims and descriptions are the poet's alone. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
