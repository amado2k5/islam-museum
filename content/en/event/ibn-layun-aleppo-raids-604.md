## Summary
In 604 AH the Armenian Ibn Layun, lord of al-Durub, raided the wilaya of Aleppo repeatedly — plundering, burning, capturing and enslaving — and al-Malik al-Zahir's troops were defeated twice in the encounter. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 319}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that Ibn Layun's raids on Aleppo's wilaya multiplied that year, so al-Malik al-Zahir Ghazi ibn Salah al-Din, lord of Aleppo, gathered his armies, summoned other kings, collected many horse and foot, and marched on Ibn Layun, who had camped at the edge of his own country reachable only through rugged mountains and hard passes. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 319} Al-Zahir camped five farsakhs from Aleppo, put his father's senior mamluk Maymun al-Qasri over the vanguard, sent provisions and arms to his fort Darbasak adjoining Ibn Layun's lands, and ordered Maymun to send a detachment with the convoy; Maymun did so and was left with few men. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 320} Ibn Layun learned of it, came with a large force, and hard fighting followed; Maymun defended himself and the baggage with few Muslims against many Armenians but was defeated with dead and captured; the Armenians then met the supply column to Darbasak unawares, put it to the sword and routed it too, returning to their mountains and fortresses with the spoils. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 320}
