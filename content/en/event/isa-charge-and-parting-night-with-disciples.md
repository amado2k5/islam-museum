## Summary
Surat Maryam ([[passage/quran-19-31|Qur'an 19:31]], [[passage/quran-19-32|19:32]], [[passage/quran-19-33|19:33]]) tells that [[person/isa-ibn-maryam|Isa ibn Maryam]] (Jesus) spoke in the cradle and declared that God had charged him with prayer and almsgiving for as long as he lived, and with kindness to his mother. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 703} The books of commentary and history, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, Ibn Abbas and others, report the night on which Isa gathered his disciples before he was raised, and what he counselled them. The Sunni position as Ibn Kathir sets it out is that God raised him alive to heaven, and that he was neither killed nor crucified. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 745}

## The charge of prayer and almsgiving
Ibn Kathir says in Stories of the Prophets that these were the first words Isa spoke, that in them he acknowledged his servanthood to his Lord, and then declared that God had given him the Book and made him a prophet. He explains prayer and almsgiving as the servant's duty to his Lord and good to creation, and says that almsgiving includes cleansing the soul of base traits and purifying wealth by giving to those in need. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 703} In his Tafsir he says this is like God telling Muhammad to worship his Lord until certainty comes. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2942} He quotes Malik ibn Anas on the verse that God told him what would be his lot until he died. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2942}

The verses add that God made him dutiful to his mother and did not make him overbearing or wretched. Ibn Kathir notes that kindness to the mother is mentioned after obedience to God because God often joins the command to worship Him with obedience to parents; he quotes Mujahid, Amr ibn Qays and al-Thawri that "blessed" means a teacher of good, and Wuhayb ibn al-Ward that his blessing was enjoining good and forbidding wrong. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2942}

## The parting night with the disciples
Ibn Jarir reports from Wahb ibn Munabbih that when God let Isa know he would leave the world, he was distressed at death and found it hard, so he called the disciples, made them food and asked them to come to him that night as he had a need of them. When they gathered he served them supper himself, and when they had eaten he began to wash their hands, make their ablutions and wipe their hands with his own clothes. They thought it too much and disliked it, and he said: whoever refuses me anything I do tonight is not of me and I am not of him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 748}

He then said that what he had done in serving them and washing their hands was to be an example to them; they regarded him as the best of them, so none of them should exalt himself over another, and each should give himself to the other as he had given himself to them. The need for which he sought their help was that they pray for him and strive in prayer that his term be delayed. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 748} When they stood to pray, sleep overcame them and they could not do it; he woke them saying: can you not bear one night with me, helping me? They said they did not know what had come over them, that they had been accustomed to staying up late in talk and could not manage it that night. He said: "the shepherd goes and the sheep scatter", and went on with words like these, announcing his own departure. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 748} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 749}

He told them that one of them would deny him before the cock crowed three times, and that another would sell him for a few dirhams and eat his price, and they went out and scattered. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 749} Ibn Kathir calls the chain of the report that continues after this "strange and remarkable", and thinks it sounder than what the Christians relate. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 749}

## From the report of Ibn Abbas
Ibn Abi Hatim reports from Ibn Abbas that when God wished to raise Isa to heaven, Isa came out to his companions, twelve men being in the house among them disciples, with water dripping from his head, and told them that one of them would disbelieve in him twelve times after believing. He then asked which of them would have his likeness cast upon him, be killed in his place and share his rank; the youngest stood, Isa told him to sit, and when he asked again the young man stood and said: I. Isa said: you are he. His likeness was cast upon him and Isa was raised through an opening in the house to heaven. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 746} Ibn Kathir says this chain to Ibn Abbas is sound by the standard of Muslim, and that al-Nasa'i also reports it. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 747}

The reports differ on who was made to resemble him. From Ibn Ishaq: among the disciples was a man named Sarjis whom the Christians hid, and he was the one upon whom the likeness was cast; some Christians claim it was Yudus ibn Kariyayuta; and Ibn Kathir adds "God knows best". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 747} In the report of Wahb ibn Munabbih, seventeen of them were in a house which was surrounded, and God made all of them look like Isa. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 748}

## The Sunni position on his being raised
Ibn Kathir, in Stories of the Prophets, under "The raising of Isa to heaven", says God stated that He raised him to heaven after causing him to sleep, "on the sound and certain view", and delivered him from those who wished to harm him, and that they did not kill him or crucify him. He bases this on the verses [[passage/quran-3-55|Qur'an 3:55]], [[passage/quran-4-157|4:157]] and [[passage/quran-4-158|4:158]]. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 745} He adds that belief in him before his death means after his descent to earth at the end of time, when he will kill the swine, break the cross, lay down the tax on non-Muslims and accept nothing but Islam, as he sets out in the hadiths in his commentary on the verse of al-Nisa and in his Book of Trials and Epic Battles. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 746} See [[concept/nuzul-isa-ibn-maryam|The descent of Isa ibn Maryam]].

On what followed the raising, Ibn Kathir gives reports about his age and the length of his stay among his people: from al-Hasan al-Basri, that he was raised aged thirty-four; from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, thirty-three; from Ibrahim, that he stayed among his people forty years. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 750} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 751} He mentions al-Fasawi's hadith, from Fatima from Aisha, that Isa lived a hundred and twenty years, calls it strange, and quotes Ibn Asakir that the sound view is that he did not reach this age and that what is meant is the length of his stay among his community. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 751}

## What is ascribed to his grave
Ibn Kathir gives a report in al-Tirmidhi, from Abd Allah ibn Salam, that it is written in the Torah that the description of Muhammad and of Isa ibn Maryam is given and that Isa is buried with him, and that Abu Mawdud said a place for a grave remained in the room; he then gives al-Tirmidhi's remark that there is a fault in the name of one transmitter, and al-Bukhari's: "this hadith is not sound with me and is not corroborated". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 760} He also records that Ibn Asakir cites from Aisha, raised to the Prophet, that he will be buried with the Messenger, Abu Bakr and Umar in the Prophet's room, but Ibn Kathir finds its chain unsound. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 760} See [[concept/graves-of-the-prophets|the record on "The graves of the prophets"]].
