## Overview
Al-Baladhuri sets out the history of the city of [[place/al-hadath|al-Hadath]]. [[person/habib-ibn-maslama|Habib ibn Maslama]] conquered it in Umar's days; the Romans demolished it in the strife of Marwan ibn Muhammad; it was then built in the time of [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]], and [[person/harun-al-rashid|al-Rashid]] rebuilt it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.267}

## The name
Al-Baladhuri says that the Umayyads used to call the road of al-Hadath "the Safety", out of superstition, because the Muslims had suffered there. He gives another account, that a young Roman boy fought them on the road, and it was named after him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The building
In 161 AH al-Mahdi sent [[person/hasan-ibn-qahtaba|al-Hasan ibn Qahtaba]] to the land of the Romans; he chose the site of the city and spoke with al-Mahdi about building it, and al-Mahdi ordered the building of al-Hadath brought forward. [[person/ali-ibn-sulayman-ibn-ali|Ali ibn Sulayman ibn Ali]], who was governor of al-Jazira and Qinnasrin, founded it, and it was called al-Muhammadiyya. It was built of mud brick. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Al-Mahdi died in 169 AH as its building was completed. [[person/muhammad-ibn-ibrahim-al-hashimi|Muhammad ibn Ibrahim]] assigned it a grant of men from Syria, al-Jazira and Khurasan, and granted them dwellings. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Ruin and rebuilding
Al-Baladhuri says that winter, snow and rain harmed the poorly built structure, so the city fell into disrepair and the Romans came down upon it. In a report other than al-Waqidi's, a Roman patrician camped against it, burned its mosque and ruined it. When al-Rashid came to power he ordered it rebuilt, fortified and garrisoned, and granted its soldiers dwellings. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The date
The order came after the raid of 162 AH; the completion was in 169 AH, according to al-Baladhuri; the conversion to the Gregorian calendar is derived. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.267}
