## Summary
Ibn Taymiyya died in the Damascus citadel, in the hall where he was held, on the night of Monday 20 Dhu al-Qa'da 728 AH, as Ibn Kathir relays from al-Birzali's history; his brother said they had completed eighty readings of the Qur'an together since he entered the citadel. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} A very large crowd gathered for his funeral; prayers were said over him in the citadel, in the Umayyad Mosque and at the horse market, and he was buried in the Sufi cemetery beside his brother Sharaf al-Din Abd Allah. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} This card belongs with [[event/ibn-taymiyya-final-imprisonment-726|his last imprisonment in the citadel]].

## Context
In the events of 728 AH Ibn Kathir gives an entry for 'the Shaykh al-Islam Abu al-Abbas Taqi al-Din Ahmad ibn Taymiyya', beginning with what Shaykh Alam al-Din al-Birzali said in his history and following it with a description of the funeral. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531} He says Ibn Taymiyya was in the citadel with his brother Zayn al-Din Abd al-Rahman, who told those present what they had read. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} Days after his death, on 20 Dhu al-Hijja, his disciple [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya]], who had been held in the citadel since a few days after the shaykh's arrest in Sha'ban 726, was released. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 535}

## The last days
Al-Birzali, as Ibn Kathir quotes him, says he died in the citadel in the hall where he was held; a great crowd came to the citadel and was allowed in to see him; some sat with him before the washing, read the Qur'an and sought blessing by looking at him and kissing him, then left; then a group of women came and did the same. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531}
The narrator whom the text then quotes says that the death came at dawn on the night of Monday; the citadel's muezzin announced it from the minaret and the guards spoke of it from the towers; people gathered round the citadel from every place, even from the Ghuta and the Marj, and the market people cooked nothing and did not open many of their shops. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 533} The viceroy Tankiz was away hunting; the citadel's deputy Ghabriyal came, condoled, and opened the citadel door to close friends and companions. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 533}
This narrator says he was present with his teacher the hafiz Abu al-Hajjaj [[person/jamal-al-din-al-mizzi|al-Mizzi]], uncovered the shaykh's face, looked at him and kissed him; he had a turban with a tail on his head and white hair that had grown more than when they last saw him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} He says the brother Zayn al-Din Abd al-Rahman told those present that he and the shaykh had read eighty complete Qur'ans since entering the citadel and had begun the eighty-first; they had reached the end of Surat al-Qamar, [[passage/quran-54-54|Qur'an 54]]. At that point the two righteous shaykhs Abd Allah ibn al-Muhibb and Abd Allah al-Zar'i the blind, whose recitation the shaykh loved, began at the start of Surat al-Rahman and finished the Qur'an while the narrator was present, listening and watching. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534}
The page I read does not name this narrator; it follows quotations from al-Birzali, who says he was away from Damascus on the Hijaz road when Ibn Taymiyya died and heard the news more than fifty days later at Tabuk. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 533} The page does not itself establish that the narrator is Ibn Kathir, the book's author, though he describes himself as a student of al-Mizzi.

## Washing, prayer and funeral
Then they began to wash him, leaving with him only those who helped, among them the hafiz al-Mizzi and a group of the great righteous. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} When it was done the citadel filled and people cried out with weeping, praise, prayer and mercy, and they carried him to the Umayyad Mosque and set him in the place of funerals near the maqsura. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} Shaykh Muhammad ibn Tammam led the first prayer in the citadel, and the second was in the mosque after the noon prayer. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531} The prayer was held on the platform, against custom, with the deputy preacher Ala al-Din al-Kharrat as imam, the preacher being in Egypt. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} Another prayer was held at the horse market, led by his brother Zayn al-Din Abd al-Rahman. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531}
The account says that someone cried out, 'This is how the funerals of the imams of the Sunna should be,' and people wept together. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} It says people threw their kerchiefs, turbans and clothes on his bier, and sandals were lost from people's feet. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531}

## The crowd and its size
The account says women were estimated at fifteen thousand, not counting those on the roofs, and men at sixty thousand, up to a hundred thousand and more, up to two hundred thousand. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} The editor's note says that 'hazar' means to estimate by guess. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} A group drank the water left from his washing and divided what was left of the lote-leaf used to wash him; a hundred and fifty dirhams were paid for the thread with mercury he wore round his neck against lice, and five hundred dirhams are said to have been paid for the cap on his head. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} People closed their shops, and only those unable to come stayed away; among scholars only a few stayed away, whom the narrator names as three, Ibn Jumla, al-Sadr and al-Qafjari, who hid for fear. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 534} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 535} The narrator compares the funeral with those of Abu Bakr ibn Abi Dawud and of Ahmad ibn Hanbal in Baghdad, and judges that had a mighty sultan gathered them they would not have reached this number. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 533}

## Burial and the grave
The bier left by the Bab al-Faraj and was carried to the Sufis' cemetery, where he was buried beside his brother Sharaf al-Din Abd Allah a little before the afternoon prayer, because so many came to pray over him from the orchards, the Ghuta and the villages. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 531} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} People went to his grave for many days, by night and by day, sleeping and rising there; good dreams of him were seen, and a number of people lamented him in poems. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} Al-Fazari and some Shafi'i scholars visited the grave in the three days. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 535} It is in the [[place/sufism-cemetery|Sufi cemetery]]; the text does not describe the grave or any structure over it.

## The comprehensive counsel
A title, al-Wasiyya al-Jami'a ('the comprehensive counsel'), is attributed to Ibn Taymiyya, and the museum has a draft card for it, [[work/al-wasiyya-al-jamia-li-ibn-taymiyya|al-Wasiyya al-Jami'a of Ibn Taymiyya]], built from Wikidata and not checked against a page. In the pages of al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya that I read I found nothing that mentions, describes or ties this counsel to his last days, so I do not link it to this death. The account does not say Ibn Taymiyya left a bequest of money or a debt.

## Where the reports differ
The pages agree that he died in the citadel on the night of Monday 20 Dhu al-Qa'da 728 and was buried in the Sufi cemetery beside his brother. The estimates of the men present range from sixty thousand to two hundred thousand and remain guesses. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 532} The text shows the washing carried out by those who helped and the prayer held in three places.
