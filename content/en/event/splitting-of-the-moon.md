## Overview
The sayings in the Sahih of al-Bukhari relate that the people of [[place/mecca|Mecca]] asked [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] for a sign, and he showed them the moon split into two halves, until they saw Hira between them. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3868}

## The reports
In another report they said: "Muhammad has bewitched us." {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3868}

[[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]] relates that the moon split while they were with the Prophet at Mina, and he said: bear witness. A group went off toward the mountain. He once said it split in Mecca. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3869}

[[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] relates that the moon split in the time of the Messenger of God. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3870}

## In the Qur'an
Muslims read this event in the opening of the surah of al-Qamar: [[passage/quran-54-1|al-Qamar 1]]. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3868}

The hadiths do not give the year of the event, which falls in the Meccan period after the mission began. Fixing the year needs an additional source.

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya'
In the sixth episode of [[work/el-hadara-el-nabaouia|al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] relates that Quraysh asked for a sign other than the Qur'an and asked the Prophet to split the moon; he prayed and pointed at it and it split into two parts that stayed apart for some hours and then joined, and the wiser among them asked arriving caravans, who said they had seen it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:03:00} He answers some exegetes who held it has not yet happened with the hadith that the Prophet's mission and the Hour are like two fingers. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:04:00} He relates from Muhammad Hamidullah a report of an Indian manuscript in the India Office library in London about a king of Malabar who saw the splitting, went to Mecca, became Muslim, died on the way back and was buried at Zafar; this is his account of the document (needs_source). {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:09:30} He mentions rilles on the far side of the moon that may in his view be traces of it, then says sensory miracles cannot be explained scientifically. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep06 00:11:30}
