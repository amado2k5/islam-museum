## Overview
The news came to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] in the night of Ibn al-Ruwayya's rout and what the men had done to him; al-Hadi was at that time gravely ill, funds for the armies had run short, and he asked the people of [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]] for help and a loan, and they gave him not one dirham {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.241}. When he knew the army could not stay without funds and that he could not provide for them, he took counsel with his companions, who saw nothing better for him than to leave; he had a heavy illness that scarcely let him stay on a horse for the blink of an eye, so he decided to leave on Thursday, told his companions to gather at his door, ordered his womenfolk and household brought out, and went out in a litter with his companions around him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.241}.

## Detail
He halted at the gate of the street until the army and the baggage had gone through, and, as the narrator reports, he said to the people of Sana'a before leaving that they would have to wait on him no longer than the interval between two milkings of a camel, that God would strike them with the garment of hunger and fear, and that their women would be sold for one, two or three dinars; some of the people of Sana'a said he meant to enslave their womenfolk as the man of Basra had done, which reached him, and he repeated that the selling of womenfolk he had spoken of was not from himself, nor from [[group/banu-tarif|Al Tarif]] nor from [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]], and that they would soon know the truth of his word as God's recompense for what they had done, and then he quoted a verse ([[passage/quran-26-227|al-Shu'ara 26:227]]) {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.241} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.242}. The museum gives this as the imam's own words in a time of war, as the narrator reports them, and passes no judgement. Al-Hadi then halted at Warwar, where [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] met him; he asked for help and that al-Da'am go out with him with his army and clans until he could return and fight the men, but al-Da'am did not agree and put forward excuses, so al-Hadi, finding no help, went on until he reached [[place/saada|Sa'da]] in the last days of Jumada II 289 AH {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.242}. Warwar is as printed and is not made a place record.
