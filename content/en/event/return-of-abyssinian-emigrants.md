## Summary
In Ibn Hisham's recension, Ibn Ishaq presents the coming of the Abyssinian emigrants to the Prophet at Khaybar after al-Hudaybiyya, then lists their names clan by clan. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3}

This page is the canonical entry for the event; the earlier duplicate page "the return from Abyssinia" was merged into it, and its id `event/return-from-abyssinia` became an alias. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3-11}

## The coming
The Prophet sent [[person/amr-ibn-umayya-al-damri|Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri]] to the Negus; the Negus carried those who remained in two ships and brought them to the Prophet while he was at Khaybar after al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3}

[[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far ibn Abi Talib]] arrived, and with him his wife [[person/asma-bint-umays|Asma' bint Umays]] and his son [[person/abdullah-ibn-jafar|Abdullah]], whom she had borne in the land of Abyssinia; Ja'far was killed at Mu'ta as the Messenger of God's commander. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3}

Under this heading it is reported that the Prophet kissed Ja'far between the eyes and embraced him on his coming. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3} See [[event/jafar-arrival-at-khaybar|Ja'far's arrival at the Prophet at Khaybar]].

## The groups
- Those carried in the two ships, sixteen men, detailed in [[event/emigrants-carried-in-two-ships|the Negus carrying the emigrants in two ships]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3-5}
- The rest of the Abyssinian emigrants, thirty-four men, not carried in the two ships: they came after Badr, or later, or died there. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/10}
- The women emigrants, sixteen women, and those born there, five men and five women. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/10-12}

The full list is in [[dataset/abyssinia-muhajirun-roster|the Abyssinian emigrants roster]], and the term in [[concept/muhajirun-of-abyssinia|the emigrants of Abyssinia]].

## Markers of weakness and doubt in the chapter
These markers as they appear in the book, all recorded:
- "Ibn Hisham said" prefaces the report of Ja'far's coming, from Sufyan ibn Uyayna from al-Ajlah from al-Sha'bi. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3}
- "It is said: Humayna bint Khalaf" for the name of Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-As's wife — she is, in his account, Amina bint Khalaf. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3-4}
- "So it was said: if any man was the first to inherit his father in Islam" — of Abdullah ibn al-Muttalib ibn Azhar. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/7}
- "It is doubted whether he was killed there or not" — of Abdullah ibn Sufyan al-Makhzumi. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/7}
- "It is said: he was killed on the day of Khaybar — it is doubted" — of al-Sa'ib ibn al-Harith al-Sahmi. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/8}
- An apparent contradiction on where Rayta bint al-Harith died: in the land of Abyssinia on p. 5, and on the road on p. 11. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/5, 4/11}

## For Sprouts
A group of the first Muslims sailed to Abyssinia to escape harm in Mecca, and they lived there for years under the rule of the just king called the Negus. After the Treaty of Hudaybiyya, the Negus sent those who remained on two ships, and they reached the Prophet while he was at Khaybar. Among them was his cousin Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, and the Prophet was very glad to see them.

## For Explorers
When harm grew heavy on the first Muslims in Mecca, the Prophet allowed them to emigrate to Abyssinia, where the Christian king called the Negus was a safe refuge, and they went in two waves around 615 and 616 CE. Many stayed there for years while some returned earlier. In the year 7 AH, after the Treaty of Hudaybiyya and the affair of Khaybar, the Negus sent the remaining emigrants on two ships, and they reached the Prophet at Khaybar. Among them was Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, who had been away about fifteen years, and the sira books relate that the Prophet said he did not know which made him happier: the victory at Khaybar or Ja'far's arrival.

## For Scholars
The return of the Abyssinian emigrants is the journey that closed the first emigration in Islamic history. The vulnerable Muslims had emigrated to Aksum in two waves around 615 and 616 CE, and the Negus's protection was an early political and religious refuge. Ibn Ishaq's account in Ibn Hisham's recension relates that the Prophet sent Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri to the Negus, who carried those who remained on two ships and brought them to the Prophet at Khaybar after al-Hudaybiyya, in the year 7 AH. The returnees included Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and his wife Asma bint Umays, and Ibn Ishaq then lists their names clan by clan. A saying transmitted in Sahih Muslim and elsewhere has the Prophet unsure which made him happier: the conquest of Khaybar or Ja'far's arrival. Some had returned earlier in stages, among them Uthman ibn Affan. For the Medinan community the return restored a group of seasoned Companions, and historians treat the event as the end of a story that had lasted about fifteen years and proof that refuge abroad was a practical policy, not an episode.
