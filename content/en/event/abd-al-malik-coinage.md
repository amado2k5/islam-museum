## Overview
al-Baladhuri and al-Suyuti describe how money in Mecca and Medina moved from Byzantine dinars and Persian dirhams to the coinage of Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}

## Before Abd al-Malik
In pre-Islamic times the dinars of Heraclius and the Persian dirhams used to reach the people of Mecca. They traded with them by weight as bullion, and their mithqal was of known weight. The account says that the Prophet approved this, and Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali approved it too, then Mu'awiya. [[person/musab-ibn-al-zubayr|Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr]] struck a few dirhams in the days of Ibn al-Zubayr. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Striking dirhams and dinars
When [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] became caliph he asked about the matter of the dirhams and dinars, and he wrote to [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]] that dirhams should be struck at fifteen qirats. He himself struck Damascene dinars. When they reached Medina, where a number of Companions and Successors were, they raised no objection. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Other accounts
[[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]] was asked who first struck dinars with inscriptions, and he said it was Abd al-Malik. al-Suyuti adds that Abd al-Malik was the first to write, at the head of official registers, the Sura al-Ikhlas together with the mention of the Prophet and the date. The Byzantine king objected and threatened to put on his own dinars what they would dislike. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}

## In Farajallah Ahmad Yusuf's study
Farajallah Ahmad Yusuf dates the real steps of Arabisation to the reign of [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] (65–86 AH), after first signs under Mu'awiya in 41 AH, and traces the stages of [[concept/arab-byzantine-and-arab-sasanian-coinage|Arab-Byzantine and Arab-Sasanian coinage]] to the pure Arab dinars of 77 AH and dirhams of 78. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 20} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 24} Abd al-Malik set up a mint at Damascus. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 23} He was thereby the first to inscribe [[concept/quranic-inscriptions-on-coins|Qur'anic verses on coins]]: [[passage/quran-112-1|Surat al-Ikhlas]] and [[passage/quran-9-33|al-Tawba 33]]. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 24} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 25}

### Reasons for the reform
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Baladhuri, Ibn Khaldun and al-Maqrizi**: the author gathers three reports from the historians: al-Baladhuri's account of the dispute with Emperor Justinian II after Abd al-Malik ordered tawhid formulas written on Egyptian papyri instead of Christian ones, and the emperor threatened to put on his dinars what Muslims would hate about their Prophet; Ibn Khaldun's report that Abd al-Malik headed a letter to Justinian with Surat al-Ikhlas and mention of the Prophet and received the same threat; and al-Maqrizi's report that Khalid ibn Yazid ibn Mu'awiya told him the scholars of the earlier scriptures found that the longest-lived caliph would be the one who sanctified God on his dirham. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 21} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 22}
- **Academic: modern scholars and Farajallah Ahmad Yusuf**: some modern scholars point to a treaty of 70 AH under which Abd al-Malik paid the Byzantines 365,000 dinars bearing Christian emblems; the author holds that the main motive was the caliphate's economic independence from foreign currency, not the quarrel with Justinian, the reform being a step in building the state. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 22} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 23}
:::
