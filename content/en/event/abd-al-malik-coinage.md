## Overview
al-Baladhuri and al-Suyuti describe how money in Mecca and Medina moved from Byzantine dinars and Persian dirhams to the coinage of Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}

## Before Abd al-Malik
In pre-Islamic times the dinars of Heraclius and the Persian dirhams used to reach the people of Mecca. They traded with them by weight as bullion, and their mithqal was of known weight. The account says that the Prophet approved this, and Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali approved it too, then Mu'awiya. [[person/musab-ibn-al-zubayr|Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr]] struck a few dirhams in the days of Ibn al-Zubayr. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Striking dirhams and dinars
When [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] became caliph he asked about the matter of the dirhams and dinars, and he wrote to [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]] that dirhams should be struck at fifteen qirats. He himself struck Damascene dinars. When they reached Medina, where a number of Companions and Successors were, they raised no objection. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Other accounts
[[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab]] was asked who first struck dinars with inscriptions, and he said it was Abd al-Malik. al-Suyuti adds that Abd al-Malik was the first to write, at the head of official registers, the Sura al-Ikhlas together with the mention of the Prophet and the date. The Byzantine king objected and threatened to put on his own dinars what they would dislike. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}
