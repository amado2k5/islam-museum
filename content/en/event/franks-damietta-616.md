## Overview
Al-Suyuti says that the Franks took the Chain Tower of [[place/dimyat|Damietta]] in 615 AH, then took Damietta itself in 616 AH. [[person/al-malik-al-kamil|Al-Malik al-Kamil]] was too weak to resist them, so he founded a city at the meeting of the two seas and named it al-Mansura. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.696}

## The tower
Al-Suyuti, quoting Abu Shama, says the tower was "the lock of the land of Egypt", a tall tower in the middle of the Nile from which two chains extended to stop ships from the salt sea. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.696}

## Ibn al-Athir's account
According to Ibn al-Athir, the Franks fought the tower for four months until they held it. Then al-Kamil left his position by night, when the emirs agreed to depose him, so the army lost its sultan and left behind the provisions and weapons. The Franks crossed the Nile on the twentieth of Dhu al-Qa'da 615 AH. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.376-377}

The Franks surrounded Damietta, and its people endured until those who remained were too few to hold, and they surrendered it under safe conduct on the twenty-seventh of Sha'ban 616 AH. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.376-377}

## The account of the Fragments of Gold
The "Fragments of Gold" says that the Franks left Damietta at the time of the Nile's flood, that the Muslims let the Nile flood over them, and that they asked for peace in return for handing Damietta over to al-Kamil. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.79}

## Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's account
In [[work/mirat-al-zaman|Mir'at al-zaman]], [[person/sibt-ibn-al-jawzi|Sibt ibn al-Jawzi]] follows the siege year by year; he was in Damascus in the service of [[person/al-malik-al-muazzam|al-Mu'azzam]].

### The landing and the chain tower
The Franks landed before Damietta in Rabi' I 615. Al-Adil, at Marj al-Suffar, sent troops to al-Kamil, and al-Mu'azzam stayed on the coast facing the Franks. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.227} At the end of Jumada I the Franks took the chain tower; al-Adil fell ill at the news and died a few days later, as told in [[event/death-al-adil-615|the death of al-Adil]]. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.228} The Franks then camped at Sharmsah; the Muslims abandoned their tents to them and they grew bold, but al-Kamil turned on them, broke them and they went back to Damietta. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.233}

### The conspiracy of Ibn al-Mashtub
Under 616 AH Sibt ibn al-Jawzi tells of Imad al-Din ibn al-Mashtub, who conspired with al-Fa'iz ibn al-Adil against al-Kamil and made the troops swear to them. Al-Kamil withdrew to Ashmun and resolved to go to Yemen. Al-Mu'azzam told him, "No harm will come to you", rode to Ibn al-Mashtub's tent and called him out, led him away from the army and sent him off to al-Ashraf with five hundred dinars, sending his baggage and slaves after him; then al-Kamil came and kissed the ground before him. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.237} Ibn al-Athir records the same episode under 615 AH. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.376-377}

### The fall
In Sha'ban 616 the Franks took Damietta. Al-Mu'azzam had sent al-Nahid ibn al-Jarkhi to it with five hundred foot soldiers; they stormed the trenches and he and his men were killed. The people of Damietta grew weak and ate carrion, al-Kamil could not relieve them, and plague broke out among them; they negotiated with the Franks to hand over the town and leave with their families and property, and made the priests swear to it. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.238} When the gates were opened, Sibt ibn al-Jawzi says, the Franks "broke faith with the people of Damietta", killing and taking captives; they took the minbar, the copies of the Qur'an and the heads of the dead and sent them to the islands, and made the mosque a church. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.238} They asked about Abu al-Hasan ibn Qufl and were told he was a holy man with whom the poor took refuge, so they left him alone. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.238}

Sibt ibn al-Jawzi says al-Kamil and al-Mu'azzam wept bitterly, and that al-Kamil told al-Mu'azzam there was no point in his staying, and that it would be better for him to go down to Syria to distract the Franks and bring troops from the east. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.238} He also reports al-Mu'azzam's own explanation of the disaster — al-Mu'azzam's view, not the author's — that what befell the people of Damietta was because of their many sins. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.238}

### The call to arms in Damascus
Al-Mu'azzam wrote to Sibt ibn al-Jawzi in his own hand asking him to rouse the people to jihad. He said he had surveyed the estates of Syria and found two thousand villages, sixteen hundred of them owned by their people and four hundred belonging to the sultan, and wanted the Damascenes to come out to defend their property. Sibt ibn al-Jawzi read the letter in the Great Mosque of Damascus; the people promised to obey, then held back, "and their holding back was the reason he took an eighth and a fifth from them". {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.239} Sibt ibn al-Jawzi then went out to the coast, where al-Mu'azzam was besieging [[place/qaysariyya|Caesarea]]; they stayed until he took it by storm, then went on to "the fortress" (al-thaghr), which he took and demolished. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.239}

### Between the accounts
The accounts agree that Damietta was surrendered on terms in Sha'ban 616: Ibn al-Athir gives the 27th, Sibt ibn al-Jawzi the month; Sibt ibn al-Jawzi adds that the Franks broke the terms, which Ibn al-Athir does not mention at this point. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.376-377} {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.238} The Ibn al-Mashtub episode falls in 615 in Ibn al-Athir and in 616 in Sibt ibn al-Jawzi. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.376-377} {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.237}
