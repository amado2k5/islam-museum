## Overview
"Then you will see me no far-off one, but one like yourselves in your midst, striking your skulls with a keen blade whose ring is heard when it prevails" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.305}.

## Detail
He says that he charges the tyrants he names in an ambush, with Yemeni tribes about him and around him the believers of high station while around them are the rabble and the ignorant, so that the Lord of the Throne helps his religion and the apostates turn back "and defended none for honour", and in captivity there is wailing for them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.305}. Then: "and I judge by the Book of my Lord" and "decide by the Sunna of the Chosen One, my grandfather" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.305}. On p. 306: "tyranny is found with its bonds torn", the right becomes bright and clear, the Majestic is pleased after His wrath, the people return together to justice, widows and orphans are filled, the naked clothed, fines and debts paid for them and their spoils divided among them all, and God is pleased, "none is His equal" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.306}. The opponents in these verses are described in terms of abuse that the museum does not repeat. It is a poetic promise and hope ascribed to al-Hadi in a Zaydi book, not a report of an event and no verdict of the museum, and the book does not say here who the enemies are. After it on p. 306 come "and also of his" lines beginning "O man of firm mind, brother of guidance", which belong to the next batch. It follows [[event/al-hadi-warns-the-unjust-governors-in-verse-and-names-the-yemeni-regiments-292|his verses to the unjust governors]]. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
