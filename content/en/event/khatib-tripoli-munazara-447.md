## Summary
On his way back from the hajj, about 446–447, he entered Tripoli and held a disputation in the art of preaching with the qadi al-Husayn ibn Bishr, holder of its Dar al-'Ilm, recorded by al-Karajaki in his rihla {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 93}.



## The exchange — what each side said
- **The recorded report**: al-Khatib al-Baghdadi entered Tripoli on his way back from the Hajj around 446–447 AH, and held a disputation in rhetoric (al-khitaba) with the judge Husayn ibn Bashir, master of the city's Dar al-Ilm; al-Karajiki recorded it in his travelogue. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 93}
- **What the text preserves**: the debate's subject — rhetoric — its venue — the Dar al-Ilm of Tripoli — the second party and his standing — the judge who headed the house — and the transmitter — al-Karajiki in his journey.
- **What is not preserved**: the wording of the exchange is not transmitted in the source read here; the actual arguments — what al-Khatib put and what the judge answered — are unrecorded in the report before us, and the full text would require al-Karajiki's travelogue itself.
- **The report's value**: it nonetheless establishes the encounter, its subject, venue and date — a travelling scholar debating the city's judge in its own house of learning, the picture of the itinerant scholarly disputation of the fifth century. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 93}
