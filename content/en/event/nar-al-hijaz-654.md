## Summary
In Jumada al-Akhira 654 AH a great earthquake struck, and then a fire broke out in the land of [[place/medina|Medina]]. al-Samhudi linked it to the Prophet's report of a fire that would come out of the land of the Hijaz, lighting up the necks of camels in Busra. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.154-156}

## The earthquake
The earthquake began at the start of Jumada al-Akhira and grew stronger on the night of Wednesday, the third of the month. al-Samhudi, quoting al-Qastallani, says that in a single day it reached eighteen tremors. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.155}

## The fire
The fire appeared at Quraydha, at the edge of al-Harra. The source describes it as flowing like a river, red and blue, with a roar like thunder that shook the mountains. It is said it was seen from Mecca and from the mountains of Busra. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.154-156}

Although it was close to Medina, a cool breeze would reach people from it, until it was turned away to the left when it reached the sacred precinct. The barrier it formed is known to the people of Medina as al-Habs. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.154-156}

## The earthquake and the judge of Medina's testimony in al-Samhudi
[[person/al-samhudi|Al-Samhudi]] devotes to this fire the sixteenth section of chapter two of [[work/wafa-al-wafa|Wafa' al-Wafa]]. He first gathers the hadith reports of a fire coming out of the land of the Hijaz that would light up the necks of camels at Busra, from Ahmad's *Musnad*, the two *Sahih*s, al-Tabarani and others, citing hadith critics' verdicts on some chains by name, such as al-Haythami on the report of Rafi' ibn Bishr al-Sulami. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.151} {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.152} {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.153} He says the appearance of this fire at Medina became so widely known that it reached the level of *tawatur*. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.154}

He then dates its beginning: the earthquakes in Medina began at the start of Jumada al-Akhira, or the end of Jumada al-Ula, 654 AH, slight at first, growing strong on the Tuesday as al-Qutb al-Qastallani relates; then on the night of Wednesday the third or fourth of the month, in the last third of the night, came a great earthquake that went on until Friday, with eighteen tremors in a single day. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.154} {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.155} He quotes Abu Shama's account of the letter of al-Sharif Sinan, judge of Medina, that a tremor struck while they were around the Chamber, so that the pulpit shook until its iron could be heard and the lamps of the sanctuary swayed. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.155} At midday on the Friday the fire appeared; dense smoke rose from it and covered the horizon, and when night fell its glow shone out like a great city to the east. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.156}

He relates from al-Qadi Sinan that he went up to the amir of Medina, Izz al-Din Munif ibn Shiha, and said to him: "Punishment has surrounded us; turn back to God"; the amir freed all his slaves and returned what had been wrongly taken from people, and, al-Qashani adds, abolished the market tax; then the amir and all the people of Medina, even women and children, spent the nights of Friday and Saturday in the Prophet's Mosque, praying around the Chamber. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.156} These are the accounts of the authors al-Samhudi names.
