## Summary
The siege against Uthman intensified until he was prevented from praying in the mosque. Ali offered to defend him with five hundred armored men, to which Uthman replied: "May you be rewarded with good; I do not wish blood to be shed on my account." When the rebels cut off water from Uthman, Ali sent him three waterskins {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 166}.

## Forms of Defense
al-Sallabi states that senior Companions sent their sons to the residence without consulting Uthman, among them al-Hasan ibn Ali and Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, and that al-Hasan was wounded on the day of the residence {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 166}. Ali sent water, and men from Banu Hashim and Banu Umayyah were wounded because of it before it could reach him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 166}.

## After the Killing
When news of the killing reached the Companions in the mosque, Ali said to the sons of the Companions: "How was Uthman killed while you were at the door?" He struck al-Hasan, went out angry to his home, and said: "O God, I declare myself innocent before You of his blood, of having killed him or abetted his killing" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 166-167}. This stance is read in the book as evidence that Ali was not party to the killing and that he defended the caliph as much as he was able {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 167}.

## Marwan's Testimony
The book cites the testimony of Marwan ibn al-Hakam—who later became one of Ali's fiercest opponents—that Ali was the foremost defender of Uthman {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 177}. al-Sallabi cites this testimony to show that the narrative attributing collusion with the rebels to Ali has no basis {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 177}.
