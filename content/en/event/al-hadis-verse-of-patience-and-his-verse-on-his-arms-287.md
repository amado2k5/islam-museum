## Overview
After the fight [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] composed verse. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.192} In the first he speaks of the times growing hard on him, says he does not bow though all people bowed, that the noble man is resolute and does not wail, that he is a victorious match for fate who neither yields to it nor falters, that fate was patient with him while he was patient with it until weariness showed in it, and that his patience is firm and renewed; he says God is his Lord and the Prophet his grandfather, and that God keeps him and wards off harm. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.192} In the second he says God suffices him, with his intent and his insight, and describes a lance, a tempered sword, a coat of mail like a pool and a strong horse, a steadfast seat and a heart that does not take fright. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.193}

## Detail
He then says that, God the Merciful helping, he may tread the land of his opponents with the hooves of his horses and break their gatherings with squadrons of horse, swords and lances, and break the fort of the foolish, who by his description betrayed God and set aside His rulings and in whom lies a standing wickedness, and strike a blow in which heads are cut and noses slit, so that they be repaid for what they sent ahead, like for like. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.193} These are the imam's own lines describing and threatening his opponents, so their sense is given and the judgement is his; the museum quotes no line and does not adopt his description of them. The mail passage names Tadmur, David and Tubba' as poetic comparison. The order of the lines is disturbed in the OCR, so only the sense is taken. It follows [[event/al-hadi-charges-the-akilis-at-al-sabra-and-the-fourteen-arrows-287]] and is followed by [[event/raid-on-afqayn-and-the-return-of-the-fifth-to-the-mahadhir-287]].
