## Overview
When the letter reached Banu al-Harith they gathered and took counsel, with Ibn Bustam among them, who urged disorder, and their opinion agreed on it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.281}. [[person/ibn-humayd-of-banu-al-harith-286|Ibn Humayd]] sent that same hour to Yam, and Ibn Bustam went back to the village to Abu Ja'far {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.281}.

## Detail
Banu al-Harith and Yam marched on Abu Ja'far that same hour, and he came out to them with his servants, having no force with him except the Madanis, that is the men of Banu Abd al-Madan as the context shows; they fought the people an hour, then Abu Ja'far charged and drove them to the side of the hill {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.281}. The book does not name the village where Abu Ja'far lived on this page; the context points to Najran, as in the earlier reports. What is told is the narrator's account in a Zaydi book, and the museum attributes no motive to anyone and gives no verdict. The OCR reads "al-Daniyyun" and the page image "al-Madaniyyun"; the second is followed.
