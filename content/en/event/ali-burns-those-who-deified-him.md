## Summary
al-Sallabi reports that a group of people stood at the gate of the mosque of Kufa claiming that Ali was their Lord; he summoned them and rejected this, then gave them three days to repent, and when they persisted, he kindled a fire and burned them {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 243}. In the book, the report is paired with Ibn Abbas's objection, Ibn Taymiyyah's statement, and the Companions' disagreement over burning as a punishment in general {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 244-245}.

## The Report
Ali was told: "There is a group at the gate of the mosque claiming that you are their Lord" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 243}. He summoned them and said: "Woe to you! I am only a servant like you; I eat food as you eat and drink as you drink. If I obey God, He will reward me, and if I disobey Him, I fear He will punish me" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 243}. They refused to recant. The next day they returned saying the same thing, so he ordered them brought in and they affirmed the same statement. On the third day he said: "If you say this again, I will surely kill you in the worst manner" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 243}. The book states that they insisted on persisting, so he threatened to burn them with fire, kindled it, and recited: "When I see a matter that is an abomination, I kindle my fire and call Qanbar" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 243}. The report includes lines of poetry not quoted here.

## al-Bukhari's Narration and Ibn Abbas's Objection
The book notes that al-Bukhari recorded the burning through the hadith of Ikrimah: heretics were brought to Ali and he burned them; when news reached Ibn Abbas, he said: "Had it been I, I would not have burned them, because of the Messenger of God's prohibition: 'Do not punish with God's punishment,' and I would have killed them because of His Messenger's saying: 'Whoever changes his religion, kill him'" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 244}. Ibn Hajar commented that Ibn Abbas may have heard the prohibition directly from the Prophet or from some of the Companions, and that "wayh" may be an expression of compassion, praise, or astonishment {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 244}.

## Scholars' Statements on Burning
The book quotes Ibn Taymiyyah as saying that "it is established from him that he burned the majority of the Rafidah who believed in his divinity" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 244}. It quotes Ibn Hajar that the early generations differed over burning: Umar, Ibn Abbas, and others disliked it altogether, while Ali, Khalid ibn al-Walid, and others permitted it; al-Muhallab held that the prohibition was out of humility rather than absolute forbiddance, and that the practice of the Companions indicates permissibility subject to restrictions mentioned in the book {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 244}. It also quotes Ibn al-Qayyim that Abu Bakr burned those guilty of sodomy, and that Khalid ibn al-Walid wrote to him regarding a man who was penetrated like a woman, so he consulted the Companions, and Ali was the strongest in advocating burning, whereupon Abu Bakr wrote ordering him to be burned {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 244-245}.

## Notes for Researchers
