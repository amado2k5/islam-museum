## Summary
[[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud]] was among the earliest Muslims and the Companion closest to the Prophet's recitation. Al-Dhahabi's Siyar preserves a short text of his written testament, a report of Uthman's visit in his illness, reports of the wealth he left, and his death in Medina and burial in al-Baqi'. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443}

## The testament and what it said
Al-Dhahabi relates through Waki' from Abu Umays from Amir ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr that Ibn Mas'ud made a will and wrote: my testament is to God and to al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam and to his son Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr; both are free of blame for what they decide in my estate; and no woman of mine is to be married except with their leave. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443} There are three clauses: the appointment of two executors, [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] and his son [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abd Allah]]; their discharge from liability for their decisions; and the condition that the women in his charge be married only with their consent. Yahya al-Himmani relates through Sharik from Abu Ishaq that Ibn Mas'ud also charged al-Zubayr with praying over him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443}

## His illness and Uthman's visit
Al-Dhahabi relates from al-Sari ibn Yahya, from Abu Shuja', from Abu Tayba, that Abd Allah fell ill and [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] visited him and asked: what do you complain of? He said: my sins. And what do you desire? The mercy of my Lord. Shall I send you a physician? The Physician made me ill. Shall I order you a stipend? I have no need of it. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443} Al-Dhahabi notes that the narrators differ on its chain: Ibn Wahb reports it from Shuja', while Uthman ibn Yaman and Hajjaj ibn Nusayr report it from Shuja' from Abu Fatima. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443} A saying attributed to him about death is: how welcome the two disliked things, death and poverty. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2531}

## What he left
Al-Dhahabi quotes Sayf ibn Umar, from Atiyya, from Abu Sayf, that Ibn Mas'ud gave up his stipend when Umar died, as did some wealthy men of Kufa, and acquired for himself an estate at Radhan, so that he died leaving ninety thousand mithqals besides slaves, goods and livestock. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 442} After his death al-Zubayr went in to Uthman and said: give me Abd Allah's stipend, for his dependants have more right to it than the treasury; Uthman gave him fifteen thousand. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443} Hafs ibn Ghiyath relates from Hisham ibn Urwa from his father that Uthman had withheld his stipend for two years. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443}

## Burial and prayer
Al-Dhahabi relates from Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah that Ibn Mas'ud died in Medina and was buried in al-Baqi' in 32 AH; he was slight, short and very dark. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 444} Ibn Abd al-Barr likewise gives his death in Medina in 32, and says that when the news reached Abu al-Darda he said: he has left behind none like him. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 410} Awn ibn Abd Allah and others put his age at a little over sixty; Yahya ibn Abi Utba says sixty-three. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 444}

## Where the reports differ
There are two dates: 32, reported by Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah and followed by a number of authorities, and 33, given by Yahya ibn Abi Utba and Yahya ibn Bukayr; al-Dhahabi thinks he may have died early in 33, and some say three years before Uthman. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 444} On the stipend there are two opposed reports: that he renounced it after Umar's death, and that Uthman withheld it for two years. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 442} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443} Al-Dhahabi adds that Ibn Mas'ud had come to Uthman and on the way witnessed Abu Dharr's death at al-Rabadha and prayed over him; see [[event/death-of-abu-dharr-at-al-rabadha|The death of Abu Dharr at al-Rabadha]]. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 2 p. 443}

## Sources and documentation
The card uses two scans of al-Dhahabi's Siyar a'lam al-nubala', one of them clearer, and Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Isti'ab. The text of Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat was not available in the scans, so the details Ibn Sa'd gives of the shroud and of his daughters are not recorded here; none of the books read gives them, and the exact spot of the grave within al-Baqi' is not stated either.
