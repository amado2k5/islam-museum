## Overview
Al-Hadi also said some lines which begin: you were struck, sons of al-Harith ibn Ka'b, by a chief {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.285}. They are ascribed to al-Hadi in this Zaydi book and the museum gives their sense.

## Detail
The poet says of himself, as the lines have it, that he is neither heedless nor given to sleep, and bold when the lances cross; that he called them to guidance and they disliked it, called them to the judgement of the Qur'an and walked the straight path, defending Islam on the way of his two grandfathers; that they had meddled by his side and were the remnant of his notched sword, and repaid his favours to them with ugliness and betrayal after betrayal, for betrayal belongs to the wrongdoer {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.285}. He says he lifted deaths from them on the day they hovered over them and sheathed his sword out of generosity, bounty and a forbearance that cannot be set beside another's, even from the killing they saw, when they stood at the brink of a vast terror {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.285}. The page ends at this point in the poem, and the book may carry it on over p. 286, to be read in the next batch; it follows the end of the poem of [[event/al-hadi-answers-and-sends-a-poem-to-banu-al-harith-292]]. The museum does not copy the lines or repeat their abuse, and gives no judgement for or against what the poet claims.
