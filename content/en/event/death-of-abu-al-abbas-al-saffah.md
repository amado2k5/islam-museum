## Overview
al-Tabari reports the death of Abu al-Abbas al-Saffah and the length of his caliphate, in the events of 136 AH. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.470} In that year Abu Muslim came to him after the season had ended. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.7 p.470}

## al-Suyuti's account
He died of smallpox in Dhu al-Hijja 136 AH, and had designated his brother Abu Ja'far as successor. In 134 AH he had moved to al-Anbar and made it the seat of the caliphate. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 419}

## In al-Ya'qubi
[[person/al-yaqubi|Al-Ya'qubi]] reports from Isa ibn Ali that when Abu al-Abbas's illness grew worse and two delegations, from Sind and from Ifriqiya, reached him on the same day, he said he would die within three days, citing a report handed down in his family. He then wrote a letter of succession and charged his uncle to keep his death secret until the letter had been read to the people and the oath taken to the man named in it, and only then to prepare his body and pray over him. {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 435} {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 436}
He fell ill that night and died on Sunday, 12 Dhu al-Hijja, aged thirty-six, or younger by another report, having been born in the days of Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik. Isma'il ibn Ali, or by another report Isa ibn Ali, led the funeral prayer; he was buried at al-Anbar in his palace. His reign lasted four years and nine months, and he left a son not yet of age and a daughter, Rayta, the wife of al-Mahdi. {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 436}
