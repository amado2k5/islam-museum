## Summary
[[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik ibn Anas]] died at Medina in 179 AH and was buried at al-Baqi'. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166} Al-Dhahabi's Siyar and Tarikh al-Islam bring together reports of his last days, his words at death, his washing and shroud, who prayed over him and what he left; most are taken from Ibn Sa'd and al-Qadi Iyad, so they are second-hand.

## His illness and last words
Isma'il ibn Abi Uways relates that Malik fell ill and that, when he asked some of the family what he said at death, they said he pronounced the testimony of faith and then recited a verse of the sura of the Romans on God's sovereign command over what has been and what will be. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} It can be consulted at [[passage/quran-30-4|Qur'an 30:4]]. Sifat al-Safwa says he was ill for a few days. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 361} Al-Dhahabi quotes al-Qadi Iyad that he died on a Sunday after twenty-two days of illness. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165}

## Death and its date
The books report that he died on the morning of 14 Rabi' al-Awwal 179 AH, which is Ibn Sa'd's account from Isma'il ibn Abi Uways. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} Ibn Sa'd asked Mus'ab al-Zubayri, who said he died in Safar, and Ma'n told him the same. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} Abu Mus'ab al-Zuhri says ten nights into Rabi' al-Awwal, Ibn Sahnun the eleventh, and Ibn Wahb thirteen nights into it. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 14 p. 332} Al-Dhahabi says the report that he died in 179 is mass-transmitted, so no weight is given to those who put it in 178, or to what is told from his secretary Habib and from Mutarrif that it was 180. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165}

## His instruction about his shroud, and the prayer
Al-Dhahabi quotes al-Qadi Iyad that Malik asked to be shrouded in white garments and prayed over at the place of funerals. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} The governor of Medina, Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Abbas al-Hashimi, son of Zaynab bint Sulayman al-Abbasiyya and known by his mother's name, deputy for his father at Medina, prayed over him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} The report adds that the governor walked in front of the bier and carried the bier, and that the shroud cost five dinars. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} Al-Dhahabi gives in his Tarikh al-Islam that the same governor prayed over him. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi scan vol. 14 p. 332}

## Washing
Al-Dhahabi relates from al-Qadi Iyad that Ibn Abi Zanbar and Ibn Kinana washed Malik, with his son Yahya and his secretary Habib pouring the water, and that a group went down into his grave. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165}

## What he left
Ibn al-Qasim says Malik died leaving a hundred turbans, besides other things. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166} Ibn Abi Uways says the carpets, platforms, cushions and the like in his uncle's house were sold for more than five hundred dinars. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166} Muhammad ibn Isa ibn Khalaf says he left five hundred pairs of sandals and, having once wished for a Qus cloak, died with seven that had been sent to him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166} Al-Dhahabi relates from Abu Amr that he left 2,627 dinars in coin and a thousand dirhams. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166} Al-Dhahabi remarks that Malik was a man of standing and means, with dignity, servants and a fine house, who accepted gifts, ate good food and did good works. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166}

## The grave
Al-Dhahabi says: he was buried at al-Baqi' by agreement, and his grave is well known and visited. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166} Sifat al-Safwa says he was buried at al-Baqi' at the age of eighty-five. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 361} Al-Baqi' is [[place/baqi-al-gharqad|Baqi' al-Gharqad]], Medina's cemetery. Al-Dhahabi relates that on the night he died a man of the Ansar heard someone reciting verses of lament, and when he woke the wailing was for Malik. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3166} The readings consulted give no location for the grave within al-Baqi'.

## Where the reports differ
His age: eighty-five for Ibn Nafi' al-Sa'igh, Ibn Abi Uways, Ibn Sa'd and Habib; eighty-four by one report; eighty-seven by another; ninety for al-Waqidi; eighty-six for al-Firyabi and Abu Mus'ab; eighty-nine for al-Qa'nabi; eighty-seven for Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim; and ninety-two for Ayyub ibn Salih, which al-Dhahabi treats as an outlier. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} Iyad relates from Asad ibn Musa that he saw Malik after his death on a camel flying between heaven and earth, saying he had come before his Lord who spoke to him directly. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3165} This is a dream report of the kind found in the biographical praise literature, and the museum gives it under its narrator.
