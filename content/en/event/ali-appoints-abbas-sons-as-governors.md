## Summary
Ali ibn Abi Talib appointed a number of the sons of his paternal uncle al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib over several provinces: Abd Allah ibn Abbas over Basra, Ubayd Allah over Yemen and Bahrain, Qutham over Mecca and Ta'if, and Tammam over Medina, along with Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, Ali's stepson, over Egypt {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 358}. al-Sallabi presents these appointments in the context of a list of forty-one governors during Ali's caliphate {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}.

## The List and the Sons of al-Abbas
The list names the governors by province, including Abd Allah ibn Abbas over Basra, Ubayd Allah ibn Abbas over Yemen and Bahrain, Qutham ibn al-Abbas over Mecca and Ta'if, Tammam ibn al-Abbas over Medina, and Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr over Egypt {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 358}. The book states that a number of the governors were from the Ansar and that four were sons of al-Abbas; I did not find a single passage that settles the exact number of governors definitively, so no specific count is adopted here {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}. The list names Sahl ibn Hunayf as governor of Medina in its first entry {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}, whereas the book mentions him in an earlier passage as governor of Syria {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 356}; this is an internal contradiction in the book that requires verification in the source before being relied upon.

## The Argument: Competence, Not Kinship
The book states that Ali appointed his cousins because of their competence, just as Uthman promoted those of his relatives whose competence was established, and that neither Uthman nor Ali promoted relatives on the basis of kinship alone, arguing that conditions in the provinces required careful selection {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}. It holds that Umar established the precedent of avoiding the promotion of close relatives, and that Uthman and Ali then set the precedent of appointing relatives when they were qualified {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}.

## Appointment of Umayyads During Uthman's Reign
The book compares the two cases: it notes that Uthman appointed five Umayyads out of eighteen governors, then dismissed al-Walid ibn Uqbah and Sa'id ibn al-As from Kufa, and holds that dismissing them from Kufa—which was never satisfied with any governor—cannot be counted as a criticism of them {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}. The book adds that some of Uthman's relatives had already held governorships earlier during the reigns of Abu Bakr and Umar {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}.

## Notes
- The book does not provide dates for every appointment; the timeframe here is derived from Ali's caliphate, so its reliability is low.
- Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr's status as Ali's stepson is mentioned by the book in the same passage, without giving details of his governorship in Egypt on this page.
- The book states that "some contemporary writers" accused Ali and Uthman of favoring relatives, without naming them on the page {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 357}.

## Author's Commentary
al-Sallabi holds that judgment on these appointments should look to competence and righteousness, and that accusations of favoritism are exaggerated in some contemporary writings, presenting this as his opinion based on the reports he cited {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}.
