After the Prophet gave his Companions leave to emigrate to Yathrib in the wake of the second Pledge of al-Aqaba — their departures coming in successive parties until only the Prophet, Abu Bakr, Ali and the powerless detainees remained in Mecca — Quraysh sensed the great danger of an impregnable seat for Islam rising on its caravan road to Syria. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

On the morning of Thursday 26 Safar of year fourteen of the prophethood (12 September 622 CE), Mecca's parliament held its most fateful session in Dar al-Nadwa, attended by delegates of all the Qurayshi tribes, to devise a decisive plan to destroy the Prophet. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Abu al-Aswad proposed banishing and expelling him; the people rejected it — he might win over other peoples and invade them with them. Abu al-Bakhtari proposed imprisoning and binding him; rejected — his companions would storm to rescue him. Then [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]] proposed that from every tribe a well-born, hardy youth be taken, each given a sharp sword, so they strike him the blow of a single man and his blood scatter among all the tribes — Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib could not war on all Quraysh and would accept the blood-money. They approved his counsel and agreed on it; concerning it came down God's word: "when the disbelievers plotted against you, to bind you or kill you or cast you out." {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
