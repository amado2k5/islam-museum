## Overview
On p. 277 al-Hadi tells Banu al-Harith to take their preparations, settle their affair and carry out their intent, and then come on to him without a delay, for a force of God's friends and party had come upon them that they could not withstand; their covenant was cast aside, their safety broken and the fire of war kindled {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.277} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.278}.

## Detail
He says he was glad to spend some of the funds he had been keeping ready for such a matter from them and others, then gives the parable of the gnat that lit on the palm: when it wished to fly it told the palm to hold fast, and the palm answered that it had not felt it land and would not know when it flew, and so, he says, are they to him, their scheme neither troubling nor burdening him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.278}. He says they are in decline and he in increase, that his armies are many, his horses numerous and his money complete, and that all of Yemen but a little of it obeys him, sending him its money and gathering its men and horsemen to his help, which he ascribes to God's favour and not to himself {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.278}. He ends that God is enough for him and that he trusts in Him, with a reference to [[passage/quran-26-227|ash-Shu'ara 26:227]] in the page note and a prayer for the Prophet and his family. The numbers and claims are al-Hadi's in a Zaydi book and are not affirmed by the museum. Manuscript S reads "some of our money" in a note and corrects two words of the base text.
