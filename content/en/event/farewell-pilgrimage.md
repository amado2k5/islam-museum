## Overview
The Farewell Pilgrimage was the only pilgrimage the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] performed after the Hijra, in the tenth year of the Hijra. In it he showed the people their rites and delivered his well-known sermon. It was called the "Pilgrimage of Proclamation" and the "Farewell Pilgrimage", because he did not perform the pilgrimage again after it. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/253} This page relays what Ibn Hisham relates from Ibn Ishaq (part 4, pp. 248–253), and for the text of the sermon refers to its own page, [[event/farewell-sermon-at-arafat|the Farewell Sermon at Arafat]].

## Leaving Medina
Ibn Ishaq says that when Dhu al-Qa'da began he prepared for the pilgrimage and ordered the people to make ready for him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/248} Aisha relates that he set out "five nights before the end of Dhu al-Qa'da". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/248} Ibn Hisham says that he appointed Abu Dujana al-Sa'idi over Medina, and it is said Siba ibn Urfuta al-Ghifari. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/248}

## At Sarif: going into the state of ihram for the umra
Aisha says that they had mentioned only the pilgrimage until, when they were at Sarif, and the Prophet had brought the offering animals with him along with a group of notable men, he ordered the people to exit the state of ihram by making the umra, except those who had brought offering animals. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/248} [[person/aisha|Aisha]] had her period that day, and he came in to her while she was weeping, and told her that she was doing everything the pilgrim does except that she would not circle the House. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/248} After entering Mecca, whoever had no offering animal came out of ihram, and his wives came out of it through the umra, and on the day of sacrifice he sacrificed cattle on behalf of his wives. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/248} On the night of al-Hasba he ordered her brother Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr to make the umra for her from al-Tan'im in place of the umra she had missed. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/249} [[person/hafsa|Hafsa]] relates that his wives asked him why he did not come out of ihram with them, and he said that he had brought an offering animal and had put on the garland, so he would not come out until he had sacrificed his offering. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/249}

## Ali on the pilgrimage
The Prophet had sent [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] to Najran to collect the alms and the poll tax {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/247}, and Ali met him in Mecca when he was in ihram. Ibn Ishaq relates from Ibn Abi Najih that Ali went in to [[person/fatima|Fatima]] and found that she had come out of ihram, so the Prophet ordered him to circle the House and come out of ihram as his companions had done. Ali said that he had said when he entered ihram: "O God, I enter ihram as your prophet entered it." The Prophet asked him whether he had an offering animal. He said no, so the Prophet made him a partner in his offering, and he kept to his ihram until they had both finished the pilgrimage, and the Prophet sacrificed the offering on behalf of both. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/249}

Ibn Ishaq relates from Yazid ibn Talha ibn Rukana that Ali had hurried to the Prophet from Yemen, and had left a man over his troops who had clothed each of them with a garment from the cloth that he had with him. When Ali saw them and saw the garments, he ordered them taken off before he reached the Prophet, so the army complained of him. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri relates that the Prophet stood among them and said: "O people, do not complain of Ali, for by God he is more rough in the cause of God, or in the path of God, than to be complained of." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/250} Ibn Hisham does not mention the account of [[event/ghadir-khumm|Ghadir Khumm]] on these pages, and the dispute over its meaning has its place on its own page.

## The sermon
Then the Prophet went on with his pilgrimage, "showing the people their rites and teaching them the ways of their pilgrimage", and delivered his sermon to the people. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/250} Ibn Ishaq gives its text. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/250}-{cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/252} In it are the prohibition of blood and property, the abolition of usury, the waiving of the blood-claim of the son of Rabi'a ibn al-Harith, the prohibition of the intercalation of months and the turning of time, the charge concerning women, and his saying that he left among them what, if they held fast to it, they would never go astray: the Book of God and the Sunna of His Prophet. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/251} See the sermon on [[event/farewell-sermon-at-arafat|its own page]] and in [[work/farewell-sermon|the work attributed to it]].

The man who shouted the words of the Prophet and repeated them to the people at Arafat was [[person/rabia-ibn-umayya-ibn-khalaf|Rabi'a ibn Umayya ibn Khalaf]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/252} Amr ibn Kharija relates that he heard the Prophet from beneath his she-camel saying that God has given every person with a right his right, that "there is no bequest to an heir", and he said: "The child belongs to the bed, and the adulterer gets the stone." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/252}-{cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/253}

## Teaching the rites
The Prophet said at Arafat: "This is the standing place" at the mountain on which he stood, "and all of Arafat is a standing place", and he said the same at Muzdalifa at Quzah and at Mina at the place of sacrifice. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/253} Then he finished the pilgrimage, having shown them their rites and taught them what God had made obligatory on them: the standing, the stoning of the pillars, circling the House, what was made lawful to them of their pilgrimage and what was made unlawful to them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/253}

## After the return
He turned back and stayed in Medina for the rest of Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram and Safar, and he called up people for an expedition to Syria, over which he set [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama ibn Zayd]] as commander. See [[event/expedition-of-usama|the expedition of Usama]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/253} Then Ibn Hisham mentions his sending of envoys to the kings. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/254}

## The pilgrimage of the tenth year in the Encyclopaedia of Islam
The article on the pilgrimage in the second edition of the Encyclopaedia of Islam, in the part written by A. J. Wensinck, places the Farewell Pilgrimage in the tenth year of the Hijra, when Muhammad himself led the [[practice/hajj|hajj]]. It notes that tradition has much to tell about this "so-called farewell pilgrimage", and that the reports of the rites he performed agree in essentials with later practice. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 42} This is the encyclopaedia's summary, set beside the Muslim reports above and not in place of them.

The article puts the pilgrimage at the end of a sequence that it draws from the work of Snouck Hurgronje. This is an academic reconstruction, and the Muslim sources reported on this page do not frame events in this way. In it, Muhammad's interest in the pilgrimage was first aroused in Medina: the success at Badr turned his thoughts to Mecca, the disagreement with the Jews of Medina led to the doctrine of the religion of Abraham, and the Kaaba, said to have been built by Abraham and Ishmael, moved into the centre of worship and became the direction of prayer. After the unsuccessful siege of Medina by the Meccans in the fifth year, the expedition to [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|Hudaybiyya]] ended in a treaty with Quraysh that opened the prospect of a visit the following year. In the seventh year he instituted the ceremonies at the Kaaba. After [[event/conquest-of-mecca|the conquest of Mecca]] in the eighth year the festival could be celebrated in public, yet in the ninth year he sent Abu Bakr to lead the pilgrim caravan, as told on the page [[event/hajj-led-by-abu-bakr|the hajj led by Abu Bakr]]. Ali overtook the caravan to read out to the pagan pilgrims a declaration that, in the article's summary, forbade unbelievers to perform the pilgrimage, except those with whom the Prophet had made special treaties. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 42}

## The end of intercalation and the lunar year
Among the arrangements made on this occasion, the article names the abolition of intercalation, [[concept/nasi|nasi']], and the introduction of a purely lunar year, and calls them important for the history of the pilgrimage. It connects them with a statement in the ninth sura about the number of months and the sacred months. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 42} The Muslim sources reported above also list the prohibition of the intercalation of months among the subjects of the sermon. As the article explains the result, the pilgrimage takes place in the first two weeks of Dhu al-Hijja, and because the [[concept/hijri-calendar|lunar calendar]] has no intercalary month, it falls ten or eleven days earlier each year and runs through the whole cycle of the seasons in thirty-two to thirty-three years. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 42} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 43} The article's text on the other ordinances proclaimed on this occasion is cut off in the scan and is not reported here.

## The standing places and the timing of the rites
The article reports a saying ascribed to Muhammad at the Farewell Pilgrimage: all of [[place/arafat|Arafat]] is a place of standing, all of [[place/muzdalifa|Muzdalifa]] is a place of standing, and all of [[place/mina|Mina]] is a place of sacrifice. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 41} It adds that tradition records his order not to leave Arafat until after sunset, where it had been usual to begin the departure before sunset, and that the departure to Mina was to begin before sunrise, where it had been usual to begin once the sun was visible. The passage does not tie these orders to a particular year. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 41}

The article then gives the academic readings of these reports. Snouck Hurgronje, it says, took the saying to mean that the particular spots where pre-Islamic ceremonies had been performed lost their importance. Snouck Hurgronje and Houtsma, as it reports, saw in the rites at these places traces of a pre-Islamic cult of the sun, and read the changes of timing as an attempt to remove it. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 41} The article itself says that inquiry into the oldest meaning of the pilgrimage goes no further than hypotheses, some of them probable. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 40} These are the views of the scholars it cites. The Muslim sources reported above present the saying as part of his showing the people their rites, and neither account is judged here.

## How the pilgrimage is remembered in later practice
The article on the pilgrimage says that a sermon is preached at Arafat in commemoration of the one given by Muhammad, and that the juridical schools differ on whether there should be one sermon or two. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 44} It adds that pilgrims often hear preachers tell of the Prophet's last pilgrimage, the hajj al-wada, in the year 10/632, with stress on the revelation of a verse of the fifth sura at Arafat and on his address there. It also notes that sermons and articles like to call the pilgrimage the annual congress of the Muslim world. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 46} Two details of practice are tied to him in the article: the order of stoning at the three pillars of Mina is said to follow a tradition of the Prophet, and the last circling before a pilgrim leaves Mecca is called the farewell circling. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 45} The article on [[concept/ihram|ihram]] adds that before entering that state a pilgrim may use a perfume that leaves no trace, as the Prophet did when he made his Farewell Pilgrimage. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 1085}

## Its importance
It was the last general gathering of the Prophet with the Muslims. In it the rules of the rites were announced to the people directly, and in the sermon he announced the principles on the sanctity of blood and property, family relations, and holding fast to the Book and the Sunna.

## For young visitors
The Prophet made the pilgrimage with thousands of Muslims, and taught them how to perform the Hajj: where to stand, how to throw the pebbles at the pillars, and how to circle the House. He gave them a sermon that gathered great advice, and then they returned to Medina. People called this pilgrimage the "Farewell Pilgrimage".

## Notes for researchers
- There is disagreement among the accounts about the order of some events (such as coming out of ihram by the umra and the position of Ali). This page follows Ibn Hisham alone, and has not yet been checked against the two Sahihs (Muslim, from the report of Jabir in particular).
- On the question of the name: "Pilgrimage of Farewell" and "Pilgrimage of Proclamation" are both names that appear in the same printed edition (p. 253).
- The report of the complaint of Ali's army (p. 250) is two accounts from Ibn Ishaq, and should be read together with what is in the page on Ghadir Khumm.

## Jabir's hadith describing it, as Mir'at al-Mafatih explains it

[[work/mirat-al-mafatih|Mir'at al-Mafatih]] opens its chapter on the Farewell Pilgrimage with the long hadith of [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abd Allah]]; its author al-Mubarakfuri calls it the greatest and most comprehensive hadith on the subject, and quotes al-Nawawi that Jabir gave the best account of the pilgrimage among the Companions, since he described it from the Prophet's departure from Medina to its end, and that it is found only in Muslim, not in al-Bukhari, Abu Dawud relating it as Muslim does. {cite:mirat-al-mafatih-v9-jabir p.1} He quotes Qadi Iyad that Abu Bakr ibn al-Mundhir wrote a large treatise on it, drawing from it more than a hundred and fifty points of law; al-Dhahabi that Jabir has a small manual of the pilgrimage transmitted by Muslim; and Ibn Kathir that it is by itself a complete manual of the rites. {cite:mirat-al-mafatih-v9-jabir p.1} {cite:mirat-al-mafatih-v9-jabir p.2} He quotes al-Albani that scholars agree the Prophet performed no pilgrimage after the Hijra other than this one, in the year 10, and that the likeliest view of when pilgrimage became obligatory is the year 9 or 10, as Ibn al-Qayyim argues in Zad al-Ma'ad, so that the Prophet hastened to it without delay. {cite:mirat-al-mafatih-v9-jabir p.2} Among details from other transmissions he notes that they set out with five days of Dhu al-Qa'da remaining, and that [[person/asma-bint-umays|Asma' bint Umays]] gave birth to Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr at Dhu al-Hulayfa. {cite:mirat-al-mafatih-v9-jabir p.3}

## For Sprouts
The Prophet made his only Hajj, and he gave the Farewell Sermon at Arafat, full of great advice about brotherhood and justice.

## For Explorers
The Prophet made his last pilgrimage, the only Hajj he performed. At Arafat he gave a great sermon in which he urged the people to brotherhood and justice, and it is called the Farewell Sermon.

## For Scholars
The only pilgrimage the Prophet led, during which he gave the sermon known as the Farewell Sermon. The longest account of its rites is the report of Jabir ibn Abdullah in Sahih Muslim, which includes the sermon at Arafat. The sermon speaks of the sanctity of lives, property and honour, of justice and brotherhood, of the rights of women and of servants, and of holding fast to the Book and the Sunna. Its words are reported in several transmissions, so each sentence has its own source. This entry presents the pilgrimage as the hadith books tell it, and does not add to what they report.
