## The story
The Two Sahihs relate that a Jew of Medina — named in the sira as Labid ibn al-Asam — bewitched the Prophet until his condition grew severe {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}.

## The cure
Gabriel told the Prophet he was bewitched; the two Suras of Refuge were revealed [[passage/quran-113|al-Falaq]] [[passage/quran-114|al-Nas]] and he was healed, the magic drawn from a well beneath his bedding {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## Confession and expulsion
The sorcerer was identified and confessed, and was expelled from Medina {cite:sahih-muslim}; the sira relates the story with its details {cite:sira-ibn-ishaq-zakkar}.

## Notes
This is the most famous report of magic in the seerah, and shows that magic could be wrought by humans, not only jinn {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
