## Overview
Al-Bukhari relates in the Book of Medicine that a man bewitched the Prophet — three people: a man, a woman and his nephew — whereupon the Prophet sought refuge in God from the evil of what He created and recited the two protective suras. He was told that Labid ibn al-Asam was in a lock of hair; he was brought and confessed. The Prophet ordered a branch of the lote-tree with three leaves burned to ash, water blown into it, and al-Baqara, Al 'Imran and the two protective suras recited over him — he recovered, while Labid's hair fell out. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

This incident — called "the sorcery worked against the Prophet" — is the most famous account of magic in the hadith and sira literature, and it embodies the Baqara verse revealed about sorcery: sorcery harms only by God's leave, and ruqyah by Qur'an is its lawful remedy.
