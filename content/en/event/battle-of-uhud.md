## Summary
The battle of Uhud, the second great battle between the Muslims of Medina and Quraysh, was fought on Saturday 7 Shawwal 3 AH at the foot of [[place/uhud|Mount Uhud]] north of [[place/medina|Medina]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 256}. It began as a complete Muslim victory and ended in heavy losses after most of the archer detachment left their post. Seventy Companions were martyred, foremost among them [[person/hamza|Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib]]; the Prophet was wounded in the face; and Quraysh withdrew without occupying the Muslim camp or entering Medina {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 284}.

## The context
Quraysh was burning with rage over the defeat at Badr: it forbade mourning its dead, delayed ransoming captives, and held back the profits of the caravan that had escaped with [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] to finance revenge {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 248}. It gathered three thousand fighters, two hundred of them cavalry and seven hundred mailed; overall command went to Abu Sufyan and command of the cavalry to [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]], then still a polytheist {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 249}. In Medina the Prophet held a consultative council in which the majority of the Companions chose to march out to meet them, despite his preference for staying.

## The plan and deployment
The Prophet halted with seven hundred fighters in the gorge of Uhud, facing Medina with his back to the mountain — the best position on the field. He selected fifty skilled archers under [[person/abd-allah-ibn-jubayr|Abdullah ibn Jubayr ibn al-Nu'man]] al-Ansari and posted them on a small hill southeast of the camp — known as [[place/jabal-al-rumma|Jabal al-Rummat]], the Archers' Hill — to protect the left flank from the polytheist cavalry, charging them: "guard our backs; if you see us being killed, do not come to our aid, and if you see us taking spoils, do not join us" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 255; cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. He placed al-Mundhir ibn Amr over the right wing and [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] over the left.

## Before the battle: the hypocrites withdraw
On the way to Uhud, close to the enemy, [[person/abd-allah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul]] mutinied and withdrew with about three hundred fighters, exposing a hypocrisy that had been hidden {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 253}. Banu Haritha of the Aws and Banu Salama of the Khazraj nearly followed, then held firm; [[person/abd-allah-ibn-haram|Abdullah ibn Haram]], Jabir's father, tried to turn the hypocrites back but they refused. Seven hundred Companions remained with the Prophet.

## The first round: Muslim victory
The weight of the early battle turned on the polytheists' standard, held by Banu Abd al-Dar. [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|Al-Zubayr]] leapt on its first bearer, [[person/talha-ibn-abi-talha|Talha ibn Abi Talha]], threw him from his camel and slew him. The sons of Abd al-Dar took up the standard in turn and were wiped out to the last — ten men — until an Abyssinian slave named Sawab carried it, kneeling on it with chest and neck until he was killed {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 258-260}. [[person/abu-dujana|Abu Dujana]] went forth in his red turban carrying the Prophet's sword, hewing through the polytheist ranks {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 256}. The polytheists fled — al-Zubayr saw the women fleeing up the mountain with their skirts raised {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 263; cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## The martyrdom of Hamza
[[person/hamza|Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib]] fought in the open like a great camel, cutting through the ranks. [[person/wahshi-ibn-harb|Wahshi ibn Harb]], an Abyssinian slave of Jubayr ibn Mut'im, lay in wait for him — Jubayr had promised him freedom if he killed Hamza in revenge for his uncle Tu'ayma, slain at Badr. Wahshi said: "I poised my javelin until I was satisfied with it, then drove it into him; it struck below his navel and came out between his legs" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 261; cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. Afterwards [[person/hind-bint-utba|Hind bint Utba]] came and cut open Hamza's liver {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 276}.

## The archers' error and the reversal
When most of the archer detachment saw the Muslims gathering spoils, they left their post — all but Ibn Jubayr and nine with him. [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] seized the opening, swept his cavalry around behind the Muslim army, destroyed Ibn Jubayr and his men, and the routed polytheists turned back on the Muslims {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 264}. The Muslims were caught as between the stones of a mill. In the confusion a rumour spread that the Prophet had been killed and some collapsed, until [[person/anas-ibn-al-nadr|Anas ibn al-Nadr]] reminded them: "what will you do with life after him? Rise and die upon what he died upon" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 265}.

## The defence of the Prophet
In the hardest hour only [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha ibn Ubaydullah]] and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas remained at the Prophet's rear. Utba ibn Abi Waqqas pelted him with stones — his front tooth was broken and his lip cut — and Abdullah ibn Qami'a struck his cheek so that two rings of his helmet sank into it {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 267-268}. [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha]] fought the fight of eleven men until his fingers were severed and he bore thirty-nine wounds {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 269; cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. [[person/abu-dujana|Abu Dujana]] shielded the Prophet with his back as the arrows fell on him, unmoving {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 271}. [[person/nusayba-bint-kab|Nusayba bint Ka'b]] fought until she took twelve wounds {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 272}. [[person/abu-ubaydah-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] pulled the helmet rings from the Prophet's cheek with his front teeth {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 270}.

## The withdrawal to the gorge and the end of the battle
About thirty Companions gathered around the Prophet and cut a path to the mountain gorge. During the withdrawal the Prophet himself speared [[person/ubayy-ibn-khalaf|Ubayy ibn Khalaf]] in the collarbone, and he died of it at Sarif {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 275}. Once settled in the gorge, [[person/fatima-bint-muhammad|Fatima]] washed his wound and stanched the blood with a piece of burned mat {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 278}. Abu Sufyan then called out to the Muslims; [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] answered him and made clear that the Prophet was alive {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 278}.

## Losses and the burial of the martyrs
Seventy were martyred: sixty-five Ansar, four Muhajirun and one Jew. The Prophet ordered them buried where they lay, unwashed, placing first in each grave whoever knew most Qur'an {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 281}. [[person/sad-ibn-al-rabi|Sa'd ibn al-Rabi']] was found at his last breath bearing seventy blows, and he sent his greeting to the Prophet {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 280}. They went looking for [[person/hanzala-ibn-abi-amir|Hanzala]] and found him dripping water; the Prophet told them the angels were washing him, and he was called "the one washed by the angels" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 281}.

## The expedition of Hamra' al-Asad
The next day the Prophet called the people to pursue Quraysh; the Muslims marched on their wounds until they reached [[place/hamra-al-asad|Hamra' al-Asad]], eight miles from Medina {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 285}. He sent Ma'bad al-Khuza'i to Abu Sufyan, who waged a war of nerves on him until the Meccan army's resolve collapsed and they kept on withdrawing to Mecca. The Prophet executed [[person/abu-azza-al-jumahi|Abu Azza al-Jumahi]], who had broken his pledge {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 286}.

## The Qur'an and the battle
Some sixty verses of Sura Al Imran came down on the subject of Uhud, beginning at the verse [[passage/quran-3-121|Al Imran 121]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 288}. The verses address the stand of the hypocrites, the cause of the reversal in disobeying the order, and the wisdom of the trial in distinguishing the truthful from the false.
