## Summary
In Shawwal of the year 411 one of the most violent moments of the reign of [[person/al-hakim-bi-amr-allah|al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah]] occurred in Egypt. Notes circulated in the lanes of Egypt asking that the slaves be given free rein in the city for three days. When al-Hakim saw them and feared what they implied, he ordered his commanders and the quarter-heads (ʿurafāʾ) to burn [[place/al-fustat|Fustat]], plunder it and kill its people. The fighting went on for three days before he rode out himself and turned the troops back. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.301-303}

## The notes and the request
Ibn al-Jawzi reports that the slaves wrote on slips of paper and threw them into the lanes, asking the state for free rein in Egypt. People were so afraid of the slaves that they said: if they got what they wanted, no one, mighty or humble, would be left without humiliation. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.301}

## Three days of fighting
The two sides fought for three days. Women, children and the city's notables cried out against the slaves. Women and boys were gathered from their houses until captives taken as booty were sold in the markets, and the nobles and elders gathered up the copies of the Qur'an. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.302-303}

## The caliph between the two lines
Then al-Hakim rode out himself and stood before one line of the slaves and cried out to them, "Yā Aghnāṭī!", and they turned back. By then a third of Fustat had burned and another third had been plundered. Ibn al-Jawzi describes how, when the slaves saw him opposing them, they understood that his aim was their destruction. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.303}
