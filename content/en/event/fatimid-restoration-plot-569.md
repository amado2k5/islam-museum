## Overview
In 569 AH a group of supporters of the Ubaydi Fatimids wanted to restore the summons to the house of al-Adid. [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din]] learned of it and had them crucified between the two palaces. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.684}

## The plan in Ibn al-Athir
Ibn al-Athir names those who took part: among them the poet [[person/umara-al-yamani|Umara al-Yamani]], the scribe Abd al-Samad, the judge al-Uwarris, and the chief of the summoners. They were joined by some of the Egyptian troops and the Sudanese, and by the household of the palace. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.53-54}

They agreed to call in the Franks with money and lands. If Salah al-Din went out against them, they would rise up in Cairo; if he stayed, they would seize him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.53-54}

## The discovery
They brought in the preacher [[person/zayn-al-din-ibn-naja|Zayn al-Din Ali ibn Naja]]. When he learned the truth, he told Salah al-Din, who ordered him to stay with them and to report to him. Then the envoy of the king of the Franks arrived, and the news was confirmed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.53-54}

Salah al-Din seized the leaders and crucified them on the second of Ramadan. The source says that al-Qadi al-Fadil doubted Abd al-Samad, so he charged Ibn Naja with the investigation. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.53-54}
