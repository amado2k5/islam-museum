## Summary
When [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] departed from Mecca after [[event/umrah-of-qada|the Compensatory Umra]], the daughter of [[person/hamza|Hamza]] followed him calling out, "O uncle," so [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] took her up and held her hand {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.103}.

## The Dispute
Ali said to Fatima, "Here is your cousin," and Ali, [[person/jafar-ibn-abi-talib|Ja'far]], and [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd]] disputed over her: Ali said, "I took her and she is my cousin"; Ja'far said, "She is my cousin and her maternal aunt is my wife"; and Zayd said, "She is my brother's daughter" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.103}.

## The Ruling
The Prophet awarded her to her maternal aunt, saying, "The maternal aunt holds the status of the mother." He said to Ali, "You are of me and I am of you," to Ja'far, "You resemble my form and my character," and to Zayd, "You are our brother and our freedman" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.103}. The book derives several rulings from the story, including that the maternal aunt takes precedence in custody when the parents are absent, that a woman's marriage does not forfeit her right to custody, and that the husband's consent is required {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.103-104}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not name the girl in this passage, and no person entry has been created for her.
- The book notes that Ali asked the Prophet to marry Hamza's daughter, and he answered that she was his milk-brother's daughter; the text in this passage is effaced, so the original should be consulted before quoting its wording.
