## Summary
Quraysh was struck by a severe hardship and Abu Talib had a large household, so the Prophet said to his uncle al-Abbas: "Let us lighten the burden of his dependents." The Prophet took Ali and brought him into his care, and al-Abbas took Ja'far; Ali remained with the Prophet until God sent him as a prophet, whereupon Ali followed him, acknowledged him, and believed in him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.31}.

## Upbringing in the Prophet's Household
Al-Sallabi considers this blessing—that the boy was granted an upbringing in the house of Islam before going out to the people—to be the first setting in which the Qur'an was reflected in Ali, for he grew up in the Prophet's household and became acquainted with the secrets of the call at an early stage {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.31}.

## How He Embraced Islam
Ibn Ishaq narrates that Ali found the Prophet and Khadija praying and asked: "What is this, Muhammad?" The Prophet presented to him the testimony of God's oneness, the rejection of al-Lat and al-Uzza, and disavowal of idols; he did so, embraced Islam, and concealed his faith out of fear of Abu Talib {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.31-32}.

## Praying in the Mountain Passes
Whenever the time for prayer arrived, the Prophet would go out to the mountain passes of Mecca with Ali, hiding from his father and his uncles, and they would pray and return in the evening. One day Abu Talib came upon them while they were praying and said to Ali: "My son, what is this religion?" He replied: "I have believed in God and His Messenger, confirmed him, prayed with him, and followed him." They claimed he said: "He has only called you to good, so stay with him," and said to his nephew that he could not leave the religion of his forefathers, but would never allow anything he disliked to befall him as long as he lived {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.32-33}. The book transmits this report with the wording "they claimed," meaning it is an attributed narration on which the book does not rely alone in passing judgment.

## Disagreement over the Order of Conversion
The author gives preference to the view that the first free man to embrace Islam was Abu Bakr, the first among boys was Ali, the first among women was Khadija—and that she was the first to believe altogether—and the first among freedmen was Zayd ibn Haritha {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.31}. Thus the order presented by the book is the author's preferred view among scholarly opinions, not a reported consensus.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not mention the year of Ali's conversion; the date recorded in the museum is derived from the beginning of the prophetic mission and marked as approximate.
- What the book transmits from Mecca regarding the secrecy of Islam and praying in the mountain passes is narrated in varying versions, and the book passes judgment only according to the view it favors.
