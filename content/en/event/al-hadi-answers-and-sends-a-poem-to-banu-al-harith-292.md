## Overview
In his answer al-Hadi gave Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah some lines which he told him to send on to Banu al-Harith, beginning: carry to Banu Harith from me a message that lifts the grief of a man in sorrow {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.284}. The museum gives their sense; they are ascribed to al-Hadi in this Zaydi book.

## Detail
The poet asks Banu al-Harith how they will flee, and says that he gave them life when their end was near, as though death had been about to gnaw at them, and that cup after cup of it had gone round them at their capture, yet their thanks to him were not as people give thanks {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.284}. He bids them take the fire of his war, for it has poured down a heavy rain of death; he likens the war to a cloud whose lightning shows and whose thunderbolts are feared, tells them to dig a tunnel in the earth and shelter from the stroke of the axe, and says the lances in the believers' hands spare no one, that the swords work on the enemy's heads when the guardians of truth, who trace themselves to the Messenger, draw them, and that the believers' banners go forward while their enemies' hearts shake {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.284}. He says he defends the faith and follows in the track of his grandfather Muhammad, and that his purpose stands fast like a smooth, towering rock whose root is set in the earth, so that people have given up hope of splitting it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.284} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.285}. The note on p. 285 explains a rare word from the Qamus and gives manuscript S's corrections. The OCR scrambles the line order and the order of the page, as read from the image, is followed. The poet's comparison of Banu al-Harith to animals is not repeated.
