## Overview
Muhammad sent to men of Banu Abd al-Madan asking them to attack Ibn Humayd before Banu al-Harith could join him. They refused, saying they would not leave their village, nor would any of their men. They advised him to close the Darb gate, gather the clients at the house door, and order his kin to come armed. They said that if Banu al-Harith meant to fight him they would not move from their village until reinforcements came to him from al-Hadi.

## Detail
Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah sent to men of Banu Abd al-Madan to tell them what the others had done, that they had camped at the Darb gate to fight him, and asked them to attack Ibn Humayd and his men before Banu al-Harith joined him. They disliked this and said they would not leave their village, nor would any of their men. He asked what they advised. They said he should order the Darb gate closed while they considered what to do, gather their clients at the house door, and order his kin to come armed. Their answer, as the book gives it, was that even if Banu al-Harith and Hamdan joined together they would not fear that they could take their village, since those men had tried it more than once without success, and could enter only if one of the village's own people opened the way. They said that Banu Abd al-Madan had never been as united with Muhammad's side as they were that day. He accepted what they offered of their own support and their stand with him, and at once wrote to his two sons.
