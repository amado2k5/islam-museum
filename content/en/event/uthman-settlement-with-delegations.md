## Summary
The rebels from the garrison provinces encamped at Dhu al-Marwa about a month and a half before Uthman was killed. Uthman sent Ali and another man to them; Ali presented the Book of God to them and they accepted, then they agreed on a settlement of five points, and Uthman made peace with each delegation separately, so the delegations departed for their homes {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 163}.

## The Delegations and Encampment
Al-Sallabi mentions that the rebels encamped at Dhu al-Marwa, that Uthman sent Ali and another man who is not named on the page, and that the other report mentions they charged at the envoy two or three times and then said: the cousin of the Messenger of God and the envoy of the Commander of the Faithful presents the Book of God to you, so they accepted {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 163}.

## Terms of the Settlement
The book repeatedly states the formula of a settlement on five points, including: that the exiled be returned, that the deprived be given their due, that state revenues be distributed properly, that justice be done among the subjects, and that Abu Musa al-Ash'ari remain over Kufa {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}. Uthman made peace with each delegation separately, and then the delegations departed {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}.

## Significance of the Settlement in the Book
The book presents the settlement as an accommodation of the provinces' demands, and then notes that the rebels were dissatisfied with its outcome and fabricated the forged letter {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 164}. See the entry "The Case of the Forged Letter in Uthman's Name."
