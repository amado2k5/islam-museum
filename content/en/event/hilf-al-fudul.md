After the [[event/harb-al-fijar|war of al-Fijar]], in the month of Dhu al-Qa'da the tribes of Banu Hashim, Banu al-Muttalib, Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza, Zuhra ibn Kilab and Taym ibn Murra gathered in the house of [[person/abdullah-ibn-judan|Abdullah ibn Jud'an al-Taymi]] — for his age and honour — and covenanted that they would not find anyone wronged in Mecca, of its people or of strangers, but they would stand with him until his wrong was returned to him. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The cause of the pact — as the book relates — was that a man of Zabid sold goods to [[person/al-as-ibn-wail|al-As ibn Wa'il]] al-Sahmi, who withheld his due; the man appealed to the parties of the pact and they did not answer him. Then [[person/al-zubayr-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Zubayr ibn Abd al-Muttalib]] began speaking of the need to conclude a pact for returning wrongs, and these tribes gathered for it. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Prophet, then a young man, witnessed the pact; the book describes his spirit as averse to Jahiliyya partisanship, and after the message the Prophet said: "I witnessed in Abdullah ibn Jud'an's house a pact for which I would not love to have red camels; and were I called to it in Islam, I would answer." {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
