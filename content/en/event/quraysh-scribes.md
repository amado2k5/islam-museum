## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates from Abu Bakr ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Jahm al-Adawi that when Islam came, there were seventeen men among Quraysh who could write. He then names men and women writers from Quraysh and its allies, and those who wrote for the Prophet. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The men who wrote
Al-Baladhuri counts among those who wrote in Quraysh: Umar ibn al-Khattab, Ali ibn Abi Talib, Uthman ibn Affan, Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, Talha, Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan, Abu Hudhayfa ibn Utba, Hatib ibn Amr, Abu Salama al-Makhzumi, Aban ibn Sa'id and his brother Khalid, Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh, Huwaytib ibn Abd al-Uzza, Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan, and Jahm ibn al-Salt. Among the allies of Quraysh was al-Ala' ibn al-Hadrami. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The women who wrote
The source says that [[person/al-shifa-bint-abdullah|al-Shifa bint Abd Allah al-Adawiyya]] was a writer in the Jahiliyya, and that [[person/hafsa|Hafsa]] wrote, as did [[person/umm-kulthum-bint-uqba|Umm Kulthum bint Uqba]] and [[person/karima-bint-al-miqdad|Karima bint al-Miqdad]]. It says that Aisha and Umm Salama could read but did not write. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Who wrote for the Prophet
Al-Baladhuri says that the first to write for the Prophet when he came to Medina was [[person/ubayy-ibn-kab|Ubayy ibn Ka'b]], and when he was absent [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]] wrote; the two wrote the revelation. The first from Quraysh to write for him was [[person/abdullah-ibn-sad-ibn-abi-sarh|Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The source says that Abd Allah ibn Sa'd later apostatised and returned to Mecca, that he would change what was dictated to him, and that a verse of al-An'am was revealed about the man who claimed he could bring the like of what was revealed. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
