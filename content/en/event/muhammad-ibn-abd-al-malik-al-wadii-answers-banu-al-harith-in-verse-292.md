## Overview
After the Harithis' decision to march on Wadi'a the narrator says that Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Tarif al-Wadi'i spoke verses on it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.287}. The name is printed here as "ibn Tarif", while the record kept for the man follows the index spelling ([[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-malik-ibn-ghitrif-al-wadii|Ibn Ghitrif]]); the museum does not decide between the two forms.

## Detail
The poet opens in the manner of an erotic prelude: an abode whose traces the east wind has erased and a bright-faced young woman without fault, then chides his own heart {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.287}. He turns to Banu al-Harith, wonders at what came from them, says they have no escape from him after raising war against a bold man, that their ways are known by trial, that they exposed themselves to ruin, and that al-Hadi had favoured them with favours whose thanks were due, which they denied, removing a governor who was to al-Hadi a friend and a kinsman by marriage {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.287}. A note from manuscript S says that "kinsman" points to Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah: al-Hadi gave him his daughter in marriage and she bore him Musa, Ja'far and al-Qasim, while Ali ibn Muhammad's mother was not al-Hadi's daughter; it cites Sayyid Ibrahim ibn Muhammad in his glosses on al-Hidaya and the biography of the martyred Sayyid Abu Ja'far called al-Rawd al-Azhar, and touches the question of a Fatimid woman marrying a non-Fatimid Alid; it is the copyist's note, and the museum gives no ruling on it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.287}. The poet says the opponents shot the governor with arrows and drew flaming swords, and that Muhammad then alighted among Ka'b in a place of honour, loved {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.287} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.288}. He praises Ka'b, of Hamdan, as protectors who do not bear wrong and who hold the head and the branch of Hamdan, helpers of al-Hadi and the House who come in dense hosts when called, whose fathers stood so before for his father, for whom standing with the sons of the Prophet is the finest pride, so that whoever sold his share has lost and whoever held to the tie has gained {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.288}. All of this is the poet's voice in a Zaydi book, neither affirmed nor denied by the museum, and no abuse is repeated; the poem is answered in [[event/ali-ibn-abi-jafar-al-alawi-answers-the-poem-of-the-wadii-292|the next event]].
