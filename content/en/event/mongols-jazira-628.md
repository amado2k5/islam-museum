## Summary
In 628 AH the Tatars fell on Jalal al-Din by night outside Amid and he fled routed; they then entered Diyar Bakr and the Jazira in pursuit of him and plundered its countryside. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 492}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that Jalal al-Din, seeing what the Tatars were doing in Azerbaijan, left it for the land of Akhlat intending to reach Diyar Bakr and the Jazira and the caliph's court to seek help, and to unite all the kings against the Tatars; reaching Akhlat he learned the Tatars were hunting him in forced marches, so he moved to Amid and posted pickets at several spots for fear of a night assault, but a Tatar party came by a road the pickets did not cover and struck him at night outside Amid, so he fled routed and his army scattered; one group headed for Harran where the amir Sawab, al-Kamil's commander there, fell on them and took their money, arms and mounts, while others made for Nisibis, Mosul, Sinjar and Erbil and were picked off by kings and subjects alike — peasants, Kurds and bedouin all taking vengeance. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 492} After Jalal al-Din's rout at Amid the Tatars plundered the countryside of Amid, Arzan and Mayyafariqin and made for the town of As'ird, whose people fought them; the Tatars offered aman, they submitted, and once in their power the Tatars put them to the sword and nearly annihilated them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 492}
