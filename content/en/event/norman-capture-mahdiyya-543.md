## Summary
In 543 AH the fleets of Roger, king of Sicily ([[person/roger-of-sicily|Roger]]), landed at [[place/mahdiyya|al-Mahdiyya]], and he seized the coastal lands. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1636}

## Ibn Khaldun's account
The fleets came in three hundred ships, commanded by [[person/george-of-antioch|George son of Michael of Antioch]], with a thousand horsemen. Roger imposed a poll tax on the people of the coast, and they remained under his rule until Abd al-Mu'min, sheikh of the Muwahhidun, rescued them in 555 AH. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1636}

Ibn Khaldun also notes that Roger kept the people of the African coasts he held under his authority, and returned those who fled to their places. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1636}

## The account of Shadharat al-Dhahab
In Ibn al-Imad, the famine in Ifriqiya in 543 AH was severe, and the king of Sicily seized the opportunity and advanced with two hundred and fifty ships. The ruler of al-Mahdiyya, [[person/al-hasan-ibn-ali-zirid|al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Zirid]], fled from him, and he took the city without a fight. The Franks came to hold the lands between Tripoli of the Maghrib and near Tunis. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.614-615}

The two accounts differ on the number of ships.
