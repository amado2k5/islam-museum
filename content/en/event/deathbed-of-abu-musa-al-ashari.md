## Summary
[[person/abu-musa-al-ashari|Abu Musa al-Ash'ari]], Abd Allah ibn Qays, was among the reciters and jurists of the Companions, and according to Ibn Kathir had the finest voice of the Companions in his day. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 425} The account of his death centres on his instruction to dig his grave wide and deep and what he said about the two possible outcomes, his advice to his sons to remember the man of the loaf, and the historians' disagreement about the place of his grave and the year of his death.

## Background
Ibn Kathir quotes al-Sha'bi that Umar wrote in his will that no governor should be kept more than a year except Abu Musa, who should be kept four years. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 425} He describes him as short, slight and beardless. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 425}

## His instruction to dig his grave
Sifat al-Safwa relates from al-Dahhak ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Arzab that Abu Musa called his young men when death came to him and said: go and dig, and make it wide and deep. They came back and said: we have dug it, wide and deep. He said: by God, it will be one of two stations: either my grave will be widened until each corner of it is forty cubits and a door to Paradise will be opened for me, so that I look upon my wives and my dwellings and what God has prepared for me of honour, and its fragrance and ease reach me until I am raised; or it will be the other, and we seek God's refuge from it, and my grave will be narrowed until I am tighter than a spear in its socket, and a door of Hell will be opened and I will look upon my chains, fetters and companions, and its blasts and boiling water reach me until I am raised. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 203}

## His counsel to his sons: the man of the loaf
It relates from Abu Burda that when death came to Abu Musa he said: my sons, remember the man of the loaf. The story is of a man who worshipped in his cell, the narrator thinks for seventy years, coming down only one day; Satan made a woman appear to him and he was with her seven days or nights; then the veil was lifted from him and he went out repenting, praying and prostrating at every step, until night found him at a bench with twelve poor men on it. Exhaustion overtook him and he threw himself between two of them. A monk used to send them loaves each night and gave each person one; the penitent was left out, and the man beside him gave him his own loaf, and in the morning the penitent was dead. The seventy years were weighed against the seven nights and the nights outweighed them; then the loaf was weighed against the seven nights and the loaf outweighed. And Abu Musa said: my sons, remember the man of the loaf. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 203} The second copy of Sifat al-Safwa gives the same report and ending. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 4 p. 221}

## His death and grave
Sifat al-Safwa says: the biographers say Abu Musa died in 52, or 42, or 44, and was buried at Mecca; it is also said he was buried at al-Thawiyya, two miles from Kufa. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 203} Ibn Kathir says Ibn al-Jawzi, in al-Muntazam, recorded his death in this year (52), which is one view; another puts it a year earlier, another in 42, and others differently. His death was at Mecca, when he withdrew from people after the arbitration, or, it is said, at a place called al-Thawiyya two miles from Kufa. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 425}

## Where the reports differ
Al-Dhahabi gives, from al-Haytham ibn Adi in Ibn Sa'd's report, that Abu Musa became Muslim at Mecca, emigrated to Abyssinia, that his first campaign was Khaybar, and that he died in 42. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 3 p. 278} So there are several opinions on the place of the grave, Mecca or al-Thawiyya, and on the year, 42, 44 or 52. The books read do not say what Abu Musa left or who washed him or prayed over him.
