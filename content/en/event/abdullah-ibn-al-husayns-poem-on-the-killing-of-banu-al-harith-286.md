## Overview
And [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] said, of al-Hadi's killing of [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]], a poem whose opening says his sleep was sweet again, the sleeplessness gone and what was in his heart of choking relieved. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.179} In the poem, most of which runs on at p. 180, he describes al-Hadi's army with its horses, its fierce fighters and men all of firm intent, the imam of justice in front of them in a long mail coat, leading the men with a spear and a Yemeni sword that sings when shaken. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.180} He says that on a second stand the wicked saw wrong and ruin, that the right destroyed a band of them so that they became routed, captive, dead, or fugitives in the hills, that God gives His party good protection. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.180}

## Notes
The editor says a verse and the prose that follows are missing from one manuscript up to the opening of the next poem. The order of the lines is disturbed in print, so the museum gives the sense only and does not repeat the descriptions of the enemy. It is attributed to Abdullah ibn al-Husayn, al-Hadi's brother, on the book's own ascription. The preceding poem is [[event/ahmad-ibn-muhammad-al-madanis-poem-faulting-banu-al-harith-286]].
