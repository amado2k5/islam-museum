## Overview
"He stayed there until, when Saturday came, he left and camped at a place of Banu Ma'mar called al-Ahsa." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134} That is, after his stay at [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]] [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] left on the Saturday for [[place/al-ahsa-banu-mamar-yemen|al-Ahsa]], a place of [[group/banu-mamar-hamdan|Banu Ma'mar]]. The account is Zaydi, in the voice of the imam's followers; the book then names [[place/hawth-yemen|Hawth]], and the editor refers to Sifat Jazirat al-Arab, p. 82. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134}

## The quarrel before Najran
"Its people had had wounds among them before he left for Najran, so he sent them his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]], who took them and brought them to al-Hadi to make careful inquiry, and he took right for some of them from others and jailed the one who had been the wrongdoer"; when al-Hadi went to Najran, Abu al-Qasim looked into their affair by his order, "reconciled them, wrote the blood-money upon them and let them out of jail". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134} Wounds and blood-money are as the book states, and it says no more.

## Back to disorder
"When they came out Satan drew them off and they exposed themselves to the anger of the Merciful"; they were "a dozen or more men" who spoiled al-Hadi's districts, sat on the roads and took the weak, while the rest of their clans of Banu Ma'mar were "hearing and obeying" al-Hadi. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134} That is the narrator's description of them, and the book names none; he ascribes what they did to Satan's prompting.

## Al-Hadi's messengers
Al-Hadi left on Saturday and camped at a place called "Hawth", then sent a party to call them and remind them of "the days of God"; they told the messengers: "We shall go to Abu al-Qasim and hear and obey." The messengers came back and he said: "If they go to Khiwan I shall send to Abu al-Qasim to bring me their heads." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134} The manuscript margin records that al-Hadi threatened disobedient men who had not come to him and asked to be taken to his son, saying that if they reached Khiwan he would order his son to bring their heads to him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134} This is a threat reported of the imam in a war setting; the museum states it and nothing more. He told them: "Go back and call them to the truth and to enter it and leave falsehood and sin", and that was on Monday; they answered: "We shall meet him at Khiwan", and he refused. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134}

## The crier and the rain
He sent the "crier", the man who calls the clans to arms, among Banu Rabi'a and Banu Sarim, and the cries came from Banu Rabi'a; {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.134} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} "rain prevented al-Hadi from going to them with those with him, so he stayed at Hawth". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.135} See [[group/banu-rabia-hamdan|Banu Rabi'a]] and [[group/banu-sarim-hamdan|Banu Sarim]].

## Notes
The book does not date the Saturday after the eighteenth of Rajab, so the date is given to the month and may run into early Sha'ban. "Abu al-Qasim" is taken as probably Muhammad ibn al-Hadi, as in earlier batches; the name is not given here.
