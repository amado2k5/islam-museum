## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] wrote to [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] telling him of God's help to him and of the support and success God gave him over [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]]; when the letter reached him Abu al-Qasim gathered the people and read it to them and they rejoiced, and he wrote al-Hadi's answer and answered it with a poem. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.173} Abu al-Qasim is probably al-Hadi's son known as al-Murtada Muhammad, though the text does not say so.

## The poem
The poet speaks of himself: that the self is turned from thought of beautiful women, that the godfearing turns from passion, that he bears every great burden for God, wades into the spearheads and charges when horsemen fail, that the thrust is sweeter to him than pleasure and play and the cutting of heads with swords sweeter still, and that he fights with brown spears in defence of the family of Muhammad; he takes pride in the stock of Hashim, who inherit the Prophet's station and show the community of Ahmad the ways of right. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.173} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.174} He ends by praising [[group/banu-hamdan|Hamdan]] as helpers and friends, and Sa'd and Khawlan as swords in every matter. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.174} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.175} The museum gives the sense and not the lines, and does not repeat their blame of opponents.
