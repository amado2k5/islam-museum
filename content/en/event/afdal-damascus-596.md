## Summary
Al-Malik al-Afdal Ali ibn Salah al-Din left Egypt for Damascus in his uncle al-Adil's absence; al-Adil beat him to the city, he besieged it, then withdrew. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 257}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that once al-Afdal was established in Egypt, a messenger from his brother al-Malik al-Zahir Ghazi of Aleppo and envoys of his cousin Asad al-Din Shirkuh of Homs urged him to march on Damascus and seize the opportunity of al-Adil's absence, offering help in money and men; he left Egypt in mid-Jumada al-Ula, stayed outside Cairo until 3 Rajab and moved slowly — had he hurried he would have taken Damascus — reaching it on 13 Sha'ban and camping at the wooden bridge. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 257} Al-Adil had left his son al-Kamil over the siege of Mardin and raced ahead, entering Damascus two days before al-Afdal. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 257} A small party of al-Afdal's troops entered the city through Bab al-Salama at the hand of the amir Majd al-Din, brother of the jurist Isa al-Hakkari, who wanted the feat for himself with some fifty horsemen; the populace shouted al-Afdal's slogan and the garrison quit the walls; al-Adil nearly surrendered but held firm, and when the intruders reached al-Barid, al-Adil's troops fell on them and drove them out. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 257} Al-Afdal pitched his tent at the Green Hippodrome then moved to the Pebble Hippodrome; the Kurdish amirs made a pact among themselves, so al-Afdal and the rest of the Asadiyya suspected an understanding with the Damascenes and withdrew. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 257}
