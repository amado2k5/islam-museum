## Summary
At a most critical moment, shortly before dawn, while the Muslim army was in the area of "al-Shawt" close to the enemy, a sudden withdrawal took place led by [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 253}. [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Ibn Salul]] withdrew with about a third of the army — roughly three hundred fighters — arguing that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] had abandoned his opinion and obeyed others in the decision to march out of [[place/medina|Medina]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 253}.

## Details
The author of al-Rahiq al-Makhtum holds that the withdrawal was not merely an objection to the military view; its instigators meant to sow confusion in the Muslim ranks before their enemy and break the morale of those who stayed with the Prophet — and they hoped leadership would return to Ibn Salul {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 254}.

The withdrawal nearly collapsed parts of the army: two factions, of the Aws (Banu Haritha) and the Khazraj (Banu Salama), nearly withdrew as well, but God made them stand firm after a spell of turmoil {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 254}. [[person/abdullah-ibn-amr-ibn-haram|Abdullah ibn Haram]] — father of Jabir ibn Abdullah — followed the deserters urging them to return, and they answered him, as the book relates: "had we known you would fight, we would not have gone back"; so he turned away saying: "may God keep you far, enemies of God — God will spare His Prophet of you" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 254}.

After this withdrawal only seven hundred fighters remained with the Prophet, continuing their march toward [[place/uhud|Mount Uhud]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 254}.
