## Overview
The sources report that the cursing of the Rafida, and then of the Ash'aris, was imposed on the pulpits of Khurasan in the Seljuk period. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 310}

## Ibn al-Imad's account
The vizier [[person/al-kunduri-amid-al-mulk|Amid al-Mulk al-Kunduri]] petitioned the sultan [[person/alp-arslan|Alp Arslan]] about cursing the Rafida from the pulpits of Khurasan, and he granted permission, adding the Ash'aris to the curse. The imams of Khurasan, among them [[person/al-qushayri-abu-l-qasim|Abu al-Qasim al-Qushayri]] and [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni]], took this badly and left Khurasan. al-Juwayni stayed in Mecca for four years, and was called Imam al-Haramayn from then on. When the Nizamiyya state came, Nizam al-Mulk brought back those who had withdrawn and honoured them. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 310}

## Ibn al-Jawzi's account
Ibn al-Jawzi says that the cursing was proclaimed in Nishapur in 445 AH, in the reign of Tughril Beg. He says that al-Qushayri wrote a letter complaining on behalf of the people of the Sunna. He relates the sultan's reply: that the one cursed is the Ash'ari who holds the opinions brought before him, and if they do not hold them, nothing falls on them. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.107-108}

The two accounts differ over the reign (Alp Arslan in Ibn al-Imad, Tughril Beg in Ibn al-Jawzi), and the date in the data is approximate. It needs an additional source.
