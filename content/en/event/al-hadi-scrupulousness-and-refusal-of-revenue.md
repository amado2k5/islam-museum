## Overview
The "chapter on the scrupulousness of Yahya ibn al-Husayn" opens with Ali ibn Muhammad's report from his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]]: part of [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s scrupulousness was that he left some of what was lawful to him, out of piety and self-restraint; the jizya of Christians and Jews belonged to him and his household and not to others, and he could spend it as he liked, yet he neither ate nor drank from it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.58} The narrator gives as his ground that he heard al-Hadi swear he had eaten nothing of what he had collected in Yemen nor drunk water from it, and say he spent only from what he had brought from the Hijaz. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.58} He adds that it reached him that "al-Hasan" said the godfearing do not attain godfearing until they leave much of the lawful for fear of the unlawful, and that "the people are more abstinent in what was made lawful to them than you are in what was forbidden you". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.58} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.59} The text does not say which al-Hasan, so he is not identified here. This is scrupulousness as a Zaydi narrator describes it, and that the jizya belongs to the Prophet's family is the book's view, not the museum's. See [[concept/jizya]] and [[concept/wara]].

## His living and his oaths
He heard him swear again that he had eaten nothing of what was collected in Yemen nor drunk water from it, and that in this task nothing sufficed him but two of the small dirhams to live on each day. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.59} He saw him go to the mosque on foot one Friday, and when he had prayed and left a group of poor people gathered and cried out; he stood with them a while and then ordered something for them, which was shared out. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.59} Once, a man having brought him a slave, he heard him swear earnestly by God that he had not committed an unlawful sexual act with male or female, eaten a dirham he knew to be unlawful, drunk an intoxicant, heard singing, played chess or any pastime, or deliberately wronged a Muslim; he added that he was not praising himself but praising his Lord for His favours, "as He said" ([[passage/quran-93-11|Qur'an 93:11]]). {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.59} This is the imam's testimony about himself as his narrator relays it.

## The ear of grain and the purchases
Ali ibn Muhammad relates from Muhammad ibn Sulayman, from Abd al-Malik ibn Abd al-Malik (whose affiliation is read doubtfully as "al-Yarsami"), that he went out one day with al-Hadi and they passed unharvested crops; he cut an ear and held it out to him, al-Hadi put out his hand and asked "Is the crop yours?" and when he said no, drew his hand back without touching it, and the man threw it away. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.59} And from Ali ibn Abi Anbasa (affiliation read doubtfully as "al-Samidi"), who bought al-Hadi's needs in the market: al-Hadi told him to fear God in what he bought for him, to take nothing from anyone except for a price as people buy, and not to add anything to the price on his behalf, lest he sin. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.59} These two names are not entered as persons because their reading is uncertain.
