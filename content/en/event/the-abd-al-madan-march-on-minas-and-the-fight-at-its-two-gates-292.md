## Overview
Two days after that Banu Abd al-Madan learned of it and said, to persons the book does not name, that they had put a man out from among them and wished their ruin, and that he had gone to Ibn Bustam, who had urged them to war and had meant ruin for them and for the Madanis {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.282}. Those who stood forward were [[person/rabi-ibn-abi-al-rukud|Rabi' ibn Abi al-Rukud]] and Ali ibn Rabi'; their opinion agreed on going to Minas, and they came upon it at dawn with the Yamis and al-Ahlaf {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.282}.

## Detail
Their wish was to kill Ibn Bustam because of the enmity that lay between them, and not to suffer what Banu al-Harith had suffered at Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah's hands; they marched on Minas all together {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.282}. Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah went out and stood with his servants at the gate of the east lane of Minas, and Ibn Bustam and Banu Rabi'a stood at the west lane; the fighting closed at the two lanes and was hard until Banu al-Harith hoped to enter Minas {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.282} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. Many were struck on every side: of those with Abu Ja'far, his servants and the Banu Rabi'a with him, who were eighty men, were hit and one man of Banu Rabi'a was killed; Abu Ja'far, the narrator says, bore himself that day as never seen, and men of Yam and al-Ahlaf were hit by many arrows {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. The fight lasted until the evening, when Banu al-Harith went back to the village {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. The clause "they would not receive what Banu al-Harith received" is hard to read in the print and is taken as their wish not to suffer what befell Banu al-Harith; it is the narrator's. Which Rabi'a the Banu Rabi'a are is not stated and they are linked to no record. The Madanis' charge against Ibn Bustam is theirs in the account, not the museum's.
