## Summary
The verse of Saba ([[passage/quran-34-14|Qur'an 34:14]]) says that the jinn did not learn of the death of [[person/sulayman|Sulayman]] (Solomon) until the creature of the earth ate his staff and he fell. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3801} Ibn Kathir explains that Sulayman stayed leaning on his staff, the "minsa'a" as Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, al-Hasan and Qatada call it, for a long time, about a year; when the termite ate it and he fell, it was known that he had died long before, and it became clear that the jinn do not know the unseen. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3801} The books give several accounts of how he died, and Ibn Kathir judges some of them to belong to reports taken from the People of the Book. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 632}

## The account ascribed to Ibn Abbas: the carob tree
Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim report, through Ibrahim ibn Tahman from Ata, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas, raising it to the Prophet, that when Sulayman prayed he would see a plant sprouting before him and ask its name and what it was for: if for planting it was planted, if for medicine it was recorded. One day he saw a tree which said it was the carob and was there for the ruin of "this house". Sulayman asked God to blind the jinn to his death so that people would know the jinn do not know the unseen. He carved the tree into a staff and leaned on it for a year while the jinn kept working; the termite ate it. Ibn Abbas used to read the verse in this sense, and the jinn thanked the termite and brought it water. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 630} Ibn Kathir says there is something objectionable in the narrations of Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, and that Ibn Asakir transmits it as a statement of Ibn Abbas himself through Salama ibn Kuhayl from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, which is "closer to the truth". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 630}

## The account of al-Suddi: the prayer chamber at Bayt al-Maqdis
Al-Suddi reports, from Abu Malik and Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas, from Murra al-Hamdani from Ibn Mas'ud, and from other Companions, that Sulayman used to retire in Bayt al-Maqdis for a year, two years, a month, two months, more or less, taking his food and drink in with him, and he took them in on the occasion when he died. Each day a new plant grew and he asked its name, until the carob grew and said it had sprouted for the ruin of this mosque. He said God would not ruin it while he lived, pulled it up and planted it in a wall of his garden, then entered the prayer chamber, stood praying leaning on his staff and died, and the devils did not know and kept working for him for fear he would come out. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 631}

The chamber had openings in front and behind, and a devil would enter from one side and leave by the other to show his daring; no devil looked at Sulayman in the chamber without burning. One passed through and heard no voice, came back, dropped into the room unburnt and saw Sulayman lying dead, and told the people. They opened the chamber, took him out and found the staff, which in the language of the Abyssinians is called minsa'a, eaten away. Not knowing how long he had been dead, they set a termite on the staff, which ate from it for a day and a night, and reckoning from that they found he had died a year before. In Ibn Mas'ud's reading they stayed toiling for him for a full year after his death. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 631} Ibn Kathir's Tafsir carries the same account and adds that what is received from the scholars of the People of the Book is to be held in suspension: only what agrees with the truth is believed and only what contradicts it is rejected. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3803} In the Stories of the Prophets he remarks that it contains Israelite material which is "neither believed nor denied". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 632} Al-Tabari gives the same report from al-Suddi. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 601} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 602}

## The account of Ibn Zayd ibn Aslam: the glass palace
Abu Dawud, in the Book of Destiny, reports from Khaythama that Sulayman asked the angel of death to tell him before taking his soul, and the angel replied that he knew no better than Sulayman, for he is only handed writings with the names of those who will die. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 632} Asbagh ibn al-Faraj and Ibn Wahb report from Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn Aslam that Sulayman asked the angel to tell him when he was ordered, and the angel came and said a little while was left. Sulayman called the devils, who built him a palace of glass with no door; he stood to pray leaning on his staff, the angel of death came in and took his soul while he was leaning, and he did this not to flee from the angel. The jinn worked in front of him, watching him and thinking him alive, until God sent the creature of the earth to his staff; it ate it, the staff weakened and grew heavy, and he fell, and the jinn saw it and dispersed. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 632} Asbagh adds that it reached him from others that the creature ate the staff for a year until he fell. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 632} The Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya gives the same. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 380}

## His age and the length of his reign
Ibn Kathir quotes Ishaq ibn Bishr, from Ibn Ishaq, from al-Zuhri and others, that Sulayman lived fifty-two years and reigned forty, and from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas that he reigned twenty. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 632} Ibn Jarir says his whole life was a little over fifty years and that he began building Bayt al-Maqdis in the fourth year of his reign, as reported. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 603} Ibn Kathir quotes Ibn Jarir that his son Rehoboam reigned after him for seventeen years and then the kingdom of the Israelites broke apart. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 380}

## The trace and the grave
The one trace named in these reports is his staff, found eaten away when his body was brought out of the chamber. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 631} The books on which this record is built do not say where he was buried or where his grave is. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 632} For what was said about his inheritance from his father, see [[event/death-of-dawud-and-inheritance-of-sulayman|Death of Dawud and Sulayman's inheritance from him]].
