## Overview
Al-Hadi ordered his brother and his son out with an army to watch the people of [[place/bayt-bus-sanaa-yemen|Bayt Bus]] and cut the supply from the people of [[place/ghayman-sanaa-yemen|Ghayman]]; they stayed two days facing the men, and on the evening of Tuesday in Jumada II of the year 289 the men marched to battle, having laid an ambush {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.240}. Abu Muhammad and Abu al-Qasim drew up their army with the Hamdanis on the right, the Ja'fari on the left and the Emigrants and Tabaris in the centre; when the men came near they charged the left, where the Ja'fari were, routed them, killed a number and the army broke in flight {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.241}.

## Detail
Abu Muhammad and Abu al-Qasim then turned with the main body of horse, drove the men off and killed some; night fell on them, they carried those of their companions who had been struck down and buried them near [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]] and went back to their camp {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.241}. On Wednesday the men came out of Bayt Bus with their armies against Ibn al-Ruwayya, fell on him and routed him and those with him, and the news reached al-Hadi in the night {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.241}. The text does not say which of the two sons of al-Ruwayya is meant (Abu al-Ushayra or Rabi'), so the museum does not tie the name to one record. Abu al-Qasim here is al-Hadi's son, probably [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] (al-Murtada). The "Ja'fari" are named as printed, as the aid that came from the Ja'fari.
