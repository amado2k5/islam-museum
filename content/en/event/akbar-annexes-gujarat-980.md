## Summary
In 980 AH Sultan Akbar ibn Humayun took Gujarat; he was four generations removed from Timur {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 391}.

## Details
Ibn al-'Imad records, citing al-Nur, that in 980 Sultan Akbar ibn Humayun took Gujarat; he was of Timur's line, four fathers between them, and was of great state, favoured by fortune, long-lived and vastly extended in his kingdom {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 391}. The source describes him as just, while noting that he inclined toward the unbelievers, approving their sayings and admiring their doings — a report attributed to the chronicler, not the compiler's own verdict {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 391}. He died in Jumada al-Akhira 1014, his sultanate having lasted fifty years, and was succeeded by his son Salim {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 391}. The taking of Gujarat was a step in the expansion of his Indian realm that his descendants carried forward after him.
