## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates the apostasy of the Banu Wali'a and Kinda under [[person/al-ash-ath-ibn-qays|al-Ash'ath ibn Qays]], which is part of the [[event/ridda-wars|Ridda wars]]. It ended in the siege of the fortress of [[place/al-nujayr|al-Nujayr]] in the caliphate of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The cause
[[person/ziyad-ibn-labid-al-bayadi|Ziyad ibn Labid al-Bayadi]] was the Prophet's agent over Hadramawt, and Kinda was added to his charge. He took from some of Kinda a she-camel as alms and branded it with the brand of alms. They asked him to return it and take another instead, and he refused, so Kinda rose against him, except for al-Sakun. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The fighting
Ziyad made a night attack on the Banu Amr ibn Mu'awiya, killing a hundred of them, among them the four sons of Ma'di Karib. Then he defeated al-Ash'ath when he met him. Ziyad wrote to [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] for reinforcement, and he ordered [[person/al-muhajir-ibn-abi-umayya|al-Muhajir ibn Abi Umayya]] to go to his aid. Al-Muhajir and Ziyad defeated al-Ash'ath's men, and they took refuge in al-Nujayr. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The siege
The Muslims besieged them until they were exhausted. Al-Ash'ath asked for safe conduct for a number of them and took himself out of that number. He came down to Ziyad and al-Muhajir, who sent him to Abu Bakr; Abu Bakr pardoned him, and married him to his sister Umm Farwa, and in another account to a cousin. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Abu Bakr ordered the hands of some women of al-Nujayr cut off, who had rejoiced at the death of the Prophet. He sent reinforcement with [[person/ikrima-ibn-abi-jahl|Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl]], who came after al-Nujayr had been taken. The captives of al-Nujayr were returned for ransom, four hundred dirhams for each head. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Abu Bakr's words
Al-Baladhuri quotes Abu Bakr as regretting three things, among them: "I wish that on the day al-Ash'ath ibn Qays was brought to me I had struck his neck." {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The source also gives the apostasy of Khawlan, to which Abu Bakr sent Ya'la ibn Umayya, and the division of Yemen among al-Muhajir, Ziyad and Ya'la. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The year of the event is derived from Abu Bakr's caliphate rather than an explicit statement, and needs an additional source.
