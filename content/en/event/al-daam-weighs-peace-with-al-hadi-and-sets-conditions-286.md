## Overview
It reached [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] that the brother of [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] had come to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] with fifty horsemen, and that weighed on him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} The report is worded "it is said of him", that is from unnamed sources; the museum attributes it to the narrator and gives no verdict.

## The council at Khiwan
The narrator says of al-Da'am that he gathered his companions and the people of [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]] and said: is it not strange that I have come to be in black and Abu al-Atahiya in white; as for me, I have resolved not to fight the son of the Messenger of God, so what do you say. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} The editor's footnote explains that the badge of the Alids was mostly white and that of the Abbasid state black. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} Some of his companions said: no, fight and we will fight with you, and do not take a rule you fought for against [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]] and others and then hand it to the Alid, in peace. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111}

## Al-Da'am's letters and al-Hadi's answer
Al-Da'am's letters had been coming to al-Hadi while he was at the al-Darb of Banu Rabi'a, stipulating terms such as the tax collection of part of the land and the governorship of part. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} Al-Hadi used to say that if it pleased him to appoint him over a span of the earth he would not have appointed him over the Muslims, and it would not be allowed him before the Lord of the worlds, unless he repented and returned to the truth. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} Al-Hadi stayed at the al-Darb of Banu Sarim some days of Muharram. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.111} These are al-Hadi's words as the narrator gives them, and the museum gives no verdict on them.
