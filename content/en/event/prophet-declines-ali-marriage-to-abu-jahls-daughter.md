## Summary
[[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] wanted to marry the daughter of Abu Jahl in addition to [[person/fatima|Fatima]], so the Prophet addressed the people from his pulpit, said that Fatima was "a part of me," and refused to grant Banu Hashim permission to marry their daughter to Ali {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 84}.

## The Sermon
The book narrates on the authority of al-Miswar ibn Makhrama that he heard the Prophet on the pulpit saying that Banu Hashim had asked his permission to marry their daughter to Ali, and he would not grant them permission unless Ali divorced his daughter and married theirs, because his daughter was a part of him: what troubles her troubles him, and what harms her harms him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 84}. The book also mentions another narration in Muslim regarding the same sermon, in which the Prophet feared that Fatima would be tested in her religion {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 84}.

## Interpretation of the Prohibition
Al-Sallabi holds that the Prophet did not forbid what is lawful, and that his statement "I do not forbid what is lawful" means he does not say anything contrary to God's ruling, but he forbade combining the two women in marriage for two stated reasons: that it would lead to harming Fatima, thereby harming the Prophet, and the fear of trial for her due to jealousy {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 85}. He cites another view that the intent was to inform them that the two would not be combined by God's grace, without naming its proponent {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 85}.

## Context
The book mentions this sermon in the context of Fatima's standing with the Prophet, including A'isha's report that she never saw anyone more truthful in speech than her except her father {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 85}.
