Three nights before the march to Khaybar, in Muharram of the seventh year of the Hijra, Abd al-Rahman ibn Uyayna ibn Hisn raided with horsemen of Ghatafan and Fazara upon the sadaqa camels and the Prophet's milch-camels at al-Ghaba; they killed their herdsman and drove them off. {cite:sahih-muslim}

The young Companion [[person/salama-ibn-al-akwa|Salama ibn al-Akwa']] saw them as he went out in the morning with his bow and arrows; he cried through Medina: "alas for the morning!", then set off running after the men on his own feet like a stormwind, shooting them with arrows and stones in the defiles and calling: "take it — I am the son of al-Akwa', and today is the day of the suckling ones." {cite:sahih-muslim}

He kept shooting them and hamstringing their horses until they dropped the milch-camels and everything they carried of cloaks and spears, and he recovered all the camels single-handed before the Muslim cavalry under Abu Qatada caught up with him. The Prophet granted Salama two shares — the horseman's share and the foot-soldier's — and said: "our best horseman today is Abu Qatada, and our best foot-runner Salama ibn al-Akwa'." {cite:sahih-muslim}
