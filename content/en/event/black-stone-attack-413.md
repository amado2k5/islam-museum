## Summary
In the pilgrimage season of 413, a man from among the pilgrims of Egypt struck the [[object/al-hajar-al-aswad|Black Stone]] with a mace and broke a piece from it, and the people killed him on the spot. A riot then broke out against the people of Egypt and the Maghrebis, in which about twenty were killed, and they were robbed on the road to Mina. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.350-351}

## The eyewitness account
Ibn al-Jawzi, citing Abu al-Ghana'im, gives an account close to the event: a large, red-complexioned man, carrying a sword and a mace, climbed to the Stone on the day of departure from Mina, which was a Friday, and struck it three times in succession, saying, "How long will the Stone be worshipped? Neither Muhammad nor Ali can stop me." The people then rushed at him and killed him, and killed about twenty of his helpers; four of them were burned. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.351}

## The fracture and repair
A fragment broke off the Stone, made up of three small pieces, and its outer layer came away on every side until something visible remained. The Banu Shayba gathered what had broken off, mixed it with musk-scented water and lac, and daubed the place where it was missing. Ibn al-Jawzi says that it remained in that state until his own day. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.351}
