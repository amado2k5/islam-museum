## Summary
The Battle of the Three Kings at al-Qasr al-Kabir, Monday 30 Jumada II 986/4 August 1578 {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 258-268}.

## The muster
[[person/sebastian-i-portugal|Sebastian]] massed tens of thousands of Spanish, Portuguese, Italians and Germans in a thousand ships to restore his deposed ally [[person/muhammad-ii-mutawakkil-saadi|al-Mutawakkil]]; Morocco's people rallied to [[person/abd-al-malik-al-sadi|Abd al-Malik]], steadied by [[person/abu-al-mahasin-yusuf-al-fasi|Abu al-Mahasin al-Fasi]], and Morocco's ulema answered al-Mutawakkil's letter with one exposing his enlistment of infidels against Muslims {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 260-263}.

## The plan
Abd al-Malik drew the enemy to a field far from its fleet, had the river's only bridge blown by a squadron under his brother al-Mansur, placed his artillery in front and kept cavalry in reserve to run down the rout {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 264}.

## The sultan's death and the victory
Ill as he was, Abd al-Malik repelled the first charge sword drawn — 'since when does sickness bar Muslims from jihad?' — then died in his tent, finger to his lips; only his brother Ahmad al-Mansur and the chamberlain Ridwan knew, the latter still relaying orders in his name. Al-Mansur struck the Portuguese rear and they broke toward the destroyed bridge; drowned, slain and taken, three kings perished in four hours and a third {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 265}.

## The results
[[person/ahmad-al-mansur-al-sadi|Ahmad al-Mansur]] was acclaimed on the field; the Sa'di star rose until Europe's embassies kept morning and evening at her palace gates, Portugal's fell, and vast ransoms flowed to redeem her captives {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 267-268}.
