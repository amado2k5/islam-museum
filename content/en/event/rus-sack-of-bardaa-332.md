## Overview
Ibn al-Athir relates in "al-Kamil" that a group of the Rus came out on the sea in 332 AH and went up the river Kur to [[place/bardhaa|Bardha'a]]. They defeated the deputy of the marzban and his Daylami and volunteer troops, and entered the city. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.308-310}

## The events
The Rus called out safety to the people of the city and behaved well at first, as Ibn al-Athir says. Then their crier called for the people of the city to leave within three days. Most of them stayed, so the Rus put them to the sword and took some ten thousand captives. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.308-310}

They gathered those who remained in the mosque and demanded a ransom of twenty dirhams for each man. When the money was not collected they killed them, and enslaved the captives. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.308-310}

## The end
Ibn al-Athir says that the Rus ate too many fruits, and a plague and death struck them. The [[person/marzuban-ibn-muhammad|marzuban]] set an ambush for them, and a great number of them were killed, among them their commander, and the rest were besieged in the fortress of Shahrastan. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.308-310}

## Miskawayh's account
[[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] tells the story among the events of 332 AH in [[work/tajarib-al-umam|Tajarib al-umam]] in greater detail, and from witnesses: he cites Abu al-Abbas ibn Nudar "and a number of reliable informants", and says he heard remarkable stories from people who had seen the Rus. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.95} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.99} Ibn al-Athir's account above follows it closely in order and figures. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.308-310}

### Who they were and how they came
Miskawayh describes the [[group/al-rus|Rus]] as "a great nation, of huge build and great strength", who know no defeat. Each of them carried his weapons and hung on himself most of a craftsman's tools — axe, saw, hammer — and fought on foot with spear, shield and sword. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.94} They crossed the sea next to their country to the river Kur, the river of Barda'a, which comes down from the mountains of Azerbaijan and Armenia. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.94}

### The fall of Barda'a
[[person/marzuban-ibn-muhammad|Al-Marzuban]]'s deputy at Barda'a went out against them with three hundred Daylamites, about as many vagabonds and Kurds, and some five thousand volunteers who came out "to wage jihad against them", thinking them no different from the Armenians and Byzantines. Within an hour the Rus charged and routed them; the Daylamites held for a while and were killed, all but the horsemen, and the Rus occupied the town. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.94} They proclaimed to the people: "There is no dispute between us and you over religion; we seek only rule. It is for us to govern well, and for you to obey well." {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.95}

### The massacre and the ransom
The common people of the town kept joining the Muslim troops when they attacked the Rus, and pelted them with stones. When this went on, the Rus proclaimed that no townsman was to remain and gave them three days. Only a few left; on the fourth day the Rus put the rest to the sword and took captive "some ten thousand and more men and boys" with their women. They shut the women and children in a fortress inside the town, gathered the men in the congregational mosque and told them: "Buy yourselves back." {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.95}

A Christian in the town known as Ibn Sam'un, who acted as go-between, agreed with the Rus that each man would be ransomed for twenty dirhams. "The sensible among the Muslims" accepted, but the others refused, saying he wanted to put Muslims on the same footing as Christians paying the jizya, and he gave up. When the Rus got nothing, they killed the men, except a few who escaped through a narrow channel that carried water to the mosque and those who ransomed themselves with hidden savings. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.96}

### Al-Marzuban's war and the ambush
Al-Marzuban ibn Muhammad gathered his army and called people to arms, marching with thirty thousand men, but for many days the Muslims had the worst of it. Then an epidemic broke out among the Rus after they gorged on fruit; Miskawayh explains that their own country is very cold and grows no trees. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.97} Al-Marzuban laid an ambush by night and arranged for his army to flee before the Rus until they had passed it; the Rus commander came out riding a donkey. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.97} Miskawayh quotes al-Marzuban himself: his men kept running, so he turned back alone with his brother, his close followers and his slaves, "resolved on martyrdom"; the Daylamites were shamed into returning, they shouted for the ambush, which came out behind the Rus, and they killed seven hundred of them, their commander among them. The rest shut themselves in the fortress. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.98}

### The withdrawal
During the siege news reached al-Marzuban that [[person/al-husayn-ibn-said-al-hamdani|al-Husayn ibn Sa'id ibn Hamdan]] had entered Azerbaijan; he left one of his commanders to fight the Rus with five hundred Daylamites, fifteen hundred Kurdish horsemen and two thousand volunteers, and marched against him. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.98} The epidemic worsened among the Rus. When one of them died they buried with him his weapons, clothes and tools, his wife or another woman, and his slave, "according to a custom of theirs"; afterwards the Muslims dug up their graves and took out swords "that are sought after to this day". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.99} Finally they left the fortress by night with all the money, jewels and captives they could carry, burned the rest, and went to their ships on the Kur. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.99}

### A witness's story
Miskawayh records a story that spread in the region and that he heard from more than one person: five Rus, among them a youth from the families of their chiefs, were trapped in a garden in Barda'a. A large force of Daylamites and others surrounded them, trying to take even one prisoner, and could not, until the five had killed many times their number. The youth was the last left; when he saw he would be taken, he climbed a tree and stabbed himself with his dagger until he fell dead. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.99} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.100}
