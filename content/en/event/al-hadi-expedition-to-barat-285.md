## Overview
The book says the matter of [[place/barat-yemen|Barat]], also in the land of Shakir, began when some of its people came to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] at [[place/saada|Sa'da]], pledged to him and asked him to send a governor with them; he sent one of his companions, a party of them rejected him and barred him from leading the Friday prayer, and he wrote to al-Hadi about it on the twenty-second of Safar. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.82} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.83} It is a Zaydi account, the praise and explanations being the narrator's, and the museum gives the event as it stands.

## The mountain and the march
He told the people to get ready, had his tent pitched and set out that day for Barat, a great, towering, rugged mountain no ruler had ever entered, with only three ways up and a great multitude of Hamdan of Shakir, called "Dahma", for its people. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.83} He reached them on Sunday and halted at a place called "al-Hajar", where some of their clan were, and sent men with them to their companions to call and inform them; then he moved to "Udla" below the mountain, and a large crowd of them came out for the way up to prevent him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.83} He sent a party of his companions, among them [[person/yusuf-ibn-muhammad-al-hasani|Yusuf ibn Muhammad al-Hasani]], Ali ibn al-Husayn, Abu Fatima al-Futaymi, Muhammad ibn al-Zubayr and Abd al-Malik ibn Abd al-Malik al-Yarsami, who climbed the mountain and took the road from the enemy, and al-Hadi climbed with his army to the summit and told them to stand where they were; he went on with eight horses and twenty-six men, drew near the people, called and admonished them, and said: "People, between us and you is the Book of God; obey us so long as we obey God, and if we disobey God we have no claim on your obedience." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.83}

## The fight
He asked them to leave him the way to the water to drink and wash, thirst having hurt him and his men, but they refused, stood between him and the water and shot at him and his companions with arrows; an arrow hit him and some companions were wounded. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.84} Seeing they wanted nothing but to fight him, he charged, his companions following, and God gave him victory; three of them were killed and some wounded and a number captured; he stopped a man of his who wished to strike some of them; the people fled and the army stripped them and meant to kill them, but al-Hadi cried "Whoever kills a man shall be killed for him", and told the narrator the people had no "fi'a" to return to, meaning an amir whose command they obey, and were a rabble gathered together. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.84}

## Security and the return of the spoils
They asked for security and to pledge, he accepted and gave them security, they pledged to him on the truth, enjoining good and forbidding wrong, and asked him to free the captives and return the spoils; he freed the captives, gathered his army and said that the spoils were lawful booty for them but they should give them to him to return to the people to win them over, hoping God would provide elsewhere and he would compensate them; they handed it over, he returned it and freed the captives. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.84} He stayed three days at Barat without anyone being harmed in crops or otherwise, none of his army lodged in their houses or fed from their fodder, so that their horses ate "mawsaj", a thorny shrub. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.84} See [[concept/ghanima]].

## Reports on the army's conduct
One of the soldiers says he let a camel of his loose, it went into some crops, and the field's owner said he would tell al-Hadi; the soldier disowned the camel from fear of al-Hadi, and when the owner saw he had abandoned it he called "Take your camel", and he went back and took it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.85} Al-Hadi was heard to say the best thing he had seen of his army was that a group camped away from him under a dawm tree, the nabiq, and took nothing from it, since he passed and saw the branches as they were. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.85}

## The return
Al-Hadi left Barat on Thursday, its people having listened and obeyed, and left a man called Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan, of Najran, as their governor. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.85} He reached Sa'da on Sunday, the last day of Safar 285 AH. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.85}
