## Overview
On Tuesday Muhammad wrote to his two sons, telling them of events and ordering them to press al-Hadi for reinforcements, and to raid, with Yam and Ahlaf, at Sawhan, the village of a clan whose name is withheld in this record. Banu al-Harith then came to Ibn Humayd from the far end of their land, and the fighting at the Darb gate lasted until sunset. Ibn Humayd withdrew to his camp and ordered a patrol around the village that no one might enter or leave.

## Detail
Muhammad at once wrote to his two sons, telling them what Banu al-Harith had done, and ordering them to write to al-Hadi and urge him to send reinforcements. This was on Tuesday, with ten days of Dhu al-Hijja remaining, which the record reads as 20 Dhu al-Hijja (derived). He ordered one of them to raid with Yam and Ahlaf against Sawhan, the village of a clan whose name is withheld in this record; the book says these men were among the most eager for the disturbance, and the record gives that as the narrator's view. Banu al-Harith came to Ibn Humayd from the far end of their land, and he led them to the village; the fighting at the Darb gate went on until the sun set. Ibn Humayd then returned to his camp and ordered a patrol around the village that no one might enter or leave. The printed day is Tuesday; the calendar conversion to the Julian date is derived.
