## Overview
[[person/nur-al-din-mahmud-ibn-zangi|Nur al-Din Mahmud ibn Zangi]] ordered a minbar for the Holy City in 1169; [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Saladin]] brought it into [[building/al-aqsa-mosque|al-Aqsa]] in 1187, where it stood until an arson attack destroyed it in 1969. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 735}

## What happened
The minbar was a masterpiece of Syrian woodwork, made by Nur al-Din's order for Jerusalem, which he hoped to regain from the Crusaders. When Saladin took the city in 1187 he reconsecrated the mosque, renewed the decoration of the whole qibla wall including the mihrab, and installed this minbar. It stood in the mosque for eight centuries until a deliberately set fire destroyed it in 1969, before it had been fully studied. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 6 p. 735}
