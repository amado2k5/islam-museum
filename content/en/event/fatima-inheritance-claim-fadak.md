## Summary
After the death of [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]], [[person/fatima|Fatima]] and [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]] came to [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] asking for their inheritance: the Prophet's land in [[place/fadak|Fadak]] and his share of Khaybar, whereupon Abu Bakr said he had heard the Prophet say, "We are not inherited from; whatever we leave behind is charity" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.129}. The book presents the Sunni response to the Imami Shi'a reports on this issue {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.129-137}.

## Abu Bakr's Report
The book states that Abu Bakr said, "I will not leave aside anything that the Messenger of God used to do without doing it," and that he feared if he left aside anything of his command he would go astray {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.129}. It mentions that the Prophet's wives sent Uthman to ask about their inheritance, and Aisha said, "Did the Messenger of God not say, 'We are not inherited from; whatever we leave behind is charity'?" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.129-130}.

## Who Narrated It
The book states, quoting Ibn Taymiyya, that the hadith was narrated from the Prophet by Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Sa'd, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, al-Abbas, the Prophet's wives, Abu Hurayra, and Hudhayfa {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.131}. The book says that Ali did not divide the estate during his caliphate, nor did he give anything from it to Fatima's children, and had it been an injustice, removing it would have been easier for him than fighting Muawiya {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.134-135}.

## Fadak in Imami Reports
The book cites Imami reports attributed to al-Baqir and al-Sadiq stating that Fatima alone inherited from the Prophet and that women do not inherit real property, and considers that these reports contradict themselves, because if Fatima did not inherit real property she would not have asked for Fadak in the first place {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.131-133}. The book likewise refutes the claim that Ali testified for Fatima, describing it as baseless {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.134}.

## Fatima's Satisfaction
The book transmits al-Sha'bi's report that Abu Bakr visited Fatima during her illness, asked permission to enter, was granted permission, and sought her goodwill, and that she was pleased with him and died while pleased {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.136}. The book considers that Aisha's report denies the visit while al-Sha'bi affirms it, and the affirmative takes precedence, and that the estrangement mentioned in the hadith refers to withdrawal rather than forbidden severing of ties {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.136-137}.

## Perspectives
**Sunnis (as presented by the book):** "We are not inherited from" is a sound hadith well known among the Companions, denied by none, and acted upon during Ali's caliphate {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.130-135}.

**The Imami Shi'a View (as transmitted by the book):** Holds that Abu Bakr and Umar usurped Fadak and the Prophet's inheritance from Fatima, and that the hadith of non-inheritance is fabricated; the book refutes this both in chain of transmission and in logic {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.129-134}.
