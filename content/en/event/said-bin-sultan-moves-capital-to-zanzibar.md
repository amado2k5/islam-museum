## Summary
[[person/said-bin-sultan|Said bin Sultan]] moved his capital from Muscat to [[place/zanzibar|Zanzibar]] in the 1830s, and it remained his capital in the years when his power was at its commercial height. {cite:wikipedia-said-bin-sultan-en} {cite:britannica-said-ibn-sultan}

## What the sources say about the move
Neither the English article nor Britannica names a single year for the move; both place it in the 1830s. Britannica says Said made Zanzibar the leading power in East Africa and the commercial capital of the western Indian Ocean. {cite:britannica-said-ibn-sultan} The English article says he introduced clove cultivation to Zanzibar and Pemba, which became among the world's largest producers, and that Zanzibar became the port for ivory, slaves and spices. {cite:wikipedia-said-bin-sultan-en} The museum records the slave trade as the sources describe it, as part of the sultanate's economy in that period, without praise or blame.
