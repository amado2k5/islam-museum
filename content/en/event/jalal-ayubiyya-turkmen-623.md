## Summary
In 623 AH Jalal al-Din struck the Aywai Turcomans who had seized Ushtur and Urmiya and cut the roads of Azerbaijan, slaughtering and enslaving them heavily. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 467}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that the Aywai Turcomans had taken control of the towns of Ushtur and Urmiya in the Azerbaijan region and levied tribute on the people of Khoi to spare them; encouraged by Jalal al-Din's preoccupation with the Georgians and his distance at Akhlat they grew greedy, spreading through Azerbaijan plundering and cutting the roads, until they cut the road near Tabriz itself and seized from its merchants goods including twenty thousand head of sheep; Jalal al-Din's wife, daughter of sultan Tughril, and his deputies in the country wrote begging rescue, telling him the Aywai had ruined the land and it would perish outright if he did not reach it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 467} This coincided with his fear of the snow, so he left Akhlat and hastened to the Aywai, who sat secure believing him still at Akhlat; suddenly the Jalali armies surrounded them and the sword took them from every side — much killing, plunder and enslavement of women and children, and countless spoil taken — then he returned to Tabriz. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 468}
