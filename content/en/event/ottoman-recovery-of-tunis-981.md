## Summary
Sultan Selim II ordered his vizier Sinan Pasha and his admiral Kilij Ali to head for [[place/tunis|Tunis]] to take it permanently and restore Ottoman authority there. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.285}

## Preparation
Two hundred and thirty-eight ships were fitted out, and oarsmen were brought. Hayder Pasha, the Ottoman governor in Tunis, who had withdrawn to Kairouan, was gathering fighters from among the people. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.285}

## The campaign
The fleet set sail on 23 Muharram 982 AH, corresponding to 14 May 1574 CE. It struck the Calabrian coast and Messina and seized a Christian ship, then crossed the open sea in five days. Hayder Pasha arrived, together with a force of Algerians under Ramadan Pasha, a force from Tripoli under Mustafa Pasha, and volunteers from Egypt. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.285}

According to the source, the fighting began in spring 981 AH. The Ottomans seized Halq al-Wadi after a tight siege. Tunis itself was besieged; the Spanish fled, together with the Hafsid king Muhammad ibn al-Hasan, to al-Bastyun, a fort the Spanish had fortified heavily. The Ottomans turned to besiege it and tightened the encirclement. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.285}

The sailing date (982 AH) precedes the start of fighting (981 AH) in the source's text; this needs an additional source.
