## Overview
al-Baladhuri relates from al-Waqidi that writing in Arabic was rare among the Aws and Khazraj. Some Jews taught it, and the boys of Medina used to learn it in the earliest times. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The scribes
When Islam came, a number of the Ansar could write, among them [[person/sad-ibn-ubada|Sa'd ibn Ubada]], [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-amr|al-Mundhir ibn Amr]], [[person/ubayy-ibn-kab|Ubayy ibn Ka'b]] and [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]]. Zayd could write Arabic and Hebrew. The report also names [[person/rafi-ibn-malik|Rafi' ibn Malik]], [[person/usayd-ibn-hudayr|Usayd ibn Hudayr]], [[person/bashir-ibn-sad|Bashir ibn Sa'd]], [[person/aws-ibn-khawli|Aws ibn Khawli]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abd Allah ibn Ubayy]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The accomplished
The "accomplished" (kamil) among them was one who combined writing with archery and swimming. The report counts Rafi', Sa'd, Usayd, Abd Allah ibn Ubayy and Aws among them. From the people of Yathrib in the pre-Islamic period were [[person/suwayd-ibn-al-samit|Suwayd ibn al-Samit]] and Khudayr al-Kata'ib. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Zayd's account
al-Baladhuri attributes to Zayd ibn Thabit that the Prophet ordered him to learn the writing of the Jews. He learned it in less than half a month, and afterwards he wrote to them on the Prophet's behalf and read what they wrote. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Note
The report rests on a single source, and the data gives no specific date for it.
