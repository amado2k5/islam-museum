## Overview
This completes the sentence cut at the foot of p. 225 in [[event/al-hadi-enters-sanaa-a-second-time-after-leaving-shibam-288|the account of the second entry into Sana'a]]: the Aqili man who had been stabbed in the belly died {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.226}. The people wondered at the steadfastness of [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] and his men in spite of their few numbers against the many horse and foot of their opponents, and when their wonder reached him he said, as the narrator gives it, that they need not wonder, for with two thousand foot and five hundred horse of steadfast believers he would subdue most of the earth {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.226}. He used to cite what [[passage/quran-2-249|Qur'an 2:249]] says of the small company that overcomes a large one by God's leave, and to recite a verse in the tawil metre whose sense is that the doors of hardship are opened by patience {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.226}.

## Detail
The narrator gives his prayer in battle: he asked God to inspire him with patience, to enlarge the reward and to accept the work as purely His, then said that God was enough for them, and when he killed a man with his own hand he said that they fought these men for God's sake, over what they had rejected of His book and after the call to the truth, and asked God to judge between him and them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.226}. The margin of the manuscript carries a heading for each of the two passages, which is the copyist's note and shows that the words and prayer are attributed to al-Hadi by the narrator. The museum describes and gives no verdict, and does not repeat the narrator's word of unbelief for al-Hadi's opponents. Al-Hadi then came back from following the men and entered Sana'a on a Friday of 288 AH, where he stayed {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.226}.
