## Overview
The French attack on Egypt was in 1213 AH (1798 CE), according to Ali al-Salabi, the first attack on an Arab province of the Ottoman state in modern history. Sultan [[person/selim-iii-uthmani|Selim III]] immediately declared jihad, and Muslims in the Hijaz, Syria and North Africa responded. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.329-330}

## The call to jihad in the Hijaz
Al-Salabi quotes al-Jabarti, in the events of Sha'ban 1213 AH, that the people of the Hijaz were alarmed when news of the French reached them. Shaykh Muhammad al-Kilani preached to the people and called them to jihad, and about six hundred fighters gathered and took ship to al-Qusayr. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.329-330}

From them, with the Muslims of Upper Egypt, the Hawara Arabs, the people of Nubia and the forces of Murad Bey, a front was formed against the French. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.329-330}

## After Cairo
The book says that Napoleon went on occupying the cities of Egypt, Gaza, Ramla and Jaffa, and tried to occupy Acre, but its people under Ahmad Pasha al-Jazzar stood in the way. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.329-330}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "A Story and an Idea"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives the twenty-ninth episode of his programme [[work/kissa-wa-fikra-program|"A Story and an Idea"]] to Napoleon and the campaign, under the idea of "civilisational shock". In the Crusades, he says, a backward Europe was shocked by the Islamic civilisation it met, and that shock helped its renaissance; later Muslims closed their minds, and woke to Napoleon's cannon at the gates of Egypt to find a deep gap between themselves and Europe. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:01:30} What follows is his account.

He says reports from French diplomats, above all the consul Charles Magallon, urged occupation, and that Magallon's report also advised respecting Egyptian customs and religion and winning people over through their scholars. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:07:00} France gave the "Army of the East" to Napoleon; it set out on 12 April 1798 with a Commission of Sciences and Arts and a press with Arabic type, the destination kept secret even from the soldiers, and 36,000 soldiers sailed from Toulon on 300 ships escorted by 55 warships. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:09:00} Nelson reached Alexandria three days ahead of them and offered help to its governor, al-Sayyid Muhammad Kurayyim, who took it for an English trick and refused. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:10:30} The French took Alexandria on 2 July 1798 after a few hours of resistance, which al-Suwaidan puts down to the gap in arms, not to any lack of courage. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:11:00} On the Nile the Mamluk boats were beaten in a first battle, with peasants attacking from both banks, and Murad Bey fought again at Imbaba on 21 July 1798, the battle the French called the Battle of the Pyramids; the authorities in Cairo surrendered the next day. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:13:30}

On 25 July 1798 Napoleon set up a diwan of nine senior Egyptian scholars with an advisory role only, forbade looting but kept the Mamluks' taxes. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:15:30} He issued a proclamation opening with the basmala, presenting the French as Muslims loyal to the Ottoman sultan; al-Suwaidan calls it deceptive, and says Egyptians at first believed it because he controlled all information. He walked Cairo's streets in a turban and attended Friday prayer. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:17:30} More than 150 scholars and more than 2,000 specialists came with the army; they produced the *Description de l'Égypte*, in more than twenty volumes of text, pictures and maps, which he calls a key historical document still, and in 1799 the Rosetta Stone was found, its several scripts becoming the key to reading the pharaonic texts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:20:30}

Nelson destroyed the French fleet at Abu Qir. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:21:00} When the people saw through Napoleon's claim, a revolt led from al-Azhar broke out; he crushed it, shelling al-Azhar, killing scholars and destroying its libraries and manuscripts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:22:00} He marched into Palestine, took Jaffa but failed before Acre after two months, then defeated an Ottoman army landed at Abu Qir. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:23:00} Called home, he left Kléber in command, and Kléber was later killed by Sulayman al-Halabi; after defeat and the loss of their fleet, the French signed a treaty handing Egypt back. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:25:00}

For al-Suwaidan the campaign began the end of Mamluk power, opened the way for [[person/muhammad-ali-pasha|Muhammad Ali Pasha]] and revived the spirit of resistance in Egypt, Syria and other Arab lands. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:25:30} It brought Egyptians their first press with Arabic type and the beginning of interest in pharaonic antiquities, and pushed Muhammad Ali to modernise and send educational missions to Europe; even the missions' shaykh, [[person/rifaa-al-tahtawi|Rifa'a al-Tahtawi]], he adds, could not tell the West's material progress apart from its lack of values. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan kissa-wa-fikra/ep29 00:27:30}
