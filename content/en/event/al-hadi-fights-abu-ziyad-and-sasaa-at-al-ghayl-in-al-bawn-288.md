## Overview
Printed p. 224 begins, after the p. 223 that is missing from the scan, with the end of a poem that names Dhu al-Fiqar and the stands of the poet's grandfather Ali; the poet is not named in the text read and the museum does not enter the lines {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.224}. The narrator then says that Abu Ziyad and Sa'sa'a, with those who were with them, were routed until they came to a village in al-Bawn called al-Ghayl, a strong fort where their camp was {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.224}. [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] followed them and fought them hard at [[place/al-ghayl-al-bawn-yemen|al-Ghayl in al-Bawn]] until evening, and then went back to [[place/raydah-yemen|Raydah]] safe and with spoil, none of his companions killed {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.224}. On the road his companions who had been routed and had left him met him; he turned back and spent the night at Raydah, and in the morning he ordered the heads of the slain cut off, and they were cut off and carried to Sa'da and Najran {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.224}.

## Detail
This al-Ghayl is in al-Bawn, not [[place/al-ghayl-saada|al-Ghayl near Sa'da]] where the men put out of Najran were jailed in 286 AH; the name is the same and nothing more is claimed. The other manuscript's reading is al-Ghayl and the scan reads it so. [[event/battle-of-raydah-288|The battle of Raydah]] of the added first pages says al-Hadi routed Sa'sa'a and the people of Qudum at the end of Jumada II, while this account does not name Raydah as the field; the museum does not reconcile the two. The count of the dead and the description of the routed are the narrator's. The carrying of the heads is mentioned briefly as the text gives it, with no description and no judgment. It follows [[event/al-hadi-leaves-shibam-fights-on-the-road-and-goes-by-najr-and-al-bawn-to-raydah-and-bayt-dhud-288]].
