## Overview
In 626 AH al-Malik al-Kamil handed Jerusalem to the emperor, king of the Franks, according to Ibn al-Imad; according to Ibn al-Athir, by a treaty concluded at the beginning of Rabi al-Akhir. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.126} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.481}

## Ibn al-Imad's account
He says that the emperor corresponded with al-Kamil asking for Jerusalem, so as to raise his standing among the kings, binding himself to send him its revenues. Al-Kamil answered him. Then the siege of Damascus followed, and the peace was concluded in Sha'ban. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.126}

## Ibn al-Athir's account
He says that the agreement between al-Kamil, al-Ashraf and the emperor decided on the handover of Jerusalem and a few other places, on condition that Hebron, Nablus, the Ghawr and Tiberias remained in Muslim hands. He says the wall of Jerusalem was in ruins, and that the Muslims were appalled by what had happened. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.481} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.482}

The two accounts differ in some details, and we present both without preferring one.

## Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's account
[[person/sibt-ibn-al-jawzi|Sibt ibn al-Jawzi]], among the events of 626 AH in [[work/mirat-al-zaman|Mir'at al-zaman]], relates that [[person/al-malik-al-kamil|al-Kamil]] gave Jerusalem to the emperor (al-Inbiratur), and that al-Ashraf went to al-Kamil, found he had already handed it over, and reproached him; al-Kamil answered: "nothing drove me to this but al-Mu'azzam", meaning that al-Mu'azzam had given the emperor the land from the Jordan to the sea, while al-Kamil gave him the estates from the gate of Jerusalem to Jaffa. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.296} He writes: "news came of the surrender of Jerusalem to the Franks, and the lands of Islam were in uproar... and mourning ceremonies were held". {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.296}

He tells that al-Nasir Dawud asked him to preach in the Damascus mosque on what had befallen the Holy House; he did so, al-Nasir Dawud attending at the gate of the shrine of Ali, on a memorable day; among his words: "the delegations of visitors to the Holy House are cut off; alas for the loneliness of those who dwell by it... may God console the Muslims; what shame for the kings of the Muslims", and he recited verses composed by a friend. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.296} {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.297}

He reports from unnamed informants ("they said") stories of [[person/frederick-ii|the emperor]]'s stay in Jerusalem: when the noon call sounded, his servants, pages and his Sicilian tutor in logic stood and prayed, being Muslims; al-Kamil had ordered Shams al-Din, judge of Nablus, that the muezzins not climb the minarets while the emperor was in Jerusalem, and when the emperor learned of it he told the judge: "you have done wrong, judge; you change your rites, your law and your religion for my sake!", gave ten dinars each to the mosque staff, muezzins and residents, stayed only two nights, and went back to Jaffa for fear of the Templars. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.300} He attributes to them the judgement that from his words he seemed "a materialist, merely playing with Christianity". {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.300} In the same year he records al-Kamil and al-Ashraf's four-month siege of Damascus until al-Nasir Dawud surrendered it on 1 Sha'ban, receiving al-Karak, Ajlun, al-Salt, Nablus, Jerusalem and Hebron. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.300}
