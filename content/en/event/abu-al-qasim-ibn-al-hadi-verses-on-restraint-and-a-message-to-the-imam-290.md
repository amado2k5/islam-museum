## Overview
At the foot of p. 259 and the head of p. 260 are two pieces in which Abu al-Qasim says that his aim was held back from meeting the lances at the time of wishes, in deliberation, that his concern is not wine nor listening to a singer, that his soul called him to every lofty matter, and that he is not one who hopes to be saved by wishing {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.259} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.260}. In a piece on p. 260 he tells the commander of the faithful not to heed his distance or nearness, to rise for God with effort, and to suppose him among the slain; he says that he is content with his trial in God, that nights pass after the trial, that the world does not last and that they will gather at the stations before his grandfather, the Messenger of God, on the Day of Reckoning {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.260}.

## Detail
The museum reads "the commander of the faithful" here as his father al-Hadi from the context; the book does not say so. The last line on p. 260 on his grandfather's demanding of them what they had deceived in his family is given by the book as the poet's speech and the museum gives no verdict on it. Before this are [[event/abu-al-qasim-ibn-al-hadi-composes-verses-in-prison-290|the earlier pieces of the same section]]. The museum does not reproduce the lines nor the words of insult in them and gives only their sense as the book has it; the book ascribes the verse to Abu al-Qasim and the museum has not checked the attribution. The praise and the epithets in the lines are the poet's, given as the book gives them, with no verdict in the museum's voice.
