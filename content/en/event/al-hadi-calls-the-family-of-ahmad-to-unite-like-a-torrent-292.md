## Overview
"And your enemies are in ease and plenty"; he calls the family of Ahmad: "so brace yourselves, guard your religion and muster, and rise, for you are keen cutting blades" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.302}.

## Detail
He says that others "agreed in one grip, helped each other and defended together in it", so the family of Ahmad should rise like a torrent let down; "honour is only patience in the press of battle", kingship is honour, command and wealth, the best of them is the one whom nature has refined, who guards and avenges and is awake in all states, and they are "the remnant of the blades and their leavings", a youth of them dying by sword or lance, "our father the Commander of the Believers and our grandfather the Messenger" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.302}. A footnote says that the verse about the life "on whose borders kingship spreads" is missing from manuscript S {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.302}. It is a poet's call to the unity of the family of Abu Talib and to fighting, ascribed to al-Hadi in a Zaydi book, and the museum does not judge what it says. It follows [[event/al-hadi-to-his-cousins-of-the-prophets-family-on-wakefulness-and-their-division-292|his description of their division]] and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-says-in-verse-he-built-a-house-of-glory-and-revived-the-book-292|verses on his building a house of glory and reviving the Book]]. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
