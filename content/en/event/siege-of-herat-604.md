## Overview
In 604 AH Husayn ibn Kharmil, lord of Herat, was killed after men of Jaldak ibn Tughril seized him, by an order that Ibn al-Athir relates from the [[person/muhammad-khwarazmshah|Khwarazm Shah]]. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334-335}

## The report
Ibn al-Athir relates that Ibn Kharmil seized the army of the Khwarazm Shah because of their ill treatment of the subjects, then apologised. The Khwarazm Shah wrote to him approving of his act, and confided to Izz al-Din Jaldak that he should contrive to seize him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334-335}

Jaldak marched with two thousand horsemen. Ibn Kharmil came out to him despite the warning of his vizier, and they dismounted to meet, and Jaldak's men mixed in with them and seized him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334-335}

The vizier shut the gate and went up to the walls, and called out the slogan of Ghiyath al-Din Mahmud al-Ghuri. He said he would not hand the city over to Jaldak or to Ibn Kharmil. Jaldak's men ordered Ibn Kharmil to surrender from the wall, and he did not, so they killed him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334-335}

## The water trick
Ibn Kharmil had fortified Herat with four walls and dug its moat. Ibn al-Athir relates that he said he feared the waters would be held back and then released all at once. The besieged heard his words and did it, and the moat filled and became a mire around the city. Ibn al-Athir describes it as among the finest stratagems. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334-335}

[[person/muhammad-khwarazmshah|The Khwarazm Shah]] took part in ordering the stratagem. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334}
