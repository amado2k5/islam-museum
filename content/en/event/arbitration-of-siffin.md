## Overview
After the lifting of the Qur'ans, the document of arbitration was written between Ali and Mu'awiya. It provided that Abd Allah ibn Qays and Amr ibn al-As should judge between the community, and that the judgement should be given by Ramadan. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.5 p.54}

## "No judgement but God's"
al-Tabari relates that a man from the side of the mosque said: "No judgement but God's." Then men followed, calling for arbitration. Ali replied that judgement belongs to God. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.5 p.73}

## What al-Mas'udi adds
The people of Syria chose Amr ibn al-As. al-Ash'ath and those with him insisted on Abu Musa, despite Ali's objection and his saying that Abu Musa was not trustworthy. The document stated that the two arbiters should revive what the Qur'an revives and cause to die what it causes to die, and should not follow their own desires. Its drafting took place a few days before the end of Safar 37 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.682-685}

al-Mas'udi mentions that the first to speak the words "No judgement but God's" was Urwa ibn Udhayna. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.682-685}

## The number of dead
The reports differ over the number killed. Yahya ibn Ma'in reported one hundred and ten thousand from both sides, over a hundred and ten days. al-Haytham ibn Adi, al-Sharqi ibn al-Qatami and Abu Mikhnaf reported seventy thousand, among them twenty-five men who had fought at Badr. al-Mas'udi preferred a higher figure. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.682-685}

The indexes point to the meeting of the two arbiters at Dumat al-Jandal. See [[event/arbitration-meeting-at-dumat-al-jandal|the meeting of the arbiters at Dumat al-Jandal]]. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.5 p.627}



## The exchange — what each side said
- **The people of Syria (choosing their arbiter)**: chose Amr ibn al-As to judge for them.
- **Al-Ash'ath and those with him (the insistence)**: insisted on Abu Musa al-Ash'ari despite Ali's objection and his words 'he is not trustworthy' — a split inside Ali's own camp before the split between the camps.
- **Urwa ibn Udhayna (the refusal)**: the first to utter 'judgement belongs to God alone' per al-Mas'udi — the cry that became the Muhakkima's banner, as men took it up in turn.
- **Ali (the reply)**: answered them that judgement belongs to God — accepting the wording and raising the dispute to its application.
- **The document (the agreed text)**: it was written that the two arbiters would give life to what the Qur'an gives life and death to what it kills, follow no desire, judge by God's Book and His Messenger's sunna, and that the community would back their ruling; meeting in Ramadan of year 37 at a midway place — Dumat al-Jandal or Adhruh.
- **What does not stand of the famous account**: the famous story of Amr tricking Abu Musa does not hold for al-Sallabi — its strongest route is al-Zuhri's mursal, who did not witness the event, its chain carries Ibn Abi Sabra accused of fabrication, al-Waqidi is abandoned, and Abu Mikhnaf is not probative; so the famous dialogue 'I deposed you, now depose me' is a report the debate's text cannot be built on.
- **The subject of the arbitration**: the dispute was not over the caliphate itself but over retribution on Uthman's killers — Ali's bay'a stood contracted, and Syria did not claim the caliphate for Mu'awiya in his lifetime. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.5 p.54-73} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.682-685} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p.110-147}
