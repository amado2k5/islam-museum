## Overview
A poem in the rajaz metre under the heading "and also of his" bidding the hearer tell Banu Ka'b, Qushayr and Banu Qurra that he is a man of high lineage tracing to the Prophet Ahmad, the Fatimid, Ahmadi youth and son of the rightly guided Commander of the Faithful, who defends his companions, and describing his sword, his horse, his army and his nearness to the fight. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.309}

## Detail
On p. 309, after the heading "and also of his", a rajaz poem opens with the command "tell Banu Ka'b all, make for them and say to them the words of a youth who is well guided", then singles out "Qushayr" and then "Banu Qurra" for the good word {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.309}. The message is that he is a man of lofty rank in a high and chiefly station when descent is traced to [[person/muhammad|the Prophet Ahmad]], and that they should make for the people who have not been made for; it includes a sword with both edges free, sharp and thundering {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.309}. He then says "I am the Fatimid, Ahmadi youth and son of the Commander of the Faithful, the rightly guided", that he defends his companions as lions defend their cubs from every aggressor, that he turns the rein of his short-haired horse to death, describes the dark horse running over the waste with clear foam about it, and says he charges in a numerous army {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.309}. The footnote on p. 309 says the original reads "to the Prophet" and the correction is from manuscript S, that "dhubab" of the sword is its edge, and that "al-fadfad" is the empty waste, or hard ground with gravel {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.309}. On p. 310 the rajaz goes on with [[event/al-hadi-names-his-hospitality-to-the-guest-and-the-neighbour-in-verse-292|the lines on hospitality and neighbours]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.310}. The book does not say who Banu Ka'b, Qushayr and Banu Qurra are here or where the message is to go, so the names stay as printed and the question is in the ledger. The poet's claim to descent from Fatima and Ahmad and to be the son of the rightly guided Commander of the Faithful is one the Zaydi book reports from him, and the museum does not discuss it. It follows [[event/al-hadi-asks-in-verse-what-draws-the-heart-to-honour-badr-and-repentance-292|the "have you a wish" verses]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
