## Overview
Al-Tabari set out on his great study journey through Syria and Palestine to Egypt, passing through Beirut and such centres of hadith as Hims, al-Ramla and Askalan; his entry into Egypt can be fixed with some certainty at 253/867 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 21}.

## Egyptian masters
In Egypt he met the leading muhaddith and authority on the qira'at Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, read Malik's fiqh with the Ibn Abd al-Hakam family, and took Shafi'i law from al-Rabi ibn Sulayman; Ibn Yunus al-Sadafi counted him in his Kitab al-Ghuraba among the scholars who came to Egypt for study {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 21} {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 326-327}.
