## Summary
Once the view settled on going out to meet the polytheists outside [[place/medina|Medina]], [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] led the people in Friday prayer and exhorted them to seriousness and exertion, promising them victory so long as they stood firm {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 252}. After the afternoon prayer, with the people of al-Awali present, the Prophet armed himself — putting on two coats of mail one over the other and girding his sword — then went out to his companions {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 252}.

## Details
The departure saw a moment of regret among some of the Companions who had pressed for going out; Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Usayd ibn Hudayr said to those who had urged the Prophet: "you pressed the Messenger of God to go out against his will", and they tried to retract their view. But the Prophet confirmed going ahead, saying: "it is not fitting for a prophet, once he has put on his war-gear — the coat of mail — to lay it down until God judges between him and his enemy" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 252}.

The Prophet divided the army of a thousand fighters into three battalions:
1. **The Muhajirun's battalion:** its banner given to Mus'ab ibn Umayr al-Abdari {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 252}.
2. **The Aws battalion:** its banner given to Usayd ibn Hudayr {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 252}.
3. **The Khazraj battalion:** its banner given to al-Hubab ibn al-Mundhir {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 252}.

The army had a hundred men in mail and fifty horsemen, and the Prophet appointed Ibn Umm Maktum to lead the prayer in Medina in the army's absence {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 252}. On reaching the station of [[place/al-shaykhan|al-Shaykhan]], the Prophet reviewed the army; he sent back those he saw too young to fight — among them Abdullah ibn Umar and Usama ibn Zayd — while he passed Rafi' ibn Khadij for his skill in archery, and Samura ibn Jundub after he proved his strength wrestling Rafi' {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 253}.
