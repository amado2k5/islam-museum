## Summary
[[person/khubayb-ibn-adi|Khubayb ibn Adi]], of the Ansar, was captured in the [[event/incident-of-al-raji|Raji incident]] and sold in Mecca; the Banu al-Harith ibn Amir bought him and kept him prisoner until the sacred months had passed, when they brought him out to kill him. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 192} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} He is famous for the two rakas he prayed before his death, so that it is said he was the first to set the practice. This record deals with what he said in his last hour as the books relate it, and is separate from the record of the wider incident.

## Capture and sale
The hadith as given in Fath al-Bari relates that Asim ibn Thabit was killed with six others, three came down under covenant and pledge, among them Khubayb and Ibn Dathina, and the men bound them and dragged a third who refused to go with them, then sold Khubayb and Ibn Dathina in Mecca after the battle of Badr. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 192} The Banu al-Harith ibn Amir ibn Nawfal bought Khubayb, since he had killed al-Harith at Badr. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 192} In that report the daughter of al-Harith says Khubayb borrowed a razor from her to shave, and a small son of hers wandered to him while she was not looking; she found the boy on his thigh and the razor in his hand and was terrified; he said: do you fear I will kill him? I would not do that. She said she had never seen a better prisoner, and that she had found him eating from a bunch of grapes while bound in iron when there was no fruit in Mecca; she used to say it was provision God gave Khubayb. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 192} Ibn Ishaq says the buyer was Hujayr ibn Abi Ihab al-Tamimi, for Uqba ibn al-Harith to kill in revenge for his father, and it is also said that several shared in the purchase. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 385}

## The two rakas
Ibn Hisham relates from Ibn Ishaq, from Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, that they took Khubayb out to al-Tanim to crucify him, and he said to them: if you think fit to leave me to pray two rakas, do so; they said: go ahead; he prayed two rakas, completing them and doing them well. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} Then he turned to them and said that but for their thinking he had lengthened it from distress at being killed he would have prayed more. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} Ibn Hisham, following Ibn Ishaq, says Khubayb was the first to set these two rakas before killing for the Muslims. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} In Fath al-Bari's version, when they brought him out of the sanctuary to kill him outside it, he said: let me pray two rakas, and he set the two rakas for every Muslim killed in bonds. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 192} Ibn Abd al-Barr relates from Hisham ibn Hassan that Ibn Sirin, asked about the two rakas at killing, would say: Khubayb and Hujr prayed them, and both were excellent men. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 174}

## His words and prayer
After they raised him on the wood and bound him he said: O God, we have conveyed the message of Your Messenger, so convey to him this morning what is done to us. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} Then he said: «O God, count them one by one, kill them scattered, and leave none of them.» {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} Ibn Hisham relates that Muawiya ibn Abi Sufyan said he was there that day with his father, and saw his father throw him to the ground for fear of Khubayb's prayer, for people used to say that a man who is prayed against and lies down on his side is spared. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} In Ibn Ishaq's account too, when Umar appointed Said ibn Amir ibn Hidhyam over part of Syria, he suffered fainting fits; Umar asked him and he said he had been present at Khubayb's killing and heard his prayer, and it never crossed his mind in any gathering but he fainted. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434}

## His verses
Ibn Ishaq relates verses Khubayb said when word reached him that the people had agreed to crucify him: that the confederates had gathered their tribes, sons and women around him, and that he complained of his exile and distress to God. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 113} Ibn Hisham says some scholars of poetry deny them to him. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 435} The best known line ascribed to him in al-Bukhari's report says that he does not mind, as he is killed a Muslim, on which side his death falls for God. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 192} {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 385}

## The killing
Ibn Hisham says Khubayb stayed in their hands until the sacred months had passed and then they killed him. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} In Uqba ibn al-Harith's account he did not kill Khubayb himself: Abu Maysara of Banu Abd al-Dar took the spear, put it in Uqba's hand, held his hand with the spear and thrust. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} Al-Isaba says al-Bukhari relates from Jabir that the killer was Abu Sarwaa, and that it is disputed whether he is Uqba ibn al-Harith or his brother. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 385}

## His body and grave
The books I read do not mention a grave. Al-Bidaya wal-Nihaya relays from Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri that after hiding two nights in a cave and going out to al-Tanim he was told of Khubayb, saw him crucified with guards around, carried him on his back about twenty cubits until the guards woke, then threw down the wood and heaped earth on him with his foot. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 490} Al-Isaba relays another report, that the Prophet sent al-Miqdad and al-Zubayr to take him down from his wood; they found about forty men drunk around it and took him down; al-Zubayr carried him on his horse, fresh and unchanged, and when the idolaters caught up he threw him down and the earth swallowed him, so he was called the one the earth swallowed. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 385} It adds from al-Qayrawani that they turned his face from the qibla and found him facing it, turned him several times and gave up. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 385}

## Where the reports differ
They differ on the year: Ibn Hisham places al-Raji in year 3, whereas this museum's record of the incident places it in Safar of year 4 from another source. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 434} They differ on who bought him, on who killed him, and on who took down his body. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 385} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 490}

## Sources and documentation
This record rests on Ibn Hisham's Sira, Fath al-Bari, al-Isaba, al-Bidaya wal-Nihaya, Hilyat al-Awliya and al-Isti'ab. The page numbers given are scan pages.
