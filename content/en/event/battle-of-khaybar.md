In Muharram of the seventh year of the Hijra the Prophet marched out with fourteen hundred fighters and two hundred horses — the people of the Pledge of al-Ridwan at al-Hudaybiyya and no others — heading for the fortified oasis of Khaybar, the chief stronghold of intrigue and warmongering against Medina. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Prophet took a road that cut Khaybar off from its Ghatafan allies, then surprised them at dawn; its mighty forts were besieged one by one: the fort of Na'im, then the fort of al-Sa'b ibn Mu'adh, then the castle of al-Zubayr, the fort of Abi and the fort of al-Nizar, then the forts of the second quarter such as al-Katiba, al-Watih and al-Salalim. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When some forts held out, the Prophet said: "tomorrow I will give the banner to a man who loves God and His Messenger and whom God and His Messenger love; God will grant victory at his hands." He called [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]], touched his eyes with his spittle and handed him the banner; Marhab came out to him declaiming verses and Ali struck him and split his crown — and the forts fell in succession. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

When the Jews were certain of ruin they asked for peace on the sparing of their blood and their evacuation, then agreed to remain on their land working it for half its produce; the spoils of Khaybar were divided among the people of al-Hudaybiyya; and the Prophet freed the lady [[person/safiyya-bint-huyayy|Safiyya bint Huyayy ibn Akhtab]] and married her after her conversion. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## For Sprouts
In the seventh year of the Hijra the Prophet set out with the people of the Pledge of Ridwan toward the fortified oasis of Khaybar north of Medina, and its forts were besieged one by one. Ali carried the flag and the forts opened in succession, and the Jews made peace on remaining in their land to work it for half the produce.

## For Explorers
In Muharram 7 AH the Prophet marched with fourteen hundred men — the people of the Ridwan pledge at Hudaybiyya — to the fortified oasis of Khaybar, the last stronghold of plotting against Medina. He took a road cutting it off from its Ghatafan allies and surprised them at dawn; the forts were besieged one by one: Na'im, al-Sa'b, al-Zubayr's castle, Abi, al-Nizar, then the second quarter. When some resisted he said 'tomorrow I will give the flag to a man who loves God and His messenger and whom God and His messenger love', and gave it to Ali; Marhab came out and Ali brought him down, and the forts opened in succession. The Jews settled on remaining in their land for half its produce.

## For Scholars
In Muharram 7 AH — al-Zuhri and Malik date it otherwise — the Prophet marched with fourteen hundred men and two hundred horses, the very people of the Ridwan pledge at Hudaybiyya, against the oasis of Khaybar, the Jews' last stronghold in Arabia and their base of plotting against Medina. He took a road cutting it off from Ghatafan and surprised them at dawn; the forts were besieged one by one: Na'im, al-Sa'b ibn Mu'adh, al-Zubayr's castle, Abi, al-Nizar, then al-Katiba, al-Watih and al-Sulalim in the second quarter. When some resisted he said 'tomorrow I will give the flag to a man who loves God and His messenger and whom God and His messenger love — God will open at his hands'; he called Ali, sore-eyed, and healed his eyes, telling him to go at his own pace and call them to Islam first; Marhab, Khaybar's horseman, duelled him and was brought down, and the opening was at Ali's hands. The Jews settled on sparing lives, then on remaining in their land for half the produce; the spoils were divided among the people of Hudaybiyya, and the Prophet freed Safiyya bint Huyayy and married her after her Islam. Al-Sallabi draws from it the Prophet's attesting of Ali's love and the blessing of his prayer, and answers — attributed to his sources — the Rafidi use of the hadith for the imamate.
