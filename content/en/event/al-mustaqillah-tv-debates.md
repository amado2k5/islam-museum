## Summary
The al-Mustaqillah channel debates in early-2000s London: al-Ar'ur and al-Khamis against al-Qazwini and al-Milani on satellite television. {cite:youtube-sunni-jewish-debate}

## Impact
The first mass-audience platform-moderated Sunni–Shia disputation; its clips remain the reference of the aired disagreement.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **The Sunni debaters (al-Ar'ur, al-Khamis and their colleagues)**: argued imamate is a branch of the faith, not a root; that the Ghadir texts bear merit, not designation; and defended the uprightness of the Companions as the foundational rule of transmission, putting the Sahih texts ahead of partisanship.
- **The Shia debaters (al-Qazwini, al-Milani and their colleagues)**: built on the designation of Ali and the Ahl al-Bayt, and on the recorded problems of the Saqifa and history, demanding an equivalent designation text for Abu Bakr.
- **The exchange (as broadcast)**: ran on three axes — designation, Companion uprightness and transmission, and the Sunni stance toward the Shia corpus — the host keeping the episodic debate format; the programme declared no verdict, leaving judgement to the viewers.
- **The value**: the episode records are the reference for the Arabic televised disputation — each side presented its school's method on air. {cite:al-islam-al-murajaat}
