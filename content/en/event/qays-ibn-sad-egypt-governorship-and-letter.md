## Summary
Ali appointed Qays ibn Sa'd over Egypt after Muhammad ibn Abi Hudhayfa was killed {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 330}. The book narrates that Qays entered Egypt and organized its affairs without forcing those who refrained from pledging allegiance {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 331}. Sources then report letters between him and Mu'awiya and accounts of his dismissal, which the book regards as abandoned, favoring the view that Ali wrote to summon him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 334}.

## The Appointment
Ali sent Qays ibn Sa'd to Egypt after the killing of Muhammad ibn Abi Hudhayfa, who had seized control of Egypt and was then captured and killed {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 330}. Ali told him: "Go to Egypt; I have appointed you over it," along with an instruction to treat the upright well and be firm with the suspect {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 330}.

## The People of Egypt
The people of Egypt split into those who entered into Ali's pledge of allegiance and those who held back and withdrew {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 331}. Qays did not force those who refrained to pledge allegiance; instead, he sent them their stipends where they had withdrawn and honored those of them who came to him, so matters calmed down {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 331}.

## The Letters and Dismissal
The book presents Abu Mikhnaf's reports about letters between Mu'awiya and Qays, describing them as abandoned and full of oddities, and also notes that the story of Qays informing the people of Egypt about Ali contains material that is not established {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 331}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 332}. The book presents another report that Ali's companions told Ali that Qays had changed, so he wrote to him: "I have need of your proximity, so appoint a deputy over your post and come," which the book treats as tantamount to dismissal {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 334}. The book favors this report, following Dr. Yahya al-Yahya {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 334}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book rejects reports attributing disparaging remarks about Uthman to Ali, and notes that Ali affirmed his innocence of Uthman's blood in well-established statements {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 332}.
- The book reports that after Qays, Ali appointed al-Ashtar, and then Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, as al-Ashtar died before entering Egypt {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 335}.
