## Overview
In al-Baladhuri's account in "Futuh al-Buldan", the Romans wrote to Constantine ibn Heraclius telling him of the small number of Muslims among them. He sent a man called [[person/manuil-al-rumi|Manuel]] with three hundred ships. He entered [[place/iskandariyya|Alexandria]] and killed the garrison of Muslims there. [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] marched against them and retook it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The events
Al-Baladhuri says that this was in the year 25 AH, and that Amr marched with fifteen thousand men. He found the Byzantine fighters raiding the villages of Egypt near by; the Muslims met them and fought until they fled to Alexandria and fortified themselves in it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Amr set up catapults and fought them until he entered by force. Manuel was killed, and Amr and the Muslims broke down the city wall, which he had vowed to do if he took it. He then imposed the land tax on its land and the poll tax on its people. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Differing accounts
Al-Baladhuri quotes some transmitters that the raid was in 23 AH, and others that they broke the truce in two years, and ends with "God knows best". On the matter of al-Muqawqis there are two accounts: the people of Alexandria withdrew from the peace when they broke it, or he had died before this raid. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

He quotes Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz that Alexandria, Kafr Tays and Sultays were among the villages conquered by peace. The poll tax of Alexandria was eighteen thousand dinars, and it reached thirty-six thousand in the governorship of Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

As for [[person/abdullah-ibn-hudhafa-al-sahmi|Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi]], the data describes him as the commander of the garrison that was slaughtered. The details of that need an additional source.
