## Summary
al-Baladhuri, in *Futuh al-Buldan*, reports that papyrus (qarattis) used to enter the lands of the Romans from Egypt, and that the Arabs used to receive dinars from the Romans. [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] was among the first to introduce writing on the tops of the papyrus sheets that mentioned God. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The letter of the king of the Romans
The king of the Romans wrote to him: "You have introduced into your papyrus a writing that we dislike. Either you leave it, or we will put on the dinars what you dislike." This weighed on Abd al-Malik, who disliked giving up a good custom that he himself had established. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The advice
He sent for [[person/khalid-ibn-yazid-ibn-muawiya|Khalid ibn Yazid ibn Muawiya]], who advised him to forbid the dinars of the Romans and to prohibit dealing in them, and to strike coins for the people. Abd al-Malik said that this had relieved him, and he struck the dinars. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Awana's account
[[person/awana-ibn-al-hakam|Awana ibn al-Hakam]] said that the Copts used to write the name of Christ at the tops of the papyrus and ascribed divinity to him, and placed the cross in place of the basmala. This is why the king of the Romans was so harsh about Abd al-Malik's change. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## al-Mada'ini's account
[[person/al-madaini|al-Mada'ini]] said, from Maslama ibn Muharib, that Khalid advised forbidding their dinars and dealing in them, and that no papyrus should enter the lands of the Romans. So for a while nothing was carried to them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The three accounts differ in their cause and detail, and the date is approximate within the caliphate of Abd al-Malik.
