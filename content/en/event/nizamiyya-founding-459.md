## Summary
In 459 AH the Nizamiyya madrasa in Baghdad was completed, and [[person/abu-ishaq-al-shirazi|Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi]] was appointed to teach in it. [[person/nizam-al-mulk|Nizam al-Mulk]] built it, and according to al-Dhahabi it was said to be the first madrasa built for jurists. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.645-646}

## Construction
Ibn al-Jawzi says that work began in Dhu al-Hijja 457 AH, and that the remaining riverside houses at Mashra'at al-Zawaya, al-Farda, Bab al-Sha'ir and Darb al-Za'farani were demolished to make way for it. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.140}

## The first lessons
When people gathered, al-Shirazi did not attend and went into hiding. Ibn al-Imad reports that a boy met him and asked: "How can you teach in a usurped place?" So Ibn al-Sabbagh, author of *al-Shamil*, taught instead. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.315}

The people then persuaded the sheikh until he agreed. Ibn al-Jawzi reports that he began teaching there on the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja 459 AH, twenty days after Ibn al-Sabbagh's lesson. Whenever the time for prayer came, he would leave and pray in one of the mosques. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.246-247}

## For Sprouts
A great academic college founded in Baghdad to teach students, providing them with books and classes freely in an environment of care.

## For Explorers
The Nizamiyya of Baghdad was one of the most celebrated collegiate academies in Islamic history, founded by the Seljuk vizier Nizam al-Mulk in 459 AH. The college provided students with lodging, food, books, and expert instruction free of charge, with illustrious scholars like Abu Hamid al-Ghazali and Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi teaching there.

## For Scholars
The inauguration of the renowned al-Madrasa al-Nizamiyya in Baghdad in Dhu al-Qada 459 AH (1067 CE), established by the Seljuk grand vizier Nizam al-Mulk al-Tusi as a pioneering endowed collegiate institution. The Nizamiyya revolutionized institutional higher education in Islam by establishing dedicated residential colleges with salaried professorships, student stipends, endowed libraries, and structured curricula. Instructed by luminary scholars including Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi and Abu Hamid al-Ghazali, it played an influential role in academic scholarship, legal training, and state administration.
