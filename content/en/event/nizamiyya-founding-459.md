## Summary
In 459 AH the Nizamiyya madrasa in Baghdad was completed, and [[person/abu-ishaq-al-shirazi|Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi]] was appointed to teach in it. [[person/nizam-al-mulk|Nizam al-Mulk]] built it, and according to al-Dhahabi it was said to be the first madrasa built for jurists. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.645-646}

## Construction
Ibn al-Jawzi says that work began in Dhu al-Hijja 457 AH, and that the remaining riverside houses at Mashra'at al-Zawaya, al-Farda, Bab al-Sha'ir and Darb al-Za'farani were demolished to make way for it. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.140}

## The first lessons
When people gathered, al-Shirazi did not attend and went into hiding. Ibn al-Imad reports that a boy met him and asked: "How can you teach in a usurped place?" So Ibn al-Sabbagh, author of *al-Shamil*, taught instead. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.315}

The people then persuaded the sheikh until he agreed. Ibn al-Jawzi reports that he began teaching there on the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja 459 AH, twenty days after Ibn al-Sabbagh's lesson. Whenever the time for prayer came, he would leave and pray in one of the mosques. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.8 p.246-247}
