## Overview
Ahmad ibn Buya entered Baghdad, and the caliph [[person/al-mustakfi-billah|al-Mustakfi]] robed him and gave him the title Mu'izz al-Dawla. Then the influence of [[person/muizz-al-dawla|Mu'izz al-Dawla]] grew and he restricted the caliph, and fixed for him five thousand dirhams a day for expenses. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.613-614}

## The deposition
In Jumada al-Akhira 334 AH Mu'izz al-Dawla went in to the caliph. Two Daylamis came forward, and he stretched out his hand to them thinking that they wanted to kiss it; they pulled him off the couch and dragged him by his turban. The Daylam attacked the caliph's residence and plundered it. Al-Mustakfi was deposed and his eyes were blinded, after a reign of a year and four months. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.613-614}

## Ibn al-Athir's account
Ibn al-Athir gives a reason for the deposition: the caliph's mother held a banquet attended by Daylami and Turkish commanders, and they accused her to Mu'izz al-Dawla of taking allegiance for al-Mustakfi so that they would depose him. In his account al-Mustakfi was handed over to [[person/al-muti-billah|al-Muti']] after his allegiance and he was blinded, and he remained imprisoned until he died in Rabi al-Awwal 338 AH. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.337}

## Miskawayh's account
In [[work/tajarib-al-umam|Tajarib al-umam]], [[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] gives as "the apparent cause" that Alam, al-Mustakfi's stewardess (qahramana), gave a great banquet attended by a number of Daylamite commanders; Mu'izz al-Dawla accused her of wanting to take their allegiance for al-Mustakfi so that they would overturn his leadership, and he grew suspicious "because of the boldness he saw in her and her readiness to overturn states". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.116} He adds a second cause: al-Mustakfi had arrested al-Shafi'i, head of the Shi'a of Bab al-Taq; the commander Isfahdost interceded for him and was refused, so he went to Mu'izz al-Dawla and said the caliph had sent word asking to meet him in disguise, "in slippers and a wrap". "From that, and from other things that did not come out, came his deposition from the caliphate." {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.116}

## The audience
On Thursday 22 Jumada II (eight nights before the month's end), Mu'izz al-Dawla went down to the palace and the people went down as usual. When al-Mustakfi had sat on his throne and the people stood in their ranks, [[person/abu-jafar-al-saymari|Abu Ja'far al-Saymari]] and [[person/abu-jaafar-ibn-shirzad|Abu Ja'far ibn Shirzad]] came in and took their places. Mu'izz al-Dawla came in, kissed the ground as was the custom, then kissed the caliph's hand, stood before him talking, then sat on a chair and gave audience to an envoy from Khurasan and an envoy from Abu al-Qasim al-Baridi. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.116}

Then two Daylamites stepped forward, stretched their hands towards al-Mustakfi and raised their voices in Persian. He thought they wanted to kiss his hand and held it out; they pulled him by it, threw him to the ground, put his turban round his neck and dragged him. Mu'izz al-Dawla rose and the crowd was thrown into confusion; the Daylamites seized his secretary Abu Ahmad al-Shirazi and Ibn Abi Musa al-Hashimi, entered the women's quarters and seized Alam the stewardess and her daughter. People rushed for the door and there was "a great crush and looting". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.116} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.117}

The two Daylamites drove al-Mustakfi on foot to Mu'izz al-Dawla's house, where he was held, and the palace was plundered "until nothing was left in it". The same day Mu'izz al-Dawla brought Abu al-Qasim al-Fadl ibn al-Muqtadir to the caliphal palace; he was addressed as caliph, received the oath of allegiance and took the title [[person/al-muti-billah|al-Muti' li-llah]]. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.117}

## After the deposition
Ibn Shirzad served al-Muti' in the place of a vizier "without the title of vizier", al-Muti' took Abu al-Abbas ibn Khaqan as chamberlain, and Mu'izz al-Dawla assigned him two thousand dirhams a day for his expenses. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.119} In the same year Alam the stewardess was blinded, and later her tongue was cut out. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.133}

## Between the accounts
At this point Miskawayh does not mention the blinding of al-Mustakfi; he records only his deposition and his detention in Mu'izz al-Dawla's house. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.117} Al-Suyuti places the blinding on the day of the deposition, Ibn al-Athir after the oath to al-Muti'. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.613-614} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.337} The caliph's daily allowance is two thousand dirhams for al-Muti' in Miskawayh, and five thousand dirhams set by Mu'izz al-Dawla for al-Mustakfi before the deposition in al-Suyuti: two reports about two different caliphs. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.119} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.613} In Miskawayh the woman who gave the banquet is Alam, al-Mustakfi's stewardess. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.116}
