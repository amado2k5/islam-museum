## Overview
al-Baladhuri reports that al-Mutawakkil appointed [[person/muhammad-ibn-abdallah-al-qummi|Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Qummi]] over the mines in Egypt, and he led a campaign to [[place/bilad-al-buja|the land of the Buja]] that ended in a truce in 241 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 333-335}

## The campaign
[[person/al-mutawakkil|al-Mutawakkil]] appointed him over the mines, al-Qulzum, and the road to the Hijaz. He carried supplies by ship to the land of the Buja and reached a coast known as [[place/aydhab|Aydhab]], and drew provisions from it. Then he reached the fortress of the king of the Buja. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 333-335}

The king of the Buja came out to him on camels. al-Qummi hung bells on the horses, and when the camels heard their sound they bolted. The Buja scattered into the valleys and mountains, and their king was killed. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 333-335}

## The truce
After him his sister's son rose; the source's original reading says his brother. He asked for a truce. al-Mutawakkil refused unless he came to him, so he came to Samarra, and a truce was agreed in 241 AH on payment of tribute and a buqt tax. He returned with al-Qummi, and the Buja entered the truce and did not prevent Muslims from working the gold mine. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 333-335}

## The account of al-Maqrizi
The two editors of the book quote al-Maqrizi: al-Mutawakkil put him in charge of the war against the Buja, and wrote to Anbasa ibn Ishaq, governor of Egypt, to help him. Their king was [[person/ali-baba-king-of-buja|Ali Baba]]. When the ships arrived, al-Qummi fought them, and their camels bolted at the sound of the bells. Ali Baba asked for safe-conduct and was granted it on condition that he pay what he owed, and the tribute of four years was delivered. al-Qummi returned to Baghdad with Ali Baba, and al-Mutawakkil gave him a robe of honour. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 333-335}

al-Maqrizi mentions that Ali Baba went back still following his own religion, with a stone idol to which he prostrated. al-Qummi stopped at Aswan, stayed there for a time, and died. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 333-335}

## Notes
The second account, in the editors' phrasing, gives the year as 141, which differs from the year 241 in the first text; it needs an additional source. The data names [[group/buja|the Buja]] as a party to the truce.
