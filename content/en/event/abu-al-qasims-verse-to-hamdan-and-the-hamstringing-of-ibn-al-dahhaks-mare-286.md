## Overview
With his letter to Banu Ma'mar [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] sent verse whose opening calls on the people of [[group/banu-hamdan|Hamdan]] that God preferred them by their help for the family of God's Messenger in the books, until their pride rose in every land where the sun rises and they were put ahead of the Arabs, that they were with Ali at Siffin, and that a band of them hung back when called to their own right. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.184} He asks them to leave error and turn to the truth and not rest in play and idleness, and to drive the slaves out of their home since their shame spreads to the good like mange; he says they do not accept wrong or tire of the fire of war, that if they get what they hope for it is theirs and if death stands in the way they are on the path of their fathers. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.184}

## The hamstringing
Ali ibn Muhammad said: when Abu al-Qasim's letter reached Banu Ma'mar they met and took counsel, knew they had no strength against [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] whose affair had risen and grown strong, and told one of their foolish men to hamstring [[person/ibn-al-dahhak-rebel-at-khiwan-286|Ibn al-Dahhak]]'s mare, and he hamstrung it by night. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.184} The sentence breaks off at the start of the next page with the words "and when it became clear", so the museum stops here and resumes from the next page.
