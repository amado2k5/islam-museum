## Summary
When the caliph [[person/al-mansur|Abu Ja'far al-Mansur]] resolved to make the pilgrimage in the year 158, he called his son and heir [[person/al-mahdi|al-Mahdi]] and charged him concerning his own affairs, his family and all the Muslims, and instructed him in running the realm and guarding the frontiers. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 511} Mortal illness seized him on the road; he entered [[place/mecca|Mecca]] gravely ill and died there in Dhu al-Hijja, {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 506} and was buried at the pass of Bab al-Mu'alla with his grave concealed so that it would not be known. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 513} This record completes the [[event/death-of-al-mansur|record of al-Mansur's death]].

## The testament before departure
Ibn Kathir relates that when al-Mansur resolved on pilgrimage he called al-Mahdi and charged him with good concerning his own person, his family and the Muslims, and taught him how affairs are managed and the frontiers guarded, with counsels too long to set out. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 511} He forbade him to open any of the Muslims' treasuries until he was certain of his death, since they held enough to sustain the Muslims for ten years even if not a dirham of land-tax were collected. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 511} He charged him to pay his debt, three hundred thousand dinars, because he did not think it right that it be paid from the treasury, and al-Mahdi did so. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 511} As he took leave he told him he had been born in Dhu al-Hijja and it had come to him that he would die in Dhu al-Hijja, and that this was what emboldened him to go on pilgrimage that year. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512}

## The illness on the road
He set out on pilgrimage driving the sacrificial animals with him; a few stages past Kufa the illness that killed him took hold, he had poor health and the heat of midday travel bore hard on him, and an excessive flux seized him so that his illness grew strong and he entered Mecca very ill. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 506} At the last halt before Mecca he found writing on the front wall of the lodging, summoned the keepers of the Ka'ba and had them read it, and they saw nothing, so he knew his end had been announced to him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512} Ibn Kathir also relates that in his palace al-Khuld he had seen a frightening dream in which someone at the palace door announced his end in verse, and some say a voice called out to him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512}

## His last words
The last thing he said was: O God, bless me in meeting You. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512} It is also said that he said: Lord, if I have disobeyed You in many things, I have obeyed You in what You love most, the sincere testimony that there is no god but God. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512} Al-Dhahabi quotes from al-Mada'ini that he said: O God, I committed grave wrongs out of boldness toward You, and I have obeyed You in what You love most, the testimony that there is no god but God, as a favour from You and not a favour to You; then he died. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2513} The inscription on his seal was: God is the trust of Abd Allah and in Him he believes. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512}

## Concealing the death and the oath
Ibn Kathir relates that he died on the night of Saturday, six or seven days into Dhu al-Hijja, and that al-Rabi' the chamberlain kept his death secret until he had taken the oath for al-Mahdi from the commanders and the heads of Banu Hashim, and only then was he buried. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 506} Ibrahim ibn Yahya ibn Muhammad ibn Ali prayed over him; he was the one who led the pilgrimage that year. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 506} Ibn Kathir reports that al-Tabari relates that a day before his death al-Mansur roused himself, leaned on support and called the commanders and renewed the oath to his son al-Mahdi. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 513} Al-Rabi' sent the oath with the courier to al-Mahdi in Baghdad, and the courier reached him on the Tuesday in mid-Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 513}

## What he left
Al-Dhahabi relates from al-Rabi' the chamberlain that he and al-Mahdi went round the storehouses after al-Mansur's death and saw in one room four hundred jars with sealed mouths holding salted livers kept for a siege. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2513} Ibn Kathir notes that he left a debt of three hundred thousand dinars that he charged al-Mahdi to settle. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 511} Ibn Kathir names among his sons Muhammad al-Mahdi, Ja'far the elder who died in his lifetime, Isa, Ya'qub, Sulayman, Ja'far the younger, Salih, al-Qasim, and a daughter, al-Aliya. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 513}

## The hidden grave
Al-Mansur was buried at Kada, at the pass of Bab al-Mu'alla in the upper part of Mecca. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 506} Ibn Kathir says his grave is not known because it was hidden, and that al-Rabi' the chamberlain dug a hundred graves and buried him in a different one so that it would not be known. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 513} According to al-Suli, as al-Dhahabi quotes him, he was buried between al-Hajun and Bir Maymun in Dhu al-Hijja of the year 158. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 2513}

## Where the reports differ
His age at death is given as sixty-three in the commoner report according to Ibn Kathir, others say sixty-four, sixty-five or sixty-eight; al-Dhahabi says he lived sixty-four years. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 506} The day of death is given as the sixth or the seventh of Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512} His last words are reported in two forms, one short, a prayer for blessing in the meeting, and one longer, a confession of wrongdoing with hope in the sincere testimony. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 512}

## Sources and documentation
The record rests on Ibn Kathir and al-Dhahabi. The volumes of al-Tabari for the year 158 were not among the pages read, so what is attributed to al-Tabari here comes through Ibn Kathir.
