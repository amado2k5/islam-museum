## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] reports that he heard [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Yahya ibn al-Husayn]] exhort people with admonitions he did not memorise in full. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.123} The account is Zaydi, in the imam's followers' voice; the margin heading in the manuscript is "his concern for how he dresses" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124}; and the sayings given to the imam are carried as their narrator tells them.

## Sincerity of intention
Among his admonitions he used to say: be wary of God in secret and in public, deal with God, and when you do anything make it purely for God; if you mend a weapon let your intention be that it is for God, if you feed an animal put the intention first that it is for God, and if one of you walks anywhere put the intention first for God, for in all you do you are acting for the good of Islam. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.123} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124}

## Holding oneself to account
He went on: attend to training your selves; were I to exhort you three years and then leave you for an hour, you would forget what I said unless you take yourselves to account in your solitude, so strive against yourselves in solitude and give up anger until you humble your selves before God. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124} He said he told them this because he had become entrusted with this community and thought on how it might be put right. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124}

## Riding out in a shirt and wrapper
He said: by God, I rode out to visit a sick man in a shirt and a wrapper, and when I came out of the house I asked God's forgiveness for riding in a shirt and wrapper, for I thought that if one of those overbearing men, or of the people who have no sense or discernment, saw me so he would look on me as small and of no account, and that is a corruption in Islam; so I asked forgiveness, because awe is the good of Islam. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124} He cited the verse on making ready strength and horses against an enemy, [[passage/quran-8-60|al-Anfal 60]], and told them to strike fear into God's enemies as they were able and to do it purely for God. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124} The citation is the imam's as the narrator reports; the verse itself is not typed here.

## Two remarks at the end of the account
The narrator saw him one night sitting at the pre-dawn writing a letter; he turned and said: "Abu Jafar, do you think the oppressors are thinking at this hour of the good of the people?" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124} Yahya also said to him: "Abu Jafar, not everyone knows the right of what we are in, and it comes over me when I recall the hardship we are in; the hardship falls on us at the striking of the sword, in making people equal and in justice, and in many places; I ask God for deliverance." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.124}

## Notes
"Abu Jafar" here is Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah, as in earlier places in the book; the word rendered "hardship" is read from its context. The account that follows, on the people of al-A'sum, opens on p. 125 after Ali ibn Muhammad notes that he asked his father about all the reports of Yahya set down above, and asked others whose names he gave. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.125}
