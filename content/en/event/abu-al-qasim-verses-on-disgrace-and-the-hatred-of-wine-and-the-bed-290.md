## Overview
On p. 269 Abu al-Qasim says that his purpose is ruin, death and wretchedness for the one who destroyed the religion and transgressed, that his horse is saddled and accustomed to enter battle at the beat of the drum, that he is a youth of Hashim in its thicket who kills whoever turned away and rebelled, that they left the head of their foe split, that their swords left captive and slain behind, and that they are lions of war who never tighten their grip on a wrongful chief without his being bound {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.269}. He then says that he dislikes wine and the soft bed, asks for modesty and holds to it, that he is the middle of Hashim and has gained loyalty and eminence, that he is quick to answer when the caller to battle cries out, that he wears a smooth supple coat of mail with a fast mount under him, and that in his hand are a Rudaynian lance and a blade that destroys a man {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.269}.

## Detail
The second piece ends on p. 270 with his saying that he is fierce when he loves the encounter, that Hashim knows him as a brother of war, that he plunges into deaths and blood, circles his equal on the day of battle and strikes when affairs twist, that he guards what is to be guarded by his victory, that his noble qualities, which the text calls "mahdiyya", refuse him content with a course of disgrace or humbleness, and that his forefathers are the best of men so that he took eminence and generosity {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.270}. These two pieces are the last of the verse the book ascribes to Abu al-Qasim in his imprisonment; after them Ali ibn Muhammad's narration from Muhammad ibn Sa'id begins with "know that I saw tonight in a dream", and the batch moves to [[event/abu-al-qasim-tells-his-dream-of-the-sons-of-yafur-and-the-rising-of-the-prisons-291|his dream and what followed]]. The note explains "madhiyya" as a smooth supple coat of mail and refers to the book al-Talkhis. The book ascribes the verse to Abu al-Qasim and the museum has not checked the attribution; the boasting and threats are the poet's, with no verdict in the museum's voice. The museum does not quote the lines nor the words of abuse. They follow [[event/abu-al-qasim-calls-the-community-to-guidance-and-the-quran-as-judge-in-verse-290|his call to the community to return to guidance]].
