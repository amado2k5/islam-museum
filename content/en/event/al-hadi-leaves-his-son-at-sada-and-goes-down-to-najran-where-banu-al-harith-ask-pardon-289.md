## Overview
When [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] reached [[place/saada|Sa'da]] in the last days of Jumada II he left his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] ("God make him mighty", in the narrator's words) as governor of Sa'da and went down to [[place/najran|Najran]], where [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]], his son and a party of Hamdan met him near al-Hisn, and he spent that night there {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.242}.

## Detail
When [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] learned of his coming to the land they were afraid for themselves, sent a party of their men to him and asked him to pardon them and accept what they brought; he agreed to what they asked and gave up to them what they had taken of the levy on condition that they pay the people their dues; they all came to him, he pardoned them and gave them safety, and went on with his armies until he entered the village of [[place/al-hajar-najran|al-Hajar]] and stayed there some days {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.242}. The narrator says their hearts were at ease at what they saw of him because there was no treachery or breaking of safety with him, that they remained grateful for his pardon, and that they promised God not to hasten to disobey him nor to befriend his enemy {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.242}; this is the narrator's description of the imam's followers, not a judgement of the museum, and the following pages show that it did not last. Abu al-Qasim here is al-Hadi's son, probably al-Murtada, not certain. See [[event/al-hadi-leaves-sanaa-for-sada-with-no-funds-and-al-daam-declines-to-help-289]].
