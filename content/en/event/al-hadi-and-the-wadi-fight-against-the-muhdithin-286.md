## Overview
After the blank folio {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.143} the book says [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] went to the houses of the "troublemakers", meaning those who had made disorder in the earlier reports, intending to pull them down; when he reached them words passed among the people and all those who had sworn to him and raised war broke their pledge, and as evening had come he went back to camp. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.143} The book names neither the people nor a date, so the event is dated to the year only.

## The rough wadi
Next morning he drew up his army with a right and left wing and went himself in the centre; the people went into a rough wadi where cavalry could not work and attacked when he drew near because they knew it. The horsemen with him fled, and everyone with him but three of his slaves, and he stood with them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.143} Then he charged alone, those in front of him fled and returned to their place. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.143}

## Fighting on foot
When he saw the horse was of no use there he dismounted, took sword and shield and stood, and the enemy came on hoping for him now that he was on foot; he fought them on foot hard until he pushed them back, and they feared his place {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.143} and despaired of him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144} His left wing, seeing his fighting, took heart, charged, drove the people off and pursued them into their fortress, and his whole army came up. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144}

## The narrator's reading
The narrator says that but for al-Hadi's stand two men would not have come together, that it was a steadying from the Merciful and one of the imam's signs by which he stood out from people. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144} This is the Zaydi narrative's own claim, attributed to its narrator; the museum gives no verdict.
