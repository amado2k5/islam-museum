## Summary
Ali ibn Abi Talib monitored his governors in their provinces through multiple means: inspectors who questioned people about the officials, confidential reports reaching him from his inspectors, a door open to every complaint, and written and oral counsel {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 360}.

## Inspectors and Reports
The book states that Ali wrote to one of his companions, named in the book as Ka'b ibn Malik, ordering him to appoint a deputy over his post and go out with a group of his companions to the land of the Sawad district to ask about Ali's officials and examine their conduct {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}. The book states that Ali received confidential reports from his inspectors over the provinces, and that these monitors could be among the governor's own staff or unknown individuals, and could reside in a province or travel between provinces {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}. The book does not identify on that page which Ka'b ibn Malik is meant, so this name cannot be taken as proof that he was the well-known companion of the expedition without verification.

## Complaints and Discipline
Ali threw open the door to complaints, and whenever a complaint about one of his officials reached him, he would say, "O God, I did not command them to wrong Your creation or neglect Your right" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}. He once imprisoned a governor, disciplined him, and struck him with the dirra when a complaint about him reached him and the charge was proven before him; the book does not mention the governor's name on that page {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 359}.

## Written Counsel
Ali used to write to his governors orders in the form of counsel explaining how to conduct their work, including his words, "For you are the treasurers of the subjects, the agents of the community, and the envoys of the leaders," ordering them not to bar people from their needs and not to strike anyone with a whip for the sake of a dirham {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 360}. When he appointed Qays ibn Sa'd over Egypt, he advised him to enter it with an army, because that would strike greater fear into his enemy {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 360}. The book notes that it is not transmitted from Ali that he performed the pilgrimage and met his governors during the pilgrimage after his pledge of allegiance as the caliphs before him had done; rather, he deputized those he trusted, such as the sons of al-Abbas {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 360}.

## Notes
- The governors of the East had more frequent contact with Ali because of their proximity to Kufa and their repeated delegations to it, according to the book {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 360}.
- The book does not specify the date of the disciplinary punishment with the dirra; the timeframe of Ali's caliphate is derived, with low confidence.
- The book mentions a directive from Ali to Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, but the text in the scan is garbled and was not relied upon here {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 360}.
