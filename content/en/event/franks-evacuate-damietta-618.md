## Summary
In 618 AH al-Malik al-Ashraf marched to help his brother al-Kamil, and the army of Syria marched with him. The Franks came out of Damietta with horsemen and foot soldiers during the season of the Nile's rise. They camped by a canal, and the Muslims breached the Nile over it, so it did not stop them from reaching Damietta. The fleet came and seized the Frankish boats, and about eight hundred horsemen were captured, among them the lord of Acre and many foot soldiers {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 79}.

When they saw their defeat, they asked for a truce on condition that they hand Damietta over to al-Kamil, and he accepted. Then his brothers al-Muʿazzam and al-Ashraf came to him with their armies in Rajab. He held a great banquet, brought in the kings of the Franks and bestowed gifts on them, and his brothers stood in his service. It was a day remembered {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 79}.

Raja al-Halli then stood up and recited a poem in which he pointed to the three brothers {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 79}.

This ended the Frankish campaign against Damietta. The Franks had taken it in 616 AH after besieging its people and after plague struck them, and they returned it two years later, having lost through the weakness they had thought was a prize. Its lesson stayed in people's minds for a long time, since they saw that the lands of Islam are not taken except through the failings of their people, not through the strength of their enemies {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 66, 79}.
