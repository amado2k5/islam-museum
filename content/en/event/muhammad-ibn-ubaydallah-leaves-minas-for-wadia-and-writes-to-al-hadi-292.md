## Overview
At daybreak Wadi'a gathered and went a short way and met Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah, who had left Minas, and his two sons asked him for the news {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}.

## Detail
He told them that Ibn Bustam had sent word that he feared for him and for himself with him, calling himself his friend and servant who would not slacken in what he owed them; if he thought fit to go among Hamdan he would be eyes and ears upon Banu al-Harith and write him their news, and if he saw otherwise he should do so {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah says: I knew the man did not want me to stay with him and I left; I thought of staying at al-Hisn but disliked it, for I feared that Banu al-Harith, Yam and al-Ahlaf would gather against them if I were there, so I judged going to Wadi'a the fitting course for us; Wadi'a were glad and turned back with him, lodged him in their homes and honoured him in the utmost degree {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.284}. He wrote to al-Hadi ila al-Haqq telling him what had come from the people, and al-Hadi wrote back letting him know that he "comes to him", the book not making the subject of the verb explicit, so that he should not grieve over what Banu al-Harith had done, and sent him some lines of verse, bidding him send them on to Banu al-Harith {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.284}. What Ibn Bustam said is Muhammad's report, and the museum gives no judgement on his intent. The speaker in the first person is Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah himself; manuscript S's note reads "from their night" in an earlier clause. The book does not locate al-Hisn or Wadi'a.
