## Summary
Surat al-Baqara reports the charge Ibrahim gave his sons and the one Ya'qub gave at the approach of death, within the passage that answers those who attribute to the prophets something other than what they held: see [[passage/quran-2-132|Qur'an 2:132]] and [[passage/quran-2-133|Qur'an 2:133]]. Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi explain what the two charges meant, who was included and when Ya'qub was born. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 621} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 616}

## The charge in the two verses
Ibn Kathir says that 'he charged them with it' means he charged them with this creed, which is submission to God, or else the pronoun refers back to the word of submission with which Ibrahim answered his Lord; they held to it until death and charged their sons after them. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 621} He explains that the charge to the sons is to do well in life and keep to this religion so that God grants death upon it, since a person usually dies on what he lived and is raised on what he died on. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 621} Al-Qurtubi says of the same wording that it is a concise expression which holds the intended meaning together with an admonition and a reminder of death, because a person knows he will die but not when; if he is told to hold to something so that death finds him on it, the command applies to him at every moment. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 617}

## Did Ya'qub witness his grandfather's charge?
Some early scholars read 'Ya'qub' in the accusative, linked to 'his sons', so that Ibrahim charged his sons and his grandson Ya'qub; Ibn Kathir thinks it apparent that Ya'qub was born to Ishaq in the lifetime of Ibrahim and Sarah, since the glad tidings came to them together. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 621} Ibn Kathir relays from al-Qushayri, as al-Qurtubi quotes him, that Ya'qub was born only after Ibrahim's death, and comments that such a claim needs sound evidence. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 621} Al-Qurtubi, giving al-Qushayri's objection to the accusative reading, says Ya'qub was not among Ibrahim's sons when the charge was given and it is not reported that he reached his grandfather; he then states that Ya'qub charged his sons too, as Ibrahim had done. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 616}

## Ya'qub's charge to his sons
Ibn Kathir says God here argues against the Arab polytheists among the sons of Ismail and the unbelievers among the Children of Israel, by noting that when death came to Ya'qub he charged his sons to worship God alone and asked what they would worship after him; they answered that they would worship the God of his fathers Ibrahim, Ismail and Ishaq, one God, and that they submitted to Him. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 622} Ismail is named among Ya'qub's fathers by way of the predominant usage, because he was his uncle, and Ibn Kathir quotes al-Nahhas that the Arabs call an uncle 'father'. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 622} Al-Qurtubi adds that God called both uncle and grandfather 'father' and put the grandfather first and then Ismail, who was older than Ishaq. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 618} On the phrase 'death was present', al-Qurtubi says it means its approach and its causes, since if death itself were present he could not speak. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 617}

Al-Qurtubi relates, in the form 'it is reported', that when Ya'qub was given the choice, as prophets are, he chose death and said: give me time to charge my sons and family; he gathered them and said it, and they replied as they did. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 617} He quotes al-Kalbi that when Ya'qub entered Egypt and saw them worshipping idols, fires and cattle he gathered his sons, feared for them and asked what they would worship after him. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 616}

## A charge to each son separately (from the People of the Book)
Ibn Kathir says the People of the Book reported that Ya'qub charged his sons one by one, told them what would come of each, and gave Judah the good news of a great prophet to come from his line whom the peoples would obey; Ibn Kathir says this is Isa ibn Maryam and adds 'and God knows best'. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 224} This is an Israelite report: Ibn Kathir takes it from the books of the People of the Book, sets it beside a criticism of their figures for Ya'qub's age, and does not treat it as part of the Qur'anic charge. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 224}

## Islam as the religion of all the prophets
Ibn Kathir links the two charges to his statement that Islam is the religion of all the prophets even where their laws differ, and cites the hadith 'we prophets are brothers of different mothers, our religion is one'; the edition's note refers to Bukhari 3442 and Muslim 2365. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 622} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 623} On the verse that follows he notes that the earlier prophets and righteous among one's forebears do not benefit those who claim descent from them without doing good. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 623}

## Sources and documentation
This account rests on the commentaries of Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi and on al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya, and what is attributed to the People of the Book is labelled as such, as Ibn Kathir labels it. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 621} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 616} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 224} For Ya'qub's death and burial see [[event/burial-of-yaqub-at-hebron|Death and burial of Ya'qub at Hebron]]; for Ibrahim's, [[event/burial-of-ibrahim-at-hebron|Death and burial of Ibrahim at Hebron]].
