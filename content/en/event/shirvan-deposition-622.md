## Overview
In 622 AH the son of the Shirvanshah rose against his father, took the kingdom from him and drove him out of the country. Ibn al-Athir describes the father as ill-conducted, much given to corruption and injustice, and preying on the property of his subjects. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 446-447}

## Refuge with the Georgians
The deposed father went to the Georgians and agreed with them that they should send an army with him to restore him to his kingdom in exchange for half the land. The son told his army that if his father prevailed he would spare none of them. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 446-447}

## The encounter
The son went out with about a thousand horsemen and met the Georgians with three thousand fighters. The men of Shirvan held firm, the Georgians were routed, and many were killed and many captured. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 446-447}

## The outcome
The Georgian commanders told the deposed king that they had gained nothing by him, and asked him not to stay in their land. The son settled in the kingdom and returned to the people their properties and what had been confiscated. Ibn al-Athir says they rejoiced at his rule. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 446-447}
