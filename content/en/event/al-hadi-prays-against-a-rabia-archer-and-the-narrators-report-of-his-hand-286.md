## Overview
The narrator calls what happened on the day [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] fell "a great sign": a man of Banu Rabi'a shot much at his companions and al-Hadi prayed that God cut off his hand. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.178} He then says some he trusts and some of [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]], after the narrator's party reached the land, told him that the man al-Hadi prayed against had his fingers affected as far as the wrists and died of what befell him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.178} The marginal gloss on the report is the single word "karama" (a marvel granted to a holy man). {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.178}

## Reading the text
The verb printed before "his fingers to the wrists" is not clear in the machine-read text, so the museum gives the general sense and does not say how they were affected. It is a report passed on by the narrator from unnamed people and from men of Banu al-Harith; the museum neither affirms nor denies it and does not repeat the narrator's phrase against the man after his death. The book does not name the man or say which Rabi'a he belonged to.
