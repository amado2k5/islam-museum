## Overview
The affair of al-Qadisiyya began in Muharram 14 AH, when Umar ibn al-Khattab consulted his Companions about marching out himself. Then the great battle took place, in which there were the day of Armath, the day of Aghwath, the day of 'Amas, and the night of al-Qadisiyya. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.3 p.480}

## The date
al-Tabari reports that al-Waqidi fixed the year as 14 AH, Ibn Ishaq said 15, and some people of Kufa said 16. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.3 p.590} The year is therefore disputed.

## Before the battle
al-Baladhuri relates that the Muslims wrote to Umar about the large number of the Persians and asked him for reinforcements. He wanted to march out himself, but al-Abbas and a group of the elder Companions advised him to stay and to dispatch the armies, so he sent Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas. The account says that the Persian army numbered about a hundred and twenty thousand, with thirty elephants, and that the Muslims numbered nine to ten thousand. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The duels and the fighting
The account mentions the dispatch of al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba to Rustam, and then of Amr ibn Ma'dikarib and al-Ash'ath ibn Qays. It tells the story of Abu Mihjan al-Thaqafi and the permission that Sa'd's wife gave him to fight. It says that Rustam was killed, that his killer was not known, and that his death was attributed to a number of men, and that the Persians fled to al-Mada'in. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

al-Mas'udi named its days and nights: the day of Aghwath, the night of al-Harir, the night of al-Sawad, the day of 'Amas, and the night of al-Qadisiyya. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.609-613}

It needs an additional source for the numbers of the two armies and the names of the slayers.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The speaker recounts Umar's letter to Sa'd at Qadisiyya: the Muslims are given victory through their obedience to God and their enemy's disobedience, and if both sides are equal in disobedience, the enemy prevails by numbers and arms {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 01:13:00}.

He says that the sins of an army are more to be feared for it than its enemy {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:25:30}. The speaker's view (opinion): religion does not separate from politics, religion covers everything, and Umar's letter to Sa'd expresses this principle {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:26:00}.

He recounts Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas praying before Qadisiyya, and the story of the companion Sawad ibn Abdullah, whom the Prophet struck on the back with a stick while straightening the rows, and who spoke to him about it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:08:00}.

In the judge's sermon as the speaker recites it, Qadisiyya is listed among the battles, under Sa'd's command {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:30:30}.

He recalls that Abu Lu'lu'a passed Persian captives in the days of Qadisiyya, in the context of his account of Umar's death (partly unclear) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:44:30}.

## In al-Dinawari
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports that about twenty thousand men gathered for Sa'd, and that Yazdagird was crowned as a boy of sixteen and gave the command to Rustam ibn Hurmuz. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 119} Rustam camped at Dayr al-A'war for four months to wear the Arabs down. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 120} An abscess on his thigh kept Sa'd from riding, so he gave command to Khalid ibn Urfuta and stayed in the palace of al-Qadisiyya. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 121} The Persians drew up in thirteen ranks and the Arabs in three. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 122} Rustam was found with a hundred wounds, and no one knew who had killed him; some say he drowned in the river of al-Qadisiyya. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 123}

## In Khalifa ibn Khayyat
[[person/khalifa-ibn-khayyat|Khalifa ibn Khayyat]] records Qadisiyya among the events of the year 15, with Sa'd ibn Malik (Ibn Abi Waqqas) commanding the Muslims and Rustam, with al-Jalinus and Dhu al-Hajib, commanding the Persians. He gives several reports on the numbers: from Abu Wa'il, that the Muslims were between seven and eight thousand against Rustam's sixty thousand; from Khalid ibn Umayr, that the Persians were forty thousand, with seventy elephants in one report; and from Ibn Ishaq, that Rustam had sixty thousand of his picked troops against six or seven thousand Muslims. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 131}
He then reports from Abu al-Hasan al-Mada'ini that Sa'd followed the defeated army from Qadisiyya: the people of al-Hira and the lord of Nahr Bistam made terms with him, he routed forces at Nars, Kutha and Dayr Ka'b, and at last camped before al-Mada'in and took it. {cite:tarikh-khalifa-umari-1985 p. 133}
