## Overview
This is the text whose opening is on p. 73, after [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] offered the settlement to the dhimma of [[place/najran|Najran]] before jurists and a group of Muslims, wrote it in his own hand and had it witnessed; see the event [[event/al-hadi-settlement-with-the-dhimmis-of-najran-at-one-ninth-284]]. It comes from a Zaydi book told by [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] and his son, and its rulings are given as the text presents them, without comparison with other schools; the museum gives no ruling on them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.73} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.74} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.75} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.76} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.77} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.78}

## The preface: provision and zakat
The letter opens with praise of God and the statement that He created creatures without need of them and lavished His provision on them, and set for the poor in the property of the rich a share, half-tenth and a tenth according to the watering of the land. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.73} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.74} It cites the verse on taking alms ([[passage/quran-9-103|Qur'an 9:103]]), the verse on giving zakat ([[passage/quran-73-20|Qur'an 73:20]]) and the verse on the eight heads ([[passage/quran-9-60|Qur'an 9:60]]), and says God made zakat a help to the believers in jihad and a help and provision for the poor. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.74}

## The reason for the settlement
It then says that property-owners among the dhimmis had made bold with much Muslim property, bought it and come to own a large part of the country, so that what they owned became free of the tithes that applied while in Muslim hands, since there is no zakat on dhimmis on coin, trade goods, pre-Islamic land, sheep, camels or cattle, and zakat is a purification for believers; having bought it they removed the help God set in it for the believers. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.74} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.75} So he judged that leaving it in the hands of those who owe no zakat was not allowed; he called them and told them, they protested at returning it and said "take from us what is due on Muslims", and he told them that was not permitted; then he judged that he would offer them either to withdraw from it or a settlement from which they would benefit and which would let the dhimmis buy Muslim property and stay on what they had bought. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.75} He feared that settling at a tenth might lead the ignorant to think he had tithed the dhimmis as he tithed Muslims and did not know there is no alms on dhimmis, so he made a settlement clearly distinct. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.75} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.76}

## The terms
One-ninth on what is watered by flowing or rain water, and a half of a ninth on what is watered by wheels, enclosures and draw-camels; the dhimmis agreed and chose it, so what they had bought from Muslims was confirmed to them, and they were allowed to buy what they liked on paying it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.76} After paying what is named they owe, on crops, palms, fruit, cane and whatever bears zakat for believers, the same whether little or much, a ninth of what rain waters, whether one farq, two, ten or twenty. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.76} The editor's footnote says a heading appears here, "latitude in buying Muslim property as they wish", which he thinks was inserted. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.76}

## Protection against extra exactions
Once they have paid it, "God's curse and the curse of those who curse, of angels and all people" falls on any governor, in his lifetime or after his death, who adds even one dirham, or deals unjustly in appraising, measuring or valuing their property, or takes anything else, or imposes a levy, task or burden. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.76} This is the imam's wording in his own document, given as it stands.

## The jizya
Between the dhimma and the believers, once they pay the jizya on the heads of their free men, not of women, slaves or boys, their well-off pay forty-eight "qafla" dirhams per man, their middle group twenty-four and their working people twelve. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.77} When they have paid the jizya on their heads and those who bought Muslim property have paid what was settled, they have secured their lives by the jizya, their property is forbidden to anyone who believes in God, they have their purchases, they may not have what is in their hands taken away or be prevented from buying further, and the Muslim governors and subjects owe them protection; no one may wrong them, and whoever demands more is "quit of God and God is quit of him". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.77} See [[concept/jizya]] and [[concept/ahl-al-dhimma]].

## Witnessing and date
Al-Hadi called God, His angels and all the believers present to witness what the letter contains after he had approved it; all the dhimma of Najran approved, the letter was read to them and to the Muslims, all understood it and witnessed to the imam's approval and that of the dhimmis present who owned property at Najran; it is stated to be valid between the Muslims and any dhimma elsewhere who accept it, no Muslim preventing them nor any but a sinner standing in the way. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.77} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.78} It was written in Jumada II 284 AH, "with seven remaining", and the page then lists about fifty witnesses by name, among them "al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Qutayma" and "Abd al-Malik ibn Abd al-Malik", names met in earlier reports with doubtful readings; the list ends by saying the people of the land were glad and paid what was due. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.78} The witnesses' names are not copied here because many are uncertain in the OCR text.

Page 78 was re-read from its image and the list agrees with the text-recognition output.
