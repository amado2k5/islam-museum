## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] returned to [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]] and stayed there, and sent for his brother [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] and Ibn al-Ruwayya, who came to him and stayed; some days later an illness struck him and grew so heavy that people were saying he had died {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.238}. After that he ordered an army out against the men's camp at Dal'; they met and fought, his army killed many of them and brought their heads to Sana'a (the margin of the manuscript reads: the carrying of the heads from Dal' to Sana'a) {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.238}. Then Abu Muhammad [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] went out to the camp of Zahr, fell on them in a hard valley, killed some of their horsemen and foot and came back {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.238}.

## Detail
Some days later al-Hadi ordered an army to lie in ambush for horse that used to go out to [[place/al-rahba-sanaa|al-Rahba]] to cut the road; the two forces met and fought, riders were struck down and their mounts taken {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.239}. The book gives no day or month for these actions and does not name the commander of the first army, so the museum places them in the year by order of the narrative. The carrying of the heads to Sana'a is reported as the narrator gives it, without comment. The reading Dal' is that of the printed text and is supported by the margin.
