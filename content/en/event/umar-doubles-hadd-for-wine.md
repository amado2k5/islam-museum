## Summary
As drinking spread in the newly opened lands, [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] took the counsel of [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and ordered that a drinker be flogged eighty lashes. Ali explained: "when he is drunk he raves, and when he raves he slanders, and the penalty for slander is eighty" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.143}.

## Reasoning
The book says that Ali held that a drinker who becomes drunk raves, that one who raves slanders, and that the penalty for the slanderer is eighty; Umar acted on this. The book describes the saying as transmitted from Ali through a chain of narrators, and says Umar took his counsel {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.143}.

## The jurists' disagreement
The book sets out the jurists' views. Malik, al-Thawri, Abu Hanifa and those who followed them make the penalty eighty by the consensus of the Companions. Al-Shafi'i, and one view of Ahmad, make it forty, with anything beyond that a discretionary penalty the imam may impose. It also reports Ibn Taymiyya's detailed position {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.143}.

## Notes for researchers
- The book does not date the decision; the period is inferred from Umar's caliphate.
- This page concerns jurisprudence; specialists should consult the books of the schools before expanding it.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The speaker recounts Umar's rebuke of men drinking behind a closed door: "O enemies of God, do you think God does not see you?" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:42:00}.

He recounts the revelation on wine: the Prophet's companions asked for a clear ruling, Umar among them, after they heard that its harm outweighed its benefit {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:28:30}. He recounts that Abu Bakr said he never drank wine, in the Jahiliyya or in Islam {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:29:00}.

He recounts a companion's error in leading the Maghrib prayer, and that Umar heard the verse forbidding prayer in a state of intoxication. The speaker says the Qur'anic term for avoidance of prohibitions appears only for idolatry and wine (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:30:00}.

He recounts Anas going round Medina announcing the ban on wine {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:31:30}. He says the jars were poured into the streets of Medina and the smell lasted about twenty-one days (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:32:00}. He recounts that Umar's son was accused of drinking privately. He was brought to Amr ibn al-As, who applied the penalty; the story reached Umar in Medina. The speaker says the drinking took place in a closed room and not before a group (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:26:30}.
