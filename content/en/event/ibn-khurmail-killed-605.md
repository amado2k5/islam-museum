## Summary
In 605 AH al-Husayn ibn Khurmil, lord of Herat, was killed by Jildak ibn Taghril, Khwarazmshah's amir, after being seized by a ruse, while his vizier refused to surrender the city. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that Ibn Khurmil, angered by the Khwarazmian troops' mistreatment of the populace, arrested them and apologized to Khwarazmshah, who — being occupied fighting the Khita — wrote back approving, while secretly ordering Izz al-Din Jildak to march on Herat and seize Ibn Khurmil at their first meeting; Jildak came with two thousand horsemen. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334} Ibn Khurmil's vizier Khwaja al-Sahib advised him not to go out to meet him, fearing treachery, but he went out; at the encounter Jildak's men seized him, his companions fled to the city and informed the vizier, who closed the gates, manned the walls and prepared for siege. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334} Jildak offered the vizier safe-conduct and threatened to kill Ibn Khurmil unless the city surrendered; the vizier shouted the slogan of Ghiyath al-Din Mahmud al-Ghuri, saying the city belonged to Ghiyath al-Din and his father before him. Ibn Khurmil was brought before the wall and ordered him to surrender; he refused and was killed — which Ibn al-Athir calls the end of his treachery. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 334}
