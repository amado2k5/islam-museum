## Summary
The sultan [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub]] died in [[place/damascus|Damascus]] in the last days of Safar of the year 589 after a short illness that struck him on his return from greeting the pilgrims; the histories tell of the Qur'an being recited to him, of his being prompted with the declaration of faith and of his smile as he died, then of his washing, shrouding and burial in his house in the citadel, and of his transfer in 592 to his mausoleum beside the [[building/umayyad-mosque|Umayyad Mosque]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} He left one Tyrian dinar and thirty-six dirhams, and no house or property. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 9} This record stands beside the two existing records of his death, [[event/death-of-salah-al-din-589|one]] and [[event/death-of-saladin-589|the other]], and adds the deathbed, burial and estate as Ibn Kathir relates them.

## The start of the illness
Ibn Kathir relates that the year began with him in perfect health, that he and his brother al-Adil went out hunting east of Damascus, and that they agreed that once the Franks were dealt with he would go to the lands of Rum and send his brother to Baghdad. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 7} When the pilgrims arrived on Monday, 11 Safar, he rode out to greet them with his nephew Sayf al-Islam, lord of Yemen, and returned to the citadel; it was the last time he rode. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 7} Then a bilious fever seized him on the night of Saturday, 16 Safar, and in the morning [[person/al-qadi-al-fadil|al-Qadi al-Fadil]], [[person/ibn-shaddad-baha-al-din|Ibn Shaddad]] and his son [[person/al-malik-al-afdal-ali|al-Afdal]] came in and he complained to them of his restlessness. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 7} The physicians attended him on the fourth day; then dryness and heavy sweating came over him, and the senior commanders were summoned and pledged allegiance to his son al-Afdal Nur al-Din Ali, who was his deputy in Damascus. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 7}

## The dying hours
His state grew grave on the night of Wednesday, 27 Safar, and he sent for Shaykh Abu Ja'far, imam of the Kallasa, to spend the night with him reading the Qur'an and prompting him with the declaration of faith. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 7} The shaykh related that he was reciting to him while he was in the throes and read a verse that God knows the unseen and the seen, [[passage/quran-59-22|Qur'an 59:22]], and the sultan said: so it is, true. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 7} When the dawn call was given al-Qadi al-Fadil came in as he was at his last breath, and when the reciter read the verse of trusting in God he smiled, his face lit up and he gave up his spirit. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} He was fifty-seven, having been born at Takrit in the year 532. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8}

## Washing, prayer and burial
Ibn Kathir relates that the markets were closed and the people of Damascus had never suffered such a loss; the preacher of the city, the jurist al-Dawla'i, washed him, and al-Qadi al-Fadil brought the shroud and the cost of the burial from his own lawful wealth, while his sons, old and young, wept. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} His bier was brought out after the noon prayer and the judge Ibn al-Zaki led the people in prayer, and he was buried in his house in the citadel. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} He had long before bequeathed that a tomb and a school for the Shafi'is be built for him near the Masjid al-Qadam; his son began it but it was not finished, and al-Afdal then bought a house north of the Kallasa and made it the tomb. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} He was moved to it on Ashura of the year 592; the chief judge Ibn al-Zaki prayed over him under the eagle dome with al-Afdal's permission, and al-Afdal himself went into the grave and buried him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} It is said that the sword with which he took part in jihad was buried with him by order of al-Qadi al-Fadil, in hope that he might lean on it on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} Condolences were held at the Umayyad Mosque for three days. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} Ibn Kathir mentions the elegy of al-Imad al-Katib at the end of his book al-Barq al-Shami, two hundred and two lines, which Abu Shama gave in al-Rawdatayn. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 8} The museum holds a draft card for the [[building/mazar-salah-al-din|mausoleum of Salah al-Din]] whose sources are not yet checked.

## What he left
Al-Imad and others said he left in his treasury no gold but one jurm, that is one Tyrian dinar, and thirty-six dirhams; another says forty-seven; he left no house, no estate, no farm, no orchard, nor any kind of property. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 9} He had seventeen sons and one daughter, others having died in his lifetime, and sixteen sons outlived him, the eldest al-Afdal Nur al-Din Ali, born in Egypt in 565, then [[person/al-malik-al-aziz-uthman|al-Aziz Uthman]] in 567, then al-Zafir al-Khidr, then [[person/al-malik-al-zahir-ghazi|al-Zahir Ghazi]], born in Egypt in mid-Ramadan 568. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 9} Ibn Kathir explains how little he left by his generosity and kindness to his commanders and others, even to his enemies. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 10} He was sparing in dress, food and mount, wearing only cotton, linen and wool. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 10} He is said not to have missed the congregation for a long time before his death, nor in his last illness, when the imam would come in and pray with him and he would struggle to stand despite his weakness. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 10}

## Where the reports differ
The reports differ on the sum he left in dirhams: thirty-six or forty-seven. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 9} What Ibn al-Athir's al-Kamil has of the sultan's consultation with his son al-Afdal and brother al-Adil before the illness, and of his asking for water five times, is in the [[event/death-of-salah-al-din-589|other record of his death]]. In the pages read Ibn Kathir gives no testament to his son al-Zahir.

## Sources and documentation
The record rests on Ibn Kathir. In the pages read from al-Kamil, Ibn Khallikan and al-Dhahabi nothing was found on a testament of Salah al-Din to his son al-Zahir, so none is given.
