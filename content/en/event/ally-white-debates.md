## Summary
The Shabir Ally–James White debate series, 2006–2014, on New Testament transmission, the deity of Christ and the crucifixion — including the Pretoria debate of 2013. {cite:aomin-pretoria-debate-2013}

## The line of the debates
A precise textual contest: criticism of New Testament transmission, the language of Jesus' deity, and the exegesis of the contested texts on both sides.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Ally**: traced the deity of Jesus historically — the earliest Jewish-Christian communities do not show the Nicene form of the doctrine, and New Testament transmission shows later ecclesial intervention at the decisive texts.
- **White**: defended New Testament transmission and its early manuscripts, that the deity is in the Gospel text itself rather than a later development, and the soundness of the parallel Masoretic record.
- **Ally (rebuttals)**: pressed the measure of textual criticism — when may a derivative reading ground an argument? — and challenged the coherence of 'preserved text' with the manuscript tradition's history of variation.
- **White (rebuttals)**: argued from the Greek evidence and transmissional criteria, holding manuscript variation does not touch the core doctrine.
- **The verdict**: a close textual-criticism series — 'text for text' as the debates styled themselves — their transcripts a reference in polemic source-criticism. {cite:aomin-pretoria-debate-2013}
