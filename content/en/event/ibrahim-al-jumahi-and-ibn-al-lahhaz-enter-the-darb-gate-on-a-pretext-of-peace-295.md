## Overview
Ibrahim al-Jumahi and Muhammad ibn al-Lahhaz entered the Darb gate on a pretext of peace and safety and asked Abu Ja'far for a private talk. He ordered the people to withdraw, and the two stayed with him a long time so that the crowd would leave its posts and the Banu al-Harith ambush could enter while the people were unaware. Then a Rabi'i rider came in at speed without greeting Abu Ja'far, and the two men rode off.

## Detail
After the two men asked Ibn Humayd to send his troops back to their place, saying that if he did so no one of the enemy's army would stay in place, they entered the Darb gate and told the people they had come for peace and safety. They greeted Abu Ja'far and said they had a request they wished to make in private. He allowed them to speak as they wished and ordered the people to clear the place. They stayed with him a long time, and their purpose was to spread the people from their posts so that the Banu al-Harith ambush could enter while the people were unaware and unprepared. Banu Abd al-Madan told their clients to leave the fighting ground. The two then asked to consult Banu Abd al-Madan and the Banu al-Harith men present, were allowed to, and met them all in private, while Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah and his companions did not know what they were planning. Then a man of Banu Rabi'a named Tanaf, as the printed name reads, came in on a galloping horse from the place where the ambush had entered, without greeting Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah. Ibrahim al-Jumahi and Ibn al-Lahhaz then rode off at once. The name Tanaf has a variant in a marginal reading and is recorded as printed.
