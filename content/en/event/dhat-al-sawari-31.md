## Overview
The battle of Dhat al-Sawari was the first great naval battle in Islam, according to Ibn al-Athir. It took place in 31 AH, or some say 34 AH, between the Muslims and the Romans. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.2 p.488}

## Preparation
Constantine, son of Heraclius, set out with a force the Romans had not gathered like since Islam began: five hundred or six hundred ships. The Muslims went out, with Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan over the people of Syria and Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh over the sea. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.2 p.488-490}

## The fighting
The two sides spent the night, the Muslims reading the Qur'an and praying, and the Romans striking the bells. The next day they drew their ships close and tied them together, and fought with swords and daggers. Ibn al-Athir says that countless Romans were killed, and Constantine fled wounded. It is also said that he went to Sicily, where they put him in a bath and killed him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.2 p.488-490}

## Its effect on the strife
Ibn al-Athir says that Muhammad ibn Abi Hudhayfa and Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr began speaking about the matter of Uthman during this expedition. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir v.2 p.488-490}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
[[person/ibn-abd-al-hakam|Ibn Abd al-Hakam]] reports from Ibn Bukayr, from al-Layth, that Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh fought Dhat al-Sawari in the year 34. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 255} In al-Layth's report from Yazid ibn Abi Habib, the Muslims had two hundred-odd ships and heard that the Byzantine ruler was coming with a thousand. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 256} Ships were then chained together in battle; an enemy ship nearly dragged Abd Allah's ship over to them, until Alqama ibn Yazid al-Ghutayfi struck the chain with his sword and cut it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 257} He adds that Constantine son of Heraclius later set out with a thousand ships for Alexandria, and a wind drowned them all except Constantine. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 258}
