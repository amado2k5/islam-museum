## Overview
Yemen's liberation from the Abyssinians under Sayf Dhu Yazan in 575 CE with Persian aid, then his assassination and Yemen's transformation into a Persian province until Badhan's Islam in 638 CE.

## The story
After the Elephant battle, the Yemenis sought Persian aid and resisted Abyssinia until they expelled them from the land, winning independence in 575 CE under Ma'di Karib [[person/sayf-ibn-dhi-yazan|ibn Sayf Dhu Yazan al-Himyari]], whom they took as their king {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

Ma'di Karib had kept a band of Abyssinians with him as servants and attendants, and they assassinated him one day; with his death kingship left the house of Dhu Yazan, and Khosrow appointed a Persian governor over San'a and made Yemen a Persian province {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

Persian governors kept succeeding one another over Yemen until the last of them, [[person/badhan|Badhan]], embraced Islam in 638 CE; with his Islam, Persian sway over Yemen's lands ended {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
The Yemenis drove out the Abyssinians with Persia's help; then the king's own guards killed the king, and Persia ruled Yemen until its last governor became Muslim.
