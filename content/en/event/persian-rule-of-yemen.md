## Summary
The liberation of [[place/yemen|Yemen]] from the Abyssinians in 575 CE, under Ma'd Yakrib ibn [[person/sayf-ibn-dhi-yazan|Sayf Dhu Yazan al-Himyari]] with Persian help; then his assassination, and Yemen's transformation into a Persian province until the Islam of its last governor, [[person/badhan|Badhan]], in 638 CE. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}

## Independence
After the battle of the Elephant, the Yemenis sought Persian help and resisted the Abyssinians until they drove them out of the land. They gained independence in 575 CE under Ma'd Yakrib ibn Sayf Dhu Yazan al-Himyari, and took him as their king. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}

## Assassination and the end of the kingship
Ma'd Yakrib had kept with him a band of Abyssinians who served him and walked in his retinue. They assassinated him one day, and with his death kingship passed out of the house of Dhu Yazan. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}

## Persian rule
Khosrow appointed a Persian governor over [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]] and made Yemen a Persian province. Persian governors succeeded one another over Yemen until the last of them, [[person/badhan|Badhan]], embraced Islam in 638 CE, and with his Islam Persian sway over the lands of Yemen ended. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25} Men of Yemen adhered to Zoroastrianism during the Persian occupation. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}

## Notes for researchers
The year of Badhan's Islam, "638 CE", is written as the author gives it. The biographical books place his Islam during the Prophet's lifetime, after Khosrow's letter (around 7 AH), so the year needs checking against other sources. It is recorded as it appears.

## For young visitors
The Yemenis drove out the Abyssinians with Persia's help. Then the king's own guards killed the king, and Persia ruled Yemen until its last governor became Muslim.
