## Overview
After [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] left Shibam he stayed some days in the land of Banu Rabi'a and then went to Batnat Hujur to set it right {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.216}. The narrator says that Al Ya'fur and Al Tarif saw that the army with [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] had grown small and were swayed by greed; they gathered, took counsel and agreed to send two men of Al Ya'fur to the land of [[place/qudum-yemen|Qudum]], where at that time, the narrator says, there was a party he calls Kharijis who did not follow the line of the Prophet's household {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.216}. By night the two got out by a ruse, reached Qudum and complained to its people of what [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] had done to them: he had jailed all of Al Ya'fur and most of Al Tarif and scattered them among Sana'a, Zahr (where Abu al-Ghasham was held with a group) and Shibam, and the narrator says things were quiet while they were jailed {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.216}. The two sent out were not among the prisoners, and many fools gathered to them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.216}. The news reached [[person/sasaa-ibn-jafar-al-hamdani|Sa'sa'a ibn Ja'far]] of Raydah, whom the narrator charges with wronging the people of al-Bawn and with immoral conduct {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.216}. When al-Bawn passed to al-Hadi, who took it from his rule and put [[person/muhammad-ibn-isa-al-tamimi|Muhammad ibn Isa al-Tamimi]] over it, Sa'sa'a was angry; the footnote gives the other manuscript's reading that he had pledged to al-Hadi and then fell silent {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.216} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.217}. When he learned that Qudum had joined the two sons of Abu al-Khayr, he rose in al-Bawn, took horses that were fed for al-Hadi and shared them among the Qudumis and others until the strife began, took grain in al-Bawn that was, in the narrator's phrase, God's property, gathered fools to himself and with them disordered al-Bawn {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.217}.

## Detail
The two pages give no day or month, so the date is derived at year precision. The narrator's charges against Sa'sa'a, the people of Qudum, Al Ya'fur and Al Tarif are his own and are attributed to him with no verdict from the museum; the words of abuse are not repeated. The two sons of Abu al-Khayr are named only so, and appear to be the two men of Al Ya'fur sent out; the museum goes no further. The earlier account of the book's added first pages, [[event/revolt-at-shibam-ibn-abbad-killed-288|the opponents' rising at Shibam and the killing of Ibn Abbad]], says a man of the line of Abu al-Khayr and Sa'sa'a allied near Bayt Dhukhar; it is the same episode told twice, and the museum does not reconcile the two tellings. It follows [[event/al-hadi-at-thath-and-bashar-and-the-return-to-sanaa-and-shibam-288]].
