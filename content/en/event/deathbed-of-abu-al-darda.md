## Summary
[[person/abu-al-darda|Abu al-Darda' Uwaymir]] was one of the Ansar who settled in Damascus and held its judgeship, as al-Dhahabi records. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 386} The books of piety and biography preserve the account of his last illness: his companions' questions about his complaint, his words as he was dying, what he said to his wife Umm al-Darda', and his advice to Mu'awiya about who should judge after him.

## His illness
Ibn al-Jawzi reports from Mu'awiya ibn Qurra that Abu al-Darda' fell ill and his companions came in and asked what ailed him. He said his sins. They asked what he desired and he said Paradise. They said, shall we call a physician for you? He said: the One who laid me down. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230}

## His words as he was dying
From Isma'il ibn Ubaydallah, on the authority of Umm al-Darda': when he was dying he kept saying, who will work for a day like this day? Who will work for an hour like this hour? Who will work for a resting place like this one? Then he recited a verse from al-An'am, [[passage/quran-6-110|Qur'an 6:110]]. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230} In Abu Muslim's report he came to him as he was breathing his last and heard him say: is there no man to work for a place of falling like this one, for an hour like this one? Then he was taken. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230}

## His counsel to his household and what he left
Umm al-Darda' relates that she prayed that God marry her to Abu al-Darda' in Paradise as He had married her in this world, and he said to her: if you wish that and I am the first to go, do not marry after me. When he died Mu'awiya proposed to her and she said she would not marry any husband in this world until she married Abu al-Darda' in Paradise. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230} She also reports asking him whether, if she were in need after him, she might eat from charity; he said no, work and eat, and if you are too weak to work, gather the gleanings of the ears but do not eat charity. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230} Abu Nu'aym gives the account of Mu'awiya's proposal and her refusal in the same sense. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 225} I found no statement of his property or belongings in the pages I read; Ibn al-Jawzi, quoting al-Waqidi, says only that he left descendants in Syria. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230}

## His advice on the judgeship
Ibn Abd al-Barr reports that when death came to Abu al-Darda', Mu'awiya asked him whom he considered fit for the office, and he named Fadala ibn Ubayd; when Abu al-Darda' died, Mu'awiya sent for [[person/fadala-ibn-ubayd|Fadala]] and appointed him judge. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 599} Ibn Kathir likewise says Abu al-Darda' had been judge for Mu'awiya since Umar's governorship and, on his deathbed, advised appointing Fadala. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 515}

## Death and grave
Ibn al-Jawzi, citing Ibn Sa'd from al-Waqidi, says he died in Damascus in the year 32 in Uthman's caliphate, and, from someone other than al-Waqidi on Khalid ibn Ma'dan's authority, that he died in Syria in 31. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230} Al-Waqidi and Abu Mushir both say 32. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 387} The pages I read do not mention his washing, funeral prayer or the site of his grave, so I do not assign him a specific tomb.

## Differing reports
The reports on his death fall between 31 and 32, as above. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 230} A separate point of confusion is that the grave at Bab al-Saghir known as Bilal's is thought by Ibn Kathir to be that of Bilal ibn Abi al-Darda', his son, rather than the Prophet's muezzin; see the [[event/deathbed-of-bilal-ibn-rabah|account of Bilal ibn Rabah's death]]. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 105}
