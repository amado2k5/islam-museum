## Overview
al-Baladhuri relates that when [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] had finished with the people of the Ridda, he sent armies to Syria and made three banners for Khalid ibn Sa'id, Shurahbil ibn Hasana and Amr ibn al-As. He placed the last two and Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan on the roads to Ayla and Tabuk, and their host reached twenty-four thousand. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.149}

## The campaign
[[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] then marched from Iraq, and they made him commander in every war. al-Baladhuri describes the battle of Ajnadayn, the peaceful conquest of Busra, the forcible conquest of Jordan by Shurahbil except Tiberias, then the battle of Fahl and Marj al-Saffar, and then the conquest of Damascus and Jerusalem. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.149}

[[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] appointed [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] over the whole command of Syria. The source covers pages 149–165. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## For Sprouts
When Abu Bakr finished with the apostates he sent the armies to Syria under three banners — Khalid ibn Sa'id, Shurahbil ibn Hasana and Amr ibn al-As — twenty-four thousand in all. Then Khalid ibn al-Walid marched from Iraq and was made commander over every war, and Umar appointed Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah over all Syria.

## For Explorers
Al-Baladhuri records that when Abu Bakr was done with the Ridda peoples he sent the armies to Syria, raising three banners for Khalid ibn Sa'id, Shurahbil ibn Hasana and Amr ibn al-As, and put the latter two and Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan over the roads of Ayla and Tabuk — twenty-four thousand in all. Then Khalid ibn al-Walid marched from Iraq and was made commander in every war. His account names the day of Ajnadayn, the taking of Busra by treaty, Shurahbil's taking of the Jordan by force except Tiberias, then the day of Fahl and Marj al-Saffar, then the conquest of Damascus and Jerusalem. Umar gave Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah charge of all Syria.

## For Scholars
Al-Baladhuri in Futuh al-Buldan (pp. 149–165) records that once Abu Bakr had finished with the Ridda peoples he dispatched the armies to Syria, raising three banners for Khalid ibn Sa'id, Shurahbil ibn Hasana and Amr ibn al-As, and placed the latter two and Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan over the roads of Ayla and Tabuk — their total reaching twenty-four thousand. Then Khalid ibn al-Walid marched from Iraq and was made commander over every war. The scenes named: the day of Ajnadayn, Busra taken by treaty, Shurahbil's taking of the Jordan by force except Tiberias, then the day of Fahl and Marj al-Saffar, then Damascus and Jerusalem. Umar ibn al-Khattab gave Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah charge of the whole of Syria.
