## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] relates that he prayed one night in the mosque with [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]; when they went into the house al-Hadi said to him: "Abu Jafar, my heart will not be healed until God is obeyed in all the lands", and then slept on his bed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51} The story comes from a Zaydi book and from a follower's report, and is the picture of the imam's asceticism and fear that the narrator wishes to give.

## The sound and the recitation
Al-Hadi heard a sound, went out of the room and asked what it was; he was told it was a person reciting, and said he had thought it was singing; then he said: "Things have opposites, and the opposite of my life is sins." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51} The text does not say where the sound came from.

## The question of salvation
He went back to his bed and said to the narrator: "Do you think I shall be saved from the Fire?" The narrator answered yes, someone like you is saved, and he replied: "And how shall I be saved while I lie on this bed?" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51} This is the narrator's way of expressing the imam's scrupulousness, not a ruling by the museum on his fate.

## Another night
The narrator begins another night at his house: al-Hadi heard a sound, sent a servant to one of his trusted men of the town to summon him at that hour, and when he arrived said to him "Go to". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51} The rest of the story is on p. 52, which has not yet been read, so it is neither given nor guessed here.
