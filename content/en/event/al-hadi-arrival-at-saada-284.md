## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] says they reached [[place/saada|Sa'da]] on the sixth of Safar 284 AH and came to Khawlan in the midst of a great feud in which men had perished, wealth had gone, the land was in drought and the earth barren; it was sowing time, and he saw some of the crops dried up for thirst. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.41}

## The reconciliation and the oath
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Al-Hadi]] pitched his tents near the town and told them to dismount, and people came out to him of their own accord, among them the two parties of the feud, named in the text "Sa'd and al-Rabi'a"; he greeted them and told them to greet one another. He then gave an eloquent sermon, praising God, praying for the Prophet, reminding them of God and exhorting them, and the narrator saw people weeping and crying out as pilgrims cry out at the Sacred House. He had a copy of the Qur'an brought and made them swear to one another to give up the feud and enmity, which they did; then he had them swear to him for obedience, support, upholding God's command and mutual aid, and they pledged allegiance to him on that spot; the two parties mingled, cried takbir and entered Sa'da together. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.41} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.42}

## The narrator's comment
The narrator says he never saw a finer day, that he had thought their affair would never be settled, and that when he saw how quickly they agreed he knew it was an awe God had given Yahya ibn al-Husayn, that is al-Hadi, for he had with him no more than fifty men. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.42} He reports that people of Sa'da told him that the commanders of [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]] used to come to them and try to make peace between them without being able to, and that one of them, with thousands of soldiers, stood between them as they fought, twenty were killed, and still no peace came, while al-Hadi reconciled them by the easiest means. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.42} He then says God sent them rain, their land became fertile, their fruit sound and their roads safe, and he relates that when justice appears the rain comes down. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.42} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.43} These are the narrator's judgements and not the museum's; the names of the people of Sa'da he cites are read with doubtful forms in the scan and are not copied here.

## Appointing governors
Al-Hadi then appointed governors in the districts, which are the villages, to collect food, and wrote each a charge. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.43} The text of the charge begins on p. 44: it appoints the man to the collection of a named village and to the gathering of the tithes God has made due, tells him to enter the town with calm and dignity, to rent a house for himself, to put no burden of maintenance on anyone of the town and to accept no gift, any gift to an official because of his office going to the treasury of the Muslims. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.44} The text of the charge continues on the following pages.
