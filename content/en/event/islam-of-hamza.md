## Summary
In the late sixth year of the prophethood, [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]] passed the Prophet at al-Safa, harmed him and reviled him while the Prophet was silent. A freedwoman of Abdullah ibn Jud'an saw it from her dwelling on al-Safa and told [[person/hamza|Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib]] when he returned from hunting. Hamza went out to Abu Jahl and struck him with his bow, wounding his head. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.100} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/290-291}

## The blow
The freedwoman had seen this from her dwelling on al-Safa. Hamza came, the most honoured young man of Quraysh and the most unyielding in temper. He entered the mosque, stood over Abu Jahl and said: "You, with the yellowed backside! Do you revile my nephew while I am on his religion?" Then he struck him with the bow, inflicting a serious wound. Banu Makhzum rose up, and Banu Hashim rose up. Abu Jahl said: "Leave Abu Umara alone, for I cursed his nephew with a vile curse." {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.101}

## His Islam
Hamza's Islam began as the pride of a man who refused to let his protégé be insulted. Then God opened his heart, and he held fast to the faith, and the Muslims were greatly strengthened by him. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.101} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul-al-najdi p.102} The book inclines to the view that he became a Muslim in Dhu al-Hijja of the sixth year. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.100}

## Notes for researchers
- The month of Dhu al-Hijja is the book's preference, not an explicit statement. The cause of his Islam, as the book mentions, is a report of pride before his heart was opened.

## For young visitors
The Prophet's uncle Hamza was strong-willed. He saw Abu Jahl harming his nephew, struck him with his bow, and said that he was on his nephew's religion. Then God guided him to Islam, and through him the Muslims became stronger.
