## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] says the people of [[place/najran|Najran]] returned to one another, unasked, what they held that had been seized, since in their feud they had taken palms, land, slaves and horses from one another; when [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] came they gave back the wrongs among them "from an awe God cast into their hearts". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70} He says a Najran man told him a palm grove had been seized from him ten years before and he had not eaten a date from it until al-Hadi came and it was returned, and that he heard groups tell similar stories too long to give. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70} This is the narrator's report, and his explanation by an awe God cast is his, not the museum's.

## The army kept from the crops
One day al-Hadi rode round the villages of Najran and ordered the army not to enter the crops or spoil people's fruit; at night he asked one of his companions whether he had seen anyone in the army touch or damage any of the people's fruit, and on hearing no said "Praise be to God, abundantly." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70} He then sent governors to the Najran villages, ordering them to fear God, enjoin good and forbid wrong. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70}
