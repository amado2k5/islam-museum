## Summary
'The Qur'an or the Bible: Which is God's Word?' — Birmingham 1988; the second Deedat–Shorrosh encounter. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}

## The line of the debate
This time the debate ran on both books at once: the case for the preserved Qur'an against the case for the two Testaments — the reference model for 'book against book' disputation in modern da'wah.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Deedat**: the Qur'an alone has a secured divine chain — textually preserved by mass transmission and a continuous recited practice — and he attacked the Bible's multiple versions.
- **Shorrosh**: defended the two Testaments and attacked the Qur'an — its sources, arrangement and history, as Christian apologists frame it.
- **Deedat (rebuttal)**: turned the criterion on the criterion: if the test is preservation and integrity, the Qur'an meets it better; and he answered the Qur'an objections on the same textual method.
- **Shorrosh (rebuttal)**: held to New Testament reliability and put his usual objections to the Qur'an's sources and history.
- **The verdict**: 'book against book' became the generation's title — the two booklets and recordings the widest reference in comparative polemics. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}
