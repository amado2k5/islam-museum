## Summary
In 595 AH the man who claimed to be 'Isa son of Maryam was crucified at Damascus; he had led a group astray, and the scholars ruled for his execution {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 799}.

## Details
Under the events of 595 Ibn al-'Imad records that the man who claimed to be 'Isa son of Maryam was crucified at Damascus; he had misled a faction — that is, drawn a following into his error — and the scholars issued the ruling for his death {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 799}. The account indicates that a pretender arose in Damascus claiming to be 'Isa ibn Maryam, won over a group of people, and the scholars then judged him to death, the sentence being crucifixion {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 799}. The source names neither the man nor the number of his followers nor the ruler who carried out the sentence, so the incident stands as reported: a terse notice of a pretender whom the scholars condemned and who was crucified at Damascus {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 799}.
