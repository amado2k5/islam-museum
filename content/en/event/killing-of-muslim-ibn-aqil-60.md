## Overview
[[person/muslim-ibn-aqil|Muslim ibn Aqil]] appeared in [[place/kufa|Kufa]] in Dhu al-Hijja 60 AH, and about twelve thousand people pledged to him, or it is said eighteen thousand. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 180} His affair ended with his killing at the hand of the governor of Kufa, [[person/ubayd-allah-ibn-ziyad|Ubayd Allah ibn Ziyad]]. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 180}

## What happened
Ibn Ziyad struck [[person/hani-ibn-urwa|Hani ibn Urwa]] with a staff and ordered him imprisoned. Muslim called out the slogan "O Victorious" and eighteen thousand men gathered to him. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 183} Most of them dispersed when Ibn Ziyad besieged him in the palace, and he was left with about a hundred men, then with three. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 183}

Muhammad ibn al-Ash'ath granted him safe conduct after he had fought until he was wounded, and he was brought to Ibn Ziyad. Ibn Ziyad ordered him taken up to the top of the palace, and ordered [[person/bakir-ibn-humran-al-ahmari|Bakir ibn Humran al-Ahmari]], whom Muslim had struck, to strike his neck. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 184-185} Hani was taken out to the market and his neck was struck. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 184}

## After the killing
Ibn Ziyad ordered Muslim's body crucified, and his head was sent to Damascus. The book says that he was the first of the Banu Hashim to be crucified and the first head of theirs to be taken to Damascus. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 185}

## Date
The book says that his appearance was on Tuesday, eight nights into Dhu al-Hijja, and it is said on the day of Arafa, the ninth of it. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p. 185}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"

[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] tells the story of Muslim ibn Aqil in Kufa in episodes 21 and 22 of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]], and draws from it lessons in planning and organisation {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:01:00}.

### Ibn Ziyad and Hani ibn Urwa
He says Yazid replaced the governor of Kufa with a harsh one, the governor of Basra, [[person/ubayd-allah-ibn-ziyad|Ibn Ziyad]], to bring things under control; Ibn Ziyad gathered information through spies, and one of them infiltrated Muslim's followers by a show of loyalty until he learned that Muslim was in the house of [[person/hani-ibn-urwa|Hani ibn Urwa]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:04:00}. Al-Suwaidan holds that the mistake was not that a spy existed but that an unknown man was trusted and let into private gatherings {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:03:30}. Ibn Ziyad detained Hani and threatened him to make him hand Muslim over; Hani refused to give up his guest and neighbour to be killed, so Ibn Ziyad struck him in the face with a cane, breaking his nose and brow, and had him shut in a room {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:05:00}. A rumour spread that Hani had been killed, and his tribe, [[group/madhhij|Madhhij]], surrounded the palace saying they had not renounced obedience but wanted to be sure he was safe; Ibn Ziyad sent the judge [[person/shurayh-al-qadi|Shurayh]], who saw him alive and told them, and they left {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:05:30}. All this happened after al-Husayn had set out, knowing nothing of it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:06:00}.

### The collapse
Al-Suwaidan says the huge numbers Muslim had gathered had no precise organisation and no sound plan {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:07:00}. When Muslim heard that Hani had been beaten he acted in haste and called his followers with their secret watchword, "Ya Mansur amit," and four thousand men gathered to him, although the original plan had been to wait for al-Husayn {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:08:00}. He led them toward the palace; Ibn Ziyad shut himself in with an organised force, having earlier gathered the notables of Kufa and kept them with him as hostages {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:09:00}. He had them appear on the palace and each call out to his tribe, and they discouraged the people, frightening them with the nearness of the Syrian army, the loss of their stipends, being sent to the frontiers and severe punishment {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:10:00}. He says women, fathers and elders began pulling their sons and brothers away from Muslim {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:10:30}, and the numbers shrank until by evening only sixty men of the four thousand were left with him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:11:30}. Al-Suwaidan holds that he should have withdrawn and regrouped, but he chose to engage {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:11:30}. Al-Qa'qa' ibn Shawr, commander of Ibn Ziyad's forces, saw that they were fighting for their lives and ordered a way opened for them; they fled toward the mosque, and by nightfall Muslim was alone in the streets of Kufa {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:12:30}.

### Capture
He relates that Muslim came to a house and a woman came out; he asked her for water and she gave it, then he stayed at her door at a loss until she told him that sitting there would arouse suspicion, so he had to tell her who he was and ask for shelter, and she took him in {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:15:00}. Her son worked for [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-ashath-al-kindi|Muhammad ibn al-Ash'ath]], one of the Umayyad commanders in Kufa, and told him where Muslim was; Ibn Ziyad sent the police, Muslim came out sword in hand refusing to surrender and fought, until Ibn al-Ash'ath granted him safe-conduct and he gave himself up {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:15:30}. On the way he wept, and when told that one who seeks what he sought does not weep, he said he wept not for himself but for his family coming to Kufa, for al-Husayn and his household {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:16:30}. He asked Ibn al-Ash'ath to send a man in his name to tell al-Husayn to turn back, and that the people of Kufa had lied to him and to Muslim {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:18:00}. Al-Suwaidan says Ibn al-Ash'ath did so, choosing a man, giving him a letter in Muslim's hand and a mount, and undertaking to provide for his family {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:03:00}.

### The killing
He relates that Ibn al-Ash'ath brought Muslim before Ibn Ziyad and told him of the safe-conduct; Ibn Ziyad said he had not been sent to grant safe-conduct, and broke it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:03:30}. He then had Muslim taken up to the top of the palace, proclaiming God's greatness and oneness, glorifying Him, asking forgiveness and praying that God judge between him and a people who had deceived and abandoned him; a man al-Suwaidan names Bukayr struck his neck, and the head was thrown from the palace, then the body {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:04:00}. Then Hani ibn Urwa was taken out to the market and called on his tribe Madhhij for help, but no one came to his aid and he was killed, and Ibn Ziyad had Hani and Muslim crucified in the market before the people {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:06:00}. Al-Suwaidan sees in this proof of Ibn Ziyad's harshness and of a state that cared for nothing but holding on to its power {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:06:00}.

### The lesson al-Suwaidan draws
He holds that Muslim did not weigh matters against what had happened to Ali ibn Abi Talib before, and was misled by the number of those who pledged, their tears and their promises; these were mass emotions, not organisation, and revolutions do not succeed on emotion but on planning, good division of tasks and careful work {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:07:00}. He counts among the mistakes that Muslim hurried to summon al-Husayn and changed the plan in a moment, and holds that changing a regime needs sound leadership, tight organisation, long-range planning, certainty about people and numbers, and material and moral preparation {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:07:30}.
