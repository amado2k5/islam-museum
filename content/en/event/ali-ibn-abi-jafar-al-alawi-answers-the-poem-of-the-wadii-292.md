## Overview
"He answered him in that Ali ibn Abi Jafar al-Alawi" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.288}: so the book introduces the reply of [[person/ali-ibn-abi-jafar-al-alawi|Ali ibn Abi Jafar al-Alawi]] to the poem of [[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-malik-ibn-ghitrif-al-wadii|the Wadi'i]], running from p. 288 to the top of p. 290 {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.289} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.290}.

## Detail
He says the Wadi'i described the abode well, naming the dwelling and the tent-rope, and the time when he himself was idle and carefree; now he has bidden farewell to that, put on forbearance after youth and taken up courtesy, and clung to al-Hadi after nearly drowning in a sea of ruin, so that he was saved from the fire {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.288} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.289}. He tells the Wadi'i he has mentioned the Harithis and the discord they made, and wonders at what they did, explaining that few people hold firm when tested, that they were deceived, and that a corrupter came to them with falsehood in word and speech {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.289}. He holds that God's wrath has fallen on them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.289}. He then turns to Ka'b of Hamdan: they and he share religion, merit and descent, they have a peak among the House of Abd al-Muttalib and a share in all good and ill, and soon his cause will show, so that they will hold the earth, the lands of the non-Arabs and the Arabs, and requite people for their deeds, the wrongdoer perishing and affection lasting {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.289}. At the top of p. 290 Ka'b are lords in war, people of faith and sense, a lofty head in the right with their opponents the tail {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.290}. The poet's descriptions of Banu al-Harith and of people at large are his own in a Zaydi book, are not repeated by the museum, and are given by their general sense only. The note explains "buzzi wa-shahab" as from stripped goods and a change of colour, and gives the base text's reading of a line whose metre the editor mends {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.290}. The poet's identity is not settled in the text; he is likely [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]], son of Abu Ja'far, a reading the museum treats as probable and does not assert.
