## Overview

The Abyssinians' spear-play in the Prophet's Mosque on the day of Bu'ath — a festival day — is a famous scene: Abyssinians displaying their spears in the mosque, the Prophet watching and Aisha watching over his shoulder until she herself tired {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the hadith

In the report of Aisha: on the day of Bu'ath — the day the Aws and Khazraj had fought in the jahiliyya — the Abyssinians came playing with their spears in the mosque; either the Prophet called me or I asked him, and I put my chin on his shoulder watching their play until it was I who tired of watching {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. In another narration Umar said: let me pelt them with stones, Messenger of God — and he said: leave them, they are safe {cite:sunan-abi-dawud}.

## Its meaning

The scholars took from it that weapon-display is permitted play even in the mosque where no harm is involved, that the mosque may carry social purposes on the festival, and the Prophet's tenderness in letting her watch {cite:mawsuat-alfiqhia-alkuwaitiya}. It is among the most famous reports on sport and joy in Islam.
