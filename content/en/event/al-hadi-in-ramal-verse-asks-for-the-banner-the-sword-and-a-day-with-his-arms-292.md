## Overview
A poem in the short ramal metre under the heading "and also of his", begun near the foot of p. 319 and ending on p. 321: al-Hadi says grey hair has marked his pleasures and part of his youth and of lives has gone, that he announces the call openly, bids the raising of the banner, the aiding of the Merciful and the giving of the sword to enemies, and wishes he might ride out one day with his arms among horses, swords, bows and arrows with war led before him by "al-Da'am", the poem ending in blows, thrusts and a stand and the hope that the sickness of his chest be healed; the lines are printed in three columns on the page. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.319}

## Detail
Near the foot of p. 319, under the heading "and also of his" (short ramal), short lines open "grey hair marked my pleasures", saying part of his youth has gone, their lives have gone, and he is not content with delay, then "announce the call openly", and "the One, the Unique, the Exalted" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.319}. On p. 320 the lines are printed in three columns because the half-lines are short, with a middle rhyme word, and he says "raise the banner" toward which the pious and righteous go, "give the enemies the sword to taste", since the misguided has long been deceived, and "aid the Merciful with aid"; the footnote says the original and manuscript S read "with aid" and that it was changed to "aid" to keep the metre {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.319}. He says that whoever comes to the truth willingly is approved and content, and that the enemies of his God have a base affair, then wishes he might go out one day "with my arms among horses", Indian swords raised, bows and weapons of the nab' wood, "war leads before me one of steady loyalty and piety", naming [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] "the generous", a Yemeni lion of war {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.320}. He says the lofty did not beget him if defence and the stroke do not show from him, and the footnote says manuscript S reads "and patience" for "and the stroke"; if they meet on a plain, exchange blows and pass round a cup whose water is death, there will be from him a stroke and a thrust "Hasani" and a stand "Fatimi" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.320}. On p. 321 the lines finish: he asks that this go on until his God, Lord of majesty and eternity, is pleased, that the horses meet, that war turn until it frightens the cowardly aggressor, that the swords and brown spears reach their due, and then that the sickness of his chest be healed {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.321}. The lines are a wish before war rather than a report of one, the book names no battle and gives no time, and the mention of al-Da'am links them to the one al-Hadi addresses in the lines that follow. They are placed after [[event/al-hadi-counsels-banu-hasan-to-rise-and-names-hamdan-khawlan-and-hashid-in-verse-292|his call to Banu Hasan to rise]]. The phrases describing the enemies are the poet's, and no word of abuse is repeated here. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
