## Overview
In his literary anthologies [[person/al-raghib-al-isfahani|al-Raghib al-Isfahani]] alludes repeatedly to contemporaries who can be identified as members of the circle of the Buyid vizier [[person/sahib-ibn-abbad|al-Sahib Ibn Abbad]] (d. 385/995), showing that he lived in the age of that salon which drew the men of letters of Persia and Iraq. There is no sound testimony that al-Raghib himself attended the circle at Rayy or ever left his native [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]]: he stands as a littérateur who received the salon's news and books, not one of its sitters {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 pp. 389-390}.

## The circle in his books
The Muhadarat al-udaba' gives pride of place to the poetry of al-Mutanabbi and al-Sharif al-Radi and to Ibn Abbad's prose and verse alongside the poets and prose-writers of every age — a mirror of the literary store that issued from the vizier's house {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 pp. 389-390}.
