## Summary
Ala al-Din Muhammad Khwarazmshah took the lands of Kirman, Makran and Sind up to the borders of Kabul, through his amir Abu Bakr Taj al-Din, who had begun his career as a camel-driver. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 362}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir admits he does not know the exact year of this event — it was this year or a little before or after — and his informant was a soldier from Mosul who had travelled to those lands and marched with the amir Abu Bakr, conqueror of Kirman. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 362} Abu Bakr, surnamed Taj al-Din, had been one of his father's amirs; he started out hiring out camels for journeys, then attached himself to Khwarazmshah, who found him hardy and trustworthy and promoted him until he became one of his chief amirs and governor of Zuzan. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 362} He proposed that Khwarazmshah give him an army to take neighboring Kirman; with a large force he fought its lord Harb ibn Muhammad ibn Abi'l-Fadl, formerly lord of Sistan in Sultan Sanjar's days, who lacked the strength and weakened, so Abu Bakr took his land quickly, then all Makran to Sind at the Kabul borders, then Hormuz, whose lord Miltak submitted, had the khutba said for Khwarazmshah and paid tribute; the khutba was also said for him at Qalhat and part of Oman, whose rulers obeyed the lord of Hormuz, a great harbor where merchants from India, China and Yemen gathered. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 362}
