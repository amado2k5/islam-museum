## Summary
[[person/sad-ibn-muadh|Sad ibn Muadh]], chief of the Aws, was struck in the arm vein on the day of the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Trench]], was made judge over [[event/siege-of-banu-qurayza|Banu Qurayza]], and then his wound burst and he died in year 5 AH, about a month after the Trench. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 278} This record gathers what the books relate of his prayer, his judgement, his death, his funeral, what the Prophet is reported to have said of it, and his grave in al-Baqi.

## His wound and his prayer
Ibn Hisham relates from Aisha that Sad went out on the day of the Trench in a short coat of mail and his mother said to him: hurry, my son, you are late; he was hit by an arrow that cut his arm vein. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 459} He names the archer as Hibban ibn Qays ibn al-Ariqa of Banu Amir ibn Luayy, as Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada reported, while Ibn Ishaq cites Abdallah ibn Kab ibn Malik that it was Abu Usama al-Jushami who hit him. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 459} In his prayer he asked that if God had left anything of the war with Quraysh he keep him for it, since there was no people he would rather fight than those who had harmed God's messenger, called him a liar and driven him out; and if He had ended the war between them, that He make this his martyrdom, and not let him die until He had cooled his eyes over Banu Qurayza. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 459} In the hadith Aisha relates that the Prophet pitched a tent in the mosque so as to visit him from near. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4122} Ibn Abd al-Barr says the Prophet visited him every day until he died. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 278}

## His judgement on Banu Qurayza
Al-Dhahabi relates from Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr ibn Sad ibn Muadh that Banu Qurayza surrendered to the judgement of the Messenger of God, who sent for Sad; he was brought on a donkey, worn out by his wound, and the Prophet asked: advise me about these men. He said: were I in charge of their affair I would kill their fighters, take their offspring captive and divide their property, and the Prophet said he had advised what God had commanded him. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 220} Ibn Abd al-Barr relays from Jabir that when Sad saw his wound bleeding he said: O God, do not take my soul until You cool my eyes over Banu Qurayza; his vein held until they came down to his judgement, and he judged that their men be killed and their women and children taken captive; the Prophet said: you have hit upon God's judgement concerning them; when it was done his vein burst and he died. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 278} Al-Dhahabi relays from Amir ibn Sad, from his father, that the Prophet said Sad had judged by the judgement of God that He judged from above seven heavens. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 220} The affair has a wider historical and legal discussion that does not belong in this record.

## His death
Al-Dhahabi relates from Amr ibn Shurahbil that when Sad's wound burst the Prophet held him close and the blood ran over him; Abu Bakr came and said: my back is broken; then Umar came and said the formula of return. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221} From Abdallah ibn Shaddad he relates that the Prophet came in to him while he was struggling with his soul and said: God reward you, chief of a people; you have fulfilled to God what you promised, and He will fulfil what He promised you. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221} Aisha relates that she heard Abu Bakr's weeping and Umar's from her room, and that the Prophet's eyes did not shed tears for anyone but when he grieved he took hold of his beard. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 220} In al-Bukhari, as this museum's passage record gives it, the Prophet said the Throne of the Merciful shook at the death of Sad ibn Muadh, and Jabir, asked about al-Bara's report that the bier shook, recalled the old grudges between the two tribes. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3803}

## The funeral
Al-Dhahabi relates from Muadh ibn Rifaa that Gabriel came to the Prophet in the middle of the night wrapped in a turban of brocade and said: who is this dead man for whom the gates of heaven were opened and the Throne shook? The Prophet got up trailing his garment and hastened to Sad and found him already taken. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 220} From Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada he relates that people walked out on foot until their sandal straps broke, one said: you have wearied people with walking, and he answered: I fear the angels will reach him before us as they reached Hanzala before us. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221} Al-Dhahabi says his mother wept for him as he was washed, and the Prophet said: every wailing woman lies except Umm Sad. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221} When they carried him they said: we have never carried a lighter dead man; the Prophet said that with him were angels who had never come down before. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221} In Anas's report in Ibn Abd al-Barr the hypocrites said how light his bier was, he being tall and heavy, and the Prophet said the angels carried him; in Sad ibn Abi Waqqas's report seventy thousand angels came down at his funeral who had not trodden the earth before. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 278}

## The burial
Al-Waqidi relates from Abu Said al-Khudri that he was with the Prophet when news of Sad's death reached him; he went out with the people and when he came out to al-Baqi said: take up the preparation of your companion; Abu Said was one of those who dug the grave, and musk rose from the earth as they dug. {cite:al-waqidi-maghazi scan vol. 1 p. 528} He relates they dug it near the house of Aqil, that the Prophet set him down by his grave and prayed over him, and that the people filled al-Baqi. {cite:al-waqidi-maghazi scan vol. 1 p. 528} Four went down into the grave: al-Harith ibn Aws ibn Muadh, Usayd ibn Hudayr, Abu Naila and Salama ibn Salama, with the Prophet standing at the grave. {cite:al-waqidi-maghazi scan vol. 1 p. 529} Ibn Shurahbil relates that someone took a handful of earth from his grave and later found it to be musk, and al-Dhahabi gives the same report. {cite:al-waqidi-maghazi scan vol. 1 p. 528} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221}

## The squeeze of the grave
Al-Waqidi relates that the Prophet's face changed when Sad was laid in the niche; he glorified God three times and said the takbir three times, and when asked said: the grave narrowed on your companion and he was squeezed a squeeze from which, if anyone escaped, Sad would have, then God eased it for him. {cite:al-waqidi-maghazi scan vol. 1 p. 529} Al-Dhahabi gives a report from Sad's family that the Prophet, asked about it, said he had been careless over some of the purification from urine, then comments that this pressure is no part of the punishment of the grave but one of the frights the believer undergoes. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 220} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221}

## Grave and resting place
Al-Waqidi relates that his mother Kabsha bint Ubayd came to see him in the niche; the people turned her back and the Prophet said leave her; she looked and said: I count you with God, the Prophet consoled her at the grave, then the grave was levelled and sprinkled with water, and the Prophet stood by it, prayed for him and went away. {cite:al-waqidi-maghazi scan vol. 1 p. 529} Al-Dhahabi relates from Muadh ibn Rifaa that he was buried by the foundation of the house of Aqil ibn Abi Talib. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 221} In the pages of al-Waqidi and al-Dhahabi I found no description of a structure on the grave.

## Where the reports differ
They differ on who shot Sad, on whether the report speaks of the Throne or the bier shaking, and on the reason for the squeeze. They agree that his death came shortly after Qurayza and that he was buried in al-Baqi. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 459} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3803} {cite:al-waqidi-maghazi scan vol. 1 p. 528}

## Sources and documentation
This record rests on Ibn Hisham's Sira, al-Bukhari as given in the museum's passage records, al-Dhahabi's Siyar Alam al-Nubala, Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Isti'ab and al-Waqidi's Maghazi. The page numbers given are scan pages.
