## Summary
At [[event/battle-of-the-trench|the Battle of the Trench]], the polytheists' cavalry broke through a gap in the trench, so [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] went out with a group of Muslims until they sealed the gap against them; when the horsemen halted, Amr ibn Abd Wudd called for single combat, and Ali dueled and killed him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.98}.

## The Duel
Ali gave Amr a respite after calling him to Islam, which he refused, and challenged him to combat; Amr said to him, "Why, O son of my brother? By God, I do not wish to kill you." Ali replied, "But by God, I wish to kill you." Amr grew furious, dismounted from his horse and hamstrung it, then advanced on Ali, and they traded blows until Ali killed him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.98}.

## Outcome
The polytheists' cavalry fled in defeat until they scrambled back across the trench in flight {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.98}. The book notes that after Amr's killing, the polytheists sent to the Prophet asking for his corpse in exchange for a price, and the Prophet answered as is well known in the biography, though the book does not give its details at this point.

## What the Book Draws from It
The book considers the episode among Ali's stands of steadfastness, heroism, and courage, and cites the verses of Amr before the duel as narrated by al-Bayhaqi and mentioned by Ibn Kathir {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.98}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not mention a specific day for the duel; the recorded date is derived from the main battle.
- Amr's verses are attributed to al-Bayhaqi's report via Ibn Kathir, and the book passes no judgment on its chain of transmission.

## Details of the Duel (al-Sallabi)
The book states, from Ibn Ishaq, that Amr ibn Abd Wudd fought at Badr until he was incapacitated by wounds, so he did not witness Uhud; then he went out on the Day of the Trench to make his mark, and when he halted with his cavalry he called for single combat {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.98}. [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] stepped out to face him, called him to Islam, which he refused, and then challenged him to combat; Amr said, "Why, O son of my brother? By God, I do not wish to kill you," and Ali said, "But by God, I wish to kill you" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.98}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The text transmits Ibn Ishaq's report via Ibn Hisham (3/248) as cited by the book, and it has not been re-verified.
