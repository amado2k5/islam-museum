## Summary
In Rabi' al-Awwal of 624 AH the Georgians entered Tiflis, left empty of Islamic troops, killed its remaining inhabitants and burned the whole city. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 472}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that the Georgians reached Tiflis in Rabi' al-Awwal with no Islamic army there to protect it; the cause was that when Jalal al-Din returned from Akhlat he dispersed his troops to warm well-pastured places for winter quarters, and his troops had treated the people of Tiflis — Muslims — badly and oppressed them, so they wrote to the Georgians inviting them to come and take the town; the Georgians exploited the inhabitants' inclination toward them and the absence of troops, assembled at Qars, Ani and other fortresses, and marched to Tiflis which stood empty. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 472} Jalal al-Din had thought the Georgians too weakened to move after so many of them had been killed; once they held the town they put the sword to whoever remained, and knowing they could not keep it against Jalal al-Din they burned it all; when the news reached Jalal al-Din he marched with the troops at hand to catch them but saw none of them — they had left Tiflis as soon as they had burned it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 472}
