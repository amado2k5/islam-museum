## Overview
"He saw a man of the Hakami's camp striking with his sword at some of the Tabaris, a party of whom had been killed, lying about in a ring, heavily wounded" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.297}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi said, as the narrator gives it, that there was surely a man to kill this man; "a man of the prostrate Tabaris rose, and when he stood upright he took the spear and thrust him with it", and "each of the two fell dead" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.297}. The book names neither man, and al-Hadi's words as reported include a term of abuse for the man that the museum does not repeat. The Tabaris here look to be the party of [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|Tabari emigrants to al-Hadi]], though the text does not say so. The account follows [[event/al-hadi-stands-with-seven-horsemen-and-the-eight-names-292|his standing with the horsemen]] and precedes [[event/the-hakamis-ambush-al-hadis-charge-and-the-pursuit-to-his-village-292|the coming out of the ambush]]. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
