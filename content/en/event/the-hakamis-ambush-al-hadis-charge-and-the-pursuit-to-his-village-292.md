## Overview
"An ambush of the Hakami's came out on al-Hadi, who had with him only these men; his companions had been routed and had left him, the enemy stood between him and them, and al-Hadi was in the middle" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.297}.

## Detail
When the ambush came out al-Hadi said to those with him, as the narrator gives it, to charge the people; "he charged and thrust a man and threw him; the people were routed, and al-Hadi followed them". Then "a crier cried that al-Hadi's companions had turned back, and those who were chasing his companions fled, and al-Hadi and his friends made the rout certain, and the chase did not stop until they drew near the Hakami's village; nearly two hundred of his companions had joined al-Hadi, and they wanted to catch the Hakami before his village" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.297}. The page ends with the editor's note that the rest of the page is blank in the original, with nothing copied on it, and the blank runs on for about six lines of the next page, the same omission being in manuscript S {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.297}. So this book does not tell how the battle ended, what the Hakami did or the toll of the dead. The next page, p. 298, goes on with a poem [[event/al-hadi-writes-verses-to-his-imprisoned-son-abu-al-qasim-and-praises-him-290|of al-Hadi to his son Abu al-Qasim]], with no visible link to the fighting. The account is in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its descriptions and its judgments on opponents are the narrator's or the poet's, and the museum adopts none of them and gives no verdict.
