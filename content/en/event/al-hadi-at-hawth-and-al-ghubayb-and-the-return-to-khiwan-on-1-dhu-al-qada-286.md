## Overview
As [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] drew near [[place/athafit|Athafit]], an army of Abu al-Atahiya met him, after the news of his war with al-Qusayb and of [[person/ibn-al-dahhak-rebel-at-khiwan-286|Ibn al-Dahhak]]'s attack on [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] and the rout of the commander with him, and went with him until he camped at a place called [[place/hawth-yemen|Hawth]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144} The printed text writes the name as "Abu al-Mutahiya"; the museum's record for Abdullah ibn Bishr uses "Abu al-Atahiya" and the identity is not asserted. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144}

## Hawth
He ordered the house of a man who had backed Ibn al-Dahhak and stood with him rousing people to disorder pulled down, which was done; he meant to cut the man's vines but some people spoke to him of pardoning that and he pardoned, and he stayed there the day. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144}

## Al-Ghubayb
Next morning he went to a place called [[place/al-ghubayb-yemen|al-Ghubayb]] in pursuit of Ibn al-Dahhak and the companions who had fought Abu al-Qasim on the night at Khiwan. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144} When the people learned he was seeking them they left their place; he found it empty and ordered their vines cut and their houses pulled down. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144} Fearing ruin, they sent elders from al-Hadi's supporters to ask for pardon and acceptance; he agreed and gave them safe-conduct. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145}

## Ibn al-Dahhak, the oath and the return
The elders went back and all the people came except Ibn al-Dahhak, who was afraid; the narrator describes him as a weak boy with no sense, living in the land of [[group/banu-mamar-hamdan|Banu Ma'mar]] with a small party of Khiwan's slaves. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145} When the people had come to al-Hadi and sworn to hear and obey, he spent the night at al-Ghubayb, called some men who had not met him and had them swear, and next day went back to [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]], on the first of Dhu al-Qa'da 286 AH. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145} The description of Ibn al-Dahhak is the narrator's opinion alone. See also [[event/al-hadi-takes-the-fortress-of-the-muhdithin-and-razes-their-houses-286]].

## Notes
The book's index lists "al-Ghubayb" at p. 145 only, so it is not asserted here that it is the [[place/al-ghubayyib-yemen|al-Ghubayyib]] of p. 136. The editor says a marginal note of the manuscript refers to what was not written on the blank folio. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.144}
