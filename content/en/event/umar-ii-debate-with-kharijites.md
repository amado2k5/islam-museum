## Overview
In the caliphate of [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]], Shawdhab the Kharijite rose in al-Jazira with a group of the Muhakkima. Umar sent two men to them with a letter, and a debate took place in his session at Khunasira. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.267-268}

## The delegation
The Kharijites sent two men with them, one of the Banu Shayban, and the book describes the other as having an Abyssinian in him. Umar searched them before the session, lest they had iron with them. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.268}

## Umar's argument
According to the book, he reminded them that Abu Bakr and Umar, whom the Kharijites follow, had approved the fighting of the apostates and the taking of their children captive; and that the people of Nahrawan, Kufa and Basra had fought one another. Then he asked them whether it is lawful to curse the people of sin, and whether they could bear to curse the people of his house and disavow them. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.268-270}

## The outcome
The Abyssinian said, according to the book, that he testified that Umar was upon the truth. The Shaybani said that he would not act against the Muslims by an affair without putting his word before them. The Abyssinian remained, and Umar gave him his stipend, and he died fifteen days later. The Shaybani rejoined his companions and was killed with them after Umar's death. {cite:bakr-umayyad-state-1134715 p.270}

The account is the book's own and has not been compared with another source. The book does not give the year of the debate, which falls within 99–101 AH.
