## Overview
Al-Baladhuri says that [[place/nuba|al-Nuba]] was a place of fierce fighting with the Muslims, and then [[person/abdullah-ibn-sad-ibn-abi-sarh|Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh]] made peace with them by a truce, not by poll tax. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The first incursion
When the Muslims conquered Egypt, Amr ibn al-As sent horsemen to the villages around it, and sent [[person/uqba-ibn-nafi|Uqba ibn Nafi]]. The Muslims met fierce fighting at al-Nuba; the Nubians shot them with arrows until most of them were wounded, and many eyes were put out. They were called "the blinded of the eyes". {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The peace
The Nubians asked Abd Allah ibn Sa'd for peace and a truce. He agreed to a truce of three hundred heads each year, and on condition that the Muslims give them food in equal measure. The matter is connected with the concept of [[concept/baqt|the baqt]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Account of a shaykh of Himyar
Al-Baladhuri relates from a shaykh of Himyar who fought at al-Nuba twice in the caliphate of Umar that he had not seen a people more fierce in war than them. He says that they shot until a hundred and fifty eyes were counted put out. Umar did not make peace with them; Abd Allah ibn Sa'd made peace with them after him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

At al-Nuba the eye of [[person/muawiya-ibn-khudayj|Mu'awiya ibn Khudayj]] was lost. The report also mentions [[group/nuba|the Nuba]]. The date is derived from Ibn Abi Sarh's governorship of Egypt.
