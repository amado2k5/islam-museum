## Overview
When Ali ibn Muhammad saw Hamdan's desertion, he sent his brother al-Qasim to al-Hadi to report it and ask for speedy reinforcements, and wrote to his father asking to leave the village. His father replied that he should hold his position until reinforcements arrived. In a second letter the father wrote that they had risen only in obedience to God, and told his son not to write again asking leave.

## Detail
When Ali ibn Muhammad saw that Hamdan had deserted him, he sent his brother al-Qasim ibn Muhammad to al-Hadi to tell him of Hamdan's desertion and ask for speedy reinforcements, and wrote to his father that Hamdan had shown its true face and asking to leave the village. His father replied: my son, I have understood your letter and what you say of Hamdan's desertion; God is the best helper, so do not worry about our affairs, for we hope for victory from God and for holding our position until supplies reach us. He wrote again: my son, your letter has come, and I understand that Hamdan has deserted you and that no one is with you but the people of the fort; may God reward them. I understand what you say of the entry of Banu Abd al-Madan and Banu Rabi'a with Banu al-Harith. Know, my son, that we rose in what we did only in obedience to God, desiring His reward and fearing His punishment; we do not wish to be steadfast in ease and to falter in trial, nor to be among those whom God censures in His book. The letter refers here to two verses of the Qur'an, in the surahs of al-Shu'ara' and al-Hajj, which are not reproduced in this record. Be warned, my son, may God keep you, not to write to me again asking for leave, for I will not answer such a letter.
