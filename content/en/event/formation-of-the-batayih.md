## Overview
Al-Baladhuri describes how the [[place/bataih|marshes]] (al-Bata'ih) between the Tigris and the Euphrates spread after breaches burst that were never closed. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The time of Qubad and Anushirvan
The Persians used to speak of the end of their kingdom, and reported earthquakes and flooding as its signs. In the time of [[person/qubad-ibn-firuz|Qubad ibn Firuz]] a great breach burst at the lower end of Kaskar, and it was neglected until many cultivated lands were drowned. Al-Baladhuri describes it as weak and little attended to. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

When his son [[person/khosrow-i-anushirvan|Anushirvan]] came to power, he ordered the water blocked with dams, and some of the lands returned to cultivation. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The time of Parviz
In the year that the Prophet sent Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi to Khosrow Parviz, which is 7 AH or, it is said, 6, the Euphrates and the Tigris rose by a great amount such as had not been seen. Great breaches burst. [[person/khosrow-ii|Parviz]] strove to close them, but the water overcame him, and it turned toward the marsh region and drowned several districts. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Al-Baladhuri says that Khosrow rode out himself to close the breaches, scattered money, and killed some of the officials and crucified some of them, and the water found no remedy. The report says, "it is said" that he crucified forty dam-builders in one day. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## After the conquest
Then the Arabs entered the land of Iraq, and the Persians were occupied with wars, so the breaches burst without anyone attending to them, and the local landowners could not close the great ones, and the marsh spread. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
