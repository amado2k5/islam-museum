## Summary
Abu Bakr gave charges to the commanders of the armies he sent out, walking beside them as he saw them off. Malik reports from Yahya ibn Sa'id that he went out on foot with [[person/yazid-ibn-abi-sufyan|Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan]], commander of one quarter of the armies for Syria, and gave him ten charges, among them not to kill a woman, a boy or a very old man and not to cut fruit-bearing trees. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p. 577} Ibn al-Athir reports that he also saw off the army of [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama ibn Zayd]] on foot while Usama rode, and then charged the army against treachery, mutilation and killing the weak. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 195} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 196}

## Seeing off Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan
Ibn Kathir says Abu Bakr set about appointing commanders and tying banners; he tied the banner of Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan, who had most of the people with him, and gave him Damascus; he went out with him on foot, advising him about the conduct of the war he had in hand with the Muslims. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 8} He adds that al-Mada'ini says Abu Bakr sent these armies at the start of the year 13. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 8} From Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from Salih ibn Kaysan, Abu Bakr went out on foot with Yazid riding, and advised him; when he finished he said, "I convey my greeting to you and entrust you to God," then turned back and Yazid went on his way. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 8}

## The report in the Muwatta
Malik relates from Yahya ibn Sa'id that Abu Bakr al-Siddiq sent armies to Syria and went out walking with Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan, who was commander of one quarter of those forces. They claimed that Yazid said, "Either you ride or I get down," and Abu Bakr answered, "You will not get down and I will not ride; I count these steps of mine as being in the way of God." {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p. 577}

He then charged him with ten things; the report names among them: do not kill a woman, a boy or a very old man; do not cut fruit-bearing trees; do not ruin what is inhabited; do not slaughter sheep or camels except to eat; do not burn bees; do not steal from the spoils; do not be cowardly. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p. 577} The passage has its own record: [[passage/muwatta-7-1292|Muwatta 1292]].

## Seeing off Usama ibn Zayd
Ibn al-Athir relates that when the army went out to its camp at al-Jurf, Usama sent Umar ibn al-Khattab to Abu Bakr to ask permission to turn back with the people, because with him were the notables of the people and he did not feel safe for the Messenger's successor, the Messenger's family and the Muslims; and some of the Ansar with Usama asked that someone older than Usama be put over them. Abu Bakr said that if dogs and wolves snatched him he would still carry out the expedition as the Messenger of God had ordered, and he would not revoke what the Messenger had decided. When Umar told him the Ansar wanted a man of greater age than Usama, Abu Bakr jumped up, took Umar by the beard and said, "May your mother be bereaved of you, son of al-Khattab; the Messenger of God appointed him, and you tell me to remove him." {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 195} For the expedition itself see [[event/expedition-of-usama|the expedition of Usama ibn Zayd]].

Abu Bakr then went out to them, saw them off and walked while Usama rode. Usama said, "Caliph of the Messenger, either you ride or I will get down." Abu Bakr said, "By God, you will not get down and I will not ride; what harm is it to me to dust my feet for an hour in the way of God?" He added that for every step a fighter takes seven hundred good deeds are written for him, seven hundred degrees raised and seven hundred misdeeds erased. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 195}

When he was about to go back he said to Usama, "If you see fit to leave Umar with me, do so," and Usama allowed it. Then he charged the army: no treachery, no breach of faith, no stealing from the spoils, no mutilation; no killing a child, a very old man or a woman; no cutting down or burning palms, no felling fruit trees; no slaughtering sheep, cattle or camels except for food. He said they would pass people who had given themselves over to monasteries and they should leave them to what they had given themselves to, and they would come upon people who had shaved the middles of their heads leaving a band around them, whom they should strike with the sword; and he said, "Set out in the name of God." {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 196}

He charged Usama to do what the Messenger of God had commanded. Usama went, fell upon the tribes of Quda'a that had apostatised, took spoils and returned, having been away forty days, or seventy by another account. Ibn al-Athir says that sending the army was one of the most beneficial things done for the Muslims, because the Arabs said that if the Muslims had had no strength they would not have sent this army, and so held back from much they had wanted to do. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 196}

## Where the reports differ
Both reports have Abu Bakr walking beside the mounted commander; the Muwatta gives his words as counting his steps in God's way, while Ibn al-Athir adds the reward for each step. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p. 577} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 195} The charges differ: the Muwatta has ten, forbidding killing the weak, cutting trees, ruining what is inhabited, slaughtering animals, burning bees, stealing from the spoils and cowardice, while Ibn al-Athir adds treachery and mutilation and the instruction to pass by monastery-dwellers. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p. 577} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 196}
