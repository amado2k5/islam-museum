## Overview
After the tribes had sought amnesty through Ibn Bistam one by one, Yam and Ahlaf agreed to kill him. The book says that on Sunday, 27 Dhu al-Hijja, he came into the village of Najran; men of Yam and Ahlaf caught him between his lodging and the house of Abu Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah and struck him with swords until he fell in the courtyard. Al-Hadi came out and stood over him. The book says Ibn Bistam was cursing those who had killed him or ordered his death.

## Detail
The book says that when the tribes had sought amnesty through Ibn Bistam one by one, Yam and Ahlaf took this badly, agreed to kill him, watched for him and set spies over him. On Sunday, 27 Dhu al-Hijja, Ibn Bistam came into the village with men of Banu Rabi'a; he sent them on to Maynas and went into the village. The men of Yam and Ahlaf went to Maynas to meet him on the road, and when they did not find him they understood that he was in the village. They entered it and found Ibn Bistam at the lodging of Abu Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah, and the gate of al-Hadi stood empty of men, because the army had gone out to cut the palms of some of Ibn Humayd's men. Ibn Bistam came out from Abdallah ibn al-Husayn's lodging on his way to his own, and they met him between his house and Abu Muhammad's. They struck him with their swords as he tried to reach Abu Muhammad's house, and he fell in the courtyard. The men took up their weapons and rushed out toward al-Hadi, while Yam and Ahlaf mounted and fled. Al-Hadi came out and stood over Ibn Bistam, who, the book says, was cursing those who had killed him or ordered his death. Abdallah ibn al-Husayn is quoted as saying that Ibn Bistam's slave Jarir struck the Ahlaf from behind, then rode his master's horse out toward Maynas. The book gives this as the account of the affair as the narrators reported it.
