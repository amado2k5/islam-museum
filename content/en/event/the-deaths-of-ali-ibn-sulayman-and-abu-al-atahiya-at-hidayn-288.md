## Overview
The original has the heading: the account of the killing of Ali ibn Sulayman and the killing of Abu al-Atahiya {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.232}. [[person/ali-ibn-sulayman-ibn-al-qasim|Ali ibn Sulayman]] went forward after the routed until he was in the midst of their horse, where they struck him with spear and sword and he fell from his mount among them; [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] charged with those with him, rescued him from among them, set him on his own mount and took him to [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]], where he died {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.232} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.233}. The men then stood their ground, and Abu al-Atahiya was hit by an arrow that killed him; the men turned on the horse that faced them on the left, caught a man of the line of al-Husayn ibn Ali and killed him, and stood at the foot of [[place/hidayn-sanaa-yemen|Hidayn]] until night fell, when each went back to his camp {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.233}. [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Al-Hadi]] stayed some days in Sana'a, then a man of Madhhij came to him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.233}.

## Detail
The narrator adds a formula of mercy after each of the two names, which the museum does not write. The man of the line of al-Husayn ibn Ali is not named in the text. Ali ibn Sulayman is in the book al-Hadi's cousin who had been driven out of Sana'a earlier (see [[event/the-rising-of-ibn-mahfuz-at-sanaa-and-ali-ibn-sulayman-leaves-for-madar-288]]), and Abu al-Atahiya is the holder of Sana'a who swore allegiance to al-Hadi ([[event/al-hadi-and-abu-al-atahiya-meet-between-the-armies-and-pledge-288]]). The text gives the age of neither and does not tell of a burial.
