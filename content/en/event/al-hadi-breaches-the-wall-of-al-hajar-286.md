## Overview
When [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s letters reached [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] and al-Mahra and Khawlan they mustered and gathered and a large army came to him from them, since they had first gone out with him and then fallen behind; the narrator calls them "turns for them", meaning they took turns. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.164} Al-Hadi had also drawn to himself men of Najran from [[group/banu-hamdan|Hamdan]], horse and foot, telling them he was keeping them for a purpose of his, a place where he thought to put them forward, or a charge, or entering the fort. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.164}

## The march on al-Hajar
When his armies came he marched with those he had to the village of [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] called [[place/al-hajar-najran|al-Hajar]], where they had gathered from every side except the camp at Minas; they went toward it when they saw al-Hadi go to it, and he drew near its pass and fighting joined at the pass gate, hard on both sides. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.164}

## The breach in the wall
Al-Hadi then moved to one side of the fort, next to his lodging in al-Hajar, and had the wall struck with bows and picks until it was pierced and an opening wide enough for a horseman was made; fighting came to the breach, and arrows and fighting grew thick, while Banu al-Harith shot arrows and threw stones from the top of the fort. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.164} Then he went into the village, called to the people and told them to go in with him, and ordered the picked band he had chosen, horse and foot, to enter with him; he had chosen three hundred foot and thirty horse for this and they were with him when he pulled down the wall. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.164} The page breaks off here and the account runs on into p. 165, which this batch has not yet read. It follows [[event/night-of-adha-at-saada-ibn-abbads-plan-and-the-prisoners-freed-286]] and the fighting in [[event/first-fighting-at-the-fortress-of-najran-and-the-three-camps-286]]. The account is Zaydi, told in the imam's followers' voice.
