## Summary
al-Sallabi quotes Ali as saying that an apostate is asked to repent three times, and if he refuses, he is executed {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 302}. He notes that Ali executed apostates in different ways depending on each case: by beheading, by beating to death, or by burning after execution {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 303}.

## Calling to Repentance
The book cites Ali's statement: "An apostate is asked to repent three times; if he returns, fine, and otherwise he is executed" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 302}. His evidence for execution is the hadith, "Whoever changes his religion, kill him," and his evidence for calling to repentance is a report from Jabir ibn Abd Allah that the Prophet asked a man who had apostatized to repent {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 302}.

## The Heretic and the Woman
The book cites two opinions from Ali regarding the heretic (zindiq) who outwardly professes Islam while concealing unbelief: that there is no difference between him and an apostate once proof is established against him, and that the heretic is not asked to repent, unlike one who openly declares apostasy {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 302}. Regarding the female apostate, it cites two opinions: that she is executed like a man, and that al-Hasan and Qatadah said she is enslaved rather than executed {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 302}.

## Methods of Execution Attributed to Ali
- Beheading with a sword {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 303}.
- Beating a Christian man who had accepted Islam and then reverted to Christianity until he died {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 303}.
- Burning al-Mustawrid al-'Ijli after executing him; the book mentions that Ali may have burned him out of fear that his people would exhume his corpse after he refused to sell it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 303}.
- Execution by burning in the story of the Saba'iyyah, which was already detailed earlier in the book {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 303}.

## Author's Commentary
The book holds that executing an apostate is "a protection for the people of the religion and the religion itself," quoting Ibn Taymiyyah to that effect {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 303}.

## Notes for Researchers
- Details of the methods of execution are drawn from multiple sources in the book, and the book does not weigh between the reports regarding burning.
- al-Mustawrid al-'Ijli is mentioned in a separate section of the book and is treated here as an apostate who was burned after execution according to what the book reports.
