## Overview
After the Eid al-Adha, Ibn Humayd sent al-Kami ibn Abi Dhira' al-Awtari from al-Nakhl, three days from Najran, to consult Banu al-Harith about the disturbance. They gave him the answer he asked for, and he found them keener than Ibn Humayd to pursue it. Ibn Humayd then withdrew to Najran and lodged at Sawhan with twenty horsemen and fifty foot soldiers.

## Detail
The book says that Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah lodged in the village of Najran with twenty-three horsemen and fifty-five foot soldiers from the army, and stayed there for days until the people kept the Eid al-Adha. Then a man named al-Kami ibn Abi Dhira' al-Awtari came to Banu al-Harith from Ibn Humayd, who was at the place called al-Nakhl, three days from Najran, to consult them about the disturbance and learn what they had. He told them why he had come, and they agreed to what he asked. He found them more intent on pursuing the disturbance than Ibn Humayd himself, and went back to report what he had seen. Ibn Humayd at once withdrew to Najran and lodged at a place called Sawhan with twenty horsemen and fifty foot soldiers. The editor's note gives another reading of al-Kami's name; the record follows the printed text.
