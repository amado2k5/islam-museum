## Summary
The Dubai International Holy Quran Award is an international award and competition in [[place/dubai|Dubai]], launched in 1418 AH (1997) by order of [[person/mohammed-bin-rashid-al-maktoum|Muhammad bin Rashid Al Maktoum]], devoted to serving the Qur'an and its sciences and honouring those who memorise it. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar} Its Wikidata item and the infobox of its article give 30 December 1998; the museum records both dates. {cite:wikidata-dubai-quran-award} {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar}

## What it does
The award runs international and local competitions for memorisers from around the world, honours memorisers, personalities and scholarly and da'wa institutions, oversees Qur'an memorisation centres in Dubai, and organises meetings, seminars and lectures. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar} Its branches include the [[award/dubai-islamic-personality-of-the-year|Islamic Personality of the Year]], the International Holy Quran Competition, the Sheikha Fatima bint Mubarak International Competition for women, the Sheikha Hind bint Maktoum Competition, a competition for the most beautiful recitation, one for Emirati memorisers, memorisation in Dubai's correctional institutions, the teaching of the readings, and a centre for Qur'anic manuscripts. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar} Its research unit publishes books in Qur'anic, sira and Sunna studies. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar}

## Sessions and winners
First place in the international competition went, at the 22nd session (2018), to Ahmad Burhan Muhammad of the United States; at the 23rd (2019) to Mu'adh Mahmud Amir of Libya; at the 24th (2021) to Muhammad Abd al-Rahman Abu Qasim of Syria; at the 25th (2022) to Boubakr Abd al-Hadi Raji' of Algeria; at the 26th (2023) to Salih Ahmad Takrim of Bangladesh; and at the 27th (2024) to Muhammad Adnan Abd Allah of Bahrain. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar} In the women's competition first place went in 2017 to Hadil Khalid Awad Jabbara of Palestine, in 2023 to Sundus Sa'id Muhammad Saydawi of Jordan, and in 2024 to Safiyya Muhammad Tahir of Sweden. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar}

## The Islamic Personality of the Year
Each year the award honours a person or institution for a distinguished contribution. The first honoured was [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Muhammad Mutawalli al-Sha'rawi]] at the first session, followed by [[person/abu-al-hasan-ali-nadwi|Abu al-Hasan Ali Nadwi]] and [[person/zayed-bin-sultan-al-nahyan|Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan]]; later recipients include [[person/alija-izetbegovic|Alija Izetbegović]], [[person/abdul-rahman-al-sudais|Abd al-Rahman al-Sudais]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]], [[person/zakir-naik|Zakir Naik]], [[person/ahmed-el-tayeb|Ahmed el-Tayeb]] and [[person/salman-bin-abdulaziz|King Salman bin Abd al-Aziz]], and among institutions [[building/al-azhar|al-Azhar Mosque]] and the [[group/king-fahd-complex-for-printing-the-holy-quran|King Fahd Complex for the Printing of the Holy Qur'an]]. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar}

## Today
In 2022 Emirates Post issued a commemorative stamp for the award's twenty-fifth anniversary. {cite:wikipedia-dubai-quran-award-ar} The award has Wikipedia articles in six languages. {cite:wikidata-dubai-quran-award}
