## Overview
The wall of the lane between the mosque and the lane was low, about three cubits, and the men grabbed its top and leapt into the mosque; among them was [[person/abu-al-wajih-of-banu-al-harith-killed-at-najran-286|Abu al-Wajih]], one of their men and chiefs, known for courage. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.166} [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] charged him and speared him before he had settled on the wall, throwing him into the lane, caught up with him in the middle of the lane and speared him down; Abu al-Wajih rose, and al-Hadi pinned him to the wall with the spear until it broke, then drew his sword and struck him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.166}

## After the killing
The people then fled, and al-Hadi's companions, the seven horsemen and the foot, came back looking for him and found him striking Abu al-Wajih; he told them to cut off the head, which they did, and sent it to his brother [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] outside the village, who stood with the horse facing the horse of Banu al-Harith, with orders to send it by a rider and throw it to them; he did so, and when they recognised the head they turned and fled. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.166} The fighting spread through the village, and the people of the lanes and of the facing houses, learning that al-Hadi had entered, shouted the takbir and charged with him and his companions; all of Banu al-Harith broke and many were killed or taken, and the village was secured. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.166}

## The search for Ibn Humayd
Al-Hadi ordered a search for [[person/ibn-humayd-of-banu-al-harith-286|Ibn Humayd]] and his companions, and Ibn Humayd was dug out of pounded straw where he was buried; nearly fifty of the leading men of Banu al-Harith were gathered from the village, the army of [[place/minas-najran|Minas]] and those at [[place/sawhan-najran|Sawhan]] dispersed, and Banu al-Harith fled into the hills and wadis wherever they were. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.166} The details of the wounding and killing are the report of a narrator who favours his imam; the museum neither confirms nor denies them.
