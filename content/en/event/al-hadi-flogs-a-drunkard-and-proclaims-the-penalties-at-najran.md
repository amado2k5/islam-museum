## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] says that a day after the pledge [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s agents seized a drunk man, and al-Hadi told one of his intimates that he was troubled that this man was seized before he had made his excuse to the townspeople and warned them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.69} He then had him given eighty lashes after the man had sobered up, and wrote a letter and ordered it read to all the people. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.69} This is the report of a punishment from a Zaydi book; the museum attributes the ruling to its narrator and imam and states no legal position on it.

## The text of the proclamation
Its heading is "a prior notice from al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, Amir al-Mu'minin Yahya ibn al-Husayn, to all the people of Najran". It says God laid down obligations He approved and commanded, and disliked things and forbade them, and fixed reward for whoever does as commanded and punishment for whoever does what He dislikes; among what carries punishment it names fornication, theft, slander of chaste women, usury, drinking wine, false witness and all sins, for which He fixed what He fixed in His Book and by the tongue of His Prophet; the penalties are all binding on whoever incurs them, "so beware, beware; whoever has warned has given excuse, so let no one blame anyone but himself"; and it tells them to enjoin good, forbid wrong and fear God. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.69} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70} See [[concept/amr-bil-maruf]].

## Its effect
When the letter was read it increased their liking for the truth, and they began to render rights to one another. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.70} That is the narrator's account, and the museum gives no verdict on the punishment or its effect.
