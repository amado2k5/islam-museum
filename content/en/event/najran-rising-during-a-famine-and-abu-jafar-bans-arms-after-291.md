## Overview
A severe famine spread over Yemen until people ate one another. The troublemakers of Banu al-Harith rose against al-Hadi ila al-Haqq's agent at Najran. Abu Ja'far came down hard on them, imprisoned those he could reach, sent them up to Sa'da, and forbade them to carry arms from the top of the valley to the bottom. No one carried arms, the people mixed, the town was secured, and the men of falsehood submitted. He had no army with him at that time except servants from Hamdan.

## Detail
Ali ibn Muhammad, the narrator, says a famine had struck Yemen and spread over the land until people ate one another there. The troublemakers and the men of falsehood among Banu al-Harith rose against al-Hadi ila al-Haqq's agent at Najran. Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah says: he came down hard on them and disapproved of what they did. He seized whom he could, threw them into prison and chains, and sent them up to Sa'da, and he forbade them to carry arms from the top of the valley to the bottom. No one carried arms, the people mixed, the town was secured, and the men of falsehood submitted. He had no army with him at that time except servants of his own from Hamdan. A marginal note in the printed text says that the agent of al-Hadi forbade Banu al-Harith at Najran to carry arms. The book does not give a year for this episode. It comes in the narrative after the arrival at Sa'da in 291, so the year is only the nearest one the narrative allows and is not an established date; the verifier should check it. Abu Ja'far, the agent of the record, is the same man who is killed at the house in 295.
