## Overview
In Dhu al-Qa'da 283 AH letters from [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] reached a group of the Banu Abi Talib and others in [[place/medina|Medina]], calling them to obey God, to strive against His enemies, to support His friends, to make His religion manifest and to revive the practices of His Prophet; they told them that God's proofs stood against them and that they should answer the caller to God, and that letters from Yemenis had come to him with some of their men, asking him to come to their country and giving him their pledges, and that they regretted their failure when they had let him leave them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.36}

## Who received them
[[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]] says his father, [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]], was among those written to, together with a man called [[person/yahya-ibn-al-husayn-ibn-yahya-al-umari|Yahya ibn al-Husayn ibn Yahya]] of the line of Umar ibn Ali, and no other was known; Ali was then a boy on whom no obligation before God had yet fallen, that is, one who had not yet come of age. When the letter reached his father he resolved to go, told his son and ordered him to follow later. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.36} His father and Yahya ibn al-Husayn set out for [[place/al-fur-medina|al-Fur']]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.36} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.37} See [[event/al-hadi-first-journey-to-yemen-280]].
