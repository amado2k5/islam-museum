## Overview
When [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s letter reached Abu Muhammad he called the people and read it to them, and they were glad. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.158} A poem follows that opens by telling al-Hadi he spoke truly and aims at piety; its speaker says he is neither slack nor asleep, will not leave war its due, will judge by the Book in every pass, will not bow before war or fear death, and that whoever wants war with him or peace with him will find the same. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.158} The book then says the answer to his letter reached al-Hadi, who wrote him verses. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.158}

## Al-Hadi's reply
The next poem, on p. 159, speaks of affection for as long as both live, of a lover in God's cause who hopes in Him and is angry when He is disobeyed, says the wrongdoers had held a forbidden place lightly through ignorance, that if God is pleased they are pleased and if He is angered they are His soldiers, points to swords, horses and lances, and says their matter is near if they submit to the truth, strong and weak, and otherwise the sword. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.159}

## Notes
The line order of pp. 158-159 is disturbed in the printed text, so the two poems are assigned by the order of printing alone: the first is read as Abu Muhammad's reply and the second as al-Hadi's answer. The abusive words are not repeated, nor the historical comparisons the poem draws. This follows [[event/safe-conduct-for-the-akilis-and-al-hadis-verses-from-saada-286]].
