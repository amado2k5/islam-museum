## Overview
On the second of Rabi al-Awwal 897 AH the Castilians entered the Alhambra, after the last of the Nasrid sultans, [[person/boabdil-muhammad-xii|Abu Abd Allah]], handed over [[place/granada|Granada]] under documents of sixty-seven conditions. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.529-531}

## The conditions
The sultan met with the people of the state and the counsellors, and their opinion agreed to take the lesser of two harms. The conditions were read out to the people of Granada, and they submitted to them. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.529-531}

The account gives some of them: the safety of the people in their persons, families and property, their remaining in their places, the upholding of their law, and the survival of the mosques and endowments. Among them was that no Christian should enter a Muslim's house or a mosque, that the captives be ransomed, and that whoever wished might cross to the Moroccan shore. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.529-531}

## After the surrender
The Castilians took about five hundred notables as pledges. The sultan went down to the Alpujarras, then crossed the sea to Melilla and settled in Fez. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.529-531}

Al-Maqqari reports that the Christians broke the conditions one after another, until they forced the Muslims to convert to Christianity in 904 AH. The people of the Bayyazin rose against the rulers and killed them, villages that refused were wiped out, and the people of the mountain of Balanqa came out under safe conduct to Fez. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.529-531}

The date of the event is converted from the Hijri date. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.530}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] tells the last months of Granada at the end of episode 6 and in episode 7 of his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]], and treats its fall as the end of the Muslim state in al-Andalus {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:13:30}.

### The siege
Al-Suwaidan says that after the king of Spain took [[place/almeria|Almería]] and [[place/wadi-ash|Wadi Ash (Guadix)]] in 895 AH, 1490 CE, the city of Granada with its villages and farms was surrounded from east, west, north and south, with no outlet to the sea left, and [[person/boabdil-muhammad-xii|Abu Abdallah Muhammad al-Saghir]] was told to hand over its keys, two years before the final fall {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:17:00}. He relates that al-Saghir resolved to fight and proclaimed jihad, and that a group inside Granada who refused humiliation and surrender also proclaimed jihad under the emir Musa ibn Abi Ghassan, so that ruler and people alike had declared it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep06 00:18:30}.

### Musa ibn Abi Ghassan and the uprising
Al-Suwaidan relates that al-Saghir had no means of defence, having built no army, and that Granada was held by a strong Islamic uprising led by Musa ibn Abi Ghassan, who stirred the people to jihad, organised them and roused them to defend their city and their identity, and the people answered him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:03:00}. The men of this uprising, he says, held Granada's fortifications for seven full months against fierce attacks, while al-Saghir watched from a distance {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:06:00}. Al-Suwaidan pauses here to say that relations between rulers and Islamic movements are always strained, that a sincere ruler would find such movements his helpers, and to call for bridges between the two {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:05:00}.

### The terms of surrender
Al-Suwaidan relates that after seven months a letter came from Ferdinand and Isabella asking for the surrender of Granada and guaranteeing safety to al-Saghir, his people and his army, with conditions including: the mosques left alone, no one enslaved, no one prevented from practising their religion, the Christian army not to enter Granada but only the king and his party, prisoners released, honour and property secured, and Abu Abdallah to be a vizier over Granada assisting the king of Spain {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:07:30}. Al-Saghir agreed, he says, on condition that the two monarchs swear to these terms and that the Pope in Rome, as the authority the Spanish monarchs respected, sign them; the agreement was sent to him and he signed it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:09:30}.

### Musa's speech and death
Al-Suwaidan relates that Musa ibn Abi Ghassan stood in the Alhambra before the king and the notables and warned them not to believe the Christians would keep their pledges; death, he said, was lighter than what awaited them, the plunder of their cities, the desecration of their mosques, the ruin of their homes, the violation of their women, prisons and pyres, and he declared that he would not live to see it but would die an honourable death {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:11:00}. He left the council for his house, al-Suwaidan says, put on his arms, mounted his horse and rode out to fight; people moved with him, but his movement had no support from within or without, the resistance collapsed, and he was left alone facing fifteen Christian horsemen, killed most of them and was then killed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:13:00}. Al-Suwaidan says he told the story in full in his book "Al-Andalus: An Illustrated History", and wishes Musa's name were kept alive among young Muslims {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:13:30}.

### The entry into the Alhambra
Al-Suwaidan relates that al-Saghir sent the monarchs his consent with lavish gifts; they entered the Alhambra preceded by monks, and he knelt and kissed Ferdinand's hand {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:15:00}. The monarchs' first official act, he says, was to raise a great silver cross over the palace's highest tower and proclaim from it that Granada now belonged to the Catholic Monarchs and that Muslim rule in al-Andalus had ended {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:16:00}. He dates this to 2 Rabi al-Awwal 897 AH, 2 January 1492 CE {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep07 00:17:30}.

### The breaking of the terms
In episode 8 al-Suwaidan relates that after Abu Abdallah left, the Christians did not keep their pledges but ignored the conditions, humiliated the Muslims and confiscated their property, and he cites in this connection the verse [[passage/quran-9-8|al-Tawba 8]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:03:00}. What followed he tells in [[group/mudajjan|the Mudéjares]] and in [[event/alpujarras-rebellion-1568|the Alpujarras revolt]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep08 00:19:00}.
