## Overview
When [[person/ubaydallah-ibn-hanash|Ubaydallah ibn Hanash]] saw the troops going back from Shibam to Sana'a he came to [[person/ali-ibn-sulayman-ibn-al-qasim|Ali ibn Sulayman]] and said: the news has reached me from these people and it is bad, and my leaving Sana'a is better for you than my staying with you; Ali ibn Sulayman said: act as you think best {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.219} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.220}. Ubaydallah ibn Hanash and Ahmad ibn Harnud went out to [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am ibn Ibrahim]] and urged him to rise to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]; al-Da'am gathered a dense army and wrote to [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]], Muhammad ibn al-Hadi, and the two met at [[place/raydah-yemen|Raydah]] and went on together to Shibam, to al-Hadi, who kept them there some days and then resolved to send them all to Sana'a to hold it and keep it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.220}.

## Detail
Ubaydallah ibn Hanash has his own record. Ahmad ibn Harnud is written as printed, confirmed by Apple Vision, and appears in prose only, with no descent given. The date is given to the month, derived, and comes before the days remaining of Jumada II in the next account. The prayer formula after Abu al-Qasim's name in the text is a mark of respect and is not reproduced. It is followed by [[event/the-rising-of-ibn-mahfuz-at-sanaa-and-ali-ibn-sulayman-leaves-for-madar-288]].
