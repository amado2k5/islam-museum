## Summary
The Mubahala of Najran is the most famous debate between Islam and Christianity in the Prophet's lifetime: an episcopal delegation from [[place/najran|Najran]] argued with the Prophet over the divinity of Jesus, the Qur'an came down with the counter-argument, the two parties were summoned to a mutual imprecation, and the Christians declined and made peace instead. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/573-577}

## Background to the delegation
In 10 AH a delegation of the Christians of Najran arrived, about sixty riders including al-Aqib Abd al-Masih, al-Sayyid al-Ayham, and their bishop Abu Haritha ibn Alqama, master of their school and a relative of theirs — who in some reports later embraced Islam. They lodged in the mosque at midday; the Prophet left them to pray toward the east, then they sat with him {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/573-574}.

## The disputation — what was said and by whom
By the Sira's account, two of the delegates — al-Aqib and al-Sayyid — opened the argument with the Prophet over Jesus, asserting his divinity: 'he is the son of God,' arguing from his having no father. The Prophet withheld an immediate reply until the opening of Surat Al Imran descended upon him: 'the likeness of Jesus before God is as the likeness of Adam; He created him from dust, then said to him "Be," and he was. The truth is from your Lord, so do not be among the doubters' — the answer turned the proof on its holders: if being fatherless entailed divinity, Adam, who has neither father nor mother, would deserve it more — yet they hold him a creature {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/574-575}. When they still would not recede came the divine command to meet proof with proof to the utmost: 'Whoever disputes with you about him after the knowledge that has come to you, say: Come, let us call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, ourselves and yourselves, then let us pray ardently and lay the curse of God on the liars' {cite:sahih-muslim 2404}.

## The imprecation — the appointed day
On the appointed day the Prophet came out carrying [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|al-Hasan]], holding [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|al-Husayn]]'s hand, with [[person/fatima|Fatima]] walking behind him and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] behind her, saying: 'when I pray, say amen.' When al-Aqib and al-Sayyid saw their faces they said to their companions: O Christians, you know Muhammad is a prophet sent, and he has brought you the decisive word about your companion; no people have ever imprecated against a prophet and had their young and old survive — if we do it we are destroyed. They said: Abu al-Qasim, we have decided not to imprecate you, to leave you on your religion and return on ours. They asked him for a trustworthy man to send with them to divide their goods, and he said: I will send you a man truly trustworthy, a trustworthy man — it was [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]] {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/576-577} {cite:sahih-muslim 2404}.

## Who said what — the recorded utterances
- **Al-Aqib and al-Sayyid**: 'he is the son of God' — arguing from the missing father.
- **The Prophet**: held his peace until the Qur'anic answer descended, then summoned them to imprecation, and came out with his four family-members.
- **The delegation**: 'no people have ever imprecated a prophet and their young and old survived' — so they sought a settlement instead of mutual cursing.
- **The verdict** was the opponent's own withdrawal — the strongest possible ruling.

## The settlement and after
He made peace with them on two thousand garments a year and whatever arms they agreed on, and the Christians of Najran remained secure until Umar evacuated those who stayed to the Najraniyya land in Iraq opposite Kufa {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya 5/45}. Sahih Muslim records that when the mubahala verse came down the Prophet called Ali, Fatima, Hasan and Husayn and said: 'O God, these are my family' {cite:sahih-muslim 2404}.
