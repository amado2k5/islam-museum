In Safar of the fourth year of the Hijra — the same month as the al-Raji' calamity — Abu Bara Amir ibn Malik, called "the player with spears", came to the Prophet at Medina. The Prophet offered him Islam; he neither became Muslim nor refused outright, but said: Messenger of God, if you were to send men of your Companions to the people of Najd to call them to your cause, I would hope they would answer you. The Prophet sent seventy men of the choicest Companions and Qur'an reciters, appointing al-Mundhir ibn Amr over them, under Abu Bara's protection. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When they halted at Bi'r Ma'una they sent [[person/haram-ibn-milhan|Haram ibn Milhan]] with the Messenger of God's letter to Amir ibn al-Tufayl, who did not look at the letter but signalled to a man, who thrust Haram with a spear from behind until it came through; Haram spattered the blood onto his face and head and said: "God is greatest — I have won, by the Lord of the Ka'ba!" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

Then Amir ibn al-Tufayl summoned Banu Sulaym (Usayya, Ra'l and Dhakwan), who surrounded the Companions and fought them until they were all martyred to the last man. None escaped but Ka'b ibn Zayd, left wounded among the dead, who lived until he was martyred at the Trench; and [[person/amr-ibn-umayya-al-damri|Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri]], whom they captured — Amir cut off his forelock and freed him against a soul owed by his mother. The Prophet grieved deeply over those reciters and made the qunut supplication in his prayers for a month, praying against Ra'l, Dhakwan and Usayya. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
