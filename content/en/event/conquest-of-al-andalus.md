## Overview
al-Tabari, in the account for 91 AH, reports that [[person/musa-ibn-nusayr|Musa ibn Nusayr]] raided [[place/al-andalus|al-Andalus]], and God opened cities and forts through his hands. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.6 p.454} al-Tabari's index places the heading "the conquest of al-Andalus" in 92 AH. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.6 p.632}

## The reports and the date
On the authority of al-Waqidi, al-Baladhuri relates that [[person/tariq-ibn-ziyad|Tariq ibn Ziyad]], the deputy of Musa, was the first to raid al-Andalus, in 92 AH. [[person/alyan|Alyan]], governor of the strait of al-Andalus, met him, and Tariq granted him safe-conduct on condition that he carry him and his companions in the ships. Then its people fought him and he conquered it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} The date is therefore disputed between 91 AH and 92 AH.

al-Salabi says that the conquest was preceded by a reconnaissance raid under Tarif ibn Malik in Ramadan 91 AH, then Tariq crossed in Rajab 92 AH, and that the book rejects the reports that make booty the cause of the conquest. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.66-70}

## Musa ibn Nusayr
al-Salabi says that Musa left Qayrawan in 93 AH and conquered Carmona, then Seville and Merida, met Tariq at Toledo at the end of 94 AH, and then reached Galicia and Asturias. al-Walid summoned him to Damascus, and he arrived there in 96 AH, before al-Walid's death. {cite:salabi-umayyad-state-2020 p.75-79}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports that [[person/musa-ibn-nusayr|Musa ibn Nusayr]] put [[person/tariq-ibn-ziyad|Tariq ibn Ziyad]] over Tangier, where he stayed on garrison duty for a long time, in the year 92. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 276} [[person/julian-count-of-ceuta|Julian]], lord of al-Khadra' on the crossing, owed obedience to [[person/roderick|Roderic]], ruler of al-Andalus, who lived at Toledo; Julian had sent his daughter to Roderic's court to be educated, Roderic made her pregnant, and Julian resolved to bring the Arabs in against him. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 276} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 277}

## The crossing
Julian carried Tariq's men across by night in merchant ships, while by day they hid on the mountain now called Jabal Tariq; the ships went back and forth until all were across, the people of al-Andalus taking them for merchant vessels, and Tariq crossed with the last of them. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 277} The cavalry of Cordoba met him and was routed, and Roderic marched from Toledo; they met at Shadhuna, on a river now called Wadi Umm Hakim, and Roderic and those with him were killed. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 278} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 279} It is also said that Roderic came out on a throne carried between two mules, wearing his crown, and that the fighting lasted from sunrise to sunset. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 280} See [[event/battle-of-shadhuna|the battle of Shadhuna]].

## The locked house
Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports from his father and from Hisham ibn Ishaq that there was in al-Andalus a house with locks on it: every king who came to power added a lock, until the king in whose time the Muslims entered refused to add one before knowing what was inside. He had it opened, and inside were pictures of Arabs and a writing saying that when this door was opened, these people would enter the land. It is one of the reports the book transmits. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 278}

## Musa in al-Andalus
Musa set out for al-Andalus in Rajab 93 with Arab notables, clients and Berber chiefs, leaving his son Abd Allah over al-Qayrawan; Tariq met him at Cordoba and told him: "I am only your client, and this conquest is yours." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 280} Musa wrote to the caliph that these were not conquests but the Gathering. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 281} He stayed in al-Andalus through 93, 94 and part of 95, and left his son Abd al-Aziz as governor. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 284}
