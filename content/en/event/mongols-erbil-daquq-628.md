## Summary
In Dhu al-Hijja of 628 AH a Tatar party came from Azerbaijan into the Erbil districts, plundered villages and killed, then withdrew unmolested through Daquq and its surroundings. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 494}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that in Dhu al-Hijja of this year a company of Tatars reached the Erbil districts from Azerbaijan, killing those they met on the road — the Aywai Turcomans, the Kurds, the Jawzaqan and others — until they entered the land of Erbil, plundered its villages, killed whomever they caught of its people, and did atrocities of a kind never heard of from others. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 494} Muzaffar al-Din, lord of Erbil, came out with his armies reinforced by Mosul's troops and marched at them; but when word reached him that the Tatars had turned back toward Azerbaijan he stayed in his own land and did not pursue, and they went on to the town of al-Karkhini, Daquq and elsewhere, returning safe — no one alarmed them and no horseman stood in their path. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 494} Ibn al-Athir calls these calamities and events such as the people had not seen their like in ancient or modern times. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 494}
