## Overview
In 566 AH [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din]] in Egypt demolished a house of the police chief called the House of Relief, where those he wished to imprison were held, and built it as a madrasa for the Shafi'is. He also built the House of Justice as a madrasa for the Shafi'is. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.32}

## The judiciary
Ibn al-Athir relates that Salah al-Din removed the judges of the Egyptians, who were Shi'a, and appointed a Shafi'i judge over Egypt. He then appointed Shafi'i judges in all the lands, on the twentieth of Jumada al-Akhira. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.32}

## The madrasas
Taqi al-Din Umar, nephew of Salah al-Din, bought the houses of al-Izz in Egypt and built them as a madrasa for the Shafi'is. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.32}

The Gregorian date is derived from the Hijri date.
