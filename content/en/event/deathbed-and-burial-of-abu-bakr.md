## Summary
This record gathers what the books say of Abu Bakr's last hours, of what he left behind and of his burial. It complements [[event/death-of-abu-bakr|the death of Abu Bakr]], which treats the cause of his illness, and does not repeat [[event/testament-of-abu-bakr-designating-umar|his covenant of succession]] or [[event/last-counsel-of-abu-bakr-to-umar|his counsel to Umar]]. He died on the night of Tuesday, with eight nights left of Jumada II in 13 AH, aged sixty-three. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 103} He ordered that his wife Asma bint Umays wash him; Umar prayed over him, and he was buried beside the Prophet in Aisha's house. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 258}

## In his illness
Abu al-Safar reports that people visited him and offered to call a doctor; he said, "He has already seen me." They asked what the doctor had said and he answered, "I do what I will." {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 259} In Ibn al-Athir, Aisha sat with him as he struggled with death and recited a line of verse that wealth does not avail a man when his breath rattles; he looked at her like someone angry, said it was not so, and recited a verse about the agony of death. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 265} For the verse see [[passage/quran-50-19|Qur'an 50:19]].

## What he left and to whom
Abu Bakr ordered what he had taken from the treasury returned. In Ibn al-Athir he ordered a plot of his land sold and its price spent in place of what he had taken from the Muslims' funds, and it is said they had fixed him six thousand dirhams a year. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 266} Aisha reports, in Sifat al-Safwa, that in his last illness he said: see what has increased in my property since I took charge and send it to the caliph after me. They found a Nubian slave who carried his children and a water-camel that irrigated a garden of his, and sent them to Umar, who wept and said that Abu Bakr had wearied those who came after him. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102} In al-Kamil the items sent were a slave, a camel and a worn wrap; Abu Bakr told Aisha that since they took charge of the Muslims' affairs they had not eaten a dinar or a dirham of theirs, only coarse meal, and worn rough clothes. Umar wept until his tears fell to the ground; Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf protested that the household of Abu Bakr was being deprived; Umar refused to take on what Abu Bakr had given up. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 265} When Abu Bakr died Umar gathered trusted men and opened the treasury, and they found nothing in it except one dinar that had fallen from a sack. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 265}

Aisha also relates that he told her the person he most wished to see rich after him was she; he had given her the date harvest of twenty wasq from his property and wished she had taken possession of it, for it was now the inheritance of her two brothers and two sisters. She asked who her second sister was, and he said the child in the womb of the daughter of Kharija, whom he took to be a girl. She was born as Umm Kulthum. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 103} In al-Kamil he tells her he had given her a garden, felt uneasy about it, and asked her to return it to the inheritance, which she did. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 265}

## Washing and shroud
Aisha relates that when he grew heavy he asked, "What day is it?" They said Monday, and he said he hoped for what lay between then and night. He wore a cloth with a saffron stain, and said: when I die wash this cloth, add two new ones and shroud me in three. They asked, "Should they not all be new?" He said it was for the decay. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 103} In Ibn al-Athir he ordered to be shrouded in his two garments with a third bought for him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} Aisha also reports that he asked the day and said: if I die tonight do not wait for tomorrow, for the days and nights dearest to me are those nearest the Messenger of God. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 261}

He ordered that his wife [[person/asma-bint-umays|Asma bint Umays]] and his son wash him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 307} Al-Muhibb al-Tabari adds that his son [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-abi-bakr|Abd al-Rahman]] poured the water. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 258} Asma reports that he told her that a certain man was a hypocrite, so he should not go down into his grave. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 261}

## Prayer and burial
He was carried on the bier on which the Messenger of God had been carried, and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] prayed over him in the Messenger's mosque with four takbirs. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib says the prayer was between the grave and the pulpit. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 258} He was buried by night; his son Abd al-Rahman, Umar, Uthman and Talha went down into the grave; his head was placed by the Prophet's shoulders and his niche joined to the Prophet's. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 103} Al-Muhibb al-Tabari says he was buried in Aisha's house with the Prophet. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 258}

Aisha and other women began wailing; Umar forbade it and they refused. He told Hisham ibn al-Walid to bring out Umm Farwa, daughter of Abu Quhafa, and struck her with his whip, and the wailing scattered. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 263} His last words were a prayer that God take him as a Muslim and join him to the righteous. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 263} For the verse see [[passage/quran-12-101|Qur'an 12:101]].

## After the burial
When the news reached his father, Abu Quhafa, still alive in Mecca, he said it was a great calamity; he lived about six months longer. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 262} Al-Muhibb al-Tabari relates that Ali came saying the formula of return and said, "Today the caliphate of prophecy has ended"; he stood at the door of the house and praised him at length. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 262} He also relates that Aisha, passing her father's grave, prayed for him. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 265}

## Where the reports differ
- Time of death: al-Muhibb al-Tabari holds that he died at night and was buried at night, and notes it is also said he died by day and was buried at the end of the day. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 261}
- The washer: Asma bint Umays at his bidding; some narrators add that she was fasting, which al-Muhibb al-Tabari says is not sound on the well-known account, because fasting is by day. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 261}
- The shroud: two of his own garments and a third new one in al-Kamil, three cloths one of which was his washed garment in Aisha's report. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 262} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 103}
- The gift to Aisha: twenty wasq of dates in Sifat al-Safwa, a garden in al-Kamil. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 103} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 265}
