## Summary
Ibn Khaldun describes the rise of the Tatars as an affair that, once their dominion swelled, sent "Hūlāgū b. Tūlī b. Genghis Khan from Khorasan to Baghdad; he took it and killed the caliph", ending the Abbasid caliphate at Baghdad in 656 {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.2110}. He sums up the sweep by saying they absorbed all the kingdoms of Islam from Syria to Transoxiana {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.2120}.

## The course of events
Ibn Khaldun relates that Hulagu marched in the year fifty and six hundred with massed armies until he reached and besieged Baghdad {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.961}. In the caliphal sequence he notes that the Abbasid daʿwa moved to Egypt, where caliphs' sons were installed under Mamluk protection {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.2124}. He closes with the turn inside the conqueror's own house: "God guided Abaghā b. Hulagu to Islam and he became Muslim," and after him the Jochids and other Tatar kings {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.2120}.

## For young visitors
A great army came from far away in Asia and captured many cities, including Baghdad the caliph's capital; years later many of the invaders' own descendants became Muslims.

## Research notes
- Text from the harvested Ibar of Ibn Khaldun (Heliopolis 1957): pp. 952, 961, 2110, 2120, 2124. A near-contemporary historian's account of the main events; battle detail (ʿAyn Jālūt 658, the Iraqi sieges) and the fall of individual cities await collation with Mamluk-era sources (Ibn al-Athir, al-Maqrizi) not fully extracted on the drive.
