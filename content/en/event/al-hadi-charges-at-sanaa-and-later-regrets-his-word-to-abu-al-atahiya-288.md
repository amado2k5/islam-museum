## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] said to Abu al-Atahiya, in the narrator's report: send to them and move them from their place, for by God if I go out against them I will string them on my spear as locusts are strung on a stick {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.209}. [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] went back to them and adjured them by God, but they would not accept it and attacked him with arrows and stones; a large crowd of the people of Sana'a joined them, which the narrator counts as ten thousand men and, with the Jafatim, six hundred horse, and when they had driven Abu al-Atahiya off they went for [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|the Tabaris]] and took a number of them. The Tabaris fought them hard, the fighting reached the neighbourhood of al-Hadi's house and they began to shoot arrows at his majlis {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.209}. Abu al-Atahiya came to al-Hadi and said: mount, may I be your ransom, the people have come on you. Al-Hadi mounted his horse, ordered his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] to mount and his companions to mount, and went out of his house; when the people saw him, who had been driving his men back into the house, they returned to their place, and his companions pressed them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.209}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi charged them alone, with [[person/ismail-ibn-al-muslim-al-hamdani|Ismail ibn al-Muslim]]; when they came near the people Ismail held back and al-Hadi went on, speared the first man he met and killed him, then another, then another, until three of their best men were down, then he caught up with the horse, speared a rider and threw him, all in that one charge. The narrator says his word was proved true and he strung them on his spear as he had promised {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.209}. [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]] then reports that he heard al-Hadi say afterwards: by God I have regretted nothing I said except my word to Abu al-Atahiya, and I regretted that word until God gave the victory over them; so I vowed to myself never to speak so again {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.209}. Both are the narrator's report, in his followers' voice, of al-Hadi's words and acts, and the museum passes no judgment; the word of abuse al-Hadi used for his opponents in the threat is not repeated.
