## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad tells that his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] met [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] when he reached [[place/saada|Sa'da]], with a party of Khawlan from [[group/banu-sad-khawlan|Banu Sa'd of Khawlan]], al-Rabi'a and a party of [[group/al-akiliyyun-saada|the Akilis]], all but their chief [[person/ahmad-ibn-abbad-al-akili|Ahmad ibn Abbad al-Akili]], who left Sa'da as al-Hadi entered and avoided meeting him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.155} The account is Zaydi and the charges in it are the narrator's; the museum neither confirms nor denies them.

## What the narrator says of his intent
The narrator says Ibn Abbad had hoped to do harm to al-Hadi and had written to Banu al-Harith and others, and that he, Ibn Humayd, Ibn Bustam and Ibn al-Dahhak had agreed and pledged, during the journey on which al-Hadi took the "troublemakers", that each would make trouble for al-Hadi in his own land, so that he wished to keep faith with his companions after what had happened. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.155} After al-Hadi reached Sa'da he wrote that he had left in awe of him and owed him hearing and obedience, wishing to slow his journey, and asked to be excused from going to Najran because Banu al-Harith were his maternal uncles and he did not wish to see them harmed; al-Hadi did not grant it and ordered his clan to go with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.155} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.156}

## The fortifying
When Ibn Abbad saw al-Hadi's resolve he gathered those he could in the night and by morning was in Sa'da, gathered his rabble, as the narrator calls them, ordered them to fortify their place and built crenels over his lanes, while al-Hadi was in his house at the side of the town. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.156} See [[event/fight-between-banu-sad-and-the-akilis-at-saada-286]].
