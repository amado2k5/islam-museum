## Overview
The poem names the Qarmatians as the source of corruption in the land and calls the imam to march against them, promising him victory.

## Detail
The poet says that corruption has appeared in the land and that the Qarmatians rose to bring it about. He charges them with having turned from the faith and with allying themselves with another party, which the printed verse names in a word the editor does not read with certainty. He then calls the imam to rise and fight them and promises him victory. The harsh charge against them is the poet's, and the museum does not repeat its wording; the book's own sense is given here only in outline.
