## Overview
Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari learned kalam from Abu Ali al-Jubba'i, shaykh of the Mu'tazila and the leading theologian of his age at Basra, and was one of his best pupils, near to succeeding him {cite:wafayat-ayan} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala 15/85} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 743}. From this period comes the report of his question to his master about three brothers, one pious, one an unbeliever, and one who died young; when al-Jubba'i answered on Mu'tazilite premises al-Ash'ari pressed him with questions until he was silenced, a debate counted among the preliminaries of his break with the Mu'tazila {cite:wafayat-ayan} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala 15/86}.

## His other teachers
Al-Dhahabi records that al-Ash'ari also took from Abu Khalifa al-Jumhi, Zakariyya al-Saji and Sahl ibn Nuh and their contemporaries, transmitting from them with isnad extensively in his tafsir {cite:siyar-alam-nubala 15/85}.
