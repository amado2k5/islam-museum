## Summary
In Muharram 409 AH the Qadiriyya Creed was read in the caliphal diwan at Baghdad, by order of the caliph [[person/al-qadir-billah|al-Qadir billah]], to affirm the doctrine of the people of the Sunna and hadith and to refute the doctrine that the Qur'an is created. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.289}

## The reading at the assembly
On Thursday, the seventeenth of Muharram, people were gathered in the procession at the caliphal palace in Baghdad, and there was read to them a statement of Sunni doctrine written by al-Qadir billah. It set out the foundations of belief, the virtues of the Companions, and a refutation of the innovators, and it stated plainly: "Whoever says the Qur'an is created is an unbeliever whose blood may be shed." {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.289}

## Doctrinal impact
In the fifth century AH the Qadiri creed became the official creedal document of the Abbasid caliphal court. It was read at public assemblies, the oath of allegiance was taken on the basis of it, and letters were sent with it to the rulers of the provinces in Khurasan and the East. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi j.7 p.289}
