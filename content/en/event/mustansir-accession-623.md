## Summary
In 623 AH, after al-Zahir bi-Amr Allah, his elder son Abu Ja'far al-Mansur was acclaimed caliph and given the laqab al-Mustansir bi'llah. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 465}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that al-Mustansir followed his father's path in goodness and beneficence toward the people: he ordered a proclamation in Baghdad that justice would flow freely, and that anyone with a need or a grievance could present it to have his need met and his wrong redressed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 465} When the first Friday of his caliphate came he wished to pray in the caliphs' maqsura, but was told the covered passage leading to it was ruined and impassable; so he rode to the palace mosque openly, seen by the people in a white shirt and white turban, letting none of his retinue walk with him — only two servants and a stirrup-holder — prayed and returned, and did the same the second Friday until the passage was repaired. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 465} Prices had risen after al-Zahir's death, the karra reaching eighteen qirats, so he ordered his own grain sold at thirteen qirats per karra, and prices fell and affairs steadied. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 465}
