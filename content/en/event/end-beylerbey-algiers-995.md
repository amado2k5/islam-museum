## Summary
Ottoman Algiers shifts from the beylerbey system to the pasha system, 995/1587 {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 272-273}.

## The end and the reason
With [[person/kilic-ali-pasha|Uluj Ali]]'s death ended the beylerbey system that had made Algiers' governors near-sovereigns; it was replaced by the pasha system as in Tunis and Tripoli — a salaried pasha sent from Istanbul for three years with no local base. Al-Salabi explains the change as the sultan's fear that strong beylerbeys, with the navy weakened, would drift toward independence {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 272-273}.

## Context
After Lepanto (978/1571) Ottoman North Africa split from a single beylerbey at Algiers into three ordinary wilayas — Tripoli, Tunis and Algiers; and after 997/1588 the soldiers and seamen overshadowed the pashas' authority, though the khutba in the sultan's name, the annual money and acceptance of Istanbul's pasha remained the marks of Ottoman sovereignty {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 273}.
