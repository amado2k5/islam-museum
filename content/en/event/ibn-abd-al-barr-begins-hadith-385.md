## The hadith quest
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr took up the quest for hadith in 'three hundred and eighty-something', years before the hafiz Abu Bakr al-Khatib was born, and he persevered in it with an obsessive devotion that outshone the Andalusians before him {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 137} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 141}.
## The shaykhs
He transmitted from Khalf ibn al-Qasim, 'Abd al-Warith ibn Sufyan, Abu al-Walid ibn al-Faradi and Abu 'Amr al-Talamanki — fewer than seventy shaykhs, and he never journeyed for hadith {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 137} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 142}; he heard the Sunan of Abu Dawud on a high isnad from Ibn 'Abd al-Mu'min and read al-Za'farani's book under Ibn Dayfun {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi p. 141}.
