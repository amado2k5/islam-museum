## Overview
After [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] returned from Siffin he lost a coat of mail, and found it with a Jew in Kufa. Ali said the mail was his, and that he had neither sold nor given it away. They went to the judge [[person/shurayh-al-qadi|Shurayh]]. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 306-307}

## In the judge's session
Ali sat beside Shurayh and said that were it not that his opponent was a Jew, he would be seated level with him. Shurayh asked him for proof, and he named Qanbar and al-Hasan as witnesses. Shurayh said that the testimony of a son is not valid for his father, and judged for the Jew. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 306-307}

## After the ruling
Al-Suyuti relates that the Jew said: the commander of the faithful brought me before his judge and he ruled against him. Then he bore witness to the truth, acknowledged that the mail was Ali's mail, and became a Muslim. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 306-307}

The date is derived from the source's phrase "after his return from Siffin" and needs an additional source.

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
[[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] tells the story in the twelfth episode as a witness to justice towards minorities: Ali ibn Abi Talib's coat of mail fell from his camel and a Jew took it; later Ali, then commander of the faithful and head of the state, found it in the Jew's house, and rather than take it by force he brought him before [[person/shurayh-al-qadi|the judge Shurayh]]. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:18:30} The Jew denied it, Shurayh asked Ali for evidence, and as Ali had neither proof nor witnesses Shurayh ruled the armour for the Jew. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:19:30} In his telling the Jew went out marvelling at a religion with such a commander and such a judge, and pronounced the testimony of faith. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:20:00} His telling differs in details, such as how the armour was lost, from the accounts given above on this page.
