## Summary
[[group/banu-al-dil|Banu al-Dil]] of [[group/banu-bakr|Banu Bakr]] surprised [[group/khuzaa|Khuza'a]] at their watering-place of [[place/al-watir|al-Watir]] in lower [[place/mecca|Mecca]], struck down one man of them and chased them until they took refuge in the sanctuary {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31-32}. [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]] supplied Banu Bakr with weapons and some of them fought alongside them by night in concealment — a breach of the [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|Hudaybiyya]] treaty and, in the narrative, the spark of the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|conquest of Mecca]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}.

## Background
Between Banu Bakr ibn Abd Manaf and Khuza'a ran an old Jahiliyya feud: before Islam, Khuza'a had killed Banu al-Aswad ibn Razn — Salma, Kulthum and Dhu'ayb, the nobles of Banu al-Dil — at Arafa by the sanctuary's boundary stones; Banu al-Aswad had paid two blood-wits for their honour {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31}. Then [[person/malik-ibn-abbad-al-hadrami|Malik ibn Abbad al-Hadrami]] went out trading and Khuza'a set upon him in their land, killed him and took his goods; his pact had been with Banu al-Aswad ibn Razn {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31}.

Then came the [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|Hudaybiyya truce]]: Banu Bakr entered Quraysh's pact and covenant, and Khuza'a entered the Messenger of God's pact and covenant {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}. Banu al-Dil seized on the truce to pursue an old revenge — in the account they said: Muhammad will never enter the House by force nor reach us {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}.

## What happened
[[person/nawfal-ibn-muawiya|Nawfal ibn Mu'awiya al-Dili]] went out with Banu al-Dil — not all Banu Bakr followed him — and surprised Khuza'a at al-Watir {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}. They struck a man called [[person/munabbih|Munabbih]], whose companion [[person/tamim-ibn-asad|Tamim ibn Asad]] cried: "go, Tamim — by God I am left a dead man", then fled until he entered Mecca {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}.

The two parties closed and fought within Mecca; when Banu al-Dil caught Khuza'a inside the sanctuary some of their men said: Nawfal, you are in the sanctuary — and he said: "your God, your God, people! Will you take your revenge on the polytheists and be kept from it by polytheists? Take your revenge!" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32} Quraysh supplied Banu Bakr with weapons and some of them fought alongside them by night in concealment, until they drove them into the house of [[person/budayl-ibn-warqa|Budayl ibn Warqa']] and the house of their client [[person/rafi-mawla-khuzaa|Rafi']] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32-33}.

## The battle's poets
[[person/al-akhzar-ibn-lut|al-Akhzar ibn La't al-Dili]] recited verse boasting of driving Banu Ka'b from al-Watir, and [[person/budayl-ibn-warqa|Budayl ibn Umm Asram]] answered him with a long ode restoring the credit to Khuza'a; Ibn Hisham notes both compositions are not from Ibn Ishaq {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/34-35}. A long ode of apology is also told, in which its speaker clears Banu Ka'b before Budayl, saying they were not present at al-Watir — attributed to [[person/tamim-ibn-asad|Tamim ibn Asad]], though Ibn Hisham notes it is also ascribed to Habib ibn Abdullah al-A'lam al-Hudhali {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/33-34}.

## Significance
The al-Watir attack was the practical breach of the Hudaybiyya truce: the Prophet's treaty party was attacked with Quraysh's backing, making the violation open {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/32}. Directly after came [[event/khuzaa-appeal-to-medina|Khuza'a's appeal]] to Medina, then [[event/abu-sufyan-medina-mission|Abu Sufyan's failed mission]], then the march on Mecca {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/36-42}.

## For young visitors
Two tribes had an old feud. When a great peace treaty was made at Mecca, one attacked the other at night by a water well, and Quraysh helped it secretly. The defeated tribe's people went to their Prophet asking for help — and from that day the road to the conquest began.

## For Sprouts
Banu al-Dil of Banu Bakr fell on Khuza'a by night at their water of al-Watir below Mecca; Quraysh backed Banu Bakr with arms, a breach of the Hudaybiyya pact and, in the account, the spark of Mecca's conquest.
