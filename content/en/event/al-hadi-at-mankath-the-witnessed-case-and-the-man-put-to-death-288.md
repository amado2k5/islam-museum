## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] halted at [[place/mankath-yemen|Mankath]], and two days later it reached him that some of the corrupt had shown themselves at [[place/al-akhtut-yemen|al-Akhtut]] and brought out drink and disorder, in the narrator's words; he sent a party to take whichever of them were in the town, and they found only two {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.213}. When the two reached al-Hadi a group testified before him against one of them that he was given to sexual vice and brought men in to women and joined them together in it; al-Hadi ordered his neck struck and the body hung up. The testimony against the other was not established in that sense and al-Hadi sent him to prison {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.213}. A man of Mankath said, in the narrator's report: how great the distance between one who gathers to himself the people of defects and one who strikes off their necks and commands the good and forbids the wrong! Al-Hadi stayed some days at Mankath {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.213}.

## Detail
The editor's footnote says the margin of the original has a side note summarising the act as the striking off of the neck of the man so described. The report is an event the narrator gives in the voice of al-Hadi's followers and counts among his acts and penalties; the museum gives what the witnesses testified to only in general terms, does not describe the matter, passes no judgment on the penalty and does not compare it with the views of the schools. The first half of the Mankath man's saying, "one who gathers to himself the people of defects", is unclear in sense, so the museum does not interpret it and takes it only as a comment on the event. The narrator names neither man.
