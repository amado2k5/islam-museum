## Summary
In Ramadan of 619 AH the Georgians marched into the land of Arran, made for the city of Baylaqan, took it and put its people to the sword. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 432}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that the Georgians set out from their country for Arran heading for Baylaqan, which the Tatars had earlier ruined and plundered; when the Tatars moved on to the land of the Qipchaqs, the surviving inhabitants had returned and rebuilt what they could of its wall, and while they were at it the Georgians came, entered the city and took it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 432} The Muslims of those lands had grown used to the Georgians accepting a sum of money and withdrawing when they took a place — the most reasonable of enemies, as he puts it — so this time they expected the same, neither resisting strongly nor fleeing; but once the Georgians held the city they put its people to the sword and did to them what the Tatars had done — all while Uzbak ibn al-Bahlawan, lord of Azerbaijan, sat in Tabriz without stirring, as Ibn al-Athir notes. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 433}
