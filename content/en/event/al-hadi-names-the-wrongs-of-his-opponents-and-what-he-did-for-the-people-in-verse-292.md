## Overview
Al-Hadi continues the kamil poem (pp. 324-325): he addresses those he calls by his own word "the community of unbelief", which is the poet's description and not the museum's, and ascribes to them the leaving of guidance, clinging to wrong, killing people, orphaning children, taking women captive, setting themselves against the verses of the Qur'an and killing the weak, and describes the Muslims as weeping from grief and harm; then he says he did not leave jihad against them from asceticism but for want of helpers, that he longed to meet their host but the turns of the age refused him, that he called people to their God and advised, divided the subjects' wealth among them and turned back their wrongdoer so that he came back peaceable. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.324}

## Detail
On p. 324 the poet says "O community of unbelief, who took on, from their youth, obedience to Satan", and says they refused guidance and the truth, held to wrong and aggression, and disobeyed, so that they became wearers of chains of fire {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.324}. The words "community of unbelief" and what follows are the poet's description of his opponents; the museum does not adopt them and the poet names no one. He says they set on the backs of Muslims by their wrong and kept wealth for themselves, killed people, orphaned their children and took women captive, did what breaks the verses of the Qur'an, that their immorality is plain and wrong in them is "the best of religions", and that they killed the weak and left him fallen like a sheep the wolves tear {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.324}. On p. 325 he says the Muslims are in a bad state, some bare and some hungry, weeping from grief and general harm, mentions a rule that he describes in terms the museum does not repeat, and says the people were stubborn and unjust together, defied the Lord of servants with slander, took God's servants from their wealth and covered them with wrong and aggression {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.324}. He then says "alas for my soul, for lamenting only adds grief on grief at every time", "God knows I did not leave jihad against them from asceticism but for few helpers", and "I longed to meet their host, but the harsh turns of time refused me"; the footnote says manuscript S reads "the pillars" in place of "the times" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}. He says he called people to their God and advised with a true tongue, divided the subjects' wealth among them and lifted them from the pressure of hunger, and turned back their wrongdoer so that he came back peaceable, and had in mind the wronged and bewildered {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.325}. The sentences "I divided the subjects' wealth among them" and "I turned back their wrongdoer" are the poet's claims about himself, and the book gives no names or places here. It continues [[event/al-hadi-bids-the-heart-be-healed-and-describes-the-warhorse-and-the-rider-in-verse-292|the description of the horse and rider]] before it, and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-says-the-two-meanings-of-guidance-are-one-and-asks-the-addressee-to-follow-the-path-292|the poem of the two meanings]]. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
