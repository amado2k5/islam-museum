## Summary
al-Sallabi describes Ali's approach to distributing funds: treating people equally in stipends, giving no preference to lineage or ethnicity, and managing public funds with transparency even with those closest to him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 229}.

## The Principle
Whenever funds reached Ali, he would distribute them equally among the people after setting aside what needed to be spent on public facilities, taking nothing from these funds for himself {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 229}. The book holds that in doing so he was following the example of Abu Bakr {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 229}.

## Arabs and Non-Arabs
Ali once gave food and dirhams in equal measure to two women, one Arab and the other non-Arab. The Arab woman protested that she was an Arab, and he replied: "By God, I find no preference for the descendants of Ishmael over the descendants of Isaac in this public revenue" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 229}. He was asked to favor the nobles of the Arabs and Quraysh over the clients (mawali), and he said: "No, by God; even if the wealth were mine, I would distribute it equally among them" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 229}.

## The Funds from Isfahan
Funds from Isfahan were brought to Ali, and he divided them into seven sevenths; finding a loaf of bread among them, he broke it into seven pieces, placed a piece on each portion, and then drew lots among them to decide who would be given first {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 228}. In the book, this report is narrated by Asim ibn Kulayb from his father as an example of equality even in the smallest things.

## Amr ibn Salamah and Umm Kulthum
Ali appointed Amr ibn Salamah over Isfahan, and he returned bringing its funds along with wineskins containing honey and clarified butter. Umm Kulthum, daughter of Ali, asked Amr for some clarified butter and honey, so he sent her a container of each. The next day, when Ali brought out the funds to distribute them, he counted the skins and found two missing; he asked Amr about them, who at first withheld the truth and then told him, whereupon Ali sent to Umm Kulthum and took the two skins back from her {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 229}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The passage concerning the pearl that Ali saw on his daughter from the public treasury is too garbled in the available text to be transmitted faithfully; it has been left for review and is not included here.
- The book presents equality as a principle, without indicating in this passage the extent to which it was applied across different provinces; this requires an academic reference.
