## Summary
The Prophet [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] was carried by night in his body, according to the sound view, from the Sacred Mosque to [[place/jerusalem|Bayt al-Maqdis]], riding al-Buraq with Gabriel. Then he ascended from Bayt al-Maqdis through the heavens. He met the prophets in each heaven, and the prayer was enjoined on him. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.138} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.139} {cite:zad-al-maad 2/47-48}. He told his people in the morning, and their denial grew fierce. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.140}

## Its date
Views differ on the date of the Isra. Some say it was in the year he was sent as a prophet; some say five years after the mission; some say the night of 27 Rajab in the tenth year of the mission; some say sixteen months before the Hijra, or a year and two months before it, or a year before it. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.137} The book prefers that the Isra took place after the death of Khadija, because the five daily prayers were enjoined in it, and because the context of Surat al-Isra indicates that it was very late. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.137}

## In the heavens
He met Adam in the first heaven; Yahya and Isa in the second; Yusuf in the third; Idris in the fourth; Harun in the fifth; and Musa in the sixth. Musa wept, because a youth sent after him would have more of his community entering Paradise than entered from Musa's own community. He met Ibrahim in the seventh. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.138} Then he was raised to the Lote-Tree of the Boundary, and then to the Almighty, who revealed what He revealed and enjoined fifty prayers on him. He kept returning to Musa and asking his Lord, until the prayers were reduced to five. He was told that he had fulfilled his obligation and had lightened it for His servants. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.138} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.139}

## The vision
The book reports what Ibn al-Qayyim relates about the disagreement over whether the Prophet saw his Lord. It notes that seeing with the eye was not established among the Companions, that the drawing near and descent in the Isra narration differ from what is in Surat al-Najm, and that what is in Surat al-Najm refers to the drawing near of Gabriel. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.139}

## What he saw
A vessel of milk and a vessel of wine were offered to him. He chose the milk, and it was said to him: "You have been guided to the natural disposition." He saw four rivers in Paradise, Malik the keeper of the Fire, eaters of orphans' property, eaters of usury, and adulterers. He also saw a caravan of Quraysh whose affairs he learned, and he told the people of Mecca what he knew of it. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.139} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.140} When they asked him about Bayt al-Maqdis, he described it to them. Then he informed them about their caravan and the time of its arrival, and it was as he had said, which only increased their aversion. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.140}

## The reason for the journey
The book's briefest explanation of the purpose of the journey is the verse in Surat al-Isra about God showing him some of His signs. The book holds that this is God's way with the prophets, so that they become among the certain, and bear in God's cause what others cannot bear. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.141} The author sees the verses as linking the Isra to Bayt al-Maqdis and to the transfer of spiritual leadership from the Children of Israel to the community of Muhammad. He holds that the Isra took place shortly before the Hijra, or between the two 'Aqabas. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.141} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.142}

## Notes for researchers
- The date is disputed. The book does not prefer among the later views, and the event card carries the status "disputed."
- The interpretations of the purpose of the journey and of the transfer of leadership are the author's own reasoning. The museum relays them as an interpretation.

## For young visitors
One night the Prophet rode al-Buraq with Gabriel to Bayt al-Maqdis, then ascended to the heavens and saw the prophets there. On this journey the five daily prayers were enjoined on him, and they became part of the practice of the Muslims.
