## Overview
[[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] handed [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] everything that was in his hand, cash, camels, horses, weapons and furnishings which, in the narrator's phrase, he and others had gathered from God's property; al-Hadi took it and offered Abu al-Atahiya a share in some of his affairs, and he said: I do not want that, Commander of the Faithful, but let me be a servant before you {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.210}. He had built a house on an estate of his and withdrew into it, wearing wool and showing, in the narrator's words, abstinence and austerity {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.210}.

## Detail
The narrator says he heard [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]], whom he names Abu al-Qasim Muhammad ibn Yahya, say: Abu al-Atahiya came in to us one day and I saw his face changed, and I said to him: why do you do this to yourself when you need yourself? Eat. He answered: no, by God, son of the Messenger of God, not until this flesh I have carried from the unlawful is gone, then I will eat {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.210} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.211}. He adds that it reached him that Abu al-Atahiya said: by God, if I had come out of this affair I was in with a woollen cloak to wear, I would think it better for me {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.211}. Both are the narrator's reports of what Abu al-Atahiya said and of what al-Hadi's son told him; the museum does not rule on the narrator's description of Abu al-Atahiya's wealth or asceticism and relays only what is said. The naming of Abu al-Qasim Muhammad ibn Yahya here supports linking him to al-Hadi's son.
