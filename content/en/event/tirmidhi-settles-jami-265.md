## Overview
Al-Tirmidhi settled back at his town after completing his scholarly journey and devoted himself to authorship and revision {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 451}.

## The Jami' and his books
He composed the Jami' known as the Sunan of al-Tirmidhi — one of the Six Books — with 3,956 traditions over fifty well-ordered chapters, appending the Kitab al-Ilal al-Saghir as a statement of his method in grading hadith sahih, hasan and gharib, an integral part and gateway to the Jami' {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 451-452} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}; he presented it to the critics of the Muslim East who approved it, holding that whoever had this book had, as it were, a Prophet speaking in his house {cite:mawsuat-a3lam-ulama-adaba p. 452}. He also gathered the Kitab al-Shama'il al-Muhammadiyya on the Prophet's characteristics {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 579}.
