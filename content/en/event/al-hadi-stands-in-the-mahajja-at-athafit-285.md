## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Al-Hadi]] was near the main track in front of the centre; when he saw [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am]] and his men about to cut off his companions on [[place/al-kitd-athafit-yemen|al-Kitd]] he had only seven horsemen with him, among them [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-futayimi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn al-Futayimi]] and some of his companions; he went forward with them and stood in the middle of the track. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} The account is Zaydi and in the voice of the imam's followers; the museum gives it as the narrators tell it, without a verdict.

## The charges on al-Hadi
Seeing that al-Hadi had only seven horsemen, al-Da'am and his men took advantage of their fewness and hoped to seize the chance, and charged him with a hundred horse and a thousand foot; al-Hadi did not budge from his place and held firm. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.102} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.103} One of his companions, whom the narrator does not name, says he was with him there and that al-Da'am and his men charged again and again, hoping because of their few numbers, but he did not move and took no account of them; a man of theirs on a driven horse came to shoot at him and hit his armour and horse armour with fifteen arrows. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.103} He adds that he was told some of them marvelled at his courage as he faced a hundred horse and a thousand foot. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.103}

## The account of Sa'id ibn Abi Sura
The narrator relates from [[person/said-ibn-abi-sura|Sa'id ibn Abi Sura]], who was with al-Da'am and was one of Yemen's counted horsemen, that he chose thirty horsemen from al-Da'am's army, men of mettle, angered by al-Hadi's standing and his unconcern for them; he shamed them and urged them to charge, and they charged as one man, hoping to take him off his horse with their lances. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.103} He said they charged until they were close, then their hands stiffened on their lances and stayed unmoving, God cast awe and fear in their hearts, and they turned back; he knew it came from God and pledged to God never to fight him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.103} The narrator adds that he worked his way gently to safety until he joined al-Hadi and served well in his rule. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.103} The narrator takes this from a man who had stood on the opponent's side; it is given attributed to its transmitter, and the museum neither affirms nor denies it.

## Al-Hadi's own words
The narrator Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Sulayman says he heard al-Hadi say that no fear had entered his heart from them and he took no account of them, because he knew God was with him, and that if he was killed it was to Paradise, and if he killed one of them that one went to the Fire. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.104} A man was facing him and throwing stones at him from near; al-Hadi told one of his companions to take an arrow and shoot the man, drew an arrow from his armour, the man shot and hit him below the navel in the belly, and he fled in the worst state. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.104}

## The narrator's comment
The narrator heard people marvel at al-Hadi's stand that day, calling it fierce and awesome, one through which God made the truth firm and abased falsehood; he counts it among the signs of the imamate in al-Hadi and a proof of it, because a coward in such a place would have fled and the people been killed, whereas al-Da'am and his army charged him so that he would flee and leave his companions. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.104} This is the Zaydi narrator's reasoning; the museum gives it as his and does not adopt it, and sets no other view beside it here because the book offers none. ## The date
It is the same day as the one before, Friday 14 Shawwal 285 AH, by the narrator's sequence.
