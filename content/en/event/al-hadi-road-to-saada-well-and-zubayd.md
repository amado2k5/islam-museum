## Overview
After [[event/al-hadi-departure-from-al-fur-for-yemen|leaving al-Fur']], [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] and his party reached [[place/al-suwariqiyya|al-Suwariqiyya]]; they had wanted a way out through Turaba and Bisha, found it hard, and turned back. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.38} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.39} They then passed a clan of the Arabs, [[group/banu-muawiya-ibn-harb|Banu Muawiya ibn Harb]], and some of them went out with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.39}

## The small well
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] says that severe thirst overcame them until they were worn out; they stopped at night and some companions went looking for water, and one found among thick trees a small well, wrapped about by trees on every side so that it could hardly be found by day, with plenty of sweet water, and they drank and watered their animals. He asked the Bedouin with them whether they knew this water or had heard of it, and they swore they had neither seen nor heard of it; he told his son: "this was the greatest of signs and the clearest of proofs". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.39} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.40} The judgement is the narrator's own. In his preface the editor compares the story with the cave in the prophetic emigration. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.9}

## The journey and its teaching
He says that at every halt al-Hadi would go off from them for a while and then weep and sob for Islam and for the community, and he would call his companions, exhort them and teach them the rules of their religion. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.40}

## The encounter at Zubayd
They came to a land the machine-read text gives as "Zubayd", where it is unclear whether a place or a tribe is meant; when its people saw them they shouted at them, met them in large numbers and closed the road on every side. [[person/yusuf-ibn-muhammad-al-hasani|Yusuf ibn Muhammad al-Hasani]] told al-Hadi that they had closed in and that he should shorten his prayer; al-Hadi did not turn, and prayed as it should be prayed, then put on his arms and mounted while the crowd was upon them throwing missiles. Al-Hadi, whom the narrator here calls Yahya ibn al-Husayn, charged them; a stone was thrown at his face, and he struck one man and threw him down and took a number with his lance, while the rest fled, God having cast fear in their hearts. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.40} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.41} The phrasing is the narrator's. A marginal note in the manuscript says this was the first man al-Hadi killed with his own hand and that he captured a number of the troublemakers; that is the annotator's wording. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.41}

When his companions asked to kill the captives he disliked it; he went on with them part of the day, then ordered them clothed and sent back, dealt kindly with them and sent them to their clans, and the party went on safely to Sa'da. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.41} See [[event/al-hadi-arrival-at-saada-284]].
