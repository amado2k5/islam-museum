## Overview
The narrator says: [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] stayed at Sana'a until the day of sacrifice, then the men of [[group/banu-yafur|Al Ya'fur]] and [[group/banu-tarif|Al Tarif]] gathered their armies, sent to the people and to those of their districts and deceived them with the claim that they had agreed with al-Hadi that he would leave [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]] to them and go to a land whose name is printed with doubtful letters, so that they might attend and pray the festival with them at Sana'a {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.234}. A great army gathered to them, which the narrator says was twenty thousand, and they marched through their night until morning found them on the parade ground of Sana'a, and they sent forward a large force that they brought in from the side of al-Surad (Ghayat al-amani has al-Sirar); al-Hadi sent out part of his army in their faces and drove them out where they came in, sent out a left wing by the Darb al-Qutay' and went out himself with the rest of his army by the Darb Zamarid {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.234}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi drew up the Hamdanis on the right and the Madhhijis on the left and took the centre; his army was little over a thousand if it was that. The men charged his right and left, which gave way, and they followed until their horse were behind al-Hadi; seeing it, he went forward before his companions, told them to follow him, charged those before him and routed them, and his right and left came back and drove the men until they reached a place the narrator calls al-Sabr {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.234}. The ranks then became mixed; al-Hadi halted his men and restored the first order, and sent one of his servants called al-Sa'di al-Ahyamar, who cried out to Ibn Khalaf: why do the people fight, it is between me and you, come out to me; if you win against me you will relieve the unbelievers of me, and if I win against you I will relieve the believers of you; and Ibn Khalaf held back in the rear of his army {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.234}. The words unbelievers and believers are the narrator's own and the challenge is as he gives it; the museum draws no conclusion from them. Ibn Khalaf is most probably [[person/ibrahim-ibn-khalaf-ibn-tarif|Ibrahim ibn Khalaf ibn Tarif]], though the text does not give the full name here. The figure of twenty thousand is given in the passive, it was said. It leads to [[event/the-night-of-safety-after-the-maydan-battle-and-its-breaking-288]].
