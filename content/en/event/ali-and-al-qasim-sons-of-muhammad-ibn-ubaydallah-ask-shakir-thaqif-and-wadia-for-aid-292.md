## Overview
Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah had sent his two sons Ali and al-Qasim to al-Hisn to be with [[group/shakir-hamdan-tribe|Shakir]]; when the news of Banu al-Harith reached them they came to their father, greeted him and took his orders {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. This Ali may be the narrator Ali ibn Muhammad, but the book does not say so, and the link to [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]] is probable only.

## Detail
He told them to meet Shakir, Thaqif and Wadi'a and ask them to help him with whoever they could, since Banu al-Harith were their near neighbours; they went to al-Hisn and told Shakir and Thaqif what their father had bidden {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. They agreed, but said they could not feel safe for their place if all went out, so half would go with them and half stay, and they divided into halves, and told the sons to go to [[group/wadia-tribe-yemen|Wadi'a]] and make their return to them, until they came out with them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. The two went until they met Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik and Muhammad ibn Rabi'a of Wadi'a and asked for help; the Wadi'a men disliked going out by night and said they would go out with them at dawn, God willing {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.283}. The Thaqif here seem to be those of Najran and are not tied to the record of the Ta'if tribe; Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik is tied to no other man of similar name. "Neighbours of the morning" in the text means those near them.
