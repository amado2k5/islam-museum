## Overview
The sources report that the battle of Fahl, in the land of Jordan, took place two nights before the end of Dhu al-Qa'da, five months after the caliphate of Umar ibn al-Khattab. The commander of the people was [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]]. It is part of the [[event/conquest-of-syria|conquest of Syria]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 158}

## The cause
al-Baladhuri relates that when [[person/heraclius|Heraclius]] reached Antioch he called up the Byzantines and the people of the Jazira, and sent over them a man of his household. They met the Muslims at Fahl. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 158}

## The fighting and the peace
The fighting grew fierce until the Muslims prevailed. The Byzantine patrician and about ten thousand men with him were killed. The rest scattered among the cities of Syria, and some joined Heraclius. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 158}

The people of Fahl fortified themselves, and the Muslims besieged them until they asked for safe-conduct in exchange for paying the jizya for their heads and the kharaj for their land ([[concept/jizya|jizya]]). They were granted safety for themselves and their property, on condition that their walls not be demolished. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 158}

## Differing reports
Abu Ubayda gave the safe-conduct, though it is also said that [[person/shurahbil-ibn-hasana|Shurahbil ibn Hasana]] did. al-Baladhuri says that a letter appointing Abu Ubayda over Syria was written to him, and that the amirs were ordered to be with [[person/amir-ibn-abi-waqqas|Amir ibn Abi Waqqas]]. Some say the appointment reached him while the people were besieging Damascus, and he kept it from Khalid for some days. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 158}
