## Summary
Ali moved the capital of the caliphate from Medina to Kufa because Quraysh would not support him in his pledge of allegiance {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.75}.

## Reasons as Presented by the Book
Al-Aqqad states that Quraysh was divided into Hashimite, Umayyad, Taymite, and Adawite factions, and that each faction had its own ambition or calculation, so they did not agree on Ali's pledge of allegiance, and he found no supporter among them {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.75}. He adds that the entire Hijaz was filled with those complaining for religious or worldly reasons, the governors who had gathered during Uthman's reign, and those coveting governorships {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.75}.

## Kufa as Capital
Al-Aqqad describes Kufa as a meeting place of peoples of every kind, a hub of trade between India, Persia, Yemen, Iraq, and Syria, and a center of culture in which schools of writing, language, Quranic readings, and genealogy flourished {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.127}. Consequently, in his view, it was the most fitting capital for a universal leadership in that land {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.127}.

## The Author's Reading
Al-Aqqad links the choice of Kufa to the idea of a leadership that unites peoples rather than tribal solidarity (asabiyya), for a worldly state is reinforced by tribal solidarity, whereas the religious caliphate is reinforced by brotherhood among peoples {cite:abqariyyat-ali-aqqad p.125}. This is the author's interpretation of the move, not a text from a historical source.
