## Summary
Sultan al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun, wearying of the senior amirs' tutelage, abdicated in 708 and installed himself as governor of al-Karak while Baybars al-Jashankir took power in Cairo; five months later the loyalist al-Nuwayri joined him at al-Karak in Rabi' II 709 (September 1309) and returned to Cairo only with the sultan when he regained the throne at the end of Ramadan 709 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 pp. 157-158}.

## Loyalty repaid
The Encyclopaedia of Islam counts this among the clearest strokes of his character: he sided with al-Nasir during Baybars II's interlude, holding him the only legitimate sultan — and was repaid after the restoration when the sultan's steward Ibn Abbada brought him into close service of the sovereign {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 158}.
