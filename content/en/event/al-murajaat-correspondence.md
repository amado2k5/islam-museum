## Summary
Al-Muraja'at: 112 letters attributed to Abd al-Husayn Sharaf al-Din al-Musawi and Salim al-Bishri, Shaykh of al-Azhar, published 1936 on the succession, the Imamate and hadith authority. {cite:al-islam-murajaat}

## The attribution question
The book appeared decades after al-Bishri's death with no external manuscript to secure it; many Sunnis deny its authenticity and hold it a one-sided composition. The museum describes this dispute without deciding it, presenting the work as the most famous modern Shia–Sunni debate text.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Sayyid Abd al-Husayn Sharaf al-Din (the Shia correspondent)**: opened with the case for succession by designation, not election — he put the Ghadir, Manzila and Thaqalayn reports from Sunni sources, asked after each report's chain and standing, then built on the answers link by link: if the report is sound, what follows?
- **Shaykh Salim al-Bishri (the Grand Imam of al-Azhar, as the book reports him)**: answered each question by weighing chain and text — accepting the virtue reports he found sound while turning the imamate argument to interpretation: texts of merit do not amount to an explicit designation of rule; and he defended the Companions' ijtihad and the soundness of consultation.
- **The letters' exchange (as the book presents it)**: the argument cycles through three grounds — designation texts (Ghadir, Manzila), reason (the need for an infallible guide), and the conduct of the Companions at the Saqifa — ending with a letter the book presents as al-Bishri's acceptance of the case.
- **The Sunni caveat (the museum's note)**: Sunni scholarship holds the letters were published decades after al-Bishri's death without external manuscript witnesses, and that the answers attributed to him do not follow an Azhari method of reply; the text is therefore classed as composed disputation by Sunni critics, however documented its events are held by Shia readers. {cite:al-islam-al-murajaat} {cite:mahajjah-al-murajaat-critique}
