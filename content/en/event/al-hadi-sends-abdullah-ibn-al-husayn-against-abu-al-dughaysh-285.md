## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Al-Hadi]] stayed at [[place/saada|Sa'da]] through Rabi' I and twelve days of Rabi' II of 285 AH; then letters came from his governor at [[place/wasaha-yemen|Wasaha]], "Abu Jafar Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah al-Alawi", saying that [[person/abu-al-dughaysh-al-shihabi|Abu al-Dughaysh al-Shihabi]] had gathered a large force and withheld the alms, that he had fought him, and asking for reinforcements. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.85} Al-Hadi told the people to go out and camped at a village called al-Buqma near Sa'da; he sent men to call Abu al-Dughaysh to God and to join the truth, and he refused and held back. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.85} The account is Zaydi, and the harsh description of Abu al-Dughaysh is the narrator's, not the museum's.

## The sending of Abdullah ibn al-Husayn
He sent his brother [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] against him with a large force, setting out on Thursday the sixteenth of Rabi' II as far as "Saqayn"; there a group of Abu al-Dughaysh's cousins, of Shihab, met him and asked him to hold back from fighting until they could ask Abu al-Dughaysh to accept the truth, and he did so and went on to Wasaha. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.86} "Abu Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah" met him there with a large army of those of Khawlan al-Qudum who obeyed him; he had no formed army himself but ten servants at his door. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.86}

## Envoys and warning
Abdullah ibn al-Husayn sent to Abu al-Dughaysh asking him to return to the truth; he disliked it and the envoys went again; Abu al-Dughaysh then sent: "Send me some men from the notables of your army to consult, and give me security, and I will come in with them"; he did so, sent a few who admonished him, gave him security and made him swear to hear and obey; some of Abu Muhammad's companions asked leave to trick Abu al-Dughaysh and seize him, but he forbade it, wishing to establish God's proof against him; Abu al-Dughaysh said he would meet Abu Muhammad next day, was waited for and did not come, and answered the messenger with a declaration of war. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.86} Abu Muhammad then sent to the village where Abu al-Dughaysh lived, known as "Afara" or "Mafara" in the sources, calling them to God three times and they refused, so when he had established the proof against them he told the army to prepare for war. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.86} See [[event/battle-of-afara-285]].
