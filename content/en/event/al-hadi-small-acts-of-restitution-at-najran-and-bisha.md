## Overview
The book gives short reports on [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s care over other people's property, carried in chains from Ali ibn Muhammad through [[person/muhammad-ibn-sulayman-al-kufi|Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Kufi]] and through [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.63} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.64} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.65} It is a Zaydi book in which narrators praise their imam; the museum does not state the praise as its own.

## Aubergines at Najran
In the house al-Hadi lodged in at [[place/najran|Najran]] there was an aubergine bush; after the family left and he moved in, the servants were slow to water it and he told them to water it; it bore aubergines, and he got up himself, cut them with his own hand, counted them and sent them by a servant to the people of the house. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.63}

## Eggs at Bisha
They lodged in a house at [[place/bisha|Bisha]] whose owner's hens laid in a place the household did not know; al-Hadi's servants found the eggs and told him, and he ordered them taken to the owner, which was done. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.64}

## Soldiers at a door
One day at Najran he rode out with a great crowd; some of the soldiers stopped at a door asking for water; he saw them, went to them himself, shouted at them and struck some with his hand, from the saddle. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.64} The text gives no reason, and none is supplied here.

## Paper and jars
A servant wanted to stop a flask of oxymel with a scrap of paper, and al-Hadi said "this paper is from the tithe and not lawful for us", and the servant threw it away. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.64} Muhammad ibn al-Hajjaj (given the affiliation "al-Khaywani" in one place and "al-Yarsami" in another, so not entered as a person) says the treasurer gave him the price of paper he bought for al-Hadi at [[place/khiwan|Khiwan]], and a small dirham was left from which he bought drinking jars; al-Hadi said the price of the paper was from the tithe and not lawful for us, and returned the jars. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.64} Ali ibn Abi Anbasa, by the narrator's account, was told by al-Hadi to buy him a separate paper to write on himself. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.65}

## The needle of the armour
Al-Hadi gave a coat of mail to a man to make for him, and the man died before he finished; his family sent the mail to al-Hadi, and the narrator found in it a needle the man had been working with and said he supposed it belonged to the dead man; al-Hadi told him to keep it until he sent it to the family, and he gave it to the man's son-in-law, who gave it to them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.64} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.65}
