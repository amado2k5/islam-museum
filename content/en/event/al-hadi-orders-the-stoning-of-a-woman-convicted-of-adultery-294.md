## Overview
Witnesses testified against a woman and she confessed to earlier marriages. Al-Hadi had the hadd of a hundred lashes applied and then ordered her stoned; the book gives the procedure in full.

## Detail
The book reports that al-Hadi asked the witnesses whether they had seen the act directly; they said they had. He then asked the woman about her earlier marriages, and she confessed to having married men who later divorced her. The hadd of a hundred lashes was applied, following the rule in [[passage/quran-24-2|Surat al-Nur]]. A pit was dug and she was buried to her breasts. When she spoke of repentance from inside the pit, al-Hadi ruled that repentance no longer availed her. There were four stones, each about half to one rotl in weight. The imam threw the first stone, the Alids and the people followed, and the body was then buried. The editor's footnote notes that a confession after the testimony does not cancel the hadd. This is the narrator's account and the museum does not endorse it.
