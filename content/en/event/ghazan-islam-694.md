## Overview
Al-Suyuti reports that [[person/ghazan-khan|Ghazan ibn Arghun ibn Abagha ibn Hulagu]], king of the Tatars, embraced Islam in 694 AH, the people rejoiced at it, and Islam spread in his army. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.731}

Ibn al-Imad, in "Fragments of Gold", quotes Sadr al-Din Ibrahim ibn Hamawiyya al-Juwayni that Ghazan became a Muslim at his hand through his deputy Nawruz, and that it was a day to be witnessed. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.437}

The "Fragments" also says that Egypt saw in the same year a severe dearth and a plague in which people ate carrion, and that the funerals reached one thousand five hundred in a single day. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.437}

The account of [[person/ghazan-khan|Ghazan]] is drawn from these two books alone, and the detail needs an additional source.
