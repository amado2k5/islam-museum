## Summary
Al-Suyuti mentions that [[person/ghazan-khan|Ghazan ibn Arghun ibn Abagha ibn Hulagu]], king of the Tatars, embraced Islam in 694 AH. The people rejoiced at this, and Islam spread in his army. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.731}

Ibn al-Imad, in "Shadharat al-Dhahab," quotes Sadr al-Din Ibrahim ibn Hamawiya al-Juwayni that Ghazan embraced Islam at his hand through his deputy Nawruz, and that it was a memorable day. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.437} In the Arna'ut edition the report is given under the year ninety-five. It mentions that Ibn Hamawiya came to the hadith scholar al-Hafiz al-Dhahabi in Syria seeking hadith, and told him of this. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.748}

The Shadharat also mentions that Egypt suffered a severe famine and a plague in the same year, in which people ate carrion, and that the funerals reached one thousand five hundred in a single day. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.437}

The account of [[person/ghazan-khan|Ghazan]] is drawn from these two books alone, and the detail needs an additional source.
