## Summary
At [[place/dhu-tuwa|Dhu Tuwa]], or at the entrance of Mecca, [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]] led his blind, aged father [[person/abu-quhafa|Abu Quhafa]] to the Prophet to declare his Islam; the Prophet received him with humility and honour and ordered his white hair changed {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/48}.

## What happened
Abu Quhafa was an old, blind sheikh who on the day of the conquest watched the army's arrival from Mount Abu Qubays with his youngest daughter; hearing the dark mass of horse and its neighing he said: "daughter, the army has spread out, by God" — and they came down {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/48}.

Abu Bakr took him by the hand and brought him to the Prophet. Seeing him, the Prophet said: "why did you not leave the sheikh in his house until we could come to him ourselves?" Abu Bakr answered: "Messenger of God, it is more fitting that he walk to you than that you walk to him" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/48}. The Prophet sat him before him, stroked his chest, and he became Muslim. His head was white as a tuft of thaghama, and the Prophet said: "change this hair of his and keep it from black" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/48}.

## For young visitors
Abu Bakr brought his old, blind father to the Prophet to declare his Islam. With all gentleness and respect for his age, the Prophet said: it would have been more fitting for us to go to him at his house and spare him the walk.
