## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] left by night by the village's Yemeni gate until he came to al-Hisn; [[person/abdullah-ibn-bustam|Ibn Bustam]] had been writing to him asking him to go to [[place/minas-najran|Minas]], and he did not answer, knowing that he meant to betray him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.231}. When he reached al-Hisn he stayed there and sent to [[person/rabi-ibn-abi-al-rukud|Rabi' ibn Abi al-Rukud]], who brought him his womenfolk and children, and he wrote to [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] telling him what Banu al-Harith had done, and al-Hadi told him to stay where he was until he came to him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.231} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.231}. Banu al-Harith then raided after his leaving Najran, taking the property of the weak and poor and the town's tax of dates, wheat and maize, and gave it to the Bedouin and sinners who gathered to them, in the narrator's words {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.231}.

## Detail
The narrator says that when Ibn Bustam saw his error and knew that al-Hadi would not leave him alone, he offered the Bedouin of Shakir money to kill Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah or some of his sons or to drive him out of al-Hisn; the Bedouin of Shakir raided him with eight hundred foot (the manuscript S has one hundred), he fought them and the people of al-Hisn of Shakir and Thaqif stood with him and told them there was no way to drive him out, so they came back to him, excused themselves and told him what Ibn Bustam had offered them, and he accepted their excuse and stayed ten months where he was {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.231}. Strife fell between Banu al-Harith in particular and Yam and Shakir in general, so that they killed one another until their dead among themselves were one hundred and six people, and trouble covered them and enemies surrounded them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.231}. The words error and sinners and the motives given to Ibn Bustam are the narrator's, which the museum gives attributed and does not affirm. The count of the dead of the strife is the narrator's. The account follows [[event/banu-al-harith-besiege-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-at-al-hajar-288]]; the narrator then returns on p. 231 to the war of Sana'a ([[event/the-raid-on-the-gate-of-sanaa-during-the-fitr-sermon-and-the-sortie-to-alab-288]]).
