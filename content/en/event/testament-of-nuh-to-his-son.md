## Summary
In both Stories of the Prophets and al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya, Ibn Kathir gives a hadith whose chain he calls sound, though the authors of the six books did not include it; in it the Prophet relates that when death came to [[person/nuh|Nuh]] he said to his son: 'I command you two things and forbid you two.' {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 123} The same section of the first book also discusses Nuh's age and the place of his grave. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 121}

## The setting of the hadith
The narrator, Abdallah ibn Amr, begins with a scene before the Prophet: a man from the desert came in a robe trimmed with silk brocade and said that this companion of yours brings down every horseman's son and raises every shepherd's son. The Prophet seized the collar of his robe and said he saw on him the dress of one who does not reason, and then recounted Nuh's testament. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120} The testament is thus told here in answer to an attitude of pride in lineage and standing. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 123}

## The testament and its content
Ibn Kathir relays from Ahmad, with the chain given, that Nuh said to his son: I am telling you a testament; I command you two things and forbid you two. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120} The first command is 'there is no god but God', and Nuh described its weight: were the seven heavens and seven earths placed in one pan and that phrase in the other, it would outweigh them. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120} The second is 'glory be to God and His praise', which Nuh said is the prayer of all things and the means by which creation is given its provision. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120} The two prohibitions are associating partners with God and pride. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120}

The hadith goes on that Abdallah ibn Amr, or someone present, asked: we know what shirk is, so what is pride? Is it that one of us has two fine sandals, or a garment he wears, or a mount he rides, or companions who sit with him? To each the Prophet answered 'no', and then said pride is 'rejecting the truth and looking down on people'. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120}

## The chains
Ibn Kathir says this chain is sound and that they, the compilers of the books, did not include it. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120} He adds that al-Tabarani transmitted it through Abd al-Rahim ibn Sulayman from Ibn Ishaq from Amr ibn Dinar from Abdallah ibn Amr, in the wording 'in Nuh's testament to his son was: I charge you with two traits and forbid you two'; that al-Bazzar transmitted it from Abdallah ibn Umar ibn al-Khattab; and that it appears to be from Abdallah ibn Amr ibn al-As, as Ahmad and al-Tabarani have it. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 121}

## His age and his grave
Ibn Kathir notes that the People of the Book claim Nuh was six hundred when he boarded the ark, that Ibn Abbas is reported to have said the same and added that he lived three hundred and fifty years afterwards, and that he finds this open to objection, saying that if it cannot be reconciled with what the Qur'an indicates about his long stay among his people before the Flood it is plainly wrong. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 121} He adds that if Ibn Abbas's report is preserved, that he was sent at four hundred and eighty and lived three hundred and fifty years after the Flood, his whole life would be one thousand seven hundred and eighty years. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 121}

On the grave, Ibn Kathir reports from Ibn Jarir and al-Azraqi, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Sabit or another Successor in a mursal report, that Nuh's grave is in the Sacred Mosque, and says this is stronger and better established than what many later authors say, that it lies in a town in the Bekaa known today as Karak Nuh, where a mosque is said to have been built for that reason. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 121} The edition's footnote says Karak Nuh is a large village near Baalbek whose people hold that Nuh's grave is there. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 121} For the report that Nuh carried Adam and Hawwa in a coffin to Bayt al-Maqdis, see [[event/testament-of-adam-to-shith|Testament of Adam to Shith]].

## Sources and documentation
This page rests on Ibn Kathir's Stories of the Prophets and al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya, which agree on the text of the hadith and its chains. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 120} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 123} I found no text of the testament in the other sources I read, so no further details are attributed to Nuh.
