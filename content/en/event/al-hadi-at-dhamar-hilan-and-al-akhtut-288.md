## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] went on until he halted at [[place/dhamar|Dhamar]], sent into its districts, and its people gathered to him; he admonished them and told them what was due to them and from them, and stayed at Dhamar some days until, in the narrator's words, God set the town right {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.212} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.213}. The people of Hilan came to him and he sent with them a man of his companions called Muhammad al-Balkhi; he left Dhamar and put [[person/ibrahim-ibn-jafar-al-futayimi|Ibrahim ibn Jafar al-Futayimi]] over it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.213}. He went on until he halted at [[place/al-akhtut-yemen|al-Akhtut]], and its people gathered to him and he admonished them and told them what was due to them and from them; the place was corrupt, the narrator says, because [[person/ibrahim-ibn-khalaf-ibn-tarif|Ibrahim ibn Khalaf]] had been living there and vice was open in it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.213}.

## Detail
When al-Hadi reached al-Akhtut a great many of the people of corruption left it, and the women of ill repute who lived there with Ibrahim ibn Khalaf, in the narrator's words; al-Hadi stayed there some days and went on to [[place/mankath-yemen|Mankath]] {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.213}. Muhammad al-Balkhi is named in prose only and is not entered as a person. The text recognition dropped the first line of p. 213; Apple Vision, run on the page image, recovers it, and it holds the stay at Dhamar, the coming of the people of Hilan and al-Balkhi's name. What the narrator charges to the people of al-Akhtut and to Ibrahim ibn Khalaf is his account; he names none of them and gives no particulars, and the museum does not repeat his words of abuse or give a verdict. The editor does not locate al-Akhtut.
