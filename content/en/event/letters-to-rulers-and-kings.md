In the last part of the sixth year after the hijra, once the Hudaybiyya truce had laid down the war with Quraysh and secured the southern front, the Islamic call turned to addressing the kings and princes of the world to convey the universal message. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Prophet had a silver seal made engraved in three lines — "Muhammad" a line, "Messenger" a line, "God" a line — and chose eight senior Companions of wisdom and eloquence, sending them out within a short span with the letters of invitation. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

He sent [[person/amr-ibn-umayya-al-damri|Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri]] to Ashama the [[person/negus-of-aksum|Negus]] of Abyssinia, who honoured the letter, became Muslim and sent the emigrants' two ships; [[person/hatib-ibn-abi-baltaa|Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a]] to al-Muqawqis, ruler of the Copts in Egypt, who gave a near-answer and gifted the Prophet two servant-girls (Mariya and Sirin), a mule and a robe; and [[person/abdullah-ibn-hudhafa-al-sahmi|Abdullah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi]] to Khosrow of Persia, who tore the letter — whereupon the Prophet prayed that his kingdom be torn utterly. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He sent [[person/dihya-al-kalbi|Dihya ibn Khalifa al-Kalbi]] to Heraclius, emperor of the Romans, who summoned Abu Sufyan in Syria and questioned him about the Prophet, who attested his truthfulness — Heraclius nearly became Muslim but for fear of his bishops over his kingdom; [[person/al-ala-ibn-al-hadrami|al-Ala ibn al-Hadrami]] to al-Mundhir ibn Sawa in Bahrayn, who became Muslim; [[person/salit-ibn-amr|Salit ibn Amr]] to Hawdha ibn Ali in al-Yamama; [[person/shuja-ibn-wahb|Shuja' ibn Wahb]] to al-Harith al-Ghassani at Damascus; and [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] to the two kings of Oman, who both became Muslim and their lands embraced Islam willingly. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
