In the last part of the sixth year after the hijra, once the Hudaybiyya truce had laid down the war with Quraysh and secured the southern front, the Islamic call turned to addressing the kings and rulers of the world to convey the universal message. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

The Prophet had a silver seal made, engraved in three lines: "Muhammad" on one line, "Messenger" on one, and "God" on one. He chose eight senior Companions, men of wisdom and eloquence, and sent them out in quick succession with the letters of invitation. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

He sent [[person/amr-ibn-umayya-al-damri|Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri]] to Ashama, the [[person/negus-of-aksum|Negus]], king of Abyssinia, who honoured the letter, became Muslim and sent the two ships of the emigrants. He sent [[person/hatib-ibn-abi-baltaa|Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a]] to al-Muqawqis, the chief of the Copts in Egypt, who gave a conciliatory reply and sent the Prophet two slave-girls (Mariya and Sirin), a mule and a robe. He sent [[person/abdullah-ibn-hudhafa-al-sahmi|Abdullah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi]] to Khosrow, king of Persia, who tore up the letter; the Prophet prayed that his kingdom be utterly torn apart. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

He sent [[person/dihya-al-kalbi|Dihya ibn Khalifa al-Kalbi]] to Heraclius, emperor of the Romans. Heraclius summoned Abu Sufyan in Syria and questioned him about the Prophet; Abu Sufyan attested to his truthfulness, and Heraclius came close to becoming Muslim, were it not for his fear of his patriarchs and for his kingdom. He sent [[person/al-ala-ibn-al-hadrami|al-Ala ibn al-Hadrami]] to al-Mundhir ibn Sawa in Bahrain, who became Muslim; [[person/salit-ibn-amr|Salit ibn Amr]] to Hawdha ibn Ali in al-Yamama; [[person/shuja-ibn-wahb|Shuja' ibn Wahb]] to al-Harith al-Ghassani in Damascus; and [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] to the two kings of Oman, who both became Muslim, and whose lands embraced Islam willingly. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}


## Ibn Hisham's account (volume 4)
Ibn Hisham reports that after the Farewell Pilgrimage the Prophet had sent envoys from among his Companions to the kings, with letters inviting them to Islam. He relates, on the authority of a transmitter he trusted, from Abu Bakr al-Hudhali, that the Prophet came out to his Companions one day after the umra from which he had been barred at Hudaybiyya, and said: "People, God has sent me as a mercy and for all the worlds, so do not differ over me as the disciples differed over Jesus son of Mary." He explained to them that Jesus had called people to the same thing he had called them to; the one sent to a near place was content, while the one sent far away was reluctant, so he complained of that to God, and the reluctant ones then each began to speak the language of the people he had been sent to. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/254} He then names the envoys: [[person/dihya-al-kalbi|Dihya ibn Khalifa al-Kalbi]] to Caesar, king of the Romans; [[person/abdullah-ibn-hudhafa-al-sahmi|Abdullah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi]] to Khosrow, king of Persia; [[person/amr-ibn-umayya-al-damri|Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri]] to the Negus, king of Abyssinia; [[person/hatib-ibn-abi-baltaa|Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a]] to al-Muqawqis, king of Alexandria; [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] to Jayfar and Iyad, the two sons of al-Jalanda al-Azdi, kings of Oman; [[person/salit-ibn-amr|Salit ibn Amr]], of the clan of Amir ibn Lu'ayy, to [[person/thumama-ibn-uthal|Thumama ibn Uthal]] and Hawdha ibn Ali of the Banu Hanifa, kings of al-Yamama; [[person/al-ala-ibn-al-hadrami|al-Ala ibn al-Hadrami]] to [[person/al-mundhir-ibn-sawa|al-Mundhir ibn Sawa al-Abdi]], king of Bahrain; and [[person/shuja-ibn-wahb|Shuja' ibn Wahb al-Asadi]] to al-Harith ibn Abi Shamir al-Ghassani, king of the Syrian frontier. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/254} Ibn Hisham adds that Shuja' ibn Wahb was also sent to [[person/jabala-ibn-al-ayham|Jabala ibn al-Ayham al-Ghassani]], and that [[person/al-muhajir-ibn-abi-umayya|al-Muhajir ibn Abi Umayya al-Makhzumi]] was sent to al-Harith ibn Abd Kulal al-Himyari, king of Yemen, and that it was he who traced the lineages of Salit, Thumama, Hawdha and al-Mundhir. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/255}

Ibn Ishaq relates, on the authority of [[person/yazid-ibn-abi-habib|Yazid ibn Abi Habib al-Misri]], that he found a document naming those whom the Prophet had sent to the lands and to the kings of the Arabs and the non-Arabs, and what he had told his Companions when he sent them. He sent it to [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]], who recognised it. It contains the Prophet's words: "God has sent me as a mercy and for all mankind, so convey from me, may God have mercy on you, and do not differ over me as the disciples differed over Jesus son of Mary." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/255} Ibn Ishaq lists those whom Jesus sent out from among the disciples and followers: Peter, with Paul, to Rome; Andrew and Matthew to the land whose people eat people; Thomas to the land of Babylon; Philip to Carthage, which is Africa; Yuhannis to Ephesus, the town of the people of the cave; Ya'qubus to Urashlim, which is Aelia, Jerusalem; the son of Talma' to the Arabs, which is the land of the Hijaz; Simon to the land of the Berbers; and Judas, who was put in the place of Judas Iscariot. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/255} The editors confirm that two manuscripts read "Thalmali", and that the older parts of the Sira end at that point in the nineteenth section. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/255}

## Notes on the transmission
- The account of the disciples from Abu Bakr al-Hudhali is introduced with "it reached me", and Yazid ibn Abi Habib's book has similar wording. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/254} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/255}
- The recipient differs: al-Harith ibn Abi Shamir in Ibn Ishaq's account, Jabala ibn al-Ayham in Ibn Hisham's. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/254} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/255}
- Ibn Hisham does not give the date of the mission in this passage; he says only "after the umra from which he was barred". Al-Baladhuri places it in the year 7 AH. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/254} {cite:ansab-al-ashraf p.531}

## For Sprouts
In late 6 AH, after the Hudaybiyya truce, the Prophet sent eight Companions with letters of invitation to the world's kings — on a silver seal ring engraved 'Muhammad Messenger of God' in three lines. Some entered Islam — the Negus, al-Mundhir ibn Sawa, the kings of Oman; al-Muqawqis came close; Heraclius nearly did; and Khusraw tore the letter.

## For Explorers

The Prophet took a silver seal engraved in three lines 'Muhammad Messenger of God' and chose eight senior Companions of wisdom and tongue, sending them close together with the letters: Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri to the Negus, who honored the letter, entered Islam and sent the emigrants' ships; Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a to al-Muqawqis, chief of the Copts, who answered warmly and gifted two maids, a mule and a garment; Abdullah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi to Khusraw of Persia, who tore the letter; Dihya ibn Khalifa al-Kalbi to Heraclius, who nearly embraced it but for his patricians; al-Ala ibn al-Hadrami to al-Mundhir ibn Sawa, who entered Islam; Salit ibn Amr to Hawdha ibn Ali; Shuja' ibn Wahb to al-Harith al-Ghassani; and Amr ibn al-As to the kings of Oman, who embraced it willingly with their land.

## For Scholars
In late 6 AH, after the Hudaybiyya truce ended the war with Quraysh and secured the southern front, the Islamic call turned to addressing the kings and princes of the world to convey the universal message. The Prophet took a silver seal engraved in three lines — 'Muhammad', 'Messenger', 'God' — and chose eight senior Companions of wisdom and tongue, sending them close together: Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri to Ashama the Negus of Abyssinia, who honored the letter, entered Islam and sent the emigrants' two ships; Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a to al-Muqawqis, chief of the Copts in Egypt, who answered warmly and gifted the maids Mariya and Sirin, a mule and a garment; Abdullah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi to Khusraw of Persia, who tore the letter — the Prophet praying his kingship be torn piecemeal; Dihya ibn Khalifa al-Kalbi to Heraclius emperor of Rome, who summoned Abu Sufyan in Syria, heard him affirm the Prophet's truth and nearly embraced but for fear of his patricians; al-Ala ibn al-Hadrami to al-Mundhir ibn Sawa in Bahrain, who entered Islam; Salit ibn Amr to Hawdha ibn Ali in Yamama; Shuja' ibn Wahb to al-Harith al-Ghassani in Damascus; and Amr ibn al-As to the kings of Oman, who embraced willingly with their land. Ibn Hisham records the Prophet's charge to the envoys — 'God sent me as a mercy to all; do not differ over me as the Apostles differed over Jesus' — and names further envoys, to Jabala ibn al-Ayham and al-Muhajir ibn Abi Umayya to the Himyarite king.
