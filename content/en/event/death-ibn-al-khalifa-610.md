## Summary
In 612 AH, on the twentieth of Dhu al-Qa'da, the caliph's younger son, titled al-Malik al-Mu'azzam and named Abu'l-Hasan Ali, died, and the caliph grieved for him as none had heard of before. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 365}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that al-Mu'azzam was the caliph's most beloved son; he had designated him heir apparent and removed his elder brother for his sake, and he was generous, charitable and beneficent, of good conduct, loved by elite and common alike; the cause of his death was diarrhea. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 365} The caliph's grief was such that he wrote to the provincial governors forbidding them to send condolence envoys, read no letter and heard no message, and secluded himself in sorrow; the dead man was carried out by day with all the people walking before his bier to his grandmother's tomb near the grave of Ma'ruf al-Karkhi, where he was buried; great wailing was heard from within the tomb — said to be the caliph's voice — and lamentation continued in every quarter of Baghdad night and day. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 365} His death coincided with the arrival of Mankli's head at Baghdad: the procession had been sent out to meet the head and all the people went forth, but as they entered with it the news of the son's death broke and the head was taken back; Ibn al-Athir comments that this is the way of the world — its joy is never free of sorrow. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 366}
