## Overview
al-Baladhuri relates that [[person/iyyad-ibn-ghanm|Iyad ibn Ghanm]] came down at al-Raqqa, and its people fought him until some of the Muslims were wounded. He went round the city, posted watch-posts at its gates and sent out raiding parties. After five or six days the patrician of the city sent asking for safe-conduct. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.238}

## The treaty
Iyad granted safe-conduct to all its people for their lives, their families, their property and their city, and left the land in their hands in exchange for the kharaj. He placed the jizya on their necks: a dinar on each man each year, together with measures of wheat, and some oil, vinegar and honey. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.238}

## The letter of safe-conduct
He wrote them a letter of safe-conduct for themselves, their property and their churches, which were not to be demolished or inhabited, on condition that they paid the jizya and did not introduce a new church or chapel, nor show a bell or a cross. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.239} In another report, each adult male owed four dinars, and al-Baladhuri holds that the more firmly established account is that Umar wrote this to Umayr ibn Sa'd. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.239}

See [[concept/dhimma|dhimma]] and [[concept/jizya|jizya]]. This conquest is part of the [[event/conquest-of-jazira|conquest of al-Jazira]]. The date is approximate and derived.
