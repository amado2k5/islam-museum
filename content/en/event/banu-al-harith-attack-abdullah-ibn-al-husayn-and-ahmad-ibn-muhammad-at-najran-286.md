## Overview
Under the heading "Abdullah ibn al-Husayn's coming to Najran from the Hijaz", [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]] relates that when al-Qusayb opposed al-Hadi and Ibn al-Dahhak opposed Abu al-Qasim, Banu al-Harith stirred up disorder against the imam, with [[person/abdullah-ibn-bustam|Ibn Bustam]] at the head of it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145} Al-Hadi had sent [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] to the Hijaz to some elders; he came to [[place/najran|Najran]], put right what was amiss and gathered its affairs, and the town grew calm. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145}

## The plan
[[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] gathered and took counsel about falling on Abu Muhammad Abdullah ibn al-Husayn and on Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Alawi, who was governor of the town, wishing to seize them as hostages against those whom al-Hadi had taken and jailed at Sa'da on his first journey. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145} Abu al-Husayn is probably the governor of the record [[person/ahmad-ibn-muhammad-governor-of-najran|Ahmad ibn Muhammad]]; the book does not say so.

## The attack
On the night with seventeen days of Ramadan 286 gone they all fell on the house, unnoticed until they were inside; a man of Madan called [[person/ibn-musaffa-ibn-ibrahim-al-madani|Ibn Musaffa ibn Ibrahim]], cousin of Ali ibn Rabi', had opened it to them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145} They took the mounts of Abu Muhammad and Abu al-Husayn and of Abu Jafar [[person/muhammad-ibn-isa-al-tamimi|Muhammad ibn Isa al-Tamimi]], who was staying in the town, and what was in the lower part of the house. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.145} Abu Muhammad learned of them after they entered, while he was at the night prayer, called Abu al-Husayn, his companions and servants, some ten-odd men, and fought them on one side while his companions fought on another. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.146}

## The fighting to dawn
He fought hard until a nephew of Ibn Humayd called [[person/al-tahir-ibn-al-tahir|al-Tahir ibn al-Tahir]] threw a stone that felled him and he fell senseless; Banu al-Harith feared his place, then grew in number, closed in and charged until his party massed, and wounded their enemy; three of the Madanis with them were killed. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.146} When [[group/banu-abd-al-madan-najran|Banu Abd al-Madan]], who were with the two men, saw their companions killed and Banu al-Harith prevailing, they asked leave to go to their homes, fearing destruction; the two agreed and went with them, and then called to their clients and fought Banu al-Harith hard until morning, when Banu al-Harith gave up on the two men and withdrew. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.146} Abu Muhammad and Abu al-Husayn stayed in the village until dawn, afraid of a party with [[person/al-abras-al-madani|al-Abras al-Madani]] and his nephew Ali ibn Rabi', who had entered the house with Banu al-Harith and shared in plundering it; al-Abras's son took a slave-girl of Abu al-Husayn. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.146}

## Notes
The narrator says individuals wished the two men harm; that is the narrator's description, not the museum's judgement. The index of the book lists al-Tahir ibn al-Tahir at p. 146 and Ali ibn Rabi' at pp. 145 and 146 among other pages, and Ibn Musaffa ibn Ibrahim at p. 145.
