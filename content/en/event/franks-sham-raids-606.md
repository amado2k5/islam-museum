## Summary
In 606 AH the Franks of Tripoli and Hisn al-Akrad raided Homs and its wilaya until they invested the city; its lord appealed to al-Zahir of Aleppo, and al-Malik al-Adil marched out of Egypt, made terms with the lord of Acre and campaigned in the land of Tripoli. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 341}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that the Franks of Tripoli and Hisn al-Akrad intensified their raids on Homs and invested the city; its lord Asad al-Din Shirkuh lacked the strength to resist and called on the kings of Syria — only al-Zahir Ghazi of Aleppo sent troops who kept the Franks off his wilaya. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 341} Then al-Malik al-Adil left Egypt with large armies, made for Acre, whose Frankish lord made peace on terms including freeing Muslim captives; he moved to Homs, camped by Lake Qadas where the eastern and Jaziran armies joined him, entered the land of Tripoli, besieged and took al-Qulay'at by treaty and demolished it, advanced on Tripoli plundering, burning and taking captives, and returned to Lake Qadas. Envoys shuttled over peace without agreement; winter came, the eastern armies went home and al-Adil wintered in Damascus. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 341} The cause of his march from Egypt was that the Cypriots had seized ships of the Egyptian fleet and captured their crews; when he demanded them from Acre's lord, the latter pleaded they belonged to the Franks of Constantinople; after Cyprus reverted to Acre's lord, al-Adil demanded them again, got no settlement and marched — whereupon Acre's lord complied and sent the captives. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 342}
