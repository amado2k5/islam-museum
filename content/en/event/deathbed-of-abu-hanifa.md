## Summary
[[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa al-Nu'man ibn Thabit]] died at Baghdad; most of those whom al-Khatib al-Baghdadi quotes put his death in 150 AH, when he was seventy. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 583} {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 453} He was buried in the Khayzuran cemetery on the eastern side of Baghdad. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 1 p. 445} The sources read for this record report no written will from him at death, but they preserve reports of his illness, his washing, his funeral and the deposits of other people that he left.

## Context: the judgeship and prison
Al-Khatib relates that al-Mansur summoned Abu Hanifa from Kufa to Baghdad to make him judge; he refused, al-Mansur swore he would do it, Abu Hanifa swore he would not, and he was sent to prison. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 450} Al-Khatib notes that common people claim he counted bricks for some days to atone for his oath, then says this is not sound in transmission and that the correct view is that he died in prison. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 450} Al-Dhahabi quotes al-Saymari that he did not accept the office, was beaten and jailed, and died in prison. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1585} In what Abbas al-Duri was told about al-Mansur, the caliph offered him the judgeship of al-Rusafa, he refused and was threatened with the whip, then sat as judge for two days and no one came; on the third a coppersmith came and he settled the case; two days later he fell ill, was ill six days, and died. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 451} {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 452}

## What he left: deposits
Al-Khatib relates from Muhammad al-Mas'udi from his father: "I never saw a better trustee than Abu Hanifa; on the day he died he held deposits of fifty thousand, and not one dirham was lost." {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 491} Yusuf al-Samti relates that al-Mansur granted him thirty thousand dirhams in installments; he said he was a stranger in Baghdad with no place for them and asked that they be put in the treasury, which al-Mansur allowed; when Abu Hanifa died, people's deposits were taken out of his house and al-Mansur said, "Abu Hanifa has deceived us." {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 491} {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 492} Al-Dhahabi says his son [[person/hammad-ibn-abi-hanifa|Hammad]] held many deposits whose owners were absent and took them to the judge to receive; the judge asked him to keep them, but Hammad insisted they be weighed and taken over so his father's account would be cleared; the judge spent days weighing and counting them and Hammad went into hiding until the judge placed them with a trustworthy man. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1585}

## Washing and prayer
Isma'il ibn Hammad relates from his father: "When my father died we asked al-Hasan ibn Umara to wash him and he did; when he had washed him he said: God have mercy on you and forgive you; you had not broken fast for thirty years, nor laid your right side on the ground by night for forty, and you have wearied those after you and shamed the reciters." {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 485} In another report al-Hasan ibn Umara and another man washed him. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 585} Sulayman ibn Abi Shaykh says al-Hasan ibn Umara prayed over him as judge of Baghdad in 150. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 584} Ahmad ibn Abd Allah al-Aslami relates from al-Hasan ibn Yusuf that prayers were said over Abu Hanifa six times on the day he died because of the press of people, the last led by his son Hammad. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 585} Ibn Kathir likewise says prayers were said six times at Baghdad. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 492}

## The grave
Describing the cemeteries of Baghdad, al-Khatib says the eastern side has the Khayzuran cemetery, holding the grave of Muhammad ibn Ishaq, author of the biography, and the grave of Abu Hanifa. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 1 p. 445} It is the oldest cemetery on the eastern side, named for al-Khayzuran, mother of Musa and Harun, sons of al-Mahdi. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 1 p. 448} Abbas al-Duri says his grave is in the Khayzuran enclosure, on the left after two or three graves as one enters by the Qattanin gate. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 452} Al-Khatib relates that al-Shafi'i said: "I seek blessing through Abu Hanifa and come to his grave every day; when I have a need I pray two rak'as, go to his grave and ask God for it there, and it is not long before it is met." {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 1 p. 445} Al-Dhahabi says a great dome and splendid shrine stand over him at Baghdad {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1585}, and Ibn Kathir says his grave at Baghdad is well known. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 492} None of these sources reports a will from him about the land of his burial, and this record says nothing of one.

## How contemporaries received his death
Rawh ibn Ubada says he was with Ibn Jurayj in 150 when news of Abu Hanifa's death came; he said the formula of return, expressed grief and said: what knowledge has gone! {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 463} Different words are reported from others: al-Awza'i is said to have remarked at his death, "Praise be to God, he used to undo Islam knot by knot" {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 548}; and Sufyan al-Thawri, told at Mecca that Abu Hanifa had died, is said to have answered: praise be to God who spared me what He afflicted many people with. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 585} Al-Khatib gives these as the words of those named, and the museum does not weigh between them.

## Where the reports differ
Year of death: 150 for most of al-Khatib's narrators, 151 for Yahya ibn Ma'in, 153 for al-Makki, and Ibn Ufayr says Rajab. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 584} Ibn Kathir prefers the first. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 492} On how many times he completed the Qur'an in the place where he died: seven thousand in al-Khatib {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 485}, seventy thousand in Ibn Kathir. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 492} On the cause of death: imprisonment and death in jail, or six days' illness after judging. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 450} {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib-gharb vol. 15 p. 452}

## The creed text attributed to him
A creedal letter known as the "Wasiyya," addressed to his student Yusuf ibn Khalid al-Samti, is attributed to Abu Hanifa. The museum's record of [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] reports, from the Encyclopaedia of Islam, that this Wasiyya is not his, that it reflects the ethos of Iranian courtiers, and that it cannot be imagined as the work of a specialist in law. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 143} This record relies not on the encyclopaedia's own text but on what the museum's record quotes from it. The museum also holds a preliminary card titled [[work/sharh-wasiyyat-abi-hanifa|Sharh Wasiyyat al-Imam Abi Hanifa]] whose sources are not yet checked; this record does not rely on it and concerns his death, not that letter.
