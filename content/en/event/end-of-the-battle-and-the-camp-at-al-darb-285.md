## Overview
After al-Hadi's long stand in the main track and the heavy shooting of arrows between the two sides, this, by the narrator's account, harmed none of al-Hadi's companions. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.104} The account is Zaydi and in the voice of the imam's followers; the museum gives it as the narrators tell it, without a verdict.

## Ibn al-Ghimr and the end of the day
A horseman of [[person/al-daam-ibn-ibrahim|al-Da'am's]] side called Ibn al-Ghimr came out against al-Hadi's men and was overbearing; God gave them the victory and they killed him, and none of al-Hadi's men was killed; fighting went on until the dark came and the late evening prayer time, then they parted, one man of al-Da'am's side having been killed and al-Hadi's men being safe. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.104} Each side went back to its camp with many wounds on both, al-Hadi to the [[place/al-darb-banu-rabia-yemen|Darb]] of Banu Rabi'a and al-Da'am to [[place/athafit|Athafit]], and each kept to his camp, since the cold had grown sharp and the winds many. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.104}

## Al-Hadi's words at al-Darb
Al-Hadi stayed at al-Darb, a place of little crops and little water, and often said, as the narrator tells it, that had the people obeyed him and been patient with him he would not have missed fighting "these wrongdoers" for a single day in heat or cold until he joined God or God gave him victory over them; he wished never to get down from his saddle, night or day, except for the time of prayer, until God made the truth appear through him or he joined Him; and God is the one asked for help against the people's weakness, their small increase and their frailty in upholding the truth. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.105} The word "wrongdoers" is the narrator's description of al-Hadi's opponents and is attributed to him.

## Water and supplies
When the people heard that al-Hadi had gone to his place they brought food, fodder and everything the army needed, and water increased there by God's kindness, by the narrator's account; the people watered their animals from a ghayl in a rough place of little water. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.105} The narrator heard some of Banu Rabi'a say that the ghayl had held only a little water, but when al-Hadi came it grew until crops were sown by it, and he saw the crops being watered from it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.105} The editor's footnote says a ghayl is what runs over the ground, a flow; the narrator says in the speech of the Hijaz the word means a spring. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.105} The increase of water is the narrator's report from the people of the place and is attributed to them.

## The request to raid Arhab's villages
Al-Hadi stayed where he was, and Banu Rabi'a and Banu Sarim came to him asking him to stay there and release them to fight and to raid Arhab's villages in al-Subay' and Khurfan, because the people of al-Subay' and Khurfan were of Arhab's party and had been with Arhab when he entered Athafit and killed Abu Umar, with whom there had been a group of Banu Rabi'a and Banu Sarim, whose weapons and clothes they took. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.105} Banu Rabi'a were seeking Arhab's side for three men killed in the fighting with al-Hadi, and kept asking him to let them raid and plunder the villages; he refused, saying that if they did he would begin by fighting them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.105} He often exhorted them and turned them from what they hoped for, and they accepted from him and did not go against him; at times they would say to him that the others had killed them and taken their goods, and he would say "yes", and there the sentence breaks at the page end and finishes on the next page, which this batch has not yet read. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.105}

## The date
The book gives no day for this stay; it follows 14 Shawwal 285 AH, so no date is recorded.
