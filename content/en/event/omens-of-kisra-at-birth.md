Al-Salihi al-Shami, quoting Ibn Jarir and others, reports that Khosrow Parviz had sealed the dam of the Great Dujla and built the arch of his palace, and that he had soothsayers, sorcerers and astrologers. When [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] was born, the arch of his kingdom split without any weight on it, and the Great Dujla burst open. {cite:subul-huda-salihi p. 353}

Kisra summoned his soothsayers, and they saw that a matter had come to them from heaven. One of them, al-Sa'ib, reported a lightning flash from the direction of the Hijaz that had reached the East, and imagined that a ruler would come out of the Hijaz and reach the East. The source relates that the soothsayers concealed from Kisra that he was a commissioned prophet, and advised him to rebuild; the dam and the arch collapsed again and again. {cite:subul-huda-salihi p. 353–354}

These reports belong to the portents (irhasat) that the biographical books record. al-Salihi transmits them from al-Tabari and others, and they are not presented here as established fact. They are read alongside [[event/year-of-the-elephant-and-birth|the Year of the Elephant and the Prophet's birth]].


## In al-Raheeq al-Makhtum (p. 54)
Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum mentions these portents in summary at the birth: fourteen battlements fell from Kisra's arch, the fire of the Magians went out, and the churches around Lake Sawa collapsed after its water had receded. It refers to a report of al-Bayhaqi, then notes that Muhammad al-Ghazali does not accept these reports. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.54} {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi} {cite:fiqh-al-sira-al-ghazali p.46} It also mentions what Ibn Sa'd reports from the Prophet's mother about a light by which the palaces of Syria were illuminated, and what Ahmad reports from al-Irbad ibn Sariya to the same effect. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.54} {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad 1/63} {cite:musnad-ahmad} The author presents them in the form "it is reported" and records the disagreement about them, and the museum presents them in the same way.
