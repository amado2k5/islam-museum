## Overview
al-Baladhuri, quoting Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam, relates a letter that [[person/al-awzai-abd-al-rahman|al-Awza'i]] wrote to the governor [[person/salih-ibn-ali|Salih ibn Ali]], arguing against the expulsion of the protected non-Muslims (ahl al-dhimma) from Mount Lebanon. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 222}

## Background
People on Mount Lebanon rose up and complained about the tax collector of Baalbek. When Salih ibn Ali learned that the Byzantines were supporting them, he sent men who killed their fighters, left the rest of them in their religion, and returned them to their villages. He expelled some of the people of Lebanon. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 222}

## The letter
al-Awza'i wrote a long letter, of which al-Qasim ibn Sallam preserved what al-Baladhuri transmits. In it he says that some of those expelled from among the protected non-Muslims had not supported the rebels. He asks how the common people can be held to account for the sins of the elite, so that they are driven from their homes and property. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 222}

al-Awza'i grounds his letter in the judgement of God and in the Prophet's charge concerning covenanted non-Muslims (mu'ahadun). He reports that he would be the opponent of anyone who wrongs a covenanted non-Muslim or burdens him beyond his capacity. The report contains further words that were not transmitted. No verse text is written here; its location needs an additional source. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 222}

## Meaning
The letter bears on the rulings of [[concept/dhimma|dhimma]]. In al-Awza'i's view it includes the principle that there is no collective punishment for an individual sin. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 222}
