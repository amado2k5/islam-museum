## Summary
The bursting of the [[building/marib-dam|Marib Dam]] in 450 or 451 CE, according to The Sealed Nectar's estimate. It was the great flood that the Qur'an mentions as the flood of al-Arim, a major event that led to the ruin of settlement and the scattering of peoples. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}

## Background
After [[place/yemen|Yemen]] gained its independence from the [[event/first-abyssinian-occupation-of-yemen|first Abyssinian occupation]] in 378 CE, breaches began to appear in the Marib Dam, until the great flood came. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} The dam had begun to be built in the time of the mukarrib kings of [[polity/kingdom-of-saba|Saba]], and it has great importance in the history of Yemen. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}

## Its effect
In the chapter on the peoples of the Arabs, the author states that the migration of most clans of [[group/kahlan|Kahlan]] from Yemen took place shortly before the flood of al-Arim, when their trade failed. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16} He also states that the flood was a major event that led to the ruin of civilisation and the scattering of peoples. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an mentions the flood of al-Arim in Surat Saba ([[passage/quran-34-16|Saba 16]]). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}

## Notes for researchers
The dating is the author's estimate, drawn from Tafhim al-Qur'an and the history of the land of the Qur'an. He notes the wide disagreement among the sources in fixing the years. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25} The academic dating of the dam's collapses from inscriptions has not yet been added.

## For young visitors
Yemen had a great dam that held back the water. When it broke, a huge flood poured out and ruined the land, and many tribes emigrated from Yemen to the rest of the Peninsula.
