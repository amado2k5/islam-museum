## Summary
The Day of Marj Halima: al-Mundhir al-Aswad ibn al-Mundhir ibn Ma' al-Sama set out seeking vengeance for his father against al-Harith al-A'raj al-Ghassani. The two men camped at the meadow. Al-Harith ordered the people of his villages to prepare food in large bowls from which the fighters would eat, and the war dragged on for days without a victory. Then al-Harith offered tribute and said: I am sending the heads of the tribes to settle the matter. He had a hundred young men, or according to some eighty, put on weapons under their clothes, and ordered his daughter Halima to perfume them. When they gathered before the king, they revealed their weapons and killed the nobles. Labid ibn Amr fought until he was killed, saying that people must not say a hundred men had routed him. Then the men of Iraq were put to flight. The genealogists differ about the two days, and Ibn al-Athir favoured the view that the one killed was al-Mundhir ibn Ma' al-Sama and that his son died in al-Hira. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 409-412}
