## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Al-Hadi]] looked for a man who "was among those who made disorder and innovated, and had fled"; he rode to the village where the man lived, called [[place/rajla-najran|Rajla]], and "sought him and sent to him, and he disliked it and gave no answer, so he pulled down his house". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.130} The editor says al-Hamdani's Sifat Jazirat al-Arab, p. 124, writes it "Rajla" without the final letter. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.130} It is a Zaydi narrator's account; the act is described as the narrator tells it, as a punishment, and the museum gives no ruling on it.

## Dividing the crop and the palms
Al-Hadi went outside the town looking for the man's crop; they showed him land held by the man, his brother and partners, with a maize crop in it, and he told some people to divide it; when it was divided he ordered what fell to the man to be pulled up, and the people pulled it up. He then asked whether the man had palms and was told he and his partners had; he ordered them divided, and when he knew the man's share he ordered it cut, and it was cut, "a little over twenty palms". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.130} He then went back, entered the village and settled matters between the people. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.130}

## The precedent cited
The narrator says al-Hadi cited that "the Messenger of God did the like when he cut the palms of Banu al-Nadir", and that the verse sent down then is the one in Surat al-Hashr, [[passage/quran-59-5|al-Hashr 59:5]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.130} That is the imam's argument as the book gives it; the museum does not take a position on it. See [[group/banu-nadir|Banu al-Nadir]].

## Notes
The book does not name the man, his brother or his partners, and gives no measure of the crop pulled up. Straight after, the narrator turns to a "great sign" worked by the imam at Najran, which is told in [[event/reports-of-a-sight-restored-and-a-tongue-loosed-at-najran-286]].
