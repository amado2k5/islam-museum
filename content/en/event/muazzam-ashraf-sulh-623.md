## Summary
In 623 AH al-Malik al-Mu'azzam, lord of Damascus, and al-Malik al-Ashraf, lord of the Jazira and Akhlat, made peace after the quarrel that arose among al-Adil's sons following his death. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 468}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir first gives the cause of the rift: when al-Malik al-Adil Abu Bakr ibn Ayyub died, his sons the kings agreed well among themselves — al-Kamil Muhammad of Egypt, al-Mu'azzam Isa of Damascus and Jerusalem and its environs, and al-Ashraf Musa of the Jazira and Akhlat — uniting to drive the Franks from Egyptian lands, a union that preserved the lands of Islam and gladdened all. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 468} Then al-Ashraf travelled to his brother al-Kamil in Egypt, passing by his brother al-Mu'azzam in Damascus without taking him along, and stayed long in Egypt; the Mu'azzam marched on Hama and besieged it, until his two brothers wrote from Egypt and moved him away against his will, which deepened his estrangement — it was said he was told they had agreed against him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 468} To this was added that the caliph al-Nasir li-Din Allah took offence at al-Kamil for what his son the lord of Yemen had done at Mecca in slighting the amir of the Iraqi hajj, so he shunned him and his brother al-Ashraf for their alliance and cut them off, wrote to Muzaffar al-Din Kukburi of Erbil knowing his defection from al-Ashraf and won him over; afterward the peace between al-Mu'azzam and al-Ashraf was settled as he narrates. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 468}
