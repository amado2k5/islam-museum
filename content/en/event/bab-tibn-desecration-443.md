## Overview
In Safar 443 the Sunni-Shi'a strife in Baghdad reignited when the Karkh finished the Bab al-Samakin gate inscribed "and upon the best of mankind"; the Sunnis demanded the removal of "hayya 'ala khayr al-'amal," and the Bab al-Sha'ir quarter cut Karkh's water until a waterskin sold for a qirat. The Tuqtaqi gathered roughs from Darzijan, stormed Taq al-Harrani, killed two men and set their heads on the new mosque's wall; the Qala'in attacked the gate and burned the stalls; and after three more were killed he crucified two on the gate and flung the heads at Karkh crying "breakfast on your heads." The city rose in arms; the Bab al-Tibn shrine was seized, looted, and corpses exhumed and burned — among the ugliest scenes of fifth-century sectarian violence in Baghdad {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi}.
