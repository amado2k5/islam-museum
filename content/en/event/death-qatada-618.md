## Summary
In Jumada al-Akhira of 618 AH, Qatada ibn Idris al-Alawi al-Husayni, amir of Mecca, died; his son Hasan succeeded him, and in those days the amir of the Iraqi pilgrimage was killed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 426}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that Qatada was about seventy; his rule had stretched from the borders of Yemen to the Prophet's city, he held the fortress of Yanbu', his armies and mamluks were many, and the Arabs of those lands feared him greatly; his rule began well — he removed the corrupt slaves, protected the country and treated pilgrims well — then he turned to bad conduct, renewed the mukus dues at Mecca and looted the pilgrimage in some years. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 426} On his death his son Hasan succeeded; another son, Rajih, contested him and offered the pilgrimage's amir Iqbash, a mamluk of the caliph al-Nasir, money for himself and the caliph to help him take Mecca; when they arrived Hasan came out with large crowds and fought them, the amir of the hajj advanced alone and Hasan's men surrounded and killed him and his army broke; Hasan sent his turban as a token of safety so the pilgrims were not plundered, admitted them to Mecca for ten days, and they returned safe; the matter grieved the caliph, but Hasan's envoys apologized and he was pardoned. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 426}
