## Summary
'Is Jesus God?' — the Royal Albert Hall, London 1985; the first of the two Deedat–Shorrosh encounters. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}

## The line of the debate
The motion confined itself to the deity of Christ: Deedat tracked Gospel wording to show the absence of an explicit formula, Shorrosh built on scriptural testimony. The transcript was printed as a booklet circulating widely in both Muslim and Christian worlds.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Deedat**: nowhere in the Gospels does Jesus declare 'I am God' — he stressed the missing explicit formula and read 'son' and 'Lord' in their wider Semitic idiom, not as divine essence.
- **Shorrosh**: built on New Testament texts — Christ's titles, his being worshipped, his divine attributes (knowing the unseen, forgiving sins, judgement) — arguing the text as a whole proclaims deity even without a single formula.
- **Deedat (rebuttal)**: worship and titles belong to widened Hebrew honorifics, and what is ascribed to Jesus of delegation answers delegation, not self-subsisting deity.
- **Shorrosh (rebuttal)**: held that the text's doctrinal sense is the early church's own proclamation and that his exegesis preserves the deity.
- **The verdict**: the audience split along the debaters; the printed booklet became the disputation's schoolbook text. {cite:answering-islam-debates-index}
