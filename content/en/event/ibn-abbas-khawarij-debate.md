## Summary
The most famous intra-Muslim debate of the first generation: [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] walks into the Khawarij camp at Harura, argues them down with the Qur'an and the Prophet's precedent, and two thousand of them return. {cite:musnad-ahmad 1/342-343} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya 7/283-284}

## The sending — the transmitted narration
Ubaydullah ibn Abi Rafi', a freedman of the Messenger of God and [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]]'s scribe, narrates: when the Khawarij gathered at Harura and seceded from Ali after the arbitration at [[place/siffin|Siffin]], Ali came and spoke to them and they would not return, so he sent Ibn Abbas. Ibn Abbas said: I came to them in the finest dress of the people of the House — a red turban. They said: welcome, Ibn Abbas, what is this turban? I said: what divides us is good; I come from the cousin and son-in-law of the Messenger of God, on whom revelation descended, and they know its interpretation better than you — I put my case to you and you argue back. Some of them turned to each other and said: do not argue with Quraysh, for God says 'they are a quarrelsome people.' One or two said: we are not forbidden to speak to him — so he spoke to them {cite:musnad-ahmad 1/342-343}.

## The disputation — everything said and its sayer
**Ibn Abbas**: I said: what do you resent? **They said**: three things. **I said**: what are they? **They said**: first, he let men arbitrate in God's religion, though God says 'judgement belongs to God alone.' **I said**: that is one. **They said**: he fought without taking captives or spoils — if those he fought were God's friends he had no right to fight them, and if God's enemies, captivity and plunder were lawful. **I said**: that is two; what is the third? **They said**: he struck 'Commander of the Faithful' from his name — if not the believers' commander he is the unbelievers' commander. **I said**: do you have anything else? They stopped there {cite:musnad-ahmad 1/343}.

**Ibn Abbas — the first answer**: I said: if I recite to you from God's decisive Book and report from your Prophet's sunna what you cannot refute, will you return? They said: yes. I said: as for your claim that he let men arbitrate in God's religion — God said 'you who believe, do not kill game while consecrated; whoever of you kills it deliberately shall pay the like of what he killed in cattle, as two just men among you judge it.' God made men's judgement over a rabbit or the like worth a trifling price — is the judgement of men over the blood of Muslims and the mending of relations not worthier than their judgement over a rabbit? And for a wife estranged from her husband God said 'if you fear a breach between them, send an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from hers; if both wish reconciliation God will bring them together' — men's judgement is sunna. Have I brought you out of this or not? They said: no {cite:musnad-ahmad 1/343}.

**The second answer**: as for your saying he fought without captives or spoils — would you take captive your mother Aisha and make lawful against her what is lawful against others, when she is your mother? If you say she is not your mother you have disbelieved in her — and if you say she is, God has forbidden her captivity to you. I have set you between two choices; have I brought you out of this or not? They said: no {cite:musnad-ahmad 1/343}.

**The third answer**: as for your saying he erased himself from Commander of the Faithful — I bring you one you accept: you heard that on the day of al-Hudaybiyya the Messenger of God made peace with Suhayl ibn Amr and Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and told Ali: write, Ali, 'this is what Muhammad the Messenger of God agreed.' The polytheists said: by God, we do not know you are God's Messenger; had we known, we would not have kept you from the House. The Messenger of God said: God, You know I am Your Messenger — erase it, Ali, and write 'this is what Muhammad son of Abdallah agreed.' By God, the Messenger of God was better than Ali, and he erased his own name without erasing himself from prophethood. Have I brought you out of this or not? They said: no {cite:musnad-ahmad 1/343} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya 7/283}.

## The return and the verdict
Two thousand of them returned and fought beside Ali; six thousand held out, rose against the Uthmaniyya, and the affair of al-Nahrawan took its course {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya 7/284}. The debate thus stands as the model of arguing with the Qur'an itself against those who raised its slogan.
