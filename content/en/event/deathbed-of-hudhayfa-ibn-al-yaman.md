## Summary
[[person/hudhayfa-ibn-al-yaman|Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman]] is quoted by al-Dhahabi as saying he knew better than anyone every trial that would come between him and the Hour. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1376} The historical and biographical books describe his last hours at al-Mada'in: what he said to his visitors, his instruction about his shroud, his weeping and his charge to Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari.

## Background
Ibn Abd al-Barr says Hudhayfa died in 36 AH after Uthman's killing, early in Ali's caliphate, or in 35 by another report, the first being more sound; his death came after the news of Uthman's killing reached Kufa and he did not live to see the Camel. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 138} Ibn Asakir reports from Ishaq that he used to come from al-Mada'in to Kufa, and had a fine mule. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106}

## His last hours and what he said
Ibn Asakir relates from Khalid ibn Rabi' al-Absi that they heard of Hudhayfa's illness and Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari rode to al-Mada'in with a party including him; they reached him at some hour of the night and he asked what hour it was. In the middle of the night, they said. He said: I seek refuge in God from a morning that leads to the Fire. Then he asked: have you brought my shrouds? Yes, they said. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106} In a report from Ziyad, freedman of Ibn Abbas, from someone who visited him: were I not sure this is the last day of the world and the first of the next I would not say this; O God, You know I preferred poverty to wealth, lowliness to honour and death to life; a beloved who came in need; he who regrets does not prosper. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 282} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 221} In al-Hasan's version, when death came he said: a beloved come in need, he who regrets does not prosper; praise be to God who let the trial, its leaders and its crowds, pass before me. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 1 p. 282} Ibn Asakir gives his words: O God, I did not love the world for digging rivers or planting trees but for sleepless nights, thirsty noons, much bowing and prostration, remembrance, and striving in God's cause. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 107} Asked in his illness what he desired he said: Paradise. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106}

## His instruction about the shroud
Ibn Asakir relates that Hudhayfa told those who had brought the shrouds: do not be extravagant over my shroud; if your companion has good with God, He will give him better than your cloth in exchange, and if not it will be stripped from him quickly. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106} Then he said: nothing is to shroud me but two white sheets. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106} Ibn Asakir quotes al-Khattabi that when the shroud was brought he said: if your brother meets good, perhaps; if not, regret will be great in its corners; al-Khattabi explains the 'corners' as the two sides of the grave. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 107}

## His weeping and charge to Abu Mas'ud
Ibn Asakir relates that when death came on Hudhayfa he was greatly distressed and wept bitterly. They asked: why are you weeping? He said: I do not weep in regret for the world, death is dearer to me; but I do not know what I am coming to, approval or anger. There was a day when death came to me and I had no doubt, but today I am mixed up with things and do not know where I stand in them. Then he said: turn me to face the qibla; they did so. He charged Abu Mas'ud: keep to what you know and do not be slack in God's command. Then he said: this is the last hour of the world; O God, You know I love You, so bless me in meeting You; and he died. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106} The report has him mention Uthman, saying: O God, I did not approve, did not witness and did not kill. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106}

## His instruction to his sons and burial
Ibn Abd al-Barr says his sons Safwan and Sa'id were killed at Siffin, having pledged allegiance to Ali at their father's instruction. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 138} The books read do not name the site of his grave; all they say is that he died at al-Mada'in, where Abu Mas'ud came to him. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 2 p. 106} See the record of [[place/al-madain|al-Mada'in]].

## Where the reports differ
The accounts of his illness combine reports with different chains: Khalid ibn Rabi's, in which Abu Mas'ud is present; Ziyad's, from an unnamed visitor; and al-Hasan's; each preserves part of his words. His death year is 36, the sounder in Ibn Abd al-Barr's view, or 35. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 138} The books read do not say what Hudhayfa left, nor who washed him or prayed over him.
