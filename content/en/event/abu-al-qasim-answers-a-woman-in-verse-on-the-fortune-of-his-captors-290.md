## Overview
A dialogue piece that begins at the foot of p. 255 ends on p. 256 among the verses the book gives for Abu al-Qasim, al-Hadi's son, in his imprisonment {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.255} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.256}. A woman says that the harm of his opponents has appeared and that trial has struck them so that they were routed, and he answers that their wrongdoing destroyed their rule, that the slaves are those whose striving God led astray, that they were captives in irons under them, that after betraying him they did not reach what they hoped and became two parties in hardship, and that in the house of [[group/khawlan-tribe|Khawlan]] there was a lesson for them had they let him go they would have been safe from the reckoning {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.256}.

## Detail
The lines are read by sense; the order of the lines is mixed in the printed text and the museum confirmed the names against the page image. The woman is not named and the book does not identify her. Before this are [[event/abu-al-qasim-ibn-al-hadi-composes-verses-in-prison-290|the earlier pieces of the same section]]. The museum does not reproduce the lines nor the words of insult in them and gives only their sense as the book has it; the book ascribes the verse to Abu al-Qasim and the museum has not checked the attribution. The praise and the epithets in the lines are the poet's, given as the book gives them, with no verdict in the museum's voice.
