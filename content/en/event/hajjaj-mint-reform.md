## Overview
[[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|Al-Hajjaj]] struck dirhams at the end of the year 75 AH, then ordered them struck in all regions in 76 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The mint
The source relates from Awana ibn al-Hakam that al-Hajjaj asked what the Persians used to do in striking dirhams, so he set up a mint and gathered the minters in it. He struck for the sultan from what came in of gold dust, from the refined base coin, the stuqa and the bahraja. Then he allowed the merchants to strike their own coins, and profited from the surplus of the fee. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

His dirhams bore the inscription "In the name of God, al-Hajjaj"; a year later the inscription was "God is one, God the eternal". The jurists disliked them, so they were called "disliked" (makruha). It is said that the Persians disliked their being underweight, and that they were called "al-Sumayriyya" after the first man who struck them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## What came before and after
[[person/musab-ibn-al-zubayr|Musab]] struck dirhams in 70 AH on the pattern of the Sasanian kings, and al-Hajjaj changed them. It is reported from Hisham ibn al-Kalbi that he struck dinars with them. Ibn Abi al-Zinad says that [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik]] was the first to strike gold in 74 AH; Abu al-Zubayr the critic says he struck some in 72 AH, then in 75 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Afterwards [[person/umar-ibn-hubayra|Umar ibn Hubayra]] tightened the refining of silver, and [[person/khalid-ibn-abdallah-al-qasri|Khalid al-Qasri]] added to it, then [[person/yusuf-ibn-umar-al-thaqafi|Yusuf ibn Umar]], who cut off hands and beat bodies over it. So the Hubayriyya, the Khalidiyya and the Yusufiyya were the finest coins of the Umayyads, and al-Mansur would accept none but them in the land tax; the first dirhams were called "disliked". {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
