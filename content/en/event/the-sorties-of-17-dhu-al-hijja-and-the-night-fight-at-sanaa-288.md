## Overview
On Saturday, seventeen days of Dhu al-Hijja gone, [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] went out to the parade ground and sent a party of his horse that challenged the men, who came out to them from [[place/bayt-bus-sanaa-yemen|Bayt Bus]]; night came between them and a number were killed, and al-Hadi returned to [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]] where he stayed some days {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235}. On Friday after the prayer al-Hadi went out to the parade ground, brought out a number of horse and mounted foot behind the horse; they rode to a place printed Sahda (the editor refers to the Mu'jam al-buldan), set the foot down and struck at a camp of Al Ya'fur there, killing some of them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235}. The men's armies raided from Bayt Bus, from Zahr and from elsewhere, the fight closed and a number of their horsemen were killed; it did not cease until part of the night was gone and the men were so mixed that they did not know one another {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235}.

## Detail
Al-Hadi's son Abu al-Qasim charged, mingled with the men, then turned back to a party of horse standing aside and stood with them thinking they were his own; when he was among them he heard a man say, where is the amir? Abu al-Qasim said, who is the amir? and the man said, [[person/ibrahim-ibn-khalaf-ibn-tarif|Ibrahim ibn Khalaf]]; Abu al-Qasim said, who is Ibrahim ibn Khalaf? charged him and struck him with the mace (the margin of the manuscript glosses it as the heavy club), then went out to his companions, who turned on the men and the men were routed, and both sides went back to their own {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.235} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.236}. A weary man of the Tabaris was taken up by al-Hadi behind him on his horse until he brought him to Sana'a, and he stayed there some days {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.236}. It is followed by [[event/the-tabari-reinforcements-arrive-and-abdullah-and-ibn-al-ruwayya-camp-at-sabal-289]]. The museum ties the name Ibrahim ibn Khalaf to the record of the son of Tarif as probable only, on the agreement of the name with what comes earlier in the book. Abu al-Qasim here is al-Hadi's son, probably [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Muhammad al-Murtada]]. A Saturday with seventeen days of Dhu al-Hijja gone does not agree with the tabular calendar (a Wednesday), so the museum gives the month only. It follows [[event/the-night-of-safety-after-the-maydan-battle-and-its-breaking-288]].
