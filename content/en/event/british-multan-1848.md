## Overview
In 1845 the British began their campaign against the disintegrating Sikh state in the Panjab; after an assault in Multan on two British officers in 1848, the city was occupied and annexed with the rest of the province in 1849 {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 603}.

## Under British rule
Under British rule Multan served at different times as a divisional or district centre; in Pakistan it became the capital of a division covering the districts of Multan, Jhang, Lyallpur and Montgomery {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 603}.

## The city's image
Since that era Multan has stood as a great provincial city of the southern Panjab; travellers had long quoted the couplet: "Four are the choice things of Multan: dust, beggars, heat and graveyards" {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 603}.
