## Summary
Luqman's counsels to his son are given in Surat Luqman, in the verses [[passage/quran-31-13|13]] to [[passage/quran-31-19|19]], which Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi explain; Ibn Kathir then gives a series of further sayings and admonitions attributed to Luqman, each through its own chain. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3608} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612} In the view of most early scholars he was a sage, not a prophet, though the commentators note a difference of opinion. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3606} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5371}

## Who was Luqman?
Ibn Kathir says Luqman was Luqman ibn Anqa' ibn Sadun and that his son's name was Tharan, according to a statement reported by al-Suhayli. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3608} Al-Qurtubi gives two lineages: Ibn Ishaq has him as Luqman ibn Ba'ura ibn Nahor ibn Tarih, and al-Suhayli as Luqman ibn Anqa' ibn Sarun, a Nubian of Ayla; he relays from Wahb that he was Ayyub's sister's son and from Muqatil his maternal aunt's son. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5371} Al-Qurtubi quotes Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib that he was a black man from the Sudanese of Egypt whom God gave wisdom but withheld prophethood, says the majority of the commentators hold that he was a friend of God and not a prophet, that Ikrima and al-Sha'bi held that he was a prophet, and gives his own view that he was a wise man and a judge among the Israelites. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5371}

Ibn Kathir says most early scholars held that he was not a prophet, that the claim for prophethood is reported from Ikrima if the chain to him is sound, and that the chain includes Jabir al-Ju'fi, who is weak. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3606} He relays from Qatada, in a report he calls strange and whose chain includes Sa'id ibn Bashir, a man with some weakness, that God gave Luqman a choice between prophethood and wisdom and he chose wisdom, and that Jibril came to him as he slept and poured wisdom over him so that in the morning he spoke it. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3607} In another report from Qatada, wisdom here means understanding of the religion, and Luqman was not a prophet and received no revelation. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3607}

## The counsels in Surat Luqman
**Against shirk.** Ibn Kathir says Luqman first charged his son to worship God alone and not associate anything with Him, warning that shirk is the greatest wrong. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3608} He relays from al-Bukhari the hadith of Abdallah ibn Mas'ud that when the verse in al-An'am mentioning clothing faith in wrong was revealed, the Companions were troubled, and the Prophet said it was not as they thought, reminding them of Luqman's words to his son; Muslim has it from al-A'mash. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3608}

**Kindness to parents.** Ibn Kathir says God joined to Luqman's charge to worship Him alone the duty of kindness to parents; [[passage/quran-31-14|Qur'an 31:14]] sets out what the mother bears and is God's speech, not Luqman's, and [[passage/quran-31-15|Qur'an 31:15]] deals with the case where the parents press their child toward shirk. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3608} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3609} Here he records a hadith from Sa'd ibn Malik that this verse was revealed about him when his mother refused food to make him leave his religion; the footnote refers to Muslim. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3609}

**Awareness of God even in what is hidden.** [[passage/quran-31-16|Qur'an 31:16]] says that a wrong or a sin even the weight of a mustard seed, in a rock or in the heavens or the earth, God will bring, for He is subtle and aware; Ibn Kathir explains that God will present it on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3609} He notes that some claimed 'rock' means a rock beneath the seven earths, a view reported from al-Suddi from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others, and says it seems taken from the Isra'iliyyat, which are neither believed nor disbelieved; he prefers that even a seed inside a rock God would bring out in His subtle knowledge. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3610} Al-Qurtubi adds, in a hedged form, that Luqman's son asked: if I commit a sin where no one sees me, how does God know it? Luqman recited this verse, and his son kept trembling until he died, according to Muqatil. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5379}

**Prayer, enjoining good and patience.** [[passage/quran-31-17|Qur'an 31:17]] charges him to keep up prayer with its limits, obligations and times, to enjoin good and forbid evil as far as he is able, and to bear patiently what harm may come from people; that is among matters of firm resolve. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3610} Al-Qurtubi says the son was charged with the greatest acts of obedience and that Luqman means him to practise them himself first. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5380}

**Humility and gait.** [[passage/quran-31-18|Qur'an 31:18]] forbids turning one's face away from people in contempt or walking about arrogantly, for God does not love the conceited and boastful; Ibn Kathir cites the hadith of Thabit ibn Qays that pride is 'rejecting the truth and looking down on people'. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3610} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3611} [[passage/quran-31-19|Qur'an 31:19]] tells him to be moderate in his pace, neither slow nor hurried, and to lower his voice. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3611} Ibn Kathir says the harshest of voices is the voice of donkeys, as Mujahid and others said, and holds that the comparison indicates the strongest censure of raised voices. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612}

## Other sayings attributed to Luqman
Ibn Kathir says many sayings of wisdom and admonition are related from Luqman, and gives samples, each with its own chain: {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612}

- Ahmad relates from Ibn Umar that the Prophet said: 'Luqman the Wise used to say: when God is entrusted with something He guards it.' {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612}
- Ibn Abi Hatim relates from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari that Luqman said to his son as he admonished him: beware of taqannu' (covering oneself with a cloak), for it is a fear by night and a humiliation by day; the footnote calls the chain weak. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612}
- In the report of al-Sari ibn Yahya, Luqman told his son that wisdom seats the poor in the seats of kings. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612}
- In the report of Awn ibn Abdallah, Luqman told his son: when you come to a gathering, shoot them the arrow of Islam, meaning the greeting, then sit to one side and do not speak until you see them speak; if they enter into remembrance of God, take your share with them, and if they speak of other things, move to others. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3613}
- From Hafs ibn Umar: Luqman set a bag of mustard seed beside him and, as he admonished his son, took out a seed with each admonition until the seed was used up, then said: my son, I have given you an admonition which, if a mountain had received it, would have split; and his son was broken by it. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3613}

Ibn Kathir also relays that a man stood before Luqman and said he was the slave of the Banu al-Hashas, a shepherd; asked how he reached his station, Luqman answered: God's decree, fulfilling trusts, truthful speech and leaving what does not concern me. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3606} In Ibn Wahb's report the man asked about people trampling his carpet, crowding his door and accepting his word, and Luqman answered: lowering my gaze, restraining my tongue, modesty in food, guarding my chastity, speaking truthfully, keeping my covenant, honouring my guest, protecting my neighbour and leaving what does not concern me. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3606}

Abu al-Darda describes him, in the report of Ibn Abi Hatim, as one who was given what he was given not through family, wealth or lineage but as a man of few words, long reflection and deep insight, who never slept by day and whom no one ever saw spit or relieve himself, play or laugh; he never repeated a statement unless it was wisdom someone asked him to repeat; he married and had children who died and did not weep for them; and he visited rulers and judges in order to observe, reflect and learn. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3606} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3607}

## Notes on the sources
What is in the verses themselves is documented by the Qur'an's own text and is not reproduced here; what is attributed to Luqman outside them is given by Ibn Kathir with chains, some of which the footnotes weaken, and the rock beneath the seven earths is classed by Ibn Kathir among the Isra'iliyyat. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3610} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 3612} I found no report of Luqman's death or the place of his grave in the two sources I read.
