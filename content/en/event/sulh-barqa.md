## Overview
After the conquest of Alexandria, [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] marched with his troops toward the Maghrib until he reached Barqa, that is Antaboulus, and made peace with its people for a poll tax of thirteen thousand dinars, and allowed them to sell whichever of their sons they wished in it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.314}

## The reports
In the report of Abd Allah ibn Hubayra, Amr besieged them and fought them for the poll tax, and then wrote them a letter of peace. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.314}

## The state of Barqa after the peace
Al-Baladhuri says that the people of Barqa used to send their tribute to the governor of Egypt without anyone urging them, and that no civil strife entered it. He quotes [[person/abdullah-ibn-amr-ibn-al-as|Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As]] saying that were it not for his property in the Hijaz he would have settled in Barqa, and that he knew of no dwelling safer than it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.314}

## The date of the event
The date is approximate, in 21 AH after the conquest of Alexandria; the conversion to the Gregorian calendar is derived.
