## Overview
Al-Hadi goes on with the poem (pp. 317-318), asking the people of prophethood what is between him and them, how they feared for him without cause when he rose calling to the Book in the footsteps of the Prophet, how they allied with ease and pleasures while their swords were sheathed from him in the hour of toil, and how they made claims before proofs against a man who never betrayed his friends with a lie; he says he acts as a tender father and would cover a fault of theirs, and that they accepted the word of a man he charges with lying; the footnote records that this line and seven after it are missing from manuscript S. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}

## Detail
On p. 317 al-Hadi addresses "the people of prophethood": "what is it with me and you, and how did you fear for one like me without cause", so that when he rose calling to the Book in the footsteps of the Prophet, having laboured in the search, "you allied with ease and pleasures and your swords were sheathed from me in the hour of toil" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. On p. 318 he says they made claims that were not clear, before the proofs, and this is "the strangest of things", against a man whom weakness never touched in his resolve and who never betrayed his friend with a lie, that he is not one to come near a foul friendship and that his deeds are those of a tender father {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. He says they accepted the word of a man he calls cursed and foul, and that God knows what he said of lies; the book names this man only by an epithet in the line, which is not carried over here, and the terms of abuse are not repeated {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.317}. He says that had he seen a shameful fault of theirs he would have covered it out of tenderness and steady guard, for they stand with him "in the place of the pole" of his hope and have rights due, kinship and lineage, that covering is their way when a slip shows in a friend and is the deed of the noble lords, and that if he blamed them one day he would be the one to make amends {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.318}. He says: "this is the Book of God, so follow its verses that save from ruin", and that he stood up for the truth "a shepherd, no player" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.318}. The footnote at the end of p. 318 records that this line and the seven after it are missing from manuscript S {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.318}. The book does not say which branch of the family is addressed and does not date the poem. It joins [[event/al-hadi-says-the-carefree-sleep-and-sends-a-traveller-from-raydah-to-banu-hasan-in-verse-292|the opening of the poem, the traveller's message to Banu Hasan]], and is followed by [[event/al-hadi-counsels-banu-hasan-to-rise-and-names-hamdan-khawlan-and-hashid-in-verse-292|his call to them to rise]]. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
