## Summary
The Prophet stood at the door of the Ka'ba after its cleansing, while the people of Mecca filled the mosque in rows awaiting his judgment over them; he addressed them in a comprehensive sermon that struck down the Jahiliyya's glories and blood-feuds, recited [[passage/quran-49-13|the verse of human equality]], and proclaimed the general pardon in his famous words: "go — you are free" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/54-55}.

## The sermon's text and themes
The Prophet praised God, then said: "there is no god but God alone without partner; He has kept His promise, aided His servant and routed the Confederates alone. Behold — every glory, blood or property claimed is beneath these two feet of mine, except the keeping of the House and the watering of the pilgrims" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/54}.

Then he set out the ruling on quasi-deliberate killing: "the slain by mistaken quasi-deliberate act — by whip and staff — has a blood-money made heavy: a hundred camels, forty of them with young in their bellies" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/54}.

Then he said: "O company of Quraysh, God has taken from you the Jahiliyya's pride and its exulting in ancestors; mankind is from Adam, and Adam is from dust," and recited His word: "O mankind, We created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another; the noblest of you with God is the most God-fearing of you" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/54-55}.

## The general pardon
Then he said: "O company of Quraysh, what do you think I will do with you?" They said: good — a noble brother and a noble brother's son. He said: "go — you are free" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/55}. Then he called Uthman ibn Talha and returned the Ka'ba's key to him, saying: "here is your key, Uthman — today is a day of loyalty and fidelity" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/55}.

## For young visitors
The Prophet stood before the people of Mecca who had fought him for long years and driven him from his home. He asked: what do you think I will do with you? They said: you are a noble brother, son of a noble brother. He answered with words that entered history: "go — you are free," pardoning them all in peace and love.
