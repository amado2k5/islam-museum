## Overview
Al-Salabi says that Sultan [[person/sulayman-al-qanuni|Suleiman the Magnificent]] ordered [[person/sulayman-pasha-khadim|Suleiman Pasha al-Khadim]], governor of Egypt, to equip a fleet at Suez and set out for India to cut off the routes of the Portuguese to Mecca. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 196-197}

## Preparation
The sultan saw that defending the holy places was a responsibility of the Ottoman state. He made an agreement with the rulers of Calicut and Cambay, who had suffered from the Portuguese invasion, to act together. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 196-197}

## The campaign
Suleiman Pasha reached Jeddah in seven days, then Kamaran, and took [[place/aden|Aden]] and put in it a garrison of six hundred soldiers. Then he besieged Diu, did not storm it, and withdrew, having lost about four hundred of his men. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 196-197}

He returned and took one of the forward fortresses and captured eighty Portuguese. Al-Salabi holds that the fortresses would have surrendered had it not been for new Portuguese reinforcements, and that the campaign enabled the Ottomans to secure the Red Sea. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 196-197}

The year of the campaign (945 AH / 1538 CE) is derived from the context of the campaign on Aden and Yemen. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 191-192}
