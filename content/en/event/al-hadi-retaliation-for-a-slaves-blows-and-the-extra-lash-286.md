## Overview
[[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] tells his son [[person/ali-ibn-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Ali ibn Muhammad]] two reports about [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|Yahya ibn al-Husayn]]: retaliation for a man struck by his slave, and amends for an extra lash in a wine penalty. Both are Zaydi, in the voice of the imam's followers; the manuscript margin carries the heading "the rule on the flogger adding to the flogging". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121}

## The slave Rizq
He said: I was sitting with him one day when a man came seeking redress against a slave called Rizq; the man said, "May I be your ransom, my slave struck me," and bared his back, where there were four blows. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.120} Al-Hadi said, "Bring him." A group went and brought the slave, and al-Hadi asked what had led him to strike this man; he said the man had taken a knife of his and he had asked for it back and it was not handed over. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.120} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121} Al-Hadi asked, "Then why did you strike him?" and kept questioning him until the slave admitted, "I struck him and I was wrong." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121} He ordered the slave struck four blows on the back as retaliation for the man's beating, and twenty strokes of a staff as correction, and said, "By God, had my son Muhammad or my father struck him, I would have exacted retaliation from him." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121}

## The extra lash
Two men were brought to Yahya ibn al-Husayn one day with testimony that they had drunk wine; he ordered them struck, and the flogger was harder on one of them by an extra lash. Yahya told him, "Had I known you meant the extra, I would have struck you," and then ordered ten dirhams for the man struck because of the extra lash. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121} A second manuscript reads "for the one flogged" where the first has "for the one struck". {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.121}

## Notes
These are reports of the imam's judging as their narrator tells them; the text compares them with nothing, and gives no day or place. For the general legal subject see [[concept/hudud-penalties]].
