## Summary
On the first of Jumada al-Ula in 589 AH Sayf al-Din Baktimur, lord of Akhlat, was killed, and Hazardinari succeeded him; his killing came two months after Salah al-Din's death. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 229}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir reports that Baktimur had shown excessive glee at Salah al-Din's death; when the news reached him he rejoiced greatly, had a throne made and sat on it, titled himself al-Sultan al-Mu'azzam Salah al-Din — his own laqab had been Sayf al-Din — renamed himself Abd al-Aziz, showed signs of derangement, and prepared to besiege Mayyafariqin when his fate overtook him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 229} The cause of his death was that Hazardinari, also a mamluk of Shah-Arman by Zuhayr al-Din, had grown strong with a large following and married Baktimur's daughter; coveting the kingdom he set assassins on him, and when Baktimur was killed Hazardinari took the land of Akhlat and its districts. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 229} Ibn al-Athir describes Baktimur as religious, good and pious, abundant in charity, loving toward religious men and Sufis and generous to them, close to and beloved by his subjects, just among them, generous, brave and of good conduct. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 229}
