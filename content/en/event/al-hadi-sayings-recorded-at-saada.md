## Overview
After the call, the book gives a run of [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]'s sayings related by [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] in the form "I heard him say"; the imam's address to him on p. 51 as "Abu Jafar" shows that the person he addressed bore that kunya; the book also calls Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Kufi "Abu Jafar" (p. 54), so the kunya alone does not settle which of the two narrates these lines. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.49} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51} It is the testimony of a follower in a Zaydi book, so the praise and the comparison with the first generation are the narrator's or the imam's words, not a historical finding of the museum. One line in which the imam speaks harshly of his opponents is left out and not repeated in the museum's voice.

## Resolve and the good of the community
The narrator says he heard al-Hadi tell a group that if they obeyed him they would lose of the Messenger of God only his person; heard him swear earnestly that he wished God to set right the affair of this community through him even if he went hungry two days and ate one; and heard him say one night that he wished God to set Islam right through him even if his hand were stuck to the Pleiades and he then dropped to the earth and arrived only in pieces. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.49}

## The banner of Badr
He heard him say that if he had three hundred and thirteen believers, or rather five hundred, he would govern Yemen with them, since that was a distinction of the Messenger of God; the editor's note says this is the number of the men of Badr and refers to al-Waqidi's Maghazi. He then told them to be patient with him, that he would go ahead of their banner and support the religion of Islam. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.50} He also said that he knew no banner that day like the banner of Badr but theirs, nor a band better than theirs after those who came before, that their concern was to make God's religion prevail, revive His Book and enjoin good and forbid wrong, and that he hoped they would stand higher with God than that. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.50} On p. 51 he is quoted as saying that God's angels in His heavens rejoice more in what they are engaged in than they do on earth. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51}

## A prayer in illness
The narrator relates al-Hadi saying that he fell ill at home with his father, his uncles and some of his household around him, asked them to clear the room and began a supplication; he did not say what he prayed except that it included: "O God, I know death is unavoidable; keep me alive until You bring me to what pleases You of jihad, then do with me what You will." {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.50} The father and uncles are, from the rest of the book and not from these lines, [[person/al-husayn-ibn-al-qasim-al-rassi|al-Husayn ibn al-Qasim]] and [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-qasim-al-rassi|Muhammad]] and [[person/al-hasan-ibn-al-qasim-al-rassi|al-Hasan]].

## Returning or going on
He heard him say that if matters in Yemen did not settle he would go back to his family or strike East and West until he established God's proof; {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.50} and that if he knew anyone in that age better able to carry this task, or any of the family who did it better, he would follow him wherever he was and fight in front of him, but he knew none. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51}

## The Qur'an between him and the people
He relates seeing him take the Qur'an and tell the people "between me and you is this, verse by verse": if he departed from it by a letter they owed him no obedience and should fight him; and he heard him ask them to guarantee him that they would set right their inner selves and obey when he commanded, and he in turn would set them on the white path and guarantee them Paradise. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.51} These are the imam's words as his narrator gives them, and the museum gives no verdict on the promise.
