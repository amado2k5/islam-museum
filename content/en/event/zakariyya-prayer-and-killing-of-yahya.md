## Summary
Surat Maryam ([[passage/quran-19-5|Qur'an 19:5]] and [[passage/quran-19-6|19:6]]) and Surat al-Anbiya ([[passage/quran-21-89|Qur'an 21:89]]) report the prayer of [[person/zakariyya|Zakariyya]] (Zechariah) that God give him a successor who would inherit from him, and the answer was the good news of [[person/yahya|Yahya]] (John). {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 663} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 664} The commentators differ on what "inherit" means: inheritance of prophethood and knowledge, or of wealth? Ibn Kathir prefers the first, on the strength of the hadith "we are not inherited from". {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2926} The books also carry the hadith of al-Harith al-Ash'ari on the five commands God gave Yahya, and differ about how Zakariyya and Yahya died and where.

## Zakariyya's prayer
Ibn Kathir says that what moved Zakariyya to ask was that, after he took charge of Maryam daughter of Imran, whenever he entered her prayer chamber he found fruit out of season with her, and so knew that the One who provides a thing out of its season could give him a child however old he was. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 665} His wife was barren. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 665} Some commentators explain his fear as fear of what his kinsmen would do among the Israelites after him against God's law, so he asked for a son of his own loins who would be righteous, godfearing and pleasing. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 665}

Al-Qurtubi reports that Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Qatada said he feared that his heirs would inherit his wealth or that distant relatives would inherit it, and was anxious that someone other than a child would inherit; a group said instead that his kin were careless about religion and he feared it would be lost after his death, so he asked for someone to uphold it, and this, in al-Qurtubi's presentation, is the sound one of the two views. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4330}

## What "inherit" means
Ibn Kathir says the hadith "we are not inherited from; what we leave is charity" is in the two Sahih collections through more than one route, and that al-Tirmidhi gives it in the wording "we, the company of the prophets, are not inherited from", so the inheritance here must be inheritance of prophethood, as in Sulayman's inheriting from Dawud; he adds that Zakariyya was a carpenter who ate from the work of his hand and is not thought to have gathered wealth, since the prophets were the most detached of people from this world. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2926} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 666} In Muslim, from Abu Hurayra, the Prophet said that Zakariyya was a carpenter. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 667}

He quotes Mujahid that the inheritance was knowledge; Abu Salih that Yahya might be a prophet as his forefathers were prophets; al-Hasan, al-Suddi and Zayd ibn Aslam that prophethood and knowledge are meant; and Abu Salih in another report that he inherits Zakariyya's wealth and the prophethood from the house of Yaqub, which he says is Ibn Jarir's choice. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2926} He also gives two mursal reports from Qatada and al-Hasan that the Prophet said "God have mercy on Zakariyya", and in al-Hasan's wording that he was not concerned about "those who would inherit his wealth", and says these are mursal and do not contradict the sound reports. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2926}

In al-Qurtubi, al-Nahhas gives three possible answers: inheritance of prophethood, of wisdom and knowledge, or of wealth. Prophethood he judges impossible since prophethood is not inherited; knowledge and wisdom he calls a good view, since scholars are the heirs of the prophets; wealth he does not rule out. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4333} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4334} Al-Qurtubi, quoting Ibn Abd al-Barr, says that the people of knowledge of Qur'anic interpretation hold that Sulayman did not inherit wealth and that Yahya inherited knowledge and prophethood, except those who differ, among them what is related from al-Hasan that "inherit" means wealth, and that any view contrary to the Prophet's saying is "rejected and abandoned". He then quotes Ibn Atiyya that most commentators hold that Zakariyya meant inheritance of wealth, and that the hadith may not be general but describe the usual condition of the prophets. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4330} The wider question is set out in [[concept/prophets-leave-no-inheritance|the record on "The prophets leave no inheritance"]].

## Yahya's five commands
Ahmad reports from al-Harith al-Ash'ari that the Prophet said God commanded Yahya son of Zakariyya with five words to act on and to command the Israelites to act on, and he was about to delay, so [[person/isa-ibn-maryam|Isa]] (Jesus) said to him: either you convey them or I will. Yahya said: I fear that if you get ahead of me I will be punished or the earth will swallow me. He gathered the Israelites at Bayt al-Maqdis until the mosque was full, sat on a high place, praised God and then gave the five. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 672}

The first is to worship God and associate nothing with Him, likened to a slave bought by his master with his own money who works and pays his earnings to someone else. The second is prayer: God turns His face toward His servant so long as he does not turn away. The third is fasting, likened to a man in a company carrying a pouch of musk so that all smell it, and the breath of a fasting person is better with God than the scent of musk. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 672} The fourth is charity, likened to a man captured by the enemy, his hands bound to his neck and led out to have his neck struck, who ransoms himself with little and much until he frees himself. The fifth is much remembrance of God, likened to a man pursued closely by an enemy who reaches a strong fortress and shelters in it. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 673}

The report goes on that the Prophet said: "And I command you five things God commanded me: the community, hearing, obedience, emigration and striving in God's path." {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 673} Ibn Kathir says Abu Ya'la related it, as did al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, al-Hakim and al-Tabarani, and that Ibn Asakir gives from al-Rabi' ibn Anas that the Companions of the Prophet spoke of Yahya being sent with five words, from what they heard from the scholars of the Israelites. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 673} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 674}

## Yahya in worship
Ibn Kathir records that he kept much to himself, felt at home in the wilds, ate leaves of trees, drank from rivers and at times ate locusts, and that his parents went out looking for him and found him at the lake of the Jordan, where he wept bitterly out of devotion and fear. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 674} From Wuhayb ibn al-Ward he reports that Zakariyya lost his son for three days and found he had dug a grave and was standing in it weeping for himself; Yahya told him that he had said there was a wasteland between Paradise and Hell that is crossed only by the tears of those who weep, and Zakariyya said: weep, my son, and the two wept together. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 674}

## The killing of Yahya and the death of Zakariyya
Ibn Kathir gives the best-known reasons for the killing of Yahya: that a king in Damascus wished to marry one of his close relatives or a woman not lawful to him, and Yahya forbade it; the woman held it against him, and when matters between her and the king stood well she asked him for Yahya's blood, and he sent someone who killed him and brought his head and blood in a basin. In another version the king's wife loved him and he refused her, and when she despaired she asked the king for him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 675} A further report, from Qasim, a freedman of Mu'awiya, which Ibn Asakir transmits, says that the king of Damascus, who had sworn a triple divorce on a wife and then wished to take her back, asked Yahya for a ruling; he ruled that she was not lawful for him until she married another husband, and the woman, at her mother's urging, held it against him and asked the king for Yahya's head. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 677}

Ibn Kathir treats these reports unequally. He gives, from Ishaq ibn Bishr, a hadith in which the Prophet sees Zakariyya in heaven on the night of the Night Journey and Zakariyya tells him of Yahya's killing and says that he himself fled the Israelites, entered a tree and was sawn in it; he calls this "very strange", says its attribution to the Prophet is rejected, and notes that what is established in the Sahih in the hadith of the Night Journey is the mention of Yahya and Isa as sons of the maternal aunt, not Zakariyya. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 675} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 676} From Wahb ibn Munabbih there are two reports on the death of Zakariyya: in one he fled his people, entered a tree, and the saw was set on it until he was cut in two; in the other, from Ishaq ibn Bishr, it was Sha'ya (Isaiah) for whom the tree split, and Zakariyya "died a death", and Ibn Kathir closes with "God knows best". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 672}

On where Yahya was killed there are two views: in Bayt al-Maqdis, for which Ibn Kathir cites al-Thawri from al-A'mash from Shamla ibn Atiyya that seventy prophets were killed on the rock at Bayt al-Maqdis, Yahya among them; or in Damascus, from Ibn al-Musayyab's report that Nebuchadnezzar came to Damascus and found the blood of Yahya boiling. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 676} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 677} Ibn Kathir remarks on the Nebuchadnezzar story that Yahya came a long time after him, so that cannot have been his blood, and that it is apparently the blood of an earlier prophet or of some righteous people. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 647} Ibn al-Athir likewise rejects the story of Nebuchadnezzar and the blood, because the chroniclers agree that Nebuchadnezzar attacked the Israelites in the time of Jeremiah, four hundred and sixty-one years before Yahya according to the Jews and Christians. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 231}

## The head and the place
Ibn Kathir quotes Ibn Asakir, through al-Walid ibn Muslim from Zayd ibn Waqid, that he saw the head of Yahya when they wished to build the mosque of [[place/damascus|Damascus]] ([[building/umayyad-mosque|the Umayyad Mosque]]): it was brought out from beneath one of the qibla corners next to the prayer niche, the skin and hair unchanged. He also notes that it was said to have been placed under the pillar known as the pillar of the Sakasika, and adds "God knows best". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 677} For what is ascribed to the prophets as graves, see [[concept/graves-of-the-prophets|the record on "The graves of the prophets"]].
