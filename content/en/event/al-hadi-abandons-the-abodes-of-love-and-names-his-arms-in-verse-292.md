## Overview
A wafir poem under the heading "and also of his": al-Hadi says he has left the abodes of Zaynab and al-Rabab and turned from error and youthful folly, that knowledge forbade him the company of women because he inclines to manliness and what is right, and that his concern is a horse and his arms, mail, sword and lance, and his thrusting and striking in the fight. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.314}

## Detail
On p. 314, under the heading "and also of his", a wafir poem opens: "I left the abodes of Zaynab and al-Rabab and turned from error and youthful folly" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.314}. He says he did not grieve over ruins that faded until they were like the scrawl of a writing, nor does he long for the company of women like one stricken, and that knowledge forbade him it because he inclines to manliness and what is right {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.314}. He knows that this world of theirs and all in it goes to an end, so his concern is "a sturdy steed", a large strong horse swift as an eagle {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.314}. He describes his mail as a pool, the blade of his sword as cutting heads after the napes of necks, his lance slender with a point shining like a meteor, his charging in the assemblies every day, his thrusting with firm shafts, his striking in the fight with death near, and his humbling of hard heads {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.314}. A footnote on p. 314 says manuscript S reads "my mention" for "my charging" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.314}. The lines are of the type of abandoned love-song from which the poet turns to horsemanship; the book names Zaynab and al-Rabab only in the opening, as poetic names that link to no person in the narrative. The same poem goes on, on this page and on p. 315, with [[event/al-hadi-reproaches-a-friend-who-withheld-twenty-men-in-verse-292|the lines of reproach over twenty men withheld]]. It follows [[event/al-hadi-counsels-the-writer-to-repent-and-hold-to-the-sons-of-al-qasim-292|his counsel to the writer to repent]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
