On the Muslim army's way back from the expedition of Banu al-Mustaliq, the people made a night halt; the Mother of the Believers [[person/aisha|Aisha]] went out for her need and lost a necklace of hers of Zufar beads, so she went back looking for it. When she returned to her mount the army had departed — those who carried her howdah had lifted it and set it on the camel thinking she was inside, for the lightness of her weight. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

She sat in her place, certain that if she were missed they would come back for her; sleep overcame her. The Badr Companion [[person/safwan-ibn-al-muattal|Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami]] had been assigned the army's rear to pick up dropped gear; he saw the dark shape of a sleeping person and recognised her — he had seen her before the hijab — and pronounced the words of return at the top of his voice. She woke and drew her veil; he halted his mount and led it, honouring her, without speaking a single word to her until they overtook the army in the blaze of midday. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

The head of hypocrisy, [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy]], seized the occasion to kindle the vicious calumny, spreading the slander through Medina; some Muslims passed it on — the poet [[person/hassan-ibn-thabit|Hassan ibn Thabit]], [[person/mistah-ibn-uthatha|Mistah ibn Uthatha]] and [[person/hamna-bint-jahsh|Hamna bint Jahsh]]. Aisha was ill for a month knowing nothing of the story except that she missed the Prophet's accustomed kindness, until Mistah's mother told her. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Her weeping grew fierce and she moved to her parents' house, until revelation came down from above the seven heavens in ten verses of Sura al-Nur absolving Aisha, proclaiming her innocence and exposing the slanderers, as in [[passage/quran-24-11|Sura al-Nur, verse 11]]; and the Prophet ordered the hadd of qadhf carried out on those who had plunged into it — eighty lashes each — while Abu Bakr forgave Mistah and restored his allowance in obedience to God's command. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}
