On the Muslim army's way back from the expedition of Banu al-Mustaliq, the people made a night halt; the Mother of the Believers [[person/aisha|Aisha]] went out for her need and lost a necklace of hers of Zufar beads, so she went back looking for it. When she returned to her mount the army had departed — those who carried her howdah had lifted it and set it on the camel thinking she was inside, for the lightness of her weight. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

She sat in her place, certain that if she were missed they would come back for her; sleep overcame her. The Badr Companion [[person/safwan-ibn-al-muattal|Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami]] had been assigned the army's rear to pick up dropped gear; he saw the dark shape of a sleeping person and recognised her — he had seen her before the hijab — and pronounced the words of return at the top of his voice. She woke and drew her veil; he halted his mount and led it, honouring her, without speaking a single word to her until they overtook the army in the blaze of midday. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

The head of hypocrisy, [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy]], seized the occasion to kindle the vicious calumny, spreading the slander through Medina; some Muslims passed it on — the poet [[person/hassan-ibn-thabit|Hassan ibn Thabit]], [[person/mistah-ibn-uthatha|Mistah ibn Uthatha]] and [[person/hamna-bint-jahsh|Hamna bint Jahsh]]. Aisha was ill for a month knowing nothing of the story except that she missed the Prophet's accustomed kindness, until Mistah's mother told her. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Her weeping grew fierce and she moved to her parents' house, until revelation came down from above the seven heavens in ten verses of Sura al-Nur absolving Aisha, proclaiming her innocence and exposing the slanderers, as in [[passage/quran-24-11|Sura al-Nur, verse 11]]; and the Prophet ordered the hadd of qadhf carried out on those who had plunged into it — eighty lashes each — while Abu Bakr forgave Mistah and restored his allowance in obedience to God's command. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## For Sprouts
On the army's way back from the Banu al-Mustaliq expedition Aisha was left behind and slept where she was; Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal found her and brought her to the army — and the chief of hypocrisy spread a slander until revelation cleared her in Surat al-Nur.

## For Explorers
On the army's way back from the Banu al-Mustaliq expedition Aisha went out at night for her need, lost a necklace of Zafar beads and searched — returning to find the army gone, her howdah loaded thinking her in it. She sat and slept; Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal of the rear-guard saw her, knew her — he had seen her before the veil — called the istirja' so she woke, knelt his mount and led her on honored, speaking not a word till they reached the army. Abdullah ibn Ubayy spread the slander through Medina; some Muslims repeated it — Hassan ibn Thabit, Mistah and Hamna. Aisha lay ill a month unknowing until Umm Mistah told her; revelation came down in ten verses of Surat al-Nur clearing her and exposing the slanderers, the eighty-lash penalty fell on those who waded in, and Abu Bakr forgave Mistah and restored his allowance.

## For Scholars
On the Muslim army's way back from the Banu al-Mustaliq expedition the people camped one night; Aisha went out for her need, lost a necklace of Zafar beads and went back searching — returning to find the army had marched, the men who set down her howdah having lifted it onto the camel thinking her in it for her light weight. She sat where she was, reckoning if missed they would come back for her, and sleep overcame her; the Badr Companion Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami, appointed to the rear-guard gathering fallen things, saw the dark of a sleeping person and knew her — he had seen her before the veil — and called out the istirja' at the top of his voice so she woke with her veil; he knelt his mount, led her on honored, and spoke not a single word until they reached the army hastening in the midday heat. Abdullah ibn Ubayy, the head of hypocrisy, seized the moment to spread the vicious slander through Medina, some Muslims passing it on — the poet Hassan ibn Thabit, Mistah ibn Uthatha and Hamna bint Jahsh — and Aisha lay ill a month knowing nothing of the talk but for the Prophet's unusual coolness until Umm Mistah told her. Her weeping grew severe and she moved to her parents' house until revelation came down from above the seven heavens in ten verses of Surat al-Nur clearing Aisha, declaring her purity and exposing the slanderers — as in verse 24:11 — and the Prophet ordered the slander penalty of eighty lashes on those who waded in; and Abu Bakr forgave Mistah and restored his allowance in obedience to God's command.
