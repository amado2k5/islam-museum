## Overview
Al-Suyuti relates that [[person/genghis-khan|Genghis Khan]] sent envoys and gifts to Khwarazm Shah asking for peace and for merchants to travel freely, and he answered him. Then Khwarazm Shah's maternal uncle, his governor over Transoxiana, is said to have seized the merchants and taken their property, claiming they were spies. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.713-714}

## The envoys
Genghis Khan sent envoys saying that he had given the merchants his safe conduct and they had been betrayed; if his uncle acted without his command, he should hand him over to them. [[person/muhammad-khwarazmshah|Khwarazm Shah]] ordered the envoys killed, and they were killed. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.713-714}

## The historians' judgement
Al-Suyuti says that this movement spilled so much Muslim blood that a river of blood flowed from every drop. The author of Shadharat al-Dhahab gives a similar account, in which Khwarazm Shah repented and the matter was of great ill omen. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.713-714} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p.69-70}

The date is around 615 AH and is derived.
