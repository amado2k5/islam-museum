## Summary
In late Ramadan of 626 AH al-Malik al-Kamil took the city of Hama after besieging it, when its lord Salah al-Din came down to him from his citadel. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 483}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir gives the cause: al-Malik al-Mansur Muhammad ibn Taqi al-Din Umar, lord of Hama, had died after making the army and the city's notables swear to his elder son titled al-Malik al-Muzaffar — whom he had sent to al-Kamil of Egypt, who had married his daughter; a second son, Qilij Arslan titled Salah al-Din, was at Damascus and came to Hama, where the city submitted and he seized town and citadel; al-Kamil ordered him to surrender it to his elder brother and he refused. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 483} When the Mu'azzam died and al-Kamil came to Syria and took Damascus, he sent an army to Hama which besieged it on the third of Ramadan under Asad al-Din Shirkuh of Homs and Fakhr al-Din Uthman with Muhammad ibn Taqi al-Din's son who was at al-Kamil's side; the siege lasted days as al-Kamil camped at Salamiyya; the lord of Hama Salah al-Din came down to him from the citadel — his companions urged him not to, but he said let me descend or I will throw myself from the fortress — and came with a small party; al-Kamil detained him until he surrendered the city and citadel of Hama to his elder brother al-Muzaffar, keeping only Ba'rin citadel, which was his. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 484}
