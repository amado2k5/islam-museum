## Overview
Until, in Jumada I of the year 292, Banu al-Harith called to one another at the coming of the fruit and swore an alliance against Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.275}. Under the heading "the report of the opposition of Banu al-Harith to Abu Ja'far": they united with [[person/ibn-humayd-of-banu-al-harith-286|Ibn Humayd]] in it and sent to Abu Ja'far announcing war; Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah wrote to al-Hadi ila al-Haqq informing him of it, and al-Hadi wrote to Banu al-Harith this letter {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.275}.

## Detail
Under the heading "the copy of the letter of al-Hadi ila al-Haqq to Banu al-Harith" the letter opens with the invocation, then "from the servant of God, the imam al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, Amir al-Mu'minin Yahya ibn al-Husayn" to Ibn Humayd and his following of Banu al-Harith, whom the letter calls a people of treachery and little gratitude; it then recalls that God does not change what is in a people until they change what is in themselves [[passage/quran-13-11|ar-Ra'd 13:11]], and says that they are a deceived and misled folk who take no lesson from others and gain nothing from their own trials {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.275}. The letter goes on in the following pages and will be read in the next batch from p. 276. The words with which the letter addresses Banu al-Harith are al-Hadi's own as copied in a Zaydi book, and the museum neither gives them as its judgement nor repeats the words of abuse. [[event/the-dearth-and-the-arms-ban-on-banu-al-harith-and-yam-at-najran-291|The ban on their carrying arms]] precedes this report. The account is ascribed to Ali ibn Muhammad in the earlier reports and the book does not name the narrator again here. The notes of the page give manuscript S's reading "twenty-six servants" for the preceding page and the reference Qur'an 13:11 for the quotation; the museum does not reproduce the verse and gives only the link.
