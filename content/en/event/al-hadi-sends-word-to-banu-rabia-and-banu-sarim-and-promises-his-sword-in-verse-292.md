## Overview
A poem in the kamil metre under the heading "and also of his" on p. 326, in which al-Hadi asks a rider on a she-camel to carry word to the chiefs of Banu Rabi'a and Banu Sarim that they are his help and his men; he praises their aid to the family of Muhammad and their faithfulness to the two pledges, says their good deeds are not lost with him, and speaks of two portions in their aid, one in this world and one at the resurrection in the neighbourhood of Muhammad; the lines run on into the top of p. 327.

## Detail
After the end of the poem of the two meanings, which closes at the top of p. 326 with lines on virtues and the hard parts of matters, comes the heading "and also of his" (kamil) and the opening "O you who go at dawn on a lean-flanked she-camel" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}, a she-camel he calls crooked and worn by travel {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}. He asks the rider to carry word to "the chiefs of Banu Rabi'a, all of them" and to "Banu Sarim" that they are "my help and my men" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}. He describes them as repelling the enemy of the family of Muhammad with Mashrafi swords and spears, helpers of their Lord, their Prophet and their imam, standing for the family's victory, keeping their covenant in full, and guarding the family's sanctuary out of religion and zeal {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}. He says their counsel and their deeds against the oath-breakers have reached him and that their fidelity to their imam and guardian in the two pledges is complete {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}, that good deeds are not lost with him and his sword is streaming {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}, and that he has in their help two known portions and does not dash hopes {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}: one in this world by which people live {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}, and one at the resurrection in the neighbourhood of Muhammad in a garden of good shade {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.327}. The footnote of p. 326 glosses a word of the lines with a quotation from the Lisan that it means spending money on what has no good in it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.326}. The book says no more of Banu Rabi'a and Banu Sarim; they may be branches of [[group/banu-rabia-hamdan|Banu Rabi'a]] and [[group/banu-sarim-hamdan|Banu Sarim]] of Hamdan, which have records in the museum, but the poem does not say so and no link is made. The book does not date the poem. It follows [[event/al-hadi-says-the-two-meanings-of-guidance-are-one-and-asks-the-addressee-to-follow-the-path-292|the poem of the two meanings]]. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
