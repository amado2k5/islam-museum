## Overview
Banu al-Harith asked for the release of Ali ibn al-Rabi' al-Madani, after they had planned to take Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah as a hostage in exchange for him, and al-Hadi was angered. The book's account says al-Hadi ordered servants to take him toward Sa'da and kill him on the road; his victim's kin had asked for retaliation. The killing increased the fear of Banu al-Harith.

## Detail
The book says that Banu al-Harith informed al-Hadi that they had planned to take Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah as a hostage in exchange for Ali ibn al-Rabi', and al-Hadi was greatly angered. Ali ibn al-Rabi' had killed a man of Ahlaf together with his brother, and the kin of the victim had asked to kill him in return. Al-Hadi, having heard what Banu al-Harith had said, ordered some of his servants to take Ali toward Sa'da and to kill him on the road, and they did so. The book justifies the order by saying that Ali had earned death under God's law. The men then told Banu al-Harith that Ali had been killed and that they should bring what they had. The killing increased their fear and alarm.
