In Jumada al-Akhira of the eighth year of the Hijra, after the battle of Mu'ta, news reached Medina that the tribes of Quda'a, Bali, Lakhm and Judham were mustering in the northern Hijaz and the edges of al-Sham to raid Medina; the Prophet appointed [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] over three hundred fighters, for his kinship to Bali, to win them over. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When Amr reached their land he found a great gathering and asked the Prophet for reinforcements; he sent him two hundred fighters of the senior Muhajirun — [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] among them — under [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah]], who yielded the command to Amr in observance of unity and good hearing and obedience. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

Amr ibn al-As showed superb military skill: he barred the army from lighting fires on the cold nights lest the enemy learn their numbers, and barred them from pursuing fugitives for fear of ambush; he launched a decisive attack that scattered the hosts of Quda'a and spread the Islamic state's awe over the tribes of the northern peninsula to the edges of al-Sham. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
