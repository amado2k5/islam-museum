## Overview
Al-Baladhuri relates the Islam of four thousand of the soldiers of the Shahanshah, known as the Red Ones of the Daylam, after al-Qadisiyya. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.393}

## Musammar ibn Kadam's account
At al-Qadisiyya, with Rustam, were four thousand men called the soldiers of the Shahanshah. They asked for safe conduct on condition that they might settle where they wished, make alliance with whom they wished, and be given stipends. They made alliance with [[person/zuhra-ibn-hawiyya|Zuhra ibn Hawiyya]], and [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd]] settled them where they chose. Their leader was Dil, so they were called the Red Ones of the Daylam. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.393}

## Al-Mada'ini's account
Al-Mada'ini says that Abarwiz brought them from the Daylam; they were his servants and his close men. When their commander was killed they withdrew, and decided to enter the religion of the Muslims. [[person/al-mughira-ibn-shuba|Al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba]] came to them and asked them; Sa'd granted them safe conduct, and they became Muslims and witnessed the conquest of al-Mada'in. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.394}

## Afterwards
Ziyad sent some of them to Syria by the order of Mu'awiya, and some to Basra. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.393}
