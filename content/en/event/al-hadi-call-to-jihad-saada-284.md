## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad relates from his father [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]] that [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] stayed at [[place/saada|Sa'da]] through Safar and the two months of Rabi' and Jumada while people of Yemen wrote to him all the time; on arriving he had written to all the people of Yemen a letter of call, summoning them and urging them to jihad with him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.48} The book is Zaydi in outlook and the call is given as its narrator relays it. The page does not state the year; 284 AH is taken from the context of the arrival.

## The text of the call
The narrator says al-Hadi used to call people and urge them to jihad and make it attractive to them, and his call ran: "People, I call you to what God commanded me to call you to, to the Book of God and the sunna of His Messenger and to enjoining good and forbidding wrong"; what the Book brings is to be followed, what it forbids avoided, and he and they are to enjoin good and do it and forbid wrong and leave it. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.48} See [[concept/dawa]], [[concept/jihad]] and [[concept/amr-bil-maruf]].

## The six conditions
Al-Hadi set four conditions on himself: to rule by the Book of God and the sunna of His Prophet; to put them before himself in what God has set between him and them, not claiming any superiority; to put them first at the distribution before himself; and to go ahead of them in person when meeting his enemy and theirs. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.48} He set two on them: sincere counsel to God and to him in secret and in public, and obedience to his command in every state so long as he obeys God; if he departs from obedience to God they owe him none, and if he inclines or turns from the Book of God and the sunna of His Messenger he has no claim on them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.48} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.49} He cites the verse of Surat Yusuf ([[passage/quran-12-108|Qur'an 12:108]]). {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.49} See [[concept/bayah]].

## What he said to gatherings
The narrator says he heard him one day, with a group around him, say that their call was the call of Muhammad "like for like": Muhammad called to the Book of God and the sunna, and so did they; then he asks what stops the owners of wealth from standing with them if only what is due from them is taken, and what stops the poor if no share of the wealth is reserved to himself, and says only what stopped earlier people from rising with the Messenger of God and with Ali ibn Abi Talib stops them. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.49} That is the imam's speech as his narrator gave it; the comparison with the Prophet is his and not the museum's judgement. A footnote says another manuscript reads "the possessors of wealth" for "the people of wealth" and adds a mention of the rich. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.49}
