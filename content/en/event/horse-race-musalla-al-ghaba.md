## Overview

The horse-race the Prophet held from al-Hafya' to Thaniyyat al-Wada': a race of trained-down (mudammar) horses and a race of strong untrained ones, within Medina and its outskirts, with Ibn Umar among the winners {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the hadith

In the report of Ibn Umar in the two Sahihs: the Prophet raced the horses that had been trained down from al-Hafya' — a place outside Medina — whose course was Thaniyyat al-Wada', and raced those not trained down from the Thaniyya to the mosque of Banu Zurayq; Abdallah ibn Umar was among those who raced {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}. Two races: one for the trained-down horses and one for the strong ones, the course from the Thaniyya at Medina's approach to the mosque.

## In fiqh

On this the jurists built the rules of racing: the sabiq is the winner, the muhallil the third party who enters between them carrying the prize, and the muzammil the finishing post — the mosque of Banu Zurayq was this race's muzammil {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 229}. It is the root of the Prophetic race that has continued in Muslim lands to today's Arabian horse-racing.
