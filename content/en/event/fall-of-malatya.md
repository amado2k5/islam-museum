## Overview
In 133 AH Constantine the Roman besieged [[place/malatya|Malatya]] and ruined its wall after its people had come out under safe conduct. This is al-Baladhuri's account, from al-Waqidi. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The siege
Kamakh was in the hands of the Muslims, and its people called on the people of Malatya for help. Two hundred horsemen came out of them, and the Roman cavalry routed them. Then Constantine came down upon Malatya and besieged it. The Jazira was in turmoil, and its governor Musa ibn Ka'b was in Harran, and could not relieve them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

He offered them safe conduct on condition that they evacuate the city and let him demolish it. They refused, so he set up catapults against it. When the distress grew severe, they asked him to put it in writing, and he did. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The departure
They carried what they could and threw much of it into the wells and hiding places, then went out. The Romans stood in two lines with drawn swords at the city gate, and then escorted them until they reached safety. The people of Malatya scattered through the Jazira. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The Romans demolished the city and left only a tower that suffered some damage, and they demolished the fortress of Qaludhiya. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
