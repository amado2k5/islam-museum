## Overview
In 399 and 400 AH [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]] was in turmoil: the Umayyads and the Qurashis deposed the caliph [[person/hisham-ii-al-muayyad|al-Mu'ayyad Hisham]], [[person/muhammad-ibn-hisham-al-mahdi-cordoba|Muhammad ibn Hisham]] appeared with the title al-Mahdi, and then [[person/sulayman-al-mustain-cordoba|Sulayman al-Musta'in]] contested with him. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.425-426} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.499-502}

## The delegation of the chamberlain
Al-Maqqari says that al-Mu'ayyad, after his death, delegated the caliphate to the chamberlain [[person/abd-al-rahman-sanjul|Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Mansur ibn Abi Amir]], whom people called Sanjul. The people of state resented moving the matter from the Mudar to the Yemen. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.425-426}

The Umayyads and the Qurashis attacked the chief of police and killed him, and deposed Hisham. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.425-426}

## The rising of al-Mahdi
In Jumada al-Akhira 399 AH Muhammad ibn Hisham appeared with twelve men, and the people gave him their allegiance and he took the title al-Mahdi bi-Allah. He imprisoned al-Mu'ayyad in the palace, then brought out the corpse of a Christian who resembled him and said it was al-Mu'ayyad; the people prayed over him and buried him. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.499-502}

The people resented several things in him, among them making wine in his palace. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.499-502}

## The struggle
His uncle Hisham ibn Sulayman rose against him with the title al-Rashid; he defeated him and killed him as a captive. Then Sulayman ibn al-Hakam al-Musta'in came seeking help from the Christians, and a famous battle took place in which thirty-five thousand were killed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.499-502}

Ibn Abd al-Jabbar was defeated and hid, and Sulayman was given allegiance in Shawwal 400 AH. The Berbers and the Romans raided Cordoba and plundered it. Then Ibn Abd al-Jabbar returned from Toledo with an army of Christians, defeated Sulayman and entered Cordoba. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.499-502}
