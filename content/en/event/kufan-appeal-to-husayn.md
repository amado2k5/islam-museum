## Overview
In 60 AH the people of [[place/kufa|Kufa]] sent their messengers to [[person/husayn-ibn-ali|Husayn ibn Ali]] while he was in Mecca, inviting him to come to them. He sent them his cousin [[person/muslim-ibn-aqil|Muslim ibn Aqil]]. {cite:tabari-tarikh-abu-al-fadl j.5 p.347}

What is not covered here: the texts of the letters and the number of messengers, which need an additional source.

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "History Taught Me"

[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] tells the story of the Kufans' call to al-Husayn in the episodes on "the revolt of al-Husayn" in his programme [[work/allamani-al-tarikh-program|"History Taught Me"]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:07:00}.

### The letters
He says the leaders of Kufa, the capital of [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] and home of his supporters, hurried after Mu'awiya's death to write to al-Husayn asking him to come to them quickly, saying they had pledged to no one but him, and that this is what moved him toward Iraq {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:07:30}. In the four months he spent in Mecca he corresponded with them and their delegations came to him {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep19 00:17:00}. He says the letters came bearing the signatures of the leaders of Kufa until they filled several chests, and that it is said they reached forty thousand {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep22 00:01:30}. Al-Suwaidan holds that the aim was not armed revolt but to declare a leading candidate other than Yazid so the community could choose {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:05:00}.

### Muslim ibn Aqil's mission
He relates that al-Husayn wanted to make sure whether they were in earnest or only carried away by feeling, which al-Suwaidan calls the height of good sense. He sent his cousin [[person/muslim-ibn-aqil|Muslim ibn Aqil ibn Abi Talib]] to write to him with the true state of things: he would come if they were sincere, otherwise he would stay where he was {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:06:00}. On first arriving Muslim stayed with one of Kufa's notables, [[person/al-mukhtar-ibn-abi-ubayd|al-Mukhtar ibn Abi Ubayd]] {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:06:00}. When Ibn Ziyad took over Kufa and began to bear down on the people, Muslim moved to [[person/hani-ibn-urwa|Hani ibn Urwa]], a Kufan notable with great tribal strength, and then for a short time to Muslim ibn Awsaja al-Asadi, one of al-Husayn's callers {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:08:00}. People came to pledge to him until twelve thousand had given a direct pledge, in secret and under close guard {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:08:30}.

Muslim then wrote to al-Husayn a letter saying "the scout does not lie to his people," telling him that all the people of Kufa were with him and urging him to come {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:09:00}. Al-Suwaidan explains that al-Husayn was then sure of the Kufans' sincerity, that they had no imam and had not pledged to Yazid, and that he had promised to come to them if Mu'awiya died and they put him forward, so he saw himself bound to keep his promise {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:10:00}. He stresses that up to that moment there was no armed revolt and no fighting, only another candidate being put forward politically {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep21 00:02:00}.

### How it turned out
Al-Suwaidan says the Companions and relatives who counselled al-Husayn agreed that the promises of the people of Kufa could not be trusted, since they had betrayed his father before {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:15:30}. He says the pro-Umayyad leaders of Kufa sensed how grave things were when al-Husayn's supporters began celebrating Muslim and pledging to him, and decided on an armed clash before al-Husayn arrived {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan alamani-al-tarikh/ep20 00:18:00}. This ended in [[event/killing-of-muslim-ibn-aqil-60|the killing of Muslim ibn Aqil]].
