## Summary
The Shabir Ally–William Lane Craig debates of 2002 and 2013 on the resurrection of Christ and the concept of God. {cite:reasonablefaith-concept-of-god-debate}

## The line of the debates
A disciplined historical-doctrinal contest: Christ's resurrection and crucifixion and the concept of God, argued with source-criticism and history on both sides.

## The exchange — what each side said
- **Ally (the resurrection, 2002)**: attacked the solidity of the Gospel evidence for a bodily rising — divergent accounts, no early independent witnesses, and the Qur'anic reading of Jesus' fate.
- **Craig**: ran his known method — four facts agreed by most historians (the burial, the empty tomb, the appearances, the disciples' faith) and resurrection as their only sufficient explanation.
- **Ally (the concept of God, 2013)**: only Tawhid preserves the essence's greatness — any division of the divine essence yields plurality; reason and revelation stand on pure unicity.
- **Craig**: re-ran the personal-creator and ontological arguments, holding the Trinity enriches rather than contradicts unity.
- **The verdict**: a sober historical-philosophical exchange; each side held its method and the question stayed between the two scriptures. {cite:reasonablefaith-concept-of-god-debate} {cite:aomin-pretoria-debate-2013}
