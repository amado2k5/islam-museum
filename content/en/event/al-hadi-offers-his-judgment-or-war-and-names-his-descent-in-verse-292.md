## Overview
The rest of the wafir poem on p. 308: if they come to "my praiseworthy judgment" they are safe from his war, and if not they should stand for the war, for the Merciful has given him victory and an army and God's party is victorious; then he says he is son of Muhammad and his father is Ali, that he is death to whoever tries to deceive him and rain to his friend, and the editor refers the reader back to pp. 158-159. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.307}

## Detail
The poet goes on over pp. 307-308: if you come to "my praiseworthy judgment" you are safe from the vicissitudes of my war and its changing fortunes, and if not, stand for the war, for he will fight them "by the power of the Possessor of Majesty" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}. He says the Merciful has given him victory and aid with might and money and an army of great strength that cannot be attacked, that "God's party is victorious and strong and the party of aggression is brought low by ruin", and that he and his are not people of betrayal {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}. He then names his descent: he is the son of [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] and his father is Ali, his grandfather the best who wore sandals, and he follows in their steps, and the line ends with a maternal uncle; the book gives this lineage as the poet's claim and the museum does not discuss it {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}. He calls himself "the death that cannot be avoided" for whoever tries to deceive or kill him, and rain for his friend when the friend comes seeking his gift, and says he plunges through every terror to his enemy and stands steady at the clash of duels {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}. In the footnote on p. 308 the editor refers the reader to what went before on pp. 158-159, where other verses of al-Hadi stand in the Najran setting, see [[event/abu-muhammads-verse-reply-and-al-hadis-answering-verses-286|Abu Muhammad's reply and al-Hadi's answering verses]]; he does not say the two poems are one {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.308}. It follows [[event/al-hadi-describes-his-armies-and-asks-them-to-spare-their-blood-in-verse-292|his verses on his armies]]. The poem is given in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
