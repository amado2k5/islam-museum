## Overview
[[person/muhammad|The Prophet]] died in [[place/medina|Medina]], in the house of [[person/aisha|Aisha]], on a Monday, after an illness that began in late Safar or early Rabi' al-Awwal of the eleventh year of the Hijra, as Ibn Ishaq relates. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/291} This page gives Ibn Hisham's transmission of Ibn Ishaq on the illness and the death, and attributes each saying to its narrator.

## The beginning of the illness
Ibn Ishaq says that the complaint began "on some nights left of Safar, or at the beginning of Rabi' al-Awwal". {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/291} What was first begun, it is mentioned, was that he went out to al-Baqi' in the depth of the night and asked forgiveness for its people, then returned, and when morning came his pain began. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/291} In a report of Abu Muwayhiba, his freedman, he was commanded to ask forgiveness for the people of al-Baqi', and he said to him that he had been given the keys of the treasuries of the world and immortality in it, then Paradise, and was given the choice between that and meeting his Lord and Paradise, and he chose meeting his Lord. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/292}

## Nursing in Aisha's house
Aisha relates that he came back from al-Baqi' while she was complaining of a headache, and he said to her: "Rather, I, by God, O Aisha, my head!" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/292} He would go round among his wives until the pain grew severe while he was in the house of Maymuna, and he asked their leave to be nursed in her house, and they gave him leave. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/292} He went out to her house walking between two men, one of them [[person/al-fadl-ibn-al-abbas|al-Fadl ibn al-Abbas]], and the other [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]], as Ibn Abbas reported, though Ubayd Allah relates the hadith from Aisha without naming him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/298}

## Going out to the people
The pain grew more severe, and he ordered water to be poured over him from seven waterskins drawn from different wells, until he could go out to the people and charge them. He was seated in a tub belonging to Hafsa and water was poured over him until he said: "Enough, enough." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/299} Then he went out with his head bound and sat on the pulpit. The first thing he spoke was to pray over the martyrs of Uhud and ask forgiveness for them. He then said that a servant of God had been given the choice by God between the world and what is with Him, and he had chosen what is with Him. [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] understood that he meant himself, and wept. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/299} Then he ordered the open doors onto the mosque shut, except the door of Abu Bakr. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300} He also counselled the Muhajirun concerning the Ansar. In one report he said: "If I were to take any of the servants as a close friend, I would have taken Abu Bakr as a close friend." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/299}

## The dispatch of Usama
He had been slow to see the people set out on [[event/expedition-of-usama|the dispatch of Usama ibn Zayd]]. People had spoken of his command, saying that he was a young man over the elite of the Muhajirun and the Ansar. He preached and said: "Send out the dispatch of Usama," even if his father had been fit for command before him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300} [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama]] set out with his army until they halted at [[place/al-jurf|al-Jurf]], and they remained there waiting for what God would decree concerning the Prophet. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300} Then Usama came down to Medina when the illness grew heavy. He came in to him when he had become unable to speak. He began to raise his hand to the sky and then place it on him, and Usama knew that he was praying for him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/301}

## The laddu
Some of his wives and some Muslim women gathered, and his uncle [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas]] was with him. They agreed to give him a laddu, that is, to pour medicine into the side of his mouth. When he recovered he said: "Who did this to me?" They said: "Your uncle." Al-Abbas said: "We feared that you had pleurisy." He said that this was a disease God would not have sent upon him, and he ordered that everyone in the house be given the laddu, except his uncle, as a punishment for what they had done. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300}-{cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/301}

## The prayer with the people
Aisha relates that he said: "Command Abu Bakr to lead the people in prayer." She said that Abu Bakr was a tender man, weak of voice, who wept much when he recited the Qur'an. He returned and repeated the order, and she repeated her words, and he said: "You are the companions of Joseph." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/301} She says she only said that because she wished that he might be turned away from Abu Bakr, and she knew that people did not love a man who had stood in his place and would think ill omens of him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/303} In the report of Abd Allah ibn Zam'a, when Bilal called the people to prayer, the Prophet said: "Tell someone to lead the people in prayer." [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] prayed, since Abu Bakr was absent. When the Prophet heard his voice he said: "Where is Abu Bakr? God and the Muslims refuse that." Abu Bakr came and prayed. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/303} Umar later told Ibn Zam'a that he had thought only that the Messenger of God had commanded it. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/303}

## Did the Prophet appoint a successor?
Ibn Ishaq relates on the authority of al-Qasim ibn Muhammad that had it not been for a saying of Umar at his death (that is, Umar's death), the Muslims would not have doubted that the Prophet had appointed Abu Bakr as successor. But Umar said: "If I appoint, one better than me has appointed; and if I leave them, one better than me has left them." So the people knew that the Prophet had appointed no one, and Umar was not suspected of partiality toward Abu Bakr. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/302} (See [[event/saqifa|the gathering at Saqifa]] for the disagreement on this.)

## Monday
In the report of Ibn Abi Mulayka, the Prophet went out on Monday, his head bound, at dawn, while Abu Bakr was leading the people in prayer. The people made way, and Abu Bakr stepped back, and the Prophet pushed him in the back and said: "Lead the people in prayer," and he sat beside him and prayed sitting on the right of Abu Bakr. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/302} In another report he lifted the curtain and opened the door, and the Muslims almost lost themselves in their prayer out of joy at seeing him. He signalled to them to stand firm, smiled, and went back. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/302} On that day Abu Bakr told the Prophet that he saw him well that morning by grace and bounty from God, and that the day was the day of the daughter of Kharija, so might he go to her? He said: "Yes." So Abu Bakr went out to his family at al-Sunh. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/304}

## The word of al-Abbas to Ali
Abd Allah ibn Abbas relates that Ali went out that day from the Prophet's presence, and people asked him how he had risen. He said: "He has risen, praise be to God, in health." Then al-Abbas took him by the hand and said: "By God, after three days you will be a servant of the staff." He said that he had recognised death in the Prophet's face as he used to recognise it in the faces of the Banu Abd al-Muttalib, and he asked Ali to go with him to ask him who would have the command. Ali said: "By God, if we are refused it, no one will give it to us after him." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/304} Ibn Ishaq relates the report without comment, and the readings of scholars of its meaning vary. See [[event/saqifa|Saqifa]].

## The death
Aisha says that he returned to her that day when he came in from the mosque and lay down in her lap. A man of the family of Abu Bakr came in with a green toothstick, and the Prophet looked at it in a way that she knew he wanted it. She chewed it for him until it was soft, and he used it to clean his teeth as strongly as she had ever seen him do. Then she found him growing heavy in her lap, and his gaze was fixed, and he was saying: "Rather, the Highest Companion in Paradise." She said: "You were given the choice and you chose." And the Messenger of God was taken. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/304}-{cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/305} Aisha says: "He died between my sahr and my nahr, and in my turn." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/305} She also related that he often said that God does not take a prophet until He gives him the choice. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/301} His death was "when the forenoon grew intense" of that day, {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/304} and it is traditionally dated to the twelfth of Rabi' al-Awwal; see the date field.

## Umar's words and Abu Bakr's sermon
When he died, Umar stood up and said that men of the hypocrites claimed that the Messenger of God had died, and that he had not died, but had gone to his Lord as Moses son of Imran went, and that he would return. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/305} Abu Bakr came when the news reached him, and he went in to the Prophet, who lay covered in a corner of the house, and uncovered his face, kissed it and said: "My father and my mother are your ransom. As for the death that God has written for you, you have tasted it, and no death will touch you after it, ever." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/306} Then he went out while Umar was speaking to the people, and said: "Gently, O Umar, be silent." When Umar refused, Abu Bakr turned to the people and they left Umar. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/306} He said: "Whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad has died; and whoever worshipped God, God is living and does not die," and then recited the verse [[passage/quran-3-144|3:144]]. Abu Hurayra said that Umar said that when he heard Abu Bakr recite it, he was struck until he fell to the ground, unable to bear his legs, and knew that the Prophet had died. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/306} In another report from Ibn Abbas, Umar explains that what moved him to his statement was that he was reading the verse about the middle community (ummah wasat), and thought that the Prophet would remain among them until he bore witness over their last deeds. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/312}; the verse is [[passage/quran-2-143|2:143]].

## After the death
See [[event/saqifa|the gathering at Saqifa]] for what passed between the Ansar and the Muhajirun before his burial, and [[event/burial-of-the-prophet|the burial of the Prophet]] for his preparation, in which those who washed him were Ali, al-Abbas, al-Fadl, Qutham ibn al-Abbas, Usama and Shuqran, his freedman, and Aws ibn Khawli came in with them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/312}

## For young visitors
The Prophet was ill for several days in Aisha's house. He kept urging people to do good, and Abu Bakr led the people in prayer during his illness. Then he died on a Monday, and the Companions were very saddened. Abu Bakr stood up and reminded them that God is living and does not die, and recited to them a verse of the Qur'an, and the people grew calm.

## Notes for researchers
- The account rests on Ibn Hisham's transmission of Ibn Ishaq. Most reports are traced to al-Zuhri from Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Utba from Aisha. For comparison with the reports in the two Sahihs, see the Sahih pages.
- The beginning of the illness: "on some nights left of Safar, or at the beginning of Rabi' al-Awwal" (p. 291), which is Ibn Ishaq's own statement with doubt.
- On p. 304 the report about al-Abbas and Ali is one of the places where the reading of the text is disputed. We do not judge it here, and we refer to the section of views on Saqifa.

## Ibn Ishaq's detailed account
Ibn Ishaq relates that the Prophet went out at night to [[place/al-baqia|al-Baqi']] and asked forgiveness for its people; then, when morning came, his pain began. [[person/abu-muwayhiba|Abu Muwayhiba]] woke him at the Prophet's command to accompany him, and he said to him: "I have been commanded to ask forgiveness for the people of al-Baqi', and I have been given the keys of the treasuries of the world and immortality in it, then Paradise, and was given the choice between that and meeting my Lord and Paradise." He said: "No, by God, I have chosen meeting my Lord and Paradise." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/291} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/292}

He relates from [[person/aisha|Aisha]] that the Prophet came back from al-Baqi' and found her complaining of a headache in her head, saying: "O my head!" He said: "Rather, I, by God, O Aisha, my head!" Then he wished that he might die before her, so that he could shroud her and pray over her. She said: "By God, I can see you doing that, you would return to my house and marry some of your wives in it." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/292}

Then when the pain grew severe he asked his wives' leave to be nursed in Aisha's house, and they gave it. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/292} Ibn Hisham gave a list of the wives of the Prophet and their names in a connected place, and that he did not consummate the marriage with two of them, and that he consummated it with eleven. He notes that Umm Habiba he married in Abyssinia, and that Zaynab bint Jahsh had been with Zayd ibn Haritha before him. Ibn Hisham says that the marriages of Sawda, Umm Salama and Hafsa were at different times, with dowries of four hundred dirhams in most of them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/293} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/294} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/295} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/296} See [[dataset/prophets-wives-ibn-hisham|the list of wives according to Ibn Hisham]] and [[event/the-choice-of-the-prophets-wives|the choice of the Prophet's wives]].

Ibn Ishaq relates from Aisha that the Prophet went out walking between two men, one of them al-Fadl ibn al-Abbas and the other Ali ibn Abi Talib, until he entered her house. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/298} Then the pain grew worse, and he said: "Pour upon me water from seven waterskins from different wells, until I go out to the people and charge them." They seated him in the tub of Hafsa and poured water on him until he began to say: "Enough, enough." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/299}

Then he went out with his head bound to the pulpit, prayed over the martyrs of Uhud and asked forgiveness for them, and said: "A servant of God has been given the choice by God between the world and what is with Him, and he chose what is with God." Abu Bakr understood it and wept, and said: "No, we will ransom you with ourselves and our sons." The Prophet said: "Gently, O Abu Bakr," and ordered that the doors opening onto the mosque be shut, except the door of Abu Bakr. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/299} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300}

In his speech that day he counselled concerning the Ansar: "O company of the Muhajirun, be good to the Ansar; for people increase and the Ansar remain as they are; they were my secret chamber to which I retired, so do good to their good ones and overlook the faults of their wrongdoers." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300}

Ibn Ishaq relates from al-Zuhri that the Prophet ordered the dispatch of [[event/expedition-of-usama|Usama]] to be carried out and said: "If you speak of his command, you have spoken of his father's command before him," and that the people hesitated in their preparations. Usama went out with his army until they halted at al-Jurf, a farsakh from Medina, and remained waiting for what God would decree concerning His Messenger. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300}

## Markers of the report
- The report of al-Baqi' comes from Ibn Ishaq with a chain to Abu Muwayhiba from Abd Allah ibn Amr from Ubayd ibn Jubayr. The report of Aisha has a chain from Ya'qub ibn Utba from al-Zuhri from Ubayd Allah. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/291}
- The phrase "except the door of Abu Bakr" is in the book, and in Ibn Hisham's text it is also given as a variant reading ("it is narrated: except the door of Abu Bakr"). {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300}
- The well-known counsel concerning the Ansar comes from Ibn Ishaq with the chain of al-Zuhri to Abd Allah ibn Ka'b ibn Malik, and the text as in the edition. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/300}

## For Sprouts
In year 11 of the Hijra the Prophet fell gravely ill in Medina, then died in the house of his wife Aisha on a Monday. His Companions grieved deeply, and Abu Bakr reminded them that God is living and does not die, and that Muhammad His Messenger had passed like other men.

## For Explorers
The Prophet died in Medina in 11 AH (632 CE) after a short illness, in the chamber of Aisha bint Abi Bakr inside the Prophet's Mosque. His last affairs had been the Farewell Pilgrimage and enjoining the people to prayer, and in his final illness he had Abu Bakr lead the prayer. He died at sixty-three on the common account, and the people of Medina were occupied that day by the meeting at the Saqifa of Banu Sa'ida and the pledge to Abu Bakr; he was buried in Aisha's chamber where he died. The sira books record his last words on prayer, those under his care and the hereafter, and the Companions' shock until Abu Bakr addressed them with the verse on the death of messengers.

## For Scholars
The death of the Prophet Muhammad in Medina in 11 AH (632 CE), after a short illness that began months after the Farewell Pilgrimage, in Aisha's chamber in the Prophet's Mosque. The sira books record his last days: severe headache and fever, advancing Abu Bakr to lead prayer in his illness, final words on prayer, those in one's charge and the hereafter, and his death on a Monday aged sixty-three on the common account — with reported differences on day and hour. On the news a great mourning struck Medina: Umar first denied it, then Abu Bakr delivered the famous address 'Whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad has died' and recited the verse on the death of messengers, and the crowds settled. The Saqifa of Banu Sa'ida met the same day ending in the pledge to Abu Bakr; the Prophet was washed, shrouded, prayed over and buried in Aisha's chamber where he died, his tomb later lying inside the expanded mosque. The sources differ on details of his illness, age and date within the sira's bounds, and the event marks Islam's passage from the era of revelation to the era of the caliphs.

## In Abla al-Kahlawi's programme 'In the Love of al-Mustafa'
[[person/abla-al-kahlawi|Abla al-Kahlawi]] closes her programme [[work/fi-hub-al-mustapha|'In the Love of al-Mustafa']] with the death of the Prophet, saying that he departed in body after establishing the worship of God on earth, and that the city his light had lit up had its lights put out at his death. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:02:00} She relates that his illness began with a pain in his head, and that Abu Bakr understood when in his last address the Prophet said that a servant of God had been given the choice between this world and what is with God and had chosen what is with God; Abu Bakr wept bitterly, and when people wondered at his weeping he said his beloved was going to leave. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:03:00} The Prophet could no longer leave Aisha's house, she says, and asked his wives' leave to stay there; when Fatima saw him she lamented her father's distress, and he told her there would be no distress for her father after that day; then he passed to the Highest Companion between Fatima's hands and on Aisha's lap. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:04:00}

She describes the grief of his Companions: Umar came out with his sword drawn, threatening anyone who said Muhammad had died; Ali sat on the ground; Uthman was struck dumb; and Abdullah ibn Unays, she says, was struck by the news as by a thunderbolt and died of grief. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:05:00} (needs_source: the speaker gives no source for Abdullah ibn Unays dying of grief.) She relates that Abu Bakr went in, uncovered his face and kissed him, then went out to the people and reminded them of the verse that Muhammad is a messenger before whom messengers have passed away, and that whoever worshipped God, God is living and does not die, while whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad has died; Umar said it was as though he had never heard the verse before, and Medina was filled with weeping. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:06:30} She recalls Fatima's reproach after the burial to those who had poured earth upon his head. {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abla-al-kahlawi fi-hub-al-mustapha/ep27 00:07:00}
