## Summary
Revelation ceased for a time after its first descent at [[place/ghar-hira|Hira]]. The Sealed Nectar prefers, on the basis of Ibn Sa'd's report from Ibn Abbas, that the pause lasted days, and holds that the well-known claim of three years or two and a half years is not sound. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} {cite:fath-al-bari 1/27} {cite:fath-al-bari 12/360}. Then Gabriel came to him a second time with the opening verses of the revelation of [[event/second-revelation-al-muddaththir|al-Muddaththir]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.70} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2/733}.

## Its duration
Ibn Sa'd relates from Ibn Abbas what indicates that the pause lasted days. This is the view the author prefers, and indeed it is settled for him after considering all aspects. The widely reported view that it lasted three years or two and a half years is, for him, not sound in any way. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} {cite:fath-al-bari 1/27} {cite:fath-al-bari 12/360}

## The Prophet's state during it
He remained during the days of the pause dejected and grieved, seized by bewilderment and amazement. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} The author cites what al-Bukhari relates in the Book of Interpretation: he grieved so much that he repeatedly went out intending to hurl himself from the peaks of high mountains. Whenever he reached the summit of a mountain, Gabriel appeared to him and told him that he was truly the Messenger of God, and his heart would be calmed and he would return. When the pause lengthened for him, he would go out to do the same. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2/1034} The author notes in a footnote that the word "ʿadā" is spelled with an unpointed ʿayn (from the root meaning to run), and in some copies with a dotted ghayn. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69}

## Its wisdom according to Ibn Hajar
The author cites Ibn Hajar that the cessation of revelation for days was meant to remove the terror he had felt and to create in him a longing for its return. When the certainty that he was a prophet was firmly established, and his watchful expectation of the coming of revelation became a cause of his steadiness, Gabriel came to him a second time. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} {cite:fath-al-bari 1/27}

## Notes for researchers
- The preference for "days" is the author's own, as he declares. The common view in the sira books is otherwise, and the event card carries the status "disputed."

## For young visitors
After the first time revelation came down, it stopped for days, and the Prophet was very sad. Then the angel Gabriel returned and reassured him, and new verses came down ordering him to stand up and call people.

## For Sprouts
The pause of revelation: its ceasing after the first descent at Hira — the book prefers days, not years — then Gabriel came again with the opening of al-Muddaththir.

## For Scholars
The pause of revelation: the revelation's ceasing after its first descent at Hira; the Raheeq prefers Ibn Sa'd's report from Ibn Abbas that it was days, and holds the famed three years or two and a half unsound. Then Gabriel came to him a second time with the opening of Surat al-Muddaththir. Ibn Sa'd reports from Ibn Abbas what means the pause was days, which the author prefers — indeed fixes on after turning the question from every side — while the famed duration of three years or two and a half he holds in no way sound. He spent the pause sorrowful and grieving, puzzlement and wonder coming over him; the author retails Bukhari's report in the Book of Interpretation: that his grief sent him out time after time to throw himself down from the mountain peaks, and each time he reached a summit Gabriel appeared telling him he was truly God's messenger, so his breast calmed and he returned — and when the pause lengthened he did the like again.
