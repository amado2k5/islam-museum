In Ramadan of the fifth year the Prophet went out to the sanctuary, where a great gathering of Quraysh sat — its chiefs and grandees — and he stood among them reciting Sura al-Najm without warning. They had never heard God's speech before, their habit being to counsel one another with the words "do not listen to this Qur'an and drown it in noise" — and they abandoned what they were about, listening, until he reached its close. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

When he recited "so prostrate to God and worship" and prostrated, not one of them could hold himself back — Muslim and polytheist fell prostrate — as Bukhari relates from Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

Then they were confounded when they felt the majesty of God's speech had taken hold of them; an account is reported claiming they had lied about him, fabricating that he had honoured their idols with the words "those exalted cranes" — which the book calls a manifest slander they invented to excuse their prostration, remarking that it is unsurprising from a people who compose falsehood. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

News of their prostration — distorted into a report that Quraysh had become Muslim — reached the [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|emigrants in Abyssinia]], who returned in Shawwal; when they learned the truth of the matter some went back, and the rest entered Mecca only in concealment or under protection. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
