## Summary
[[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]] was one of the Ansar's chieftains, fought at Badr, held the judgeship of Palestine and settled in Syria. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 391} This card gathers two pieces of his late counsel, his words to his son about belief in the decree and his words to al-Sunabihi in an illness, then his death and what is said of his grave. The texts of the Sunan and of Sahih Muslim were not among the scans read, so both reports are given as the biographical books and the Hilya have them.

## Background
Umar sent Ubada to Syria as judge and teacher; he stayed at Homs and then moved to Palestine. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 1 p. 461} Ibn Abd al-Barr reports from al-Awza'i that he was the first judge of Palestine, and that he disagreed with Mu'awiya over a question of currency exchange; Mu'awiya answered him roughly and Ubada said: I will not live in one land with you ever. He went to Medina, and Umar sent him back to his post, writing to Mu'awiya: you have no authority over Ubada. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 1 p. 461} He was, as far as al-Dhahabi had heard, a tall, large and handsome man. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 391}

## Counsel to his son on belief in the decree
Abu Nu'aym relates from Ibrahim ibn Abi Abla from Abu Hafs that Ubada said to his son: my son, you will not find the reality of faith until you know that what befell you could not have missed you and what missed you could not have befallen you. He then said he had heard the Prophet say that the first thing God created was the pen; it was told to write, and asked what to write, and was told to write the measures of everything until the Hour; and he closed by saying he had heard the Prophet say: whoever dies on other than this is not of me. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 5 p. 248} Abu Nu'aym calls this route gharib, Yahya being alone in relating it from al-Walid, and notes that Ibrahim also related it from Abu Yazid al-Awdi from Ubada in similar words. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 5 p. 248} He does not say it was spoken as Ubada was dying; he gives it as advice to his son.

## His words to al-Sunabihi in his illness
Abu Nu'aym relates from Mahmud ibn al-Rabi': we were with Ubada ibn al-Samit when he fell ill, and [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-usayla-al-sunabihi|al-Sunabihi]] came in. Ubada said: whoever would like to look at a man as if raised above seven heavens and who then acted according to what he saw, let him look at this one. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 5 p. 129} In a report from Ibn Muhayriz: we visited Ubada, and when he saw him coming he said the same. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 scan vol. 5 p. 129} Ibn al-Athir and Ibn Abd al-Barr add that when al-Sunabihi reached him Ubada said: if I am asked I will testify for you, if intercession is granted I will intercede for you, and if I am able I will do you good. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 1357} {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 841} Sifat al-Safwa gives the first part under al-Sunabihi's entry. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 803} The books read say he was ill; they do not say he was on his deathbed.

## His death and grave
Al-Dhahabi says Ubada died at al-Ramla in 34 AH aged seventy-two. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 3 p. 12} Elsewhere he says he died at al-Ramla, or it is said at Bayt al-Maqdis. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 391} Ibn Abd al-Barr gives his death at al-Ramla in 34, or it is said at Bayt al-Maqdis. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 2 p. 469} In another place he says he died in Palestine and was buried at Bayt al-Maqdis, where his grave is well known to this day, and quotes Damra, from the men of Abu Salama, that his grave is at Bayt al-Maqdis. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 1 p. 461}

## Where the reports differ
The place of death is given as al-Ramla or Bayt al-Maqdis; Ibn Abd al-Barr also mentions a report that he died in Medina, but holds the Palestine death to be the better known and more widely reported. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 1 p. 461} On the date, al-Dhahabi quotes Ibn Sa'd as having heard some say he lived on until he died in Mu'awiya's caliphate. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 3 p. 12} The books read do not give what Ubada left, nor who washed or prayed over him.

## Sources and documentation
The card rests on Abu Nu'aym's Hilyat al-awliya', Ibn al-Athir's Usd al-ghaba, Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Isti'ab, al-Dhahabi's Siyar and Ibn al-Jawzi's Sifat al-safwa. Page numbers are scan pages as read. The Sunan and Sahih Muslim were not in the scans, so nothing particular to them is stated; checking the Hilya wording against those texts remains for a later pass.
