## Overview
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports, introducing it with “they say”, that [[person/ubayd-allah-ibn-ziyad|Ubaydallah ibn Ziyad]]'s client Mihran advised him to seek the protection of the Azd through al-Harith ibn Qays. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 281} Lamps were left burning in his house that night as a decoy, and al-Harith took him to Mas'ud ibn Amr, chief of the Azd, who had earlier sheltered his father Ziyad. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 282} {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 283}

## Basra without a governor
The crowd broke open the prison, and Basra was nine days without a governor before choosing Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 283} Ubaydallah went to Syria with a guide of Yashkur. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 284} In Damascus he urged Marwan, who had been about to go over to Ibn al-Zubayr, to claim the caliphate. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 285} Later a Kharijite stabbed Mas'ud ibn Amr at the dawn prayer; the Azd blamed Tamim, and al-Ahnaf paid a thousand camels, the blood-money of kings. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 287}
