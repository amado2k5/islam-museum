## Overview
Ali ibn Muhammad relates from [[person/muhammad-ibn-sulayman-al-kufi|Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Kufi]] that they reached Yemen fifty-odd days before [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] arrived; the crops had dried from thirst, and he saw cattle, sheep and other beasts falling from leanness and exhaustion. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.65} They came to a people in a great feud, and when these learned they were al-Hadi's men they put the feud aside, so that not two of them fought, awaiting him; al-Kufi and his companions told them of him and that he was coming, and saw in them a longing for safety and awe of him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.66} The text names neither the people nor the place; the context of the preceding reports ties it to his first journey in 280 AH, but not by an explicit statement.

## The narrator's praise
The book then gives a long passage of praise in which the narrator ascribes to al-Hadi signs and proofs that dazzled minds, brilliant knowledge, shining scrupulousness and all-embracing goodness, and calls his coming to Yemen "a mercy to the worlds and a proof of God against the wrongdoers"; it adds that the Yemenis wrote asking him to come to their land to set things right among them after strife, the loss of religion, killing, seizure of property, violation of what is sacred and cutting of the roads. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.66} This is praise from a Zaydi narrator, attributed to him and not stated by the museum.

## The delegation from Najran
Those who came to him from [[place/najran|Najran]] were Shakir, Thaqif, Wadi'a, Yam, the Ahlaf and a group of Banu al-Harith; al-Hadi agreed to what they asked, they having written to him in his own country asking it, and this was on the last day of Jumada I, a Monday, of a year given as "four"; the sentence continues on p. 67, not yet read, so the date is not fixed here. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.66}
