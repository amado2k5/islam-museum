## Overview
al-Suyuti, quoting al-Dhahabi, says that in 143 AH the scholars of Islam began to record in writing hadith, jurisprudence and exegesis (tadwin). {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424}

## What al-Dhahabi mentions
According to the report, Ibn Jurayj wrote in Mecca, Malik wrote the Muwatta' in Medina, and al-Awza'i wrote in Syria. Ibn Abi Arawba, Hammad ibn Salama and others wrote in Basra, Ma'mar in Yemen, and Sufyan al-Thawri in Kufa. Ibn Ishaq wrote the Maghazi, and Abu Hanifa wrote on jurisprudence and opinion. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424}

Shortly after, Hushaym, al-Layth and Ibn Lahi'a wrote, then Ibn al-Mubarak, Abu Yusuf and Ibn Wahb. Knowledge was increasingly recorded and arranged into chapters, and books of grammar, language, history and the days of the people were written down. Before this era, the imams used to speak from memory, or narrate from unarranged written pages. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.424}

The event took place in the caliphate of [[person/al-mansur|al-Mansur]]. The Gregorian date is derived.

## Other accounts of the beginning
- In the introduction to [[work/irshad-al-sari|Irshad al-Sari]], [[person/al-qastallani-shihab-al-din|al-Qastallani]] says the first to order hadith compiled in writing was [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|'Umar b. 'Abd al-'Aziz]], fearing it would be lost, citing his letter to Abu Bakr b. Muhammad b. 'Amr b. Hazm in Muhammad b. al-Hasan's recension of the Muwatta' and his letter to the provinces reported by Abu Nu'aym and cited by al-Bukhari; he quotes al-Harawi that the Companions and Successors did not write hadith down except the book of alms-dues and a little else. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 9}
- He relays from the introduction to Fath al-Bari that the first compilers were al-Rabi' b. Subayh, Sa'id b. Abi 'Aruba and others, each chapter separately, and that then Malik compiled the Muwatta' in Medina, Ibn Jurayj in Mecca, al-Awza'i in Syria, al-Thawri in Kufa and Hammad b. Salama in Basra. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 9-10}
- In the introduction to [[work/jami-al-usul|Jami' al-Usul]], [[person/ibn-al-athir-al-jazri|Ibn al-Athir]] reports the disagreement over the first book written in Islam: Ibn Jurayj's, or Malik's Muwatta', while some say the first to compile by chapters was al-Rabi' b. Subayh in Basra. {cite:jami-al-usul-arnaut-2016 p. 50}
