## Overview
Al-Baladhuri reports that [[person/uqba-ibn-nafi|Uqba ibn Nafi al-Fihri]] raided [[place/ifriqiya|Ifriqiya]] with ten thousand Muslims, conquered it, and laid out [[place/qayrawan|al-Qayrawan]]. The motive was [[person/muawiya-ibn-khudayj|Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj]], and it is said that Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan appointed him over the Maghrib. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.319-320}

## The site
The site had been a thicket of trees and tamarisk that no beast of prey or serpent could approach. Uqba was a righteous man whose prayers were answered, so he prayed to his Lord, and all of it went away, until beasts carried their young away in flight. This is what al-Baladhuri relates. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The building
Al-Baladhuri relates from Musa ibn Ali that Uqba laid it out, and the people built houses and dwellings with him, and he built the congregational mosque. Several people of Ifriqiya narrated that he was shown in a dream the one who would call to prayer on the site, so he built the minarets there and then the mosque. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## After Uqba
Maslama ibn Mukhallad al-Ansari appointed his client Abu al-Muhajir governor of the Maghrib. Then Yazid restored Uqba to his post. Abu al-Abbas Muhammad ibn al-Ash'ath al-Khuza'i repaired the city and mosque, and then al-Mansur removed him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.322}

## The date
The date is about 50 AH, in the governorship of Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj over Egypt; the conversion is derived. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.319-320}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports that Uqba did not like the camp Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj had set up, and came to the site of al-Qayrawan as it is today, a wooded valley haunted by wild beasts and vermin. He called out three times at the top of his voice, "O people of the valley, depart, for we are settling here," then planted his lance and said, "This is your qayrawan." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 264} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 265} In al-Layth's version the snakes and scorpions came out while the people watched; Ziyad ibn Ajlan reports from al-Layth that for forty years afterwards no snake or scorpion could be found there even for a thousand dinars. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 265} Later Hassan ibn al-Nu'man settled at the site of al-Qayrawan and built its congregational mosque. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 271}
