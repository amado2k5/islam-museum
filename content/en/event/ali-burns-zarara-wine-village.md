## Summary
al-Sallabi reports that Ali ordered the burning of a village in Kufa called Zurarah where wine was sold {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 269}. This report is presented in the book under the chapter on Ali's market oversight and is quoted from Abu 'Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 269}.

## The Report
Ali looked toward Zurarah and asked about it; they told him it was a village where meat was butchered and wine was sold, so he walked until he reached it and said: "Bring me fire and set it ablaze, for evil consumes itself" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 269}. The book states that the fire burned from the western side of the village until it reached the orchard of Khawsta ibn Jabrun {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 269}.

## Context
The book notes that Zurarah was a quarter in Kufa named after its founder, Zurarah ibn Zayd {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 269}, and that Ali was severe in condemning anyone who sold wine {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 269}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not provide legal evidence for burning property because of selling wine, confining itself to attributing the report to al-Amwal and works on hisbah (market and moral oversight).
- In its original source, the report may be abridged, and the book al-Amwal should be consulted before relying on it.
- This material is a subject of debate regarding the assessment of fixed and discretionary punishments; the book presents its view as is without settling the ruling here.
