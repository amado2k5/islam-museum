## Overview
Two months after [[person/muizz-al-dawla|Mu'izz al-Dawla]] entered Baghdad and deposed [[person/al-mustakfi-billah|al-Mustakfi]], [[person/nasir-al-dawla|Nasir al-Dawla]] al-Hasan ibn Abd Allah ibn Hamdan, lord of [[place/mosul|Mosul]], marched on Baghdad with Tuzun's Turks. A war followed from late Sha'ban 334 AH to the end of Dhu al-Hijja of that year. [[person/ibn-miskawayh|Miskawayh]] narrates it in detail in [[work/tajarib-al-umam|Tajarib al-umam]]; it ended when the Daylamites crossed to the east bank and Nasir al-Dawla was routed. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125} It is part of the struggle between the Buyids and the Hamdanids for Iraq after [[event/buyid-entry-baghdad-334|the Buyid entry into Baghdad]].

## The two sides
On Mu'izz al-Dawla's side were the Daylamites, his vizier [[person/abu-jafar-al-saymari|Abu Ja'far al-Saymari]], the commander Isfahdost, and the caliph [[person/al-muti-billah|al-Muti' li-llah]], who marched with him to Ukbara. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.123} On Nasir al-Dawla's side were Tuzun's Turks; [[person/abu-jaafar-ibn-shirzad|Abu Ja'far ibn Shirzad]], who ran Baghdad on his behalf; his cousin [[person/al-husayn-ibn-said-al-hamdani|Abu Abd Allah al-Husayn ibn Sa'id ibn Hamdan]]; Yanal Kusa; Takin al-Shirzadi; and "the Qarmatians among Nasir al-Dawla's men". {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.122} Miskawayh notes that Nasir al-Dawla did not have the Friday sermon read for al-Muti' or mention his name in it, and that he struck dinars and dirhams with the die of the year 331 in the names of al-Muttaqi, Nasir al-Dawla and Sayf al-Dawla. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.122} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.123}

## The war begins
On Friday, nine days before the end of Rajab, Mu'izz al-Dawla sent Musa Fiyadha and Yanal Kusa to [[place/ukbara|Ukbara]] as his vanguard towards Mosul; Yanal Kusa and Ibn al-Barid fell on Musa Fiyadha, took his baggage and went over to Nasir al-Dawla. Ibn Shirzad went into hiding on Monday 9 Sha'ban. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.120} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121} Nasir al-Dawla camped at [[place/samarra|Samarra]] four days before the end of Sha'ban and fighting began at Ukbara; on Thursday 4 Ramadan Mu'izz al-Dawla marched to Ukbara with the caliph. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121}

Nasir al-Dawla reached Baghdad on Wednesday 10 Ramadan and camped on the west bank below Qutrubbul, after burning his own stores and those of his men that were in the boats, because the Daylamites had got the upper hand. The Daylamites crossed to the west bank, sacked [[place/tikrit|Tikrit]] and then Samarra, and came down to Baghdad on the west bank with Mu'izz al-Dawla and the caliph, while the Turks faced them on the east bank. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121} Once Mu'izz al-Dawla was on the west bank, Nasir al-Dawla crossed to the east and camped at Raqqat al-Shammasiyya. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.122}

## Blockade and dearth
On Sunday, two days before the end of Ramadan, al-Husayn ibn Sa'id attacked Mu'izz al-Dawla's force on the water, drowned some of them and took their boats. On Thursday 2 Shawwal, fifty Daylamites in Nasir al-Dawla's army turned their shields against his troops at the trench of Qati'at Umm Ja'far, and his men fled. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.122} But the Qarmatians and Takin al-Shirzadi surrounded Mu'izz al-Dawla's camp on the west bank so that he could get neither fodder nor anything else; Miskawayh reports from Abu Ali al-Hasan ibn Harun that he bought a kurr of fine flour for Mu'izz al-Dawla for twenty thousand dirhams. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.122} On the east bank bread was five ratls a dirham because flour came down by boat from Mosul; on the west bank a single ratl cost a dirham and a quarter, when it could be found, since Nasir al-Dawla stopped supplies and his Bedouin roamed the west bank. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.123}

Ibn Shirzad enlisted the common people and the ayyarun (street gangs) of Baghdad and put some of them on the payroll. Every day he went out on the river with armed boats full of Turks, shooting arrows at the Daylamites on the west bank. Nasir al-Dawla sent Safi al-Tuzuni across with a thousand men to surprise Mu'izz al-Dawla's camp, but Isfahdost and al-Saymari routed him; Miskawayh quotes Ja'far ibn Warqa' as saying he had never believed one man could be worth a thousand until he saw Isfahdost's charge. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.123} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.124}

## The ruse of the crossing
Mu'izz al-Dawla built some fifty-odd armed boats at Qati'at Umm Ja'far, which went out on the Tigris on Wednesday, three days before the end of Dhu al-Hijja. Al-Saymari said they were so exhausted that Mu'izz al-Dawla resolved to withdraw to al-Ahwaz. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.124} Then Mu'izz al-Dawla set up pontoons in the Sarat canal and floated them down by night to the place of the date-sellers, "because it is the narrowest place on the Tigris". He arranged with al-Saymari, Isfahdost and his picked Daylamites to cross there, while he pretended to cross from above Qutrubbul, marching at night with trumpets and torches, so that most of his enemies moved opposite him. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.124} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125}

At dawn on Saturday, the last day of Dhu al-Hijja, al-Saymari and Isfahdost crossed with a handful of men to the island opposite al-Mukharrim and from there to the east bank. Al-Saymari "was the first to risk himself", and his men, ashamed, followed. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.124} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125} Yanal Kusa had been drinking that night and fled. Ibn Shirzad sent word to Nasir al-Dawla to ride out against those who had crossed; he replied: "It has become the rule that when I ride out, the men run." So Ibn Shirzad rode out, saw the men in flight and fled with them; Nasir al-Dawla fled, and the Daylamites took the east bank. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125}

## Looting and flight
The Daylamites burned and looted on the east bank, some of the common people were killed, and many men, women and children died after fleeing barefoot in the great heat towards Ukbara, afraid because of the abuse they had heaped on the Daylamites during the fighting. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125} Miskawayh says Mu'izz al-Dawla "acted with his usual clemency" and ordered the killing and looting stopped, but the troops did not stop until al-Saymari rode out, killed several and crucified some Daylamite soldiers. The plunder was estimated at ten million dinars, because the looting fell on the merchants' quarters. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.125} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.126} Ibn al-Jawzi names Bab al-Taq and Suq Yahya among the places looted. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.646-647}

## The peace and after
Nasir al-Dawla, Ibn Shirzad and the Turks went up to Ukbara. At the end of Muharram 335 Nasir al-Dawla wrote to Mu'izz al-Dawla asking for peace without the Turks' knowledge; when they resolved to attack him, he fled from them in haste towards Mosul. Mu'izz al-Dawla had a victory letter written in al-Muti's name and sent to Imad al-Dawla and to every province. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.126}

Miskawayh also tells of "a strange ruse" in this war: a man slipped into Nasir al-Dawla's tent at Bab al-Shammasiyya by night unseen by the guards, marked where his head lay, put out the candle and came back to drive his knife into the pillow; Nasir al-Dawla had turned over in his sleep and was unhurt. The man went to Mu'izz al-Dawla to announce the killing; Mu'izz al-Dawla said, "Such a man cannot be trusted", and handed him to al-Saymari, who killed him. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.126} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.127} The war was followed by a severe famine in Baghdad, [[event/baghdad-famine-334|the Baghdad famine of 334]]. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.127}

## Between the two accounts
Miskawayh puts the crossing at dawn on Saturday, the last day of Dhu al-Hijja 334; Ibn al-Jawzi puts the rout on the first day of Muharram 335 — one day apart, at the turn of the year. {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.124} {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.646} Ibn al-Jawzi has Nasir al-Dawla coming "to plead for the caliph al-Muti'", whom Mu'izz al-Dawla had confined; Miskawayh places the caliph in Mu'izz al-Dawla's camp and says Nasir al-Dawla did not name him in the sermon. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.646-647} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.121} {cite:tajarib-al-umam-surush-v6 p.122}
