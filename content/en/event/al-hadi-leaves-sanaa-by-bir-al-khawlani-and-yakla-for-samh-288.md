## Overview
[[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] stayed some days at [[place/sanaa|Sana'a]], and on Sunday, six days remaining of Safar, he left Sana'a with [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]], leaving his brother [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] there {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.211}. He went on until he halted at a place called Bir al-Khawlani and spent the night at it; the next morning he went on until he halted at a place called Yakla, and ordered its people to gather, spoke to them, admonished them and told them what was due to God from them, and appointed over them a man of [[group/tabari-emigrants-with-al-hadi|the Tabaris]], devout, Muslim and chaste, with a party with him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.212}.

## Detail
He left the next day and halted at a place called [[place/samh-yemen|Samh]], ordered its people to gather to him and told them what was due to them and from them; he stayed at Samh some days until the month of Rabi I began and gave the people their stipends {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.212}. The editor's footnote says Bir al-Khawlani is a place with wells and that Yakla is a town and a tribe in the eastern watershed of Yemen, citing Sifat Jazirat al-Arab pp. 80 and 240 {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.212}. The book gives no coordinates for any of them and the museum does not guess them. The narrator does not name the Tabari he put over Yakla.
