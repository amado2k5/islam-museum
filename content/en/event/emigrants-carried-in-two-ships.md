## Summary
Ibn Ishaq says the Prophet's Companions who had remained in the land of Abyssinia stayed there until the Prophet sent [[person/amr-ibn-umayya-al-damri|Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri]] to [[person/negus-of-aksum|the Negus]]; the Negus carried them in two ships and they came to the Prophet while he was at Khaybar, after al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3}

## Who was in the two ships
Ibn Ishaq enumerates those who came in the two ships by their clans — sixteen men in all. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3-5}

With them were "women of the wives of the Muslims who had died there", in his wording. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/5}

The clans named in the two ships: [[group/banu-hashim|Banu Hashim]], [[group/banu-abd-shams|Banu Abd Shams]], [[group/banu-asad|Banu Asad]], [[group/banu-abd-dar|Banu Abd al-Dar]], [[group/banu-zuhra|Banu Zuhra]], [[group/banu-tahm|Banu Taym]], [[group/banu-jumah|Banu Jumah]], [[group/banu-sahm|Banu Sahm]], [[group/banu-adi|Banu Adi]], [[group/banu-amir|Banu Amir]], [[group/banu-al-harith|Banu al-Harith ibn Fihr]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3-5}

## The journey
See [[journey/return-from-abyssinia-by-sea|the return of the Abyssinian emigrants to Khaybar]]. Ibn Ishaq names neither the port nor the journey's route. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3}

## The numbers
The chapter's closing lists gather thirty-four men who missed Badr, arrived afterward, or were not carried by the Negus in the two ships, and sixteen women among the emigrant women. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/10}
