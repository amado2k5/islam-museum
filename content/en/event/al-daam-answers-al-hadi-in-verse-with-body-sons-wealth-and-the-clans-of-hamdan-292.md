## Overview
Under the heading "its answer on the tongue of al-Da'am, for Ahmad ibn Abdullah al-Tamimi, and it is said for Abdullah ibn Ahmad al-Tamimi" (p. 322) comes a poem which says, in al-Da'am's voice, that the letter of a truthful imam of Banu al-Hasan, Abu al-Husayn the pure Hashimi, has reached him and that obedience to him is a duty, promises aid with body, sons, wealth and the clans and horsemen of Hamdan, recalls his forebears who aided the imam's grandfather and says he is their successor, regards whoever withdrew from his allegiance as a denier, and urges him to resolve on the guidance God shows him. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}

## Detail
On p. 322, after al-Hadi's lines, comes the heading "its answer on the tongue of al-Da'am, for Ahmad ibn Abdullah al-Tamimi, and it is said for Abdullah ibn Ahmad al-Tamimi" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}. The "for" before Ahmad is read here as attributing the verse to al-Tamimi, in al-Da'am's voice; this is a reading of the heading that the book does not settle, and the heading gives two readings of al-Tamimi's name. The answer says "there came the letter of a truthful imam, taught, ordering us in it to the duty and the practices", says his father is the Messenger of God, best of mankind, and that he is an imam of Banu al-Hasan, "Abu al-Husayn the pure Hashimi" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}, which is the kunya of al-Hadi Yahya ibn al-Husayn; it says that forsaking him is not lawful and that "at Khumm obedience to him was made a duty on us" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}, which is the poet's reference to Khumm in the Zaydi account, and the museum gives no judgment on it. He addresses him: "you are the foremost, O son of the chosen, for we have none but you", and says "go forward to guidance and success, relying on God, for I have victory with my body, with sons, with all my wealth and with the clans of Hamdan", adding that he has "horsemen of Hamdan, sincere for God and true", that he is "your lance" and the keen edge against whoever opposes him in Yemen {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.322}. On p. 323 he says "I lead your horses and guard them from harm with a knotted lance and a keen edge", and the footnote glosses "arin" as lively {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.323}. He says al-Hadi's letter healed the breasts and woke them from the sleep of drowsiness, that he recalled the forebears who strove to aid his grandfather "in time past" and that he is their successor "in the offspring of their leader", and addresses al-Hadi "O son of the Hatim, the Hijr and the Rukn ... O son of the vicegerent, Commander of the Faithful, and offspring of al-Batul" {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.323}. He says his rope is joined to his without falsehood and his love is led by the halter, that he sees whoever withdrew from their allegiance as "a denier who turned from ignorance to an idol", that al-Hadi is "the favoured" with him, so he should trust the word of the sincere adviser and resolve on the guidance God shows him "until you are sifted and the trials uncovered", and his deeds will show in their joy {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.323}. These are words in al-Da'am's voice by the heading; the book does not say whether he spoke them himself or whether they were composed for him, and it does not identify Ahmad ibn Abdullah al-Tamimi or Abdullah ibn Ahmad al-Tamimi. It answers [[event/al-hadi-urges-al-daam-to-rise-and-to-seize-the-chance-of-yemen-in-verse-292|al-Hadi's urging of al-Da'am]]. The verdict on those who withdrew from the allegiance is put in al-Da'am's voice alone and the museum does not adopt it. The poem stands in a Zaydi book in the voice of the imam's followers; its claims, descriptions and judgments on opponents are the poet's or the narrator's, and the museum adopts none of them, repeats no word of abuse and gives no verdict.
