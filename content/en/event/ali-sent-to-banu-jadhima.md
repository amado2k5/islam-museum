## Summary
After [[event/conquest-of-mecca|the conquest of Mecca]] in the eighth year of the Hijra, [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] sent [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] to the Banu Jadhima to call them to Islam; they did not manage to say "We have embraced Islam," and instead said "We have become Sabians," so Khalid began killing some of them, and the Prophet sent [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] to look into their affair and rectify Khalid's mistake {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.108-109}.

## The Mistake
The book states that the people of Banu Jadhima did not pronounce the formula of Islam properly and said "We have become Sabians," so Khalid did not recognize that this answer meant Islam and killed some of them. The book notes that the Prophet disavowed what Khalid had done before his Companions {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.108-109}.

## Ali's Mission
The Prophet sent Ali to look into their affair and put right what had happened {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.109}; the details of what Ali did with them appear in the remainder of the report, which has not all been reviewed yet. The book mentions this incident in the chapter on Ali's activities between the conquest of Mecca and the Battle of Hunayn.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book mentions the eighth year of the Hijra without specifying a day; the recorded date is derived from the conquest of Mecca.
- The remainder of the report and the outcomes of Ali's mission have not all been read yet; the book should be consulted before expanding on them.

## What Ali Did (al-Sallabi)
The book states that Ali went to them with the wealth the Prophet had sent, paid blood money to them, and compensated them for the lives and property harmed, noting that part of the text in this passage is effaced; when he returned to the Prophet and informed him, he said, "You have done right and acted well" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.109}. The book considers that this mission removed the grief in the hearts of Banu Jadhima, and that Khalid's killing of them was a misinterpretation and mistaken judgment for which the Prophet neither punished nor dismissed him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.109}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book refers the report to Ibn Hisham and states that its chain is weak while having corroborating reports; this is presented as is.
