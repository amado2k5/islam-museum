## Overview
The fortress called Babylon is the one that came to stand in al-Fustat. Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports from al-Layth ibn Sa'd that the Persians began building it and the Romans completed it after the Persians left Egypt. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 52} Inside the Qasr was a Roman commander called [[person/al-uayrij|al-U'ayrij]], serving under [[person/al-muqawqis|al-Muqawqis]]. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 85}

## The road to the fortress
[[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] reached the fortress after long fighting at [[place/al-farama|al-Farama]], [[place/bilbays|Bilbays]] and [[place/umm-dunayn|Umm Dunayn]], and wrote to [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] for reinforcements. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 87} Umar sent four thousand, each thousand under a man worth a thousand: [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]], [[person/al-miqdad-ibn-amr|al-Miqdad ibn Amr]], [[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]] and [[person/maslama-ibn-mukhallad-al-ansari|Maslama ibn Mukhallad]]; others say the fourth was [[person/kharija-ibn-hudhafa-al-adawi|Kharija ibn Hudhafa]]. He wrote that twelve thousand would not be defeated for lack of numbers. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 91} In a report from Yazid ibn Abi Habib, Umar sent al-Zubayr with twelve thousand. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 92}

## The siege
The Romans had dug a trench round the fortress with gates in it, and strewn iron caltrops in the gate yards. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 88} On arriving, al-Zubayr rode round the trench and posted men along it, and a mangonel was set up. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 92} Ibn Abd al-Hakam adds that Amr stood under the banner of Bali, "as they claim". {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 93}

The reports differ on how long it lasted. Uthman ibn Salih, from Ibn Wahb from al-Layth, says the Muslims stayed at the gate of the Qasr for seven months until they took it; Khalid ibn Yazid, from a group of the Successors, says they fought for a month. Ibn Abd al-Hakam says he heard a third account that differs from both. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 95}

## The ladder
Al-Zubayr said he gave himself to God, set a ladder against the fortress on the side of the Bath Market, climbed it and cried the takbir. Amr held the men back from crowding the ladder lest it break. Hearing the takbir, the defenders thought the Muslims had all got in and fled, and al-Zubayr came down and opened the gate. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 94} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 95} With him climbed [[person/muhammad-ibn-maslama|Muhammad ibn Maslama]], Malik ibn Abi Salsala al-Sulami and men of Banu Haram, and Shurahbil ibn Hujayya al-Muradi set up a second ladder. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 95}

Another report, from Ibn Wahb from Abd al-Rahman ibn Shurayh, says the Romans took refuge in the fortress after the Muslim horse outflanked them; Amr besieged them until they asked that some ten households be let out, then they opened to him, and he levied on them, for each man of his army, a dinar, a gown, a burnus, a turban and a pair of boots. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 88}

## Al-Muqawqis withdraws
Al-Muqawqis and the Coptic notables left by the southern gate for the Island, where the shipyard stood in the author's day, and cut the bridge; the Nile was in flood. Some elders of Egypt claimed that al-U'ayrij stayed in the fortress and left later in ships moored to it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 96} Then came the exchanges that ended in [[event/ubada-embassy-to-al-muqawqis|Ubada ibn al-Samit's embassy]] and [[event/treaty-of-babylon|the peace]]. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 97} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 103}
