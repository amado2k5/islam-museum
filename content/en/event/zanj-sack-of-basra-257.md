## Overview
In the days of al-Mu'tamid the Zanj entered Basra and its districts. Al-Suyuti describes what happened as devastation, burning and the taking of captives. The Zanj were led by the [[person/sahib-al-zanj|Sahib al-Zanj]], and [[person/abu-ahmad-al-muwaffaq|Abu Ahmad al-Muwaffaq]] was the commander of the army. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.564-565}

## Accounts of the numbers
Al-Suyuti relates from al-Suli very large figures for the dead, and that a pestilence and then earthquakes followed. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.564-565} Al-Mas'udi says that the conquest of [[place/basra|Basra]] was in 257 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.568}

Al-Mas'udi also says that people differed on the number of the dead: some who exaggerate say it cannot be counted, and some who minimise say five hundred thousand. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.577-578}

## After the battle
Al-Mas'udi describes the hunger of the people of Basra, until they ate dogs, mice and cats. He says that slave-girls descended from al-Hasan, al-Husayn and al-Abbas were sold for small sums by public auction according to their lineage. He relates from a sermon that was delivered for the Sahib al-Zanj, with its praise and its curses of some Companions and others. All of this is from the reports of al-Mas'udi and al-Suyuti. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.577-578}

## In al-Ya'qubi
[[person/al-yaqubi|Al-Ya'qubi]], among the events of 257 AH, says the man who claimed descent from the family of Abu Talib entered Basra, plundered it and burned the congregational mosque, and that a Turk called Muhammad al-Muwallad marched against him, but on hearing of it the rebel withdrew without meeting him. Among the events of 258 AH he adds that Muhammad al-Muwallad entered Basra and drove the claimant and his followers out, and that some of its people came back to find no house left to live in. {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 622} {cite:tarikh-yaqubi-houtsma-1883 vol. 2 p. 623}
