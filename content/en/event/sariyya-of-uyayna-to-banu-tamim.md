## Summary
A raid the Prophet sent in Muharram of year 9 AH under [[person/uyayna-ibn-hisn|Uyayna ibn Hisn al-Fazari]] with fifty horsemen — no Muhajir or Ansari among them — against [[group/banu-tamim|Banu Tamim]], who had incited the tribes and withheld the jizya {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 425}.

## The raid and the captives
Uyayna set out travelling by night and lying hid by day until he fell on them in the desert; they turned in flight and he took of them eleven men, twenty-one women and thirty boys, driving them to Medina where they were lodged in the house of Ramla bint al-Harith {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 425}.

## The contest of pride and the conversion
Ten of their chiefs came, called out to the Prophet and spoke with him, showing their wish for a contest of boasting; they put forward their orator [[person/utarid-ibn-hajib|Utarid ibn Hajib]], and Islam's orator [[person/thabit-ibn-qays|Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas]] answered him; then they put forward their poet [[person/al-zibriqan-ibn-badr|al-Zibriqan ibn Badr]], who recited his boasts, and Islam's poet [[person/hassan-ibn-thabit|Hassan ibn Thabit]] answered him extempore {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq pp. 425-426}.

When the two orators and two poets had finished, al-Aqra' ibn Habis said: his orator is more eloquent than ours, his poet more poetic than ours, their voices louder than ours and their words loftier than ours; so they became Muslim — the Prophet rewarded them generously and restored their women and children {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 426}.

## For young visitors
The chiefs of Banu Tamim came to Medina to recover their captives, and the two sides' orators and poets competed; when they heard the Muslims' words they admitted they were more eloquent and more truthful, entered Islam — and the Prophet restored their families to them and honoured them.
