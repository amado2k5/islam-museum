## Summary
In 582 AH Salah al-Din removed his son al-Malik al-Afdal Ali from Egypt to Damascus and granted it to him as iqta', and sent his brother al-Adil with his son al-Aziz Uthman to Egypt as his deputy. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 139}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir explains the cause: Salah al-Din had appointed Taqi al-Din deputy in Egypt with his elder son al-Afdal Ali beside him; Taqi al-Din complained that he could not collect the land tax alongside al-Afdal, who was forbearing and generous and stopped him punishing anyone, so Salah al-Din summoned his son and grew cold toward Taqi al-Din, suspecting he wanted al-Afdal out so as to hold Egypt alone. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 139} When this suspicion hardened he brought his brother al-Adil from Aleppo and sent him to Egypt with his son al-Aziz Uthman, and summoned Taqi al-Din to Syria; Taqi al-Din refused, gathered troops and prepared to march to the Maghreb to his mamluk Qaraqush, who had seized the Nafusa mountains and Barqa. Salah al-Din wrote asking him to come for a farewell and counsel; when he came, Salah al-Din detained him and enlarged his iqta' to Hama, Manbij, Ma'arra, Kafar Tab, Mayyafariqin and Jabal Jur with all their districts. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 139}
