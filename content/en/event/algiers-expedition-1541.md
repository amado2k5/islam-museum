## Summary
[[person/charles-v|Charles V]]'s great expedition against Algiers, 948/1541, and its defeat {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 224-226}.

## Preliminaries
Charles had concluded the ten-year [[event/truce-nice-945|Truce of Nice]] with France (945/1538) and turned to North Africa, landing before Algiers on 28 Jumada II 948/15 October 1541. Earlier he had secretly offered Khayr al-Din recognition as ruler of North Africa for a small tribute — hoping to detach it from Istanbul — and failed {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 223-224}.

## The two letters
The emperor wrote to governor [[person/hasan-aga-al-tushi|Hasan Agha]]: 'You know me — I am sultan… every Christian nation under my hand… if you would meet me, hand me the castle at once.' Hasan Agha answered: 'I am the servant of Sultan Süleyman… come and take the castle — but this land has a custom: an invader is given only death' {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 224}.

## The storm and withdrawal
A gale blew for days, tearing up the tents and dashing the ships together; waves cast some ashore where the defenders took their stores, and rain spoiled the powder. Every assault failed — the commander [[person/al-hajj-bashir|al-Hajj Bashir]]'s exploits standing out — and the emperor withdrew to Italy. Algiers' letter to Süleyman likened the rout to that of the People of the Elephant {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 225-226}.

## Aftermath
João III's Ramadan 948 letter to his Madrid ambassador justified evacuating Safi and Azemmour by the Sa'dis' Ottoman-supplied strength; Charles never again planned a campaign against Algiers, and so great grew the fame of Khayr al-Din and Hasan Agha — in al-Salabi's telling — that people blamed them for every calamity that befell them {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 227-228}.
