## Summary
A woman named Shuraha confessed to adultery; she was pregnant, and she had a husband who was absent in Syria {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 303}. Ali sentenced her to be flogged one hundred lashes on Thursday, then stoned her on Friday {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}.

## The Incident
The book reports from al-Sha'bi that Shuraha's patron brought her to the Commander of the Faithful, Ali, and Ali said: This woman has committed adultery and confessed. In the report, Ali said that stoning is a practice established by the Messenger of God, and that if witnesses had testified against her, they would have been the first to throw by virtue of their testimony, and the stones would have followed their testimony, "but she confessed, so I am the first to throw at her" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}. Then he threw a stone at her, and then the people threw; in the report, the stoning took place row after row {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}.

## Manner of Execution
The book mentions another report in which Ali dug a pit for her in the market, then said, "Stoning is not done like this; if you do this, some of you will kill others" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}, and then ordered the people to line up as they line up for prayer {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}.

## Pregnancy and Postponement of the Hadd
Al-Sallabi states in a separate chapter that Ali does not carry out the hadd on a pregnant woman until she gives birth, and he narrates from him a hadith concerning a slave woman of the Prophet who committed adultery, stating that the hadd is carried out after the postnatal period {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}. This hadith appears in Musnad Ahmad, and the book notes its grading in the footnote as the work of the editor, not the main text of the book {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}.

## Disagreement Among Jurists
The book presents combining flogging and stoning as a judgment of Ali, and states that the majority of scholars hold that they are not combined, without naming the reference {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 304}. Thus the view of combining them is attributed to Ali in the book, and the view against combining them is attributed to the majority without a chain of transmission in the book.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not specify the time or place of the incident.
- The phrasing "they claimed" and the attribution to Ahmad and al-Bukhari appear in the book with different wording; the original source of the text is subject to review before being cited.
