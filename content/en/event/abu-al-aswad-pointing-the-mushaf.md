## Summary
This report appears in the books of Qur'anic sciences at the start of their treatment of the vowelling of the codex, always in the hedged form 'it is said'. Al-Sabt gives it as 'it is also said that Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali...', and refers to the books of al-Anbari, al-Dani, al-Sakhawi and others. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 330} For al-Sabt it explains how vowelling began in the early Umayyad age.

## The story as told
After new peoples entered Islam, among them non-Arabs who did not know Arabic, foreignness began to threaten the language of the Qur'an; it is said that Ziyad, governor of Basra, asked [[person/abu-al-aswad-al-duali|Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali]] to set marks by which people could know God's Book, and he was slow to answer. Then Abu al-Aswad heard a reader recite a verse of [[passage/quran-9-3|al-Tawba]] with a genitive on the final conjoined word, so that the sense became God disowning His messenger, and the glaring error alarmed him; he said: God's face is too exalted to disown His messenger. Then he went to [[person/ziyad-ibn-abi-sufyan|Ziyad]] and said: I have granted what you asked. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 330}

## What he did
He applied himself and his effort ended in making the sign of fatha a dot above the letter, of kasra a dot below it, of damma a dot among the letter's parts and of sukun two dots. People began to follow his course. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 330} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331} Al-Suyuti says al-Dani followed this and that vowelling in the first age was by dots. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249}

## Several accounts
Ibn Atiyya, in the account set out in [[question/who-first-pointed-the-mushaf|Who first pointed the codex?]], gives three: Abd al-Malik and al-Hajjaj, or Abu al-Aswad by Ziyad's order, or Nasr ibn Asim. Al-Suyuti says there is disagreement: Abu al-Aswad by order of Abd al-Malik, or al-Hasan and Yahya ibn Ya'mar, or Nasr ibn Asim. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2245} Al-Sabt says many of the reports of the dotting of codices go back to Ziyad's governorship of Basra (44–53 AH), some to the time of Ubaydallah ibn Ziyad (d. 67 AH), and takes it that Abu al-Aswad's dotting was dotting for vowels; all this is probable judgement. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 329} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331}

## What can be drawn
The report does not give a basis for exact timing: it is hedged with 'it is said', and I did not read its chain in the early books (al-Anbari, al-Dani); I give it as al-Sabt relates it. It shows that the need for marking arose when error appeared in recitation, and that marking began not to change the text but to protect it; see [[concept/naqt-al-irab|dotting for vowels]] and [[concept/tajrid-al-mushaf|keeping the codex bare]].
