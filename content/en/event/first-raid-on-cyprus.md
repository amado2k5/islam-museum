## Overview
[[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]] raided [[place/qubrus|Cyprus]] by sea. It was the first naval raid of the Muslims into the Roman sea, according to al-Baladhuri from al-Waqidi. Its date is 28 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Permission
Mu'awiya asked Umar's permission to raid by sea, and he did not give it. When [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] became caliph, Mu'awiya wrote to him with the same request, and he gave permission on the condition: "If you ride the sea with your wife, then ride it with my permission, and if not, then not." {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.269}

## The raid
He set out from Acre with many ships, and his wife [[person/fakhita-bint-qaraza|Fakhita bint Qaraza]]. [[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]] took with him his wife [[person/umm-haram-bint-milhan|Umm Haram bint Milhan]]. [[person/abu-ayyub-al-ansari|Abu Ayyub al-Ansari]] and [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr al-Ghifari]] and others raided with them. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.269}

## The treaty
The lord of the island sent asking for peace, and [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] made peace with them for seven thousand two hundred dinars every year. The Muslims stipulated that its people should not fight on their behalf against whoever wanted them, and should warn the Muslims of the movement of their enemies, the Romans. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Umm Haram's mule stumbled and died, and her grave in Cyprus is called the grave of the righteous woman. Then Mu'awiya made a permanent peace with them for seven thousand dinars. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Al-Suyuti's account
In al-Suyuti, Umar wrote to Amr ibn al-As asking him to describe the sea and its riders. He answered with something that frightened him, and Umar wrote to Mu'awiya that he should never carry a Muslim on it. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.269}
