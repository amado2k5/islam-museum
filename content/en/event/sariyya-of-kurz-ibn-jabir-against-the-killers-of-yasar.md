## Overview
A report that Ibn Hisham relates, not Ibn Ishaq: "some people of knowledge told me, from one who told him, from Muhammad ibn Talha, from Uthman ibn Abd al-Rahman." {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}

## Yasar and the milch camels
In the raid on Muharib and Banu Tha'laba the Prophet took a slave called [[person/yasar-mawla-al-nabi|Yasar]], and he put him to tend his milch camels that grazed on the side of al-Jama'. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290} Some men of the Qays, a tribe of [[group/bajila|Bajila]], came to the Prophet and fell ill with the epidemic, and their spleens swelled. The Prophet said to them: if you went out to the milch camels and drank from their milk and urine, you would be well. They went out to them. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}

## Killing and punishment
When they recovered and their bellies were calm, they attacked the Prophet's herdsman Yasar, slaughtered him, drove in thorns into his eyes, and seized the milch camels. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290} The Prophet sent [[person/kurz-ibn-jabir-al-fihri|Kurz ibn Jabir]] after them, and he caught up with them, and brought them to the Prophet on his return from [[event/expedition-of-dhu-qarad|the expedition of Dhu Qarad]]; he cut off their hands and feet and blinded their eyes, that is, put out their eyes. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Ibn Hisham**: He relates the report without comment. It is in the Sahihs as the report of the "Uraniyyin", attributed to Ukl and Urayna rather than to Bajila, and in it the blinding is the requital for what they did to the herdsman. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}
- **Sunni: the jurists**: Commentators read the punishment either as a like-for-like retaliation, or as the penalty for banditry in [[passage/quran-5-33|al-Ma'ida 33]], and some say that the blinding was abrogated by the prohibition of mutilation; the specific references have not yet been added (needs_source). {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}
- **Researchers**: Modern studies discuss the variation in the reports about the people's tribe, the form of the punishment and its link to the verse on banditry, and read the report in the context of protecting herds and caravans around Medina; a specific academic reference has not yet been added (needs_source). {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}
:::

## In the museum
The report is read under three headings: prophetic medicine (camels' milk and urine for a disease of the spleen), the ruling on the apostate who wages war, and the limits of bodily punishment. The museum sets out the act and the punishment as they are related and refers the judgement to those who hold it. The report is placed in the first review tier for the blinding. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}

## Marks of the report
- "Al-Jama'" in one copy, "al-Hamy" in all the other sources. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}
- The tribal attribution of the people as "Qays Kabba of Bajila" in Ibn Hisham; in the Sahihs it is Ukl and Urayna (needs_source).
- The chain contains two unnamed narrators. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/290}
