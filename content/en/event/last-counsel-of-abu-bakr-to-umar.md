## Summary
After Abu Bakr had designated Umar as caliph he called him in alone and charged him to fear God. The counsel is short: it speaks of God's rights by night and by day, of heavy and light scales, and of the believer keeping hope and fear together. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102} It is repeated in nearly the same words in three books, and Ibn al-Jawzi traces it to the report of Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Sabit. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102}

## Setting
It was given in Abu Bakr's last illness. In Ibn al-Athir, after Abu Bakr's covenant had been read to the people he brought Umar in and told him that he had appointed him over the Messenger's Companions, urged him to fear God, and then said what follows. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} For the covenant itself see [[event/testament-of-abu-bakr-designating-umar|Abu Bakr's testament designating Umar]]. Ibn al-Jawzi reports from Ibn Sabit that when death came to Abu Bakr he called Umar and said what follows. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102}

## What the counsel says
Abu Bakr begins by telling Umar to fear God, then reminds him that God has a right in the day that He will not accept at night and a right in the night that He will not accept by day, and that no voluntary act is accepted until the obligation is fulfilled. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102} Ibn al-Jawzi's wording speaks of a deed by day and a deed by night rather than a right. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102}

Then he speaks of the scales. On the Day of Resurrection the scales of those whose scales are heavy are heavy because they followed the truth in the world and it was heavy on them, and a scale in which only truth will be placed deserves to be heavy; the scales of those whose scales are light are light because they followed falsehood, which was light on them, and a scale holding only falsehood deserves to be light. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 268} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102}

He then recalls how the Qur'an describes the two groups: God names the people of Paradise by their best deeds and passes over their bad ones, so that when Abu Bakr remembers them he tells himself he fears he will not join them; He names the people of the Fire by their worst deeds and sets aside their best, so that when he remembers them he says he hopes not to be with them. The purpose, in his words, is that a servant be hopeful and fearful, neither wishing on God what is not his nor despairing of His mercy. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102} {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 259}

Ibn al-Athir adds that the verse of ease was sent down together with a verse of hardship, and the verse of hardship with a verse of ease, so that the believer would be hopeful and fearful, neither hoping for what is not his from God nor fearing so as to throw up his hands. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 268}

## The closing
Abu Bakr ends by telling Umar that if he keeps this counsel, nothing absent should be dearer to him than death, which is coming to him; if he loses it, nothing absent should be more hateful to him than death, and he cannot escape it. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 268} Al-Muhibb al-Tabari notes that al-Razi reported it from Ibn Abi Najih with the added clause that if Umar did not keep the counsel nothing absent should be more hateful to him than death, and that it also mentions the verse of ease alongside the verse of hardship. {cite:riyad-nadira-ilmiyya-1984 scan vol. 3 p. 260}

## After the counsel
Ibn al-Athir relates that Abu Bakr then died, and when he was buried Umar went up the pulpit and spoke. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 268} See [[event/death-of-abu-bakr|the death of Abu Bakr]] and [[event/deathbed-and-burial-of-abu-bakr|his last hours and burial]].

## Another bequest in the same illness
Ibn al-Athir relates that al-Muthanna came to Medina when Abu Bakr was ill and near the end; he summoned Umar and said he hoped to die that day, and charged him that if he died he should not let evening fall before calling the people to join al-Muthanna, that no misfortune should distract them from their religion and their Lord's command, for Umar had seen what happened when the Messenger died, and that when God opened Syria he should send the people of Iraq back to Iraq, for they were its people, its governors and bold against its enemies. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 260} Abu Bakr then died at night, Umar buried him and called the people to join al-Muthanna. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 260}

## Where the reports differ
The reports differ in order and some wording. Al-Kamil says "right" and places the counsel inside the account of the appointment; in Sifat al-Safwa the text is ascribed to Ibn Sabit, whose report brings it close to the deathbed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 267} {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 102} I did not read a full chain for this counsel in the early books.
