## Overview
The book says [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] then spoke verse. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.176} In the first he says his comfort is the chase over the tops of the horses, tells one who blames him for meeting in battle not to blame him, says he and his people, the Fatimids, never tire of the meeting when the faint-hearted tire, and that if he were seen in his gear and arms on his charger he would be called a lion. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.176} In the second he calls [[group/banu-al-harith-ibn-kab|Banu al-Harith]] to come before the day of the feast, names al-Muhalhil calling out to Bakr, says peace is gone unless they return or submit to judgement, boasts that he is a Hashimi who names himself Yahya, and threatens that their livers will be trodden by horses of war. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.177}

## Notes
The printed text has the order of the lines disturbed, so the museum gives the sense and not the lines, and does not repeat the threats in them. The mention of al-Muhalhil refers to the poet of the Basus war, as the name is given in the text. It is followed by [[event/al-hadi-cuts-the-palms-of-minas-and-al-kharba-after-adha-286]].
