## Overview
al-Baladhuri, in "Futuh al-Buldan", relates how [[person/yazdegerd-iii|Yazdegerd]] ibn Shahriyar fled from al-Mada'in to Hulwan, then Isfahan, then Istakhr, then Kirman, then Sistan and Khurasan, until he was killed near [[place/merv|Merv]] at the hands of a group of followers of its marzban Mahuwayh, according to one account. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Flight and pursuit
Yazdegerd fled from al-Mada'in to Hulwan and then to Isfahan, and after the Muslims finished with the affair of Nihawand he fled to Istakhr. [[person/abd-allah-ibn-amir|Abd Allah ibn Amir]] set out for Basra in 29 AH, when all of Fars had been conquered except Istakhr and Jur. Then Yazdegerd resolved to go to Tabaristan, but went to Kirman, and Ibn Amir sent after him Mujashi' ibn Mas'ud and Hurm ibn Hayyan. The army of Mujashi' perished at al-Damaq in Bayamand, and only a few survived. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Khurasan
He went to Sistan, and its king welcomed him, but then turned against him when he asked him about the kharaj. He went to Khurasan, and [[person/mahuwayh|Mahuwayh]], marzban of Merv, received him with honour, and [[person/nizak-tarkhan|Nizak Tarkhan]] hosted him for a month. Then Nizak asked for his daughter in marriage, and Yazdegerd grew angry and called him a slave, and ordered Mahuwayh to be called to account for the revenues. Mahuwayh wrote to Nizak to stir him up, and the two combined to kill him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The death
A battle with the Turks took place at al-Janabidh, which ended with the rout of his companions and the plundering of his camp. Merv did not open to him, so he walked until he entered the house of a miller on the river al-Raghab. There are several accounts of the killing in al-Baladhuri: it is said that the messengers of Mahuwayh killed him in the miller's house; it is said that the miller was ordered to kill him and then killed the miller; it is said that the miller killed him out of greed for his crown, by dropping a millstone on him; and it is said that men of Mahuwayh found him in the water and strangled him with a bowstring and threw his body into it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

According to the same report, his son [[person/firuz-ibn-yazdgard|Firuz]] fell into the hands of the Turks, who married him to one of their women, and he stayed with them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Note
al-Baladhuri presents the accounts without preference, and the museum's entry transmits them attributed to him. The date is not stated in the file, and it needs an additional source.
