## Summary
[[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]], called the Sword of God by the chroniclers, died in his bed after his dismissal from Syria. The books preserve his words as he was dying and his bequest of his weapons, horse and house, with [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] as his executor. Reports differ on whether he died at Homs or in Medina; the majority favour Homs. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 118}

## Context
Ibn Kathir notes that Umar dismissed Khalid from Syria; Ibn Sa'd relays from al-Waqidi that Khalid came to Medina after his dismissal, performed the lesser pilgrimage, returned to Syria and stayed there until he died in the year 21. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 118} Sifat al-Safwa says that after Umar dismissed him he kept watch at Homs until he fell ill. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 235}

## His words as he was dying
Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi al-Zinad reports that he wept as death came and said: I have met so many battle-lines and there is not a handspan of my body without a sword-blow, an arrow or a spear-thrust, and here I am dying on my bed, as a camel dies; may the eyes of the cowards never sleep. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 235} In Ibn Abd al-Barr he says he had fought a hundred battle-lines or thereabouts and there was no handspan of his body without a blow, thrust or arrow-wound. {cite:isti-ab-ibn-abd-al-barr scan vol. 3 p. 199} Ibn al-Mubarak relays from Abu Wa'il that he said: I sought death where it is found and it was not decreed for me but to die on my bed; and after the profession of faith nothing in my deeds is dearer to my hope than a night spent in armour with the sky pouring rain until morning, when we raided the unbelievers. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 116}

## His bequest
Sifat al-Safwa relates that Abu al-Darda' visited him in his illness and Khalid told him that his horses and weapons stood as he had set them in the cause of God, that his house in Medina was a charity on which he had called Umar to witness, and that he had made Umar the executor of his testament; Abu al-Darda' brought the bequest to Umar, who accepted it and prayed for mercy on him. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 235} In Abu Wa'il's report he said: when I die, look to my weapons and my horse and make them provision for the cause of God. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 116} Ibn al-Athir says that as death came he set aside his horse and weapons in God's cause and appointed Umar executor. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 334}

## What he left
Juwayriya reports from Nafi' that when he died nothing was found of his but his horse, his slave and his weapons. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 118} Al-Zubayr ibn Abi Bakr, as quoted by Ibn al-Athir, says Khalid's descendants died out until none was left and that Ayyub ibn Salama inherited their houses in Medina. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 334}

## Washing, funeral and burial
Whoever washed him reported that there was no sound place on his body, only sword-cuts, spear-wounds and arrow-wounds. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 235} The reports agree that Umar came out for his funeral: in Ibn al-Mubarak's account he came to the funeral and recalled what he had said about the women of Banu al-Mughira, {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 116} and in al-A'mash's report from Shaqiq the women of Banu al-Mughira gathered in Khalid's house to weep for him, Umar was told to forbid them, and he said that there was no harm in their shedding tears for Abu Sulayman so long as there was no throwing of dust or shrieking. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 117} It was also said that no woman of Banu al-Mughira failed to lay her hair on his grave, meaning she cut it. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 334} In Sayf's report, when Umar went out to the funeral he saw a woman weeping for him in verse, was told she was his mother, and said: have women borne the like of Khalid? {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 117}

## The grave
Sifat al-Safwa says he was buried in one of the villages of Homs, a mile from the city, in the year 21. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 235} Ibn Kathir says that al-Waqidi and his scribe Ibn Sa'd, Abu Ubayd and others hold that he died at Homs in the year 21 and bequeathed to Umar. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 118} See also the card for the [[building/mashhad-khalid-hims|shrine outside Homs ascribed to Khalid]], which records al-Umari's rejection of that ascription, and the draft card for [[building/masjid-khalid-ibn-al-walid|the Masjid Khalid ibn al-Walid]], whose sources are not yet checked.

## Differing reports
Ibn Kathir sets out the two views: that he died in Medina, held by Duhaym Abd al-Rahman ibn Ibrahim al-Dimashqi and supported by reports that Umar returned from the pilgrimage and found him dead in Medina and that his mother wept over him there; then he says the view widely held by the majority is that he died at Homs. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 118} As to the year, it is 21 for al-Waqidi and 23 for another. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 114} Ibn Hajar says most hold that he died at Homs and some say Medina. {cite:isaba scan vol. 3 p. 258}
