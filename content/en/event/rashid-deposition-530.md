## Overview
After the return of the sultan [[person/masud-ibn-muhammad-saljuqi|Mas'ud]] to Baghdad, the caliph [[person/al-rashid-billah|al-Rashid]] went out to Mosul. The judges, notables and scholars gathered and wrote a record with the testimony of a group on the injustice attributed to al-Rashid: seizing money, shedding blood and drinking wine. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 669-670}

## The fatwa and the allegiance
They consulted the jurists: is the imamate of one who did this valid, and may the sultan of the time depose him if his wickedness is proven? They ruled that he could be deposed, and the city's judge Ibn al-Karkhi gave the judgement. Then they gave their allegiance to his uncle Muhammad ibn al-Mustazhir, who was titled [[person/al-muqtafi-billah|al-Muqtafi li-Amr Allah]], on 16 Dhu al-Qa'da 530 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 669-670}

Al-Suyuti quotes Ibn al-Jawzi that al-Suli mentioned the saying of the people that every "sixth" is deposed, and Ibn al-Jawzi comments that he found this strange. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 669-670}

## Note
The Gregorian date is derived from the Hijri date. The record of testimony is as al-Suyuti gives it, and passing judgement on it needs an additional source.
