## Overview
After the food had been gathered, [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] ordered each town's governor to distribute a quarter of what he had collected among the poor of his town. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.47} The report is in a Zaydi book, and its rulings are attributed to the imam as his followers relate them; they are not the museum's ruling.

## The quarter and the fodder
The narrator, who in the sequence of the chain is [[person/abu-jafar-muhammad-ibn-ubaydallah-al-abbasi|Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah]], says one of al-Hadi's servants came to consult him about "qadb", fodder, that had piled up, the editor glossing it as al-qatt. Al-Hadi told him to take all the fodder he had received that fell to the poor, set it aside, sell it and divide its price among them, since that served them better; given the fodder itself they would sell it for less. The narrator asked whether the same held for everything collected as sadaqa, raisins and the rest, and al-Hadi said yes: whatever bears the name of sadaqa has a quarter set aside for the poor; had God laid the burden on us and on the Muslims we would set aside half, and had the Muslims not needed it we would give all of it to the poor. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.47} See [[concept/sadaqa]].

## Merchants' zakat
Ali ibn Muhammad then gives what [[person/muhammad-ibn-sulayman-al-kufi|Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Kufi]] told him: that al-Hadi ordered the zakat on merchants' property to be taken and told al-Kufi to have them swear to the amount. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.47} See [[concept/zakat]].

## The jizya
Al-Kufi was also told to collect the jizya of Jews and Christians: from their well-off, the merchants, twenty-four "qafla" dirhams (full-weight dirhams, per the editor's note from al-Qamus), nothing from anyone owning less than five dinars, and twelve qafla dirhams from the middle group. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.47} As to Jewish and Christian landowners, those who held land by inheritance from their forefathers and had bought nothing from Muslims were not to be touched; for those who had bought from Muslims the ruling was that they hand it back to the Muslims and take the price, unless the earlier settlement made before al-Hadi's time was followed, since freeing them to buy Muslim property would empty Muslim tithes and property of their effect; so they were to be settled on terms the book promised to explain at its end. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.47} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.48} See [[concept/jizya]] and [[concept/ahl-al-dhimma]].
