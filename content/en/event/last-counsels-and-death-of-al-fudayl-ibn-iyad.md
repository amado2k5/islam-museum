## Summary
Al-Fudayl ibn Iyad, a man of Khurasan who settled first in Kufa and then in Mecca, stayed in Mecca until he died there at the start of 187 AH, in the caliphate of Harun, as al-Dhahabi records. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3043} The books I read do not describe his dying hours in detail as they do for others, but they gather from his sayings counsels that concern death and thinking well of God, and they report that his grave in Mecca became a place beside which people were buried.

## Context
Sifat al-Safwa says al-Fudayl was born in Khurasan in the district of Abiward, came to Kufa as an older man and heard hadith there, then gave himself to worship and moved to Mecca, where he died. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 386} Al-Dhahabi adds that he lived on gifts from Ibn al-Mubarak and similar brothers and refused the rulers' awards. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 344}

## Sayings and counsels about death
Among the sayings reported in this vein is one Ibrahim ibn al-Ash'ath heard from him: fear is better than hope while a man is healthy, but when death comes upon him hope is better. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3045} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 344} Al-Dhahabi comments that this accords with the Prophet's saying that none of you should die except thinking well of God. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3045}
Abd al-Samad ibn Yazid heard him say: do not make men your executors; how can you blame them for losing your testament when you lost it yourself in your lifetime. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3045} It is one of the few counsels attributed to him that bear directly on a testament.
Al-Dawraqi reports that he would say he wished it were spread among people that he had died; the sentence is cut off in the scan at this point. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3046} Ibrahim ibn al-Ash'ath heard him repeat in his illness a supplication taken from the Qur'an, the prayer of Job in the verse linked below, whose wording I do not reproduce. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3046} [[passage/quran-21-83|Qur'an 21:83]]
In Sifat al-Safwa, asked about his age, he recited lines saying that he had reached eighty or passed it, so what was there to hope for or await, and that the years had worn him; then his tears choked him, and Ali ibn Khashram, who was with them, finished the verse. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 387} Al-Dhahabi gives the same report as an answer to a question about his age. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 344}

## Death
Al-Dhahabi quotes Hisham ibn Ammar that al-Fudayl died on the day of Ashura in 187 AH, and says Yahya ibn al-Madini and others dated it to that year. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 344} In the Siyar he says he died at the start of 187 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3043} Ibn Khallikan, as the scanned text shows through some distortion, places the death in Muharram of that year. {cite:wafayat-ayan scan vol. 4 p. 432} Ibn Asakir quotes Ahmad ibn Hanbal as saying he reached Mecca in 187 on his first pilgrimage and found al-Fudayl already dead. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 4 p. 266}

## The grave
Sifat al-Safwa says that Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Ali al-Alathi, who visited the graves in Mecca, would come to al-Fudayl's grave, draw a line with his staff and say, 'Lord, here'; when he died at Arafat in 503 AH he was carried to Mecca, taken round the House and buried on the day of sacrifice beside al-Fudayl. {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 scan vol. 1 p. 501} Al-Khatib says Abu Muslim al-Baghdadi died at Mecca in 375 AH and was buried in al-Bata' near Fudayl ibn Iyad. {cite:tarikh-baghdad-khatib scan vol. 4 p. 815} Al-Dhahabi mentions a Sufi who stayed in Mecca as a neighbour of the sanctuary, died there and was buried by al-Fudayl. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 29 p. 369}

## Where the reports differ
The books agree that he died in Mecca in 187 AH, and differ on the day: Ashura in Hisham ibn Ammar, Muharram in Ibn Khallikan, the start of the year in al-Dhahabi's Siyar. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 15 p. 344} {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3043} I found no bequest of property in these books, nor an account of his washing and funeral prayer.
