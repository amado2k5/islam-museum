## Summary
In Mecca before the Hijra, [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] was commanded to present himself to the Arab tribes. He went out with [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] and [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] to Mina, and they visited the tribal gatherings until they reached a gathering of Banu Shayban ibn Tha'laba {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.35}.

## The Gathering
Abu Bakr stepped forward, greeted them, and asked: Who are the people? They replied: Shayban ibn Tha'laba. Abu Bakr praised them after noting that Mafruq was among them—Mafruq was eloquent and wore two braids—and asked about their numbers and strength; Mafruq said they exceeded a thousand and valued thoroughbred horses over children and weapons over milch camels, then asked: Are you the brother of Quraysh? Abu Bakr replied: If word has reached you that he is the Messenger of God, here he is {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.35}.

## The Prophet's Call and the Tribe's Response
The Prophet said he was calling them to testify that there is no god but God and that he is the servant and Messenger of God, and to shelter and support him, and he recited verses from Surat al-An'am {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.35-36}. Mafruq said he had never heard finer speech, then referred the reply to Hani' ibn Qabisa, who said they disliked abandoning their religion for a single meeting whose beginning and end were unknown; he then referred it to al-Muthanna ibn Haritha, who noted that they had a treaty with Khosrow not to cause trouble or shelter a troublemaker, and that they lay between the rivers of Khosrow and the waters of the Arabs {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.36}.

## The Prophet's Reply
The Prophet told al-Muthanna that the religion to which he called them might be disliked by kings, and then said: "Indeed, God's religion will only be aided by one who encompasses it from all its sides" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.36}, while al-Muthanna offered to protect the Prophet by the waters of the Arabs but not the waters of the Persians. The talks did not yield an agreement.

## What Ali Learned
The author states that Ali learned three things from the incident: that the Prophet did not accept from a people ready to offer support any guarantee that they personally would receive a share of rule in return, because the call is to God and its condition is sincerity; that the Prophet did not agree to a treaty that contradicted the call; and that the impact of Islam appeared in al-Muthanna and his people after they embraced Islam, for al-Muthanna became one of the commanders of the conquest of Iraq during the caliphate of Abu Bakr {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p.36-37}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book does not mention a date for these negotiations; the recorded date is derived from the Meccan period and marked as approximate.
- The naming of the negotiators and their dialogue are transmitted in the Sira; the book presents the lessons it draws from them, and no alternative analysis of why the negotiations failed is offered here.
