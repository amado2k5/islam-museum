## Overview
al-Baladhuri relays from al-Sharqi ibn al-Qatami a report about the origin of the Arabic script. Three men of Tayy gathered at Baqqa, namely Maramir ibn Murra, Aslam ibn Sidra and Amir ibn Jadra. They devised the script, measuring the spelling of Arabic against the spelling of Syriac. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## How the script spread
A people from al-Anbar learned it from them, and then the people of [[place/al-hira|al-Hira]] learned it from the people of al-Anbar. [[person/bishr-ibn-abd-al-malik-al-kindi|Bishr ibn Abd al-Malik]], brother of Ukaydir lord of Dumat al-Jandal, who was a Christian from the people of al-Hira, also learned it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Bishr then came to Mecca and taught the spelling and then the script to [[person/sufyan-ibn-umayya|Sufyan ibn Umayya]] and [[person/abu-qays-ibn-abd-manaf|Abu Qays ibn Abd Manaf]]. In al-Ta'if, [[person/ghaylan-ibn-salama|Ghaylan ibn Salama al-Thaqafi]] learned it from them. Bishr then went to the lands of Mudar, and [[person/amr-ibn-zurara|Amr ibn Zurara]] learned it from him, and was called Amr the Scribe. People in Syria learned it too. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

A man of Tabikha, one of the three Tayyi, learned it and taught it to a man of Wadi al-Qura, who settled there and taught its people. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Note
This is a single report attributed to a single narrator, and the data gives no date for it. It needs an additional source.
