## Summary
In Stories of the Prophets and al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya, Ibn Kathir tells of the deaths of [[person/harun|Harun]] and [[person/musa|Musa]] in the wilderness of the Tih, Harun first and Musa after him; Musa's death has a hadith in both Sahihs about the angel of death's visit and about his grave by the red dune. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 529} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 321} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531} I found no address or farewell speech by Musa in the books I read; the sources say rather that he disliked death at first and then came to love it, as set out below. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 509} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 158}

## The death of Harun
Al-Suddi relates, from Abu Malik and Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas, from Murra from Ibn Mas'ud and from some Companions, that God inspired Musa that He would take Harun and told him to bring him to a certain mountain; they went and found a tree like none seen before, a built house, and a bed with bedding and a sweet fragrance. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531} Harun liked the place and told Musa he wanted to sleep on the bed, but feared the owner of the house would come and be angry; Musa told him he would deal with that, and slept beside him. Death took Harun, who said 'Musa, you tricked me'; when he was taken the house was lifted, the tree vanished and the bed was raised to heaven. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532} In Ibn al-Athir's version God commands this, Harun 'died and was raised on the bed to heaven', and the editor's footnote reads the words 'you tricked me' in a sense fitting for a prophet like Musa. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 157} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 158}

When Musa returned to the Israelites without Harun they said he had killed him out of their love for him, since Harun had been gentler with them than Musa, who had some harshness; Musa answered: woe to you, he was my brother, do you think I would kill him? When they pressed him he prayed two rak'as and called on God, and the bed came down until they saw it between heaven and earth, and they believed him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532} Ibn Kathir comments that this account has 'something objectionable and strange' about it, 'and God knows best'. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532}

Al-Tabari has another report, from Amr ibn Maymun, that Musa and Harun both died in the Tih, Harun first, after they had gone out together to some caves; Musa buried him, and when he returned the Israelites told him he had killed him. Musa complained to his Lord and was told to take them to the grave; he called Harun, who came out of the grave shaking his head and said: no one killed me, I died; Musa told him: go back to your resting place. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 511}

## Musa and the angel of death
Ibn Kathir relays from al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayra, that the angel of death was sent to Musa and when he came to him Musa struck him; the angel returned to his Lord and said: you sent me to a servant who does not want death. God said: go back and tell him to put his hand on the back of an ox, and for every hair his hand covers he will have a year. Musa asked: Lord, then what? He said: then death. Musa said: then now. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 529} The hadith adds that Musa asked God to bring him near the Holy Land, a stone's throw; Abu Hurayra says the Prophet said: 'were I there I would show you his grave beside the road at the red dune'. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 529} Ibn Kathir says Muslim relates the first path through Abd al-Razzaq and Ahmad relates it from Hammad ibn Salama from Abu Hurayra as the Prophet's words. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 529} The text in [[passage/muslim-2372b|Muslim 2372b]] has the angel of death come to Musa, say 'answer your Lord', and Musa strike the angel's eye and put it out; the angel returned to God and said: you sent me to a servant who does not want death, and he has put out my eye. {cite:sahih-muslim 2372b}

Ibn Kathir also gives Ahmad's version, which he says is a Companion's statement in this wording: the angel of death came to Musa and said 'answer your Lord', Musa struck his eye and put it out, God restored his eye and said: go back to My servant and say: do you want life? If so put your hand on the back of an ox and you will live a year for each hair it covers. He said: then what? He said: then death. He said: then now, Lord, from near. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 529} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 530}

In another report of Ahmad, from Hammad ibn Salama from Abu Hurayra as the Prophet's words, the angel of death used to come to people openly; he came to Musa, who struck him and put out his eye; he complained to his Lord, who restored his eye and told him to tell Musa to put his hand on the hide of an ox for a year for each hair it covered; when Musa asked what came after, he said death, and Musa said 'now', and the angel 'smelled him once and took his soul'; Yunus says the angel came to people unseen after that. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 533} Al-Tabari has it from Abu Kurayb from Musab ibn al-Miqdam from Hammad ibn Salama. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 533} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 511}

## Ibn Kathir on the blow
Ibn Kathir sets out Ibn Hibban's explanation: Musa did not know the angel, who came in a form he did not know, as Jibril came as a bedouin and the angels came to Ibrahim and Lut as young men, and he struck him because he had entered his house without leave. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 530} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 321} He then mentions a second interpretation of Ibn Hibban and says it does not fit the wording of the hadith. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 530} He prefers that Musa did not recognise him in that form, and that he was hoping for things he wished to see in his lifetime, such as leaving the Tih and entering the Holy Land, while it had already been decreed that he would die in the Tih after Harun. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 530} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 535}

Ibn Kathir rejects the claim that Musa himself led the Israelites out of the Tih into the Holy Land, saying it contradicts what the People of the Book and most Muslims hold, and uses as evidence his request to be brought near the Holy Land by a stone's throw. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 321}

## After death, and the grave
Ibn Kathir gives from Ahmad, from Anas, that the Prophet said: 'On the night I was taken up I passed Musa standing in prayer in his grave by the red dune', and says Muslim relates it through Hammad ibn Salama. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 322} Al-Tabari relates from al-Suddi that God took him and none of creation knows his grave. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 518} Ibn Kathir quotes Wahb ibn Munabbih that Musa passed a company of angels digging a grave and saw none finer; they said it was for a servant honoured by his Lord, and that if he wished to be that servant he should enter it, lie down, turn to his Lord and breathe as easily as he could; he did, and died, and the angels prayed over him and buried him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 2 p. 322} Al-Tabari and Ibn al-Athir give something close to this. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 510} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 158}

Ibn Kathir adds that the People of the Book and others say he died aged one hundred and twenty, and al-Tabari gives the same figure. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 512}

## His dislike of death, then love of it
Al-Tabari relates from Ibn Ishaq, as Salama told it, that Musa disliked death and found it a great matter, so God wished to make death dear to him, and prophethood passed to Yusha' ibn Nun, who would come to him morning and evening; Musa would ask: what has God newly revealed to you? and Yusha' would say: have I not been with you so many years, and did I ever ask you about anything God newly revealed to you until you yourself began it? When Musa saw this he disliked life and loved death. {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 509} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 510} Ibn al-Athir has something like it in the form 'it is said'. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 158} Ibn Kathir comments on Ibn Jarir's report from Ibn Ishaq of prophethood passing to Yusha' that it is open to objection, because revelation, legislation and God's speaking to Musa never ceased until God took him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 534}

## The black wind and Yusha' (Isra'iliyyat)
Ibn Kathir relays, and Ibn al-Athir has in the form 'it is said', that as Musa walked with Yusha', his young companion, a black wind came; Yusha' thought it was the Hour and clung to Musa; Musa slipped out from beneath his shirt and left the shirt in Yusha's hand; the Israelites accused Yusha' of killing him until each guard saw in a dream that Yusha' had not killed Musa and that he had been taken up to God. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532} {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 3 p. 158} Ibn Kathir says of this account that it has something objectionable and strange. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532}

## Sources and documentation
What stands in the Sahihs is the hadith of the angel of death, the request to be brought near the Holy Land and the mention of the grave by the red dune; the rest, the bed and house, the angels who dig the grave and Yusha', comes from al-Suddi, Wahb and others, whom Ibn Kathir presents in hedged forms. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 529} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532} Details attributed to the People of the Book, such as his age and the days in the Tih, are no guide to the grave, whose place no one knows. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532} {cite:tarikh-al-tabari scan vol. 2 p. 518}
