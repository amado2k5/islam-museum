## Overview
On p. 257 the book gives a piece that begins "convey to the commander of the faithful", in which Abu al-Qasim addresses his father the imam and says that he has not turned aside from what he held before, that his chest was not narrowed by kinds of destruction, and that Ibn Khalaf hoped for his submission and tried to turn him by a change of harshness {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.257}. On p. 258 he asks what excuse his opponents will have before the Prophet tomorrow when they do not stand to help him, says that he will see them in some days scattered in every horizon, and says that what befell him is an opening for him in God, with his patience {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.258}.

## Detail
Ibn Khalaf of the lines is probably [[person/ibrahim-ibn-khalaf-ibn-tarif|Ibrahim ibn Khalaf ibn Tarif]], who was named in [[event/abu-al-qasim-ibn-al-hadi-is-chained-at-sanaa-and-moved-to-bayt-bus-290|the chaining of Abu al-Qasim at Sana'a]]; the link is probable only, since the lines give the name without more. The lines then address the slaves on what they may do, which is the poet's speech. Before this are [[event/abu-al-qasim-ibn-al-hadi-composes-verses-in-prison-290|the earlier pieces of the same section]]. The museum does not reproduce the lines nor the words of insult in them and gives only their sense as the book has it; the book ascribes the verse to Abu al-Qasim and the museum has not checked the attribution. The praise and the epithets in the lines are the poet's, given as the book gives them, with no verdict in the museum's voice.
