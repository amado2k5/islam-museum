## Summary
In 619 AH Badr al-Din Lu'lu', lord of Mosul, took the citadel of Shush in the district of al-Hamidiyya, twelve farsakhs from Mosul. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 433}

## Details
Ibn al-Athir explains that the citadels of Shush and al-Aqr, adjoining one another, had belonged to Imad al-Din Zangi ibn Arslanshah, with whom there was the earlier quarrel already related; this year Zangi travelled to Azerbaijan to serve its lord Uzbak ibn al-Bahlawan, who received him, gave him iqta' grants and kept him at his court. Badr al-Din then marched on Shush and besieged it — it stands atop a high mountain — and his stay grew long because of its strength, so he returned to Mosul leaving his army besieging it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 433} When the matter dragged on for its garrison and they saw no one to lift the siege or come to their aid, they surrendered it on agreed terms involving iqta' grants and robes of honor; his deputies received it on the recorded date, settled its affairs and returned to Mosul. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 433}
