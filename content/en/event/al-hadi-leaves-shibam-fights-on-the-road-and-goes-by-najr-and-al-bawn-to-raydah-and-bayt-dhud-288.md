## Overview
Al-Hadi left Shibam with [[person/abu-al-atahiya-ibn-bishr|Abu al-Atahiya]] and al-Da'am; when he left, those at Shibam gathered and made for him, and when he saw this he turned back on them with Abu al-Atahiya, they charged and killed a number of them, al-Hadi killing most {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.222}. Two of al-Hadi's camels dropped behind, burdened and unable to walk, and the men took them {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.222}. Al-Hadi went on until he was near Najr and then sent for Ali ibn Abi al-Abbas, who met him with the force that was with him {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.222}. When he came to [[place/al-bawn-yemen|al-Bawn]] the people of al-Bawn stood across his way, and he ordered his son [[person/al-murtada-muhammad|Abu al-Qasim]] to charge them; he charged with a body of men, drove them off, and they killed some {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.222}. Then they returned and went together to [[place/raydah-yemen|Raydah]], where al-Hadi spent the night {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.222}. In the morning he went from Raydah to [[place/bayt-dhud-yemen|Bayt Dhu'd]] and stayed there some days, sent the women to the passage of [[group/banu-sarim-hamdan|Banu Sarim]] and ordered Abu al-Atahiya to go back to Raydah, where he stayed some days {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.222}.

## Detail
At the foot of the page the sentence on moving from Bayt Dhu'd to Raydah and on sending Abu al-Atahiya back is printed twice; the text and Apple Vision agree that it is printed so, so it is a copyist's repetition, and the page breaks off in mid sentence. The next printed page, p. 223, is missing from the scanned file, so the account resumes on p. 224 in the middle of a poem; the museum records nothing for the gap. [[event/battle-of-raydah-288|The battle of Raydah]] of the added first pages tells a stay after Shibam and then the coming of Sa'sa'a and the people of Qudum, which resembles what follows; the museum does not assert that they are the same. Ali ibn Abi al-Abbas is probably Ali ibn al-Abbas al-Alawi of the earlier page. The figures of the dead and the remark that al-Hadi killed most are the narrator's, attributed to him, with no judgment.
