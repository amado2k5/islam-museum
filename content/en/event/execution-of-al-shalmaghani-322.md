## Overview
In Shawwal 322 AH the vizier Ibn Muqla arrested al-Shalmaghani, and in Dhu al-Qa'da he and Ibn Abi Awn were crucified and burned, as Ibn al-Athir relates. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 218-219}

## The proceedings
The source says that the house was searched and papers and letters were found from his followers, addressing him in terms no human uses toward another. Al-Shalmaghani denied his doctrine and professed Islam. He and his companions were brought several times before the jurists and judges, and at the end the jurists ruled that his blood was lawful to shed. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 218-219}

## What is attributed to his doctrine
Ibn al-Athir attributes to him the view that God indwells everything to the degree each thing can bear, and that the divine appears in five human forms, corresponding to five devils. This is the source's description of his doctrine, and according to the same report al-Shalmaghani denied it. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 218-219}
