## Overview
The [[event/greek-landing-izmir-1338|Greek occupation of Izmir]] took place in 1338 AH (1919 CE), with British permission. The Greeks then pushed into Anatolia, until Mustafa Kemal led the resistance and drove them back. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.468}

## Occupation and advance
The Greeks occupied Izmir in 1919 CE and pushed into Anatolia with British permission, as al-Salabi reports. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.468}

## Resistance
Mustafa Kemal stirred the spirit of jihad among the Turks, so that he drove the Greeks back, and the forces of the Allies retreated before him. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.468}

## Its echo in the Islamic world
The Islamic world rejoiced at the victory. Mustafa Kemal was given the title "al-Ghazi", Ahmad Shawqi praised him and compared him to Khalid ibn al-Walid, and his victory was likened to the day of Badr. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.468}

## Notes for researchers
- The entry rests on al-Salabi's summary and has not yet been checked against Greek and British sources on the landing and on Britain's part in it.
- The exact day of the landing is not given here; al-Salabi gives only the year.
