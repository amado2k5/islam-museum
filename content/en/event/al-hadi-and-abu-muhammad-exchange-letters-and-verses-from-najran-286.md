## Overview
After the fighting at Minas {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.147} [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] wrote to Abu Muhammad [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-husayn-al-rassi|Abdullah ibn al-Husayn]] that he was coming himself, and enclosed verses. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.147} As far as a text disturbed in printing can be read, they declare his care for his horse, spear and mail and his readiness to give himself, his resolve to strike every stubborn tyrant, that he will vex Abu Muhammad's oppressors with the point of his spear, that he relies on Hashid, Banu Bakil, Khawlan and al-Ahlaf, whom he calls men of the blow and of great deeds, and that those who broke their pledges thought he was not of Ali's line and will find otherwise. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.148} He calls himself the Hasani, sword of God, says he was angry for his Creator and drew his sword against the people of disobedience, and promises to rule by the Qur'an over his enemies. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.149} This is the museum's reading of the meaning of the lines, which are not reproduced here.

## Abu Muhammad reads and replies
When the letter reached Abu Muhammad he called the people and read it to them; they rejoiced and were reassured, and he wrote back to al-Hadi in verse. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.149} The following page gives two poems that, from the order of the printing, appear to be his: in the first he says he has put dwellings, yards, family and wealth out of mind, that he is seeking vengeance for God, and he calls the imam generous to creation {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.149}; in the second he hopes for deliverance from God, says that al-Ahlaf have come to him and spoken of obedience, and declares that his one care is to chase the pledge-breakers. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.149} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.150} The museum does not assert the author of every line on p. 149, because the order of the lines is disturbed in the printing.

## Al-Hadi's reply
Al-Hadi wrote a long reply {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.150} in which he says he does not forget the one who is absent, that a man who does not make God's pleasure his guide is lost, and that what kept him from Abu Muhammad was a confirmed duty, which he performed in fighting people who changed the religion by force. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.150} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.151} He lists his army of Arabs and non-Arabs, of Hamdan and Khawlan, and says they seek God's face and vengeance for the Chosen One. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.151} He then addresses Ibn Bustam and the one-eyed man of Harith with warnings and charges Ibn Bustam with treachery and with ingratitude for favours given; these lines are summarised and their words of abuse are not repeated. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.151} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.152} He ends by affirming that he will set out toward them within two days, quickly, with every armed horseman, and tells Abu Muhammad to expect a victory near at hand. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.152}

## What follows
Page 152 ends with the narrator saying that when the letter reached Abu Muhammad he read it to the people and answered in verse, beginning with the first line of a poem. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.152} The account continues beyond this batch. The text is poetry with much rare vocabulary, and the editor's footnotes explain terms such as the forelock's edge, rain cloud, mouths of a water-skin, sharp, bow and the bow tree. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.148} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.151} See [[event/pledge-at-najran-and-the-fight-below-minas-286]].
