## Summary
King al-Adil Abu Bakr ibn Ayyub died, having reached a great old age. His son al-Kamil took Egypt, al-Muazzam took Damascus and al-Ashraf took al-Jazira. Most of them obeyed al-Kamil, and the authority of the Ayyubids was gathered in him {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p.393}.

## Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's account
[[person/sibt-ibn-al-jawzi|Sibt ibn al-Jawzi]] tells of the death in [[work/mirat-al-zaman|Mir'at al-zaman]] as a witness: he was sitting beside al-Mu'azzam on the day of the burial. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.232}

### The illness
At the end of Jumada I 615 the Franks took the chain tower of Damietta. Al-Kamil sent the shaykh al-shuyukh Sadr al-Din to his father with the news and a plea for help; "when he met al-Adil and told him, he beat his breast with his hand and fell into his last illness". {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.228} He lay ill until Friday 7 or 8 Jumada II and died at Aliqin; two or three days before, al-Mu'azzam had defeated the Franks at al-Qaymun, as told in [[event/battle-of-al-qaymun-615|the battle of al-Qaymun]]. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.231}

### The death kept secret
No one knew of the death but Karim al-Din al-Khilati, who sent a carrier pigeon to [[person/al-malik-al-muazzam|al-Mu'azzam]] at Nablus. Al-Mu'azzam came to Aliqin on Saturday, secured the treasuries, and put his father in a litter with a servant fanning him, giving out that he was ill. They brought him into Damascus on Sunday, people greeting the servant and the servant gesturing towards al-Adil, took him into [[building/qalat-dimashq|the citadel]] and kept the death secret. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.232}

Sibt ibn al-Jawzi counts it among the wonders that they looked for a shroud and could not find one, so they shrouded him in the turban of the jurist Ibn Faris and wrapped him in cotton taken out of a pillow; they had no pickaxe, so Karim al-Din stole one from the moat and they dug his grave with it in the citadel. Ibn Faris led the funeral prayer and he was buried there. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.232}

### The mourning
Sibt ibn al-Jawzi writes: "I was sitting beside al-Mu'azzam at the door of the house with the iwan, and he was silent." When his father had been buried he stood up, tore his clothes and struck his head and face; the mourning was held for three days in the northern iwan. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.232} Sibt ibn al-Jawzi spoke on the first day, and afterwards al-Mu'azzam reproached him for having spoken alongside "Ibn al-Hanbali", al-Nasih, and told him that if he must speak it should be on the third day, with no one else speaking. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.232} In Baghdad it was proclaimed that whoever wished to pray for "al-Malik al-Adil, the ghazi who fought in God's path" should come to the Palace Mosque; the people prayed the funeral prayer in absentia, and only the caliph stayed away. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.232}

### Final burial and his children
Al-Adil remained in the citadel until 619 AH and was then moved to the tomb he had built by the house of al-Aqiqi and his madrasa. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.233} Sibt ibn al-Jawzi lists among his sons Shams al-Din Mamdud, al-Kamil Muhammad, al-Ashraf Musa, al-Mu'azzam Isa, al-Awhad Ayyub, al-Fa'iz Ibrahim, Shihab al-Din Ghazi, al-Aziz Uthman, al-Amjad Hasan, al-Hafiz Arslan, al-Salih Isma'il and others, and among his daughters Dayfa Khatun, lady of Aleppo. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.233}

### What changed after him
When Rajab came, al-Mu'azzam restored the market dues and the wine trade that his father had abolished. Sibt ibn al-Jawzi reminded him of Sayf al-Din Ghazi, Nur al-Din's nephew, who had not done so after his uncle's death; al-Mu'azzam excused himself by "lack of money, and the Franks". {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.233} In his notice on al-Adil, Sibt ibn al-Jawzi had recorded that he abolished market dues, wine and similar revenues throughout his lands, which in Damascus alone had yielded a hundred thousand dinars. {cite:mirat-al-zaman-risala-2013-v22 p.230}
