## Summary
Ibn Sina sorts the forearm's muscles into bending, straightening, pronating and supinating, and likewise those of the wrist {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.69-70}.

## The forearm
The straightening muscles are a pair, one acting with an inward lean since it arises from under the front of the arm bone, the lowest rib and the shoulder blade, the other with an outward lean; together they straighten fully. The bending muscles are a pair, the greater with an inward lean, arising from the shoulder blade and the coracoid beak, the second two-headed, with an outward lean. The supinating muscles are a pair, one between the two forearm bones and one fine and long running to the wrist; the pronating muscles are a pair set outside, one starting from the upper inner part of the arm bone's head and a shorter one from the lower forearm bone itself {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.69-70}.

## The wrist
The muscles that move the wrist bend, straighten, pronate and supinate it. Of the straighteners two are joined as if one, the first from the middle of the lower forearm bone with its tendon at the thumb, drawing it from the index finger, the second from the upper forearm bone with its tendon at the first carpal bone by the thumb; a third lies over the upper forearm bone and sends a two-headed tendon to the middle of the metacarpus. The benders are a pair on the outer side of the forearm ending at the metacarpus before the little finger, with a muscle whose two ends cross and end between index and middle fingers. The same benders and straighteners also pronate and supinate when two opposite ones move obliquely {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.70}.
