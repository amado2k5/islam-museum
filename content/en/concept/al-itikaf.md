## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that i'tikaf confines movement to a time and a place, and that the mosque is for worship, not for worldly business. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.793}

## The mosque is for worship
Sha'rawi says that one who enters the mosque leaves the affairs of the world at its door, does not speak of worldly matters there, and holds the intention of worship; the retreat is most complete in the mosque because it joins time and place. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.793} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.794}

## Sitting where the gathering ends
Sha'rawi holds that the one in retreat does not climb over people's necks, sits where the gathering places him, and leaves his standing with his shoe. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.794}

## Intimacy during retreat
Sha'rawi says intimacy is forbidden to one in retreat in the mosque; God's limits are His prohibitions; and approaching the causes of a prohibition is itself forbidden. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.793} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.795}

## Limits of this batch
He reports that some scholars required fasting, a set length and the mosque; their views are not entered by name, nor the academic view or other views. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.793}
