## Overview
Islamic geometric knot patterns that build stars and polygons from interlacing lines. They were common in Safavid and Seljuk architecture and rest on girih tiles. {cite:wikidata}

## For Sprouts
Beautiful geometric patterns where stars and lines interlace to decorate the walls of mosques and historic buildings with harmony.

## For Explorers
Girih is an intricate Islamic geometric design system based on interlocking strapwork tiles that form repeating star and polygon patterns. Used to decorate domes, prayer niches, and wooden doors across Central Asia, Iran, and Anatolia, it demonstrates the sophistication of Muslim builders in mathematics, geometry, and abstract aesthetics.

## For Scholars
An Islamic geometric ornament system consisting of interlaced strapwork lines generating intricate symmetrical polygons, decagons, and stars governed by rigorous geometric ratios. Prominent across Seljuk, Ilkhanid, Timurid, and Safavid architecture, it employed a standardized set of five polygonal shapes (the girih tiles). Modern scientific analysis has revealed that these tessellations embody quasi-crystalline structural principles, illustrating profound mathematical intuition centuries ahead of modern geometric theory.
