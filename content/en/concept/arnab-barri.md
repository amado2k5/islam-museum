## Nature and selection
The wild rabbit's rennet does all that is listed under rennet, more finely and better, and has additional actions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. The book also lists the blood of the wild rabbit, the ash of its head and its brain among its uses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}.

## Temperament and actions
The passage gives no temperament for it at this point.

## Uses by organ
Ornament: its blood clears freckles; the ash of its head is a good remedy for alopecia, especially the sea kind; if its belly with its guts is taken and burned lightly over a frying pan, it is a remedy that grows hair when crushed and used with rose oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. Joints: its brain roasted helps the tremor that follows an illness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. Head: when the gums of children are rubbed with its brain, it hastens teething and eases it without pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. Drunk with vinegar, its rennet helps epilepsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. Excretory organs: the wild rabbit's rennet, drunk with vinegar for three days after cleansing, prevents conception and clears watery discharge from the uterus. The book states this; it is the book's listing, not a recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. Its blood, roasted, helps excoriation, swelling of the intestines and chronic diarrhoea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. Poisons: its rennet with vinegar is a theriac against poisons, and its roasted blood helps the poison of Armenian arrows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}.
