## Summary
After the meridian line al-Battani draws a second line through the centre at right angles to it, so that the circle is divided into four equal quarters; this line is the direction between east and west, and the four horizon directions east, west, north and south are then written at the line ends. The figure that follows repeats the work with letters: the south and north points at the ends of the meridian line, and the east and west points found by bisecting an arc. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.39-40}.

## The crossing line and the four directions
He says: then quarter the circle with another line through the centre at right angles; the two lines divide the circle into four equal quarters, and this line is the direction between east and west. Then write on the ends of the lines the horizon directions, east, west, north and south. When the rod's shadow falls on the meridian line it is always midday. The letters of the figure are unclear in the scan and are not given here. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.39-40}.

## Midday by the meridian line
On the time he says: whenever the shadow of the rod at the centre falls on this line it is the time of midday, whether the shadow is long or short. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.39}.
