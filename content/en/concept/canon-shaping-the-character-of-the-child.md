## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter four, on the regimen of children once they pass into boyhood, by saying that the chief care should go to attending to the child's character so that it is balanced, for two benefits: the good of its soul, raised from childhood in good character, and the good of its body, since bad character follows an ill temperament {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.220}.

## What it is guarded from
The chief care must be given to attending to the child's character so that it is balanced: it is guarded from violent anger, violent fear, grief or sleeplessness, and at every time one considers what it wishes for and longs for, and brings that near, and what it dislikes, and removes that from its sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.220}.

## The two benefits
There are two benefits in this. One is for its soul, that it grows up from childhood with good character and that becomes a settled disposition in it. The other is for its body: as bad character follows kinds of ill temperament, so when it arises from habit it brings on the ill temperament that matches it, for anger heats greatly, grief dries greatly, and dullness slackens the soul's power and tips the temperament toward the phlegmatic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.220}.
