## Summary
Zij is the Arabic name for a class of [[concept/astronomy|astronomical]] books that set out higher spherical astronomy and the methods of computing celestial motions, contain all the tables required, and usually leave aside the rudiments of cosmography. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 40}

## The name and its rendering
Nallino says Europeans wrongly translated zij as "Tables" (Tabulae), whereas the Arabs always call a table a jadwal, from the likeness of its columns to jadawil, little water channels. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 40} He holds that [[person/al-battani|al-Battani]] called his own book simply a zij, and that early authors knew [[work/al-zij-al-sabi|his Zij]] by that name. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 40}

## Zij and Almagest
Nallino contrasts the zij with the [[work/al-majisti|Almagest]]: the Almagest tries to embrace all of ancient astronomy so the student needs no other book, with long demonstrations mixed with numerical examples, whereas a zij is written not for beginners but for practitioners of computation. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 53} Zijes also customarily treat problems of spherical astronomy that astrological computation requires and that Ptolemy and Theon omit — fixing the houses, the revolutions of years, the projection of rays; Nallino thinks the Arabs thereby fostered a celestial mathematics that had been slight among Greek astrologers. {cite:nallino-albatenii-opus-astronomicum p. 54}
