## Overview
Shaykh Shaltut holds that though wealth is owned in its owners' names, the right of benefit from it is shared between them and the poor and the public interest {cite:azhar-quran-building-society-shaltut p. 118}. Exploiting the needy man's need — lending him at interest — is an absolute prohibition: the rich man would lie in perpetual ambush for people's needs, growing wealth without work credited to society {cite:azhar-quran-building-society-shaltut p. 120}{cite:azhar-quran-building-society-shaltut p. 121}. He cites "the Muslim is the Muslim's brother — he does not wrong him nor hand him over": to leave one's brother hungry and naked while able to feed and clothe him is to hand him over.
