## Summary
Ibn Sina explains that the matter of an internal swelling may move from one organ to another, that the move may be good or bad, and how it is recognised {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.167}.

## Good and bad
It is good when it moves from a noble organ to a lesser one and bad when it moves to a nobler organ or one that endures less, as from pleurisy towards the heart or to the lung; he names favourable sites such as behind the ears, and for swellings of the liver, the groins {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.167}.

## Signs of direction
When it leans downward, stretching and heaviness show in the epigastrium; when it leans upward, the signs are poor, tight and difficult breathing, a tight chest, a burning rising from below, heaviness in the region of the collarbone and headache. Upward movement that takes hold of the brain is bad and dangerous, but if it turns to the soft flesh behind the ears there is hope of recovery, and nosebleed is a good sign in swellings of the viscera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.167}.
