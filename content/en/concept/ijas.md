## Nature and selection
Known. The garden kind is stronger than the dark; the yellow is stronger than the red; the dull white is heavy and purges little; the Armenian is the sweetest and purges most; the best are large and plump {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}. A footnote describes a fruit tree of the rose family {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}.

## Temperament and actions
Cold at the start of the second degree and moist at the end of the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}.

## Uses by organ
Properties: its gum is thinning, cutting and irritating. In the Damascene kind it binds and astringes according to Dioscorides, less so according to Galen; the unripe kind is astringent, its food value is small, and it is eaten before a meal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}. Wounds and ulcers: its gum closes ulcers; with vinegar it cuts herpes, especially with honey or sugar, and especially in children {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}. Head: rinsing with its leaves stops rheum from falling to the tonsils and uvula {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}. Eyes: its gum used as kohl strengthens the sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}. Stomach: the long-standing kind is more useful against yellow bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}. Excretory organs: the sweet kind purges yellow bile more; the moist kind purges more than the dry; some say the Damascene binds the bowel; the wild kind binds while unripe. Its gum crushes bladder stone, and its water brings on menses, and the smaller the fruit the less it purges {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. The book states that its water brings on menses; this is the book's listing, not a recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Galen is recorded as correcting Dioscorides on the Damascene kind, saying it purges rather than binds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}.
