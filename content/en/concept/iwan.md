## Overview
The iwan is a vaulted hall open on one side to a courtyard, an ancient architectural feature going back to pre-Islamic Parthian and Sassanian times. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} Yeomans writes that the introverted plan of iwans facing one another across a court derives from Persia. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116}

## In the madrasa
Teaching took place in the iwans: at the [[building/al-madrasa-al-salihiyya-cairo|Salihiyya]] in Cairo (1242) each court had two iwans, on the qibla and north-west sides, with student rooms on the other two; in the northern wing the north-west iwan served the Malikis and the qibla iwan the Shafi'is. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.117} Yeomans notes that the Salihiyya was not the first madrasa to bring the iwan courtyard to Egypt but is the earliest surviving one, with great influence on later Cairene architecture; the asymmetry of its plan comes from reconciling existing public streets with the direction of Mecca. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.116} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.117}
