## Summary
"Proportional script" (al-khatt al-mansub) is the theory by which the shapes of letters are calibrated and controlled through a series of ratios; Maryam Ekhtiar credits it first to the Abbasid vizier and calligrapher [[person/ibn-muqla|Abu Ali Muhammad ibn Muqla]] (886–940). With [[person/ibn-al-bawwab|Ibn al-Bawwab]] (d. 1022) and [[person/yaqut-al-mustasimi|Yaqut al-Musta'simi]] (d. 1298) he became a model of character and fine penmanship for later generations; the passing of their styles to pupils was the basis of calligraphic study from the tenth century on, showing the weight of the master–pupil bond. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 25}

## Geometric basis
Ibn Muqla built his system on two shapes: a circle whose diameter is the alif, and a rhomboid dot made by one stroke of the reed pen's nib. From these he canonised the six pens: [[concept/naskh-script|naskh]], [[concept/thuluth-script|thuluth]], [[concept/muhaqqaq-script|muhaqqaq]], [[concept/rayhan-script|rayhan]], [[concept/tawqi-script|tawqi']] and [[concept/riqa-script|riqa']]. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 25}

## Pairs and uses
The six were sometimes presented as complementary pairs of large and small — thuluth–naskh, muhaqqaq–rayhan, tawqi'–riqa' — each suited to a purpose: naskh for ordinary books and small Qur'ans, muhaqqaq and thuluth for large Qur'ans, and thuluth also for objects and buildings. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 25} Rayhan, tawqi' and riqa' served chancery documents, colophons, book covers and carpet pages. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 26}

## Refinement after Ibn Muqla
Ibn al-Bawwab added an aesthetic dimension; the only extant work in his hand is a small Qur'an copied at Baghdad in 1000–1001 (Chester Beatty Library, Dublin, MS 1431), in a clear, compact naskh. Yaqut refined the system further; Qadi Ahmad credits him with first cutting the nib at an angle, which gave letters refinement and varied line. He trained many, among them [[person/ahmad-al-suhrawardi|Ahmad ibn al-Suhrawardi]]. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 26} See also [[concept/aqlam-al-kitaba|the pens of writing in Ibn al-Nadim]].
