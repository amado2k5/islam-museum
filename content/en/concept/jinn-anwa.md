## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an does not sort the jinn into kinds, but mentions them collectively in many places, and names the 'ifrit' in Solomon's hoopoe story when an ifrit of the jinn told him 'I will bring it to you before you rise from your place' [[passage/quran-27-39|al-Naml 39]].

## In hadith and the Prophet's biography
Chroniclers and jurists listed kinds of jinn: the marid the strongest, the ifrit in old reports, the ghul that haunts lonely places, and the si'lat and hallas among their tribes; each kind was given a dwelling and temperament {cite:muruj-al-dhahab} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## Among commentators and jurists
Jurists agree the jinn are accountable like humans, among them Muslim and disbeliever, and mention the etiquette of their dwellings in the books of fiqh {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## Notes
The classification is largely literary and scholarly; it does not mean the Qur'an fixed these names — they come from books of lore, language and law {cite:muruj-al-dhahab}.
