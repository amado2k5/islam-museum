## Overview
This section is the introductory chapter of the author's book, in which he defines the place of Islamic economics relative to doctrines, systems, and sciences. A doctrine is a set of fixed principles upon which a theory rests, a system is its institutional application, and a science is the study of what actually occurs in reality {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 44} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 45}.

## His Position on Capitalism
The author holds that the capitalist economy shares freedom, competition, and ownership with Islam, but differs in values and goals, entrenching power and wealth and exploiting the poor through riba, monopoly, and taxes {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 46} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 47}.
