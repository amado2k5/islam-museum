## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat presents the cooperative society as a form of economic enterprise, stating that it is an Islamic reformist idea aimed at curbing the dominance of capitalists and middlemen {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 91}.

## Purpose and Scope
In his view, the cooperative society falls within both the framework of consumption and the framework of production; its purpose is democratic management and decision-making, and ending the dominance of middlemen whose only work is delivery and extortion in exchange for a large profit margin that drives up prices {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 92}. He adds that curbing the profit motive helps stop extortion and exploitation {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 92}.

## The Question of Precedence
The author notes that some economists experimented with cooperation in order to achieve reform and eliminate profit, naming among them the English businessman Robert Owen, who he says was called "the father of cooperation in the world" and called for abolishing profits as the motive for monopoly and exploitation {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 91}. He then argues that Islam established this idea before Owen, and that it founded the economy and material life on cooperation {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 92}. This is a claim by the author for which the book presents no historical sources, and it should be treated as his opinion.

## Relation to the People's Enterprise
In the author's framework, the cooperative society stands between the individual enterprise and the people's institution; see [[concept/al-mashru-al-iqtisadi|The Economic Enterprise]] and [[concept/al-mashru-al-shabi|The People's Enterprise]].
