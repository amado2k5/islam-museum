## Summary
Radkh in the lexicon is a small gift; in the jurists' usage it is a gift from the spoils whose amount the imam judges, given to those who are not bound to fight but do useful work in the war, such as women, boys and a dhimmi. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8781} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8782} Most jurists hold it a binding right for one who has done useful work, and a view of the Shafi'is makes it recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8782} A mount other than a horse has no share, but its rider is given a radkh after the foot-soldier's share. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8783} The jurists differ on where it comes from: the Hanafis and Hanbalis, and one Shafi'i view, take it from the whole booty, and the preferred Shafi'i view takes it from four-fifths. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8783} Its amount is less than a full share according to the majority, and is left to the imam by the Malikis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8784}
