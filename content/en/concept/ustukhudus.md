## Nature and selection
A plant with thin red stalks like barley-grain stalks, with longer leaves, and with dusty twigs as in aftimun but without flower. It is pungent with a slight bitterness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}. A footnote gives its name as al-dirm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot in the first degree and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}.

## Uses by organ
It resolves and thins by its bitterness; its drink opens obstructions and clears, with a slight astringency that strengthens the body and organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}. Joints: its decoction calms pains of the nerve and ribs, and its drink is the best of remedies for cold diseases of the nerve, so a person with a weak nerve should take it steadily {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}. Head: it helps melancholia and epilepsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}. Stomach: it distresses those whose yellow bile predominates and makes them vomit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}. Excretory organs: it strengthens the urinary organs and purges phlegm and black bile; Galen did not mention it in this way {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}.
