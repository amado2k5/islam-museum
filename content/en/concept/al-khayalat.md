## Its definition and two divisions

Khayalat are hues sensed before the sight as though scattered in the air. Their cause is something non-transparent standing between the ice-like humour and the objects seen; and that thing is either of the sort not perceived at all in the ordinary way — perceived only by a strong sight beyond the ordinary — or of the sort ordinary sights perceive when middling, without being of the utmost keenness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.207}

## What only the keen perceive, and what lies in the layers

As for the first: when sight is strong it perceives the faint hidden things flying in the air near it — the motes the air never lacks and the like — so they appear to it; and likewise what is within of the traces of the few vapours no temperament and nature is ever free of; and this is not counted a harm. As for the other division: it is either in the layers — very faint traces on the corneal layer remaining from smallpox or from ophthalmia and pustules and the like, not appearing to the eye from outside but appearing to it from within, since the place does not transmit — or in the moistures {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.207} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.208}

## What lies in the moistures, and the varieties of khayalat

What lies in the moistures is of two sorts: either the very substance of the moisture has transformed — through a bad temperament changing its colour and removing its clearness, by cold, moisture or heat boiling that part and rousing airiness in it, the way of airiness when mixed with a thin transparent body being to make it colour-thick, foamy and non-transparent, or by a parching dryness — or else something incoming to it from elsewhere: either passing, not settled, of the kind of vapours rising from the whole body or the stomach or the brain when they are light, arising and dispersing, as in crises and after vomiting and anger; or settled in it, heralding the water. These khayalat differ in quantity — small and large; in consistency — dense and thin hidden; in arrangement — loose and clustered misty; and in shape — granular, fly-like, thread-like and hair-like lengthwise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.208}
