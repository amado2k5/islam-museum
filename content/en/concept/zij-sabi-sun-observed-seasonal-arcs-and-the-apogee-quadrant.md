## Summary
In chapter 28 al-Battani follows Ptolemy's school in measuring the variation in the Sun's motion and the point of its greatest distance from the centre of the earth, and relies on measurements of its passage made in successive years: the Sun runs from the autumn to the spring equinox point in about a hundred and seventy-eight days and fourteen and a half hours, and from the spring to the autumn equinox in a hundred and eighty-six days and fourteen mean hours, a half and a quarter {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.68-69}.

## What the arcs show
He then measures from the first of Aries to the first of Cancer, that is from the spring equinox to the summer solstice, and finds about ninety-three days and fourteen hours (the word before ninety is damaged in the printed text, so the figure is read from the sum), longer than the quarter from the summer solstice to the autumn equinox. From this it follows that the point of greatest distance lies in the slower half of the circle, and in this quarter that takes longer than the remaining quarter, that is between the spring equinox and the summer solstice {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.69}. The two half arcs together come close to a whole year, in agreement with [[concept/zij-sabi-battani-year-shorter-than-ptolemys|the year he took from observation]] {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.69}.
