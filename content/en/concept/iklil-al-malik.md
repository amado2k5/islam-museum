## Nature and selection
Ibn Sina describes iklil al-malik as the flower of a straw-coloured plant, crescent-shaped, with some firmness in spite of its looseness; it may be white or yellow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.341}. He reports Dioscorides as saying that some people call it isqifun, and that it is a dry herb with many four-angled branches leaning to white, with leaves like quince leaves but tending to length and slightly rough, and with a whitish down, growing in rough places {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.341}. The best is the firmest, slightly whitish, more bitter and with the clearer smell; Dioscorides is reported as saying that the best has a saffron colour and a sharper smell, although the smell of its kind is weak by nature, and that its colour should be that of fenugreek {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.341}.

## Temperament and actions
It is hot in the first degree and dry in it; as a whole it is compound, its heat outweighing its cold; Budighurus is reported as saying that it is balanced between heat and cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}. It has a slight astringency with resolving power, and for that reason it ripens; Budighurus says it dissolves superfluities by its special property; and they say that its juice with mibakhtaj soothes pains, and that it resolves, refines and strengthens the organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}.

## Uses by organ
It is useful for hot and hard swellings, especially with mibakhtaj, and mixed with egg white, fenugreek flour, flaxseed and poppy seed according to the place. It is useful for moist ulcers, especially the honeycomb-like (shahdiyya) ones, applied with water or with some drying agents joined to it, such as oak gall, dry clay and lentils {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}. For the head, it helps swellings of the ears and soothes their pain as a poultice with mibakhtaj and as drops of its juice, and its relief of pain is said to be quicker; a lotion made from it soothes headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}. It helps swellings of the eyes as a poultice with mibakhtaj {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}. It helps swellings of the anus and testicles as a poultice with mibakhtaj and with what was named with it, boiled in wine; the water of its decoction soothes the itching that befalls the testicles, and the decoction of its stalks and leaves, when drunk, promotes urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that the decoction of its stalks and leaves, when drunk, promotes menses and expels foetuses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation.
