## Overview
In al-Baladhuri, the *jizya* is what the People of the Book pay on their heads under the terms of a treaty, in return for being left to their religion and their land. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The first to pay it
He relates from al-Zuhri that, as far as is known, the first of the People of the Book to pay the *jizya* were the people of [[place/najran|Najran]], who were Christians. Then the people of Aylah, Adhruh, and Adhri'at paid the *jizya* during the expedition of Tabuk. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 79-80}

## Forms of the settlement
One of its forms, in his account, was a dinar on every adult male each year. The people of Aylah made peace for three hundred dinars; the people of Jarash made peace for a dinar per adult male, together with the hospitality of the Muslims; and the people of Adhruh made peace for a hundred dinars each Rajab. The Jews of Muqanna made peace for a quarter of the hunt of the 'Uruk, a quarter of the women's spinning, and a quarter of the fruits, and after that they owed neither *jizya* nor forced labour. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 79-80}

## Najran in the time of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz
When [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]] came to power, he ordered that the people of Najran be counted, and found them at a tenth of their earlier number. He said that he regarded this settlement as a *jizya* on their heads, not a treaty over their land, and that the *jizya* on the dead and on Muslims was dropped. He therefore imposed on them two hundred garments. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 91-92}

## Syria
Al-Baladhuri states, citing al-Awza'i, that at the beginning in Syria the *jizya* was a *jarib* (measure of grain) and a dinar on each head. He then reports accounts of Umar ibn al-Khattab's assessment of it by the classes of people. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 170-171}

## For Scholars
Jizya in Islamic fiqh is what the People of the Book pay per head under treaty and dhimma, in exchange for remaining on their religion and land under protection — al-Baladhuri records its first collection from the Christians of Najran and the peoples of Ayla, Adhruh and Adhra'at at Tabuk. The schools differ on its amount, on whom it falls and how it is taken: adult free able-bodied men of the People of the Book — not women, children, monks or the poor on the common view; the Hanafis grade it across rich, middling and poor, the Shafi'is set a dinar per head; some allow it on the Zoroastrians and differ on the Arab polytheists. Jurists agree the contract rests on the dhimmi's security — protection of life, property and worship in churches and synagogues under rules — and historians place its emergence inside the fay' arrangements and earlier Byzantine and Sasanian treaties; it is debated today in the fiqh of minorities and citizenship.

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
At the end of the twelfth episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] says that there is no state, old or new, whose inhabitants pay no tax, that the jizya paid by minorities was lower than the zakat paid by Muslims, and that the one who paid it took no part in fighting but was defended by the Muslims, and if he took part in defending the state it was lifted from him. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:23:30} He lists those on whom it does not fall: the woman, the boy even if rich, the poor man, the monk, the disabled and the very old, whereas a Muslim with wealth pays zakat whoever he is. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:24:30} He concludes that there is no injustice in the jizya. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep12 00:25:30}
