## Overview
The source transmits from Ibn al-Muqaffa' a description of the office of the *zimam* (register) and the seal among the Persians. It notes that Ziyad ibn Abi Sufyan was the first of the Arabs to adopt an office of the register and the seal, following the practice of the Persians. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The procedure among the Persians
When the king of Persia ordered something, the keeper of the signature stood before him and recorded it in his servant's monthly register, which the king sealed and which was stored. The signature was then sent to the keeper of the register, who held the seal, and he sent it on to the official in charge of the province, who wrote a letter of the king from it. It was copied in the original, then returned to the keeper of the register, who presented it to the king and compared it with what was in the register; then it was sealed in his presence or in the presence of the most trusted of the people near him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The seals
The king had a seal for secrets, a seal for messengers, a seal for perpetuity with which records, grants of land, and documents of honour were sealed, and a seal for the land tax. The keeper of the register had charge of them, and sometimes the seal of secrets and letters was given to one of the king's intimates. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

It is related that Khusraw Parviz was troubled by the smell of the land-tax ledgers, and ordered that they be brought to him in pages tinted with saffron and rosewater. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Older vocabulary and the Abbasid centre
The preceding command,memorandum and seal account belongs to the Persian/Ziyad zimam narrative; it is not renamed an original description of al-Mahdi's zimam al-azimma {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}. Duri gives departmental zimam in162AH and a168AH/784–85centre checking accounts,supervising and coordinating with the vizier/other bureaus {cite:tdv-divan-duri Abbasid administration}. Bozkurt describes connecting provincial zimam offices to that centre {cite:tdv-mehdi-bozkurt p.377–379}. Shared vocabulary invites comparison,not proof of identical offices,personnel or unchanged procedures.

## What is being compared?
The older account preserves an original against which a command is checked before sealing; the Abbasid description names accounts,supervision and coordination between offices {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} {cite:tdv-divan-duri Abbasid administration}. The [[invention/al-mahdi-zimam-oversight|central oversight record]] therefore explains a separate institutional improvement,not the first preservation or comparison in history. No tie asserts every Persian procedure was applied in this centre: the read evidence is not an operating ledger establishing that. A seal alone does not make content true,just as matching accounts do not establish events outside the paper. This teaching comparison preserves the older account without letting shared terminology obscure different functions.

## Reading evidence,not inventing a form
The162AH stage precedes the168AH centre; Duri renders it778–79 and Bozkurt779,each source's rendering remaining explicit {cite:tdv-divan-duri Abbasid administration} {cite:tdv-mehdi-bozkurt p.377–379}. Words do not establish fixed columns,signature order or a dispatch schedule. An institutional name or ledger cover cannot replace the missing original register. Explanation of function stays separate from imagining paper,and image permission separate from historical attribution. This compares institutional descriptions,not a preserved Abbasid form's design.
