## Summary
Ibn Sina sets out the three states of the human body according to Galen, health, disease and a state that is neither, then divides diseases into simple and composite, the simple falling in three genera: bad temperament, bad composition and breaking of continuity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.104}.

## The three states
The states of the human body according to Galen are three. Health is a condition in which the body, in its temperament and composition, is such that all acts come from it sound and whole. Disease is a condition opposite to this. And there is a state that is neither health nor disease, either because neither is at its extreme, as in the bodies of the old, the convalescent and children, or because both are present together, whether in two organs, or in one organ but in two distant genera, as when the temperament is sound and the composition diseased, or in one organ and two near genera, as when it is sound in shape but not in size and position, or sound in the two passive qualities but not the two active; or because the two alternate at different times, as with one who is well in winter and ill in summer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.104}.

## Simple and composite
Some diseases are simple and some composite. The simple is one kind of disease of temperament or one kind of disease of composition; the composite is where two or more kinds combine and unite as a single disease {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.104}.

## The three genera of simple diseases
The genera of simple diseases are three. The first are the diseases attributed to the organs of similar parts, the diseases of bad temperament, so attributed because they befall these first and by themselves and through them befall the composite organs, so that they can be conceived as occurring in any similar-part organ one likes, which cannot be said of the composite. The second are the diseases of the instrumental organs, the diseases of composition in organs made up of similar-part organs that are the instruments of acts. The third are the common diseases that befall similar parts and befall instrumental organs as instrumental without their befalling the latter following from their befalling the former, namely the breaking of continuity, which may befall a joint without befalling the similar parts it is composed of, and may befall the nerve, bone or vessels alone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.104}.

## In sum
In sum, diseases are of three genera: those that follow bad temperament, those that follow bad form of composition, and those that follow breaking of continuity. The diseases of bad temperament are known, sixteen in all, as he has already described {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.104}.
