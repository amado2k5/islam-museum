## Summary
In [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]]'s teaching the "bad leader" comes in two kinds: a leader weak in leadership competence, however good his character and religion, and a leader bad in his ethics and aims, however strong; the two can meet in one person. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:13:30} For the strong bad leader he gives Hitler as an example, and he rejects the view he ascribes to Machiavelli's *The Prince*, that weakness alone is bad leadership. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:12:30} He set this out in episodes 3 and 4 of his programme [[work/asrar-al-qiyada-al-nabawiyya-program|"Secrets of Prophetic Leadership"]] as a way into the study of followers, since there is no bad leader without bad followers. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:03:00}

## The three drivers
Al-Suwaidan says modern studies have confirmed what [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|Abu Hamid al-Ghazali]] said: that all human emotions go back to three drivers, fear, greed and desire. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:16:30} For fear he cites [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy]]: when Banu Qaynuqa surrendered to the Prophet's judgement after breaking their pact, he pressed for his allies and seized the Prophet's armour at the neck until the Prophet grew angry, and then the Prophet gave them to him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:20:00} For greed he cites the gifts from the [[event/distribution-of-hunayn-spoils|spoils of Hunayn]]: the Prophet gave those whose hearts were to be won a hundred camels each, [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] among them, and gave [[person/al-aqra-ibn-habis|al-Aqra' ibn Habis]] less, who grew angry, so the Prophet ordered that his tongue be "cut off" with more. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:23:30} For desire, the strongest of which in his view is the desire for power, he cites [[person/musaylima|Musaylima]], who came with the delegation of Banu Hanifa and made his following conditional on succeeding the Prophet. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:26:30}

## The seven types
He says he took the names of the types of bad leadership from Barbara Kellerman's *Bad Leadership*, describing her as head of the leadership research centre at Harvard University, and that the measures and examples are his own. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:30:00} Each type has three measures:
- **The incompetent**: low competence and a strong wish to lead. His example is Ka'b ibn Asad, chief of Banu Qurayza, who could not persuade his people to accept any of the three ways out he offered them during the siege, and told them that he and they had both failed. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:37:00}
- **The rigid**: measured by flexibility, creativity and handling of information. His example is al-Aqra' ibn Habis refusing to give back the captives of Hawazin, until his own tribe, Tamim, overruled him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:41:00}
- **The intemperate**: measured by self-management, bad habits and visible conduct. His example is [[person/al-jadd-ibn-qays|al-Jadd ibn Qays]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:45:30}
- **The callous**: measured by mercy, selfishness and empathy. His example is Abu Lahab. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:47:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:48:00}
- **The corrupt**: measured by theft, lying and cheating. His example is al-As ibn Wa'il. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:49:00}
- **The insular**: cares only for himself and his own followers, generous to them and indifferent to everyone else. His example is [[person/uyayna-ibn-hisn|Uyayna ibn Hisn]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:49:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:50:00}
- **The evil**: measured by atrocities, the infliction of pain and domination. His example is [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:51:00}

## Why people follow a bad leader
Al-Suwaidan opened episode 4 with two questions: why people follow a bad leader while knowing he is bad, and how bad leadership can be reduced. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:03:00} He showed the Milgram experiment, which he describes as carried out in 1961 on volunteers asked to give shocks rising to 450 volts, 65% of whom went on to the end without any compulsion; he concludes that people have a natural readiness to submit to authority. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:09:00} He cites the follower of Musaylima who said "the liar of Rabi'a" was dearer to them than a truthful man of Mudar, and calls it tribal partisanship. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:12:00} Other reasons he lists: the followers are bad like him, their interests are tied to him, or he gives them security. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:14:00} Research shows, he says, that getting rid of bad leaders depends on reducing bad followers, and that the worse a leader becomes the more loyal his followers grow, because their interests are bound to him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:16:30} The episode's detailed example is Uyayna ibn Hisn, whom the Prophet called "the obeyed fool". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:22:00}

## A model for reducing bad leadership
He proposes a model in two parts. **Reforming systems**: no one holds an office for more than two terms of four to six years each, family firms excepted; a balance of powers; an independent oversight body that does not answer to the leader, for which he gives Kuwait's Audit Bureau as an example; binding shura; and limits on the leader's freedom to dispose of resources. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:43:00} **Supporting leaders**: complementary aides who make up for what he lacks, an information system, personal balance between spirit, mind, emotion and body, creative advisers able to say "no", and the study of history. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:46:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:47:00} He closes with a saying he attributes to al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf when asked why he did not rule with Umar's justice: that they should first be like Abu Dharr before he could be like Umar; for al-Suwaidan it means that followers make the leader. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep04 00:49:00}
