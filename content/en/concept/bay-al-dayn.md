## Summary
The author distinguishes between a loan and a deferred sale: an interest-bearing loan is prohibited, whereas a deferred sale is permitted because it involves a debt backed by a commodity, while a loan is not backed by a commodity {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 354}. Therefore, it is permissible to increase the price for the sake of time, since a sale is an exchange between two different things {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 354}.

## Increase for Deferral and Discount for Early Payment
The author notes that jurists stated that time has a share of the price, and that if the deferred price falls due and the debtor is unable to pay, it is not permissible to charge him late-payment interest {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 351}. Just as the majority of scholars permitted an increase for deferral, some scholars permitted a discount for early payment {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 351}. In a bilateral sale between seller and buyer, a discount for early payment is permissible just as an increase for deferral is {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}.

## The Financing Intermediary
If an intermediary enters between the seller and the buyer to act as a financier, his role is that of a lender, and he cannot offer loans with interest in any form {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}.

## The Author's Conclusion
Among the causes of the financial crisis in his view are trading in debts, selling them, and expanding them; he adds the role of credit cards and personal loans, and argues that resolving the crisis requires putting an end to this debt trading {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}.
