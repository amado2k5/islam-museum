## Summary
Ibn Sina explains that when we say a thing taken or applied is hot or cold, we mean it is so in potency and not in act: it is potentially hotter or colder than our bodies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.132}.

## Senses of potency
He gives several senses of this potency: a potency reckoned at the time our body's heat acts on it; a potency meaning good readiness, as when we say sulphur is hot in potency; a mere reference to what predominates in its temperament among the first elements, without regard to our body's action on it; and a potency in the sense of an acquired capacity, like that of a scribe who has stopped writing, as when we say aconite is corrupting in potency {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.132-133}.
