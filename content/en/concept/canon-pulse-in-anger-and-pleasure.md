## Summary
In the eighteenth chapter, on the pulse of psychic conditions, Ibn Sina begins with anger and then pleasure and joy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.

## Anger
Anger, by stirring power and spreading the spirit at once, makes the pulse great, very lofty, quick and frequent; no unevenness need occur, since the passion is uniform, unless fear mixes with it, so that now one and now the other prevails, and likewise if shame mixes with it or reason contends and tries to hold back {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.

## Pleasure and joy
Pleasure moves outward gently and so does not match anger in bringing speed or frequency; its greatness may even suffice for the need, so the pulse is slow and widely spaced. The pulse of joy too most often grows great with softness and inclines to slowness and wide spacing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.
