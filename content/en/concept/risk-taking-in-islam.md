## Summary
The author holds that risk is the entrepreneur's return, and that Islam accustomed people to taking legitimate risks by prohibiting gain without risk; thus, a loan with guaranteed interest is impermissible, whereas qirad for a share of potential profit is permissible, with the capital provider bearing the loss {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 116}. Islam linked risk to reward in the hereafter and to worldly social security {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 113}.

## Overview
The author considers risk a dependent factor of production because it does not produce on its own, but rather joins labor, capital, or land to increase the independent factor's share of output {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 116}. All enterprises require varying degrees of risk, and "high stations are inseparable from risk," as he puts it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 116}.

## Prohibition of Gain Without Risk
The author deems a loan with guaranteed interest impermissible and permits qirad for a share of potential profit, so that the risk rests on the capital provider and any financial loss falls on them {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 116}. This position is part of his discussion of riba, which has been given a separate entry in the encyclopedia.

## Leasing and Risk
The author permits leasing leasable capital, such as a machine or a beast of burden, for a fixed rent or a share of profit; the lessor bears the risk because they remain the owner of the leased asset, whereas a lender transfers ownership to the borrower {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}.

## Agricultural Risk and Social Solidarity
He cites the hadith "No Muslim plants a tree or sows a seed..." in which whatever is stolen or eaten by birds or wild beasts from the crop counts as charity for its owner {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}. He views social solidarity and Islamic insurance as factors that encourage risk-taking {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}, and this will be addressed in its proper place in the chapter on distribution {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}.

## Uncertainty in Commerce
The author quotes Al-Ghazali that a merchant "is certain of his toil, and uncertain of his profit," and Al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam that people act on good faith because what is most likely is its truthfulness {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 154} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 155}. He considers profit without liability to be prohibited, for productive trade is the transformation of goods across time and place, not profit without ownership or liability {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 179}.



## Principles of Risk and Return
The author enumerates principles related to risk: encouraging legitimate investment, undertaking agricultural and commercial risk with a religious incentive, distinguishing between legitimate risk (commercial risk) and gambling, and recognizing that profit is tied to risk and that return corresponds to it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 202} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 203}.

In his view, risk is a dependent productive factor associated sometimes with capital and sometimes with labor, increasing its bearer's entitlement to profit. A partnership entitles one to profit through capital, or through labor and liability derivatively {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 283} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 286}. The author permits mitigating risk by stipulation, such as what Al-Abbas did in prohibiting traveling with capital by sea {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 284}.

The author observes that choosing risk over safety requires a higher return, and that the reward in a ju'alah (conditional reward contract) for an undertaking whose outcome is unknown is greater than a known wage {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 203} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 285}.
