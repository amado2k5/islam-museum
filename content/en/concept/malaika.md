## Overview
The angels in Islam are creatures of God, and belief in them is a pillar of faith for the Sunnis and the Community {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §72}. They are honoured servants who do not disobey God in what He commands them and do what they are commanded, and they have duties assigned to them in the heavens and on earth: among them are the messengers bearing revelation such as Jibril and Mika'il, and among them the recorders of deeds, the bearers, and those charged with the destinies of creation.

## The lexical and technical origin
"Malak" in the language is used for a messenger and for the strong; from it come "muluk" (kings), and "mala'ika" (angels) is the plural of "malak"; it is said among the Arabs that "malak" is among the most hidden of names on the tongue for the height of its meaning {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14974}. In the technical sense: a creature of God made of light, dwelling in heaven, who does not eat or drink, does not marry, and does not sleep, worships God and obeys Him {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14975}.

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an mentions of their attributes and works:
- Their creation before Adam and their prostration to him by God's command, and Iblis's refusal {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-2-34|Al-Baqara 34]], [[passage/quran-7-11|Al-A'raf 11]], [[passage/quran-15-29|Al-Hijr 29]], [[passage/quran-17-61|Al-Isra' 61]], [[passage/quran-38-71|Sad 71]], [[passage/quran-38-72|Sad 72]], [[passage/quran-38-73|Sad 73]], [[passage/quran-35-1|Fatir 1]].
- Their mission: they descend with revelation and the Books upon the prophets {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-2-97|Al-Baqara 97]], [[passage/quran-26-193|Al-Shu'ara' 193]], [[passage/quran-69-4|Al-Qari'a 4]], [[passage/quran-81-19|At-Takwir 19]], [[passage/quran-16-2|Al-Nahl 2]].
- Their worship: they glorify night and day without flagging, they do not disobey God in what He commands, and they seek forgiveness for the believers {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-21-20|Al-Anbiya' 20]], [[passage/quran-66-6|Al-Tahrim 6]], [[passage/quran-40-7|Ghafir 7]], [[passage/quran-7-206|Al-A'raf 206]].
- Their works: recording deeds, being charged with destinies, and guarding {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-50-17|Qaf 17]], [[passage/quran-50-18|Qaf 18]], [[passage/quran-82-10|Al-Infitar 10]], [[passage/quran-82-11|Al-Infitar 11]], [[passage/quran-82-12|Al-Infitar 12]], [[passage/quran-86-4|At-Tariq 4]], [[passage/quran-32-11|As-Sajda 11]], [[passage/quran-41-30|Fussilat 30]].
- Their presence in the great scenes: the Isra' and Mi'raj, reinforcing the Muslims, the Night of Decree {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-97-4|Al-Qadr 4]], [[passage/quran-8-9|Al-Anfal 9]], [[passage/quran-8-12|Al-Anfal 12]], [[passage/quran-3-124|Al 'Imran 124]].
- Their station: each of them has a known station, noble recorders, and Throne-bearers {cite:tanzil-quran} — [[passage/quran-37-164|As-Saffat 164]], [[passage/quran-69-17|Al-Haqqa 17]].

## In hadith and Sunna
- In creation: the reports say that God created the angels of light before He created Adam of clay, and there are hadiths about Jibril having existed two thousand years before Adam; the sound position is that the details of creation are among what God kept to Himself, and what is preserved is that their creation preceded Adam's {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:mustadrak-al-hakim}.
- In worship: "the angels neither sleep nor flag" in glorification; they bless whoever blesses the Prophet, seek forgiveness for the believers night and day, and pray mercy for them {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}.
- In number: it is reported that seventy thousand of the people of the Frequented House circumambulate it and never return, and that around the Throne are places of angels too many to count; the counting is of the unseen {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.
- In obedience: "God sends forth... and the angels... who do not disobey God in what He commands and do what they are commanded" is an established Qur'anic description; and in hadith that among them are the noble recorders, among them the keeper of the covenant, and among them those who roam the earth seeking gatherings of remembrance {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud}.

## In the creed
The Tahawiyya creed states it in settled wording: "we believe in the angels and the prophets and the Books sent down upon the messengers, and we bear witness that they were upon manifest truth" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §72}; Ibn Abi al-Izz's commentary elaborates the meaning of belief in them and their attributes {cite:sharh-tahawiyya-ibn-abi-al-izz}.
- Belief in them includes: that they are honoured servants who do not disobey God, and that denying their existence or reviling them is unbelief in the majority view {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14978}.
- Those who deny them: denying the existence of the angels or calling them liars in what the Prophet brought is unbelief, and whoever reviles them is killed in the majority view {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14978}.

## Their attributes and standing
- They do not eat or drink, do not marry, do not sleep, do not give birth and are not born {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14975}.
- They have the ability to take form — they assume human shapes by God's permission, as in the story of Jibril with Maryam and the story of Jibril with the Prophet in the form of a Bedouin {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14976}.
- They have wings, measures and command as in the Qur'anic description, and they have works in which they differ: from revelation to the clouds to recording deeds to taking souls {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14976}.
- That it never occurs to them to precede God in word or deed, as in the sacred hadith: "they speak nothing but truth" and in the Qur'anic etiquette "nor do they precede Him in speech; they act by His command" [[passage/quran-21-27|Al-Anbiya' 27]].

## Their functions in the Qur'an and hadith
The exegetes have gathered the functions of the angels in the Qur'an; among what has been reported: bearing the Throne, prostration to Adam, the mission of revelation, recording, guarding, descending with mercy and rain, blowing the trumpet, the Angel of Death, the keepers of Paradise and the Fire, the angels of reinforcement, the angels who bless the Prophet, and roaming the earth in gatherings of remembrance {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:27:30}.
- Every believer has with him two recording angels who write his deeds, and every son of Adam has angels who guard him {cite:sunan-abi-dawud} {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}.

## Notes for researchers
The functions reported in the Qur'an and hadith are those mentioned above; any further detail about the names and numbers of those charged is related in books of tafsir and reports and needs a chain given with each claim.

## In the Prophet's biography
- Jibril is the angel who was most in the Prophet's company: he brought him the first revelation in the cave of Hira', ascended with him on the Mi'raj, reviewed the Qur'an with him in Ramadan, stood beside him on the day of Uhud, and came to him in the form of a Bedouin asking about Islam, faith and excellence {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}.
- The angels of Badr and Hunayn descended for reinforcement, and the angels of revelation went back and forth between heaven and earth on the nights when revelation came; the Isra' and Mi'raj are at [[event/isra-and-miraj|the Isra' page]], and the reinforcement at Badr at [[event/badr-battle-day|the Badr page]].

He holds that a session of knowledge is better than the worship of angels, because the person there struggles against himself (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:42:30} He says that if the insight of the Companions were ours, angels would fill the gathering, and that an hour of lesson guides (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:09:30}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)
The Sheikh proposes an idea he says occurred to him on the spot: to give the angel of evil deeds a day off, by avoiding on that day backbiting, tale-bearing, listening to evil and unlawful food {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:56:00}. He compares this to an employee who takes a day or two of leave {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:56:00}. He says that whoever keeps this up for two or three months would see that the angel is a pure being who has been wearied by him all his life, writing against him since the age of fifteen {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:56:30}. He urges lengthening these days to two and then three, and putting to work the angel of good deeds, whom he says has been on leave for thirty years, by having him write down prayer, remembrance of God, good works, Qur'an reading and night prayer (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:57:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 7)
The Sheikh mentions that the ascent of the angels to the heaven takes place in a day measuring fifty thousand years, and says the Qur'an brings such matters close to understanding (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:12:00}. He then returns to the verse he had stopped at, which speaks of the standing of the Spirit and the angels [[passage/quran-78-38|An-Naba 38]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:12:00}. ## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 9)
The Sheikh holds that the believer swims against the current in this life, and that the angels take his soul so that it swims in the dominion of the heavens and the gates of heaven are opened for it, as he explained in last Monday's lesson {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:27:00}. He asks what it is that the angels manage on God's behalf [[passage/quran-79-5|An-Nazi'at 5]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:27:00}. He says that when the functions of the angels in the Qur'an are gathered together they come to eighteen (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:27:30}. He names the first as the two angels who sit on the right and on the left, the angel of good deeds and the angel of evil deeds, who are with a person twenty-four hours a day [[passage/quran-50-18|Qaf 18]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:27:30}. He says that on the Day of Judgment the angels tell their Lord they did not worship Him as He deserves (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:37:30}. He relates that the Prophet's mule once bolted with him, the Companions said its temper had turned bad, and the Prophet denied it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:37:30}. He reports from the Prophet that the mule bolted because it hears in the graves what takes place of punishment (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:38:00}. He then cites the hadith "the heaven groans" and attributes it to Muslim (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:38:00}. He explains the groaning as the creak of timber in a ceiling under a heavy load {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:38:30}. He cites from the hadith that the heaven groans, that there is no space of four fingers in it without the forehead of an angel in prostration glorifying God, and that when they are gathered before their Lord on the Day of Judgment they say they did not worship Him as He deserves (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:38:30}. He says the Companions responded with hearing and obedience, unlike the Children of Israel {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:41:30}. Among the functions of the angels he lists the roaming angels on earth who look for gatherings of remembrance, and says that were the veil lifted we would see angels covering the heavens {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:41:30}.

## With the exegetes
Ibn Kathir details their attributes, mentioning that they are angels charged with matters, that they possess for themselves neither harm nor benefit, and that God called them "messengers" in places because they carry His command to whom He wills {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}. Al-Qurtubi holds in his Mufhim that belief in the angels entails affirming what the Prophet reported of their states and attributes {cite:mufhim-qurtubi-v7}.

## In the context of the unseen
Created from light, never disobeying God; among them the Throne-bearers, the noble recorders, and Jibril the trustee of revelation.

## Notes
- Their number is uncountable; counting them is of the unseen, and the Qur'an and hadith have established their existence and works without counting them out.
- Each of them has a name and a charge, as in books of tafsir and hadith, and some names are reported in the athar rather than the Qur'an, such as Ridwan and Malik, keeper of the Fire.
