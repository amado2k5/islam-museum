## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi gives the scholars' definition of a greater sin: one for which God threatens punishment in the next life, or which carries a prescribed penalty. He holds that avoiding greater sins expiates lesser ones. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2150}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2155}

## Avoidance and expiation
He explains that avoidance means keeping away from the occasions of sin, that expiation removes punishment and that the cancelling of good deeds removes reward. He holds that persistence in a lesser sin makes it grave. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2152}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2155}

## The list drawn from the Qur'an
He lists greater sins drawn from verses, among them shirk, murder, disobedience to parents, adultery, usury, eating orphans' wealth, flight from battle, false witness, wine, neglecting prayer, breaking covenants and severing kinship. He also treats sorcery and withholding zakat in other passages. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2157}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2158}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2166}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2174}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2175}

## The exchange with Ja'far al-Sadiq
He recounts that Amr ibn Ubayd asked Ja'far al-Sadiq about greater sins from the Book of God, and that Ja'far listed them with their proofs. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2156}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2160}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2161}
