Scholars differ on whether the clear interval between two bleedings is menstruation: the Hanafis and Shafiis count it as menses, the Malikis and Hanbalis as purity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7478} The menses of a pregnant woman: the Hanafis and Hanbalis treat it as illness, a point from the hadith forbidding intercourse with a pregnant woman until birth, while the Malikis and Shafiis treat it as menses when its conditions are met. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7478} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7479} Hanafi purity is sound if it lasts fifteen days or more between sound bleedings and unmixed, unsound if shorter or mixed, and complete if of fifteen days even if unsound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7479} Menstruation is a mark of puberty that brings duty; a woman's bath to lift major impurity during menses is invalid for the majority; the bath after menses is due when the flow stops, with the place perfumed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7479} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7480} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7481}
