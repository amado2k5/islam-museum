## Summary
This entry gathers what the museum holds on the principle that declaring something lawful or unlawful belongs to God and not to the words or whims of people. Verses of the Qur'an, among them [[passage/quran-16-116|al-Nahl 116]], rebuke those who say with their tongues that this is lawful and that is unlawful, and count it as an invention against God {cite:tanzil-quran}. In the report of Salman, the lawful is what God made lawful in His Book, the unlawful is what He forbade in it, and what He was silent about is among what He pardoned {cite:sunan-ibn-majah 3367}. Al-Shatibi records in al-Muwafaqat that forbidding the lawful and permitting the unlawful belong to God's right, because they are a first act of legislation {cite:muwafaqat p.445}. The books of hadith report that the Prophet declared something unlawful for himself, and a verse came down asking why he forbade what God had made lawful for him {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 5267}.

## In the Qur'an
[[passage/quran-16-116|al-Nahl 116]] forbids people to speak a falsehood with their tongues by calling one thing lawful and another unlawful, counts this as an invention against God, and says that those who invent a lie against God will not succeed {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-10-59|Yunus 59]] asks about the provision God sent down, part of which people made unlawful and lawful: did God permit them, or are they inventing a lie against Him {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-5-87|al-Ma'ida 87]] forbids the believers to declare unlawful the good things God made lawful for them and to transgress, and states that God does not love transgressors {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-66-1|al-Tahrim 1]] addresses the Prophet and asks why he forbids what God made lawful for him in seeking the pleasure of his wives, and ends by saying that God is forgiving and merciful {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-6-119|al-An'am 119]] says that God has set out in detail what He forbade to people, except what they are driven to, and that many go astray by their own desires through ignorance {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-9-31|al-Tawba 31]] mentions people who took their rabbis and monks as lords beside God {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-42-21|al-Shura 21]] asks whether they have partners who laid down for them in religion what God did not allow, and warns the wrongdoers of a painful punishment {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In the Sunnah
In [[passage/ibnmajah-api-3367|Sunan Ibn Majah 3367]] Salman al-Farisi reports that the Prophet was asked about ghee, cheese and furs, and said that the lawful is what God made lawful in His Book, the unlawful is what God forbade in His Book, and what He was silent about is among what He pardoned {cite:sunan-ibn-majah 3367}. The same appears in [[passage/tirmidhi-api-1726|Jami' al-Tirmidhi 1726]], where al-Tirmidhi calls it a gharib report that he knows as a raised report only by this route {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 1726}. In [[passage/abudawud-api-3800|Sunan Abi Dawud 3800]] Ibn Abbas says that the people of the Jahiliyya ate some things and left others out of distaste, then God sent His Prophet, sent down His Book, made lawful what He made lawful and forbade what He forbade, so what He made lawful is lawful, what He forbade is unlawful, and what He was silent about is pardoned {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3800}. The report in [[passage/arbaun-30|Arba'un 30]] says that God laid down obligations, set limits and forbade certain things, and was silent about others, as a mercy and not out of forgetfulness {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam 30}. In [[passage/muslim-api-6116|Sahih Muslim 6116]] the gravest offender among the Muslims against the Muslims is the one who asked about something not forbidden to them, and it was then forbidden because of his asking {cite:sahih-muslim 6116}. In [[passage/tirmidhi-api-2664|Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2664]] the Prophet speaks of a man reclining on his couch who hears a report from him and says that between us and you is the Book of God, so what we find lawful in it we treat as lawful and what we find unlawful we treat as unlawful; the Prophet then says that what God's Messenger forbade is like what God forbade, and al-Tirmidhi calls the report hasan gharib {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 2664}. [[passage/tirmidhi-api-3095|Jami' al-Tirmidhi 3095]] explains the verse of al-Tawba mentioned above: those people did not worship their rabbis and monks, but when these made something lawful for them they treated it as lawful, and when they forbade something they treated it as unlawful; al-Tirmidhi calls it a gharib report {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 3095}. [[passage/bukhari-api-3110|Sahih al-Bukhari 3110]] and [[passage/abudawud-api-2069|Sunan Abi Dawud 2069]] record a report about Fatima and about a son-in-law of the Prophet from the Banu Abd Shams whom he praised, in which the Prophet says that he does not make the lawful unlawful nor the unlawful lawful {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3110} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 2069}. In the episode of the honey, [[passage/bukhari-api-5267|Sahih al-Bukhari 5267]], [[passage/bukhari-api-6691|Sahih al-Bukhari 6691]], [[passage/muslim-api-3678|Sahih Muslim 3678]] and [[passage/abudawud-api-3714|Sunan Abi Dawud 3714]] report that some of the Prophet's wives told him they smelled maghafir on him, and he said that he had drunk honey at the house of Zaynab bint Jahsh and would not return to it, whereupon the verse came down asking why he forbade what God had made lawful for him {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 5267} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 6691} {cite:sahih-muslim 3678} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3714}. In [[passage/nasai-api-3959|Sunan al-Nasa'i 3959]] Anas says that the Prophet had a slave woman with whom he lay, and Aisha and Hafsa kept at him until he forbade her to himself, so God sent down the verse {cite:sunan-al-nasai 3959}. In [[passage/nasai-api-3420|Sunan al-Nasa'i 3420]] a man tells Ibn Abbas that he has made his wife unlawful for himself; Ibn Abbas answers that he is mistaken and she is not unlawful for him, recites the verse, and says that the heaviest expiation is due from him, the freeing of a slave {cite:sunan-al-nasai 3420}.

## In the reports of the Companions, the Successors and the scholars
Al-Shatibi says in [[passage/muwafaqat-halal-2-445|al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 445]] that forbidding the lawful, permitting the unlawful and the like belong to the right of God Most High, because they are a first act of legislation and the founding of a legal rule imposed on the servants, in which they have no free judgement, since minds have no power to call a thing good or bad so as to make it lawful or unlawful {cite:muwafaqat p.445}. In [[passage/muwafaqat-halal-2-821|al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 821]] and [[passage/muwafaqat-halal-6-172|al-Muwafaqat, vol. 6, p. 172]] he says that the effect of a fatwa lapses when it collides with a decisive text, and counts among such cases forbidding the lawful and permitting the unlawful, giving as examples the permission of mut'a and of riba and the forbidding of the good things of provision {cite:muwafaqat p.821} {cite:muwafaqat p.172}. [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-1-273|Hilyat al-Awliya', vol. 1, p. 273]] and [[passage/sahaba-hayat-sahaba-halal-1-530|Hayat al-Sahaba, vol. 1, p. 530]] record a saying about trial, to the effect that whoever wants to know whether the trial has struck him should look: if he sees as unlawful what he used to see as lawful, or sees as lawful what he used to see as unlawful, then the trial has struck him {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.273} {cite:hayat-al-sahaba-kandahlawi-sabbani p.530}.
