## Nature and kinds
Nature: Dioscorides is cited as deriving the name from a word meaning excellent, in the sense of excellence in benefit to a woman after childbirth. The book records this as a name-claim and not as an established use. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.470}
The madahraj has leaves like those of a plant called qasus, fragrant with a sharpness and round in outline; its root is like turnip in its shoots; its flower is white with a red inside and the smell is foul. The tawil has longer leaves than the madahraj, slender stems about a span long, and a root about a span or more long and as thick as a finger; it is called the male. The tayyib has slender stems with many round leaves, a flower like that of rue, and long slender roots with a thick fragrant bark that perfumers use to scent oils. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.470}
Temperament: all its kinds are hot in the third degree and dry in the second. The book says the tawil is equal to the madahraj in heating and may surpass it, except in fineness, where the madahraj is finer. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.471}

## Actions
Actions: it is a cleansing, thinning, opening, rarefying and drawing remedy. The tawil is better for growing flesh and for ulcers, because it is clearer and hotter; in the other actions the madahraj opens and thins more strongly. The madahraj is stronger in calming the pains of wind. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.471}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it helps vitiligo, cleans the teeth and their filth, especially the madahraj, and clears the complexion. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.471}
Wounds and ulcers: it cleans foul, malignant and scaling ulcers, grows flesh, especially the tawil, and prevents the malignancy of deep putrid ulcers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.471}
Joints: it helps dislocation of muscle; it is applied as an ointment on gout, especially the madahraj, helps weakness of the muscles, and gout sufferers drink it with benefit. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.471}
Organs of the head: it cleans the filth of the ear, strengthens hearing when put in with honey, and prevents matter from forming there. Used with pepper it cleans the superfluities of the brain, helps epilepsy and firms the gums. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.471}
Organs of the chest: good for asthma, especially the madahraj, clears the chest, and helps pain of the side drunk in water; the madahraj is stronger in this. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.472}
Organs of digestion: good for hiccup, and likewise for the spleen with oxymel; it is also applied to the spleen with vinegar and helps greatly; the madahraj is stronger in all this. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.472}
Excretory organs: when a measured amount is taken ground and drunk, it purges phlegmy and bilious humours and helps the seat; the amount is left out because the museum does not transcribe measured doses. Drunk with bitter and pepper, the tawil or the madahraj cleans the superfluities of the womb after childbirth, brings the menses and expels the foetus. This is a labelled listing about the womb and menses, set aside for the founder's ruling. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.472}
Poisons: it helps the sting of the scorpion, especially the tawil, and helps the bites of vermin and poisons when drunk in wine or applied. The drinking amount is left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.472}
Substitutes: the book gives weight proportions for the substitutes of the madahraj and the tawil; these are left out as measured quantities. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.472}
