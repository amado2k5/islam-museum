## Summary
In Ka'b's account he was saved by truthfulness when he knew nothing else would save him, and counted his truthfulness with the Prophet the greatest blessing after Islam. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/177, 180-181}

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen
In the second episode the Sheikh says that the Prophet disliked lying and loved truthful speech. He faults people who lie even in worship, and gives as an example the opening takbir of prayer, said without attention to its meaning. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:13:00}

He tells of a man who hid under a heap of palm fibre to escape people who wanted to kill him. When the pursuers asked him, he answered truthfully, so they took him for a madman and left him. He uses the story to show the merit of truthful speech. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:15:00} (needs_source: anecdote-unsourced)

He holds that a person who keeps to truth is recorded before God as truthful (siddiq), and one who lies until lying becomes a habit is recorded as a liar. He describes the truthful person as balanced within himself, and in the same passage refers to a hadith without naming its narrator. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:18:30}

He gives the example of a habitual liar who, asked the price of a watch, lies about it and changes the lie in every gathering. Repeated lying, he concludes, records its owner as a liar. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:19:00}

He criticises managers who promise staff promotions and do not keep their word, and holds that this is the promise of Satan. He closes by saying that the Muslim is truthful in speech; most of this passage is garbled in the transcript. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:14:30} (needs_source: garbled-passage)

He holds that clear speech is preferable to the affected language of meetings, with its reports, strategies and frameworks, which the listener cannot follow. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:24:00}

He tells of a security firm that interviewed young Muslim and non-Muslim candidates and asked them about drinking alcohol. One candidate answered truthfully and was hired, because security needs truthfulness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:39:00} (needs_source: anecdote-unnamed)

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 6)
The Sheikh says that lying leaves a person confused and tense in this world. He claims that the liar uses seven areas of his brain to back up his lie and must then remember it and match it against the next lie, while the truthful person engages only three (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:43:00}.

The Sheikh relates a hadith to the effect that lying leads to wickedness and wickedness to the Fire, and that a man keeps on lying until he is recorded with God as a liar; he prays that we be truthful in word, deed and work {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:43:30}. He explains idle talk (laghw) as whatever a sensible person strikes out of his reckoning and does not waste his time on {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:43:30}.

## From the series Al Naba Al Safi: the degrees of truth
The Sheikh says that truth has degrees: truth in speech, then in intention, then in resolve, then in fulfilment, and then in the whole of religion: prayer, zakat, fasting, kin ties, and avoiding backbiting, envy and hatred. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:27:00}
He says that truthfulness begins with speech and then moves on to intention, resolve and fulfilment, and that God is the one who makes the truthful truthful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:33:00}
He holds that truth in intention is no less important than truth in speech, and that visiting a sick man in order to win favour with someone is not truthful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:23:00}
He says that truth must be free of any flaw in belief, of any doubt about provision, and of any defect in deeds, and that a person must not doubt the Provider. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:41:00}

## Defining truth in the Sheikh's teaching
The Sheikh defines true truthfulness as telling the truth at the moment when a lie seems the only way out. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:19:30}
He says that truth in moments of hardship is speaking the truth when a lie seems the only way out, and that God rewards the truthful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:40:00}
He cites a saying that truthfulness leads to righteousness and that a truthful person is recorded as truthful, and he names Mary and Abu Bakr as examples. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:17:00}
He cites a saying that truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise, and that one who keeps on lying is recorded as a liar. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:21:30}
He tells of a man held in confinement whom the ruler wanted to release. The man insisted on the truth and did not deny his fault, and he was released because he had spoken the truth. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:20:30}

## Lying and its effects
The Sheikh says that the tongue records every word. He criticises those who divide lies into white, red and green, and insists that a lie is a lie. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:27:00}
The speaker's view is that a liar robs a person of his mind as a thief robs him of his money, and he mocks exaggerated claims about the weather and prices. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:21:00}
He cites a saying that lying will spread among people until a trustworthy person in a clan becomes rare. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:18:30}
He cites a saying that lying leads to wickedness, and wickedness leads to the Fire. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:18:30}
He criticises lying without need, such as lying about the price of an item. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:33:30}
He says that when lying becomes common in dealings between spouses, neighbours, employers and workers, the whole community is harmed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:45:30}
He advises someone who took money by a lie to return it if possible, and if it is not possible, to leave the matter to God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:43:00}

## The speaker's view: lying to keep peace and life
The Sheikh takes up lying in two cases: a spouse praising the other to keep affection, and lying to keep life going as an exception. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:34:30}

## The speaker's view: truth in work and worship
The Sheikh says that the community should be truthful in work and worship, in small matters and in great. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:45:00}
He criticises employees and managers who work without sincerity, and says that the condition of the nation would change if people were sincere. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:20:30}
He sums up the qualities as a framework of truthful speech, honest work, sincere intention, trustworthiness, and eating only what is lawful. He notes that the prophets were told to eat good things, and asks whether these qualities become a method of conduct. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:36:30}
