## Summary
Ibn Sina mentions constipation in infants and its treatment with suppositories, honey, terebinth gum, gentle oiling of the belly and smearing the navel, then a stinging of the gum and its fomenting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.212}.

## Treatment
Retention of the bowels may befall them; they are given suppositories of mouse droppings, or a suppository of boiled honey alone or with pennyroyal, or of the root of the sky-blue iris as it is or burnt; or a little honey is fed, or a chickpea's measure of terebinth gum; the belly is gently anointed with oil, or the navel smeared with ox gall and bukhur maryam {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.212}.

## Stinging of the gum
At times a stinging comes in the gum, and it is fomented with oil and wax {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.212}. A short sentence follows about salted, spoiled meat whose sense is not clear in the scanned text, and it is left out.
