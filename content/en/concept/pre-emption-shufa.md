## Definition
Shufa, with a damma on the shin and a sukun on the fa, is a verbal noun meaning acquisition, from shaf' (the even, the opposite of the odd). The jurists define it as the compelled transfer of a plot to the buyer at what he paid, or as a compulsory right of acquisition that belongs to the old partner over what came into another's ownership for a consideration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10108}
It differs from a forced sale, which is wider, and from resale at cost (tawliya), which is the sale of what one owns at what one paid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10108}

## Its basis and purpose
Pre-emption is established by the sunna and by consensus, and its holder may claim it or leave it. Al-Shabramallisi of the Shafi'is says that if leaving it leads to sin, as when the buyer is known for wickedness, taking it is recommended, and obligatory if it is the only way to stop what he intends. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10108}
The jurists cite a report of Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah that the Prophet gave judgement of pre-emption in what has not been divided, and another version that it applies to every partnership in a house or garden not yet divided, and that a partner may not sell until his partner is asked, so that he may take or leave. Ibn al-Mundhir says scholars agree that a partner who has not divided the land, house or orchard that is sold has the right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10109}
The purpose is to lift the harm that partnership usually brings. Partners may divide the property, or the one who wishes to take the whole may take it when the other is not harmed. When one partner sells his share, his partner has the better right over the stranger, so the seller still gets his price and the harm to both ends. The Shafi'is add that the purpose is to avoid the cost of division and the making of conveniences, and some say it is to avoid the harm of bad partnership. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10109}

## Which partnership gives the right
All agree that a co-owner with an undivided share in the property has the right so long as the property is not divided. They differ on property that cannot be divided, such as a well, a small bath or a road. Malik in one report, al-Shafi'i in the sounder view, and the Hanbalis in their school view deny the right there, since allowing it would harm the seller, who could not escape by division, and the buyer may refuse, so the sale may fall through and the right with it. The Hanafis, Malik in another report, al-Shafi'i in the sounder view and the Hanbalis in one report give it in such property too, whether or not divided, because the harm is greater in undividable property. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10110} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10111}

## Usufruct and rights in the sold thing
The partnership that grants the right is partnership in ownership, so it applies to the thing itself. Most jurists deny it to partners in usufruct, though one view of Malik allows it. Some Malikis allow it for a hired thing only if it can be divided and the claimant takes it to live in. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10111}
Jurists differ on whether a neighbour or a partner in some right of the sold thing, such as water or a path, has the right. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis deny it to the neighbour and to the partner in rights. Their evidence is the report of Jabir: when boundaries are set and roads are fixed, there is no pre-emption; they add that pre-emption would harm the buyer, who needs a house for himself and his household. The same view is attributed to the people of Medina, to 'Umar and 'Uthman, and to Ibn al-Musayyab, al-Zuhri and al-Awza'i, among others. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10111} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10112}
The Hanafis, Ibn Shubruma, al-Thawri and Ibn Abi Layla give it to the adjoining neighbour and to a partner in a right of the sold thing. The order they give is: partnership in the thing, then partnership in its rights such as water and path, then neighbourhood. Their evidence is a report of 'Amr ibn al-Sharid about Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Abu Rafi', the freed man of the Prophet, and the saying that the neighbour has more right to his neighbour's land. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10112} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10113}

## Conditions of neighbourhood for the Hanafis
The Hanafis treat neighbourhood as a ground, but only where the two properties touch at some point of some boundary, however short. A house that touches the front and one that touches the far end of the courtyard stand alike. A neighbour across the road has no right, because the harm of bad neighbourhood needs one owner's property to join the other's. Shurayh held that pre-emption follows the doors, so the nearest door has the better right, and the Hanafis cite a report of 'A'isha about a gift to the nearer of two neighbours. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10113} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10114}
A neighbour across the way has no right in Hanafi doctrine; but where all the houses lie on a cul-de-sac, the neighbour across has one. Only a neighbour who owns the property has the right, so a tenant or borrower does not, since residence by hire is not lasting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10114}
Owners of storeys in a building have the right by neighbourhood; if the upper storey falls before the lower owner claims, Abu Yusuf holds the right lapses, and Muhammad holds it stands, since it rests on the site and not on the building. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10114}

## Parties, object and the pre-emptor's title
The parts of pre-emption are three: the pre-emptor, who takes; the buyer from whom it is taken, who holds the property; and the property taken. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10115}
The pre-emptor must own the property by which he claims at the time of the purchase, since the claim rests on joining ownership, and he must still own it until he takes the property by consent or by judgement. He has no right in a house he lives in by hire or loan, or one he sold before this purchase, or a house made a mosque or an endowment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10115} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10116}
An endowment has no right of pre-emption, either by partnership or by neighbourhood, neither its administrator nor the beneficiary. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10116}

## Division of the deal
All agree that taking by pre-emption may not split the deal. If the property is one plot and the buyer one person, the pre-emptor may not take part and leave part. If there are several buyers, he may take one share, more, or all. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10116}

## Whom it may be taken from; transactions covered
Pre-emption may be taken from any buyer, whether a relative of the seller or a stranger, since the texts are general. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10116}
The transaction that gives pre-emption is an exchange, sale or its like. There is none in a gift, charity, inheritance or bequest, since one cannot take by the same means as the other party. One report of Malik gives it in every transfer for consideration or without it, except inheritance. Jurists differ on dower, the arsh of a wound, settlement by compromise and khul' payments: the Hanafis and a Hanbali view deny them, and the Malikis, Shafi'is and another Hanbali view admit them by analogy with sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10116} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10117}

## Gifts with a return, and the option of sale
- A gift on condition of a return: most jurists hold that pre-emption arises when both have taken possession; if only one has taken possession, the Hanafis deny it, while Zufar holds it arises at the contract, which is the more likely Shafi'i view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10117}
- An option of sale: if the option belongs to the seller, or to both, there is no pre-emption until the sale is binding. If it belongs to the buyer, the Hanafis give pre-emption, the Malikis do not, the Shafi'is have two views of which the sounder gives it, and the Hanbalis do not before the option ends. If the seller grants the option to the pre-emptor, the Hanafis give him no right; if he approves, the sale stands as if made afresh. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10117} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10118}

## Some kinds of sale
- A sale at public auction: the jurists do not bar pre-emption in it, since they do not exclude it from the conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10118}
- A property bought to be made a mosque: the Hanafis and Malikis, and Abu Bakr among the Hanbalis, hold that the pre-emptor may pull the mosque down and take the house, in the apparent view of the Hanafi school; a contrary report from Abu Hanifa exists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10118} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10119}

## Kinds of property
The jurists agree that pre-emption applies to real property and what is like it that is fixed; they differ on movables. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10118}

## Shufa in movables
The jurists differ on whether pre-emption applies to movables. The Hanafis, Shafi'is, and in the sounder view the Malikis and Hanbalis deny it: they cite Jabir's report on boundaries and roads, the saying "there is no pre-emption except in a house or real property", and the lasting and great harm in real property, unlike movables. Malik and Ahmad in one report allow it, relying on the general "what has not been divided". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10119} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10120}

## Stages of claiming
The claimant first shows his wish as soon as he learns of the sale: the immediate demand (talab al-muwathaba). Then he confirms it and calls witnesses: the demand for record and witness (talab al-taqrir wa-al-ishhad). If the right is still not secured he goes before the judge to press his claim and take the property: the demand of litigation and taking (talab al-khusuma wa-al-tamallul). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10120}

### Immediate demand
- Its time is when the claimant learns of the sale, by hearing it himself or being told. Abu Hanifa requires for the informant either two men, or a man and two women, or an upright person; Abu Yusuf and Muhammad require neither. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10120}
- For the Hanafis it must be immediate once he learns and is able; silence then voids it in the basic report, while another report of Muhammad allows the time of the session. The Hanafis excuse delay in some cases, such as hearing of the sale during the Friday sermon or greeting the buyer first, or an obstacle such as a dangerous river or a lion-infested field. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10121}
- The Malikis regard the time as wide; their view on whether it is limited varies, and the best known limit is one year. The sounder Shafi'i view is that it is immediate; the alternatives are three days after being able, a period enough for reflection, or no limit so long as it is not waived. Some Shafi'is list ten cases where immediacy is not required, such as a seller's option, a sale on credit, and a claimant who did not know he had the right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10121} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10122}
- The Hanbalis hold, in the sounder view, that the claim must be made at once when he learns of the sale or it lapses; in one report of Ahmad it stays open so long as no sign of consent occurs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10122}
- Reasons that delay the claim, such as night, hunger, thirst, purification, relieving oneself or attending prayer with its sunna, do not void it, so long as the buyer is not present. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10123}
- Calling witnesses to the immediate demand is not a condition of validity for the Hanafis and Shafi'is; it serves to prove the claim if it is denied. The Hanbalis require it. Any words that show the demand suffice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10123}

### Demand for record and witnesses (Hanafi)
The claimant calls witnesses against the seller if the property is in his hand, or against the buyer if it is in the buyer's hand, or at the property, that he now claims it. He may demand from either party or at the property, but not from the seller once the seller has lost possession. Naming and describing the property are not conditions in the apparent view; Abu Yusuf makes them conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10124} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10125}
The effect is to fix the right, so that delay before the judge does not void it, according to Abu Hanifa, until he waives it in words. Muhammad and Zufar say it is voided after a month without excuse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10125}

### Claim and taking before the judge
The claimant asks the judge, who asks whether the buyer admits the property by which he claims; if not, proof is required, since possession is an uncertain sign. If he cannot prove it, the buyer swears. The judge then asks the buyer whether he bought; if he denies it, the claimant must prove the sale, and if he cannot, the buyer swears. The claimant need not bring the price to the session; he may bring it after the judgement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10125} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10126}

## Pre-emption for a dhimmi against a Muslim
Muslims and dhimmis are agreed that a Muslim may claim against a dhimmi, and a dhimmi against a dhimmi. The Hanafis, Malikis and Shafi'is allow a dhimmi to claim against a Muslim, relying on the general reports, on a judgement by Shurayh for a dhimmi against a Muslim that 'Umar approved, and on the equal cause and wisdom. The Hanbalis deny it, citing a report in the Ilal of al-Daraqutni that there is no pre-emption for a Christian, because pre-emption is given for the claimant's ease, which a dhimmi does not accept, and because it would give a non-Muslim power over a Muslim. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10126} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10127}

## More than one claimant
### When the cause is the same
Claimants of the same rank, such as partners, share by share in ownership according to the Malikis, the Shafi'is in the sounder view and the Hanbalis in the sounder view, because the right follows ownership like the price. The Hanafis, and one view each of the Shafi'is and Hanbalis, divide by heads, since the cause, the partnership itself, is equal. Among neighbours the Hanafis divide equally whatever the length of the adjoining boundary. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10127} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10128}
### When the causes differ
The Hanafis give priority to the stronger cause: a partner in the thing sold, then a partner in its rights such as water or path, then the adjoining neighbour, following the saying that a partner has more right than an associate. If the first declines, the right passes to the next, and each must have demanded the pre-emption with the partner when he learned of the sale. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis give it only to a partner in ownership. The Malikis rank a partner in the share sold above a partner in the original property, as among heirs. A lender of land to a builder or planter ranks above the seller's other partners. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10128} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10129}
### When the buyer is a claimant
A buyer who is himself a claimant competes by the strength of his cause: he ranks above those weaker than himself and below those stronger. If all rank equally, he shares like them, by heads for the Hanafis and by share for the others. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10129} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10130}

## How the property is taken
For the Hanafis the right passes only by the buyer's consent or the judge's decision. If the property is in the buyer's hand, the claimant takes it from him and pays him the price; the first sale stands and the warranty is on the buyer. If it is in the seller's hand, he pays the seller and the warranty is on the seller. A judgement cannot be given without the defeated party present: both seller and buyer if the property is with the seller, the buyer alone if it is with the buyer. The title comes by the judgement itself and does not wait for delivery. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10130} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10131}
The judgement is given on the claim even if the price has not been brought; the buyer may keep the property until paid, and the judge may give a short time to pay. Muhammad says the judge should not decide until the claimant brings the price. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10131} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10132}
For the Malikis the taker owns the share by a judgement, by paying the price to the buyer, or by a witnessed taking. The Shafi'is need no judgement and no presence of the buyer, but need the claimant's word, such as "I have taken it", with the payment, or the buyer's acceptance of the price as a debt, or a judgement. The Hanbalis hold that a word showing the taking, with the price and property known, is enough, with no judgement; al-Qadi and Abu al-Khattab hold that ownership comes by the demand. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10132} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10133}

## Building and planting on the pre-empted property
If the buyer built or planted on the property before the judgement, the Hanafis (in the apparent report) give the claimant the choice of taking the land with the building at its value pulled down, or of forcing the buyer to pull it down and taking the bare land. Abu Yusuf denies the forced pulling down and gives the choice between paying the price and the value of the building, or leaving the claim. The crop is left until harvest. The Malikis say the taker owes the value of the building and planting, and the buyer keeps the produce until the taking. The Shafi'is give the claimant, if he knows, the right to pull down what was built or planted at no cost when the work was on common land; if built on the buyer's own share after division, it may not be pulled down for nothing. The Hanbalis give the claimant the value of the building or planting, unless the buyer prefers to take his building back. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10133} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10134} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10135}

## Warranty of the taken property
When the taker pays the price and the property proves to belong to another, the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that the claimant recovers the price from the buyer, who recovers it from the seller, so the warranty lies on the buyer. The Hanafis hold that if the claimant paid the buyer, the buyer is liable to him; if he paid the seller while the property was in the buyer's hand, the seller is liable. Ibn Abi Layla and 'Uthman al-Batti hold that the warranty lies on the seller, since the right arose from the seller's offer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10135} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10136}

## Loss, damage and what lies on the property
In Hanafi doctrine, if the buyer pulls down the building or uproots the trees, the taker takes the site at its share of the price, and the materials go to the buyer. If the house falls or the trees dry up without fault, the taker takes it at the price divided between the value of the property on the day of the sale and the materials on the day of the taking. If part of the land is lost, the price is reduced by the lost share. The Malikis do not make the buyer pay for loss not his fault. The Shafi'is: if part of a house is damaged, the taker takes all for the full price or leaves it; if part is lost, he takes the rest for its share. The Hanbalis: loss in the buyer's hand is the buyer's, and the taker takes what remains for its share. The Hanafis let the taker take land with fruit and crop at the first price while they are attached, but not once detached. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10136} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10137} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10138}

## Inheritance of the right
The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold the right of pre-emption heritable; the Hanbalis add that the claimant must have demanded it before death. The Hanafis hold that the right lapses if the claimant dies before taking it, because it is not property but a choice and an intention, which do not outlive the holder. If he dies after the judge has decided for him or after the buyer has delivered, his heirs may take it. If the buyer dies while the claimant lives, the claimant keeps the right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10138} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10139}

## Causes that void the right
- Leaving one of the three demands at its time: the immediate demand, the demand for record and witness, or the demand before the judge and for taking. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10138}
- Demanding part of the property when it is one plot and the buyer is one person, since pre-emption does not admit division. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10138} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10139}
- The death of the claimant before taking, in Hanafi doctrine. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10139}
- Release and waiver: a general release by the claimant voids the right; an express waiver such as "I waive it" voids it; an implied waiver is any sign of consent to the sale, such as bargaining with the buyer to buy the house. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10139} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10140} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10141}
- A waiver before the sale: most jurists hold it does not void the right, since a right cannot be waived before it arises. Ahmad is reported to hold that it voids the right, and al-Hakam, al-Thawri, Abu 'Ubayd and some scholars of hadith agree, citing the saying that a partner who wants to sell must offer the share to his partner first. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10139} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10140}
- Settling the right for money: the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that the settlement is void and the right is lost; Malik allows a settlement for a return. A waiver after the claim and before judgement or the buyer's consent voids the right; after judgement or consent it does not, since the property has then become owned. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10141}
