## Summary
Ibn Sina states that reducing and withholding food is most often undertaken in acute diseases and less so in chronic ones, where more care is given to the strength because the crisis is far off, and then shows how feeding is graded through the stages of an acute disease {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.267-268}.

## Chronic diseases
In chronic diseases food is also reduced, but less than in acute ones, because more care is given to the strength: the crisis and the end are far off, and if the strength is not kept it will not hold out until the time of crisis nor suffice to ripen what takes long to ripen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.267}.

## Acute diseases and their stages
In acute diseases the crisis is near and it is hoped the strength will not fail before the end; if that is feared, food is cut back sharply. The nearer the disease is to its start, the more feeding that strengthens the power is possible; as disease and symptoms increase, feeding is reduced, relying on what was given earlier and easing the power in its struggle; at the height the regimen is made very light. The sharper the disease and the nearer the crisis, the lighter the regimen, unless causes arise that forbid it, to be treated in the particular books {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.268}.
