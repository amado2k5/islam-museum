## Summary
Al-Battani takes the interval between his autumn observation and Ptolemy's to be seven hundred and forty-three Egyptian years, so that his observation comes about seven days ahead of whole quarters; the share of one year is three parts and twenty-four minutes of the three hundred and sixty parts of a day and night, and taking this from ninety parts leaves eighty-six parts and thirty-six minutes, so that the year's excess over three hundred and sixty-five days is eighty-six parts and thirty-six minutes of the three hundred and sixty parts of a day {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.67-68}.

## Reading the result
The figures the text gives for the span, the share and the remainder are read clearly and agree with each other: three parts and twenty-four minutes, multiplied by seven hundred and forty-three years, gives about seven days. The last statement in minutes and seconds of the length of the year itself is in disturbed letters in the printed text and is not given here. The result is that al-Battani's year is [[concept/zij-sabi-battani-year-shorter-than-ptolemys|shorter than Ptolemy's]] {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.68}.
