## Summary
For al-Sha'rawi, the kalala are collateral heirs of a deceased person who has neither ascendant nor descendant, and the rule concerns siblings. He separates uterine siblings from full or paternal siblings. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2028}

## The meaning of kalala
He derives the word from the crown (iklil), what surrounds the head: the kalala are the relatives who surround a person without being his root or branch. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2028}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2030}

## Uterine siblings and full or paternal siblings
He holds that the verse giving a sixth to one and a shared third to several concerns uterine siblings, and that the verse giving two sisters two-thirds concerns full or paternal siblings. Each has its own place. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2031}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2032}

## Answering the charge of contradiction
He says that anyone who reads the kalala rulings without distinguishing their sides may think the Qur'an contradicts itself, and that the distinction resolves this. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2032}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2033}
