## Nature and cause
Some physicians call it shukhus, but it is not, for shukhus is a kind of stiffness (jumud) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87} This is a sarsam-like illness made of the cold and the hot sarsam, because the swelling comes from the two humours together, that is, from phlegm and bile, and its cause is fullness bred by gluttony and by much eating, drinking and drunkenness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87} The two humours may be balanced or one may prevail and its signs prevail; if the phlegmatic prevails it is called subat suhari, and if the bilious prevails it is called suhar subati {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87}

## Signs
It may happen in a single illness that each has its turn over the other: at times phlegm prevails and makes coma, heaviness, laziness and closing of the eyes, so that answering what is said to him is hard and his answer is slow and considered; at times bile prevails and makes sleeplessness, raving and a continual stare, and does not let him sink into coma, but his coma is one from which he is roused when roused {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87} When bile prevails he is quickly roused, raves and aims at movement, opens his eye without blinking or closing it, his upper edge drawn up as with those who have sarsam, wishes to lie on his back, and his lying is not natural; his face flushes and tends to green and red, and in most of his states his lid is drawn up and he snores, and when he opens his eye he opens it as those with shukhus do, without blinking {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87} When he speaks his speech has no order, and he chokes on water, so that the water may come back from his nostril, and likewise he chokes on broths; this is a sign of his badness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87}

## How it differs from others
Retention of urine and stool together, or little of them, often comes in it, and tightness of breath comes upon him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88} This illness resembles lithargus, but is told from it in that the face in it is not as it was, as it is in the sufferers of lithargus, and in them sleeplessness and an open, unblinking eye come, and the fever in it is stronger; it resembles qaranitus, but is told from it in that the coma in it is more and the raving less {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88} As for its pulse, it is quick and frequent from the swelling and the feverish mixing, so it differs from the pulse of lithargus, and broad and short from phlegm and its swelling, so it differs from qaranitus, and it is stronger than the pulse of lithargus and weaker than the pulse of qaranitus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Ibn Sina says this illness resembles, in many of its states, suffocation of the womb, that in suffocation of the womb the face stays as it was, with the other signs given in its own chapter, that here the sick person can be made to speak of something and be asked to understand, and that this cannot be done with the woman whose womb is suffocated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88}
