## Nature and selection
Wood brought from India and from the western lands. It is hard, speckled and pleasant-smelling, with a bark like skin marked in varied colours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.356}.

## Temperament and actions
The passage gives no temperament for it at this point {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.356}.

## Uses by organ
Ornament: chewed, or used as a mouthwash in decoction, it sweetens the breath; it can be made into a powder dusted on the body to scent it, and used in incense in place of frankincense {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.356}. Stomach: a measured dose of the root, drunk, prevents stickiness of the stomach and helps liver pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.356}. Excretory organs: drinking it helps ulcers of the intestines and gripes; the book says Dioscorides testifies to this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.356}.
