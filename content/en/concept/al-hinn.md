## The name and the problem
Books usually speak of "al-jinn" with a jim, but in some reports about the time before Adam the word appears as "al-hinn" with a ha', a spot where scribes and editors have disagreed. The word al-hinn is not in the Qur'an; it belongs to the dictionaries and to some transmitted reports. I searched for the pairing "al-hinn wa-l-binn" and did not find it in the sources I read, so I attribute it to no book; what follows is what I verified.

## In al-Tabari
Commenting on [[passage/quran-2-30|al-Baqara 30]], al-Tabari relates from al-Dahhak on Ibn Abbas that Iblis belonged to a clan of the angels called "al-Hinn", created from the fire of the scorching wind among the angels; his name was al-Harith and he was a keeper of Paradise. The jinn named in the Qur'an, in this report, were created from a flame of fire; the first to dwell on earth were the jinn, who spread corruption, and God sent Iblis against them with a force of angels, this very clan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 459} The editor notes that the printed text read the word with a jim in both places, which he calls a mistake shown by the report's own logic, since it separates Iblis from the jinn of the Qur'an. He quotes al-Jahiz in al-Hayawan that some people divide the jinn into "jinn" and "hinn", making the hinn the weaker, while others see two distinct kinds, and gives a line of verse that couples "jinn and hinn". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 459} In al-Suddi's other report the angelic tribe is called "al-jinn" because they were keepers of Paradise; the editor says the manuscript has the ha' there but the explanation that follows shows a jim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 462}

## In the dictionaries
Lisan al-Arab, under h-n-n, gives al-hinn as a clan of the jinn, or a kind of the jinn, and cites the line coupling "jinn and hinn". Ibn Sida remarks that the line does not show the hinn to be the lowest of the jinn, only that they are another kind besides the jinn; it also reports al-Farra' that the hinn are a creation between jinn and humans, and cites a report from Ali and another from Ibn Abbas that some dogs are of the hinn. {cite:lisan-al-arab-alexandria-137940-v15 scan p. 705} The scan's text is poor, so I give this by meaning.

:::perspectives
- **Sunni transmitted view (al-Tabari, from Ibn Abbas):** the hinn are a clan of angels made from the fire of the scorching wind, Iblis among them, who fought the jinn who held the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 459}
- **Lexicographers:** the hinn are a class of the jinn, or a creation between jinn and humans. {cite:lisan-al-arab-alexandria-137940-v15 scan p. 705}
- **Another Sunni reading on the jinn:** al-Hasan al-Basri held that Iblis was never an angel and is the origin of the jinn, so there is no need for an angelic clan called al-hinn. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-alexandria-024188 p. 77}
:::

## What remains unknown
In what I read I found no independent chained report about the hinn beyond the above, and no hadith traced to the Prophet on them. The spelling is uncertain in some manuscripts, as the editor warns.
