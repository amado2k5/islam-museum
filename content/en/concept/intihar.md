## Definition
Intihar in the lexical sense is the verb "to slaughter oneself", but the jurists use it as the killing of a person by himself by any means. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3420}

## Kinds
- Killing by an act: doing a forbidden deed that takes life. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3421}
- Killing by refusal: refusing a duty that keeps life, such as refusing food and drink until death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3421}
- By intention, it is deliberate or by error. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3421}

## Examples of killing by refusal
- One who refuses what is lawful until he dies has killed himself, in the view of all scholars; one who is forced to eat what is forbidden to keep himself alive, and refuses until he dies, does the same. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3421}
- One who stops moving in water or fire while able to escape and dies is a self-killer; the one who threw him in bears no retaliation or diya, according to the general view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3421}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3422}
- Refusing treatment in illness is not self-killing for most jurists; neglecting a simple, reliable treatment for a wound is treated by some like self-killing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3422}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3423}

## Ruling
Self-killing is forbidden by agreement and counted among the greatest sins after polytheism, citing al-Nisa (4:29). The jurists hold the self-killer's burden greater than that of one who kills another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3423}

## Acts that resemble it but are not it
- Moving from one cause of death to another: one who is between fire and drowning may choose, according to the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3423}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3424}
- A single fighter who charges enemy ranks while believing he will be killed, if he will inflict harm on the enemy; the Hanafis allow it on that condition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3424}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3425}
- Fighting unbelievers who use Muslims as cover, provided the aim is the unbelievers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3426}

## Ordering or compelling a killing
- If a man says to another "kill me" and is killed: the Hanafis treat it as a killing without retaliation but with a diya from the killer's property; Zufar and a Maliki view impose retaliation; the Hanbalis and the preferred Shafi'i view remove both. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3426}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3427}
- One who compels another by a complete threat to kill the compelled is treated as a self-killer by the majority: no retaliation and no diya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3428}

## Joint wounds and killing
The jurists differ on one who wounds himself and is then wounded by another and dies of both; retaliation and diya depend on whether each wound was deliberate or by error. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3429}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3430}

## Effects of self-killing
- Most jurists hold that the self-killer does not leave Islam, so the hadith on his eternal fire are taken for one who makes the act lawful, or as a warning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3430}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3431}
- One who attempts self-killing and survives is punished; no diya is due if he dies, according to most jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3431}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3432}
- Expiation for killing oneself, by error or deliberately, is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3432}
- The self-killer is washed and prayed over by most jurists; some refuse the prayer, and the Hanbalis have the imam refrain while others pray. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3433}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 6)

The Sheikh asks whether it is permissible to tell a bereaved mother that her son was a suicide, and says that in his understanding the sharia ruling on someone who rode the sea while it was rough and died is that he is a suicide; he adds that prevention is better than cure {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:30:30}.
