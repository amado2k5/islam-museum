## Summary
For the shoulder joint Ibn Sina lists three muscles coming from the chest and drawing it down, two from the flank, five from the shoulder blade, and a two-headed muscle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.68-69}.

## From the chest and flank
Three muscles from the chest draw the upper arm down: one from under the breast, bringing the arm toward the chest with a downward pull; one from the top of the sternum, circling the inner side of the arm's head with a slight lift; and a great double muscle from the whole sternum which, acting by its upper part, brings the arm to the chest lifting it, by the other part lowering it, and by both, straight. Two muscles from the flank: one great, drawing the arm toward the back ribs, and a fine one that helps {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.68-69}.

## From the shoulder blade
Five muscles arise from the shoulder blade: one carries the arm away with a lean inward; two carry it away with a lean outward; a fourth fills the hollow of the blade and turns the arm backward; a fifth draws the top of the arm's head upward {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.69}. The arm also has a two-headed muscle coming from under the collarbone and from the neck, one head within and the other outside on the back of the blade; acting together they lift straight. Some people add two further muscles, a small one from the breast and another buried in the shoulder joint {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.69}.
