## Summary
Sha'rawi reads the verse that threatens those who sell God's covenant and their oaths for a small price ([[passage/quran-3-77|Al Imran 77]]) as covering anyone who sells a sign of God for gain. He does not limit it to one occasion {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1552}.

## The occasion of revelation
Sha'rawi reports that a group in a time of drought and famine came to [[person/kab-ibn-al-ashraf|Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf]] asking for food and clothing. He told them that he wished to feed and clothe them, but that God had withheld great good from them because they had professed faith in the Prophet. After a while they preferred food and clothing to faith and claimed that they had misread their own books about him. Sha'rawi reads this as buying a price with the signs {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1553}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1554}. He names no collection for this report, so it needs a source (needs_source).

## The lesson lies in the general wording
Sha'rawi says the lesson lies in the general subject and not in the particular cause. Anyone who makes a sign of God an article for sale in order to take a price falls under the text, whether by altering a ruling to appear modern or by following desires {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1553}.

## Covenant and disposition
Sha'rawi offers two readings of the covenant: the covenant of primordial nature, or the covenant God took from the People of the Book to believe in the Prophet if they reached him. He then explains khuluq as a settled disposition in the soul, from which acts come with ease, such as truthfulness or generosity. To be without khalaq in the Hereafter means that this disposition has no share for its holder {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1554}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1555}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1556}.
