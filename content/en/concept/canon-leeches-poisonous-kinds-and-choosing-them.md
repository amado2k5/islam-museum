## Summary
Ibn Sina begins chapter 22 on leeches by reporting what the Indians said of their poisonous kinds and what they cause, then shows where leeches should be caught and how to choose them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.

## The poisonous kinds
The Indians said that some leeches are poisonous by nature, so he advises avoiding those with a large head and a black-blue or a green colour, the hairy ones, the one like the eel-like fish marmahij, those with azure lines, and those coloured like the water bird abu qalamun. All of these are poisonous, and putting them on brings swellings, fainting, bleeding, fever, slackness and bad ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.

## Where they are caught
Those caught in bad, muddy waters are to be avoided, and those caught in algae-bearing waters are chosen. He adds that the draw of a leech on blood goes deeper than the draw of cupping {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.

## The kind to choose
A leech is suitable when its colours are like a gem, with green above and two arsenic-yellow lines running along it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.
