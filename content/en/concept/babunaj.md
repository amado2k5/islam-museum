## Nature and selection
A herb of several colours: some with yellow flowers, some white, some purplish. Its leaves and flowers are kept by pressing them into cakes, and its root is dried and stored {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.382}. Galen is quoted as saying that it is close in fineness to the rose, but hot, with a heat suited to the body like the heat of oil. It grows in rough places near the edges, and is picked and gathered in spring {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.382}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot and dry in the first degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.382}. It opens, thins, and softens the thickening of humours, and dissolves them with little or no drawing. The book says this property is its own among the drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.382}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: it calms hot swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.382}. Joints: it relaxes the stretched parts and strengthens all the nerve organs. The book calls it the most useful drug for fatigue, because its heat is like the heat of the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. Head: it strengthens the brain, helps cold headache, and suits thrush {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. Eyes: as a poultice it heals an explosive wound of the eye, and it helps inflammation, irritation, pustules, itch, pain, and scabies when applied {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. Chest: it eases the expelling of phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. Food organs: it cures jaundice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. Excretory organs: it promotes urine and expels stone, and its flowers, especially the purplish kind and the chamomile, are used as a fomentation on the bladder for cold and hot pains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. The book also says it brings on the menses when drunk or sat in its water, and expels the foetus and the afterbirth. These are recorded as the book's own statements and not as advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. Fevers: its oil is rubbed on in recurring fevers, and it is drunk for old fevers at their end, and helps any fever that is not very sharp {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}.

## Substitutes
The substitute for strengthening the brain and for headache is barinjasf, which the book calls the qaysum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}.
