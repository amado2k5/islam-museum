## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that the build of an organ fixes the strength of the drug it needs: an organ with easy passages and a loose texture is enough served by a mild, moderate drug, while a dense organ with no cavity needs a strong one, and he ranks organs from those needing the strongest drug to those needing least {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.268-269}.

## Easy and hard organs
Some organs, by their build, have easy passages and an empty place within or beside them, so the surplus is driven off by a mild, moderate drug; others are not so and need a strong one. Some are loose in texture and a mild drug is enough, others are dense and need a strong one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.268}.

## The ranking
The organs most in need of a strong drug are those with no cavity on either side and no open space; then those with it on one side; then those with space on both sides but compact and dense, like the kidney; then those with a cavity on both sides that are loose, like the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.269}.
