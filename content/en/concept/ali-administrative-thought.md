## Summary
Al-Sallabi derives from 'Ali's directive to Malik al-Ashtar al-Nakha'i when he appointed him governor of Egypt, and from his letters to his officials, a set of administrative concepts, presenting them as his reading of the texts rather than a formally structured system {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 361}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 376}.

## Ministers and Consultation
The book states that appointing ministers is among the governor's powers, stipulates that a minister must not have been among those who served wicked rulers before him, advises that he be the one least inclined to assist in what God dislikes, and recommends choosing one among the ministers to serve as deputy and assistant {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 361}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 362}. Regarding consultation, the governor is advised to confer with scholars and discuss matters with the wise {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 362}. The book states that the rule of shared decision-making applies to every undertaking, and that "partnership in opinion leads to what is right" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 374}.

## The Army and Foreign Policy
The book states that the province must have a military force, that forming, preparing, and appointing its commanders is the governor's responsibility, and that it is funded from the public treasury (bayt al-mal) {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 362}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 363}. The directive also instructs the governor to honor treaties, and the book supports this with two Qur'anic verses referenced here: [[passage/quran-16-91|Surah al-Nahl: 91]] and [[passage/quran-17-34|Surah al-Isra': 34]] {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 364}.

## Land Tax, Development, and the Public Treasury
'Ali says: "All people are dependents of the land tax (kharaj) and its payers," and the book notes that developing the land is more important than collecting the land tax, because whoever seeks the land tax without development ruins the country {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 366}. The book considers the public treasury (bayt al-mal) "the heart of the province," from which the salaries of officials, judges, and the needy are paid, while the surplus is sent to the capital {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 365}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 366}.

## Officials and Oversight
'Ali advises that officials be appointed "on probation" rather than through favoritism, that they be given ample provision so they have no need for treachery, and that trustworthy and loyal informants keep watch over them and submit reports without the governor rushing to judge individuals {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 366}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 367}. The book cites the story of the hoopoe with Solomon and refers to [[passage/quran-27-20|Surah al-Naml: 20]] {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 367}.

## Social Classes, Trade, and Punishment
'Ali says: "Know that the subjects consist of classes, none of which can thrive without the others," listing soldiers, scribes, judges, tax collectors, merchants, protected non-Muslims (ahl al-dhimmah), and the needy {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 368}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 369}. Regarding markets, he says: "Forbid hoarding, for the Messenger of God forbade it," and the book states that the state needs an apparatus to oversee merchants and prevent hoarding {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 368}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 369}. 'Ali affirms that "the well-doer and the wrongdoer" are not equal, and that incentives are for the well-doer and punishment for the wrongdoer according to the law of reward and punishment {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 369}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 370}.

## Direct Administration and Bureaucracy
The book states that the governor must not seclude himself from his subjects, and that a rigid administrative hierarchy turns things upside down so that "the small becomes great and truth becomes falsehood," as the book quotes 'Ali, calling on the governor to dedicate a public assembly for those with needs to speak to him directly {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 372}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 373}. Among 'Ali's sayings cited in the book is: "Whoever obeys procrastination forfeits rights" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 373}.

## Selection and Safeguards
The book states that selection is based on clear standards rather than personal ties, that the criterion is truth and trustworthiness, that office is a trust upon the official's shoulders, and that the official is provided with sufficient livelihood and standing so he does not covet bribes {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 373}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 374}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 375}. It adds that seeking the help of experienced people is a source of practical knowledge, and that the governor is a father before being a holder of authority {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 375}{cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 376}.
