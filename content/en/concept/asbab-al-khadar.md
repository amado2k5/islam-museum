## From the power's side and the instrument's
The cause of numbness is either from the power's side — that it weakens, as in the strong acute fevers that end in numbness, in one about to faint and near death — or from the instrument's side: its temper corrupted by severe cold or severe heat, or the nerve's substance thick so the spirit does not pass through well, which is why what is in the foot's touch compared to the hand's touch is like a numbness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.159}

## Obstructions and effusion
Or it is from obstructions of thick humours — blood, phlegm, black bile, possibly yellow bile — or an obstruction from the pressure of a swelling or abscess, the pressure of a band or ligature, a posture twisting or squeezing the nerve, or a posture that pours blood or another humour onto the organ so the passages are blocked; most of this is from blood, which is why changing the posture removes it and sensation returns; it may also arise from dryness, the passages closing as the fibres gather — a bad kind — and an obstruction may befall the relaxation that comes of a temperate moisture without matter, the closing of the channels following that relaxation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.159}

## Seats of the causes and what it warns of
The causes may be in the brain itself: if universal, covering the whole body, it kills that day; they may be in the spinal cord, begin from one vertebra or lie in a nerve's branch; if the cold numbness becomes chronic it leads to relaxation; prevalent numbness warns of apoplexy, epilepsy, convulsion, tetanus or general palsy; any organ's numbness persisting warns of palsy or convulsion striking it; facial numbness warns of facial palsy; numbness often follows pneumonia, pleurisy and the cold brain-fever; and numbness that persists then is followed by dizziness warns of apoplexy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.159}
