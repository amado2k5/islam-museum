## Summary
Ibn Sina counts rain water among the excellent waters, especially summer rain from thundering cloud, as opposed to rain from cloud driven by violent winds, which is turbid and of mixed substance. Because it is so thin, however, putrefaction comes quickly to rain water and harms the chest and the voice. It is less open to putrefaction if boiled promptly, and its harm is made safe by taking sour things {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.136}.

## Wells, underground channels and seepage
Waters of wells and underground channels are poor compared with spring waters, because they are pent up and mixed with earthy matter for a long time and were brought out by contrivance and art rather than by any power in them tending to emerge. Worst are those given passages in lead, which take up its power and bring on ulcers of the intestines. Seepage water is worse than well water: a well's source is renewed by drawing, so its movement continues, while seepage lingers long in the pores of foul earth and moves slowly because of the abundance of its matter, and occurs only in corrupt ground {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.136-137}.
