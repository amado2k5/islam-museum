## Summary
Al-Battani defines multiplication as doubling one number as many times as the units of the other, and then shows that degrees times degrees give degrees, degrees times minutes minutes, degrees times seconds seconds, and that each order below the degree multiplied by itself falls as far below the degree as it sits, so minutes times minutes give seconds and minutes times seconds give thirds. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.12-13}.

## The definition and its cases
He says multiplying whole numbers by whole numbers is doubling one as many times as the units of the other; multiplying fractions by whole numbers is doubling the fractions by the units or splitting the units by the fractions' share of the unit; and multiplying fractions by fractions is splitting one of them by the other's share of the unit. So multiplication of fractions works as taking a part of the unit. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.13}.

## The order of the product
He shows that degrees multiplied by degrees give degrees, by minutes give minutes, by seconds seconds, and likewise with thirds, fourths and onward, the product taking the order of the lower one multiplied in. Among orders below the degree, each multiplied by itself falls as far below it as it lies below the degree: minutes times minutes are seconds and minutes times seconds thirds; seconds times seconds are fourths and seconds times thirds fifths. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.13}.
