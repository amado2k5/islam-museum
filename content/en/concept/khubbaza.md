## Nature
A kind of mulukhiya, and it is said that khubbaza is the wild and mulukhiya the garden kind; a kind of khubbaza is called the mulukhiya of the sorcerers and it is al-khitmi, and the Jews' herb is not far from being one of its varieties, and it is red {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Choice
The wild is subtler and drier, and the great wateriness of the garden kind lessens its strength {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Temperament
Cold and moist in the first degree; it is said that the garden kind is hot and dry, and the one who says this is Paulus, who may have gone to the Jews' herb, which is called mulukhiya {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Actions and properties
There is softening in it, and it is said to be subtler than sarmaq and thicker than chard, the wild being subtler and drier; it is said that the garden kind warms a little and descends quickly because of its moisture and stickiness, and it is moderate in digestion, and its moisture is said to be thicker than the moisture of lettuce; the book quotes Paulus that it contracts, peels and dissolves without sting, and the likelihood is that he means the Jews' herb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Swellings and pustules
It benefits namla and erysipelas, the leaf of the wild with olives benefits a burn by fire, and likewise its decoction as a pouring, and the garden kind benefits the beginning of a hot swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Wounds and ulcers
Chewed raw with salt and put on fistulae, especially the small, and in the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Organs of the head
Ulcers of the head are poulticed with it with urine and it benefits greatly, and it is chewed for thrush {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Ocular organs
When its leaf is chewed and used with a little salt it cleans fistulae of the eye and grows flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Organs of the chest
Its leaf and flower are all softening to the chest and calming to cough that arises from heat and dryness, and its seed is better than it in removing roughness of the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Organs of nourishment
The garden kind is bad for the stomach, and there is in it an opening of obstructions of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Excretory organs
Its flower benefits ulcers of the kidneys and bladder, drunk and beaten with oil; the seed of mulukhiya benefits abrasion and ulcers of the gut; the stalks of the garden khubbaza benefit the intestines and bladder and soften the belly and its pains when its water is drunk or a drink is made of it; there is in it a power to promote urine; and of the wild khubbaza, the kind that turns with the sun loosens raw humour and bile, and may overdo it and loosen blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Poisons
Its leaf calms the sting of "al-zaytun" as a poultice (so printed; the sense is not settled); its seed is drunk with continual vomiting for poisons, and it benefits the sting of the tarantula; a report attributed to the book {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that its leaf and flower increase milk, and that its decoction benefits hardness of the womb by sitting in it and by enema; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}
