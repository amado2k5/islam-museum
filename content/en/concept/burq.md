## Nature and selection
It is stronger than salt and of the same kind in its power, but it has no astringency. It can be burned on potsherd over glowing coals until it is roasted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. The best kind is the light, flaky, friable, spongy Armenian, white, rose-coloured or purplish and pungent. The African kind is related to the other boraxes as borax is to salt. The foam of borax is finer than borax itself, and the best of it is glassy and breaks up quickly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot and dry at the end of the second degree, and its dryness sometimes reaches the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. It polishes strongly and washes, especially the African kind, and it scours, cleanses and cuts thick humours. Among the borax kinds there is a small astringency with good polishing. The African kind has no astringency and only strong polishing, and salt has astringency with little polishing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: it thins the hair out to the surface of the body, improves the colour and helps wasting, but it can darken the colour when taken in excess {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. Wounds and ulcers: it helps itch by dissolving pus, especially the African kind, with vinegar, and helps scabies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. Joints: a plaster made from it is used for paralysis, especially the late kind, and it helps twisted nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. Head: it helps lichen, and its foam with honey dropped into the ear cleanses, opens, and helps deafness, and with wine or sage water helps ringing in the ears {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. Food organs: it is harmful to the stomach and spoils it; the African kind causes vomiting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. Excretory organs: it loosens the bowels if taken, and with drink, cumin, or a decoction of rue and dill it calms colic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}. It kills worms when brought out by vomiting, and when the belly and navel are rubbed with it and one sits near a fire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}. Poisons: every borax helps diphtheria, especially the African kind, and it is drunk in water against the bites of the insects the book calls dhurarih, and drunk with the anjadan against the harm of bull's blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}. (The book's word for the insects is dhurarih; its identification is not given.)

## Substitutes
No substitute is given in this entry, and the measures of the boraxes are not repeated here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}.
