## Summary
In the rulings, dhikr is the servant's remembrance of his Lord by statement, recitation, supplication or praise. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8427}
It is recommended at all times, except in certain places and conditions, such as relieving oneself, intercourse and the Friday sermon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8430} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8431}

## Heart and tongue
Dhikr is by tongue and heart together, and the fullest form joins them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8434}
By the majority, the tongue's dhikr counts only when the speaker says it and hears himself. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8458}

## The best dhikr
The Qur'an is the best dhikr for one who acts on it, and then come the four words: glory be to God, praise be to God, there is no god but God, and God is greatest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8443} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8444}
A dhikr prescribed for a time or a cause is best kept to that time or cause, and following the prescribed dhikr is better than inventing one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8446}

## Forms
The forms include the tahlil, the tasbih, the tahmid, the takbir, the hawqala, the istirja', the basmala and blessing on the Prophet. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8435} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8436} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8440} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8441}
Dhikr that implies shirk, or deficiency towards God, is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8455}
In general dhikr a correct form not implying deficiency may be used even if not transmitted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8446}

## Counting and beads
Counting by the fingers, by pebbles or by stones is allowed, and some scholars allowed the string of beads as well. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8466} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8467}

## Gathering for dhikr
Some scholars allow gathering for dhikr in unison, if it does not disturb and no one holds it obligatory; al-Shatibi calls a fixed unison of a form an innovation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8460} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8461}
