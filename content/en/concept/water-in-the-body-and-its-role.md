## Summary
In the sixteenth chapter, on the states of waters, Ibn Sina says water is one of the elements and is singled out among them because it alone enters into what is taken in, not because it nourishes but because it carries the food through and mends its consistency {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.134}.

## Why it does not nourish
What nourishes is what is potentially blood and, more remotely, a part of a human limb, and a simple body cannot be changed into the form of blood or of a human limb. Yet water helps to liquefy and thin the food and to escort it into the vessels and the outlets, and the completion of nourishment cannot do without this help {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.134-135}.
