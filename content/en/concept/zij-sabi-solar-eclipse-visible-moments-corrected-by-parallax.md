## Absolute and visible moments
The absolute moments are found first, and then the visible moments by correcting them for parallax. If at the absolute beginning the distance between the Sun and the Moon is less than the minutes of fall, the Moon has covered the Sun before the absolute beginning; a sixth of an hour is taken from the absolute beginning, the parallax in longitude is found on the figure, and the reckoning is repeated until the place is right. If the distance is greater, the Moon has not yet reached the covering position and a sixth of an hour is added. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.166-168}

## The corrected motion and the repetition
The motion is then restated in each pass according to the second and third parallaxes; if the third equals the second the hours obtained are the exact hours, and if the third exceeds or falls short of the second the difference is added or taken away as a fraction of an hour. The same rule as for the lunar eclipse is repeated here. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.167-168}
