Wudu' (from wada'a, to be fair and clean) is the act, and the water used for it, in the lexical sense. The jurists define it: the Hanafis as washing and wiping specified members; the Malikis as a water purification of four members in a specific way; the Shafi'is as specific acts opened by intention; and the Hanbalis as using pure water on the four members in the prescribed way, in order and in succession. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17364}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17365}

Ghusl is washing the whole body in a specific way. Both wudu' and ghusl remove a ritual impurity, the minor and the major. Tahara is the wider term for removing impurity and removing its effect. Tayammum is wiping the face and hands with clean earth or sand in a specific way, and it serves for either impurity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17365}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17366}

Most jurists hold that wudu' belongs to earlier revelations, and that what is peculiar to this community is its specific manner or the mark of wudu', the white marks on the limbs on the Day of Resurrection, citing accounts of the prophets and of Sarah and Jurayj. A Maliki view, the opposite of the sounder Shafi'i view, and some Hanafis hold that wudu' itself is peculiar to this community. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17365}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17366}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17367}

Wudu' was made obligatory in Mecca along with prayer; the preposition 'with' refers to place, not time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17366} Its legal basis is the Qur'an, the sunna and consensus. The verse of sura al-Ma'ida (cited by sura and aya) is taken by some as the proof of obligation, while most read it as addressed to those who are in a state of impurity when they rise to pray. The sunna includes the saying that no prayer is accepted without purity. The people of biography agree that it was made obligatory in Mecca through Gabriel's teaching, and the community agrees on its legality and obligation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17368} Denial of its obligation for prayer is disbelief; for other purposes, such as touching the mushaf, the Hanafis do not count it as disbelief, because the verse is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17368}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17369}

Whoever deliberately leaves wudu' and prays in a state of impurity: the Hanafis say one who makes light of religion is a disbeliever, as in deliberately praying without wudu'; the Malikis say he is killed for abandoning purity like prayer; the Shafi'is say he is asked to repent and killed as a hadd punishment, not as disbelief; the Hanbalis say one who abandons an agreed condition or pillar is treated as one who abandons the act, and one who leaves prayer is called to it for three days and killed if he does not pray. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17369}

The legal ruling varies with the purpose of wudu'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17369}

First, where wudu' is obligatory: for prayer, the jurists agree it is obligatory for one in a minor impurity who wishes to stand for an obligatory or a voluntary prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17369} The Hanafis require it for the funeral prayer, which they count as a prayer; the majority also require it for the prostration of recitation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17369}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17370} For tawaf, the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that it is obligatory for obligatory and voluntary tawaf; the Hanafis count it as necessary (wajib) and penalise: an obligatory tawaf in a minor impurity requires a sacrifice, and in a major impurity a camel. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17370}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17371} For touching the mushaf, the majority hold it obligatory, citing the verse of sura al-Waqi'a. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17370}

Second, where it is sunna: the Hanafis, Hanbalis and Shafi'is, other than al-Baghawi, hold that wudu' before sleep is sunna, on the hadith about lying down on the right side after wudu', reported in the two Sahihs as cited in the book's footnote. The Malikis call it recommended; one Maliki view is that for a junub before sleep it is sunna; another that it is obligatory. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17371}

Third, where it is recommended: a wudu' that is not a condition for the validity of the act but part of its perfection. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17371} It is recommended for reciting the Qur'an and reading hadith, for remembrance of God, for the call to prayer, and for the iqama (on which the jurists differ), for the sermon, for studying religious knowledge, for standing at 'Arafat and for the sa'y between Safa and Marwa, and for visiting the Prophet's mosque and his tomb. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17371}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17372} The renewal of wudu' is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17372} For a junub, the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis (the majority) recommend wudu' before eating, drinking, resuming intercourse and sleeping; the Malikis hold that the junub has no wudu' for eating, drinking or resuming intercourse, but washes his hands and his private parts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17373}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17374} The Hanafis also recommend keeping wudu' constant by renewing it whenever it is broken, and recommend it to leave a legal difference. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17374}

Fourth, where it is permitted: the Malikis hold that the permitted wudu' is for cleanliness and cooling; Qadi Iyad mentions wudu' before entering rulers' presence or boarding a ship in danger, and says that it may be recommended; al-Hattab leans towards recommendation for all except cleanliness and cooling. Such a wudu' does not remove the impurity unless it is intended to. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17374}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17375}

Fifth, forbidden wudu': for the Malikis, renewing wudu' before an act of worship has been performed with it, or wudu' for a purpose for which it was not prescribed or permitted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17375}

Merit of wudu': many hadiths on the removal of sins by it: purity is half of faith; the report from 'Uthman that whoever performs wudu' as the Prophet did has his earlier sins forgiven; the hadith of 'Umar on the eight gates of Paradise; and the hadith of Abu Hurayra on the radiant marks of the community. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17375}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17376} The conditions of wudu' are of three kinds: conditions of obligation, of validity, and of both; the first of the conditions of obligation is intellect, since no address is made to one who lacks it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17375}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17376}

The required washing: the majority (Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis) hold that it is enough for water to flow over a limb, without rubbing; the Malikis and al-Muzani require rubbing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17382} For the Hanafis washing means pouring water so that it drips, even a single drop; Abu Yusuf accepts merely wetting the place. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17382} For the Shafi'is washing means the limb being washed, whether by the worshipper, another person or no act at all, such as rain falling on it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17382}

The face is what is faced; its whole outer surface is washed. Its width runs between the ears, and its length from the hairline at the top of the head to the lower chin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17382}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17383} The Malikis and Hanbalis measure the length for a beardless man to the hairline and for a bearded man to the end of the beard. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17383} Hanging hair beyond the outline of the face: the Hanafis do not require it to be washed; the Shafi'is require the outer and inner surfaces to be washed if it is thin, and only the outer surface if it is thick, with one view requiring nothing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17383}

Hair on the face: the Hanafis do not require washing the roots of thick eyebrow, moustache and beard hair because of hardship, but thin hair must be washed outside and inside to the skin. The Malikis require combing thin hair so that water reaches the skin, and say thick hair needs only outer washing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17384} The Shafi'is wash the eyelashes, eyebrows, moustache, sideburns and the hair of the lower lip outside and inside, and the same for a woman's or intersex person's facial hair; for a man's beard, thin hair is washed both sides and thick hair only outside, and where thin and thick hair are mixed the whole is washed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17384}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17385} The Hanbalis require washing the beard and any hair hanging beyond the outline of the face, and the thick hair of the moustache, eyebrows, lower lip and of a woman or intersex person; combing the beard is recommended, the hadith on the handful of water under the chin being cited in the book's footnote. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17385}

The eye: the lid margin is washed with the face; crust on the lid must be removed. The majority do not wash the inside of the eye with the face, because the Prophet did not do so and because hot or cold water may harm it; a report from Ahmad requires it when there is no harm. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17385}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17386} If the inside of the eye is soiled with impurity, the Malikis, Shafi'is and one Ahmad view require washing it, while the Hanafis and the Hanbali school excuse it in prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17386}

The hairline growth (ghumum), the hair that falls below its usual place, is washed with the face up to the usual limit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17386} The temples (tahdhif), the thin hair between the sideburn and the receding hairline, are disputed: the majority of Shafi'is, a Maliki view and the Hanbali sounder view treat them as part of the head and not washed with the face; the Hanafis, another Maliki view, a Hanbali view and a Shafi'i view, which Ghazali and Rafi'i call the sounder, treat them as part of the face. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17386}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17387}

The white skin between the sideburn and the ear: the Shafi'is, the Hanbalis and the Hanafi view on which fatwas are given hold it is part of the face; a report from Abu Yusuf excludes it; the Malikis wash the skin level with the earlobe. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17387}

The lips: the red part visible when the lips close naturally is washed; one Hanafi view says the lip follows the mouth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17387} Washing part of the head with the face is obligatory in the majority view, and the Shafi'is add part of the throat, the area under the chin and the ears, since what the obligation cannot be completed without is itself obligatory. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17387}

The sideburn is washed with the face. The Shafi'is wash the part of the nose exposed by an amputation; the Malikis wash the septum between the nostrils. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17388} The majority hold that the inside of the mouth and nose are not part of the face and need not be washed in wudu'; the Hanbalis hold that the mouth and nose are part of the face, so rinsing and snuffing water are required in both minor and major purification, citing a hadith in which the book notes al-Daraqutni judged the connection to be a mursal narration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17388}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17389}

The temple (sudgh), the bald part and the receding hairlines: the majority hold these are part of the head, not the face; one Hanbali view holds the temple is part of the face. Al-Shirbini says it is recommended to wash them with the face because of the disagreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17388}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17389} The Malikis wash the folds of the forehead and any hollow of the eyelid if water can reach it by rubbing, and the rest falls away. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17389}

The ears: al-Zuhri included the ears in the face, citing a hadith about the face's creation. The majority reply that the ears are part of the head, citing the saying that the two ears belong to the head and the report that the Prophet wiped his ears with his head, and that the ears were attached to the face only because they neighbour it, as a thing takes the name of what adjoins it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17389}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17390}

If the hair is washed and the skin below it becomes visible afterwards, the Hanafis, the Maliki preferred view and the Hanbalis hold that nothing more is required and wudu' is not repeated, because the duty passed to the hair; the Shafi'is in the sounder view, one Maliki view and Ibn Jarir require washing the newly visible skin, by analogy with a foot showing above a wiped sock. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17390}

The second obligation: washing the hands to the elbows. It is a pillar and an obligation agreed by the Qur'an, by the report of Abu Hurayra describing the Prophet's wudu', and by consensus. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17390}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17391} The majority (the Malikis, Shafi'is, Hanbalis in the school view and the Hanafis except Zufar) hold that the elbows must be washed with the hands, whether 'to' in the verse means 'with' or marks a limit that is of the same kind as what it limits; they cite the hadith of 'Abbad al-Abdi describing the water running over the elbows. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17391} Zufar, a Maliki view and a view of Ahmad hold that the elbow is not included, by analogy with night in the verse on the fast; a further Maliki view includes it as a precaution. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17391}

If part of the limb to be washed is cut off, the rest must be washed by agreement, on the hadith 'do what you can of what I order you' and because the easier is not dropped for the harder. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17391} The rule for an arm cut at the elbow continues beyond p.17391 and is not yet read; this part of the headword is not complete.
