## Its causes, and telling the arterial from the venous
Nosebleed may come as drops or as a violent torrent, from a strong domination of high blood; the bursting may come from the network of the brain's vessels and arteries, which mostly does not accept treatment. Most of it follows the arising of headache, inflammation and illness, and it may be from hot vapours rising. What comes from the arteries is told from what comes from the veins by its thinness, redness and heat; it may return in periodic bouts or return all at once {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.236} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.237}
## Its benefit, harm and signs
The flow of nosebleed is among the states that benefit and harm: whoever finds lightness of head and a colour settling from redness after it has benefited — especially in the sanguine and bilious diseases of the brain, then the liver, then the diaphragm, then the lung; its benefit in pleurisy exceeds its benefit in pneumonia. Nosebleed is a frequent crisis in many acute diseases, above all smallpox and measles. But if it runs to excess and is followed by an unaccustomed yellowness, a leaden hue or a dusky one, with blackness, wasting beyond reckoning and cold extremities, then even if it stops its outcome is feared. Whose colour turns yellow, yellow bile dominates him and his harm from the lost blood is less; whose turns leaden, phlegm dominates; whose turns dusky, black bile dominates — these two harm severely for the blood lost. All who over-bleed are at risk of liver-weakness diseases, dropsy and the like. The body readiest for nosebleed is the bilious thin-blooded one, which profits from the moderate bleed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.237}
## The foretokens
Nosebleed has foretokens: sparkles flashing before the eyes and white, yellow and red lines, especially after a headache, together with all that was detailed where we spoke of the acute diseases and their crises; from the nosebleed and its states the states and crisis-days of the acute diseases may themselves be inferred, as set out in their own place {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.237}
