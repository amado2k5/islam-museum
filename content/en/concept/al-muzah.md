## Definition
Muzah is playful joking in word and deed with no intent to hurt, the opposite of earnest. The jurists distinguish it from hazl, where a word is used without intending its meaning, which has its own record in the museum: [[concept/hazl-fiqh|hazl]].

## In the Qur'an
The word muzah does not occur in the Qur'an; what occurs is the discipline around it: the ban on one people mocking another and on offensive nicknames in [[passage/quran-49-11|al-Hujurat 11]], and the description of the Qur'an as a decisive word and no jest in [[passage/quran-86-14|al-Tariq 14]].

## Prophetic jesting
The Sunnah books in the museum preserve prophetic jesting that never leaves the truth. The Companions said: you joke with us; the answer was that he says only what is true {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 1990}. He called Anas ibn Malik 'you with the two ears', which the narrator explains as teasing him {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 1992}. He asked Jabir ibn Abd Allah, newly married, about playing with a wife {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2967}.

## The limits of the joke
The same Sunnah sets clear limits: a warning to whoever tells a tale and lies to make people laugh {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 4990}, a ban on taking a brother's belongings in jest or in earnest {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 5003}, and the rule that lying fits neither earnest nor jest {cite:sunan-ibn-majah 46}. The jesting these texts accept is truthful, frightens no one and mocks no one.

## In the books of adab
The books of adab give jesting its own chapters, among them the chapter on jest and humour in Ibn Qutayba's 'Uyun al-Akhbar and the anecdotes al-Nuwayri gathers in volume 4 of Nihayat al-Arab. The Juha and Islamic Folklore Browser lists their passages with the texts of the Qur'an and the Sunnah.
