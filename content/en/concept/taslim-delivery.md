## Summary
In the lexicon taslim means delivery, handing over and enabling. For jurists it names the greeting of peace, the closing of prayer, the enabling of possession in contracts, and the handing over of pledges, sureties, wages and dowries. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5084}

## The greeting
Initiating the greeting is a strongly recommended practice, and returning it is obligatory for the one greeted; when a group is greeted, the reply is a communal duty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5085}

## Closing the prayer
The first closing salutation is required for the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, and the Hanbalis add the second. The Hanafis hold that closing with the greeting is obligatory rather than required. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5085} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5087}

## Taking possession in sale
Possession comes by enabling. Real property is taken by enabling the buyer, for instance by handing over the keys; movables are taken as custom has it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5088}

## Payment in salam and pledge
The capital in a salam must be handed over in the session of the contract; the Malikis allow up to three days. In a pledge the debt is paid first, then the pledged item is handed over. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5090} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5092}

## Sureties and agency
A surety for a person must produce the bailed person at the agreed time. An agent earns his fee on handing over what he was appointed to deliver. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5094} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5095}

## Where it is discussed
Each part is treated in its own place: sale, salam, pledge, surety, hire, found property, foundling, dowry and guardianship. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5095} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5096}
