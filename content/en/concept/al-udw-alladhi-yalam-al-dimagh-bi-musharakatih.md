## The stomach and the other organs
The organ that most harms the brain by sharing is the stomach, so one must infer it from the state of appetite, belching, digestion and rumbling, the state of hiccup and nausea, and the state of gastric palpitation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22}

It is also inferred from emptiness and fullness, for the brain's sharing with the stomach when it is full or windy appears when it is full {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22} Its sharing through heat and yellow bile and their pains appears when the stomach is empty, and fullness is often a cause of tempering and a barrier between the hot vapour and the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22}

Most particular of what is used for inference is the place of the pain at its start and where it settles: diseases of the brain by sharing with the stomach may be shown by the pain when it begins at the fontanelle, then pours down to between the shoulders, and grows worse at digestion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22} The head may be ill by sharing with the liver, in which case the pains lean to the right, and by sharing with the spleen the lean is to the left {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22} The brain may often share with the soft lower belly and what lies by the rib cartilages, and the pain then leans very far forward {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22}

Most sharing of the brain with the organs occurs by vapours that rise to it; their way up is either what lies in front of the rib cartilages, where one first feels a stretching upward and tension and throbbing in the vessel beside it and feels the start of the pain in front, or what lies toward the nape, where one feels the start of the pain behind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22}

Ibn Sina warns that the physician may err and take a complaint to be of the brain by sharing with the stomach when it is a hidden complaint of the brain, and refers to the principles he gave in Book I by which primary diseases are told from diseases of sharing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.22-23}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Ibn Sina says the head may share with the womb, that the signs of this are given in its own chapter, and that the pain then stands at the very fontanelle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22}
