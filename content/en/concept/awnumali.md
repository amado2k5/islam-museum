## Nature and selection
A very hot oil, thick like honey and thicker than it, which exudes from the trunk of a sweet tamarisk-type tree. An oil is made from it by mixing in the oil of its flower; it is called awnumali and honey oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}. The best is the clearest, thickest and oldest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}. A footnote explains the name as wine and honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot and moist, with more heat than moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}.

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: it helps scabies that has broken into ulcers, applied as a salve and a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}. Joints: it helps pains of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}. Head: it has a sleep-inducing and dulling effect {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}. Eyes: it suits darkness of the eye when applied as kohl {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}. Excretory organs: taken in a measured amount it purges once, makes the body sluggish and relaxed; the book says it is not to be feared when the back is sound, and one should not sleep on it at all {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.362}.
