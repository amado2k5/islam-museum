## Nature
The book quotes Dioscorides that it is a plant with a leaf like plantain-tongue or wild chard but shorter, thick, black tending slightly to red, with a closed, hollow stem about four fingers long, which peels when it begins to dry, and many thin roots from one elongated head like an onion; it grows in mountain places and is dug at the time of the wheat harvest; the book says that the earlier skilled men described its strength and uses, and that the clearest in description to its author is Philonides the physician; it also names a drug that the people of Antiqun call smirndas of khurbaq because it is mixed with the white, a plant like fotanj with long leaves, a white flower, a thin root of no use and a seed like sesame in taste {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.705-706}

## Choice
The chosen is that of moderately flat surface, white, quick to crumble, large and thin, which does not sting the tongue strongly at once and draws saliva; the one that stings strongly at once is choking {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.706}

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the middle of the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.706}

## Actions and properties
The white is more bitter and the black hotter; the book relates that a mouse that eats it dies; the book gives graded ways of taking it from the weakest to the strongest and a regimen for the one who takes it and what to guard against, convulsion and choking, all left out here, it being enough that the book treats taking it as a matter needing great caution {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.706}

## Cosmetics
It acts in this matter as the black does {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Wounds and ulcers
It acts in this matter as the black does {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Organs of the head
When its powder is smelt it provokes sneezing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Ocular organs
It sharpens sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Organs of nourishment
The white makes one vomit strongly and there is danger in it because it chokes; the one for whom choking is feared must not be given it on an empty stomach, and these are the weak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Poisons
Excess of it kills people, and it is a poison to dogs and pigs; the book relates that what one vomits of it kills chickens; a report attributed to the book {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}
