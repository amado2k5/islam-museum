## Summary
Al-Battani sets two arranged tables for finding the order of the product of any two orders of degrees and fractions without computing, and explains their use: take one order from the first table's row along the width, run straight out from it until it faces the other order in the second table, and the cell where they meet is the order of the product. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.13-14}.

## The table's purpose
He says that whoever wants to multiply one order of degrees or fractions by another should know what order the product becomes, taking it from the one of the two rows of the table where the wanted order is written. It is a two-way table of the kind familiar in astronomical tables, and it does not replace the computing but only shortens finding the order. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.13}.

## Example: fourths by thirds
He gives an example: if you want to multiply fourths by thirds, take either order from the table across the width of the sheet, say the thirds first, and go out from it parallel to the fourths in the table along the length of the sheet; in the cell facing it you find sevenths, the order of the product. He adds that the reverse also holds: starting from the fourths in the other table and going out facing the thirds also gives sevenths. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.14}.
