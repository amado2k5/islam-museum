## Nature and selection
One kind is smooth and one curly. The curly is clearer than that called snake's luf. The smooth has a large earthy part and so its cleansing is less than the curly's, though both cleanse. The author quotes Dioscorides that its leaf resembles that of dracontium but is smaller, its root a span long, and the fruit of the curly smaller, like an olive {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Temperament
The smooth is at the end of the first degree in heat and drying, the curly at the end of the second in warming. Its seed is its strongest part and its root its most useful {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Actions and properties
It opens obstructions, cuts thick sticky humours moderately, and cleanses; the curly is stronger in all of this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Cosmetic uses
The root of the curly cleanses freckles, vitiligo and spots, especially with honey, and is smeared with wine on chilblain cracks {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Swellings and pustules
It helps swellings that need cleansing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Its root, the curly in particular, is mixed with bryony and goes into ointments for malignant ulcers. The moist kind is better for wounds than the dry. Pounded, it is used in place of a wick for ointments of ulcers and fistulas, and plugs for fistulas are made from its root; its leaf is good for bad wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Joints
Luf with cow dung is used on gout and weakness of the muscles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Head, ears and teeth
The juice of the cluster of the garden kind helps earache; put in the nose with rose oil it helps erosion and the cancer in it. Its juice mixed with oil and dropped into the ear quiets pain, and its root is among the remedies that cleanse earwax, dry the ear's ulcers and help deafness. The seed of luf is drunk for piles that form in the nose, even the cancerous ones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Eyes
Its root helps ulcers of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Chest and breathing
It helps expectoration, asthma and orthopnoea when boiled several times until its drug-like quality goes, and then fed to one with old expectoration and asthma. Its root does this, and the curly's more strongly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.540}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
A thick humour is produced from eating it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.541}.

## Excretory organs
The curly cleans the kidney, helps piles, and may bring on urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.541}.

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says the curly stirs desire in wine; that its fruit, taken as a drink, is said to cause miscarriage, and that a pessary made of it may do so; and that smelling the plant as its flower wilts may too {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.541}.

## Poisons and bites
If its root is rubbed on the body the viper will not bite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.541}.
