## Summary
Chapter 6 opens with the equator line, the line of the earth beneath the equator of the day, which has no latitude. Al-Battani describes day and night equal all year, the upright gnomon casting no noon shadow at the two equinoxes, every star rising and setting because the poles of the sphere lie on the horizon circle, and the rotation of the heavens there being wheel-like. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.25}.

## The line with no latitude
He names the line beneath the equator of the day the equator line, and says the circuit of the equator of the day runs over it from east to west, with day and night always equal on it through all the days of the year. It is the line over which, when the Sun passes, day and night are equalised over the whole earth, for the Sun then stands at the common point of the zodiac and the equator of the day, the head of Aries and the head of Libra. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.25}.

## Shadow at the equinoxes and the southern bound of the inhabited quarter
He says that at the equinox the Sun stands over the heads of those who live on this line at noon, so an upright gnomon there has no shadow; when the Sun runs in the northern half of the zodiac the noon shadows of upright gnomons lean south, and when it runs in the southern half they lean north. This line alone, he says, bounds the south of the whole inhabited quarter, and every star rises and sets there because the two poles of the sphere lie in the plane of the horizon circle itself. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.25}.
