## Summary
In the seventh chapter Ibn Sina states that differing waters can bring a traveller more illnesses than differing foods, and he deals with this by clarifying the water often, straining it through porous pottery and boiling it, the most effective of all being distilling it by sublimation, or by a woollen wick joining a full vessel to an empty one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.265}.

## Clarifying, boiling and distilling
Among the ways of dealing with water are clarifying it often, straining it often through porous pottery, and boiling it, the most effective of all being distillation by sublimation; sometimes a wick of wool is twisted with one end in the full vessel and the other in the empty one, so the water drips into the empty one, a good kind of clarification, especially if repeated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.265}.

## Free clay and wool
When bitter and bad water is boiled and free clay and balls of wool are thrown in while it boils, then taken out and pressed, they yield water better than the first; likewise plain water with a clay that has no bad quality added, especially clay baked in the sun, which is then strained off {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.265}.
