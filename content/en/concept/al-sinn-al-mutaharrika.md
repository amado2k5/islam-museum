## The treatments
Chewing on that tooth is avoided, speech lessened, the hand and tongue kept from fussing with it, and food kept to sipping as far as can be. If the cause is corrosion, the corrosion is treated and the firming astringent dental drugs are used — mouthwashes, rubs and the like; if wasting, it is made up with foods, though that is of the hard-to-repair kind, then moistening drugs are used — pastes, rubs and ear-drops of this kind, or the astringents; for the tooth's own wasting the drugs hardly avail and it is treated with the cool astringents, and likewise what came of a blow; what came of a relaxing moisture is treated with the warming astringents — mouthwashes and decoctions and a cooked wine of this kind — and of the pastes and dentifrices is what he described (names and recipes left out; the editor glosses kizmazik as the tamarisk tree's fruit) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.274}
