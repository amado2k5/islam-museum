## Nature and selection
Galen said: it is the water cabbage, called the bride's seed as is said, though there is disagreement; the root of the Indian nilufar is in the ruling of yabruh. Its strongest is the one with a white root, which is stronger than the black-rooted, and its seed is stronger than its grain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Temperament
Cold in the third degree, its syrup strongly quenching; the temperament of the Indian kind is that of yabruh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Actions and properties
Its syrup is very thinning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Cosmetic uses
Its root with water on vitiligo, the black especially; its root with pitch on alopecia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Swellings and pustules
Its root helps hot swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Its seed and root are for ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Sleep-bringing and calming for hot and bilious headache, though weakly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Chest and breathing
Its syrup is good for cough and pleurisy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its root helps swellings of the spleen, drunk and as a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Excretory organs
Its root helps chronic diarrhoea and ulcers of the bowel, and pains of the bladder as a poultice. Its syrup loosens the belly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
It lessens nocturnal emission and breaks the desire for intercourse (the stated measure is not carried over here), and congeals semen by a property in it, its root especially. Its seed is stronger in everything, so that it stops the flow of menses. The root of the yellow kind and its seed, drunk with milk several times, helps the chronic flow of moisture from the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Fevers
Its syrup helps acute fevers, being strongly quenching {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.
