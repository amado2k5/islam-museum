## Summary
Ibn Sina lists considerations in evacuating: that what must be evacuated is evacuated, along with what may follow, such as exhaustion of the vessels or a flare of heat; the direction in which the matter inclines, nausea being cleared by vomiting and gripes by purging; and the organ of exit, such as the right basilic vein for liver ailments, which must be of lower rank than the organ evacuated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.274}.

## Direction and exit
The direction of the matter's inclination is observed, nausea being cleared by vomiting and gripes by purging. The organ of exit is chosen from its inclination, the right basilic vein for liver ailments and not the right cephalic, since an error in this may bring danger. The organ of exit must be of lower rank than the one evacuated so the matter is not turned toward something nobler, and the exit should be a natural one, as the urinary organs are for the convexity of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.274}.
