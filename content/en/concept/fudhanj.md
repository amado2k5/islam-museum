## Nature
Of it there is a river kind and a mountain kind resembling hyssop in size, and its leaf resembles it likewise; one kind is called ghulayjan and another the he-goat's fudhanj; its power is like the power of the others, pungent, and the power of its wine is like that of the wine of hasha; fudhanj is a fine substance, and the mountain kind is stronger than the river kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}

## Actions and properties
It attenuates strongly by its sharpness and bitterness, especially the wild, and it likewise reddens and ulcerates; drunk alone it promotes sweat, warms strongly, draws from the depth of the body, cuts and dries {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}

## Cosmetics
Cooked, especially fresh with wine, and applied as a poultice, it removes the black marks from the body and the darkness that comes under the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}

## Wounds and ulcers
The mountain kind benefits head wounds and hernias, and one bathes in a decoction of the mountain kind for itch and scab {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}; drinking fudhanj benefits leprosy, not only by dissolving but also by cutting and attenuating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Joints
Drinking its decoction benefits bruising of the muscles in their flesh and ends, and it is applied as a poultice for sciatica, burning the skin, changing the temperament of the limb and drawing from the depth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}; eaten, with whey drunk after it on successive days, it benefits elephantiasis and varicose veins; the kind known as ghulayjan, drunk, benefits spasm, and gout is smeared with it and benefits by the reddening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Organs of the head
Its juice kills the worms in the ear, though it causes some headache; the mountain kind benefits ulcers of the mouth and draws the superfluities from the nostrils, and the ash of ghulayjan firms the gums greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Organs of the chest
Its decoction benefits orthopnoea; it is strong in bringing the thick, viscous humours out of the chest, especially eaten with figs, and benefits pain in the ribs, the mountain kind being stronger in this; ghulayjan benefits in all of this, and the he-goat's fudhanj benefits palpitation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It benefits poor appetite and weakness of the stomach, especially the wild, and hiccup; it benefits those with jaundice by cleansing, opening and attenuating the black and yellow bile; it benefits dropsy when eaten with figs, and the mountain kind stirs the appetite for food; ghulayjan calms nausea, and a poultice is made of it on the spleen which shrinks it, as the he-goat's fudhanj does too, which is also of great benefit for the stomach's palpitation, distress and nausea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Excretory organs
Its decoction promotes urine and benefits colic and cholera, and it may make one vomit phlegm; the wild kind loosens the bowels well and kills worms, especially small ones; the wild and mountain kinds loosen black bile; the kind known as ghulayjan brings out the black humour by way of the urine; and the wild fudhanj may do all these actions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that fudhanj, pounded as it is or cooked and drunk with honey, kills the foetuses and brings on the menses; it passes on from some that the garden kind cuts desire, especially the wild, and prevents nocturnal emission, and that the wild kind benefits the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Fevers
Its decoction is drunk for shivering, and likewise rubbing with an oil in which it has been cooked {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}

## Poisons and bites
Drunk or applied as a poultice it benefits the bites of vermin, and as a poultice it comes near cautery in that; taken beforehand with wine it repels deadly poisons; fumigating with its leaf drives vermin away; the wild kind is good for scorpion stings, and the water the mountain kind is boiled in, drunk with cooked wine, benefits the bites of beasts of prey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.631}
