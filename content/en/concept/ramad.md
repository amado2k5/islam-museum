## Actions and properties
All of it is cleansing and drying though it differs; washing lessens its cleansing and gives it a gluey quality, and the drying is without stinging; ash-water enters the putrefying drugs, the strongest being the ash-water of the fig and of yatu, with the cleansing of the other ash-waters, their dryness being less than in these two; the ash of mazariyun is cleansing and putrefying; and the ash of astringent wood such as the oak and others stops blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Swellings and pustules
The ash of the lizard is for scab, and it is smeared on ringworm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}

## Wounds and ulcers
The ash-water of the fig heals hidden ulcers, eats away the excess flesh in ulcers, and benefits deep and large ulcers, because it reaches the spoiled flesh in ulcers, grows flesh and glues {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}

## Joints
Ash-water, especially of the fig ash, is given to drink for a fall from a height and for weakness, and it benefits pain of the nerve and palsy clearly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}

## Organs of the head
Ash-water firms the gums, especially the ash-water of the oak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}

## Ocular organs
The ash of mazariyun sharpens sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
The ash-water of the fig with oil, when drunk, benefits clotted blood in the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}

## Excretory organs
The ash-water of the fig or the oak is used as an enema for ulcer of the intestines, and for chronic flux, haemorrhoids and fistulas {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}

## Poisons and bites
It may be drunk for the bite of the tarantula, and likewise the ash-water of the oak and the fig benefits the drinking of gypsum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.666}
