## Summary
In the eighth chapter Ibn Sina shows how obstructions are recognised when matter has gathered but the signs of fullness do not appear in the whole body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.165}.

## Reasoning to them
If matter is pent up, the signs point to it, and stretching is felt without the signs of fullness of the whole body, then there are obstructions without doubt. Heaviness is felt when the blockage lies in channels through which much matter must pass, as with obstructions of the liver: food reaching the liver and held from passing gathers, is held back and weighs far more than a swelling would, and it is told from a swelling by the great heaviness and the absence of fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.165}.

## In other channels
When the blockage is in other channels, no heaviness is felt but rather a holding back of the blood's passage and stretching; most people with obstructed vessels are yellow in colour because the blood does not move along its channels.
