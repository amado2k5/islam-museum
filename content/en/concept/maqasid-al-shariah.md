## Summary
The maqasid of the Shari'a are the purposes and wisdoms that the usul scholars take the Lawgiver to intend across all or most of the rulings, not confined to a single kind of ruling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14891}

## Kinds
Al-Shatibi holds that the duties of the Shari'a return to the preservation of its purposes among people, and that these fall into three kinds: necessities, needs and refinements. The detail is in the usul appendix. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14891}

## The objectives in al-Shatibi's al-Muwafaqat
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-shatibi|Abu Ishaq al-Shatibi]] makes the third part of [[work/al-muwafaqat|al-Muwafaqat]] a book of objectives, dividing them into those of the Lawgiver and those of the legally responsible person; the Lawgiver's are of four kinds: his purpose in instituting the law, in making it understood, in imposing obligations by it, and in bringing the responsible person under its rule. {cite:muwafaqat-draz-ilmiyya p.219} He prefaces this with the claim that laws were instituted for people's welfare in this world and the next together, noting that [[person/fakhr-al-din-al-razi|al-Razi]] held God's rulings are not caused by any cause, and that the Mu'tazila agreed they are caused by regard for people's welfare; he then says that what counts is that induction from the law shows it was instituted for people's welfare, an induction neither al-Razi nor anyone else disputes. {cite:muwafaqat-draz-ilmiyya p.220} He then holds that the obligations of the law serve to preserve its objectives in creation, which are of three kinds - necessary, needed and refining - each with complements; see [[concept/al-daruriyyat-al-khams|the five necessities]]. {cite:muwafaqat-draz-ilmiyya p.221} {cite:muwafaqat-draz-ilmiyya p.223} In his introduction Abd Allah Draz holds that legal theorists before al-Shatibi neglected this pillar apart from a hint in the chapter on analogy, and that al-Shatibi set it out in sixty-two questions and forty-nine sections. {cite:muwafaqat-draz-ilmiyya p.6} {cite:muwafaqat-draz-ilmiyya p.7}

## A precedent in al-Juwayni's al-Burhan
Before al-Shatibi, [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni]], in the chapter on "the divisions of causes and bases" of [[work/al-burhan-fi-usul-al-fiqh|al-Burhan]], divides the bases of the law into five kinds: those whose meaning comes down to necessity, such as retaliation and sale; those tied to general need, such as hire; those showing an aim of securing a noble trait, such as purification; those explicitly recommended, such as the manumission contract; and those in which no meaning appears, such as purely bodily worship. {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p.551} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p.552} {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p.553}

## In "The Academy for Preparing Leaders 2": Essam Telima's introductory lecture
The first session of [[work/leadership-development-academy-2-program|the second Leaders' Academy]] is an introductory lecture on maqasid given to the young participants by a guest, Dr Essam Telima. An opening framing says the aim is to open the door to the terms and keys behind fiqh, not to make specialists. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:06:00} Telima says he relies chiefly on Jamal Atiyya's book on putting the objectives of the Sharia into effect, among other references. He defines the objectives as "the aims that the texts intend by their commands, prohibitions and permissions", which the particular rulings serve in the lives of individuals, families, groups and the community. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:08:00}

He traces the idea to the Prophet's lifetime, to the order to pray the afternoon prayer only at Banu Qurayza. Some Companions took the order by its purpose and others by its wording, and the Prophet rebuked neither group. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:11:30} See [[event/siege-of-banu-qurayza|the siege of Banu Qurayza]]. The study of maqasid, he says, then began with [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|al-Juwayni]]. It was written up clearly by [[person/al-shatibi|al-Shatibi]] of al-Andalus in [[work/al-muwafaqat|al-Muwafaqat]], in four volumes, and then lay neglected for a long time. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:12:00} In his account [[person/muhammad-abduh|Muhammad Abduh]] came upon the book with a scholar in Tunis and drew his students' attention to it. [[person/rashid-rida|Rashid Rida]] published al-Shatibi's al-I'tisam, and Abdullah Daraz worked on al-Muwafaqat, followed by his son. Today there are research centres and master's and doctoral theses on the subject. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:13:00}

He sets out three approaches to the texts of the Sharia. One keeps to their outward wording. A second sets texts aside in the name of their purposes. The middle approach reads texts in the light of their objectives. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:14:00} He states the rule that there is no censure in matters of ijtihad on which scholars differ. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:23:00} On the claim that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] suspended texts for the public good, he reads Umar's decisions differently: a ruling turns on its cause, present when the cause is present and absent when it is absent. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:47:00} The middle approach, he says, links the texts and rulings together, understands a text in the light of its causes and circumstances, and distinguishes the fixed objective from the changeable means. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:49:00} He ends this first part with the three levels of interests (necessities, needs and refinements) and the necessities, beginning with the preservation of religion and of life. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep01 00:49:30}
