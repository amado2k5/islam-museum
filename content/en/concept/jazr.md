## Nature and selection
Known; its wild seed is the strongest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}. Kinds named: a wild one whose leaf is like fennel, a garden one like Roman parsley, and a third whose leaf is like coriander {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}. The editor's footnote quotes a modern nutrition book; that footnote is not used.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot at the end of the second degree and moist in the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}.

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: the seed and the leaf, pounded and placed on eating ulcers, help them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}.
Chest: it helps pleurisy and chronic cough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}.
Food: difficult digestion; the preserved kind is easier to digest and helps dropsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}.
Excretory organs: it calms colic, especially the wild kind, and strongly drives urine and the menses; the menses is a book listing, labelled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.430}.
Desire: it stirs desire, especially the garden seed, which is more flatulent; the wild seed does not do this; these are labelled book listings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.430}.
