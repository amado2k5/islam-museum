## Summary
Hammam is lexically a bath-house with hot water, and later any bathing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7321} The Hanafis (sound view), Malikis and Shafiis allow building, selling and renting bath-houses without uncovering the awrah; the Hanbalis dislike renting or buying them, and the earnings of a bath-keeper are disliked, the women's bath more so. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7321} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7322} Pre-emption does not apply to an indivisible bath according to the majority, and the Hanafis allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7322} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7323} Men and women may enter a bath if they are sure of avoiding what is forbidden, covering the awrah; some jurists require full cover, a woman may enter for an excuse, and a dhimmi woman is barred from entering with Muslim women. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7323} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7324} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7325} There are rules of entry and exit, the purity of its water is accepted by the Hanafis and Hanbalis, Quranic recitation aloud there is disliked, and greeting in it is not recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7326} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7327} Prayer in a bath-house is valid for the majority; in the preferred Hanbali view it is not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7327} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7328} The schools differ on whether a thief from a bath-house is hand-cut, the Hanafis distinguishing night and day. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7328} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7329} The entry closes at the next headword, hamw (حمو), on p.7329.
