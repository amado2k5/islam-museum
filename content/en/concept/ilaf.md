## Overview
In the exegetes' usage the *ilaf* is Quraysh's covenant and accustomed security: al-Razi, at the verse *li-ilafi Quraysh*, explains that Quraysh, as the people of the Sanctuary, were never raided, so they travelled to Yemen in winter and to al-Sham in summer in safety — that security is the ilaf for which God blessed them {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-quran-tafsir-net}.

## The two journeys
Quraysh ran two journeys each year: the winter caravan to Yemen bringing perfumes, frankincense and Yemeni cloth, and the summer caravan to al-Sham — and on to Abyssinia — bringing oil, grain and fabrics; the two journeys made Mecca the Arabs' season market {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-quran-tafsir-net}. Ibn Hisham reports, from other than al-Waqidi, that Hashim b. Abd Manaf first instituted the two journeys: he would lead the merchants to al-Sham, Iraq and Egypt and make pacts with the neighbouring rulers for the safety of his people's caravans {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}.

## The ilaf as a blessing
The whole of Surah Quraysh is a call to remember this security: worship of the Lord of the House who fed them against hunger and secured them against fear — so the commentators treat the two journeys as a divine blessing before an economic arrangement {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-quran-tafsir-net}.
