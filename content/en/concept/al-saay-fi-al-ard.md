## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat links striving in the land with productive labor, arguing that dispersing through the land after prayer, as stated in the Qur'an [[passage/quran-62-10|al-Jumu'ah 10]], is a command to work and seek sustenance, not to engage in trade and importation alone {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 65}.

## Time and Worship
The author considers time spent in prayer, worship, and gratitude not to be wasted, but rather a period of calm, rest, and contemplation of the dominion of the heavens and the earth, from which the movement of work then begins {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 39}.

## Types of Labor
The author divides labor into manual, such as work on the land, in construction, and in mines; intellectual, such as the work of engineers, teachers, and doctors; and mechanical, which enters as an aid to both {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 52}. He observes that the modern human being uses the plow, the tractor, and medical devices, so labor requires continuous training on every new tool {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 52}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 54}.

## Specialization and Training
The author regards specialization in labor as a law of modern life, noting that it has raised productive efficiency and human skill {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 53}. At the same time, he criticizes the resulting nurseries that separate a mother from caring for her child, noting that the Qur'an set nursing at two full years {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 53}.

## Its Place in the Book
The author includes striving in the land within natural activity, which forms the foundation for industrialization. See [[concept/anasir-al-intaj|Factors of Production in the Qur'an]] and [[concept/al-nashat-al-tabii|Natural and Unnatural Activity]].

## From the book of Zaydan Qa'dat: pp. 83–164 of the PDF file (Striving in the Land in the Context of the Book)
Qa'dat states that the earth belongs to God, so every human being must be given the opportunity to work on it, and it is not right that land be given to some and withheld from others {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 100}. He argues that ownership is granted for use and labor, and that seizing vast tracts of land without cultivating or utilizing them is acceptable to neither religious law nor worldly law {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 150}.
