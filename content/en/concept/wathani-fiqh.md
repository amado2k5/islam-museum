## Summary
A wathan is an idol with a body of wood, stone or other material; the wathani is the worshipper of idols and one who follows that religion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16604}

## Distinct notions
Idolatry is one kind of unbelief; the idolater differs from the associator, the unbeliever, the apostate, the Zoroastrian and the atheist. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16604} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16605} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16606}

## Belief and the call
Scholars agree that the idolater is an unbeliever. Muslims must call him to Islam, and war is not declared on him before the call. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16606} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16607}
Idolaters acknowledge the Creator but deny God's oneness; one who dies in idolatry is held to abide in the Fire. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16607} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16608}

## Rules in war and peace
In war, fighting men may be killed after the call; women, children and the insane may not, by agreement, and most jurists spare the old. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16608}
The women and children of a conquered people may be enslaved. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16609}
Most jurists accept the jizya from a non-Arab idolater but not from an Arab. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16608}

## Conversion and marriage
On conversion, the two testimonies suffice in the four schools. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16610}
Marriages among idolaters are valid for most jurists, and a Muslim may not marry an idolatress. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16612}

## Slaughter and game
The slaughter of an idolater is not lawful, nor his game, except fish and locusts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16613} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16614}
