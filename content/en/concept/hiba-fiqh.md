## Summary
Hiba in the lexical sense is giving a thing to another without return; technically it is transferring property without return at once. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16772}

## Related terms and lawfulness
Hiba differs from the wider atiya, from hadiya in that most jurists require acceptance in a gift, and from sadaqa, which seeks reward in the hereafter. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16772} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16773}
Gifts are lawful by the Qur'an, the sunna and consensus; they are recommended, forbidden when meant for sin or bribery, and disliked when meant for show. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16773} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16774}

## The donor and the donee
The donor must be competent to give; a minor or insane person cannot. The schools differ on the debt-bound and feeble-minded, on a dying man's gift beyond a third, and on a father's gift of his son's property for a return. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16774} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16775}
The gift of an unauthorized donor is void for most jurists and suspended on approval for the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16776}

## The thing given
The thing given must exist, be owned by the donor and have value; most jurists disallow gifts of the non-existent or unknown, while the Malikis allow what can be moved. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16778} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16779} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16780}
The schools differ on giving a debt to one other than the debtor, and on an undivided share. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16780} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16781}

## Possession and formula
The schools differ on whether possession completes a gift: the Hanafis, Shafi'is and some Hanbalis require it, while the Malikis hold the gift binding by the contract. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16782} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16783}
Acceptance may be by act; delayed acceptance is allowed by the Malikis and Hanbalis; a conditional gift is refused by the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16786} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16789}

## A gift for a return
The schools differ on whether a gift may be made for a return and on its nature: a sale, according to the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis; a gift begun and a sale completed, according to the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16792} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16795}

## Retraction
Most jurists forbid retraction except for a father's gift to his son; the Hanafis allow it with disapproval, and list the impediments. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16800} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16801} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16802} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16803}
For the Hanafis a retraction is a dissolution when made by judgment or by consent, and the thing returns to the donor. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16807} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16807}
