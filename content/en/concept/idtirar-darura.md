## Summary
This record gathers what the museum holds on necessity in the matter of the lawful and the forbidden, that is, what is said about a person whom severe need drives toward something forbidden to him. The verses of prohibition list the forbidden foods and then except the person in necessity; the Sunnah relates an episode of men who ate from a sea creature when compelled; and the books of jurisprudence and the reports discuss who counts as compelled, treatment with the forbidden, and begging.

## In the Qur'an
[[passage/quran-2-173|al-Baqara 173]] says that God forbade carrion, blood, the flesh of swine and what was slaughtered for another than God, and then makes clear that whoever is driven by necessity, without wanting it and without exceeding its measure, bears no sin, and that God forgives and shows mercy {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-16-115|al-Nahl 115]] repeats the same meaning in close wording {cite:tanzil-quran}, as does [[passage/quran-6-145|al-An'am 145]], which makes the forbidden for an eater carrion, poured-out blood, swine flesh and what was slaughtered for other than God, and says the compelled person who neither desires it nor oversteps finds his Lord forgiving him and showing mercy {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-5-3|al-Ma'ida 3]] gives a longer list, adding the strangled, the beaten to death, the fallen from a height, the gored and the prey of wild beasts, except what was reached alive and slaughtered, and then speaks of one pushed by hunger toward the forbidden with no leaning to sin, and says that God forgives and shows mercy {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-6-119|al-An'am 119]] says that God has explained to them what He forbade them, with the exception of what necessity forced them to {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In the Sunnah
[[passage/abudawud-api-3840|Sunan Abi Dawud 3840]] relates that a party reached the seashore and saw what looked like a huge dune, which proved to be a creature called al-'anbar {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3840}. Abu 'Ubayda first said it was carrion and not lawful for them, then said no, we are envoys of the Messenger of God and in the path of God, and you are compelled, so eat {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3840}. They stayed on it a month, three hundred men, until they grew fat; when they came to the Messenger of God they told him of it and he said that it was a provision, and the recorded excerpt breaks off at that point {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3840}. [[passage/abudawud-api-3874|Sunan Abi Dawud 3874]] relates from Abu al-Darda that the Prophet said God sent down the disease and the remedy and made a remedy for every disease, so seek treatment, but do not treat with what is forbidden {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3874}. In [[passage/muwatta-api-787|Muwatta Malik 787]] the permission concerning carrion is described as given for the state of necessity, after which Malik says that game killed or slaughtered by a person in ihram is not lawful to eat for anyone, in ihram or not, because it is not properly slaughtered, and that one who kills game and then eats it owes only one expiation {cite:muwatta-malik 787}.

## In the reports of the Companions, the Successors and the scholars
In his [[passage/bidayat-mujtahid-halal-1-16|Bidayat al-Mujtahid]] Ibn Rushd divides his treatment of forbidden foods into two parts: the first on what is forbidden in the state of choice, the second on their rulings in the state of necessity {cite:bidaya-bakri-bayt-al-afkar p.16}, and page [[passage/bidayat-mujtahid-halal-4-515|515]] repeats the same division {cite:bidaya-bakri-bayt-al-afkar p.515}. In the [[passage/musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-halal-2-4819|Musannaf of Ibn Abi Shayba]] a report from Abu Ja'far says that whoever is compelled to what has been forbidden to him, it is lawful for him {cite:al-musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba p.4819}, and pages [[passage/musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-halal-2-5270|5270]] and [[passage/musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-halal-2-5445|5445]] repeat it {cite:al-musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba p.5270} {cite:al-musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba p.5445}. The latter page also records from Ibrahim, on a person compelled to eat carrion, that he eats what sustains him {cite:al-musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba p.5445}.

In [[passage/fiqh-sunna-halal-3-21|Fiqh al-Sunna]] Sayyid Sabiq quotes Ibn Hazm that a person driven to some forbidden thing who finds no property of a Muslim or a protected non-Muslim may eat until sated and take provisions until he finds something lawful, and once he finds it that forbidden thing becomes forbidden again; the same passage mentions the duty to give one's food to the person in necessity and retribution if the other kills him {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah p.21}. In Ibn Qudama's [[passage/mughni-halal-2-935|al-Mughni]], as far as the scanned text can be read, a person in ihram who is compelled and finds both game and carrion eats the carrion {cite:mughni-ibn-qudama p.935}. In al-Salihi's [[passage/subul-huda-halal-4-3628|Subul al-Huda wa-l-Rashad]] what a person is compelled to is not forbidden to him, like carrion for the compelled, and the passage goes on to cite al-Hafiz that breaking the fast in Ramadan is forbidden and yet permitted for a lawful reason such as travel {cite:subul-huda-salihi p.3628}.

On treatment, the [[passage/mawsua-fiqhiyya-halal-2-123|Kuwaiti Encyclopaedia of Fiqh]] says that Ibn Mas'ud's saying that God did not place your cure in what He forbade you may have been said of an illness whose remedy was known to be something not forbidden, since then the sick person can do without the forbidden, and that when it is known that there is no other remedy it is permitted {cite:fiqh-mawsua-noor-doc p.123}. [[passage/mawsua-fiqhiyya-halal-2-236|Page 236]] mentions a remedy containing snake flesh and says the jurists it cites did not permit eating or drinking it, since snake flesh is forbidden and treatment with the forbidden is not allowed, on the strength of the report {cite:fiqh-mawsua-noor-doc p.236}. On begging, al-Ghazali says in the [[passage/ihya-halal-2-101|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]] that asking is forbidden in principle and is permitted only by necessity or an important need close to necessity {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p.101}, and [[passage/ihya-halal-2-249|elsewhere]] he refers back to his rule that begging by the poor is forbidden except in necessity {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p.249}.
