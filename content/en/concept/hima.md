## Overview
A *hima* is land that the ruler reserves, so that none may graze it except those for whom it has been reserved. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/234}

## In the Sira of Ibn Hisham
When the people of [[place/jurash|Jurash]] embraced Islam, the Prophet reserved for them a *hima* around their village, marked by known signs, for the horse, the riding camel, and the plough-ox (*muthira*). Whoever grazed his livestock there would have gained unlawfully. See [[event/surad-campaign-against-jurash|the campaign of Surad against Jurash]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/234} In the Prophet's letter to the delegation of [[event/delegation-of-hamdan|Hamdan]] it is stated that they have the open pasture of their land and its lowlands and highlands, that is, its heights and its depressions; they may eat its fodder and graze its pastures as long as they maintain prayer and pay the alms. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/245}

## What is not recorded here
The rulings on the *hima* in jurisprudence, the *hima* of al-Naqi', and the saying of the Prophet "there is no *hima* except for God and His Messenger," would be added from the books of hadith and jurisprudence.
