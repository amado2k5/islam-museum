## Summary
According to the author, wages are the return on labor and may be a fixed amount or a share of profit, while a stipend is a payment paid by the state from the public treasury {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 244} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 248}.

## Types of Wages
Payment for property is called rent, and payment for labor is called a wage; labor wages are based on time—daily, weekly, or monthly—or on the unit produced, which is a task-based reward contract {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 244}. A private employee works for one employer, whereas a shared contractor works for more than one, such as an engineer, a lawyer, or a tailor {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 244}. In early forms of production, the worker receives the product of their labor, such as a hunter, a woodgatherer, or a diver {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 244}.

## Task-Based Reward Contracts
A task-based reward contract (ja'alah) is an exchange contract for human labor that is indivisible from the perspective of the offering party, such as saying, "Whoever returns my lost item shall have such-and-such," and the compensation may be unspecified, such as spoils taken from a slain enemy {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 245}. It is valid whether addressed to a specific person or to the public at large {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 246}, including saying, "Sell it, and whatever exceeds such-and-such dirhams is yours," which was validated by Ahmad, Ishaq, Ibn Sirin, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Qudamah, and Ibn al-Qayyim {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 246}.

## Determining Wages and Sufficiency
Wages are determined by mutual consent and must be clearly specified {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 246}. The author holds that it is not required for a wage to cover all the needs of the worker and their dependents, because ensuring sufficiency falls under redistribution through zakat and other mechanisms {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 246}. Islam warned against defrauding a worker, delaying their wages, and burdening them beyond their capacity, as well as against taking bribes, idleness, and begging {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 246} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 247}.

## Appointment and Drawing Lots
The work must be lawful and the worker qualified for it, and rulers must appoint those who are qualified, or else they betray their trust {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 246}. When candidates are equal, lots are drawn among them {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 246} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 247}.

## State Stipends
A stipend is a payment paid by the state from the public treasury, grounded in God's words concerning the guardian of an orphan (see [[passage/quran-4-6|al-Nisa' 6]]), which Umar ibn al-Khattab cited when stating that he takes from it in moderation if he is in need {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 248}. The author holds that a volunteer whose competence is equal or close should be given priority {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 248}. He notes that Abu Bakr gave up trade to attend to the affairs of the Muslims, so he spent from the Muslims' funds what sufficed for himself and his dependents, and al-Tabari reports that six thousand dirhams per year was allotted to him {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 259}.

## Minimum Wage
The ruler may set a minimum wage to protect the weak worker from exploitation by the employer; it applies only to full-time workers, while part-time workers are entitled to it in proportion to their hours {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 257}. He notes that Ibn al-Qayyim held that price-fixing for labor did not occur in Medina during the Prophet's lifetime {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 257}, and that jurists permit wage-fixing when workers collude to drive up wages {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 256} (see [[concept/ajr-al-mithl|prevailing wage]]).

## Hardship and Wages
The general rule is that wages follow benefit and usefulness rather than mere fatigue and hardship, because hardship in itself is not a benefit, as al-'Izz ibn 'Abd al-Salam stated {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 249} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 250}.

## Note
The author left the issue of sufficiency to the third category of distribution, noting that the hadith on providing sufficiency for someone in charge of Muslim affairs is weak, that its meaning is appealing to full-time officials, and that the matter requires a general theory of wages, reward contracts, and stipends {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 258} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 259}.
