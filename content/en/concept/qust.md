## Nature
Dioscorides is reported as saying that costus is of three kinds: the Arabian, white, light, fragrant, leaning to yellow; the Indian, black and light like the cucumber; and a third that comes from the land of Syria, of a diffusing smell; among the lesser kinds is one whose smell is the smell of aloes and which tends to black, and the Syrian resembles a nail. Good costus is adulterated with the hard roots of rasan and is known by its form, since rasan does not sting the tongue and its smell is neither strong nor diffusing. There is also a bitter-tasting kind thought to be Indian. The editor adds in a footnote that it is the Indian kust {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Choice
The best is the Arabian, white, new, full, neither eaten nor greasy, which stings and bites the tongue; then the black light Indian, then the black Syrian; the best of all is the sea kind with a thin bark {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Temperament
Hot in the third degree and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Actions and properties
It has a very bitter, acrid quality and a heat so strong that it ulcerates, and it is beneficial for every organ that needs to be warmed and to have the humour drawn out of its depth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Cosmetics
It clears freckles from the skin, smeared with water and honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Wounds and ulcers
It ulcerates, and the bitter kind of it dries moist ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Joints
It is beneficial for slackness of the muscles and the nerve and tearing of the muscles, and good for sciatica as a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Organs of the head
It benefits lethargy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits pains of the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Excretory organs
It induces urine, brings out tapeworm and worms, and moves the bowel when drunk with wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it induces the menses, drunk and as a fumigation through a funnel, and kills the foetus, and strengthens desire by a surplus windy moisture in it, and that it is carried for pain of the womb and benefits cold pain of the womb, drunk and sat in its decoction {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Fevers
It benefits rigors, smeared with oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Poisons and bites
It benefits bites of every kind, the bite of the viper and others, when given with wine and wormwood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}

## Substitutes
Its substitute is pellitory, aqirqarha {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.648}
