## Definition
In language, shahada means a certain report, presence and seeing, an oath, an admission, and death in God's cause. In the legal sense it is a statement of one person's right against another, made before the judge, in the word "I testify" (ashhadu). It is also called bayyina (evidence), since it clears what is confused and uncovers the right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10186} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10187} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10188}
It differs from an admission (iqrar), which reports a right owed by the speaker, and from a claim (da'wa), which seeks a right from another. All three are reports, and are told apart by whether the speaker gains by it and whether the ruling binds only him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10188}

## The duty
Bearing and giving testimony is a communal duty (fard kifaya), on the basis of the verses of the surah of the Cow (282) and (283) and the surah of Divorce (2) that forbid refusing or hiding testimony. When enough witnesses have given it the sin is lifted from the rest; if all refuse, all are sinful. A refuser sins only if giving it harms him not and his testimony would help; if it harms him or would not help, he is not bound, citing the verse that forbids harming the scribe or the witness. It becomes an individual duty where no other witness is present and the right is at risk. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10189}

## Its basis
Testimony is established by the Qur'an, the sunna, consensus and reason: the verse of the Cow (282) on two men of your own, and the verse of Divorce (2) on two just men among you; the report of Wa'il ibn Hujr, "your two witnesses or his oath"; the report of Ibn 'Abbas, "the proof is on the claimant and the oath on the defendant". Consensus holds it for proving claims, since people deny one another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10190}

## Pillars, cause and force
The majority count five pillars: the witness, the party who benefits, the party against whom, the matter testified to, and the wording; the Hanafis take the wording alone, "I testify". The cause of giving is the claimant's request, or fear of losing the claimant's right if the witness is not known as a witness. Testimony is a proof that shows the right but does not create it; yet it obliges the judge to rule by it, since the judge is ordered to rule by the right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10190}

## Conditions of bearing
- Sanity at the time of bearing, since bearing is understanding and retaining the event. A madman or a child who does not understand cannot bear it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10191}
- Knowledge by seeing or by hearing the matter itself, not by another's word, following the report of Ibn 'Abbas: testify only to what shines for you like this sun. Other than in matters that may be known by hearsay, such as marriage, descent and death, the witness must have seen. A witness may not testify to what he finds in his own handwriting unless he remembers it and is sure, since writing can resemble writing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10191} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10192}
- Sight: the Hanafis do not allow a blind person to bear testimony at all; Zufar and the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow it in matters of hearsay, if he is sure of the voice. The Hanbalis allow a blind witness who knows the speaker's voice from long acquaintance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10191} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10193}
- Age, freedom, Islam and uprightness are not required at the time of bearing: a child, a slave, an unbeliever or a sinner who bore it, and later came of age, was freed, became Muslim or repented, has his testimony accepted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10192}

## Conditions of giving
These concern the witness, the testimony, the matter testified to, and the number of witnesses.
- Age: a child's testimony is not accepted, since God says "two men of your own" and a child is not counted a man; and one who cannot be trusted with his own property cannot be trusted with others'. Some Malikis and Hanbalis allow children to testify among themselves about wounds and killing before they separate, the Malikis adding that no adult should have joined them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10192} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10193}
- Sanity: the testimony of one who is not sane is not accepted by consensus, whether the cause is madness or drunkenness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10193}
- Freedom: the majority do not accept the testimony of a slave, since testimony is a form of authority and a slave is busy with his master's service; the Hanbalis accept a slave's testimony in all matters except penalties and retaliation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10193}
- Sight: the Hanafis do not accept a blind man's testimony at all; the Shafi'is do not accept it on acts, but do on words that are known by hearsay and in interpreting before the judge; the Malikis accept it on words, not acts, for a sharp person whose hearing is not confused and who is sure of the parties; the Hanbalis accept it if the voice is certain. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10193} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10194}
- Islam: the basis is that the witness is a Muslim, since the verses speak of "men of your own" and "just men among you"; the Malikis, Shafi'is and the well-known Hanbali view follow this. They make one exception: a non-Muslim's testimony on a bequest during a journey, by the verse of the surah of the Table Spread (106). The Hanafis accept the testimony of dhimmis against one another, even of different religions, and of belligerents against their like; an apostate's is never accepted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10194} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10195}
- Speech: the majority do not accept a mute's testimony; Malik accepts it if his sign is understood; the Hanbalis accept it if he gives it in writing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10195}
- Uprightness: all agree it is required, citing the verse "two just men among you"; a sinner's testimony is refused. The Malikis define uprightness as religious restraint from major sins, avoidance of minor ones, keeping trusts and good dealing, with more good than bad in the person. The Hanbalis define it as religious rectitude in observing the duties and avoiding major sins and persisting in minor ones; the Shafi'is make manliness (muru'a) a separate condition. Uprightness is a condition for the judge's duty to accept. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10195} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10196}
- Care: the testimony of one who is heedless and rarely retains anything is refused; one who errs seldom is accepted, since no one is free of error. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10196}
