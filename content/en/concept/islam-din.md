## Summary
"Islam" is the name of the religion brought by [[person/muhammad|the Prophet Muhammad]]. In Gabriel's hadith, Gabriel asked him "What is Islam?" and he answered: "That you worship God and associate nothing with Him, establish the prayer, give the imposed zakat, and fast Ramadan" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}.

## How the hadith defines it
Abu Hurayra relates that Gabriel came to the Prophet in a man's form and asked him about faith, Islam, ihsan and the Hour's time; the chapter heading reads: "Gabriel came to teach you your religion, and he made all of that religion" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}. Al-Bukhari made the chapter heading "Gabriel's questioning the Prophet about faith, Islam and ihsan" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}.

In another hadith the Prophet describes Islam as a building standing on five supports, saying: "Islam is built on five" {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.192}. The detail of these supports is in [[concept/five-pillars|the five pillars of Islam]].

## The hadith of the Najdi man
A man of the people of Najd came asking the Prophet about Islam; the Prophet told him of five prayers in the day and night, the Ramadan fast, and the zakat. After each the man asked "Is anything else upon me?" and the Prophet said: "No — unless you volunteer" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 46}. The commentator records that this man was said to be Dimam ibn Tha'laba {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 46}.

## The ranks of religion in the hadith
Gabriel's hadith contains three questions: about [[concept/iman|faith]], about Islam, and about [[concept/ihsan|ihsan]]. Al-Bukhari counted all of that as religion, as the chapter heading shows {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}.

## What is not covered here
The relation between Islam and faith is a subject treated in the creed books. A presentation of their authors' sayings, attributed to them, will be added in a later batch; this entry presents only what is documented from al-Bukhari's text.

## Sha'rawi on submitting the face to God
Sha'rawi holds that the religion with God from Adam to the Hour is the submission of the face to God, since the face is the noblest part of a person, and placing it on the ground in prostration is a sign of submission. He says that Islam is surrendering one's leadership to God, so that one's choices in this world match what God wants. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.593} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.594}
