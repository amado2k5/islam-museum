## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents Pareto's law — the 80/20 rule — as a law that began as a theory confirmed by studies, named after the Italian thinker Pareto {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:03:30}.

## Its applications
He states that twenty per cent of decisions produce eighty per cent of effectiveness, twenty per cent of time eighty per cent of output, twenty per cent of customers eighty per cent of sales — indeed four per cent of customers yield sixty-four per cent of purchases — twenty per cent of employees eighty per cent of the work, and likewise twenty per cent of employees cause eighty per cent of the problems (needs_source: the figures as stated in the lesson) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:10:00}.

## Jack Welch's rule
He recounts that Jack Welch of General Electric applied a yearly 20/70/10 division of the staff: the productive twenty per cent are honoured, promoted and never allowed to consider leaving; the ordinary seventy are trained with limited raises; and the weakest ten per cent are dismissed every year — some thirty-five to forty thousand employees annually (needs_source) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:09:30}.
His practical conclusion is that the leader concentrates his time and mind on the effective twenty per cent and hands the eighty per cent to management {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:11:30}.
