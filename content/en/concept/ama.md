## Summary
In the lexicon, ama is the loss of sight {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11803}.

## Legal capacity and obligations
Blindness does not remove legal capacity; obligations fall away only where an excuse or harm requires it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11803} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11804}.

## Friday prayer, the call to prayer and leading the prayer
Most jurists hold the Friday prayer obligatory on a blind man who can guide himself or has a guide; Abu Hanifa does not {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11803}. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis dislike a blind man calling to prayer unless a sighted person tells him the time; the Malikis allow it. The Hanafis and Hanbalis dislike his leading the prayer unless he is the most learned {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11804}.

## Testimony, contracts and lian
The testimony of a blind man is not accepted for what is seen, but is accepted for what is known by other senses in some views {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11804}. His sale by description is valid according to most jurists, and he may validly swear lian for adultery he is sure of {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11804} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11805}.

## Where to look further
Friday prayer is treated under «salat al-jumu'a», testimony under «shahada», and lian under «li'an» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11805}.
