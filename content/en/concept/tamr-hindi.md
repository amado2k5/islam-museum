## Nature
Well known; it is brought from India {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## Choice
The best and finest is the new, fresh one that has not withered or shrivelled, and whose sourness is true {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## Temperament
Cold and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## Actions and properties
A purgative subtler than the ijjas and less moist {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It benefits vomiting and thirst in fevers, and tightens the stomach slackened by much vomiting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## Excretory organs
It loosens yellow bile; the book states the amount of a draught of its decoction, left out here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## Fevers
It benefits fevers with fainting and distress, especially when softening of the bowels is needed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}
