## Summary
The sabab, in the terms of jurists and usulis, is an apparent and regular attribute to which the Lawgiver attached a ruling; its presence entails the ruling and its absence entails none. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1472} Unity of the cause means that several causes are alike, belong to one class, or are one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1473}

## Sabab and illa
Scholars disagree whether sabab and illa are synonyms, distinct, or related as general and particular. On the first view the same definition serves both and neither needs a connection to the ruling; on the second, the sabab conveys a ruling without influence while the illa conveys it with influence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1473}

## Overlap of effects
Overlap (tadakhul) is one effect following from two different causes. Unity of causes and overlap cross each other: repeated theft, which calls for amputation, is a case of several similar causes whose effects overlap. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1473}

## The unrestricted and the restricted text
When an unrestricted and a restricted text give different rulings, the unrestricted is not read as restricted. The Shafi'is and those who agree with them read the unrestricted text in the light of the restricted one when cause and ruling are the same, to join the two texts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1473} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1474}

## One cause, two rulings
The preferred view allows one cause to produce two rulings, either affirmed or denied, provided the rulings do not contradict each other. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1474}

## Where it is discussed
The jurists treat it under purity, fasting, ihram, confession, the hudud, oaths and offences against persons; the usulis treat it under the unrestricted and restricted texts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1474}
