## Summary
In chapter 10 al-Battani shows how to find the shadow from the altitude by calculation: the chord of the altitude's complement is multiplied by the gnomon's parts and divided by the chord of the altitude. He says the calculators, whom the zij follows, made the gnomon twelve parts, shadow parts being reckoned against the gnomon's. Conversely, the altitude comes from the horizontal shadow through the root of the sum of the shadow's square and the gnomon's square, then by dividing the radius times the gnomon by the result. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.35-36}.

## The shadow from the altitude
He says: to find the shadow from the altitude, find the chord of the altitude and the chord of what remains to ninety, then assume the gnomon's parts as you wish, multiply the chord of the altitude's complement by the gnomon's parts and divide by the chord of the altitude; the result is the spread and extent of the shadow along the plane of the ground, in the measure by which the gnomon's parts are those parts. He says the calculators worked, and this book works, on a gnomon of twelve parts, though it may be divided into fewer or more as the calculator wishes, since the shadow's parts are only reckoned against the gnomon's. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.35}.

## The altitude from the horizontal shadow
He says: if you want the altitude from the horizontal shadow, multiply the shadow by itself and add the gnomon's parts multiplied by themselves, which is one hundred and forty-four when they are twelve; take the root of the sum, which is the diameter of the shadow's triangle, and keep it. Then multiply the gnomon's parts by the radius, which on this base is always seven hundred and twenty, divide by the diameter of the shadow's triangle, and the arc of the result is the altitude. Or multiply the shadow by the radius and divide by the diameter of the triangle; the arc is the Sun's distance from the zenith on the circle of altitude, and taken from ninety it leaves the altitude. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.36}.
