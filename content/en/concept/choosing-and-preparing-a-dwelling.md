## Summary
Ibn Sina closes the chapter on dwellings with advice for whoever chooses and prepares a dwelling, then turns to the causes counted after air and dwellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.

## What the chooser should know
One who chooses dwellings should know the soil of the land and its state of height or lowness, exposure or shelter, its water and the substance and state of that water, whether it is open to the winds or sunken in the earth, its winds and whether they are sound and cold, and what lies near it of seas, marshes, mountains and mines {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.

## The people of the place
He should learn the people's state of health and disease, which diseases are habitual among them, their strength and digestion and the kind of their foods, and the state of its water, whether wide and open or of narrow entrances and stifled vents {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.

## Preparing it
Then he should make the windows and doors face east and north, relying on letting the east winds into the buildings and the sun reach every part, for it is what sets the air right. Nearness to sweet, noble, running, abundant, clean water that cools in winter and warms in summer, unlike still water, is good and useful {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.
