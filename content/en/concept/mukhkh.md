## Nature and selection
The most suitable is the marrow of the calf and the deer, then the ox, then the goat, then the sheep. The marrows of he-goats and bulls, especially the stud animals, are drier, and the marrow of the limbs is fattier {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.

## Actions and properties
Warming, softening, cleansing and very nourishing if digested {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.

## Swellings and pustules
Good for hardnesses and petrification, such as the marrow of the calf and the deer, not like the marrow of he-goats and ibexes, which are dry and of no good {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It coats the stomach and takes away appetite, and should be eaten with spices and condiments {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
From the good marrows a pessary is borne in the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.

## Poisons and bites
It is said that smearing with deer marrow drives away vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.
