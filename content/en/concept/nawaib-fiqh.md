## Summary
Nawa'ib are the plural of nawaiba, a calamity or an extraordinary matter that befalls people. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16657}

## Rightful and unjust levies
A levy may be rightful, such as one the imam imposes to equip the army or ransom captives when the treasury has no money; or unjust, such as an exaction imposed without right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16657}

## The ruling on imposing and paying
Imposing a levy is obligatory when there is public benefit and the treasury cannot meet it, and a rightful levy must be paid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16658} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16660}
A permitted form is a fee for escort or guard for pilgrims, if customary and not crushing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16660}
An unjust levy need not be paid; Ibn Abidin held that paying it helps the oppressor. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16660} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16661}

## Guarantee, distribution and reimbursement
Guaranteeing a rightful levy is permitted by the Hanafis; they differ on guaranteeing an unjust one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16662}
The Hanafis allow one who paid another's share with his leave to reclaim it from him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16664}
