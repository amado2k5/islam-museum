## Summary
The other, larger artery growing from the heart Ibn Sina calls, by Aristotle's name for it, the aorta; at its start it sends two branches round the heart and then divides into a greater descending and a smaller ascending part, and its outlet carries three hard membranes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}.

## Its branching
First growing from the heart it sends two branches: the larger circles the heart and spreads in its parts, the smaller circles and spreads in the right cavity. What remains divides in two: a greater part fit for descending and a smaller fit for ascending. The descending was made larger because it goes to organs more numerous and greater in size, those set below the heart {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}.

## The membranes
At the outlet of the aorta stand three hard membranes. Were there one or two, the intended benefit would not be reached unless they were enlarged, and the movement would grow heavy with them; were there four they would be very small and useless, and if enlarged they would narrow the way. The venous artery has two membranes facing inward, since there the need is for smoothness rather than for a tight seal, so that smoky vapour and the blood going to the lung are pushed out easily {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}.
