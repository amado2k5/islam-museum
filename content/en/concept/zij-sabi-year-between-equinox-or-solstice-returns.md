## Summary
Al-Battani states that the year is the time from the Sun's leaving an immovable point of the sphere until it returns to it, either from one of the two equinox points to the same point, or from one of the two solstice points to the same, since no beginnings on the zodiac are better than these points {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.65}.

## Why these points
The text justifies the choice by saying that these points are the natural beginnings of the zodiac: they divide the seasons, and the return to them is the cycle of the seasons itself. This is the measure he sets against [[concept/zij-sabi-year-ancients-fixed-star-return-rejected|the year counted by the return to a fixed star]], and from it the observations of al-Battani, Ptolemy and Hipparchus (the book's Arkhus; the identification is read from the name) begin {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.65}.
