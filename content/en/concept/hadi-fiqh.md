## Summary
In the technical sense, hadi is a Maliki term for a whitish fluid that comes from a pregnant woman near childbirth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16764} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16765}

## Distinct notions
Hadi differs from madhi, wadi, semen, menses and postpartum bleeding in coming from the womb; menses requires bathing, hadi does not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16765} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16766}

## Whether it breaks ablution
The Malikis have two views: the accepted one says it breaks ablution; the other says it is nothing and needs no ablution, since it is not habitual. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16766} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16767}

## Impurity
The Malikis hold it impure; the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis agree on the impurity of what comes from a pregnant woman before the birth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16767} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16768}
