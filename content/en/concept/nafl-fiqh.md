## Summary
Nafl, in the lexicon, is an addition. The Hanafis define it as what is neither obligatory nor required; the Shafi'is as everything beyond the obligations, also called sunna, recommended or voluntary. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15929} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15930}

## Rulings on nafl
The obligation is better than the voluntary act by agreement; voluntary works may complete what is lacking in obligations; the jurists list cases where a recommended act outranks an obligatory one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15931} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15934}
Starting a voluntary pilgrimage or lesser pilgrimage makes completing it binding. For voluntary prayer and fasting the Hanafis and Malikis require completion, while the Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow breaking it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15934} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15936}
Voluntary acts while owing an obligation: the Hanafis dislike voluntary prayer while owing missed prayers; the Malikis forbid it, except the sunnas; the Hanbalis dislike it before making up the obligatory prayer, except the dawn sunna. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15936} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15937}
Specific and unrestricted prayers: specific prayers are tied to a cause (eclipse, rain prayer, greeting the mosque) or a time (the two festivals, tarawih, witr, duha); unrestricted prayers have neither. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15938}
Number of rak'ahs: twos are preferred by the Shafi'is, Hanbalis and Malikis; the Hanafis prefer fours by day and allow more with conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15939} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15940} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15941}
Length of standing or number of prostrations: the Hanafis, most Shafi'is and Malikis prefer longer standing; the Hanbalis and some Shafi'is and Malikis prefer more prostrations. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15941}
Separating obligatory and voluntary prayer: the Shafi'is and Hanbalis recommend a break by speech, standing or moving; the Hanafis recommend joining the sunna to the obligatory prayer with only a short pause. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15942} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15943}
Almsgiving, fasting and pilgrimage: voluntary almsgiving is recommended at all times, and most jurists forbid giving away what is needed for obligatory maintenance or debts; voluntary fasting is recommended on the excellent days; voluntary pilgrimage is among the best works, and its precedence over jihad or almsgiving is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15944} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15945} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15946}
