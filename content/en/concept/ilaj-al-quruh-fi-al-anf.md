## The rule of drying
The nose is a member moister than the ear and drier than the eye, so the treatment of its ulcers must lie between the treatments of the ear's and the eye's ulcers: the drying drugs for the nose's ulcers need to be less drying than the ear's and more drying than the eye's — for the ear's ulcers need something at the utmost of drying and the eye's the opposite. Then, if the cause is matters flowing or vapours rising, they are treated by evacuating them and drawing them toward another quarter as is known; in sum, the first need is to dry the head and strengthen it by what you have learnt, then the two nostrils are bled ⁦(as the book describes)⁩. Know that all the drugs useful for the piles and the arbiyan which we shall mention are useful for the ulcers too if strong, and if cooked with saliva-like agents till soft they suit all the light ulcers as well {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.246}
## The dry, the flowing and the stinking
The dry ulcers are treated with ointments made of the known kind ⁦(the fat, oil and wax names are not carried)⁩. The ulcers to which a sharp, bad or stinking matter flows are hard to treat: evacuation and venesection are indispensable, and sometimes the great electuary laxatives are needed; their washing must be kept up with the natron-and-soap kind, especially the soap ascribed to Asclepiades and the soap ascribed to Quintus ⁦(carried as the book's attributions)⁩, then the strongly drying drugs are used — among them a compound of the known kind soaked days in ox bile till it ferments, then used ⁦(an animal-derived ingredient carried as the book's description; the drug names are not)⁩ — and Rufus's tried drug, whose recipe is of the known kind ⁦(attributed as in the book; the measures are not carried)⁩. The very painful ulcers are treated with a thing of the known kind, the pustular with a thing of the known kind, the apparent ulcers with an ointment of the known kind. Among the shared drugs: sour pomegranate water cooked in a copper vessel till reduced by half, a tent smeared with it and used; and tablets of the known kind, sometimes dissolved in wine, sometimes in vinegar, sometimes in vinegar-and-water, as you see fit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.246} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.247}
