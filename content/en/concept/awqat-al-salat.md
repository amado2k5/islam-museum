## Definition
Waqt is a fixed portion of time for a purpose. The times of prayer are the periods the law set for performing a prayer on time; the time is the cause of the obligation, so a prayer before its time is not valid, and one performed after it is a makeup. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3666}

## The prescribed times
The obligatory prayers are five, by agreement. The Gabriel report sets out their times. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3666}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3667}
- Dawn: from the true dawn to sunrise; the Hanafis end it just before sunrise, and the Malikis count the lightening of the sky. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3667}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3668}
- Noon: from midday to the point at which a thing's shadow equals its height, and for the Hanafis to twice its height besides the midday shadow. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3668}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3669}
- Afternoon: from the shadow exceeding the height, and for the Hanafis from twice the height, until sunset for the Hanafis, and until the yellowing of the sun for the Hanbalis and in one Maliki report. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3669}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3670}
- Sunset: from sunset until the twilight fades, the red twilight for the majority and the two disciples, the white for Abu Hanifa. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3670}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3671}
- Night: from the end of twilight until true dawn by agreement, and to a third of the night as the preferred limit for the Hanbalis and Malikis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3671}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3672}
- The witr: for Abu Hanifa from the end of twilight; for the majority, after the night prayer until dawn. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3674}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3675}

## Wide and narrow time
For the Hanafis a prayer's time is wide until only enough remains for the opening takbir, and then narrow; the obligation to perform it is tied for them to the end of the time, and for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis to its beginning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3672}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3673}

## Recommended and disliked times
- Dawn is recommended at first light for men by the majority, and women pray at home. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3673}
- Noon prayer is cooled in summer and hastened in winter, except on cloudy days. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3673}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3674}
- Afternoon prayer is hastened by the majority, and delayed by the Hanafis while the sun stays bright. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3674}
- Sunset prayer is hastened by agreement; night prayer is recommended before the third of the night. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3674}
- Disliked times are three for the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis: at sunrise, at midday, and at yellowing; the Malikis count two. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3676}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3677}
- Disliked for other reasons: voluntary prayer after dawn and after the afternoon prayer, before sunset, while the preacher is preaching, at the call to start the prayer, and when the time for the obligatory prayer is nearly over. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3678}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3679}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3680}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3681}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3682}

## Combining two prayers
- The majority allow it on a journey or in rain, following the Prophet's practice on journeys. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3683}
- The Hanafis allow it only at Arafat and Muzdalifa, and Abu Hanifa adds conditions of pilgrimage, congregation and the imam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3684}

## Where a time cannot be found
The Hanafis disagree about people in the polar regions: some estimate times for each prayer from the nearest inhabited lands, and others drop the prayers they cannot time; the Malikis and Shafi'is estimate the twilight from the nearest land. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3684}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3685}
