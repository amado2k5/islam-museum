## Summary
Ibn Sina says food changes the state of the body by its quality and by its quantity. By quantity it does so either by excess, which brings indigestion and obstructions and then putrefaction, or by deficiency, which brings wasting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.134}.

## Cooling and putrefaction
An excess of food always cools, unless putrefaction arises from it, in which case it heats, since putrefaction, just as it arises from a foreign heat, also gives rise to a foreign heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.134}.
