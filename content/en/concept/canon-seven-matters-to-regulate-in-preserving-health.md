## Summary
Ibn Sina makes the keystone of the art of preserving health the regulation of the general necessary causes, with most care given to seven matters, and then begins with the management of a newborn of the most balanced temperament {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.202}.

## The seven
The keystone of the art of preserving health is regulating the general necessary causes, and most care goes to seven matters: regulating the temperament, choosing what is taken in, clearing the superfluities, keeping the composition, correcting what is breathed, correcting what is worn, and regulating bodily and psychic movements, with sleep and waking entering in some way {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.202}.

## No single point of balance
He recalls from earlier that balance has no single limit, nor does health, and not every temperament counts as some health and some balance at some time; the matter lies between the two extremes. So he begins with the management of a newborn of the most balanced temperament {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.202}.
