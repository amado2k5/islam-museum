## Summary
Ibn Sina goes on through the places of cupping, naming the leg, the occiput, the crown, the place under the chin and the loins, what each helps and harms, and the difference made by scarifying, by not scarifying and by fire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.300-301}.

## The leg
Cupping on the leg is close to venesection: it cleanses the blood and brings on the menses. For a woman who is white, loose-fleshed and thin of blood, cupping the legs suits her better than opening the saphenous vein {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.

## The occiput and the crown
Cupping on the occiput and the crown helps what some claim for it, confusion of mind and dizziness, and they said it slows graying; Ibn Sina says this happens in some bodies and not in others. It helps diseases of the eye, which is its chief benefit, since it helps eye mange and pustules, but it harms the mind and brings dullness, forgetfulness, poor thought and chronic illnesses, and harms those with water in the eye, unless it meets the time and the state in which it ought to be used {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.

## Under the chin
Cupping under the chin helps the teeth, the face and the gullet, and cleanses the head and the jaws {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.

## The loins
Cupping on the loins helps boils of the thigh, its mange and pustules, gout, piles, elephantiasis, winds of the bladder and the womb, and itching of the back. When done with fire, with scarification or without, it helps in these too: the one with scarification is stronger except against wind, and the one without scarification is stronger at dissolving and rooting out cold wind, here and in every place {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.300-301}.
