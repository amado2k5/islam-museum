## Summary
In the usage of legal theory, the implied meaning (mafhum) is what a wording indicates without being in the place where it is spoken: a ruling for something not mentioned, or a state of it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14843}

## Agreement (mafhum al-muwafaqa)
This is an implication that agrees with the spoken ruling and is accepted by consensus. It is called a stronger implication (fahwa al-khitab) when it applies more strongly, and an equal implication (lahn al-khitab) when it applies equally. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14843} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14844}

## Contrariety (mafhum al-mukhalafa)
This gives the opposite ruling to what is unspoken, and is also called the indication of the speech. Most jurists accept it except the implication from a name alone, which some accept. Most Hanafis reject it in the speech of the Lawgiver only. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14844}
