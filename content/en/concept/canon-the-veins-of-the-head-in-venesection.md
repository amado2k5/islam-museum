## Summary
Ibn Sina names the veins of the head that are opened, the vein of the forehead, the two veins of the temples, the two of the corners of the eyes and the small veins behind the ear, with their uses, says that the vessels about the head, except the jugular, are opened obliquely, and cites Galen's denial of a claim about two veins behind the ears {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## How they are opened
As for the opened vessels in the regions of the head, it is best, except for the jugular, to open them obliquely. Among these vessels some are veins and some arteries {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## The forehead vein and the crown
Of the veins there is the vein of the forehead, which stands upright between the eyebrows, and opening it helps against heaviness of the head, heaviness of the eyes and persistent chronic headache. The vessel on the crown of the head is opened for migraine and sores of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## The temples and the eye corners
The two veins of the temples, which twist over the temples, and the two veins of the eye corners mostly show only on throttling. The cut must not be sunk deep in them, for it may become a fistula; only a little blood runs from them. Their benefit in opening is in headache, migraine, chronic ophthalmia, tearing, filminess of sight, scab of the eyelids and their pustules, and night-blindness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## Behind the ear
There are three small veins whose place is behind where the ear's edge meets its hair when pressed to it. One of the three is plainer. Opening them also helps in sores of the ear and the nape and disease of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## Galen's denial
Galen denies what is said, that two veins behind the ears are opened by the celibates to end procreation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.
