## Summary
In the eighth chapter Ibn Sina describes the pulse of the hot, cold, moist and dry temperaments {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.177}.

## Hot and cold
The hot temperament has greater need; if power and instrument help, the pulse is great, and if one of them opposes, it is as set out earlier. If the heat is natural and not an ill temperament, the constitution is strong and sound and the power very strong, and one should not think that growth of the innate heat reduces the power; it brings power in the spiritual substance and spirit in the soul, whereas heat that follows an ill temperament makes the power weaker as it grows. In the cold temperament the pulse inclines to the sides of decrease, above all smallness, slowness and wide spacing; if the instrument is soft its breadth, slowness and spacing are greater, and if hard, less. The weakness brought by a cold ill temperament is greater than that of a hot one, since the hot is closer in agreement with the innate heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.177}.

## Moist and dry
The moist temperament is followed by the wave quality and breadth, the dry by narrowness and hardness. Then, if the power is strong and the need severe, the double-beat, spasmodic and trembling pulses arise; the reader is to combine them while keeping the principles in mind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.177}.
