## Definition
Ahl al-harb are non-Muslims who have not entered the covenant of protection and enjoy neither the safe-conduct nor the treaty of the Muslims. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3600}

## Distinctions
- The protected people (ahl al-dhimma): unbelievers who live in the land of Islam by paying the poll tax. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3600}
- The rebels (ahl al-baghy): a group that rises against the lawful imam by force. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3601}
- The people of treaty (ahl al-'ahd): those with whom the ruler has agreed to end war for a fixed period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3601}
- Those under safe-conduct (musta'manun): those who enter the land of Islam under safe-conduct, or a Muslim who enters the land of war under it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3601}

## Change of status
- A protected person, a treaty-holder or a holder of safe-conduct becomes a combatant by joining the land of war and staying there, or by breaking the covenant. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3601}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3602}
- A combatant becomes protected by consent, by a year's residence in the land of Islam, by marriage, or by conquest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3602}

## Blood and property
- Without a covenant, the combatant's blood and property are lawful; his fighters may be killed and his property becomes spoil. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3604}
- With a covenant, his blood and property are protected, and betraying one who has given safe-conduct is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3605}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3606}
- Destroying their trees and property in war is allowed by agreement where it serves the Muslims. The Hanafis, Malikis and Ahmad in one report allow it without such purpose, while the Hanbalis in one report, al-Awza'i, al-Layth and Abu Thawr forbid it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3606}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3607}

## Bequests, endowments and alms
- A bequest to a combatant is forbidden by the Hanafis and Malikis, and allowed by the Shafi'is in the sounder view and the Hanbalis, for a named individual. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3607}
- An endowment in their favour is void by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3608}
- Gifts and alms to them are sound by the consensus of the four schools. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3608}

## Inheritance, trade, marriage and maintenance
- The difference of the two lands does not prevent unbelievers from inheriting from one another, by the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3608}
- Trade with combatants under safe-conduct is allowed; arms may not be supplied to them in war, and the export of food is allowed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3608}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3609}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3610}
- A Muslim man may marry a Jewish or Christian woman of the land of war, as he may marry one of the protected people, on the Qur'anic permission in sura al-Ma'ida (5:5). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3610}
- The wife of a combatant of the People of the Book is owed maintenance as a Muslim wife is. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3610}
