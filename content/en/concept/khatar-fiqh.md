## Summary
Khatar is lexically the brink of ruin and fear of loss, a matter hanging between happening and not happening; wagering is mukhatara. Exposing oneself to mortal danger is forbidden by agreement, since preserving life is among the highest aims of the law; the verse on not casting oneself into ruin is cited. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7729} Dispensations for danger: carrion may be eaten by one forced by hunger, wine may be used to swallow a choking morsel when life is at risk, and a diseased organ must be cut if leaving it risks life. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7729}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7730} Hardship that reaches the danger to life or limb brings relief: dry ablution is required when water threatens life or a limb, the Hajj lapses when the journey is risky, sea travel is forbidden if death is likely, and fasting lapses for the sick, the nursing and the pregnant if their life or the infant is endangered. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7730} Jihad is an exception, and it is allowed to risk life in it, since hardship is its nature; flight from a larger enemy is forbidden; and repelling an aggressor is also excepted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7730}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7731} Removing a gland or a diseased organ: forbidden when cutting endangers life more than leaving it, and allowed when there is no danger or leaving it is worse; a stranger who cuts it without leave owes retaliation if the patient dies. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7731}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7732} Contracts of risk are those that hang between being and not being and between gain and loss, like wagers and gambling; the race is lawful on conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7732}
