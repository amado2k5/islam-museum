## Nature
There is a Kabuli and a Basri kind, and it may not grow in Basra, since it is brought from India to Basra and to other lands {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Choice
The best is what is taken from the cane (qasab), the more shining and the oilier, and the best of its cane is also the shining and smooth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Temperament
Moderate in hot and cold, and moist {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Actions and properties
Dissolving and softening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Swellings and pustules
It benefits hot swellings in the organs, especially in the throat when gargled with fox-grape water, and is applied to hard swellings and benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Joints
It is applied on gout and painful joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Organs of the chest
Steeped in the water of fresh coriander with the mucilage of the seed of qatuna and then gargled, it benefits quinsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Organs of nourishment
It cleanses the liver and benefits jaundice and liver pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Excretory organs
It loosens the belly and brings out burnt humour and phlegm, and its purging is purging without harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that it is fit for pregnant women and loosens them; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}

## Substitutes
The book names as its substitutes taranjabin, the flesh of the raisin and turbud, and some claim that the rob of liquorice is put in place of the raisin; the proportions and weights are left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.707}
