## Summary
In chapter seven Ibn Sina sets out what the amount of urine shows: scanty shows weak strength, abundant shows melting or purging of waste, told apart by the state of strength, and he describes erratic and dripping urine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.196-197}.

## Scanty and abundant
A scanty amount shows weakness of strength; less than what was drunk shows much dissolution, a loose bowel and a readiness for dropsy. A large amount may show melting or the purging of dissolved waste, told apart by the state of strength. A badly coloured urine is safer the more of it there is, and worse when it comes in drips. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.196}.

## Erratic, dripping, and scanty in the healthy
Urine that is sometimes copious, sometimes scanty and sometimes held back is a sign of a wearing struggle of the innate power, and bad. Copious urine in acute disease that brings no ease shows consumption or spasm from inflammation, and urine dripping drop by drop shows an affliction of the brain. If a healthy man's urine becomes scanty and thin and stays so with heaviness and pain in the loins, a hard swelling about the kidney is shown; if it grows copious in colic, it may give good news, especially if white and easy to pass. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.196-197}.
