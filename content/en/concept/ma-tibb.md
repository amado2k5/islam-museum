## Nature and selection
The excellent waters were treated in Book One. The bad waters are the stagnant marsh waters, those dominated by a strange taste and smell, the turbid, thick and heavy, those that quickly turn to stone, and those on which bad scum floats {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.557}. The harm of borax waters is made good by milk, thick wine and starch; thick turbid waters are corrected by thinning foods like garlic, onion and leek, and drinking wine on them removes their harm. Bitter water is corrected by sweet things, salty water by carob, myrtle seed, hawthorn, pure clay and barley flour, and bad water in general by vinegar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.557}.

## Temperament
Sea water is pungent and sharp; borax water is warming and drying; copper and iron waters help the viscera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.557}.

## Actions and properties
Cold water harms those with obstructions but helps those with laxity and flows, of whatever kind and from whatever organ; in moderation it strengthens all the faculties in their work, I mean the digestive, attractive, retentive and expulsive {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.557}.

## Cosmetic uses
Sea water helps cracks from cold before they ulcerate, kills lice and dissolves blood clotted under the skin; sulphur waters are good for vitiligo and leukoderma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Swellings and pustules
Sulphur waters help swellings of the joints, hardnesses and hanging warts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Plain water is bad for ulcers because it moistens, against what the treatment of ulcers requires. Sea water helps itching, scab and ringworm, and sulphur waters too are good for scab and ringworm by bathing in them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Joints
Sea water and the like helps nerve diseases, especially when bathed in, such as tremor, palsy and numbness, and helps all cold pains of the joints and nerves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Epileptics benefit from tepid water and are harmed by hot; the steam of sea water helps cold headache, and copper water helps the mouth and the ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Eyes
Desert water is bad for the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Chest and breathing
Very cold water is bad for the chest; tepid water is good for swellings of the throat, uvula and chest. Sea water is poured over swellings of the breast, borax water sometimes helps the lung, and alum water helps spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Iron water helps the spleen and stomach, and copper water is close to it; very cold water harms those with obstructions. The steam of sea water helps dropsy; alum water helps and prevents vomiting, as do astringent hot springs, and sulphur waters help swellings and pains of the spleen and liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Excretory organs
Sea water is used as an enema for cramps, and drunk it loosens, after which chicken broth is drunk to calm its sting. Salty water loosens, then holds by its drying. Iron and copper waters are good for the kidneys and colic, and turbid waters produce stone in the kidney and bladder. Water in which iron has been quenched helps spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.559}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists alum water as preventing miscarriage and flooding of the menses, sulphur waters for pains of the womb, very cold water as bad for sexual capacity and quieting the movements of semen, and all mineral water as making urine, menses and childbirth difficult {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.558}.

## Fevers
Sulphur, muddy and stagnant dead waters cause fevers, and thick waters cause the quartan {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.559}.

## Poisons and bites
One stung by a viper who sits in sea water benefits, and likewise from other deadly vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.559}.
