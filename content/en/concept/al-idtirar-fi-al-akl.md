## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that permission for the one compelled by necessity is a concession from God, limited to what the need requires. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.719}

## The measure of necessity
Sha'rawi says the compelled person takes only what keeps him alive, does not fill his belly with the forbidden, and does not hold that the forbidden has become lawful. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.719}

## The owner's right
He gives the example of a man who owns a cup of water that suffices only for himself: a stronger person in need may not take it, since necessity may not push harm onto another, and the owner who came first has the prior claim. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.720}

## The closing clause of the verse
Sha'rawi reads the closing clause of the verse as showing that the matter still carries sin, permitted for necessity, so that the forbidden is not made lawful for good; when necessity ends the original ruling returns. He reads the close of the verse with forgiveness and mercy as fitting one who committed no sin. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.720}
