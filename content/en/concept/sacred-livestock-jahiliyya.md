## Overview
The bahira, the sa'iba, the wasila and the hami: livestock the Jahiliyya people venerated and forbade themselves the use of, as an innovation — which the Qur'an disavowed.

## The story
[[person/amr-ibn-luhayy|Amr ibn Luhayy]] instituted for the Jahiliyya people the sacralising of kinds of livestock: the bahira, a she-camel whose ear is slit and which is freed if she yields after five pregnancies; the sa'iba, freed for a vow; the wasila, a ewe joined in dedication if she bears male and female; and the hami, a camel sire protected once a fixed count issues from his loins {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

They would assign the idols shares of crops and livestock which they brought to nothing else, and would claim "this is forbidden" over whatever beasts and crops they wished {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## In the Qur'an
He Almighty said: "God made no bahira, no sa'iba, no wasila and no hami; but the unbelievers fabricate lies against God" ([[passage/quran-5-103|al-Ma'ida 103]]); and He disavowed the dividing of crops and livestock between God and the idols in [[passage/quran-6-136|Surat al-An'am]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
They used to set aside camels and sheep for the idols and never use them; the Qur'an told that God never ordained that.
