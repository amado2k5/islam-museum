## Summary
What was found on Arabic in Bahrain: Article 2 of the Constitution as the Constitute Project shows it, and the names of the Bahraini Gulf and Baharna varieties in Glottolog's tree, with a statement that no description of the speakers, no shares and no other languages were found. The museum has a place record for the state: [[place/kingdom-of-bahrain|Bahrain]]. For the Arab states in general see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|The Arab states and the League of Arab States]]. The constitutional text is added in its original wording, as supplied in a research summary that the museum did not check against the page.

## Official status
Article 2 of Bahrain's Constitution, in the translation on the Constitute Project titled "2002 (rev. 2017)", says the official language is Arabic, and the same article gives Islam as the religion of the State {cite:constituteproject-bahrain}. The article on the Semitic languages in the Encyclopaedia Britannica lists Bahrain among the states where Arabic is official {cite:britannica-semitic-languages}. The article on the Arab League says Bahrain joined it in 1971 {cite:britannica-arab-league}. The Library of Congress study says the name Bahrain comes from the Arabic word for "two seas" {cite:loc-bahrain-geography}, which concerns the origin of a name, not the language today.

## Constitutional text
The Constitution of the Kingdom of Bahrain, Article 2. The research summary supplied to the museum gives this Arabic wording: «دين الدولة الإسلام، والشريعة الإسلامية مصدر رئيسي للتشريع، ولغتها الرسمية هي اللغة العربية.», which the museum translates: "The religion of the State is Islam, the Islamic Shari'a is a main source of legislation, and its official language is the Arabic language". The summary names the Legislation and Legal Opinion Commission of Bahrain as the official source; the museum did not open that page, so the wording is taken from the summary as supplied, and the founder approved using it so on 21 November 2026 {cite:official-constitution-bahrain}.

## The spoken varieties
Glottolog's tree lists, under Gulf Arabic, "Bahraini Gulf Arabic", and elsewhere in the Peninsula tree "Baharna Arabic" as a variety with its own ISO code {cite:glottolog-arabic}. The tree does not say who speaks either in Bahrain or elsewhere, and no source saying so was read. So no description of the dialects of Bahrain and no shares for them were found.

The same Manchester page divides the Arabic dialects into geographical units, one of them the dialects of the Arabian Peninsula; it does not name the states inside that unit, so placing this state in it is an inference from its location, and it says that in most Arab countries urban and rural dialects differ, as do those of settled and formerly nomadic (Bedouin) people {cite:manchester-arabic-structural-variation}. Glottolog's tree shows separately named varieties under "Arabian Peninsula Arabic" {cite:glottolog-arabic}; these are two different classifications, one geographical group at Manchester and distinct varieties at Glottolog, and the museum does not choose between them. The Ethnologue page was not read, so the SIL classification is not known here. For more on the dialects see [[concept/lahjat-al-khalij|The Gulf dialects]].

## Other languages
No other language of Bahrain and no language shares were found; the pages read say nothing of any language but Arabic there.

## The standard and the spoken varieties together
The University of Manchester's page on the dialects of Arabic describes the general situation across the Arabic-speaking world as three-layered: Modern Standard Arabic, the language of the state, education, public services and the media; Classical Arabic, found in the Qur'an, religious texts and worship; and the colloquial dialects of everyday exchange, which the page says people usually regard as of little value even though they are common in song, theatre and film {cite:manchester-arabic-sociolinguistic}. This is a description of the Arab world in general, not of this state alone, and no study measuring it here was read. For the relation of the standard to the spoken varieties see [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Standard Arabic and the spoken dialects today]], and for the differences among the tribes' speech before the standard [[concept/lughat-al-qabail|The dialects of the tribes and the tongue of Quraysh]].
