## Summary
Ibn Sina lists when an emetic turns into a purgative, with a strong stomach, a drink taken in great hunger or a heavy fast-descending drug, and when a purgative turns into an emetic, with a weak stomach, dry waste or a repugnant drug, and warns that a purgative that fails to purge stirs the humour and multiplies it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.277-278}.

## The turning
An emetic can turn into a purgative when the stomach is strong, when it was drunk in great hunger, when the drinker has a loose flux or soft bowels or is not used to vomiting, or when the drug is heavy and quick to descend. A purgative turns into an emetic through weakness of the stomach, great dryness of the waste, a repugnant drug or a drinker who suffers indigestion. Any purgative that does not purge, or purges unripened matter, stirs up the humour it would purge and spreads it through the body, so that it takes over and other humours change into it and that humour increases {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.277-278}.

## Season and build for vomiting
Ibn Sina quotes Hippocrates as saying that the lean person's nature yields readily to vomiting, so the better way to cleanse him is to use vomiting in summer, spring or autumn and not in winter. For someone of moderate build, purging is better; if something does call for vomiting him, he waits for summer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.277}.
