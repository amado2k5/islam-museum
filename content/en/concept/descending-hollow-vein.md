## Summary
In the fifth chapter Ibn Sina turns to the descending part of the hollow vein and names its branches to the kidneys, the two renal veins, the testicles and the spine, then its division at the end of the vertebrae into two parts toward the thighs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.92}.

## To the kidneys and testicles
He says the first branches of the descending part, as it rises from the liver and before it rests on the spine, are hair-like branches to the folds of the right kidney that feed it and the bodies near it; then a great vessel goes to the left kidney with hair-like branches from it; then two great vessels called the risers go to the kidneys to purify the watery part of the blood, since the kidney draws its food from them, which is the watery part of the blood. A vessel may branch from the left riser to the left testicle, whether in males or females; the one to the left testicle always takes a branch from the left riser and in some people both come from it, while the one to the right rarely takes a branch from the right riser and mostly does not mix with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.92}. In it is the channel in which sperm ripens, turning white after redness through the many turns and rounding of its vessels, and most of this vessel disappears into the penis and the neck of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.92}.

## Along the spine
The hollow vein then rests on the spine and descends, with branches at each vertebra entering it and spreading in the muscles set there, so that vessels come to the flanks and end in the abdominal muscles, and others enter the holes of the vertebrae to the spinal cord. At the end of the vertebrae it divides in two, each going aside to right or left toward a thigh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.92}.
