## Summary
Gross disparity (ghabn) in a sale means that the price is much higher or lower than the true value; the author quotes scholars that ghabn in a bargained sale does not void the contract, whereas ghabn in a trust-based sale (istirsal) does {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 293} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 294}.

## Bargained Sale
Ghabn is of no legal consequence in a bargained sale; the author quotes al-Hattab in Mawahib al-Jalil that the well-known position of the Maliki school is that there is no claim for ghabn, and that this is also the view of Abu Hanifah and al-Shafi'i {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 293} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 294}. He notes al-Hattab's remark that mutual hard bargaining among people remains valid even when frequent {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 294}.

## Trust-Based Sale
The author notes that the prohibition of ghabn applies to a sale of trust or reliance (istirsal), where the buyer trusts the seller and relies on them; if the seller overcharges them, doing so is injustice and riba, citing the hadith "Overcharging the trusting buyer is riba" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 293} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 294}. He attributes it to Sunan al-Bayhaqi without stating its grading {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 294}.

## Modern Law
The author cites al-Sanhuri's statement that most Western legal systems do not recognize ghabn except in rare cases {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 294} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 295}. He advises buyers to prepare with information before purchasing, because the seller is not obligated to rescind the contract if buyers suffer a bad bargain without deception {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 295}.
