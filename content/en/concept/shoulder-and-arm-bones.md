## Summary
Ibn Sina passes from the chest to the upper limbs and describes the collarbone, the shoulder blade, the upper arm, the forearm and the elbow joint {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.53-55}.

## Collarbone and shoulder blade
The collarbone lies on each side of the top of the sternum; at the throat its curve leaves a gap through which pass the vessels rising to the brain and the nerve coming down from it, and then it turns outward and joins the head of the shoulder blade, so that the shoulder blade is tied to it, and the upper arm to both {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.53}. The shoulder blade was made for two uses: to hang the upper arm and hand from it, so that the arm is not stuck to the chest and its directions of movement are wide, and to be a secure shield for the organs enclosed in the chest, in place of the spines and wings of the vertebrae. It has two processes: the acromion, to which the ligament joining the shoulder blade to the collarbone is tied and which prevents the upper arm dislocating upward, and another, inward and downward, that also prevents the head of the arm bone dislocating; on its back is a triangular process called the spine of the shoulder blade {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.53-54}.

## The upper arm
The arm bone was made round to be further from harm. Its upper end is convex and enters the hollow of the shoulder blade in a loose joint, not very firm, so dislocation often happens to it. The looseness has two uses: the need for ease of movement in all directions, and safety, since the upper arm is mostly still while the rest of the hand moves, so the other joints of the hand were made firmer than the shoulder. Four ligaments secure the joint {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.54}. Hippocrates calls the two hollows of its lower end eyes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.55}.

## The forearm and the elbow
The forearm is made of two bones lying side by side lengthwise, called the two zinds. The upper, next to the thumb, is thinner and called the upper zind; the lower, next to the little finger, is thicker because it carries the load and is called the lower zind. The editor puts the foreign terms in the margin. The use of the upper one is the movement of twisting and turning the palm down; of the lower, bending and straightening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.55}. The elbow joint is formed from the joint of the two bones with the arm bone. The lower one has two processes with a notch between, like the Greek sigma, which fits into a notch on the end of the arm bone; when it moves back and down the hand straightens until the wall of the hollow stops it and the arm and forearm stand straight, and when it moves forward and up the hand bends {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.55}.
