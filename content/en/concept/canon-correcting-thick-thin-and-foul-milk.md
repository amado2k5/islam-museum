## Summary
If one must use milk that lacks the praised qualities, it is managed through how it is given and through treating the nurse: thick milk is thinned with oxymel cooked with attenuating herbs and exercise, thin milk by rest and thickening the blood, hot or cold temperament by the opposite diet, and foul-smelling milk by scented wine and sweet-smelling food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.207-209}.

## By how it is given
Milk that is thick and ill-smelling is best given after it has been drawn and exposed to the air, and milk that is very hot is best never given on an empty stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.207}.

## Thick and thin milk
If the nurse's milk is thick she is given seed oxymel cooked with attenuating herbs such as pennyroyal, hyssop, ground pine and wild thyme, a little radish goes into her food, and she is told to vomit with hot oxymel and to take moderate exercise. If her temperament is hot she is given oxymel with thin wine; if her milk is thin she is rested, kept from exercise and fed what makes thick blood, perhaps given sweet wine or grape syrup, and told to sleep more {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.207}.

## By temperament
If the signs show heat in her she is fed with barley gruel and spinach and the like; if they show a cold temperament, obstructions or a weak drawing power, her light, warm-leaning food is increased and cupping glasses are set below the breasts without roughness, and carrot seed and carrot itself help {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.207-208}.

## Smell of the milk
Foul-smelling milk is treated by giving scented wine and sweet-smelling food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.209}.
