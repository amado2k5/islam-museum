## Nature
It has kinds: a red kind of good taste and scent, a kind whose taste resembles that of rue, a black kind inclining to purple with a scent like the rose, a black foul-smelling kind with thin cracked bark, a kind inclining to white with the smell of leek, and a kind with a fine hollow tube. They mention that something like it turns into cinnamon, and some say it is found on the cinnamon tree itself and sometimes joined to it. The author says he heard from a trusted person that it is the bark of a tree like the cinnamon tree, brought from the direction of China, with the power of a weak cinnamon, the good kind ranking with cinnamon. He reports from Dioscorides that there are many kinds in the Arab lands that grow aromatics, with a stem of thick bark and a leaf like that of a kind of iris, the other kinds being poor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.603–604}

## Selection
The best is the red, clear, smooth one, long in the stick, thick in the tube and fine in the pore, full at the break and sharp in scent, which stings the tongue and tightens it; the black is poor, and what is used is its bark, there being no good in its wood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Actions and properties
It dissolves thick winds, with a little astringency and more pungency, much subtlety and a cutting power; by its astringency it helps the astringent drugs and by its dissolving it helps the purgatives, and by the dissolving, astringency and subtlety in it, it strengthens the organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Swellings and pustules
It dissolves hot and cold swellings in the viscera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Wounds and ulcers
It is applied with honey to soft ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Ocular organs
It enters eye drugs for the astringency in it together with its dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its drink is good for the liver, and a wine in which salikha is put benefits the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Excretory organs
It promotes the urine, especially where thick humours are the cause, benefits pains of the kidneys and the bladder, and the wine in which it is soaked is good for difficult urination {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Poisons and bites
It is given to drink for the venom of the viper {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists sitting in its decoction as helping widening and slipperiness of the womb, and likewise its smoke, and says that it promotes the menses and that some claimed it causes foetuses to be lost {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.604}
