## Summary
Tadhkiya is the lawful cause that makes the flesh of a land animal, one fit to be slaughtered, allowed to eat. It covers slaughter, the thrust into the base of the camel's neck, wounding an animal beyond reach, and hunting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4910} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4911}

## Definition
Tadhkiya means completing a thing and slaughter; the saying "the slaughter of the unborn is the slaughter of its mother" uses the word. In law it is the cause that makes eating a land animal lawful by choice. For the Hanafis it is the lawful way that keeps the animal pure and makes its flesh, or its hide and hair if inedible, lawful to use. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4910}

## Kinds
- Slaughter (dhabh): cutting the windpipe from the inside at the joint between neck and head; it is slaughter by choice and narrower than tadhkiya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4911}
- Nahr: thrusting into the hollow at the base of a camel's neck and chest, cutting the veins low in the neck; it is another form of slaughter by choice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4911}
- Aqr: wounding an animal that cannot be reached, anywhere on its body; it is slaughter by necessity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4911}
- Hunting (sayd): killing a wild animal by sending an arrow, a dog or a falcon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4911}

## Ruling
Tadhkiya allows the flesh of a permitted animal that is fit to be slaughtered, whether by slaughter, thrust or wounding. Fish and locusts are lawful without tadhkiya, since they are not of the kind slaughtered. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4911}

## Conditions on the one who slaughters
The jurists require that the one who slaughters be a Muslim or a People of the Book. The Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis, and one view of the Shafi'is, require him to be discerning, able to understand naming God and slaughter; the sounder Shafi'i view does not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4911} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4912}
Most jurists require naming God at the time of slaughter, unless one has forgotten; the Shafi'is hold naming recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4912}

## The instrument
Slaughter is lawful with any sharp instrument that cuts, such as iron, copper, gold, wood, stone or glass. A tooth or nail still attached to the body is not lawful by agreement; if detached, the jurists differ. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4912}
The rules are treated in the chapters on hunting, slaughter and sacrifice; the Malikis treat them under the heading of slaughter. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4912}
