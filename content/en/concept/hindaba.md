## Nature and selection
Nature: there is a wild and a garden kind, each in broad-leaved and narrow-leaved forms. It is like lettuce, though said to be inferior in its qualities; the author holds that it surpasses lettuce in opening and in helping blocked liver, though it falls short in cooling and nourishment. Choice: the most bitter is the most useful for the liver. Temperament: cold at the end of the first degree; its dry kind is dry in the first; its moist kind moist at the end of the first. The garden kind is colder and moister; the wild is less moist, and is called tarkhashquq. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
Temperament: cold at the end of the first degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}

## Actions
Opens blockages of the viscera and vessels; it has good astringency, not strong, and its water with lead white and vinegar is remarkable for cooling what is to be cooled when applied. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}

## Uses by organ
Joints: applied as a poultice to gout. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
Eyes: helps hot inflammation; wild hindaba milk clears the whiteness of the eye. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
Breathing and chest: a poultice with barley flour helps palpitations and strengthens the heart; cucumber-cress steeped in its water as a gargle helps throat swellings. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
Digestive organs: it soothes nausea and bile agitation and strengthens the stomach; it is one of the best remedies for a stomach with hot ill temper, and the wild is better for the stomach than the garden. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
Excretory organs: eaten with vinegar it binds the belly, especially the wild. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
Fevers: helps quartan and cold fevers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
Poisons: as a poultice with its roots it helps the sting of scorpions, insects, wasps and snakes; likewise with barley gruel for the gecko's poison. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.449}
