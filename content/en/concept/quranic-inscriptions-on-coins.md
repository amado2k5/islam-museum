## Summary
Farajallah Ahmad Yusuf holds that [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] was the first to inscribe Qur'anic verses on Islamic coins, to express the independent identity of the state, when in 77 AH he issued Arab dinars free of any image. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 24} He inscribed [[passage/quran-112-1|Surat al-Ikhlas]] — in the author's view a reply to the Trinitarian formulas of Byzantine coins — and [[passage/quran-9-33|al-Tawba 33]] to affirm the universality of the mission and the prevailing of Islam, a verse that became one of the most widespread on Islamic coins. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 24} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 25} Pure Arab dirhams followed in 78 AH. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 24}

## A political instrument
The author says that Abd al-Malik thereby set a precedent, and verses came to express the political and sectarian orientations and economic and social conditions of many states. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 25} His examples include:
- [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-muhammad-ibn-al-ashath|Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn al-Ash'ath]], rising against Abd al-Malik, inscribed [[passage/quran-4-139|al-Nisa' 139]], protesting, the author says, al-Hajjaj's levying of jizya on Muslims. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 25}
- The Abbasid da'wa took [[passage/quran-42-23|al-Shura 23]] as a slogan on its coins from 127 AH; it stayed on Abbasid coins until 136, and was then inscribed by al-Hasan ibn Zayd in the Alid state of Tabaristan and by al-Hasan ibn al-Qasim. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 25} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 26}
- Ibrahim ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Hasan inscribed [[passage/quran-17-81|al-Isra' 81]] on coins struck at Basra in 145 AH, and it became an Alid slogan; the Alid rulers of Tabaristan inscribed [[passage/quran-22-39|al-Hajj 39]] and [[passage/quran-33-33|al-Ahzab 33]], the latter appearing also on coins of the Sa'dian sharifs of Morocco. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 26} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 27}
- [[person/abd-allah-al-mamun|Al-Ma'mun]], after his conflict with his brother al-Amin, inscribed [[passage/quran-30-4|al-Rum 4–5]], which became a hallmark of Abbasid coins until 656 AH and was used also by Abd al-Rahman al-Nasir in al-Andalus after he was proclaimed Commander of the Faithful in 316. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 27} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 28}
- Ahmad ibn Abd Allah al-Khujistani inscribed [[passage/quran-3-26|Al Imran 26]], which later appeared on the coins of Hulagu and his son Abaqa — Buddhists, the author notes, addressing their Muslim subjects — and of the Nasrids of Granada. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 28}
- The leader of the Zanj revolt inscribed [[passage/quran-9-111|al-Tawba 111]] and [[passage/quran-5-44|al-Ma'ida 44]]; the author judges the second choice mistaken, since it was revealed about the Jews and a Muslim is not declared an unbeliever for a grave sin. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 29}
- [[passage/quran-61-13|Al-Saff 13]] appeared on a dirham of Rayy of 329 AH for the Samanid Nasr ibn Ahmad, then on coins of al-Mustansir and al-Musta'sim as the Mongols advanced; [[passage/quran-3-126|Al Imran 126]] appeared on Marinid coins and on Mamluk coins from al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun until 923 AH. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 29} {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 30}
- Al-Radi bi-llah inscribed [[passage/quran-35-34|Fatir 34]] on appointing Muhammad ibn Ra'iq amir al-umara'. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 30}

## Note
These readings of motive are the author's interpretations of each verse's choice in its historical setting, as set out in his introduction. {cite:yusuf-ayat-quraniyya-maskukat-2003 p. 25}
