## Nature
Dioscorides said that it has many kinds. One is the garden kind, a plant that is eaten and is not large, with many branches and a leaf inclining to black, larger and broader than the leaf of basil, and a round fruit that appears green, then turns black, and when ripe turns red; eating it does no harm. The second kind is called ta'fin, with a leaf like the first but broader, whose rods bend downward when long, and a round fruit like a bladder, red and smooth like a grape; it is used in garlands, its power is like that of the first, except that it is not eaten. The juice of both kinds is extracted and each is dried in the shade and stored, and their action is one. The third kind is sleep-inducing, with many dense branching branches hard to crush, full of fat leaves, large red flowers and fruit in husks the colour of saffron, growing in rocky places. The fourth kind is the maddening one, called kubril by the people of Tabaristan, with a leaf like that of arugula but larger, branches coming out of the root with heads at their ends like olives, covered with down, round soft black seeds like those of ivy, and a good thick root, growing in mountain places and places scorched by winds among plane trees. The fifth kind some people call writmus, like an olive tree when it first grows, very rough, with a curled white flower like the flower of the chickpea and a smooth hard seed like the chickpea in mixed colours, growing among rocks not far from the sea or water; this too induces sleep and kills if much of it is eaten, and some people claimed that its root is used for love {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.612–613}

## Selection
What is used of it is the green-leafed, yellow-fruited, and it is, as we mentioned, of five kinds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Temperament
Cold in the first degree and dry in the second, and the narcotic is cold and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Actions and properties
The seed of the garden kind is astringent; there is a narcotic sleep-inducing kind that resembles opium in its qualities but is weaker, and a deadly kind, as we said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Swellings and pustules
Its poultice is good for all hot swellings, outer and inner, its water is drunk for hot inner swellings, its water with ceruse and rose oil is applied to erysipelas and herpes, the bark of its root is strongly drying, and its leaf with gentian is likewise useful for erysipelas and herpes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Organs of the head
If the narcotic kind is drunk to excess it produces madness; gargling with its water benefits swellings of the tongue; the bark of its roots drunk with wine brings sleep; nightshade, finely pounded and applied as a poultice, cures headache and dissolves swellings at the base of the ear and swellings of the membranes of the brain, and as drops it benefits earache; the bark of the root of the third kind cooked in wine and its decoction held in the mouth benefits toothache; and if the fourth kind is drunk with wine it makes the drinker imagine visions that are not wild, and he sees dreams that are not harmful and are friendly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Ocular organs
It cures a bursting lacrimal fistula; the juice of its kinds, even the sleep-inducing one, used as a collyrium strengthens sight; and the eye salve made for pains of the eye is sometimes dissolved with it in place of water and in place of egg white {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Applied alone as a poultice it benefits inflammation of the stomach and the kidneys {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Excretory organs
The seed of the narcotic kind promotes urine and cleanses the kidneys and the bladder, and it is among what cools and prevents nocturnal emission {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Poisons and bites
A kind of nightshade other than the kakanj, the garden kind and the narcotic kind mentioned kills if a certain amount is eaten, and less than that brings on madness, and it has none of the benefits of nightshade except as a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists all its kinds, taken as a suppository, as cutting the flow of the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.613}
