## Definition
Ihsar (being prevented from completing a rite) means, in the lexicon, being kept from reaching the rites by sickness or the like; the jurists give the word the same sense, though they differ on what counts as prevention. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845} Jurists used the root h-s-r and its derivatives for barring a pilgrim from the pillars of the rite, following the Qur'an, until ihsar became an established technical term. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845}
The Hanafis define it as being prevented from both standing at Arafat and circling the House after entering the obligatory or voluntary hajj, and in umra as being prevented from circling; the definition is not disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845} The Malikis define it as being prevented from standing and circling together, or from one of them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845} The Shafi'is follow al-Ramli's definition: being prevented from completing the pillars of hajj or umra; the Hanbalis agree, since they hold ihsar applies to any pillar, with a slight difference on how to release oneself when prevented from standing but not circling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845}
The legal basis of the consequence of ihsar is release in the manner described later. The root of the subject is the episode of Hudaybiyya; the verse in al-Baqara (196) on completing hajj and umra was revealed then, and it orders what offering is easy to find when prevented and forbids shaving until the offering reaches its place. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} Al-Bukhari reports from Ibn Umar that the Quraysh unbelievers barred the House, and the Prophet slaughtered his offering and shaved his head. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
What makes ihsar occur: it occurs when its pillar exists, which is being prevented from continuing the rite, hajj or umra, provided certain conditions hold, some agreed and some disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
The pillar of ihsar. The jurists differ on whether the prevention that causes ihsar includes prevention by an enemy and by illness or similar causes, or applies only to an enemy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} The Hanafis say ihsar occurs by an enemy and by other causes, such as illness, running out of money, or the death of a woman's mahram or husband on the road; so it occurs with any restraint that holds the pilgrim from continuing the requirements of ihram. This is also the view of Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn al-Zubayr, Alqama, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, Urwa, Mujahid, al-Nakha'i, Ata, Abu Thawr, and a narration from Ahmad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
The Malikis hold that ihsar occurs by an enemy, by civil strife, and by unjust imprisonment; so do the Shafi'is and the prevailing Hanbali view, with other causes of compulsion, such as a husband preventing his wife from following. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} The three schools agree that one who cannot reach the House because of a restraint other than an enemy, such as illness, lameness or lost funds, may not release himself by that. Those who make release conditional on a restraint have a special rule, explained later. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} This view denies that illness counts, and it is the view of Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Tawus, al-Zuhri, Zayd ibn Aslam and Marwan ibn al-Hakam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
The Hanafis argue from the Qur'an, the sunna and reason. From the Qur'an, the verse on ihsar: the word in its root sense is for illness, and the enemy is included in meaning. From the sunna, the hadith of al-Hajjaj ibn Amr al-Ansari: «whoever is broken or lamed has been released, and must make up the hajj the following year»; another version adds «or ill». From reason, illness is analogous to the enemy, since both hold back from the pillars of the rite. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1847}
The majority argue from the Qur'an, reports and reason. From the Qur'an, the verse itself was revealed at Hudaybiyya about an enemy, and the sick person is included in the general command to complete hajj and umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1847} From reports, Ibn Abbas said: there is no ihsar but the ihsar of the enemy; the one who falls ill or hurts or goes astray owes nothing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1847} Malik's Muwatta reports that Ibn Umar, Marwan and Ibn al-Zubayr ruled for Ibn Hazzaba al-Makhzumi, who was thrown down in a part of the road to Mecca while in ihram, that he should treat himself with what he needs and pay a ransom; when he recovers he should perform umra and be released from his ihram, and must make up the hajj the following year and sacrifice an offering. This chain is sound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848} From reason, al-Shirazi argued: if one enters ihram and illness prevents him, he may not release himself, since by release he does not escape the harm he is in, and he is like one who has lost his way. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Conditions of ihsar. The jurists did not state the conditions explicitly, but they can be drawn out. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
First, the rite must have been entered before the prevention: hajj, umra or both. If an obstacle arises before ihram, nothing is owed. Ihsar applies to a defective ihram as to a valid one, with its consequences. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Second, if he is in hajj ihram, he must not have stood at Arafat before the obstacle arose from continuing. The Hanafis and Malikis hold this; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that ihsar from circling the House applies. For umra, ihsar occurs by being prevented from most of the circling, by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Third, he must despair of the removal of the obstacle, by certainty or strong likelihood that it will not be removed before the hajj is lost, so that there is no time between him and the night of sacrifice in which he could travel if the excuse ended. The Malikis and Shafi'is state this; al-Ramli put the period for umra at three days. An obstacle whose removal is expected soon is not ihsar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Fourth, a condition the Malikis alone state: that the pilgrim did not know, when he entered ihram, of the obstacle to completing hajj or umra. If he knew, he may not release himself; he remains in ihram until he performs hajj the following year, unless he expected it would not stop him and it did, in which case he may release himself then, as the Prophet did at Hudaybiyya, when he entered umra knowing of the enemy but expecting it would not bar him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Types of ihsar by the pillar prevented from. There are three. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849}
First, ihsar from standing at Arafat and from the Ifada circling together, which is true ihsar with its consequences; the imams agree, though they differ on some causes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849}
Second, ihsar from standing at Arafat but not from circling. The Hanafis say this is not ihsar, a view also reported from Ahmad, since the pilgrim can release himself by the acts of umra with his earlier ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849} The compiler of al-Maslak al-Mutaqassit said: one barred only from standing is in the position of one who has missed hajj; he releases himself after the standing time has passed by the acts of umra, owes no offering and no umra for the make-up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849} The Malikis and Shafi'is count him as prevented and release him by the acts of umra, but the results differ: the Hanafis treat it as a release of one who missed hajj with no offering, while the Malikis and Shafi'is treat it as ihsar release with an offering. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849} The Hanbalis may turn the hajj intention into umra with no offering; if he circled and ran for arrival and was then prevented until he missed hajj, he releases himself by another circling and running. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849}
Third, ihsar from the pillar circling. The Hanafis and Malikis hold that one who has stood at Arafat and is then prevented is not in ihsar, since the risk of missing hajj is gone. He does the other rites of hajj, and remains in ihram as far as women are concerned until he circles the Ifada. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} The Shafi'is say that if the pilgrim is barred from Mecca but not from Arafat, he stands and releases himself; there is no make-up, in the sounder view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} The Hanbalis distinguish: if prevented from the House after standing and before the stoning, he may release himself; if prevented from the Ifada circling after the stoning at Jamrat al-Aqaba, he may not, because his ihram then concerns only women, while the law provided release only from full ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} Once the prevention ends he circles, and his hajj is complete. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850}
Types of ihsar by cause. Ihsar by a cause of compulsion or authority includes: the enemy, civil strife, imprisonment, a ruler's prevention, being attacked by a wild beast, a creditor preventing his debtor, a husband preventing his wife, the death or disappearance of the pilgrim or husband, a sudden waiting period, a guardian preventing a child or a fool, and a master preventing his slave. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} The Malikis limit the ihsar that allows release to three causes: the enemy, strife, and unjust imprisonment; these are agreed across the schools. The Hanafis alone add the ruler's prevention, the wild beast and the sudden waiting period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850}
a. Ihsar by an unbelieving enemy: the enemy seizes a place on the road of pilgrims, cuts the way and bars them from completing their rites. This form is agreed to be legal ihsar, and the Qur'an was revealed about it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851} The Hanafis and Malikis hold that if the enemy blocks one road to Mecca or Arafat and the pilgrim finds another, one must examine it: if taking it harms him, by its length or difficulty, in a considerable way, he is legally prevented; otherwise not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851} The Shafi'is oblige the pilgrim to take the other road even if it is longer or harder, so long as he has money for it; the Hanbali wording is absolute and points to the same obligation, even if the road is longer or harder. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851} If he takes the longer road and misses hajj because of its length or roughness, there are two views in each school; the sounder Shafi'i view is that he owes no make-up and releases himself as one prevented; the other view requires make-up, as if he had taken it from the start. If the two roads are equal in every way, make-up is owed without dispute, since the loss is a plain missing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
b. Ihsar by civil strife: war breaks out between Muslims and the pilgrim is prevented by it, as the strife between al-Hajjaj and Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr in 73 AH. This is legal ihsar by agreement, like the enemy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
c. Imprisonment: the pilgrim is jailed after entering ihram. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis distinguish imprisonment with right and imprisonment without right: if he is imprisoned unjustly, or is a debtor whose insolvency is proven, he is prevented. If he is imprisoned for a right he owes and can get out of, he may not release himself and is not prevented; his case is like illness. The Hanafis allow imprisonment as a cause of ihsar without distinction. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
d. A creditor preventing his debtor: the Shafi'is and Hanbalis count debt as a bar to the rite within the law of ihsar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
The creditor. The Malikis count imprisonment for debt as ihsar only if it is unjust; the Hanafis make imprisonment a cause in general, so the question is settled for them by imprisonment as such. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1852}
The husband preventing his wife. This makes her ihsar by agreement of the four schools, in voluntary hajj or voluntary umra for all, and in umra of Islam for the Hanafis and Malikis, who deny its obligation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1852} If the husband gave his permission from the start for a voluntary hajj or umra, and she has a mahram, he may not prevent her after entry, because that would be deceiving her, and her prevention by him does not count as ihsar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1852} For the obligatory hajj, or a hajj owed by vow, entered without the husband's permission while she has a mahram, the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis do not count her as prevented, since they do not make his permission a condition of the obligation; he may not stop her from an obligatory hajj nor release her by a forbidden act of ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1852} The Shafi'is make the husband's permission a condition of the obligation of hajj; if he did not permit it before her entry and then prevents her, she is in the first case, by the sounder Shafi'i view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1852} If she entered for the obligatory hajj with no mahram and her husband prevented her, she is prevented according to the Hanafis in the apparent narration, and so for the Shafis and Hanbalis; the Malikis do not count her as prevented if she travels with a trusted company and is herself trusted, since they accept this for a woman's obligatory journey. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1852}
The father preventing his son. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that a parent or both may prevent a son from voluntary hajj but not from obligatory hajj; one Maliki narration extends it to obligatory hajj. The Malikis and Hanbalis do not count him as prevented by their prohibition, since the Malikis limit the causes of ihsar to what does not include this. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1852} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853} The Hanafis say it is disliked to set out for hajj if a parent objects and the father needs his son's service; if he does not need it, there is no harm. Obligatory hajj takes precedence over obedience to parents, and obedience to them takes precedence over voluntary hajj. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853}
The emergent waiting period. This means a waiting period from divorce arising after entry. If a woman entered for the hajj of Islam, a vowed hajj or a voluntary one, and her husband divorced her so that she owed a waiting period, the Hanafis hold she is prevented even with a mahram, without any limit of journey distance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853} The Malikis treat the waiting period after divorce as after the husband's death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853} The Shafi'is: if she entered for hajj or qiran and was then divorced or widowed, and fears missing the rite because of a short time, she must set out, while still in her waiting period, because the ihram came first; if the time is ample and she is safe from missing it, she may set out for it, since fixing the delay would impose the hardship of holding ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853} The Hanbalis distinguish: a woman with a finally divorced status may set out for hajj in her waiting period; for a revocable divorce she is in ihsar as a wife. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853}
Causes that bar continuing the rite. These include breaking or lameness, illness, loss of funds, loss of the mount, inability to walk, and losing the road to Mecca or Arafat. The Hanafis hold that a pilgrim so barred is in ihsar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853} The majority say these do not make a pilgrim prevented in law; if he is held back by any of them he may not release himself until he reaches the House; if he catches the hajj in time, well; otherwise he releases himself by the acts of umra and his case is that of missing the rite. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853}
Breaking or lameness. Lameness here means a condition that prevents walking; the basis is the earlier hadith: «whoever is broken or lamed is released». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853} Illness. The illness that counts here is one that prevents going on, judged by strong likelihood or by the report of a skilled and religious physician; some reports of the hadith add «or ill». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853}
Loss of funds or mount. If a pilgrim's funds are stolen or lost on the road after he has entered ihram, or run out, he is not prevented if he can walk; if he cannot walk, he is prevented, as stated in al-Tajnis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1853} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1854}
Inability to walk. If he enters ihram unable to walk from the start, and has the means for expenses but not for a mount, he is then prevented. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1854} Losing the road: anyone who loses the road to Mecca or Arafat is prevented. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1854}
The rules of ihsar cover two matters: release, and what the barred pilgrim owes after release. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1854}
Release. In the lexicon it means doing what takes one out of sanctity; by technical definition it is ending the ihram and leaving it by the way the law set. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1854}
Permission to release. Once the mark of ihsar is present, release is allowed. This is agreed, each school by the causes it counts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1855} The basis is the command to complete hajj and umra, which is the rule; release before completing is an exception by evidence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1854} The evidence is the verse on ihsar in al-Baqara (196): what is meant is that if you are barred and wish to release yourselves, sacrifice what offering is easy. The ihsar alone does not require an offering, since he may remain in ihram until the obstacle lifts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1854} The sunna: the Prophet at Hudaybiyya released and ordered his companions to release when the polytheists barred them from umra at the Ancient House. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1855}
Choosing between release and enduring ihram. The Hanafis say the barred pilgrim may release, that it is a concession for him so that his ihsar does not drag on and hurt him, and that he may also remain in ihram, return home without release, and stay in ihram until the fear passes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856}
The Malikis: if one of their three causes (enemy, strife, imprisonment without right) bars him from completing the hajj by standing and the House together, or the umra by the House or the running, he may release by intention wherever he is, near Mecca or not, and he may remain for the following year, though release is better. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856} If he is barred by something other than these three, such as illness, he is advised, when near Mecca, not to keep his hajj ihram to the next year, and to release by an umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856}
The Shafi'is distinguish between a wide and a narrow time. With a wide time it is better not to hasten release, since the obstacle may lift and the hajj be completed; with a narrow time it is better to hasten release lest the hajj be lost, unless he expects to reach it after the ihsar, or the umra within three days, in which case he must wait. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856} The Hanbalis say the preferred course is to stay in ihram hoping the ihsar lifts; if it lifts before release, he must go on to complete his rite. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856}
In sum, the permission to release is agreed; the schools differ on whether it is better than remaining in ihram. If the barred pilgrim chooses release, he may release whenever he wishes, once he has done what release requires. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856} This applies alike to one barred from hajj, umra, or both. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856}
Release from a defective ihram. A pilgrim whose ihram was defective may release when barred. If he had intercourse spoiling a hajj and was then barred, he releases, owing an offering for the spoiling and an offering for the ihsar, and he owes a make-up for the spoiling, agreed, since the dispute about make-up concerns ihsar after a valid ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1855} If he did not release until he missed standing and could not circle, he releases in place as one barred, owing three offerings: one for the spoiling (a camel), one for the missing, and one for the ihsar, with one make-up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1855} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856} The Malikis: in the first case one offering, that of spoiling, suffices, since they hold the barred owe no offering; in the second case two, the spoiling and the missing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856}
Remaining in ihram. If the barred pilgrim chooses to stay in ihram and endure until the obstacle lifts, there are two cases for hajj. He may catch the hajj by reaching standing at Arafat, which is well. Or he may miss it by missing standing, and then the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis agree he releases by the acts of umra as one who missed hajj. The Hanafis say he owes no offering because that is the rule for missing, and he owes the make-up. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis say he owes the missing offering, not the ihsar one; the sounder Shafi'i view is no make-up, while the Hanbalis require it, by their rule that one who does not release until he misses the hajj owes the make-up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856} The Malikis hold that if he stays in ihram until the next year's ihram time and the obstacle lifts, he may not release by umra; the earlier ihram for hajj serves for the following year. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1856}
If the obstacle continues and the hajj is missed: the Malikis and Shafi'is allow him to release as one barred, without make-up, the Shafi'is requiring an offering, and in one view make-up. The Hanbalis require make-up. The Hanafis treat it as missing, and the barring has no effect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1857}
The wisdom of permitting release. The barred pilgrim needs to be released, as Kasani said, because he is held from continuing the requirement of ihram in a way he cannot push aside. If release were not allowed he would remain in ihram, unable to do what ihram forbids, until the obstacle lifted, which would bring great harm and hardship; so need leads to release, to remove harm and hardship. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1857}
The release of a barred pilgrim: the kinds of ihram. Ihsar is of two kinds by the way ihram was entered: ihram that is absolute, in which the pilgrim did not stipulate his right to release if an obstacle arose; and ihram in which he did stipulate it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1857} Ihsar in absolute ihram is of two kinds, as Hanafi jurisprudence draws out: barred by a real or legal obstacle that is God's right, with no part of the servant's right; and barred by a legal obstacle that is the servant's right, not God's. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1857} The result of this division, as to ruling, matches the other schools in general, where they agree it is ihsar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1857}
How the barred pilgrim releases. First, the intention to release. The start of intention to release is agreed as a condition; the disputes lie beyond it. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis require the intention at slaughtering the offering, so that the offering is for release, and then shaving; the Shafi'is also require it at shaving. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1857} The Malikis hold that intention alone is the pillar of release for one barred by enemy, strife or unjust imprisonment; without the intention, slaughtering and shaving do not release. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858} The Hanafis say: when a barred pilgrim wants to release he must send the offering; they tie release to sending and slaughtering the offering on the intention to release, and exclude one who wants to stay waiting for the obstacle to lift. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858}
Second, the offering. The offering in the lexicon is what is brought to the sanctuary, animals or otherwise; in the rites of hajj it specifically means camels, cattle, sheep and goats. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858} Most scholars hold the offering must be slaughtered for the barred pilgrim to release; sending or buying it does not release him until it is slaughtered. This is the view of the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, and a view of Ashhab among the Malikis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858} The Malikis hold that the barred pilgrim releases by intention alone; the offering is recommended, not required. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858} The majority rely on the verse on the offering, and on the sunna: the Prophet did not release nor shave his head at Hudaybiyya until he had slaughtered the offering, so that slaughtering it is a condition of release if he has it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858} The Malikis argue by analogy that it is an authorised release without fault and without shortfall, so no offering is due, as for one who completes his hajj. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858}
What an offering in ihsar may be: a sheep suffices for one person, and goats likewise by agreement; a camel or cow may stand for seven according to the majority, including the four imams. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1858}
