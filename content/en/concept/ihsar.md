## Definition
Ihsar (being prevented from completing a rite) means, in the lexicon, being kept from reaching the rites by sickness or the like; the jurists give the word the same sense, though they differ on what counts as prevention. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845} Jurists used the root h-s-r and its derivatives for barring a pilgrim from the pillars of the rite, following the Qur'an, until ihsar became an established technical term. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845}
The Hanafis define it as being prevented from both standing at Arafat and circling the House after entering the obligatory or voluntary hajj, and in umra as being prevented from circling; the definition is not disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845} The Malikis define it as being prevented from standing and circling together, or from one of them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845} The Shafi'is follow al-Ramli's definition: being prevented from completing the pillars of hajj or umra; the Hanbalis agree, since they hold ihsar applies to any pillar, with a slight difference on how to release oneself when prevented from standing but not circling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1845}
The legal basis of the consequence of ihsar is release in the manner described later. The root of the subject is the episode of Hudaybiyya; the verse in al-Baqara (196) on completing hajj and umra was revealed then, and it orders what offering is easy to find when prevented and forbids shaving until the offering reaches its place. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} Al-Bukhari reports from Ibn Umar that the Quraysh unbelievers barred the House, and the Prophet slaughtered his offering and shaved his head. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
What makes ihsar occur: it occurs when its pillar exists, which is being prevented from continuing the rite, hajj or umra, provided certain conditions hold, some agreed and some disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
The pillar of ihsar. The jurists differ on whether the prevention that causes ihsar includes prevention by an enemy and by illness or similar causes, or applies only to an enemy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} The Hanafis say ihsar occurs by an enemy and by other causes, such as illness, running out of money, or the death of a woman's mahram or husband on the road; so it occurs with any restraint that holds the pilgrim from continuing the requirements of ihram. This is also the view of Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn al-Zubayr, Alqama, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, Urwa, Mujahid, al-Nakha'i, Ata, Abu Thawr, and a narration from Ahmad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
The Malikis hold that ihsar occurs by an enemy, by civil strife, and by unjust imprisonment; so do the Shafi'is and the prevailing Hanbali view, with other causes of compulsion, such as a husband preventing his wife from following. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} The three schools agree that one who cannot reach the House because of a restraint other than an enemy, such as illness, lameness or lost funds, may not release himself by that. Those who make release conditional on a restraint have a special rule, explained later. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846} This view denies that illness counts, and it is the view of Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Tawus, al-Zuhri, Zayd ibn Aslam and Marwan ibn al-Hakam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1846}
The Hanafis argue from the Qur'an, the sunna and reason. From the Qur'an, the verse on ihsar: the word in its root sense is for illness, and the enemy is included in meaning. From the sunna, the hadith of al-Hajjaj ibn Amr al-Ansari: «whoever is broken or lamed has been released, and must make up the hajj the following year»; another version adds «or ill». From reason, illness is analogous to the enemy, since both hold back from the pillars of the rite. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1847}
The majority argue from the Qur'an, reports and reason. From the Qur'an, the verse itself was revealed at Hudaybiyya about an enemy, and the sick person is included in the general command to complete hajj and umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1847} From reports, Ibn Abbas said: there is no ihsar but the ihsar of the enemy; the one who falls ill or hurts or goes astray owes nothing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1847} Malik's Muwatta reports that Ibn Umar, Marwan and Ibn al-Zubayr ruled for Ibn Hazzaba al-Makhzumi, who was thrown down in a part of the road to Mecca while in ihram, that he should treat himself with what he needs and pay a ransom; when he recovers he should perform umra and be released from his ihram, and must make up the hajj the following year and sacrifice an offering. This chain is sound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848} From reason, al-Shirazi argued: if one enters ihram and illness prevents him, he may not release himself, since by release he does not escape the harm he is in, and he is like one who has lost his way. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Conditions of ihsar. The jurists did not state the conditions explicitly, but they can be drawn out. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
First, the rite must have been entered before the prevention: hajj, umra or both. If an obstacle arises before ihram, nothing is owed. Ihsar applies to a defective ihram as to a valid one, with its consequences. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Second, if he is in hajj ihram, he must not have stood at Arafat before the obstacle arose from continuing. The Hanafis and Malikis hold this; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that ihsar from circling the House applies. For umra, ihsar occurs by being prevented from most of the circling, by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Third, he must despair of the removal of the obstacle, by certainty or strong likelihood that it will not be removed before the hajj is lost, so that there is no time between him and the night of sacrifice in which he could travel if the excuse ended. The Malikis and Shafi'is state this; al-Ramli put the period for umra at three days. An obstacle whose removal is expected soon is not ihsar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Fourth, a condition the Malikis alone state: that the pilgrim did not know, when he entered ihram, of the obstacle to completing hajj or umra. If he knew, he may not release himself; he remains in ihram until he performs hajj the following year, unless he expected it would not stop him and it did, in which case he may release himself then, as the Prophet did at Hudaybiyya, when he entered umra knowing of the enemy but expecting it would not bar him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1848}
Types of ihsar by the pillar prevented from. There are three. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849}
First, ihsar from standing at Arafat and from the Ifada circling together, which is true ihsar with its consequences; the imams agree, though they differ on some causes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849}
Second, ihsar from standing at Arafat but not from circling. The Hanafis say this is not ihsar, a view also reported from Ahmad, since the pilgrim can release himself by the acts of umra with his earlier ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849} The compiler of al-Maslak al-Mutaqassit said: one barred only from standing is in the position of one who has missed hajj; he releases himself after the standing time has passed by the acts of umra, owes no offering and no umra for the make-up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849} The Malikis and Shafi'is count him as prevented and release him by the acts of umra, but the results differ: the Hanafis treat it as a release of one who missed hajj with no offering, while the Malikis and Shafi'is treat it as ihsar release with an offering. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849} The Hanbalis may turn the hajj intention into umra with no offering; if he circled and ran for arrival and was then prevented until he missed hajj, he releases himself by another circling and running. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1849}
Third, ihsar from the pillar circling. The Hanafis and Malikis hold that one who has stood at Arafat and is then prevented is not in ihsar, since the risk of missing hajj is gone. He does the other rites of hajj, and remains in ihram as far as women are concerned until he circles the Ifada. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} The Shafi'is say that if the pilgrim is barred from Mecca but not from Arafat, he stands and releases himself; there is no make-up, in the sounder view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} The Hanbalis distinguish: if prevented from the House after standing and before the stoning, he may release himself; if prevented from the Ifada circling after the stoning at Jamrat al-Aqaba, he may not, because his ihram then concerns only women, while the law provided release only from full ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} Once the prevention ends he circles, and his hajj is complete. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850}
Types of ihsar by cause. Ihsar by a cause of compulsion or authority includes: the enemy, civil strife, imprisonment, a ruler's prevention, being attacked by a wild beast, a creditor preventing his debtor, a husband preventing his wife, the death or disappearance of the pilgrim or husband, a sudden waiting period, a guardian preventing a child or a fool, and a master preventing his slave. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850} The Malikis limit the ihsar that allows release to three causes: the enemy, strife, and unjust imprisonment; these are agreed across the schools. The Hanafis alone add the ruler's prevention, the wild beast and the sudden waiting period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1850}
a. Ihsar by an unbelieving enemy: the enemy seizes a place on the road of pilgrims, cuts the way and bars them from completing their rites. This form is agreed to be legal ihsar, and the Qur'an was revealed about it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851} The Hanafis and Malikis hold that if the enemy blocks one road to Mecca or Arafat and the pilgrim finds another, one must examine it: if taking it harms him, by its length or difficulty, in a considerable way, he is legally prevented; otherwise not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851} The Shafi'is oblige the pilgrim to take the other road even if it is longer or harder, so long as he has money for it; the Hanbali wording is absolute and points to the same obligation, even if the road is longer or harder. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851} If he takes the longer road and misses hajj because of its length or roughness, there are two views in each school; the sounder Shafi'i view is that he owes no make-up and releases himself as one prevented; the other view requires make-up, as if he had taken it from the start. If the two roads are equal in every way, make-up is owed without dispute, since the loss is a plain missing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
b. Ihsar by civil strife: war breaks out between Muslims and the pilgrim is prevented by it, as the strife between al-Hajjaj and Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr in 73 AH. This is legal ihsar by agreement, like the enemy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
c. Imprisonment: the pilgrim is jailed after entering ihram. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis distinguish imprisonment with right and imprisonment without right: if he is imprisoned unjustly, or is a debtor whose insolvency is proven, he is prevented. If he is imprisoned for a right he owes and can get out of, he may not release himself and is not prevented; his case is like illness. The Hanafis allow imprisonment as a cause of ihsar without distinction. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
d. A creditor preventing his debtor: the Shafi'is and Hanbalis count debt as a bar to the rite within the law of ihsar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1851}
