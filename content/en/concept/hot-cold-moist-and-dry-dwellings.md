## Summary
Ibn Sina sets out the state of the people of dwellings according to their primary quality, hot, cold, moist or dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.127}.

## Hot
Hot dwellings darken and curl the hair and weaken digestion; when dissolving is very great and moistures few, old age comes quickly to their people, as in Abyssinia, where people grow old in thirty years and their hearts are fearful because the spirit is so much dissolved. Their people are softer of body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.127}.

## Cold
The people of cold dwellings are stronger, braver and better in digestion; if the dwelling is also moist they are fleshy and fatty, with deep-set veins, stiff joints, and fresh, plump bodies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.127}.

## Moist
The people of moist dwellings have good complexions and soft skins and quickly become slack in their exercise; their summers are not very hot nor their winters very cold. Among them are many chronic fevers, diarrhoea, menstrual bleeding, haemorrhoids, sores, putrefaction, thrush and epilepsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.127}.

## Dry
The people of dry dwellings have dried temperaments, their skin shrivels and cracks, dryness reaches their brains first, and their summers are hot and their winters cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.127}.
