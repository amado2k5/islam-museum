## Principles of cure
The common treatment is evacuation of blood as was taught, then evacuation by purging, its sharpness and gentleness raised in the measure of what the matter shows by the signs mentioned, when it is learned whether bile or phlegm prevails {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88} Food is withheld as in qaranitus, especially if its cause is eating too much; in that case the patient is made to vomit and his stomach cleansed, and if its cause is drunkenness he is not treated at all until the drunkenness ends, then moistening alone is used, and last he is treated as the end of the hangover (khumar) is treated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88}

## Blending the two extremes of cure
Its kinds share the gentle evacuations and the other things that are known, and the remedies in it are neither at the limit of the cold that is ordered in qaranitus nor at the limit of the warmth ordered in lithargus, but are compounded of the two, and in them prevails what is needed according to which of the two humours is shown to prevail {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88} At the end of the illness, after it has declined, the cold is avoided and the lightening means alone are used, then he is managed with the regimen of convalescents {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88}

## Note
Recipes, quantities, the names of drugs and plants, the names of veins and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and methods of application are left out here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.88}
