## Summary
Ibn Sina counts among harmful purgatives black hellebore, yellowish turbith, blackish agaric and mazarion, and when bad signs appear orders the drug be driven out by vomiting or purging and treated with theriac, very cold water and what breaks the sharpness by gluing, softening and fattiness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.284}.

## The harm
Some purgatives carry great harm, such as black hellebore, turbith when it is not a good white but of the yellow sort, agaric when it is not pure white but tends to black, and mazarion; these are bad. If any of them is drunk and bad signs appear, the right course is to expel the drug from the body as far as possible by vomiting or purging, and to treat with theriac; much of their evil and their ruin of the self is turned aside by giving very cold water, and by whatever breaks their sharpness, with gluing, softening and a gluey fattiness, which helps here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.284}.
