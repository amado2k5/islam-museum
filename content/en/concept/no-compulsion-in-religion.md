## Summary
On the verse that denies compulsion in religion, Sha'rawi holds that belief cannot be forced, and that God did not compel anyone into it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1111} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1112}.

## Force and the heart
He says force governs moulds, not the heart. One who forces people onto a principle is the first to doubt it, and when the hand of force weakens, the structure collapses {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1111}.

## Duty after entry
He distinguishes compulsion into religion from compulsion to what religion requires. One who has entered Islam must perform its rulings, as one who drinks wine while believing must answer for it, and this is not compulsion in belief {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1113}.

## Early weakness and jizya
He says the early Muslims were persecuted, so their weakness at first was intended. He takes the jizya taken from lands the Muslims conquered as evidence that those who kept their religion were not forced into Islam {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1114}.
