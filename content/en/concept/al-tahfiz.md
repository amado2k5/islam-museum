## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan defines motivation as a person receiving a reward — verbal, positional or material — and it covers for him both encouragement and deterrence {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:32:00}.
He opens the lesson with the 'six-four-one' game to prove that motivation is not always effective: an eager player who does not know the game's rules loses however much he is motivated; motivation fails without inner desire, and without knowledge, planning and experience preceding it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:14:30}.

## Conditions of its success
He sets three conditions: an inner desire in the person concerned — one who does not want is unmoved by the strongest encouragement or deterrence, citing the Muslims killed under torture who never consented — then knowledge and planning, then experience; the effect of motivation only begins after all that {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:15:00}.
He holds that sermons rousing the ummah with its past glories — without a plan, knowledge, leadership or experience — will not build a renaissance: the Islamic armies used to prepare, train and plan, and the motivation came at the battle (the speaker's view) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:16:30}.

## The ranks of motivators
He ranks the motivators: money motivates, praise motivates, ambition, inner satisfaction and self-respect motivate; second-highest is bearing responsibility joined to fitting authority; and the highest degree of motivation, to him, is belief in an idea, a cause or a creed — for one who believes in an idea does not need others' motivation and does not stop if it is absent {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:32:00}.
He generalises the principle to every deep belief in an idea — true or false — citing a Palestinian youth convinced of his cause, the Japanese Red Army, the Basques and the Tamils — and, in his reading, Abu Jahl as one driven by tribal solidarity, not by the idols {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:36:30}.
To deepen an idea in souls he lists means: persuasion, logic and dialogue; stories and parables; the living and the earlier exemplar — whence the stories of the prophets — and sometimes the threat of a profound loss {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:33:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:34:00}.

## The 'ideal employee' critique
He counts the end-of-year honouring of a single 'ideal employee' or 'top student' a failed philosophy: honour him again and the rest are aggrieved; honour another and he is broken {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:13:30}.
He attributes to Stephen Covey the story of a company that honoured fifty of its nine hundred and eighty employees at its annual ceremony; Covey asked its head: did you hire successes or failures? and if you picked successes, what did you do to them that they became failures {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:41:00}.
He recounts his own experiment at the American Creativity Academy: a students' bank dealing in points, not money — six points for every A, five for a B, plus points for conduct and activity — and prizes at year's end including travel tickets and shares, a special prize for the most improved student, and the option of saving points for the next year; so everyone is rewarded, not one {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:44:00}.
Of his own children he says he does not reward success in its moment but links them to the idea and the goal, not the prize (his view, which he admits may depart from custom) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:37:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 00:38:30}.
