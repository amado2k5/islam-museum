## Summary
The author states that private interest is legitimate, and indeed part of a Muslim's economic prudence, but when it conflicts with the public interest, the public interest must take precedence {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 357}.

## The Invisible Hand
The author holds that when individuals pursue their private interests in harmony with—or without contradicting—the public interest, an invisible hand serves the public interest and reconciles the two {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 357} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 358}. He states that Muslims articulated this centuries before Adam Smith {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 358}. This is a claim by the author, and the book cites no source proving this precedence.

## Limits of Profit Maximization
He sees no objection to maximizing profits and benefits within the bounds of Islamic rulings, ethics, and constraints, and does not consider it sound to abolish private interest in favor of the public interest, nor to abolish the principle of utility maximization altogether {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 358}.
