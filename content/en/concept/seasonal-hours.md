## Summary
Seasonal hours are hours that follow the length of day and night each day, dividing the arc of the day into twelve hours and the arc of the night likewise, so daytime hours lengthen in summer and shorten in winter {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 86}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 85}.

## Calculation
Al-Farghani states that seasonal hours are those by which each of day and night in both summer and winter always has twelve hours, while their lengths vary according to the lengthening and shortening of day and night {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 86}. Thus, when the day is longer than the night, its hours are longer than the hours of the night, and vice versa {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 86}.

## Difference Between Them and Equinoctial Hours
Equinoctial hours are equal every day, whereas seasonal hours change in length even though their number remains the same each day. A seasonal hour lengthens or shortens according to the clime and the day of the year, because the length of the day varies {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 85}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 86}.
