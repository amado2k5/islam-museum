## Summary
In the lexicon mulazama is to cling to a creditor. In law it is a measure a creditor takes to recover his right, close to imprisonment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14992}

## Related terms
Habs, in the lexicon restraint, is in law the restriction of a person from disposing of himself and going about his business. Both are measures used to secure the payment of rights. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14992}

## Ruling on shadowing a debtor
The majority agree on its legality and differ on conditions. The Hanafis allow the creditor to shadow a debtor even when his insolvency is proved; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis forbid anyone to pursue or shadow an insolvent debtor once the judge has found insolvency, and grant respite instead. The Malikis mention no such rule in the books consulted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14993} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14994}

## Method
For the Hanafis the creditor follows the debtor wherever he goes, does not confine him to a particular place, since that would be imprisonment which belongs only to the judge, and shadows him by day, not by night. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14994}

## Other cases
The creditor of a surety may shadow the surety if the principal is absent and the surety cannot produce him; the assignee may shadow the assignor's debtor. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14994} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14995}
