## Summary
Ibn Sina says what is done when bleeding is unavoidable though strength is weak, then sets the narrow incision, which keeps strength, against the wide incision, which is quicker to bring faintness and works more in cleansing, and gives what suits winter and summer and the position of the patient {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.

## Weak strength
When bleeding is forced upon one whose strength is weak, for a fever or for other bad humours, the bleeding should be divided. When strength weakens from much bleeding, many humours are generated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.290-291}.

## Narrow and wide incision
The narrow incision keeps strength better, but it may let the thin clear blood run and hold back the thick turbid. The wide one is quicker to bring faintness, works more in cleansing and is slower to close, and is better for one bled as a precaution and for the stout {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.

## Season and position
Indeed widening is better in winter so that the blood does not congeal, and narrowing is better in summer if needed. The patient should be bled lying on his back, for that is likelier to keep his strength and not bring faintness on him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.
