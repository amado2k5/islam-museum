## Summary
Ibn Sina gives chapter 29 to cautery: he lists its benefits, then describes cautery in deep places with a mould, what the cauterizer guards against, and what is done in cauterising a flow of blood, in removing corrupt flesh and in the skull {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.309}.

## Its benefits
Cautery is a useful treatment to prevent the spread of corruption, to strengthen an organ whose temperament is to be restored, to dissolve corrupt matter clinging to the organ and to stop a flow of blood. The best metal to cauterise with is gold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.309}.

## Deep places
The site of cautery is either visible, so that the cautery is applied by sight, or deep inside an organ, like the nose, the mouth or the anus. For the latter a mould is needed, on which talc and ochre soaked in vinegar are boiled, then cloths are wrapped round it and it is cooled thoroughly with rose water or some juices; the mould is entered into that passage until it grips the site of the cautery, and the cautery iron is then pushed into it to reach its place without harming what lies around. The iron is thinner than the walls of the mould, so it does not touch them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.309}.

## What the cauterizer guards against
The cauterizer must take care that the power of his cautery does not pass to the nerves, the tendons and the ligaments {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.309}.

## A flow of blood and corrupt flesh
If the cautery is for a flow of blood, it must be made strong so that the eschar has depth and thickness and does not fall quickly, for the fall of the eschar of a cautery for bleeding brings a worse affliction than the first. If the cautery is to remove corrupt flesh and the limit of the sound is wanted, it is where pain begins; sometimes the bone beneath must be cauterised along with the flesh until all its corruption is undone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.309}.

## The skull
If the bone is like the skull, the cautery is made gentle so that the brain does not boil and the membranes do not convulse; elsewhere no care is taken to be thorough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.309}.
