## Summary
Chapter 21 begins with finding how much of the night has passed at the time of observing a star, and with finding the ascendant. One of its requirements is the altitude of the star at midheaven: its distance from the equator is added to the altitude of the first of Aries in that place if the distance is northern and taken from it if it is southern, and the result is its altitude at midheaven. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.54-55}.

## When it exceeds ninety
If this altitude comes out above ninety degrees it is taken from one hundred and eighty and the remainder is its altitude from the northern horizon; the star is then on the north side of the zenith. Before this the text requires the degree culminating with the star, its half day arc and the degree from which it rises, all known from the earlier chapters. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.55}.
