## Nature
Migraine is a pain in one side of the head that flares, and Galen defines it, in the printed words, as "al-satira al-mutawassita" {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.74} Its cause may be inside the skull or in the membrane that covers the skull, and it is most often in the muscles of the temple; the outer kind may reach the point where touch cannot be borne {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.74}

## Causes
The matters reach the place either from the outer veins and arteries or from the brain itself and its membranes, and most of this rises by way of the sutures (durud); it may also come from vapours driven out from the whole body or from an organ on that side {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.74} Migraine is mostly periodic and mostly from humours; no migraine of any account comes from a simple ill-temperament, and what comes from humours comes from hot humours, from cold humours, or from winds and vapours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.74}

## Signs
He says the signs are already known: with the cold kind one finds quiet from warming and a stretching close by, and with the hot kind heat to the touch, throbbing in the temples and relief from cooling things; cold is felt with the cold kind and heat with the hot kind, when the pain grows strong {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.74}
