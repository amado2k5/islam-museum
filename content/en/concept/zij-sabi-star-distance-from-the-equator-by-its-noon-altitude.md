## Summary
Chapter 25 deals with finding the part of the ecliptic in which a star stands and its latitude, from its distance from the equator and the part culminating with it, when this is known by measuring the altitude of the star at midheaven. The first step is to take its altitude at midheaven and find the excess between it and the altitude of the first of Aries in that place; that is its distance from the equator, northern if the altitude is more than the altitude of the first of Aries and southern if less. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.58-59}.

## The part at midheaven
The part that culminates with the star is known from what is at midheaven at that hour from the signs, by measuring another star of known place or by other measurements, or from the star's rising or setting on the horizon circle as shown in the previous chapter. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.59}.
