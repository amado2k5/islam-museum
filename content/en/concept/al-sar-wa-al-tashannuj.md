## Why it is a convulsion, not a relaxation
Epilepsy runs the course of convulsion, not relaxation: it makes a contraction from the brain, not a relaxation and expansion, because the brain is trying thereby to expel something from itself, and expulsion is only achieved by contraction and squeezing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.119}

## The convulsion descending to the organs, and recovery
The cause of the convulsion descending to the organs that accompanies epilepsy is that the matter overlaying the brain, or the harm reaching it, reaches the nerves too, so their state is its state, for three reasons: their following of the brain's substance, their being hurt by what hurts it, and their filling from the humour driven into them at their beginnings, so that their breadth increases and their length decreases. Recovery occurs either for the expulsion of the humour, or the dissolution of the wind, or the expulsion of the harmful {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.119}

## It is helped by fever, and turns into melancholia
Every material convulsion is helped by fever, and epilepsy is a material convulsion, so it is helped by fever and by swellings if they appear in it, for these may dissolve it and lessen its matter. Melancholia often turns into epilepsy and epilepsy often turns into melancholia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.119}

## There is no epilepsy without matter
Some people have thought that there is epilepsy which is not from a matter: if by this is meant that its cause is a vapour and a quality harming the brain, the saying has a sense; but if it means that the cause is the bare temperament itself in the brain, that has no ground, for if the brain had taken on that quality the epilepsy would have to cling to it and not be something that passes at once — whereas the cause of epilepsy is what comes suddenly and passes at once, or prevails and kills; so it is a matter and a quality that reaches the brain and then breaks off {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.119}

## Epilepsy and the sneeze
What occurs in epilepsy is a disturbance of the soul's motion, not a choking of it — the disturbance being the disturbance of the convulsion — whereas in apoplexy it occurs by choking and forced holding of the breath; the motion of sneezing is a slight epileptic motion, and epilepsy is, as it were, a great strong sneeze, except that most of the sneeze's drive is forwards, because the power is strong and the matter weak, while the drive of epilepsy is to whichever side is easiest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.119}
