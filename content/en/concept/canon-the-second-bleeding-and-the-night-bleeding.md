## Summary
Ibn Sina explains how the second bleeding is done, the renewed letting of blood from the same incision after a delay, what the lancet is oiled with, its benefits and the sleep proper between the bleeding and the second, and says that the bleeding of the mad and of those bled at night in their sleep is to be narrow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.293}.

## Opening the incision lengthwise
Whoever wants a second bleeding and has had no harm from the first, such as palsy or the like, should have the vessel bled lengthwise so as to keep the movement of the muscle from closing it, and the incision should be widened. If, even so, quick closing is feared, a cloth wetted with oil and a little salt is put on it and bandaged over {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.293}.

## Oiling the lancet
If the lancet is oiled at the bleeding it prevents quick closing and lessens pain; that is, oil or the like is wiped lightly on it, or it is dipped in oil and then wiped with a cloth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.293}.

## Benefits of the second bleeding and sleep
Among the benefits of the second bleeding is keeping the strength of the one bled while completing the evacuation due to him, and the best second bleeding is the one put off two or three days. Sleep between the bleeding and the second prevents the superfluities that are drawn when sleep draws the humours into the depth of the body from being driven into the blood. Much bleeding in the second brings faintness unless the one being bled again has taken something {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.293}.

## Forms of the cut
The oblique cut suits one who wants the second bleeding within the day, and the lengthwise cut suits one who does not wish to stop at a single second bleeding and means to let blood seep for several days, each day; and the more painful the incision, the slower it is to close {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.293}.

## The mad and bleeding in sleep
Know that the bleeding of the mad, and of those who need to be bled at night while asleep, must be narrow lest a flow of blood occur, and so too for everyone who has no need of the second bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.293}.
