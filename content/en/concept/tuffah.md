## Choice
The most temperate is the Shami; the insipid is bad with few benefits and does only its own particular action, and so does the unripe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Temperament
The insipid is colder and moister for its wateriness; the astringent, tart and sour is cold and thick, and the sweet is watery and nearer to heat than the rest, though cold prevails; they differ, as do their leaves and trees; and the dominant in its substance is a cold superfluous moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Actions and properties
It holds back superfluities, especially its leaf, and brings bloating, especially the kinds that are not sweet; the astringent and tart is watery and earthy, the insipid very watery, which is why its juice quickly ferments and honey preserves it; the astringent and tart generate an earthy humour, and the sour and unripe generate putrefactions and fevers through the rawness of their humour and its taking putrefaction; its drink is better old than fresh as the bad vapours dissolve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Swellings and pustules
Its leaf and juice benefit the beginning of hot swellings and erysipelas {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Wounds and ulcers
Its leaf and bark heal, and likewise the juice of the astringent kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Joints
Constant eating of apples brings nerve pain, especially the spring kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Organs of the chest
It strengthens the heart, especially the fragrant Shami and the fragrant sweet and sour, and where there is a flood of heat it is of great benefit, and so is its sawiq {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It strengthens weakness of the stomach, and the astringent kind benefits it whether the weakness is from heat or moisture, and so do the tart and sour when there is a thick humour in it that is not very cold; baked in dough it benefits poor appetite, worms and dysentery, and its sawiq strengthens the stomach and prevents vomiting; the sweet and sour, meeting a thick humour, may send it down in the stool, and if the stomach is empty it holds; the most suitable for dysentery is the tart and its sawiq, unless the softness of sugar overcomes it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.691-692}

## Fevers
From the raw fruit many fevers may arise, through the rawness of its humour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.692}

## Poisons and bites
It benefits against poisons, and so does the juice of its leaf {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.692}
