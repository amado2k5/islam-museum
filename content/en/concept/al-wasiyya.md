## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi treats the bequest within inheritance law. The verse mentions it before the debt; in execution the debt is paid first because it has a claimant. He says Islam limited the bequest to a third so that heirs are not harmed. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2029}

## Order of bequest and debt
He says the bequest is named first in the verse out of care for it, since it has no claimant, while a debt has one. In execution the debt comes first because it is a duty owed, whereas a bequest is a gift. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2029}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2033}

## The third and the prohibition of harm
He states that Islam fixed the bequest at a third so that heirs are not harmed, and that the words "without harm" mean the testator must not intend to injure his heirs. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2033}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2034}

## A false debt
He describes a man who acknowledges a debt that does not exist in order to deprive relatives. A court may enforce it, he says, but it does not clear the man before God. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2034}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2035}
