## Summary
In the modern age Arabisation means making Arabic the language of teaching and science, and coining scientific terms in it. This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum reports on two experiences, Syria's and Morocco's, then on the language academies and institutions that led the coining of terms: the academies of Damascus and Cairo, the Iraqi Academy of Science, the Rabat institute for studies and research on Arabisation, and ALECSO's bureau for coordinating Arabisation {cite:pmc-articles-pmc9975733} {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021}.

## Syria
The summary says Syrian universities were pioneers in the Arabisation of the applied sciences, including medicine and engineering, from the 1910s and 1920s, and that it quickened after independence in the late 1950s. It sees in this proof that Arabic can serve as a university medium for science, but it passes on researchers' notes on continuing difficulties, among them the scarcity of up-to-date translated scientific books and the difficulty for graduates of keeping pace with fast-moving global research. The country has the page [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-syria|Arabic in Syria]] {cite:pmc-articles-pmc9975733} {cite:boston-college-ihe-arabization-syria-6859} {cite:awej-translation-special-issue-2-paper-2}.

## Morocco
After Morocco's independence in 1956 and the constitution of 1962, Morocco carried out Arabisation in primary and secondary schools, so that Arabic replaced French in general education, but French remained dominant in the university's science, medicine and engineering, according to the summary. More recently Moroccan higher education has widened English-medium teaching alongside French in scientific and postgraduate programmes. The country has the page [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-morocco|Arabic in Morocco]] {cite:hks-student-review-multilingual-education-morocco} {cite:encyclopedia-com-arabization-policies} {cite:pmc-articles-pmc9975733}.

## The academies and institutions
The summary names the academy of Damascus, founded in 1919, the Academy of the Arabic Language in Cairo in 1932, the Iraqi Academy of Science in Baghdad in 1947, the Rabat institute for studies and research on Arabisation, and ALECSO's Permanent Bureau of Coordination of Arabisation in 1962, and says they led the effort to coin and standardise modern scientific terminology. The museum holds records: [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-dimashq|the Damascus academy]], [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-qahira|the Cairo academy]], [[group/al-majma-al-ilmi-al-iraqi|the Iraqi Academy of Science]] and [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|the Union of Academies]] {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021} {cite:wikipedia-list-of-arabic-language-academies-en}.

## The date of the coordination bureau
This summary dates the founding of the Bureau for the Coordination of Arabisation to 1962, while another research file supplied to the museum dates it to 1961. The museum does not decide between them: each date is attributed to its own summary, and neither has been checked against a primary source {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021} {cite:wikipedia-list-of-arabic-language-academies-en}.

## What holds Arabisation back
The summary says that despite extensive lexicons, uneven adoption across Arab states, competing coinages and the pace of global scientific innovation continue to limit a unified Arabisation {cite:academypublication-tpls-vol05-10-03} {cite:pmc-articles-pmc9975733}.

## A reading of the dates
The first three academies by the summary, Damascus 1919, Cairo 1932 and Baghdad 1947, are separated by thirteen years and then fifteen, a figure derived from the years. Syria Arabises from the 1910s, so the start of Arabisation in Syrian universities and the Damascus academy are close in date. Morocco began Arabising schools after 1956, that is after independence, which puts the two experiences in different times: Syria before and after independence, Morocco after it. The page offers no explanation for these differences, for the summary gives none {cite:pmc-articles-pmc9975733} {cite:alhawary-arabic-applied-linguistics-2021}.
