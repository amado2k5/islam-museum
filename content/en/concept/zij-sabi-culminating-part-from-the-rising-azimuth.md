## Summary
Once the star's distance from the equator is known, its half day arc is found in the way given for the half day arc of a star. If the star is on the eastern horizon its half day arc is added to the rising times of the degree rising with it in the clime; if on the western horizon its half day arc is taken from the rising times of the part with which it sets. The result is brought into the rising times of the right sphere, and the degree of the signs against it is the part that culminates with the star. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.58}.

## The condition of the chapter
It is required that this be known by at least one measurement of the azimuth of rising or setting, and that the part of the ecliptic that rises or sets with the star be known by the earlier chapters; the chapter is therefore a step after the chapters on the half day arc and the rising degree. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.58}.
