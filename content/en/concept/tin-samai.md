## Nature
Dioscorides describes this earth as stone-like, used by goldsmiths for polishing. It comes in kinds: one grey-white, thin and in plates; another very white, smooth and quick to crumble, which dissolves quickly when moistened. Bathers rubbed it in the bath in place of the ashnan plant and natron. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.506}

## Properties and choice
It is astringent, cooling and drying. Choose the white hard kind; of the other, the white-grey kind. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.506}

## Adornment
It cleanses and beautifies the body and polishes the face. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.506}

## Head and eye
It thickens the senses, the book says. Taken with milk, it helps whiteness and ulcers that form in the eye. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.506}

## Food organs
Drunk, it helps the pain of the stomach. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.506}

## Womb and childbirth (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book reports a belief that hanging the earth on a woman in labour speeds the birth, and that hanging it on a pregnant woman prevents miscarriage. This is recorded as the book's report only. The hanging practice itself is not described as a method here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.506}
