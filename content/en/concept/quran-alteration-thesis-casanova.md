## Overview
A thesis Ghallab attributes to the orientalist Paul Casanova's *Muhammad and the End of the World*: that the Prophet believed the Hour was near and so named no successor, and that Abu Bakr then 'Uthman altered Qur'anic verses to fit what came after {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 79} {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 90}. Ghallab sets out its three proofs — the Qur'an's silence on political rule, the hadith "between me and the Hour," and the verses on death and resurrection {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 92} {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 104} {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 114} — and answers that the Prophet's mission was religious, the Qur'an commands shura, and the hadith likens nearness rather than fixing a term.
