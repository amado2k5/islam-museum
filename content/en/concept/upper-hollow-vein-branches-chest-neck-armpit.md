## Summary
Ibn Sina follows what remains of the rising hollow vein after the heart: hair-like branches in the dividing membranes and the thymus, two branches toward the collarbone, then five branches on each side, among them the great branch to the armpit that divides in four, while the remainder rises to the neck and becomes the two jugular veins {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.89-90}.

## Around the chest
What passes on from the hollow vein after the three parts, going up past the heart, sends hair-like branches into the upper dividing membranes of the chest, the upper part of the sac, and the soft flesh called the thymus. Near the collarbone two branches leave toward it, slanting and drifting apart as they go, and each becomes two: one on each side descends along the edge of the breastbone to the end called al-hanjari, leaving on its way branches that spread in the muscles between the ribs and meet the mouths of the vessels spread there, and a group of them comes out to the muscles outside the chest. At al-hanjari one group comes out to the heaped muscles that move the shoulder and another goes down under the straight muscle and spreads in it, their ends joining the rising parts of the sacral vein he mentions later {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.89}.

## The five branches
As for what is left of each of the two, each one leaves five branches: one spreads in the chest and feeds the upper four ribs; one feeds the place of the shoulders; one goes toward the deep muscles of the neck; one passes through the holes of the upper six vertebrae of the neck and beyond them to the head; and a great branch, the greatest, goes to the armpit on each side and divides in four {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.89}.

## Branches of the armpit
The first spreads in the muscles over the breastbone, of those that move the shoulder joint; the second in the soft flesh and membranes of the armpit; the third descends past the side of the chest to the soft flank; the fourth, the greatest, divides in three: a part in the hollow of the shoulder blade, a part in the great muscle of the armpit, and the third, the greatest, passes over the upper arm to the hand and is called the axillary vein. What remains of the first branching rises toward the neck and divides in two: the outer and the deep jugular veins {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.89-90}.
