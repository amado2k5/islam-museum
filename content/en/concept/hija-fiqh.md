## Summary
Hija', in the lexical sense, is the opposite of praise: abuse and the enumeration of faults. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16809} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16810}

## Distinct notions
It differs from sabb, which is wider; from qadhf, the accusation of unchastity or of a false lineage; and from ghiba, which is said in the absence of the person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16810} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16811}

## The ruling
Satirising an unbelieving non-protected person and an apostate is allowed, since the Prophet bade Hassan satirise the unbelievers; satirising a Muslim is forbidden, the Shafi'is excepting an innovator and an open sinner, the Hanafis allowing the satire of a hypocrite. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16812}
Satirising the dead is judged as satirising the living. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16812}

## Mutual abuse and discipline
If people abuse each other, the sin falls on the one who began, and retaliation is allowed to the extent of the wrong. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16813} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16814}
The imam may discipline one who satirises people without right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16814}
