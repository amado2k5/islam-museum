## The kinds of abscess in the canthus
An abscess may appear in the eye's canthus: sometimes hard, moving under touch and not bursting, of the gland kind, most often seen as a protrusion in the canthus hurt by pressing, its pressing painful, with much ophthalmia; sometimes a pustular abscess gathering then bursting and mostly becoming a fistula. Both share that each loosens under touch, sinks when pressed and protrudes when released; the substance and protrusion of this pustule may be deep so that no protrusion shows outside, but the itch indicates it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.181}

## The definition of gharb and what it leads to
The gharb is a fistula occurring in the eye's inner canthus, mostly after an abscess called akhilus; because that organ is thin in substance it leads from its inside to its outside like a socket found on the side of the nose bone and the side of the eyeball. When it bursts it leaves behind — or makes hard to heal — a fistula, because the organ is moist and, with its moisture, ever-moving. Its bursting may be outward, or inward to right or left, or to both sides; often it bursts toward the nose and flows into it; the malignity of its pus may reach the bone, corrupt it, blacken it then eat it, corrupt the lid's cartilages, and fill the eye with pus brought out by pressing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.181} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.182}
