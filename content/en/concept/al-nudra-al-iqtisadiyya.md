## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat presents the economic problem as depicted by modern economists, namely the depletion and finiteness of resources, or what is called "commodity scarcity" {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 57}. He then argues that this problem did not arise by chance, but has causes that he identifies in seven respects.

## The Seven Causes
First: the multiplicity and growth of human desires, which reflects the structure of modern societies in extravagance and waste, especially in luxuries {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 57}. Second: confusing basic and non-basic needs, as some people do not attain what satisfies their basic needs while others go far beyond them {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 57}. Third: the maldistribution of production among its partners, so that some seize most of it while others receive only a paltry share {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 57}. Fourth: the misuse of raw resources without regard for future generations, such as the misuse of oil {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}. Fifth: excessive greed and fear of a commodity running out {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}. Sixth: monopoly and the hoarding of wealth, both already explained {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}. Seventh: exorbitant profits, which the author views as the "problem of problems" because they drive people to conceal goods or dump them into the sea {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}.

## The Author's View on Scarcity
The author states that the issue of scarcity does not exist in Islamic thought, citing the Qur'anic statement that blessings cannot be counted [[passage/quran-14-34|Ibrahim 34]], that provision is sent down in a known measure [[passage/quran-15-21|al-Hijr 21]], and that the disorder in Islamic societies is the making of Western colonialism and people's ingratitude for blessings {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 59}. This is a view based on a reading of texts rather than economic measurement, and is presented as his opinion.
