## Summary
In the tenth chapter Ibn Sina separates outer swellings from inner ones, gives the signs of hot ones and puts off the cold ones to the discussion of the individual organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.

## Outer and inner
Outer swellings are shown by sensation and sight. A hot inner swelling is shown by continuous fever and heaviness if the organ has no sensation, or heaviness with a stabbing pain if it has; helping signs are damage to that organ's functions and a feeling of swelling in its region {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.

## Cold swellings and swellings in the nerves
A cold swelling is not necessarily painful and its general signs are hard to indicate, so he defers it to the organ by organ discussion. If heaviness is felt without pain and the signs of dominant phlegm accompany it, one may guess it phlegmatic; if the signs of black bile accompany it, it is of black bile, above all when it is hard. When hot swellings are in the nerves, pain is severe, fevers strong, thought confused, and the movements of contraction and extension are harmed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.
