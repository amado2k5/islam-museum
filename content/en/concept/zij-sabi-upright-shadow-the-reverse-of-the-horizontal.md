## Summary
Al-Battani says the upright shadow, the standing one, is the reverse of the horizontal: it is longest at noon and shortest at sunrise. It is found from the altitude by multiplying the chord of the altitude by the gnomon's parts and dividing by the chord of the altitude's complement, and the altitude is found from it conversely through the root of the sum of the two squares. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.36}.

## The standing shadow from the altitude
He says the upright shadow, the standing one, is the reverse of the horizontal, being longest at noon and shortest at sunrise. To get it from the altitude, multiply the chord of the altitude by the gnomon's parts and divide by the chord of what remains of the altitude to ninety; the result is the shadow in the gnomon's parts. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.36}.

## The altitude from the standing shadow
He says: to find the altitude from this standing shadow, multiply the shadow by itself and add the gnomon's parts multiplied by themselves, then take the root, the diameter of the shadow's triangle. Then, if you wish, multiply the gnomon's parts by the radius and divide by the triangle's diameter; the arc is what lies between the Sun's degree and the zenith on the circle of altitude, and taken from ninety it gives the altitude. Or multiply the shadow by the radius and divide by the triangle's diameter, and the arc is the altitude itself. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.36}.
