## Overview
An empty, decorated litter carried on a camel in the pilgrimage caravan, symbolising the sovereign's care for the Hijaz. The best known was the Egyptian mahmal until the middle of the twentieth century. {cite:wikidata}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam defines the mahmal as a palanquin that symbolised the protection which the rulers of the Ottoman Empire, Egypt, Syria and Yemen gave to pilgrim caravans travelling from their lands to Mecca. In the years 1266 to 1952, it adds, the mahmal carried the kiswah too, the cloth for covering the Kaaba, usually embroidered with Qur'anic verses and set with gold and jewels, so that the ruler's protection reached the Kaaba and Islam as well. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1384}
