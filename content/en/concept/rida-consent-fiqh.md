## Summary
Rida' in the lexicon is contentment and a glad heart; in the Hanafi usage it is the choice brought to its full end so that its effect shows, and for the majority it is intent to do an act without coercion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8752} The Hanafis distinguish choice from consent: a choice is valid under coercion short of compulsion, while consent is defective under it, so marriage, divorce and return are valid without consent in their view; the majority hold intent and consent the basis of all contracts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8753} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8754} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8755} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8756} The lawful taking of another's property depends on consent, by the verse 'unless it be trade by mutual consent'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8757} Defects of consent are coercion, ignorance, error, deception and exploitation; some make a contract void and others only open to annulment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8758} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8759} Consent is inward and is expressed by word, act, writing, sign, or silence in a place where speech is needed; a virgin's silence counts as consent to marriage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8759} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8760} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8761}
