## Nature
Known, and from its leaf, when crushed, a strong gum may come out; the editor of the book says in his note that khilaf is al-safsaf {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Actions and properties
Its fruit and leaf are astringent without sting and have sufficient drying, its ash is strongly drying, applied fresh as a poultice it stops the flow of blood, and its leaf may be crushed so that a strongly subtilising gum comes out of it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Cosmetics
Its ash removes warts, applied with vinegar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Wounds and ulcers
A poultice for wounds that fall on bones, especially its fruit and leaf, and its ash removes namla when it is applied with vinegar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Organs of the head
Its blossoms and water calm headache, and the juice of its leaf, nothing is more effective than it for the sores of pus that flow from the ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Ocular organs
Its fruit and water are put on a blow to the pupil, and its gum is of great benefit to weak sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Organs of nourishment
Its water benefits obstructions of the liver and jaundice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}

## Excretory organs
Its fruit benefits those with flux of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.712}
