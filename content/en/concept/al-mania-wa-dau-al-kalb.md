## Definition
The meaning of mania is beastly madness; the editor's footnote glosses it as madness and raving. As for the "dog illness" (dau al-kalb), it is a kind of it, with anger mixed with play and mischief and harm mixed with soliciting, as is the nature of dogs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101}

## The matter
Know that the effective matter of beastly madness is of the substance of the effective matter of melancholia, for both are black-bilious, except that the effective matter of beastly madness is black bile burnt from yellow bile or from black bile, and it is the worse; the effective matter of melancholia is much natural black bile or burnt, but from phlegm or from sweet blood, and seldom from burnt phlegm and madness, though melancholia may come from it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101} Most melancholia comes from the black matter being in the vessels, and most mania from its being in the front of the brain and its substance, for its reaching the brain is like the reaching of the matter of qaranitus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101}

## Differences
Melancholia is with ill supposing, corrupt thought, fear and stillness, and has no severe agitation; mania is all agitation, leaping, mischief, beastliness and a look unlike the look of people, most like the look of beasts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101} It is set apart from a kind of qaranitus that resembles it in the madness of its sufferer in that this illness is mostly without fever, while qaranitus is never free of it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101} The "dog illness" is a kind of mania with severe obstinacy and contrariness together with accommodation and agreement, with not all the ill belief that is in mania, and as if nearer to the sanguine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101}

## Season and what resolves it
This illness mostly comes in autumn through the badness of the humours, and may be frequent in spring and summer, and at the blowing of the north wind it has a flare from the drying of the north wind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101} It is often resolved by piles and varices, and if dropsy follows it, it is resolved by the moisture of the dropsy, especially if its cause was the heat and dryness of the liver; and this illness often arises from the sharing of the stomach, and vomiting cures it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.101}
