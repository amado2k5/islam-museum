## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the penalty for wilfully killing a believer is Jahannam, with no expiation, and that the punishment has grades. He reports that scholars disagreed about whether a wilful killer can repent, and that Ibn Abbas gave two different answers according to the questioner's state {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2549}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2550}.

## What Sha'rawi says
He holds that wilfulness means the killer lived the idea of killing in his imagination before doing it, so his inner restraint was absent, and whoever is absent from God's mind is absent from His mercy {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2549}. He tells the story of Miqyas ibn Dubaba, whose blood the Prophet declared lawful to shed, and who was ordered killed at the conquest when found holding the Ka'ba's curtains {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2549}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2550}.

## Repentance of a wilful killer
Sha'rawi reports that scholars disagreed: some said there is no repentance for him, and others that there is {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2550}. He reports Ibn Abbas answering one questioner before the killing that there is no repentance, and another after the killing that there is, because the first was intent on killing and was frightened, while the second had killed and was not left in despair {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2550}. He also reports Amr ibn Ubayd saying there is no repentance for a wilful killer, and later reconsidering after a vision {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2552}. He does not name the scholars who disagreed, and gives no chain for these answers.
