## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter 3, on when to begin and stop exercise, by requiring a body clear of raw chymes, the evening meal digested and the time of another meal come, which the maturity of the urine in consistency and colour shows; he judges exercising full less harmful than exercising empty {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.224}.

## Conditions for starting
When one begins exercise the body must be clean, with no raw bad chymes in the region of the viscera and vessels for exercise to spread through the body, and the previous evening's meal digested in stomach, liver and vessels, the time of another meal come. The maturity of the urine in consistency and colour shows this, and that is the first time of this digestion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.224}.

## When the last meal is long past
If the meal is long past and the innate faculty has been idle in it for a time, and the fiery quality has flared in the urine past the natural yellow, exercise harms. It was said that when the situation calls for hard exercise the stomach should not be quite empty but hold a little food, thick in winter and light in summer, that exercising full is better than exercising empty, and that exercising warm or moist is better than exercising with the body cold or dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.224}.
