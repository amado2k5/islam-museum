## Definition
Ilham in the lexical sense is the verb "to inspire": God inspired him with good, meaning He caused it to occur to him. It is God putting into the soul a matter that moves to act or to refrain, a kind of revelation He grants to whom He wills. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3327}
For the usulis it is a thing cast on the heart that quiets the breast, reserved by God for some of His chosen servants. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3327}

## Related terms
- Whispering (waswasa): a thought put into the soul through a cause that the devil started. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3327}
- Careful search (tahari): effort and thought; ilham comes without effort. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3327}

## Ruling
The usulis agree that the ilham of the prophets is true; it is binding on them and on their community. One who denies its reality is an unbeliever, and one who leaves it unpractised is a sinner. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3327}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3328}
For most scholars the ilham of others is not binding, since one who is not protected from error cannot be trusted in his impulses. Some hold it binding on the one inspired alone, if no text or reasoning contradicts it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3328}
