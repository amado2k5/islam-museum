## Summary
Ibn Sina gives practical rules: pain that comes with a disease is eased first, someone with an acutely sensitive organ is fed what thickens the blood, help is taken from whatever strengthens the powers of the soul, the patient is set postures and movements that straighten the organ, and a change from one country to another is sometimes of use {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.271}.

## Pain and sensitivity
When a disease comes with pain or a cause of pain such as a blow or a fall, the pain is eased first, and if numbing is needed it does not go beyond something like poppy, which, besides numbing, is familiar and eaten. If an organ is very sensitive, the patient is fed what thickens the blood greatly, such as harisa, or, if the regimen is not feared, cooling foods such as lettuce {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.271}.

## The soul, posture and change of place
A good, effective treatment is to draw on what strengthens the powers of the soul, and keeping company with dignified people before whom one feels shy may keep the patient from things that harm him. Close to this are moving from one country or air to another, and taking on postures and movements that straighten an organ, as a squinting child is made to look hard at something held before him, and a person with facial palsy to look in a narrow mirror to set his face and eyes straight, so that effort may restore soundness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.271}.
