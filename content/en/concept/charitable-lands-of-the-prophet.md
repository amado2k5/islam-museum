## Summary
At his death the Prophet left lands which the historians did not count as an estate to be inherited but as charity to be spent in its proper ways. The key text is the long report of 'Umar, narrated by Malik ibn Aws, kept as [[passage/bukhari-4033|al-Bukhari 4033]] with its commentary in Fath al-Bari and its presentation in al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4033} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 315} This page gives the Sunni account of these lands and how they were run after him; the dispute over the claim to inheritance has its own record: [[event/fatima-inheritance-claim-fadak|Fatima's claim to the Prophet's inheritance and Fadak]].

## The three special properties
Fath al-Bari, from Abu Dawud's version, says the Messenger of God had three special properties (safaya): Banu al-Nadir, Khaybar and Fadak. Banu al-Nadir was set aside for his needs and Fadak for wayfarers; Khaybar he divided among the Muslims, then set a part for his family's maintenance and put the surplus to the poor among the Emigrants. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 238} Ibn Hajar sets this beside the report of 'Amr ibn Dinar that the property of Banu al-Nadir was his alone, that he spent a year's maintenance on his family from it and put the rest into weapons and horses, and sees no conflict between them. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 238} Ibn Kathir adds, from 'Umar, that these were goods God had given to his Messenger as fay, for which the Muslims had driven neither horse nor camel. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 447}

## How they came to him
For Fadak, Ibn Hisham reports from Ibn Ishaq that its people, hearing what had befallen Khaybar, sent to make peace for half the land; he accepted, and it was his alone because no horse or camel had been driven against it. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 522} For Khaybar, Ibn Kathir says the Jews asked to stay and work the land, and he gave them the land for half the crop and the date palms, with 'Abd Allah ibn Rawaha coming each year to estimate the yield. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 640} Ibn Hisham says he divided the Katiba among his kin, his wives and men and women of the Muslims. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 521} Ibn Kathir adds, in the same report, that he gave each wife eighty wasq of dates and twenty of barley every year. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 640}

## What Abu Bakr did
After his death Fatima and al-'Abbas asked for his land at Fadak and his share of Khaybar. Abu Bakr told them he had heard the Messenger of God say that we are not inherited, what we leave is charity, and that the family of Muhammad eat from this property; and that, by God, he would not leave aside anything he had seen the Prophet do with it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 313} In Ahmad's report Fatima asks him who will inherit from him; he says his children and family, and she asks why they do not inherit from the Messenger of God; he says he heard the Prophet say a prophet is not inherited, but that he supports whom the Messenger of God supported and spends on whom he spent on. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 317} Ibn Kathir also sets out what is said of Fatima's anger and later contentment; that is covered by the event record linked above. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 317}

## 'Umar, 'Ali and al-'Abbas
In Malik ibn Aws's report, 'Ali and al-'Abbas come to 'Umar while 'Uthman, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, al-Zubayr and Sa'd are with him. 'Umar asks the group by God whether they know that the Messenger of God said we are not inherited, what we leave is charity, and they say yes; he asks 'Ali and al-'Abbas and they say yes. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 315} He then says God had given his Messenger something of this fay that He gave to no one else, and that the Prophet spent on his family from it for a year and put the rest where the property of God goes. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 315} Abu Bakr took charge and did as the Messenger of God had done; 'Umar himself held it two years. Then the two came, one asking his share from his nephew and the other his wife's share from her father. 'Umar says he gave it to them on condition that they act on it as the Messenger of God, Abu Bakr and he had, swore he would give no other judgment until the Hour, and said that if they could not manage it they should return it to him. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 315} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 316}

## Ibn Kathir's reading of what they asked
Ibn Kathir says that what they asked, once the management had been entrusted to them, was that it be divided between them, and thinks 'Umar shrank from dividing the management in a way that resembled dividing an inheritance, even in appearance, so as to keep to the words we are not inherited, and refused. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 316} He adds that 'Ali and al-'Abbas went on managing it together until 'Uthman's time, when 'Ali prevailed over it and al-'Abbas left it to him on the advice of his son 'Abd Allah, as Ahmad reports in his Musnad; it then stayed with the descendants of 'Ali. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 316} This is Ibn Kathir's reading and the Sunni presentation in the books I read; I found no other account of these details in the scanned pages.

## What the reports show
They agree that before his death the Prophet had divided what God gave him as fay into two: a year's upkeep for his household, and the rest for the Muslims' readiness and interests. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 315} They agree that Abu Bakr and 'Umar did not change this arrangement, and that 'Umar handed the Medina land to 'Ali and al-'Abbas as a trust of management, not as ownership. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 316} For the legal sense of fay and endowment see [[concept/fay|Fay]] and [[concept/waqf|Waqf]]; for all that the Prophet left, see [[concept/estate-of-the-prophet|What the Prophet left at his death]].
