## Summary
Hadiyya is a thing of value sent to another as an honour; technically it is the transfer of a thing without return, a kind of gift (hiba) carried to the recipient as a courtesy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16507} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16508}

## Validity and retraction
A gift is complete when the giver sends it and the recipient takes it; no formula is required. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16511}
The majority allow the giver to retract before receipt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16511} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16512}
Where a gift comes in a container, the container is returned or held in trust depending on custom. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16511} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16512}

## Gifts at a circumcision or wedding
Gifts suited to children belong to the child; others follow the kinship of the giver, to the father or the mother. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16512} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16513}

## Gifts to officials and judges
Gifts that officials receive by virtue of office are forbidden, on the basis of the saying 'the gifts of officials are plunder'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16513} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16514}
The imam may not accept gifts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16515}
The mufti, the preacher and the teacher may accept, though refraining is better. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16515} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16516}

## Other cases
Gifts from non-Muslims in war are spoils; those given outside war belong to the recipient. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16517}
A gift given out of fear or shame is forbidden, as it is in the nature of seizure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16517}
