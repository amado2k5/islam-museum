## Definition
Inthar is informing a person of a matter; it is most often used for warning and frightening of what is evil. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3466}
It differs from i'dhar (the judge asking the one bound whether he has anything that cancels the liability), from nabdh (informing an enemy that a truce is ended), and from munashada (warning with an appeal). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3466}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3467}

## Ruling
- Obligatory: warning a blind man from falling into a well; warning unbelievers who have not heard the call before fighting; warning an apostate, in the view of those who require it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3467}
- Recommended: calling unbelievers who have heard the call to Islam; giving the apostate three days to repent; warning the imam in prayer when he intends to leave what is recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3467}
- Permitted: admonishing a rebellious wife by words or otherwise, or warning the owner of a leaning wall. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3467}
- Forbidden: where the warning causes greater harm than the wrong it prevents. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3467}

## Means
Warning is by speech, as in admonishing disputants, asking an apostate to repent, or calling unbelievers to Islam; it is by act where speech is not allowed in prayer, such as a gesture to warn of a scorpion; and by hand for one who has the power. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3468}

## Who may warn
Warning is mostly a warning of harm or of what is not lawful, and every Muslim has the right to it within commanding right and forbidding wrong, which is a communal obligation. It is an obligation for the officer of hisba, and the husband, teacher and father hold a right of hisba. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3468}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3469}
