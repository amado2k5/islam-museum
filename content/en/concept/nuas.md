## Summary
Nu'as, in the lexicon, is the first of sleep or light sleep: the senses weaken without being lost, and a sign of it is hearing what is said nearby. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15795}
Sleep is a domination of the mind through the loss of the senses; drowsiness is a weakening of them; fainting is an ailment that disables the perceiving and moving faculties. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15795} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15796}

## Rulings on drowsiness
Effect on ablution: most jurists hold that drowsiness does not break it, and recommend renewing it. The Hanafis separate heavy drowsiness, which breaks ablution, from light drowsiness, which does not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15796}
Drowsiness in the mosque on Friday: most jurists recommend moving from the place; some Shafi'is dislike remaining seated while drowsy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15796}

## Where sleep is treated
Sleep is treated in its own entry, since most of the rules on sleep and ablution are discussed there. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15796}
