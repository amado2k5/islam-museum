## Overview
*Ikrah* (compulsion) is forcing another, by a certain threat, to an act or a statement. It occurs in the hadith of Ibn Abbas, the thirty-ninth of the Forty, among the three things for which God has pardoned the community: "and what they were compelled to do." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.796}

## In the commentary
Ibn Rajab notes that the rulings on compulsion differ according to where it applies. In the word of unbelief, one who is compelled while his heart remains at rest in faith is excepted. In transactions and statements it has an effect on validity and binding force. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.796}

What unites them is that compulsion negates the purposeful consent, so the ruling does not settle on the one compelled as it settles on one who chooses. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.796}

The hadith is connected to the chapter on [[concept/khata-nisyan|error and forgetting]]. The verse text is not given here. The details of the schools are not recorded here and need a further source.
