## Summary
Ibn Sina completes the diseases of composition with diseases of association, then gives the fourth chapter to diseases of broken continuity and lists their names by organ, direction and depth: scratch, abrasion, wound, ulcer, broken bone, severed nerve, rupture, hernia and dislocation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.105-107}.

## Diseases of association
Diseases of association include every state of an organ, in relation to a neighbouring organ, of nearness or distance not on the natural course. They are of two kinds: the organ is prevented from moving toward the other, or finds it hard, after it was possible, as a finger is prevented from moving to touch its neighbour; or it is prevented from moving away and parting from it, or finds it hard to draw apart, as in slackness of the eyelid and slackness of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.105-106}.

## In skin, flesh and bone
Breaks of continuity may occur in the skin and are called scratch and abrasion; in flesh and what is close to it, without pus, they are called a wound, and with pus an ulcer, the pus arising because matters are driven to the organ in its weakness and inability to use its food and digest it, so that surplus turns in it. They may occur in bone, which may be broken in two or into large pieces, crushed, or split along its length; in cartilage, in the same three ways; or in nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.106}.

## In nerve, muscle and vessels
If it falls across a nerve it is called a severing; along its length without great depth, a split; deep, a crushing. At the end of a muscle, whether in a sinew or tendon, it is called a tearing; across the muscle, a cutting; lengthwise with few parts and great depth, a rending; with many parts spreading and deep, a bruising; and loosely the words wrenching, bruising and rending are used of whatever occurs in the middle of a muscle. In the arteries or veins it is called a rupture, and when the blood poured out fills the space that holds it and returns to the vessel on squeezing, it is called the mother of blood, the aneurysm. Not every organ can bear dissolution of unity: the heart cannot, and death goes with it. In membranes and coverings it is called a hernia; between two parts of a composite organ, separating one from the other, it is called separation or dislocation, and in a nerve that has moved from its place, unhinging {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.106-107}.

## Healing
A break of continuity may be in the channels and widen them, or outside them and make channels that were not there. Breaking of continuity and ulceration heal quickly in an organ of good temperament and resist for a time in one of bad temperament, especially in the bodies of people with dropsy, poor condition or leprosy. Summer ulcers, if prolonged, are overtaken by the eating ulcer; the reader will find the full account in the detailed books {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.107}.
