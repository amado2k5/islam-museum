## Summary
Ibn Sina distinguishes condensation from cold and astringency, whose sign is pallor, slow warming and sweating and a return of the red colour on exercise, from that due to exercise, whose sign is the absence of those signs and a dirty skin, and from that due to dust or hard rubbing, and describes a remedy for each {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.246-247}.

## From cold and astringency
Where it comes from cold and astringency the sign is pallor, slowness in warming and in sweating, and a return of red colour on exercise. These people should bathe in a bath, and the text goes on to say they roll upon its tiers of moderate heat and on its bedding until they sweat, and anoint themselves with fine, hot, dissolving oils. The words at the start of this passage are garbled and are not taken {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.246-247}.

## From exercise, dust or rubbing
Those who fall into it through exercise show none of those signs and have a dirty skin; the remedy is to shake out any excess and to use what dissolves, such as the bath and anointing. Those who fall into it through dust or the force of rubbing need bathing more than anointing with oils, and should rub themselves gently before the bath and after it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.247}.
