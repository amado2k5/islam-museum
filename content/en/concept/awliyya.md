## Definition
Awliyya is lexically the state of one thing being more fitting than another: more deserving, nearer and more apt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3690}

## Usage
- The usulis and jurists use awla in the sense of better or more fitting, without the comparative form, since its opposite, khilaf al-awla, carries a slight dislike. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3690}
- They use it in the sense of having the right to a thing over others. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3690}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3691}
- They call a light recommendation awla, and the leaving of a recommended act contrary to the better course; the Hanafis hold that whoever does so has done ill. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3691}

## Kinds of indication and analogy
- Fahwa al-khitab: a ruling of what is stated extended to what is not stated, through the cause understood from the language; the prohibition of a word of contempt implies the prohibition of beating. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3691}
- Qiyas al-awla: the analogy in which the new case has a stronger claim to the ruling than the original; it is a kind of clear analogy for those who hold it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3691}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3692}

## The phrase "there is no harm"
For the Hanafis the phrase "there is no harm" usually signals that the leaving of the act is the better course, though it may be used of a recommended act. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3692}
