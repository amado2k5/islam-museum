## Summary
Taʿlim 1 of Fann 3, on upbringing, opens with chapter 1, the management of the newborn until it stands. Ibn Sina reports what a group of learned men said: cut the cord four fingers from the body, tie it with clean wool, powder it with a drug, and salt the body with thin brine to firm the skin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.203-204}.

## Cutting the cord
A group of learned men said one must begin by cutting the newborn's cord four fingers above, tying it with clean wool twisted gently so it does not hurt, and laying on it a cloth dipped in oil and cumin. Among what was ordered at the cutting is to take equal parts of yellow roots, dragon's blood, sarcocolla, lichen and myrrh, grind them and sprinkle them on the cord {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.203}.

## Salting
He hurries to salt the body with thin brine so that the skin is bound and its covering strengthened; the best salts are mixed with a little hematite, costus, sumac, fenugreek and thyme, and the nose and mouth are not salted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.203-204}. The reason for preferring a firmer body is that at first the baby is hurt by anything that touches it as rough and cold, because its skin is thin and its heat high, so everything seems cold, hard and rough to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.

## After salting and when the cord falls
If the baby is very dirty and moist the salting is repeated; then it is washed in lukewarm water, the nostrils are cleaned with fingers with trimmed nails, a little oil is dropped in the eyes, the anus is tickled with the little finger so that it opens, and the baby is kept from cold. When the cord falls after three or four days it is best to sprinkle on it ash of shell, ash of calf's heel or burnt lead, ground, with wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.
