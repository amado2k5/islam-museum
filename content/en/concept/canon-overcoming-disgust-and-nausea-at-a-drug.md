## Summary
Ibn Sina gives devices for someone who loathes a drug or fears nausea: smelling mint, rue, celery and quince, stopping the nostrils, chewing tarragon, coating pills in honey or sugar, smearing them with cerate, or swallowing them after filling the mouth with water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.280}.

## The devices
One who is to take a drug should smell scents that prevent nausea, such as mint, rue, celery, quince and Khurasani clay sprinkled with rose water and a little wine vinegar; if he recoils at the smell of the drug when drinking, he stops his nostrils. Whoever loathes the drug should chew some tarragon until it numbs the strength of his mouth, and if he fears vomiting he binds his limbs and takes an astringent after drinking. Physicians may coat the pills in honey or run a honey or sugar syrup over them, and smearing them with cerate is another good device; the finest is to fill the mouth with water or something else and drink the pill upon it, so that all is swallowed without the drug's taste showing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.280}.
