## Overview
Tadmir is feeding a horse until it is fat and strong, then reducing its feed, stabling it and covering it with cloths until it sweats, so its flesh lightens and it can run. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5297}

## Definition
Damr is leanness and a tucked belly; the Arab period for tadmir is forty days, and the place of training is called a training ground. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5297}

## Related terms
Racing (sibaq and musabaqa) has the same sense, and sabq with a vowel is the stake raced for; tadmir is sometimes done to win a race. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5297}

## Ruling
Most jurists allow tadmir without restriction, and recommend it for horses kept for war. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5298} The hadith of Ibn Umar: the Prophet raced horses that had been trained from al-Hafya to Thaniyyat al-Wada, and untrained ones from Thaniyyat al-Wada to the mosque of Banu Zurayq, and Ibn Umar raced among them. This answers the objection that training is unlawful for its hardship. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5298}

## Cross-reference
Whether training is a condition of racing, and the difference of distances between trained and untrained horses, belongs in the entry on racing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5298}
