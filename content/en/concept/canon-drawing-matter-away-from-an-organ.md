## Summary
Ibn Sina distinguishes matter that has already poured wholly into an organ from matter still pouring, and for the second says it is drawn from its place once conditions are met, the first being the opposite direction, as from right to left and from above to below, the second regard for sympathy, then keeping the two places far apart, and the pain of the organ drawn from is soothed first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.269}.

## The conditions and examples
While matter is still pouring it is drawn off once four conditions are met, among them the opposite direction, regard for sympathy, and keeping the two places far apart so that the place drawn to is not too near the place drawn from. The page gives examples: the right basilic vein in liver ailments and the left basilic in spleen ailments, venesection of the saphenous vein in ailments of the womb, and the vein under the tongue for swelling of the tonsils. When drawing to the opposite side is wanted, the pain of the organ drawn from is first soothed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.269}.
