## Summary
Ibn Sina lists as causes of a drug falling short narrowness of the channels by make, temperament or a neighbouring illness, as in palsy, warns against two purgatives in one day, says a drug for a particular humour that does not find it disturbs and purges with difficulty, purges first what it is specific to and then what follows, but holds blood back, and says drawing a distant humour is hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.281}.

## The causes
Among the causes of a drug falling short is narrowness of the channels, whether from the person's make, from temperament or from an illness lying nearby; in palsy the channels by which drugs reach their destinations are narrow, so purging them is hard. Putting two purgatives together in one day is dangerous and wrong. A drug specific to one humour, if it does not find it, disturbs and purges with difficulty, and so it does when it finds the humour drowned among its opposites. Every drug purges first the humour it is specific to, then the next in abundance, scantiness and thinness in that order, except blood, which it leaves for last and which nature holds back. Drawing a distant humour is hard. If a long time passes and the drug has not begun to purge, he may ease off without stirring anything, and if he fears something it is right to sip honey water or its syrup, or water in which natron is dissolved, or to use a suppository or an enema {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.281}.
