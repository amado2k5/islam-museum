## The curable and the unhoped
Palsy arising from a displaced vertebra is curable in most cases; that from a shock that did not badly crush the nerve may heal, but if it went too far it is not expected to heal; the one hoped for must begin with venesection {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.139}

## Transitions into it
Palsy may be arrived at from apoplexy, from epilepsy, from colic and from chronic fevers, also by way of crisis {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.139}

## Womb suffocation (labelled listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book counts suffocation of the wombs among the things from which palsy follows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.139}

## Its pulse and urine
The palsied man's pulse is weak, slow and varying; if the illness exhausts the power the pulse weakens, quickens and unordered dropped beats occur; the urine is mostly white, sometimes very watery for the liver's or the vessels' weakness, or for a pain with it, or another concomitant disease {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.138}
