## Nature
It is the gum of wild rue; the book gave it before this, in the letter ta, without the tha {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.698}

## Choice
It is of use only fresh, and when time has passed over it it weakens and is not of use, as the superfluous moistures in it have dissolved {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.698}

## Temperament
Very hot and burning, strong in heating and drying, with a strange superfluous moisture because of which it does not sting at once {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.698}

## Actions and properties
It cleanses, purges, ripens and bursts; because of its superfluous moisture it does not burn at once but after a while, it draws violently from the depth of the body though after a time, and has no equal in changing the temperament toward heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.698}

## Cosmetics
It grows hair and benefits alopecia greatly, and rarely is an equal found for it in that; it benefits blood-bruise marks, and likewise scars, freckles and baras {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.698}

## Joints
It is rubbed on slackness, on gout and on cold joints, and used as an enema for sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.698}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits the spitting of pus, difficult breathing and pain of the two sides, especially the old pains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.699}

## Excretory organs
In its root, bark and tear there is loosening of the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.699}

## Fevers
The book gives amounts for its bark, juice and tear, left out here, and says that taking much of it harms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.699}

## Substitutes
The book names tragacanth and hurf as its substitute, the amount left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.699}

## End of the letter
This is the end of the discussion of the letter tha, and the number of them is seven drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.699}
