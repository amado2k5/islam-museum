## Summary
Ruqba in the lexicon comes from watching; one says: I grant you this house as a ruqba: if I die first it returns to me, and if you die first it is yours and your heirs'. For the Malikis each says to the other: if I die first your house is mine, and if you die first mine is yours. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8851} It is close to the 'umra, to gift, to loan and to the milk-ewe. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8851} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8852} It was known in the pre-Islamic period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8852} The Shafi'is, Hanbalis and Abu Yusuf hold it valid, and the condition of return void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8852} Abu Hanifa and Muhammad hold it void, as the making of ownership hangs on a doubtful event; the Malikis treat it as a loan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8852} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8853}
