## Definition
A letter's makhraj is the place in the vocal tract from which its sound issues; it is what distinguishes one letter from another. Ibn al-Jazari states in the Jazariyya that knowing the makharij and the sifat is a duty on the reciter of the Qur'an before he begins {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## The count and the chapters
Ibn al-Jazari sets the makharij at seventeen, "as the expert chooses", arranged in five chapters {cite:ar-wikisource}: the empty space of the mouth and throat (al-jawf), holding the alef and its two sisters waw and ya — the letters of madd that end in air; the throat (al-halq) with three points: its deepest part for the hamza and the ha, its middle for the ayn and the ha, its nearest part for the ghayn and the kha; the tongue (al-lisan) with ten: its deepest part for the qaf, its lowest for the kaf, its middle for the jim, the shin and the non-madd ya, the dad from its edge, the lam from its nearest to its tip, the nun from its tip beneath, the ra close to that, the ta, dal and ta from it with the upper incisors, and the sibilant letters sad, sin and zay from between its two edges; the lips (al-shafatan) for the waw, ba and mim, the fa joining them with the edges of the upper incisors; and the nasal passage (al-khayshum) for the ghunna.

## In the tradition
Later writers counted between fourteen and seventeen makharij; Ibn al-Jazari's count of seventeen is the one the later tajwid manuals settled on, and al-Shatibi's Shatibiyya likewise treats the makharij and sifat before anything else {cite:matn-shatibiyya-ghawthani-2010}.
