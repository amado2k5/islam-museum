## Summary
In the rulings the dhimma is a quality in a person, or a self with an obligation, by which he is fit to bear rights for and against him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8482}
It lasts from conception to death, each person has one, and it has no limit of capacity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8484} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8485}

## Its senses
Jurists use dhimma for covenant, safe-conduct and guarantee, and some principalists use it for the capacity to be owed rights. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8482} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8483}
Capacity to bear obligations has two sides, the fitness to have rights held for one and the fitness to have them held against one, and the second needs the dhimma. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8483} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8484}

## Its properties
The dhimma attaches to the person, not to his property, so a debt does not stop the debtor trading except by a legal bar such as a pledge or a restraint. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8485}
Debts in the dhimma are, in principle, equal, and one does not rank above another by being earlier. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8485}

## Death
The majority hold that the dhimma survives death until the estate's rights are settled, some Hanafis that it is weakened, and a Hanbali narration that it ends at death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8485} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8486} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8487}
