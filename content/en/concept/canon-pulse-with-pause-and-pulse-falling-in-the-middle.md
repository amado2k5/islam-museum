## Summary
Ibn Sina distinguishes the pulse that falls in the middle from the gazelle pulse, then names the spasmodic, the trembling and the taut, and the compounds that are almost without limit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.173}.

## How it differs from the gazelle
The difference between the pulse falling in the middle and the gazelle pulse is that in the gazelle the second beat catches up before the first has ended, while in the one falling in the middle the intruding beat comes in the time of rest after the first knock has ended {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.173}.

## The spasmodic, the trembling and the taut
Of these kinds are the spasmodic and the trembling pulse, uneven in earliness and lateness, in position and in breadth. The taut is a genus within the twisted and resembles the thrilling pulse, except that its expansion is less evident, as is its departure from evenness of position in rise, while its stretching is plain and its lean is perhaps to one side only. Taut, twisted and side-leaning pulses occur mostly in dry diseases. Of compound pulses there are kinds almost without end that have no names {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.173}.
