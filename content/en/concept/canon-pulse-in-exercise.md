## Summary
In the thirteenth chapter Ibn Sina describes how the pulse changes through moderate exercise, then long or severe exercise, down to the ant-like pulse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.180}.

## The start of moderate exercise
At the start of exercise, while it stays moderate, the pulse grows great and strong because the innate heat increases and gathers strength, and it grows very quick and frequent for the excess of need that the movement brings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.180}.

## Long or excessive exercise
If exercise lasts, grows long or is severe, what power brings about fails; the pulse turns weak and small as the innate heat dissolves, yet it stays quick and frequent for two reasons, the sole rule of need and the failure of power to meet greatness. Speed then keeps diminishing and frequency keeps growing in step with the weakening of power, and in the end, if exercise goes on and exhausts, the pulse becomes ant-like from weakness and extreme frequency. If it is so excessive that it nears destruction, it does what dissolutions do, slowness with weakness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.180}.
