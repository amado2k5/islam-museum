## Overview
Imam Ahmad said in his epistle that the Qur'an is the speech of God, that what God has spoken is not created, and that what is in the codex and in people's recitation is the speech of God, uncreated. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.53} He added that whoever says the Qur'an is created is a disbeliever, and whoever does not declare him a disbeliever is also a disbeliever. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.53} The author notes that the scholars agreed that the Jahmiyya split into three groups over the question of the Qur'an. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.54} One group said the Qur'an is the speech of God and that it is created; another said the Qur'an is the speech of God and remained silent on whether it is created or uncreated; and a third said the words of the Qur'an are created. {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p.54}

## In al-Lalaka'i's Sharh Usul I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna
In *[[work/sharh-usul-itiqad-lalakai|Sharh Usul I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna]]*, [[person/al-lalakai|al-Lalaka'i]] lists the names of those who said that the Qur'an is God's uncreated speech and that whoever calls it created is an unbeliever. He then says that they number 550 or more Successors, their followers and imams, not counting the Companions, about a hundred of them imams, and that he dropped the chains for brevity. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 256} He states that the community agrees that the first to say the Qur'an is created was [[person/jad-ibn-dirham|al-Ja'd ibn Dirham]], "in the years of twenty-odd", as he puts it (without naming the century), and then [[person/jahm-ibn-safwan|al-Jahm ibn Safwan]]. Al-Ja'd, he adds, was killed by Khalid ibn Abd Allah al-Qasri, and al-Jahm was killed at Merv in the caliphate of Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 256} He then gives a chapter on the rulings about whoever held that view. It names jurists such as Malik ibn Anas, Ibn Abi Layla and Sufyan ibn Uyayna, the caliph Abu Ja'far al-Mansur, and a number of hadith scholars. {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira p. 257}
