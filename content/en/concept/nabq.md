## Nature and selection
It is a great thorny tree with a fruit like the hazelnut, red in colour, eaten and pleasant in taste, found mostly in hot lands; at the edges of those lands it has names according to the tongues of the people, and some call it ketar. The editor's footnote says nabq is the fruit of the lote tree, used for identity only {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Temperament
Fresh and dry both have drying and thinning in them, and this holds in all parts of its tree; the smoke of the lote is strongly astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Actions and properties
Astringent, its meal especially {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Adornment
It stops the falling of hair, lengthens it, strengthens it and softens it; the lote has a gum that removes scurf and dandruff and reddens the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Swellings and pustules
The leaf of the lote softens the hot swelling and dissolves it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Head, ears and teeth
The gum of the lote removes burning heat when used as a wash, cleans the head and curls the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Chest and breathing
Its leaf is for asthma and diseases of the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It strengthens the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Excretory organs
It restrains the bowels and helps ulcers of the intestines, its meal especially, and helps diarrhoea that comes from weakness of the stomach. The lote is used as an enema from its decoction and drunk for these ills. The fresh fruit follows what is akin to it in quince, hawthorn, apple and pear: a moderate amount restrains, and a great amount, because it is not digested and nature drives it out, stirs up cholera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
It helps flow of menses and the bleeding of the courses, and the flow from the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.
