## Summary
Ibn Sina says that when the child wants something besides milk it is given gradually, first bread chewed by the nurse, then bread in water and honey, wine or milk; after weaning it moves to gruels and light meats, and weaning is done gradually {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.210}.

## The first food
When the child desires something besides milk it is given gradually without strain; when its front teeth begin to appear it is moved by degrees to stronger food, without being given anything hard to chew. The first is bread chewed by the nurse, then bread with water and honey, or with wine or with milk, and a little water is given with it, at times with a small amount of wine mixed in; it is not allowed to fill itself, and if satiety and swelling befall it, it is anointed and bathed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.210}.

## Weaning
When it is weaned it is moved to what is of the kind of gruels and light meats. Weaning should be gradual, not at once, and the child is occupied with small cakes made of bread and sugar. If it clings to the breast, wants to nurse and cries, a dirham each of myrrh and pennyroyal is ground and smeared on the breast {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.210}.
