## Summary
Ibn Sina says one who must eat before exercise should take only bread, enough to be digested before he moves; movement after a meal should be only light and gentle; a stout person should not eat straight after leaving the bath but wait and nap briefly; and he warns against violent movement on food and against much water over it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## Before exercise
One who needs to eat before exercise should eat bread alone, an amount digestion takes up before he begins to move. Just as movement before a meal should not be feeble, so movement after it should be only light and gentle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## Corrupt appetite
For a corrupt appetite that leans to the pungent and loathes the sweet and fatty, he says nothing mends it better than vomiting with oxymel and radish after fish {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## The stout and the bath
A stout person should not eat as he leaves the bath but wait and take a light nap; the single meal suits such people best, and one should not sleep on food that is still floating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.
