## Summary
In four short chapters Ibn Sina sets out the muscles of the testes, bladder, penis and anus, in plain anatomical description {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.73-74}.

## The testes and bladder
In men the muscles of the testes are four, made to keep the testicles and lift them so they do not hang slack, a pair for each. Women need one pair, one muscle for each testicle, since theirs do not hang down and stand out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.73}. At the mouth of the bladder there is one muscle with transverse fibre around the opening; its use is to hold the urine until it is willed. When emptying is willed it relaxes from its contraction, the abdominal muscles press the bladder and the urine runs, helped by the expelling power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.73}.

## The penis and the anus
Two pairs move the penis: one whose muscles run along its two sides and, stretched, widen the passage and straighten it so that the seed flows easily; and one growing from the pubic bone and joined obliquely to the root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.73}. The anus has four muscles: one clinging to its mouth and mixing with its flesh much as the muscles of the lip do, which closes and tightens the ring and squeezes the remains of stool out; one set deeper above it; and an oblique pair above all, whose use is to lift the anus upward; prolapse of the anus happens only through slackness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.73-74}.
