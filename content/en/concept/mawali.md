## Overview
The *mawali* (clients) in al-Baladhuri, in the account of the captives taken at 'Ayn al-Tamr, are captives whose masters freed them, so that their clientage (*wala'*) passed to them. The account is connected with [[event/siege-of-ayn-al-tamr|the siege of 'Ayn al-Tamr]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Among the examples are Humran ibn Aban, who belonged to al-Musayyib ibn Najaba, was bought by Uthman and freed, and so became his client. Sirin and his brothers were clients of Anas ibn Malik. Yasar, the grandfather of Muhammad ibn Ishaq, was a client of Qays ibn Makhrama. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Nusayr, the father of Musa ibn Nusayr, was a client of the Umayyads. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
[[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] gives the eighth episode to the contribution of non-Arabs to Islamic civilization, saying that their dense emergence began at the end of the first century AH, when the children of Persian, Berber and other fathers and mothers grew up inside Islamic civilization speaking Arabic and learning its sciences. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:05:00} He relates that [[person/hisham-ibn-abd-al-malik|Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik]], who he says reigned from 105 to 125 AH, asked [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]] who led each region, and for each region al-Zuhri named a man of the mawali, such as Ata ibn Abi Rabah in Mecca and Ibrahim al-Nakha'i in Kufa, until Hisham said the mawali would come to lead the Arabs; al-Zuhri answered that it is God's command and His religion: whoever keeps it leads and whoever loses it falls. This is his telling of the report. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:08:00} He says the Arabs ruled the Muslim state for about its first two hundred or two hundred and twenty years and non-Arabs for twelve hundred years and more after that, and that, leaving aside the age of the Companions, non-Arabs make up without exaggeration four-fifths of Islam's great figures in the religious and applied sciences, in jihad, architecture and philosophy; these are his estimates. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep08 00:23:30}
