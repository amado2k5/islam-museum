## Sharing
If the organs that share with the brain are strong, the brain is strong; if they have many afflictions with no apparent causes reaching them, the brain is weak or afflicted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.21} Afflictions in the other organs may be by sharing in an affliction of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.21}

For example, the patient does not rise to urinate or to stool when he needs to, for lack of sensation, as happens in lethargus and in the sleepless coma and the like, or because movement is heavy on him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.21} In faranitus there is inability to swallow, and choking and gagging in these diseases {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.21}

So too the signs of breath: breath may stop and fail because of an affliction in the brain that extends to the diaphragm and the organs of breath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.21} A large, wide breath is more indicative of sabar, and a narrow, small one of the sleepless coma and lethargus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.21}

The state of the brain may also be inferred by way of sharing in pains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.21-22} and from the manner of sharing; for example, if in headache the pain reaches the roots of the eyes it shows that the cause is outside the skull {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22} It may also be inferred from fullness and emptiness of the vessels and from the colour of the skin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.22}
