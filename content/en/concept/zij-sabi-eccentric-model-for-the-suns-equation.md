## Summary
Al-Battani then explains the second manner on the eccentric sphere: he draws the circle of the zodiac and upon it the eccentric circle, with a diameter through both centres on which lie the points of greatest and least distance, and reckons the variation in the same two cases, thirty and a hundred and fifty parts from the apogee, and in the case whose figures read clearly, the second, arrives at the same result {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.74-76}.

## The result and what he says
In the second case a perpendicular is dropped from the circle to the line through the two centres, so that the sine of the angle is known and the right-angled triangle has known sides; he finds the line from the first centre to the Sun's apparent place at about fifty-eight parts, twelve minutes and thirty-four seconds, and a perpendicular of one part, four minutes and seventeen seconds on the line of sixty parts, and the arc on it is the amount of the variation, so that the Sun on the zodiac stands about a hundred and fifty-one parts, one minute and twenty-four seconds from the apogee {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.76}. At the close he says that this is sufficient for this variation {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.76}. The two pictures give one value for the equation, as he said in [[concept/zij-sabi-two-pictures-of-uneven-motion-epicycle-and-eccentric|the two pictures of uneven motion]].
