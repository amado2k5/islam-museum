## Summary
Khida'a (deception) lexically is showing the opposite of what one hides, or guile with the intent of harm; war is a deceit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7556} It is distinguished from treachery (ghadr, breaking a covenant), undervaluation (ghabn, which concerns sale), breach of trust (khiyana), delusion and inducement (ghurur, taghrir), and deceitful adulteration (ghish). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7556} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7557}  Deceiving a treaty-bound enemy is forbidden; if the imam senses treachery by clear signs he must announce the end of the truce before fighting; after that, deceit in war is lawful, since war is deceit, and equivocation is allowed when a lawful purpose requires it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7558} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7559}
