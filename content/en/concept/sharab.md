## Nature
The author says: I mean by it al-qahwa. Everything below is the book's account of the drink, set down as a report attributed to it and not as advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Actions and properties
It balances the superfluities of the bitter-bile kind, while fresh nabidh and the thick turbid kind gather in the veins fullness and raw humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Choice
The best is the old, thin, clear one made of grape; taking it differs by temperaments, and the book says it is better for a person to take a moderate measure of it, since in much of it there is great harm; the details of what suits the young and the old are left out because they are quantities and mixing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Cosmetics
It improves the complexion, fattens some people, removes bahaq and baras together with the drugs mentioned, and cleanses the skin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Wounds and ulcers
Pouring it on malignant and eating ulcers to which superfluities flow benefits them, washing the fistula with it benefits it, and so do milky ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Organs of the head
It intoxicates and brings stupor, removes memory and lowers the faculties of the soul {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Joints
Constant drinking of it harms the nerves and brings tremor, and constant intoxication every day brings slackness and weakness of the nerve, while the honeyed sharab benefits pain of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Ocular organs
Ibn Masawayh said: the very old sharab harms sight; with the old sharab the drugs for the pterygium are kneaded, the salve known as qaysar is rubbed with it, and chronic pterygium is treated with it, for it benefits it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Organs of the chest
It increases innate heat and gladdens the heart, and the sweet sharab cleanses the passages of the lung and spreads the breath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It is quick to descend and to be digested, nourishes much and generates good chyme; at times it causes nausea and vomiting and cleanses the stomach of superfluities, and it whets appetite for food when drinking is moderate; much of it brings blockages in the liver and the kidneys, while little of it carries the nourishment through, improves digestion, hastens its turning to blood and fosters the whole appetite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.685}

## Excretory organs
The white thin one promotes urine and is good for burning in the bladder, the old one harms the bladder, and the honeyed one softens the belly; that made with sea water inflates and loosens the belly and removes slackness of the anus; the watery promotes urine more than the neat, the sweet does not promote it, and the mixed harms the intestines by relaxing and inflating them, while the neat strengthens them by its astringency, warms them and dissolves the flatulence from them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.685-686}

## Poisons and bites
The old sharab benefits the stings of all vermin, drunk and as a wash; that made with sea water benefits one who drank numbing poisons, one who drank murtak, one who ate mushrooms, and the stings of cold vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## End of the letter
Here the book ends the letter shin, and says that the total of what it mentioned in it is thirty-two drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that the honeyed benefits pains of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.686}
