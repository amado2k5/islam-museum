## Nature
It is the tree of qatran; the book has said a full account under qatran and here gives the actions particular to its tree. This tree is of the genus of the pine and has a fruit like the cypress fruit but smaller, with a thorn, in two kinds, tall and short. Dioscorides said: it is a great tree like the cypress, and from some of it comes qatran; its fruit is like the cypress fruit but much smaller, and some of it is small and thorny, with a fruit like that of the juniper, round like the myrtle berry. The best qatran is thick, clear, strong and foul-smelling, whose drops, when dropped, stay as they were and do not scatter. In Persian this tree is called awars {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.681}

## Actions and properties
In the bark of this tree there is astringency. Dioscorides said: qatran has an astringent power opposing putrefaction, tightening living bodies and preserving dead bodies, and for that reason some called it the life of the dead {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.681}

## Organs of the head
Whoever takes much of the fruit of this tree gets a headache from the heating and from the stomach's sharing in its stinging; and if one gargles with vinegar in which its leaves were cooked, toothache is soothed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.681}

## Organs of the chest
Its fruit benefits cough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.681}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its fruit is bad for the stomach, stinging it, but it benefits the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.681}

## Excretory organs
Its fruit benefits dribbling of urine, and drunk with pepper it promotes urine; when drunk it binds the belly, and perhaps holds back urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.682}

## Poisons and bites
Its fruit is given with the sharab to one who has drunk the sea hare, and the book reports that mixed with deer fat and rubbed on the body, vermin do not come near it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.682}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that when fumigated with its bark it brings out the foetus and the placenta {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.682}
