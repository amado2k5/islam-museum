## The Claim

Zaqzuq notes that most Orientalists incline toward claiming that Islamic law was influenced by Roman law, differing only on the degree of that influence. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 144} Among those holding the strongest version of this view, he names Goldziher, von Kremer, and Sheldon Amos, who contend that Sharia is derived from Roman law. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 144} {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 145}

## The Author's Response

Zaqzuq views this claim as part of attempts to undermine the two foundational sources of Islam, the Quran and the Sunnah, arguing that doubt cast upon them leads to doubt about Islam at its core. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 144} The book then moves on to another claim concerning Islamic philosophy. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 145}
