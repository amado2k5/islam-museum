## Summary
Ibn Sina describes swaddling the newborn: the midwife first handles its limbs gently and shapes them, then swaddles it and lays it in a room of balanced air leaning to shade, with the head higher than the body, taking care that the bed does not twist the neck, limbs or spine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.

## The midwife's work
If we wish to swaddle it, the midwife should begin by touching its limbs gently, broadening what should be broad, narrowing what should be narrow, and shaping each limb into its best form by soft pressure with her fingertips, repeating this again and again. She keeps wiping its eyes with something like silk and presses its bladder so that urine is easily passed, then spreads its hands, lays its arms against its knees, and wraps its head with a fitted cap {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.

## Room and bed
It is put to sleep in a house of balanced air, neither cold nor hot, leaning to shade and dim so that no strong ray falls in. Its head in the bed should be higher than the rest of its body, and care is taken that the bed does not bend its neck, limbs or back {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.
