## Summary
This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum says about Arabic in Chad: its legal status, the varieties spoken and the official figures, each as the summary gives it. The picture is mainly legal: Arabic and French are the two official languages, and no official count of speakers was found. The museum did not open the pages the summary names, and the founder approved using it as supplied on 21 November 2026. Where the summary says that no official source or figure was found, this page says exactly that and supplies no number of its own.

## What this page is
This page is an article on the subject "Arabic in Chad", in the world chapter of the Arabic browser, one of the pages that treat Arabic outside the states counted as Arab. It answers three questions: what is the legal status of Arabic there, which varieties of it are spoken, and what official figures have been published on speakers, if any. The museum keeps here to what it keeps to on all its pages: a describing voice, not a commanding one, every statement ascribed to its page, and no verdict on a disputed question. All the material comes from one research summary, so the page describes what the summary said and does not claim to have read the sources.

## Background and dates
The summary gives no history of Arabic in Chad beyond the dates of the two documents it relies on: the 2009 census (RGPH2) and the 2023 constitution in its version consolidated in October 2025. The page therefore reads a standing legal situation rather than a historical course, and the museum attributes to the summary no story it did not tell about how Arabic came to Chad; that story is left to the historical pages of the Arabic browser, the first being [[concept/intishar-al-arabiyya-baada-al-fath|The spread of Arabic after the conquests]].

## Legal status
Article 10 of the 2023 Constitution of Chad, in the version consolidated in October 2025, makes French and Arabic the two official languages and leaves the promotion of national languages to national law, according to the research summary {cite:constitution-chad-2023-mjp}. The summary adds that in practice Modern Standard or Classical Arabic is the co-official written language of legislation and administration, while Chadian Arabic is a spoken vehicular language between communities {cite:cefan-tchad}. This is what the summary says, ascribed to its two pages; the museum did not read the article itself and passes no judgement on the description of practice.

## Varieties spoken
The summary names two contrasting kinds: Modern Standard or Classical Arabic, and Chadian Arabic, also called Shuwa Arabic or Western Sudanic Arabic, which includes urban varieties and Bagirmi Arabic and shows influence from Sudanese and Libyan Arabic {cite:wikipedia-chadian-arabic} {cite:cefan-tchad}. Wikipedia is used here only for names and places; the description of influence and function is ascribed to the summary. For comparison with the eastern neighbour see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-sudan|Arabic in Sudan]], and with the northern one [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-libya|Arabic in Libya]].

## Official figures
The summary found no official government census and no UN or UNESCO count of speakers, and says so. What it found is the 2009 census, RGPH2 of INSEED, which reports literacy shares among literate adults aged 15 and over rather than speaker counts: 10.3 percent literate in Arabic alone and 2.1 percent in both French and Arabic {cite:inseed-rgph2-2009-global-indicators}. These are literacy shares, not counts of speakers, and the museum neither converts them into a count nor infers anything from them.

## How the sources differ
The pages named for this record are of different kinds. Official texts and statistics: 2023 Constitution of Chad (consolidated Oct. 2025) {cite:constitution-chad-2023-mjp} and INSEED RGPH2 Global Indicators (2009 census) {cite:inseed-rgph2-2009-global-indicators}; Reference, academic and press pages: CEFAN / Université Laval page on Chad {cite:cefan-tchad}; Wikipedia pages: Chadian Arabic {cite:wikipedia-chadian-arabic}. The official texts and statistics speak for the state or the statistical office; the reference, academic and press pages describe and interpret; Wikipedia supports only names, places and dates. Where the kinds differ, the museum reports each in its own voice without choosing between them, and it opened none of them.
