Yawm al-jumu'a is a compound of day and jumu'a. Al-jumu'a names a day of the week, its last if Saturday is its first, and before Islam it was called the day of al-'Aruba. Al-Suhayli reports that Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy, the Prophet's forebear, first gathered the day of al-'Aruba and first named it al-jumu'a, Quraysh gathering to him that day while he preached and reminded them of the Prophet's coming mission; Salman al-Farisi is reported to have been asked by the Prophet what the day of jumu'a was and answered "the day your father was gathered by," while others say it was named in Islam for the gathering in the mosque. The technical sense does not depart from the lexical one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18016} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18017}

The related term is the week, seven days; Friday is one of them or their last, so the two stand in full genus and species. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18017}

Friday carries rules of its own. On its merit, Ibn Abidin calls it the best day of the week and a feast day: in it is an hour of answered prayer, souls are gathered, graves are visited, one who dies in it or its night is safe from the trial and punishment of the grave, hell is not stoked in it, Adam was created in it, taken out of paradise in it, and in it the people of paradise visit their Lord. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18017}

The Friday prayer is an individual duty on adult Muslim males, its time that of the noon prayer on Friday; its conditions and pillars are detailed under its own headword. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18017}

On the washing: the jurists agree it is legally required in some form, on the hadith "when one of you comes to Friday let him wash." The majority hold it sunna, while a narration from Ahmad makes it obligatory on "the washing of Friday is a duty on every adult." On its time, the majority place it after the second dawn and do not credit an earlier wash; al-Awza'i is reported to credit it before dawn; Malik requires it only if going to the prayer follows it; the Shafi'is place it from true dawn, best near the going, with a view from midnight as on the feast; and all agree a wash after the Friday prayer does not count. As to whether it belongs to the day or the prayer, the majority hold it to the prayer, unlike the feast-day wash, so it is not recommended for one who does not attend; some hold it to the day; Ibn Abidin calls the prayer view sound, it being Abu Yusuf's position, while al-Hasan ibn Ziyad attributes "for the day" to Muhammad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18018} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18019}

On travel: most jurists hold it forbidden to set out on Friday after noon before performing the prayer, for one bound to it, since the duty attaches at the entry of the time; the Hanafis call it prohibitively disliked, and they differ on travel before noon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18019}

On fasting: most jurists dislike fasting Friday alone, on Abu Hurayra's hadith "let none of you fast Friday unless with a day before it or after it"; joining a day lifts the dislike by agreement. Al-Khaniyya reports from Abu Hanifa and Muhammad that there is no harm in fasting it even alone, on Ibn Abbas's practice, and the Malikis and al-Hasakafi hold fasting it alone recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18019} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18020}

On supplication and Qur'an reading: prayer has hours when answer is more hoped, among them Friday; they differ on the hour of answer — between the imam's sitting and the end of prayer, which Ibn Abidin calls the soundest, or the time of the afternoon prayer. Reading surah al-Kahf on Friday is recommended, on the hadith that it lights for its reader the light between the two Fridays, and much supplication is recommended in hope of meeting the hour. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18020}

On adornment: it is recommended on Friday to wear one's best clothes, the turban, perfume, shaving, cutting the nails and the tooth-stick, on the hadith "this is a day God made a feast for the Muslims, so wash." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18020} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18021}

The Shafi'is and Hanbalis recommend contracting marriage on Friday, as a group of the early Muslims recommended it and it is a noble day, a feast. They also recommend reading surah al-Sajda and surah al-Insan at the dawn prayer on Friday, as the Prophet is reported to have read them, though Ahmad disliked making them constant lest people think them distinguished by the prostration; the Hanafis commend reading them sometimes as a blessing but dislike constancy lest the ignorant suppose nothing else is allowed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18021}

On sale: the jurists agree to bar selling at the call to Friday prayer, on God's word in surah al-Jumu'a (verse 9); the majority state it is forbidden and the Hanafis call it prohibitively disliked. They differ on when the ban begins — the majority say the second call, the Hanafis the first call after noon. The jurists extend the ban to contracts and dispositions generally, while the Hanbalis on the school view confine it to sale, so marriage and hire are not forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18021}

On the standing at Arafa falling on Friday: the Hanafis say a Friday standing has the merit of seventy pilgrimages and the best of days is the day of Arafa when it falls on a Friday; the Shafi'is report that when they coincide God forgives all the people of the standing ground without an intermediary. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18022}
