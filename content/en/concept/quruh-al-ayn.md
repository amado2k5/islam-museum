## Its seven kinds
The eye's ulcers are bred mostly by sharp burning humours, and are seven kinds: four in the cornea's surface which Galen calls ulcers and some before him roughness — the first an ulcer like a smoke on the eye's black, called the dim and sometimes called qattam; then a deeper, whiter, smaller kind called the cloud, also called qattam; the third the coronal, on the corona — the circle of the black — which may take on some of the conjunctiva's white, showing white on the pupil and red on the conjunctiva; the fourth called the burning, also the woolly, on the pupil's face like a little tuft of wool; and three sunken ones: lopoyon — the deep of depth — a deep narrow clean ulcer; lopoma — the digger — less deep and wider taking; and uquma — also burning — a dirty ulcer with a roughness whose cleansing is a risk, for the humour runs out as the membranes are eaten and the eye is ruined with it; the ulcers arise in the eye after ramad, inward and bursting outward, or the reverse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.177}

## Its signs
The sign of ulcers in the eyeball: a white point if on the cornea, red if on the conjunctiva or the corona, with severe pain and pulsation; if the pus found at the dressing is white it indicates weak pain and strong pulsation; if yellow, livid or thin it is lighter in all that; if red the pain is lightest of all; if dusky the pain is severe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.177}
