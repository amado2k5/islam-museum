## Summary
Ibn al-Nadim opens the Fihrist with the scripts of the nations. Reporting from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, he says the first Arabic scripts were the Meccan, then the Medinan, then the Basran, then the Kufan, and he describes the Meccan and Medinan by their alifs: a turn toward the right, raised ascenders, and a slight slant in the form. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 20} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 147}

## In the Fihrist
What follows is headed "scripts of the mushafs" and gives a list of names that begins with the Meccan and the Medinan and includes Kufan and Basran forms and others; the OCR of this list in the copy read here is unclear for several names, so they are not all listed. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 21} Ibn al-Nadim then names Khalid ibn Abi al-Hayyaj, described as of fine hand, as the first to copy mushafs in early times, and says he saw a mushaf in his writing. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 21} Blair adds that, in her reading, this is the man who designed mosaic inscriptions for the caliph al-Walid. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 147}

## What modern scholarship says
Sheila Blair notes that the nineteenth-century orientalist Michele Amari already used this passage to identify Meccan script; that the hand was later called ma'il and then renamed hijazi; and that Francois Deroche divided hijazi into four sub-types. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 147} {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 148} Estelle Whelan argued that hijazi was largely a scholarly artifact built on two methodological errors: the features of one letter, the alif, are not enough to define a script, and in this passage Ibn al-Nadim was describing the earliest Arabic writing in general, not Qur'an manuscripts; the earliest known copy of the Fihrist shows the basmala in a rounded hand, not the angular script of Qur'an copies. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 148} Blair adds that Ibn al-Nadim wrote some three centuries after the events and that his text is too vague to identify the early Qur'an scripts. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 148}
