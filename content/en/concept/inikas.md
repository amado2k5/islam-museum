## Definition
Inikas is lexically the reflexive of 'aks, which is turning the beginning of a thing back on its end. Among the usulis the reverse analogy (qiyas al-'aks) is the establishment of the reverse of a ruling for a like thing, because the two are reversed in their cause. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3512}

## Among the usulis
Inikas is the absence of a ruling through the absence of its cause, as the prohibition of wine ends with the end of its intoxication. Its opposite is ittirad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3512}

## Status as a way of knowing the cause
- Most usulis hold that inikas together with ittirad is a way of knowing the cause. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3512}
- The Hanafis and many Ash'aris, including al-Ghazali and al-Amidi, do not count it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3512}
- Some hold that inikas is a condition of the cause, and others do not require it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3512}
