## Overview
Ibn al-Nadim transmits from Abd Allah ibn al-Muqaffa' that the languages of the Persians are: Pahlavi, Dari, Persian, Khuzi, and Syriac. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.30-32}

Pahlavi is attributed to *Pahla*, a name that applies to five cities. Dari is attributed to *dar*, meaning the gate, and it was the language of the royal court and of the city of al-Mada'in. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.30-32}

Ibn al-Nadim gives opinions on who first spoke or wrote in Persian. Among them Jiyumart, whom the Persians regard as Adam, the father of mankind; some say Bivarasp, who is al-Dahhak; some say Afridun; some say Jamshid. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.30-32}

He reads in the hand of al-Jahshiyari that books were few before the reign of Gushtasp, and that when Zoroaster appeared, writing spread. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.30-32}
