## Definition
The maw'uda is the newborn girl whom some Arabs of the Jahiliyya buried alive — out of fear of poverty or of what they counted as shame. The Qur'an names the crime in Surat al-Takwir within the scene of the Day of Resurrection, and asks her by what sin she was killed — a question that knows its answer, making the spilled blood alone the witness and the judge. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Daughters in the Jahiliyya
The Qur'an depicts the annunciation of a daughter: the father's face darkens and he holds back his grief, he hides from his people at the evil of the news — will he keep her in humiliation, or bury her in the dust? Two verses gather the whole inner scene from shame to crime; then the ruling abolished it: whoever buried his daughter alive in the Jahiliyya and then entered Islam is not held for what passed, as reported in the hadith. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:sahih-muslim}
