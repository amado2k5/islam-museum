## Summary
The verses say the lowest heaven was adorned with stars and guarded against every devil, and that whoever steals a hearing is followed by a flare. In the hadith the angels beat their wings in submission when God decrees a matter in the heaven, and some of the jinn used to catch a word from the highest assembly and cast it to soothsayers. The disagreement among the exegetes is not over the guarding itself but over a narrower question: were shooting flares seen before the mission and only became missiles against devils after it, or did they begin with the mission? This page sets out the texts and then Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and al-Razi by name; it adds no modern astronomical explanation, since none was read in a verified source in this pass. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## What the verses say
In [[passage/quran-72-8|Al-Jinn 8]] and [[passage/quran-72-9|Al-Jinn 9]] the jinn say they sought the sky and found it filled with strong guards and flares, and that they used to sit in places there to listen, but now whoever listens finds a flare lying in wait. In [[passage/quran-37-6|Al-Saffat 6]] to [[passage/quran-37-10|Al-Saffat 10]] the lowest heaven is adorned with the beauty of the stars and guarded against every rebellious devil, who cannot listen to the highest assembly and are pelted from every side and driven off with a lasting punishment, except one who snatches a snatch and is followed by a piercing flame. [[passage/quran-15-17|Al-Hijr 17]] and [[passage/quran-15-18|Al-Hijr 18]] speak of the heaven being guarded from every accursed devil except one who steals a hearing and is followed by a clear flame. [[passage/quran-67-5|Al-Mulk 5]] says the lowest heaven was adorned with lamps which were made missiles against the devils. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## The hadith
Al-Bukhari, number 4701, with the same report at 4800 in Surat Saba and 7481 in the Book of Tawhid, relates from Abu Hurayra, attributing it to the Prophet, that when God decrees a matter in the heaven the angels beat their wings in submission to His word, like a chain on a rock; when fear has left their hearts they say what their Lord said, and are told the truth; those who steal the hearing catch it, one above another, and sometimes the flare reaches them before they pass it on and sometimes they pass it on first, until it comes to the sorcerer or soothsayer, who adds a hundred lies to it and is believed because of the one word heard. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4701} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4800} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 7481}

Al-Bukhari 4921, with a similar report at 773, relates from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet set out with a group of his companions toward the market of Ukaz, when the devils had been barred from the heaven's news and flares had been sent against them. They returned to their people and said something had happened, so they should search east and west. Those who headed toward Tihama went to the Prophet at Nakhla as he was leading his companions in the dawn prayer; they heard the Qur'an and said, this is what stands between you and the news of heaven, and went back to tell their people. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4921} In Muslim, number 2229, Ibn Abbas relates from a man of the Ansar that they were sitting one night with the Prophet when a star was thrown and lit up; he asked what they used to say about such a thing in the pre-Islamic period, and they said they used to say that a great man was dying or being born. He said it is not thrown for anyone's death or life, and explained how the word passes from the Throne down to the lowest heaven, where the jinn snatch it and cast it to their allies. {cite:sahih-muslim 2229} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 p. 467}

## What the exegetes said
In Surat al-Jinn Ibn Kathir says the heaven was filled with strong guards and flares during the time the Qur'an was being revealed, so that devils could not steal any of it and cast it on the tongues of soothsayers and confuse the matter, and that this was God's kindness to His creatures and His guarding of His book; he adds that stars had been thrown before that, but only now and then, not in numbers. He relays from al-Suddi that the heaven was never guarded unless a prophet or an openly practised religion of God was on earth, and gives the report of the people of Ta'if who were frightened when they saw the flares moving in confusion. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 72:8-9, pp. 383-384} In Surat al-Mulk he says the pronoun in "we made them missiles" refers to the kind, lamps, not to the very stars, since the stars of the sky are not thrown, but flares from beneath them which may draw on them, and he relays Qatada's saying that the stars were made for three things, as adornment of the heaven, as missiles against devils and as signs for guidance, and that whoever reads another meaning into them speaks from his own opinion. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 67:5, pp. 312-313}

On Al-Hijr 17 and 18 al-Qurtubi says the scholars differed over whether the throwing of flares took place before the mission; most said yes, and some said no, that it happened only after it. He relays al-Zajjaj's view that the throwing is one of the Prophet's signs that began after his birth, since the old poets never mentioned it, and that it is not far-fetched to say stars falling happened in older times but were not then missiles against devils, and became so afterwards. He also says the scholars note that we see stars fall, so it may be as we see and then turn to fire when it reaches the devil, or they may be struck with a flame of fire from the air so that to us it looks like a moving star. In the same place he records another difference: Ibn Abbas said the flare wounds, burns and confuses but does not kill, while al-Hasan and some others said it kills, and al-Qurtubi favours the first. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-alam-kutub-bukhari vol. 10 pp. 11-12}

:::perspectives
**Sunni: Ibn Abbas and Ubayy ibn Ka'b, as reported by al-Razi.** Al-Razi relays that the flares did not exist before the mission, that the jinn used to ascend and hear the revelation and were barred from their places at the mission, and that stars had not been thrown before. He cites from Ubayy ibn Ka'b that no star was thrown from the time Jesus was raised until the Prophet was sent. He adds that holders of this view suppose that the poems attributed to the Jahiliyya and the books of earlier peoples may have been tampered with. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 pp. 467-468}

**Sunni: al-Razi prefers that they existed before the mission and were increased after it.** He sets out the arguments of those who say they existed before: the earlier philosophers' discussion of their cause, the verse's giving the stars two benefits, adornment and the stoning of devils, their appearance in Jahili poetry, and Ibn Abbas's hadith of the Prophet asking the Ansar what they used to say in the Jahiliyya. He then says the closer to correct is that they existed before the mission but were increased after it and made fuller and stronger, which is what the word for "filled" in the verse indicates, and "places to listen" implies that some places were empty of guards. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 pp. 467-468}

**Sunni: Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi.** Ibn Kathir holds that the throwing happened before only now and then and became frequent with the mission, and al-Qurtubi sets out the majority view that they existed before it and the view that they began after it without deciding between them. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 72:8-9, p. 383} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-alam-kutub-bukhari vol. 10 p. 12}
:::

## What the sources do not specify
The texts do not say how the angels of the hadith relate to the flares of the verses, how many guards there are, or where the guarding is placed. Ibn Kathir took the pronoun in al-Mulk to return to the kind, lamps, not the stars themselves, which is his reading of the verse. A link between these flares and what is today called meteors or meteorites was not read in a verified source, and so it does not enter this page.
