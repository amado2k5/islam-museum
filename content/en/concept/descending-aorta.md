## Summary
In the fifth chapter Ibn Sina follows the descending artery from the heart along the spine to the sacrum and lists the branches it leaves on the way to the lung, diaphragm, stomach, liver, spleen, intestines, kidneys and testicles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.86-87}.

## In the chest
It runs first straight until it hangs on the fifth vertebra, whose position faces the apex of the heart; there the thymus is like a prop and support for it, standing between it and the spinal bones and the gullet. Reaching that place it turns aside to the right and does not pass it, then stands free, hung by membranes where it meets the diaphragm so as not to crowd it. It then bends downward along the spine to the sacrum; as it faces the chest it leaves a small, fine branch spreading in the vessel of the lung, and a branch at each vertebra until it reaches the space between ribs and spinal cord; past the chest two arteries come from it to the diaphragm and spread in it right and left {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.86}.

## In the abdomen
Next it leaves an artery whose branches spread in the stomach, liver and spleen, and from the liver a branch goes to the bladder; then one grows that reaches the channels around the small intestines and the colon. Then three arteries part from it: the smallest belongs to the left kidney and gives it life, and the other two go to the kidneys so that each kidney draws from them the watery part of the blood, since the kidneys often draw impure blood from the stomach and intestines. Then two arteries go to the testicles: the one to the left always takes a piece of the one to the left kidney and may arise from the left kidney alone, while the one to the right always arises from the great artery and rarely takes something of what goes to the right kidney. Then arteries go to the channels around the rectum, branches to the spinal cord entering the holes of the vertebrae, vessels to the flanks, and a small pair ending at the pudendum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.86}.
