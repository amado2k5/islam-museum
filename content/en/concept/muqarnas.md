## Overview
An Islamic architectural element of cellular muqarnas, resembling stalactites, through which corners, domes and mihrabs transition. It is one of the most distinctive features of Islamic architecture. {cite:wikidata}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes muqarnas as a three-dimensional form of architectural ornament, found from Spain to India, built from tiers of small geometric niches laid one above another and often combined with pendant elements. It began as a structural device, used in niches and in the squinches beneath domes, and later grew into a highly characteristic kind of architectural decoration throughout the Islamic world. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1617}
