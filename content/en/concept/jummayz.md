## Nature and selection
Dioscorides says in his book that the jummayz is a great tree like the fig, with much milky sap; its leaf resembles the mulberry leaf, and it bears three or even four times a year {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425}. Its fruit comes from the trunk rather than from the branch shoots, as the fig's does; it looks like the wild fig, is sweeter than the unripe fig, and has no seed in the stone as the fig does {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot and moist, it is said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425}. Properties: the tree is said to have a sap, which can be drawn before it fruits by bruising its outer bark; the sap is collected with wool, dried, made into lozenges and used; it has a very softening and dissolving power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425}.

## Uses by organ
Food: Dioscorides says the jummayz gives little nourishment and is bad for the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Wounds and ulcers: its sap is said to bind and seal stubborn wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Swellings and pustules: dissolves stubborn swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Excretory organs: the jummayz purges the belly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426} [book listing].
Fevers: the sap of this tree helps the goosebumps of fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Poisons: rubbed on for the bites of vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
