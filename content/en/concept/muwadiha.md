## Summary
The muwadiha is a wound that reaches the bone and lays it bare; the ba'diha cuts the flesh without reaching the bone. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15284}

## Retaliation
All agree that the muwadiha carries retaliation when intended, since it can be measured at the bone; the Hanafis add that where it impairs a limb such as the sight there is no retaliation for that, but blood money for both. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15284} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15285}

## How retaliation is taken
Retaliation is not taken with a sword for fear of excess; it is taken with an instrument made for it by one who knows how, and an unskilled executor is told to appoint a deputy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15285}

## Compensation
A muwadiha wound carries a fixed five camels when unintended, on the letter of the Prophet to 'Amr ibn Hazm; the scholars agree the sum is fixed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15286}

## Head and face
Most jurists hold that the head and the face are alike for the muwadiha. The Malikis add to the due when the face is disfigured; Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab doubles the face sum. Compensation is owed whether the wound is small or large, visible or under hair. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15286} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15287}

## Wounds elsewhere
Most jurists fix no sum for a wound outside the head and face. Al-Layth and al-Awza'i extend the rule to the body. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15287} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15288}

## Spread and several wounds
A cut that runs from the head to the nape carries the compensation for the wound and an assessment for the nape; a partition between two wounds fixes the sum by whether it is removed, by whom, and whether it has healed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15288} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15289}

## Agency in settlement
A settlement made by an agent beyond his instructions is void beyond what he was authorised to accept; a settlement in wine or swine is a pardon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15289} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15290}
