## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the second part of the first book, on diseases, causes and general symptoms, by defining cause, disease and symptom with examples, and by showing that one thing may be, in relation to others, a cause, a disease and a symptom {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.103-104}.

## The definitions
A cause in medicine is what comes first and from which a state of the human body, or its persistence, necessarily follows. A disease is an unnatural condition in the human body from which harm in the act follows by itself and primarily, being either an unnatural temperament or an unnatural composition. A symptom is what follows this condition and is itself unnatural, whether opposed to the natural, like the pain of colic, or not opposed, like the redness of one cheek in pneumonia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.103}.

## The examples
As an example of a cause, putrefaction; of a disease, fever; of symptoms, thirst and headache. Again: a cause, fullness in the vessels going to the eye; a disease, blockage in the uvea, an organic disease of composition; a symptom, loss of sight. Again: a cause, an acute catarrh; a disease, an ulcer in the lung; symptoms, redness of the cheeks and curving of the nails. A symptom is so called in itself or in relation to what it befalls, and is called a sign in relation to the physician's study of it and his way from it to knowing the nature of the disease {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.103}.

## How the roles pass
A disease may become the cause of another disease, as colic of fainting, palsy or epilepsy; a symptom may even become a cause of disease, as severe pain becomes a cause of swelling because matter pours to the painful place. A symptom may itself become a disease, as the headache arising from fever may settle and harden until it is a disease. A thing may, in relation to itself, to what is before it and to what is after it, be a disease, a symptom and a cause: consumptive fever is a symptom of an ulcer of the lung, a disease in itself and a cause of weakness of the stomach; and the headache from fever, once hardened, is a symptom of the fever, a disease in itself and sometimes a cause of what brings on pleurisy or brain fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.103-104}.
