## Overview

Jiwar covers the rights a neighbour has over a neighbour. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis set its limit at forty houses on each side. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6676}

## Shared walls and screens

A shared wall may be used only to the extent of the partner's benefit. Roofs need a screen against sight into a neighbour's house, and the schools differ on compelling the owner to build one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6679} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6680} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6682}

## Passage and pre-emption

A neighbour must let people pass along a public watercourse. Pre-emption belongs to the adjoining neighbour in the Hanafi view; others disagree. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6683} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6684}

## Effect on what an owner may do

The neighbour's rights limit how an owner may use his property where it harms the neighbour. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6681}

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen

The Sheikh says that good neighbourliness covers the wife, the husband, the children, siblings, neighbours, colleagues and travel companions, and he counts it among the blessings {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:30:00}. He describes forms of bad neighbourliness inside the family: a wife complaining to her mother and sister, a son complaining of his father, and a father complaining of his children's disobedience. He says that giving a gift to a non-Muslim neighbour is part of good neighbourliness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:31:00}.

He describes the harmful neighbour, who hides his neighbour's good deeds and spreads his faults, and the good neighbour, who lowers his gaze and guards his neighbour's honour and household; he holds that good neighbourliness and gratitude increase blessings {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:32:00}. Asked how a neighbour's complaint about us can end, he answered that knowing the neighbour's right comes before knowing one's personal rights, and that complaint ends only when religion is understood correctly {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:35:30}.

He relates that Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf saw a man in private conference with the Prophet and asked to stand aside; when the man left, the Prophet told him that Gabriel kept recommending the neighbour until he thought the neighbour would inherit (needs_source: garbled narration) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:36:30}. He asks where mercy, bearing with a neighbour and kindness to one who wronged us are, and holds that these values are missing from neighbour relations (needs_source: garbled passage) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:37:00}.

He describes manners in houses: a person should not enter a neighbour's house secretly, should give gifts to him, and should not harm him with the smell of his cooking. He bases this on a hadith without naming its narrator {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:37:30}. He criticises a company, not named, that builds a tall tower beside a small house without asking, blocking air from its neighbour, and asks whether its builders feel the neighbour's right {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:38:00}. He answers those who call his speech impractical with what he saw in rural villages in Germany, where neighbours are considered on Sundays and during quiet hours, and he acknowledges deviations in the West {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:38:30}.

He holds that harming a neighbour is harming oneself, and recalls that the Arabs in pre-Islamic times supported their neighbours; he cites a line of verse whose author is not clear in the recording (needs_source: garbled attribution) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:40:00}.
