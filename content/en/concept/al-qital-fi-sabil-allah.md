## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that fighting in Islam repels aggression and protects people's choice of religion, not compelling them into it. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.815} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.816}

## Fighting those who fight
Sha'rawi reads [[passage/quran-2-190|2:190]] as limiting fighting to those who fight, holds that aggressors are not fought, and that when the enemy stops there is no aggression except against wrongdoers. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.816} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.826}

## No compulsion in religion
Sha'rawi holds that Islam compels no one to enter it, and argues from the jizya taken from a non-believer that he is allowed to keep his religion; that Islam shielded choice from forces that impose a faith; and that the body may be compelled but the heart may not. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.816} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.817} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.818}

## The delay of fighting
Sha'rawi says fighting was delayed in Mecca so that the believers learned the way and became a model, so that no house would become a battlefield between believer and unbeliever, and because strife in religion is worse than killing; he gives examples of companions who were enemies and later embraced Islam. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.817} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.818} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.819} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.822}

## Sacred for sacred, and protected blood
Sha'rawi holds that a sacred month or place is met with its like, and that one who embraces Islam has his blood protected; he recounts Umar's words about the killer of Zayd and the story of Wahshi in the footnotes, without a checked chain. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.823} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.825} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.826}

## Limits of this batch
Sha'rawi holds that fighting is defence; the jurists' views on offensive jihad, the academic view and the other views that dispute this characterisation are not entered. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.826}
