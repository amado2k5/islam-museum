## Summary
Labba, in lexicon, is the middle of the chest and the place where a necklace sits; the plural is labbat or libab. In law it is the place of slaughter on the chest, the hollow between the base of the neck and the chest {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13566}.

## The general ruling: slaughter at the labba
Most jurists hold that in ritual slaughter of camels the sunna, when one is able, is to stab at the labba; they cite Abu Hurayra's report of a call in Mina that ritual is at the throat and the labba, a report Ibn Abd al-Hadi called very weak {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13566}. For the Hanafis, the real nahr is cutting the veins at the labba; for the Malikis it is a thrust at the labba that leads to death, even without cutting the throat and the two veins {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13566}. The detail is in the entry on slaughter {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13566}.
