## The amount expelled or withheld
The signs taken from the acts of nature appear in the expelling of superfluities in their amount and quality, or in their withholding, from the palate, nose and ear, and from the sores, pustules and swellings that appear on the head, and from the hair that grows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15}

When these superfluities are many they indicate plentiful matter, the cause by which superfluities are many in the organ, and that the expelling power is not weak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15}

When they are withheld or few and there is with it heaviness, pricking, stinging, stretching, throbbing, dizziness or ringing, this indicates blockages, weakness of the expelling power and fullness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.15-16}

Its kind is inferred thus: what stings, pricks, burns, with little heaviness and yellowing of the colour of the face and eye indicates a bilious matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} What throbs, is heavy, reddens the colour of the face and eye and swells the vessels indicates blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} What makes lazy and dull, turns the colour leaden and brings sleep and drowsiness indicates phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} If the colour is dull and memory spoilt, the head lighter and sleep not overpowering, it indicates black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} If any of this comes with ringing, dizziness and shifting, the matter is generating wind, inflation and vapour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} If superfluities are withheld with lightness of the head it indicates dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16}

## Quality, smell and what shows on the skin
As for the quality of what is expelled, what tends to yellowness, thinness, heat, bitterness and stinging indicates that it is bilious, and what tends to redness and sweetness, with redness of the face and eyes, flowing sweat and heat, that it is of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} What is salty or sweet, with none of the other signs, indicates phlegm on which heat has acted, and what is insipid, thick and cold to the touch indicates raw phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} As for smell, a putrid or sharp smell indicates heat and absence of smell may indicate cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16}

The sores, pustules and swellings that appear on the skin of the head mostly indicate matters that were and have been expelled, and do not indicate the present state clearly, unless they are increasing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16} On the hair Ibn Sina refers to Book I and to particular chapters, to avoid length {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.16}
