## Summary
In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lesson, firasa is a kind of inspiration given to the believer who has cleansed his heart of rancor, envy and spite; it ranks below prophethood, and some in the West call it the «sixth sense» {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:08:00}. He holds that the Companions reached it through the purity of their souls, and that many sins block it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:30:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 8)

The Sheikh holds that the Companions reached a degree of faith at which their souls lit up and worked as God created them; they had purified them and removed the barriers of rancor, envy, spite and resentment that block a person from the world of inspiration {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:07:30}.

He says that earlier nations had «muhaddathun» (people who received inspiration), that this nation has such people too, that this degree is below that of the prophets, and that it is inspiration; he adds that some in the West call it the «sixth sense» while here it is called the believer's insight (firasa) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:08:00}.

He relays the warning to beware the believer's insight because he sees by God's light, and says the Companions' hearts grew tender and were cleansed of impurities, unlike someone who looks down on people with pride and is not forbearing with them, whose heart is hard; he says Ibn Masud reached a high degree of perception in this way {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:08:30}.

He relates that Ibn Masud said they never thought any of them wanted this world until a verse was revealed showing that some of them did, and the Sheikh explains that Ibn Masud had assumed everyone was thinking of the hereafter and was struck when the Qur'an showed otherwise {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:09:00}.

The Sheikh holds that many sins set up barriers between a person and insight, and he tells how Anas came in to see Uthman after glancing at a woman; Uthman told him there was a trace of adultery in his eyes, Anas asked in surprise whether there was revelation after the Messenger of God, and Uthman answered that it was the believer's insight, which sees by God's light {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:30:30}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] titles a reflection of [[work/sayd-al-khatir|Sayd al-Khatir]] "The blessing of perspicacity". He sums up Ibn al-Jawzi's point that when a man's mind matures his intelligence and acumen grow, so that he escapes when he falls into a predicament, while the heedless man harms himself; he says the core of the reflection is the blessing of firasa and the consequence of heedlessness, and that firasa may be inborn or acquired. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:05:30} He says that the saying "knowledge comes by learning and forbearance by practising forbearance" has been graded sound as a Prophetic hadith by some scholars, but that the correct view is that it is the saying of a Companion. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:06:00} He quotes al-Shafi'i's saying that he learned firasa before he learned jurisprudence, says that the mufti is "the captive of the question", that whoever teaches or judges must study firasa, and that since there are no books devoted to it one should read the biographies of scholars. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:18:00}

His examples include Solomon's judgement between the two women over the child, which he places in the two Sahihs from Abu Hurayra; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:11:30} the perspicacity of [[person/iyas-ibn-muawiya|Iyas ibn Mu'awiya]] and [[person/yahya-ibn-aktham|Yahya ibn Aktham]]; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:23:00} the story of [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj]] and a man seized for his brother's offence, who answered him with two verses of the Qur'an from the story of Joseph, so that al-Hajjaj ordered his chief of police, Yazid ibn Abi Muslim, to rebuild the man's house, restore his stipend and proclaim: God spoke the truth and the poet lied; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:33:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:34:00} and [[person/wahshi-ibn-harb|Wahshi]]'s recognising a masked man by his feet. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:38:00} He says that since he read al-Shafi'i's saying early in his studies he has collected stories of firasa from scholars' biographies and never seen one like this. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:41:30} He then counts the moments of heedlessness of Joseph's brothers, says that Ibn al-Jawzi relied for some of them on unsound Isra'iliyyat, and contrasts them with the alert middle brother among the owners of the garden in Surat al-Qalam. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:45:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep09 00:48:00}
