## Overview
*Tafwid* (delegation) in the attributes of God is affirming the wording of the attributes found in the Qur'an and the Sunna, while leaving aside any inquiry into their modality and committing their knowledge to God, as Ibn Qudama sets out in *Lum'at al-i'tiqad*. Within the question there is disagreement among the people of the Sunna over the interpretation of the sayings of the early generations: did they deny knowledge of the meaning, or only of the modality? {cite:lumat-al-itiqad-ibn-uraiyir-2022 p.49}

## The question
Is it enough to affirm the wording of the attributes while delegating their modality, or must their meanings be affirmed as befits God?

## Ibn Qudama's position
[[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|Ibn Qudama]] says in [[work/lumat-al-itiqad|Lum'at al-i'tiqad]] that what is obscure among the attributes must be affirmed in wording, and that its meaning is left alone. He attributes this to the early generations, naming al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, Malik, Sufyan al-Thawri, al-Awza'i, and others, with the phrase "let them pass as they came, without asking how." He rejects the method of the theologians in interpretation. {cite:lumat-al-itiqad-ibn-uraiyir-2022 p.49}

## Ibn Taymiyya's position
[[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]], in *al-Hamawiyya*, as the editor cites in the margins, argues that the saying of the early generations "the *istiwa'* is not unknown and the modality is not conceivable" shows that the meaning is known, for otherwise *istiwa'* would be unknown like the letters of the alphabet. For him, the early generations denied knowledge of the modality, not knowledge of the reality of the attribute. {cite:lumat-al-itiqad-ibn-uraiyir-2022 p.54}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
