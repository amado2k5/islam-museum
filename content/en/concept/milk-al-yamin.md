## Overview
For al-Sha'rawi, what the right hand owns belongs to the aim of ending slavery. He holds that Islam widened the ways of manumission, that the owned woman must be fed and clothed as the household is and not burdened beyond her capacity, and that the source of such ownership no longer exists in his time {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2007}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2008}.

## Ending slavery
He holds that Islam aims to bring slavery to an end, and that the expiations and other ways of freeing slaves widen the means of manumission, so that the law removes slavery rather than keeping it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2007}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2008}.

## The owned woman's due
He notes that the owned woman is fed from what the master eats, clothed as the household is clothed, and not burdened beyond her capacity, and that help given to her is a favour {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2008}.

## A child by the master
He holds that an owned woman who bears a child by her master is freed together with the child, and that this is part of ending slavery {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2008}. The word that carries this point is read from the scan and needs checking against the page image.
