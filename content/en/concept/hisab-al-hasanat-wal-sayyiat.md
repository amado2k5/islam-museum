## Summary
The Qur'an and the Sunnah speak an explicit language of account: deeds are written, figures are named, multiples are applied, bad deeds are erased, a Scale is set up and a record is read. This record gathers that language under the three things a visitor sees on the page: a list of every text that counts, writes, erases or weighs a deed; a calculator that reads the figures the texts name for particular acts; and a counter with which the visitor keeps a tally in the browser. The record describes what the texts contain. It is not a ruling and not a promise: every figure is attributed to its text, and every text is linked to its own page.

## The rule of the count
The root of the subject is a hadith qudsi related by Ibn Abbas: God has written the good deeds and the bad deeds and then made it plain. Whoever intends a good deed and does not do it has one full good deed written; if he does it, ten good deeds are written, up to seven hundred times, up to many times more; whoever intends a bad deed and does not do it has one full good deed written; if he does it, one bad deed is written [[passage/muslim-0131]] [[passage/bukhari-6491]]. Abu Hurayra's narration frames it as an instruction to the recording angels [[passage/muslim-0128]]. The Qur'an states the same base: a good deed has ten like it and a bad deed is repaid only its like [[passage/quran-6-160]] [[passage/quran-40-40]]; whoever brings a good deed has better than it [[passage/quran-28-84]]; and God wrongs no one by an atom's weight and multiplies a good deed [[passage/quran-4-40]]. The two sides are unequal from the first line: good deeds start at ten, bad deeds at one.

## Figures the texts name
After the rule come texts that name a figure for a particular act. A letter of the Qur'an is one good deed counted ten, and alif is a letter, lam a letter and mim a letter [[passage/tirmidhi-api-2910]]. A hundred tasbihs make a thousand good deeds [[passage/muslim-api-6852]]. A hundred tahlils in a day bring a hundred good deeds, a hundred bad deeds erased and the equal of freeing ten slaves [[passage/muwatta-1-560]]. Spending in the way of God is written seven hundred times over [[passage/tirmidhi-api-1625]], like the grain that grows seven ears in al-Baqara [[passage/quran-2-261]]. Prayer in congregation is better than prayer alone by twenty-seven ranks in one narration and twenty-five in another [[passage/muslim-0650]] [[passage/bukhari-api-646]]. One blessing upon the Prophet is answered with ten, with ten sins shed and ten ranks raised [[passage/nasai-api-1297]]. Fasting is taken out of the count: every deed of the son of Adam is multiplied except the fast, which is God's and which He rewards Himself [[passage/ibnmajah-1638]].

## Erasure and expiation
The texts give bad deeds a way out of the ledger. The Qur'an states that good deeds take away bad deeds [[passage/quran-11-114]], that avoiding the major sins remits the lesser ones [[passage/quran-4-31]], and that the one who repents has bad deeds exchanged for good [[passage/quran-25-70]]. In the Sunnah: follow a bad deed with a good one and it wipes it out [[passage/arbaun-18]]; sins leave with the water of ablution [[passage/muslim-api-577]]; each step to the mosque raises a rank and sheds a sin [[passage/bukhari-api-647]]; the five prayers, Friday and Ramadan expiate what lies between them when the major sins are avoided [[passage/muslim-api-552]]; fasting Ramadan and standing its nights forgive what is past [[passage/bukhari-api-38]]; a hajj without lewdness or wrongdoing returns a person as on the day his mother bore him [[passage/bukhari-api-1521]]; the fast of Arafa expiates two years and Ashura one [[passage/muslim-1162]]; and a thorn that pricks the believer raises him a rank or sheds a sin [[passage/muslim-api-6562]]. Most of these texts name no figure for what is erased, so the calculator shows them as written results, not as numbers.

## Measures that are not numbers
Some texts weigh in something other than the good deed. The qirat is a measure of reward: one for praying over a funeral and two for staying until the burial, like two great mountains [[passage/bukhari-api-1325]]; and a qirat is lost each day from the deeds of one who keeps a dog for neither farming, herding nor hunting [[passage/bukhari-api-2322]]. Equivalence is another measure: Surat al-Ikhlas equals a third of the Qur'an [[passage/muslim-api-1888]], and an umra in Ramadan equals a hajj [[passage/muslim-api-3037]]. Weight is a third: two phrases light on the tongue are heavy in the Scale [[passage/muslim-2694]], and praise of God fills the Scale [[passage/muslim-0223]].

## The scribes, the record and the Scale
The counter in the texts is not a figure of speech. Over every person are noble recording guardians who know what he does [[passage/quran-82-11]]; no word is uttered without a watcher at hand [[passage/quran-50-17]]; and the angels of night and day follow one another in turn [[passage/bukhari-0555]]. The outcome is a record that leaves out nothing small or great [[passage/quran-18-49]], handed to its owner to read and to be his own reckoner [[passage/quran-17-14]]. Then the Scale is set: the scales of justice in which no soul is wronged at all [[passage/quran-21-47]]; those whose scales are heavy prosper and those whose scales are light lose [[passage/quran-7-8]] [[passage/quran-101-6]]; and an atom's weight of good or of evil is seen [[passage/quran-99-7]] [[passage/quran-99-8]].

## Settling between people
A balance does not necessarily stay with its owner. The bankrupt is the one who comes with prayer, fasting and alms but has wronged people; they are paid from his good deeds, and when these run out their bad deeds are laid on him [[passage/muslim-2581]]. Whoever calls to guidance has the like of the rewards of those who follow him, and whoever calls to error bears the like of their sins [[passage/muslim-2674]]; the Qur'an speaks of those who carry their own burdens and some of the burdens of those they mislead [[passage/quran-16-25]]. The first thing a servant is reckoned for is his prayer [[passage/abudawud-0864]], and whoever is examined closely in the reckoning is punished [[passage/bukhari-api-6536]].

## How to read the list, the calculator and the counter
The list is automatic: any text in the corpus that carries a counting mark enters it the day the book that holds it is harvested, with no manual tagging. The calculator adds only what a text names: it multiplies the number of times by the stated figure, shows a range where the text gives a range, and writes the result out where the text gives no number. The counter keeps the visitor's tally in the visitor's own browser. None of this is a judgement on a deed or a person: the texts themselves hang the figure on intention, acceptance, the avoiding of major sins and the settling of wrongs, and leave what lies above the figure to God's will.
