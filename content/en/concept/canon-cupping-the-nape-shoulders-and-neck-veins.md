## Summary
Ibn Sina says cupping on the nape stands in for the median cutaneous vein, on the withers for the basilic and on the neck veins for the cephalic, and lists the benefits and harms of each place {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.

## The nape
Cupping on the nape stands in for the median cutaneous vein; it helps heaviness of the brows, lightens the eyelid and helps mange of the eye, foul breath and hardening in the eye. It brings forgetfulness, as is said, because the back of the brain is the seat of memory {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.

## The withers
The withers stand in for the basilic vein, and cupping there helps pain of the shoulder and the throat. It weakens the mouth of the stomach, and when done on the withers it helps the bloody diseases of the chest and bloody asthma, but weakens the stomach and causes palpitation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.

## The two neck veins
Cupping on one of the two neck veins stands in for the cephalic; it helps trembling of the head and the organs of the head, such as the face, teeth, molars, ears, eyes, throat and nose. The neck cupping may bring on trembling of the head, so the cupper should place the nape cup a little lower and the withers cup a little higher, unless the aim is to treat a flow of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.
