## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat critiques the capitalist doctrine in terms of its goal and its means, arguing that its aim is to achieve wealth and material well-being, its means is unleashing individual freedom without restriction, and the result is the loss of the human being himself {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 13}.

## Absolute Freedom
The author states that capitalism gave human beings absolute freedom of action to acquire wealth, and that the naive thought this the highest meaning of freedom, though practice showed that the loser is the human being {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 13}. He argues that excess in individual freedom leads to chaos, summarizing this with the maxim "laissez-faire, laissez-passer" {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 13}.

## Its Effects as Presented by the Author
The author enumerates effects he observes: class conflict between factory workers and employers, the spread of exploitation, monopoly, bribery, and nepotism, the proliferation of venues for alcohol and gambling, and the degradation of trade {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 13}. He describes the worker's condition as laboring for a meager wage from dawn until night under coercion, which gave rise to the labor movement that raised its voice {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 14}.

## Comparison with Communism
The author views capitalism and communism as two faces of modern idolatry: the first revolves around the individual to the point of deification, and the second revolves around matter to the point of deification {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 16}. See [[concept/al-shuyuiyya|Communism in Qa'dat's Critique]].

## Note
This is the author's critique of the doctrine, containing value judgments and general characterizations that have not been measured. It is presented here as his opinion; neither the response of defenders of the free economy nor an analysis of its schools is presented in this section, so it needs to be supplemented.

## From the book of Zaydan Qa'dat: pp. 83–164 of the PDF file (Working Capital in the Context of the Book)
Qa'dat stipulates that legitimate saving must take the form of productive working capital that contributes to building society, and not be harnessed for monopoly or grave sins {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 129}. He regards working assets in the hands of their owner, such as a tractor and agricultural machinery, as private property that may not be seized, because they constitute the working capital without which the means to satisfy needs cannot be realized {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 153}.
