## Summary
Al-Battani defines the root as the number which multiplied by itself gives the given number, then shows that the root of degrees is degrees, that the root of the even orders below the degree, seconds, fourths and sixths, falls in the higher order at half the distance, and that the odd orders, minutes and thirds, have no definite root unless they are extended to the order beneath them until they become even. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.14-15}.

## The definition
The root of any absolute number is what, multiplied by its like, gives the given number. Al-Battani adds that rooting the orders does not follow from the condition of multiplying some by others, since only the degree order is required, because the root of degrees is degrees: degrees multiplied by degrees give degrees. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.14}.

## Even and odd orders
For fractions below the degree, those of the even kind, seconds, fourths and sixths, have a root in the order higher by half the distance, so seconds have minutes for roots and fourths have seconds; those of the odd kind, minutes and thirds, have no definite root unless extended to the order beneath until they reach the even kind, so minutes are extended to seconds and thirds to fourths. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.15}.
