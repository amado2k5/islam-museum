## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter 19 of the second lesson, on the causes that call for bathing, basking in the sun, burial in sand and rolling in it, soaking in oils and sprinkling water on the face, by naming the natural action of the bath, warming through its air or moistening through its water, and the nature of its three rooms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.140}.

## The natural action
He quotes the saying of some pedants that the best bath is the one with an old building, wide air and sweet water, to which another added that the furnace should be set to the temperament of whoever is to enter. He then states that the natural action of the bath is warming by its air or moistening by its water, and that the first room cools and moistens, the second warms and moistens, and the third warms and dries {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.140}.

## Against denying moistening
He says no heed should be paid to those who hold that water does not moisten the principal organs by being absorbed or wrapped around them, because after its first effects the bath produces other changes, some by accident and some in itself. By its air it may cool, through heavy dissolving of the innate heat, and it may dry the substance of the organs by dissolving much of their innate moisture, even if it supplies foreign moisture. When its water is very hot the skin shivers and closes its pores, so none of its moisture reaches the body and the dissolving is poor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.140}.
