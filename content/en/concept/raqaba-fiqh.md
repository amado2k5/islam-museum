## Summary
Raqaba in the lexicon is the neck, and by a figure of speech the whole person; it also means a slave, as in the phrase 'freeing a neck'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8853} Wiping the neck in ablution is recommended by the Hanafis, disliked by the Malikis and not recommended by the Hanbalis; the Shafi'is add washing the side of the neck to the lengthening of the white mark. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8853} If a husband attaches divorce to his wife's neck, the divorce takes effect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8853} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8854} If he likens his wife's neck to his mother's, the majority hold it a zihar, while the Hanafis do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8854} The term raqaba names a slave in manumission, the expiations and contracts of writing-off. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8854}
