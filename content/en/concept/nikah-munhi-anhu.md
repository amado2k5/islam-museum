## Summary
A forbidden marriage is one the lawgiver has forbidden. The encyclopedia sets out its kinds, among them the marriage of banners, the marriage of a group, the marriage of seeking offspring, and the marriage of exchange (shighar), with each school's ruling on each kind. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16154}

## Forms of marriage forbidden in the pre-Islamic period
**The marriage of banners.** Prostitutes set up banners at their doors as signs; whoever wanted them went in. If one conceived and bore a child, a group gathered, the physiognomists were called, and the child was attributed to whichever man they judged, and he could not refuse. The forbidding of it rests on the verse "leave the outward sin", and on Aisha's report, which says that the Prophet pulled down all the marriages of the age of ignorance except the marriage people practise today. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155}
**The marriage of a group.** Some men, fewer than ten, from one tribe or several, shared intercourse with a woman, with her consent; when she bore a child she named the man she wished, and he could not refuse it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155}
**The marriage of seeking offspring.** A man told his wife, after her purification, to seek intercourse with a noble or brave man, and he kept away from her until her pregnancy showed, for the sake of a noble child. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15155} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155}
**The marriage of exchange (shighar).** A man gives his daughter to another on condition that the other gives his daughter, with no dower between them. The jurists agree that it is forbidden in Islam, on the hadith of Ibn Umar that the Prophet forbade shighar, on the hadith of Anas "there is no shighar in Islam", and on the hadith of Jabir. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16156}
**The ruling on shighar and its ground.** The majority of Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that shighar marriage is invalid. Its agreed form is that one says "I marry you my daughter, on condition that you marry me your daughter, and the sexual part of each is the other's dower", and the other accepts. They differ on its ground: the Malikis say it is the absence of a dower, a view also held by some Shafi'is; most Shafi'is say it is the sharing of the sexual part, since each sexual part becomes the object of the contract and its dower, and the prohibition is on the marriage itself, not on the dower, since an invalid dower does not void a marriage; others say it is conditioning and suspension. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16156} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16158} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16159}
**Kinds of shighar in the Maliki school.** The pure shighar, in which no dower is named for either, is void and is dissolved at all times, before and after intercourse, by an irrevocable divorce; it has the effects of a valid marriage for prohibition and inheritance if one party dies before the dissolution. The shighar with a dower, in which each is named a dower but one marriage depends on the other, is dissolved before intercourse without dues, and after it stands on the higher of the named dower and the dower of the like. The mixed kind, in which one is named a dower and the other is not, follows the pure rule for the one and the mixed rule for the other. The child is attached to the father and the prescribed penalty is averted in all three kinds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16157} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16158}
**Shighar in the Shafi'i school.** If the sexual part is not made a dower, the sounder view is that the marriage is valid and only the named dower is void, owing the dower of the like to each; if the sexual part and a sum of a thousand dinars are both made dower for each, both contracts are void in the sounder view, since the sexual part is shared; if one makes his daughter's contract conditional on the other's daughter's sexual part being his own daughter's dower, his daughter's marriage stands in the sounder view and the other is void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16159} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16160}
**Shighar in the Hanbali school.** Among the void forms: one who marries another his ward on condition the other marries him his ward, with no dower; or the sexual part of each made the dower of the other. Ahmad's report never varied that shighar is void; a report from Umar and Zayd ibn Thabit says they separated the two spouses in a shighar. If a dower is named for each without sharing, both marriages stand with the named dower, which is the school's view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16160}

## The Hanbali account of shighar, the marriage of a companion, and temporary marriage
**The Hanbalis, continued.** If a dower is named for each that is separate from the sexual part of the other, the marriage stands; if the dower named is coins and the other's sexual part, it does not. The named dower must not be a trivial trick, since tricks do not make lawful what is forbidden. Al-Kharaqi holds the whole marriage void. If a dower is named for one and not the other, the marriage of the one named stands and the other is void; Abu Bakr of the Hanbalis holds both void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16161}
**The Hanafis: shighar is valid.** Their form is that a man marries his daughter on condition the other marries him his daughter, with each sexual part the other's dower and no other dower. The marriage stands and the dower of the like is owed to each, since a non-dower is named; the prohibition, they say, targets the empty form of shighar, so a dower of the like is owed, as if the wife were married for something that is not property, such as wine or carrion. If the marriage has a dower, or the sexual parts are not made dowers, or each names a dower, it is not shighar but a valid marriage by agreement. This is the view of al-Zuhri, Makhul, al-Thawri, al-Layth, a report from Ahmad, Ishaq and Abu Thawr. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16161} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16162}
**The marriage of a companion (nikah al-khidn).** A khidn is a man who secretly has relations with a woman; the Arabs disapproved of open fornication but not of having lovers, and God forbade both in the verse "do not approach the indecencies, outward or hidden", and allowed intercourse only in a valid marriage or with a right hand's possession. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16162} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16163}
**Temporary marriage (nikah al-mut'a).** This is a man's saying to a woman, "I give you such and such, on condition that I enjoy you for a day, a month or a year", or for an unknown term, such as the season of the pilgrimage, or while the man stays in the town, or until a certain man arrives; when the term ends the separation happens without divorce. It was a practice of the age of ignorance, lawful at the beginning of Islam, then forbidden. Ali reported that the Prophet forbade it on the day of Khaybar; it was permitted on the day of the conquest; some say at the farewell pilgrimage; then forbidden for ever, on the report of al-Rabi' ibn Sabra. Al-Shafi'i said he knew nothing forbidden, then permitted, then forbidden, except temporary marriage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16162} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16163}
**The first view: prohibition.** The majority (the Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is, and in the sounder school view the Hanbalis) hold temporary marriage forbidden and void. Their evidence: the report of al-Rabi' that God has forbidden it until the Day of Resurrection; Ali's report that it was for one who could not find a wife and was abrogated by the rules of marriage, divorce, waiting and inheritance; Abu Hurayra's report that marriage, divorce, the waiting period and inheritance forbid it; the verse that the lawful is only the spouses or the slave-women; Ibn Abbas's word that every private part besides these is forbidden; and that marriage is not made for appetite alone. They explain the verse "whom you enjoy of them, give them their wages" as enjoyment by marriage, not hire, and the dower as called a wage within marriage itself; it is abrogated, according to Ibn Abbas by the verse of divorce, and according to Ibn Mas'ud by the verses on divorce, dower, waiting and inheritance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16163} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16164} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16165}
**The second view: permission.** Reported from Ibn Abbas, held by most of his companions, such as Ata' and Tawus, and by Ibn Jurayj, and reported from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Jabir. They cite the apparent words "whom you enjoy of them, give them their wages", Umar's saying "there were two temporary enjoyments in the Prophet's time, I forbid them and punish for them: the temporary marriage of women and the temporary pilgrimage", Ibn Mas'ud's report of the leave given in the campaign, and Jabir's "we enjoyed a handful of dates and flour". They say temporary marriage is a contract on a benefit, so a term may be set, as in hire. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16165} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16166}

## The prohibition of temporary marriage, its form, effects, penalty, the fixed-term marriage, and the marriage with intent to divorce
**The argument for permanent prohibition.** The majority hold that the permanent prohibition of temporary marriage is established from the Prophet, by the report of Sabra al-Juhani at the conquest of Makka: "I did not leave until the Messenger forbade it". The dissent of some Companions does not invalidate the report, since most Companions remembered the prohibition and acted on it; Umar said "the Messenger allowed us temporary marriage three times, then forbade it, and by God, I will stone anyone I know who enjoys it while married." Abu Hurayra's report says that marriage, divorce, the waiting period and inheritance abolish it; Jabir's report says the women they enjoyed were forbidden until the Day of Resurrection. Ibn Mas'ud's report of the permission does not mention a date, so the reports of prohibition govern it. Al-Jassas argues from the Companions' agreement that if permission remained they would not have agreed to forbid it, and that Ibn Abbas withdrew his view. Al-Mazari says the reports establish its abrogation, and that consensus fixed its prohibition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16167} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16168} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16169}
**The form of temporary marriage.** The Malikis and Shafi'is hold no difference in its nullity between the word "enjoyment" and the words "marriage" or "giving in marriage" with a term; "I marry you for ten days" is void, and they call it fixed-term or time-limited marriage. The Hanafis hold that temporary marriage is void: it is saying to a woman free of impediments "I enjoy you for ten days for such a sum"; they distinguish it from a time-limited marriage, which is stated with the words of marriage and witnesses, while enjoyment is stated with the word for enjoyment and without witnesses. Ibn al-Humam adds that the reports do not specify the wording; the sense governs, and no Companion is known to have said "I have enjoyed you". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16169}
**The Hanbali forms of temporary marriage.** (a) marrying a woman for a known or unknown term; (b) stipulating the divorce at a time, as "I give you my daughter for a month, or until the season ends"; (c) intending in the heart the divorce at a time; (d) saying "let me enjoy you", and she says "I have enjoyed you", without a guardian or witnesses. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16170}
**The effects of temporary marriage.** (a) No divorce, no ila', no zihar, no inheritance, no li'an, no muhsan status, and no lifting of a triple-divorce bar for the first husband; by agreement of those who call it void, and they are separated. (b) Nothing is owed before intercourse; after it, the dower of the like is owed according to the Shafi'is, a Hanbali report and a Maliki view, since the term harms the dower; the Hanafis owe the lesser of the named dower and the dower of the like, or the dower of the like if none is named; the Malikis and the school's view of the Hanbalis owe the named dower, since its invalidity comes from the contract. (c) A child born of it is attributed to the man, whether he believed it a valid marriage or not, by the shubha of the contract; the period is counted from intercourse, according to Muhammad of the Hanafis, the view on which the fatwa rests. (d) Intercourse establishes in-law prohibition between the man and the woman and their ascendants and descendants. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16170} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16171}
**The penalty for temporary marriage.** The majority (the Hanafis, the Hanbalis, the school's view of the Malikis, and the sounder Shafi'i view) hold that there is no prescribed penalty for one who enters a temporary marriage, man or woman, since penalties are averted by doubt, and the doubt here is that of disagreement; but one who knows the prohibition is disciplined for a sin with neither penalty nor expiation. The Shafi'i counterpart and a weak Maliki view hold that the prescribed penalty is due for the man and woman, since its abrogation is established. The Shafi'is say the penalty falls if the contract was made with a guardian and two witnesses, and is due if he had intercourse knowingly without them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16171}
**The fixed-term marriage.** The Hanafis define it as marrying a woman with two witnesses for ten days, a month or a year. Temporary marriage differs from it in using the word for marriage and witnesses; Zufar says temporary marriage exists only with its own word. If the term is one they never reach, the Hanafis, Hanbalis, Abu al-Hasan of the Malikis and al-Bulqini and some later Shafi'is hold the marriage valid as if unrestricted, since an unrestricted marriage is no more than that; the Malikis and the Shafi'is other than al-Bulqini hold it void. If the term is one they reach, such as ten days, it is void according to the Hanafis (other than Zufar), the Malikis, the Shafi'is and the Hanbalis, since it is a form of temporary marriage; Zufar holds the marriage valid and permanent and the condition void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16171} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16172}
**The marriage with intent to divorce.** The Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is and, in a view the Mughni affirms, the Hanbalis hold that marrying a woman intending to divorce her after a month or more or less is a valid marriage, whether the woman or her guardian knows the intent or not, since the contract carries no condition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16172}
