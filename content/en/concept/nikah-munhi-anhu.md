## Summary
A forbidden marriage is one the lawgiver has forbidden. The encyclopedia sets out its kinds, among them the marriage of banners, the marriage of a group, the marriage of seeking offspring, and the marriage of exchange (shighar), with each school's ruling on each kind. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16154}

## Forms of marriage forbidden in the pre-Islamic period
**The marriage of banners.** Prostitutes set up banners at their doors as signs; whoever wanted them went in. If one conceived and bore a child, a group gathered, the physiognomists were called, and the child was attributed to whichever man they judged, and he could not refuse. The forbidding of it rests on the verse "leave the outward sin", and on Aisha's report, which says that the Prophet pulled down all the marriages of the age of ignorance except the marriage people practise today. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155}
**The marriage of a group.** Some men, fewer than ten, from one tribe or several, shared intercourse with a woman, with her consent; when she bore a child she named the man she wished, and he could not refuse it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155}
**The marriage of seeking offspring.** A man told his wife, after her purification, to seek intercourse with a noble or brave man, and he kept away from her until her pregnancy showed, for the sake of a noble child. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15155} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155}
**The marriage of exchange (shighar).** A man gives his daughter to another on condition that the other gives his daughter, with no dower between them. The jurists agree that it is forbidden in Islam, on the hadith of Ibn Umar that the Prophet forbade shighar, on the hadith of Anas "there is no shighar in Islam", and on the hadith of Jabir. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16155} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16156}
**The ruling on shighar and its ground.** The majority of Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that shighar marriage is invalid. Its agreed form is that one says "I marry you my daughter, on condition that you marry me your daughter, and the sexual part of each is the other's dower", and the other accepts. They differ on its ground: the Malikis say it is the absence of a dower, a view also held by some Shafi'is; most Shafi'is say it is the sharing of the sexual part, since each sexual part becomes the object of the contract and its dower, and the prohibition is on the marriage itself, not on the dower, since an invalid dower does not void a marriage; others say it is conditioning and suspension. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16156} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16158} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16159}
**Kinds of shighar in the Maliki school.** The pure shighar, in which no dower is named for either, is void and is dissolved at all times, before and after intercourse, by an irrevocable divorce; it has the effects of a valid marriage for prohibition and inheritance if one party dies before the dissolution. The shighar with a dower, in which each is named a dower but one marriage depends on the other, is dissolved before intercourse without dues, and after it stands on the higher of the named dower and the dower of the like. The mixed kind, in which one is named a dower and the other is not, follows the pure rule for the one and the mixed rule for the other. The child is attached to the father and the prescribed penalty is averted in all three kinds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16157} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16158}
**Shighar in the Shafi'i school.** If the sexual part is not made a dower, the sounder view is that the marriage is valid and only the named dower is void, owing the dower of the like to each; if the sexual part and a sum of a thousand dinars are both made dower for each, both contracts are void in the sounder view, since the sexual part is shared; if one makes his daughter's contract conditional on the other's daughter's sexual part being his own daughter's dower, his daughter's marriage stands in the sounder view and the other is void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16159} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16160}
**Shighar in the Hanbali school.** Among the void forms: one who marries another his ward on condition the other marries him his ward, with no dower; or the sexual part of each made the dower of the other. Ahmad's report never varied that shighar is void; a report from Umar and Zayd ibn Thabit says they separated the two spouses in a shighar. If a dower is named for each without sharing, both marriages stand with the named dower, which is the school's view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16160}
