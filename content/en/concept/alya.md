## Definition
Alya is the rump, the flesh that rises between the back and the thigh, the flesh and fat that sit over the buttock. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3332}

## Rulings
- Sleep: the Hanafis and Shafi'is hold that a person who has performed ablution and sleeps with his rump on the ground does not void it, since nothing that voids ablution is likely to emerge. The Malikis look at the sleep alone, heavy or light; the Hanbalis look at both the posture and the sleep. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3332}
- Sacrifice: the jurists differ on a goat with no rump, or whose tail is cut, in four views: the Malikis forbid it outright; the Shafi'is allow it if created without a rump and forbid it if the rump was cut off; the Hanafis distinguish a cut of most or of a little; the Hanbalis allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3332}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3333}
- Injury: for a deliberate injury to the rump, qisas is due in the majority view; al-Muzani denied qisas, since the rump is flesh joined to flesh like the thigh. For an accidental injury, half a diya is due for one rump and the full diya for both. The Malikis hold a fair assessment for the rump, except that Ashhab distinguished a man's rump from a woman's. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3333}
- Other places: the sitting positions in prayer, and the binding of the two rumps in the shroud with cotton placed between them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3333}
