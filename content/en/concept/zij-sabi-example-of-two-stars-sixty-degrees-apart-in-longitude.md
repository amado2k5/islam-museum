## Summary
Al-Battani draws a piece of the ecliptic with two points on it and a pole of the ecliptic, and puts one of the two stars in its place on the circle and the other off it in latitude on an arc of the circle through the poles. He then takes an example: the arc of longitude between the two stars is sixty parts and the latitude of one of them is thirty parts, and he shows by proportions in the spherical figure that the true distance between them differs from the sixty of longitude alone. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.61-63}.

## The result of the example
The working ends in a root that is turned into an arc and gives the true distance, which is not the sixty that lay between the stars in longitude only. The intermediate figures of the example are disturbed in the scan and are not given; it is enough that the text uses the example to show that latitude changes the distance from the mere difference of longitude. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.63}.
