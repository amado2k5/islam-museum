## Summary
Rasul in the lexicon is one sent to carry a message; in technical use it has two senses: one sent from one person to another with money or a message, and one of the messengers of God, a free male to whom God revealed a law and whom He commanded to convey it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8733} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8734} The messenger must convey the call, and those who receive it must believe in and obey the messengers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8734} The scholars agree that whoever insults or mocks a messenger disbelieves, and the insulter of the Prophet is killed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8734} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8735} An animal may not be slaughtered in the name of the Messenger of God. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8735} The Prophet set aside pastures at al-Naqi' for the horses of the Muslims and at al-Rabadha for camels of charity, and the majority forbid later rulers to cancel what he protected. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8735} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8736} Envoys of the enemy who come with a message are safe until they deliver it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8736}
