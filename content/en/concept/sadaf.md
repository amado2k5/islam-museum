## Actions and properties
The flesh of the wild shell, ground and smeared on the body, dries strongly; the burnt purple shell has a dispersing, cleansing power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Cosmetics
All the covers and shells of molluscs, burnt, clear vitiligo, and the purple shell cooked in oil and rubbed on the hair holds back its falling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Swellings and pustules
The viscosity of the snail, called its pus, with frankincense, aloes and myrrh, dries the swellings that arise at the root of the ear, and even where it meets a deep moisture in them it heals it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Wounds and ulcers
The ash of the purple shell cleanses ulcers, purifies them and heals them; the burnt shell with salt as a powder benefits burns from fire; every shell ash is beneficial for scab, and the shell with its flesh benefits wounds, especially those on the nerve, ground with frankincense and myrrh so that it sticks, and likewise with mill-dust; Galen tried the whole snail as it is {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Joints
The shell calms the pains and swellings of gout and is applied as it is as a poultice on all swellings of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Organs of the head
The ash of the purple shell cleanses the teeth, especially what is burnt with salt, and the shell ground as it is with vinegar stops nosebleed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Ocular organs
The ash of any shell with its flesh, washed, enters eye salves and melts the thickness of the lid, the white spot and the film; the burnt flesh of the kind known as old taylas, mixed with tar, ground and dropped on the lid, does not let the hair grow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}; the viscosity on the wild kind glues the hair turned over on the lid, and the snail's viscosity, if the forehead is smeared with it, checks the matters flowing to the eye and glues the hair too {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.639}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
The flesh of the shell known as farufus is good for the stomach, and the flesh of shells, neither cooked nor roasted, calms pain of the stomach; the purple shell drunk with vinegar removes the spleen, and when dropsy is bound with the shell it does not leave until it lowers it; the wild shell is strong in this for its great drying {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.639}

## Excretory organs
The flesh of the purple shell does not loosen the bowels, but the flesh of the shell called talibis in Syria, when fresh, softens the belly, especially its broth, and likewise the broth of small shells {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.639}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that fumigation with the purple shell benefits women with suffocation of the womb and that this fumigation brings out the afterbirth, that the sweet-smelling fumigation and the Babylonian qulzumi on the coast also benefit suffocation of the womb and rouse the epileptic, and that the shell brings on the menses used as a pessary {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.639}

## Intestines
It is said that the kind known as fuhil, burnt as it is and its ash mixed with green gall-nut and white pepper, benefits greatly the ulcers that arise in the intestines while they are fresh and have not spoiled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.639}

## Poisons and bites
Its flesh benefits the bite of a rabid dog {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.639}
