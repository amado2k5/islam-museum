## Summary
Niyaha in the lexical sense is wailing aloud, as in keening; the jurists' definitions differ. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16701}

## Definitions by school
For the Hanafis it is weeping with praise of the dead; for the Shafi'is and most Malikis it is raising the voice in lament, even without weeping; for the Hanbalis it is raising the voice in lament with a chanted or rhymed cry. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16701}

## Related terms
Niyaha differs from weeping, elegy, condolence and the announcement of death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16702} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16703}

## Ruling
The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold wailing forbidden; the Hanafis hold it disliked in the sense of forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16703}
The prophetic prohibitions are many, among them the report of Umm Atiyya at the pledge and the report of Aisha on throwing dust into the mouths of the mourners. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16703} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16704} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16705}

## Weeping over one who is dying
Some Malikis distinguish mere weeping from weeping with raised voice or ugly words, and allow loud crying before death when no ugly words are added. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16706}

## The punishment of the dead for wailing
Most jurists hold that the dead is punished for wailing over him only if he bequeathed it; they interpret the reports to the contrary. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16707} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16708} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16709}
A bequest to wail is void and forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16709}

## Punishment of the wailing woman
Most jurists hold that the imam must discipline a wailing woman; others counsel her and give respite without beating. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16709} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16710}

## Hire and earnings
Hire for wailing is void, and its earnings are unlawful according to the majority; the Hanafis allow a fee where custom makes it expected. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16710} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16711}
