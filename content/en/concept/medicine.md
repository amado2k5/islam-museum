## Summary
In the introduction to his book "The Canon," Ibn Sina defines medicine as the science by which the states of the human body are known, insofar as it is healthy or departs from health, so that the health one has may be preserved and what has been lost may be recovered {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print p.2}.

## Medicine as theory and practice
Ibn Sina explains that medicine is divided into theoretical and practical parts. He means by this not a division between teaching knowledge and carrying out the work, but that one of its two parts is the science of the principles of medicine and the other is the science of how to practise it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print p.2}.

## Its subjects and causes
Among the subjects of medicine in his view are the elements, temperaments, humours, organs, spirits, powers and actions, together with the states of the body in health, sickness and the middle state, and their causes. The physician needs to know the causes of health and sickness in four kinds: material, efficient, formal and final {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print p.3} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print p.4}.

## What is accepted and what is demonstrated
The physician takes certain principles from natural science, such as the elements and the nature of the temperaments and humours. He is not required to prove them within his craft. In medicine, what is demonstrated concerns diseases and their causes, signs and treatment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print p.4}.
