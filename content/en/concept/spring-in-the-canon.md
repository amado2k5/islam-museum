## Summary
Ibn Sina calls a spring that keeps to its temperament the best of the seasons, lists the chronic diseases it stirs and what eases them, and says it suits boys {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.119}.

## Its temperament
A spring on its own temperament is the best season, matched to the temperament of spirit and blood. In its moderation it leans to a gentle, heavenly warmth and a natural moisture, and it reddens the colour because it draws blood moderately without dissolving it as summer does. When spring is long and moderate, summer's diseases are fewer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.119}.

## Chronic diseases
Chronic diseases stir in spring because it sets stagnant humours moving and flowing; melancholia is stirred, and those whose humours piled up in winter from greed and little exercise are readied for illness. Its diseases include bloody flux, nosebleed, rupture of vessels, spitting of blood and cough, especially in a spring that resembles winter, and those with obstructions fare worse. In phlegmatic people it moves the phlegm and brings apoplexy, palsy and pains in the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.119}.

## Regimen
Excess of bodily or mental motion and the taking of warming things aid the nature of the air. What eases spring's diseases is bleeding, evacuation, less food, more drink, and weakening intoxicating wine with water. Spring suits boys and those near them in age {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.119}.
