## Summary
Ibn Sina reports that Hippocrates orders vomiting on two consecutive days each month so that the second makes good what fell short in the first, lists its benefits (clearing phlegm and bile, cleansing the stomach, brightening sight, driving off indigestion and more), and advises once or twice a month when full, with no fixed cycle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.

## Hippocrates' instruction
Hippocrates orders the use of vomiting on two consecutive days in the month, so that the second makes good what was missed or hard in the first and brings out what flows to the stomach; Hippocrates guarantees health will be kept with it, and overdoing it is bad {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.

## Benefits
Such vomiting empties phlegm and bile and cleanses the stomach, which has nothing to clear it as the bowels have the bile poured into them; it removes heaviness in the head, brightens the sight, drives off indigestion and helps one whose stomach receives bile that spoils his food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}. It removes the stomach's aversion to fat, the loss of healthy appetite and the craving for pungent, sour and astringent things, and helps bodily flabbiness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.

## Diseases
He calls it a strong treatment for leprosy, a bad complexion, gastric epilepsy, jaundice, orthopnoea, tremor and paralysis, and a good one for those with ringworm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.

## Frequency and temperament
It should be used once or twice a month on fullness, without keeping a known cycle or fixed number of days; vomiting suits best one whose original temperament is bilious and lean {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.
