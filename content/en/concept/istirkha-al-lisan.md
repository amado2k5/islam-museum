## Its cause and the matter's signs
The tongue's relaxation is among the kinds of relaxation already named, from the known cause: it may come from a sanguine watery moisture, from a cause in the brain, or in its moving nerve or the branch coming from it to the tongue; what comes with the brain's sharing and what without is told as in the other organs drawing sense and movement from the brain. That the matter is sanguine is shown by the tongue's redness and heat; that it is thin and watery by the great flow of thin saliva, little benefit from the dissolvers and benefit from what has astringing power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.255}
## The speech defects and the boys' state
The relaxation may carry the tongue to the loss of speech, its difficulty or its alteration — among them the fa'fa', the tamtam, the muta'ti' and the lisp (the fa'fa' is one who much repeats the fa' or whom speech's exit defeats; the tamtam who errs in pronunciation as if his letters were ta' and mim; the muta'ti' who is helpless with his speech; the lisper who cannot utter a sound ra' and says it lam or ghayn, by the copyists' explanation). Some boys are long unable to speak; among the stammerers there is one on whom a hot disease falls and whose tongue is released by the melting of the stammering moisture held in the roots of his nerves; and so the lisping boy whose moisture moderates as he grows returns eloquent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.256}
## Its treatment
The body is purified, then the head's quarter is aimed at with its own drugs as taught in the management of the head's diseases; as for the drugs specific to the place, most often it is rubbing with the cutting dissolvers, gargling their waters and rinsing with them of this kind, and the tongue may profit by being rubbed until much saliva flows from it; these drugs and their like are also laid on the neck, and pills may be made of them kneaded with what slows their dissolving, a pill held under the tongue; and if the boy is slow to speak, his tongue must be moved and rubbed and his salivas made to flow (the recipes, names and quantities are left out) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.256} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.257}
## A report carried
A man was cupped and the lancet struck a nervous filament beside the membrane joined to the tongue, and it relaxed the tongue (a happening the book carries as a report, not as a procedure) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.258}
