## Nature
A resin like sandarac, broken into yellow, white and pale colours, sometimes reddish, which draws straw and chaff to itself; hence its Persian name, the straw-taker. It is the gum of the Roman walnut tree, compounded of a warm watery part and a fine earthy part, and the book says it is made of a fine earthy part and a dry watery part. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}

## Temperament and actions
Slightly hot and dry in the second degree. It is an astringent especially for blood from any site; its strength is compared with the flower of its tree, the Roman walnut's flower, but it is colder than that. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}

## Swellings, head and eye
Some say that it is used on hot swellings with benefit. It stops nosebleed and the downward flow from the head to the lungs, and it enters remedies for the eye. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}

## Chest and digestion
The book says it helps palpitation and strongly prevents the spitting of blood. It stops vomiting and keeps bad humours away from the stomach, and with mastic it strengthens the stomach. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling): the book says it stops bleeding of the womb and the anus, and helps tenesmus. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}
