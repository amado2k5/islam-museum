## Summary
Ibn Sina lists what the colours of the body show about temperament: white, red, yellow and fair, dusky, aubergine, lime-white, leaden and ivory, then the colour of the tongue and of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.160}.

## Colours and what they show
White shows an absence or scarcity of blood with cold, since with heat and bilious humour it would be yellow. Red shows abundant blood and heat. Yellow and fair show much heat, yellow pointing more to bile and fair to blood or bilious blood; yellow may also show absence of blood without bile, as in convalescents. Duskiness shows severe cold, so that blood is scant and the little there is congeals and turns black {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.160}.

## Further colours
Aubergine shows cold and dryness since it follows pure black bile. Lime-white shows pure cold and phlegm. Leaden shows cold and moisture with some black bile, being white with a touch of green. Ivory shows phlegmatic cold with a little bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.160}.

## Tongue, eye and the single organ
Mostly colour changes to yellow and white through the liver, to yellow and black through the spleen, and to yellow and green in haemorrhoidal ailments, though not always. Reading the tongue's colour gives a strong sign of the temperament of the quiet and the beating vessels; reading the eye's colour a strong sign of the brain's. Two organs may differ in colour in one illness, the tongue turning white while the face turns dark in jaundice from intense burning of bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.160}.
