## Summary
Ibn Sina warns against bleeding on a fullness from food, lest unripe matter be drawn into the vessels, and on a stomach and gut full to the point of felt weight, and bids them be emptied by vomiting and by the enema, and the one with indigestion be given time until his indigestion is digested {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## Fullness from food
Bleeding must be guarded against on a fullness of food, so that unripe matter is not drawn into the vessels in place of what is evacuated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## A full stomach and gut
It is also to be avoided when the stomach and gut are full to a weight felt or near to it; rather one strives to empty it, from the stomach and what lies next to it by vomiting, and from the lower intestines by whatever is possible, even by the enema {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## The one with indigestion
The bleeding of one with indigestion is avoided; rather he is given time until his indigestion is digested {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.
