## Overview
The wali in the creed is one of the friends of the Merciful; the Tahawiyya affirms 'all the believers are friends of the Merciful, the noblest to God the most obedient and most observant of His law', and 'we do not prefer any wali over any prophet' {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §§86,127}.

## In the sources
The creed affirms all believers are friends of the Merciful, the noblest the most obedient, and that no wali is preferred over any prophet {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §§86,127}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
In the reflection "On the friends of God" from [[work/sayd-al-khatir|Sayd al-Khatir]], [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] reads Ibn al-Jawzi's statement that God chooses for His friendship only a person perfect in form, handsome of face and balanced in stature; that some follow the path of renunciation and worship and others the path of knowledge and following the Sunna; and that God rarely joins both in one person. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:04:30} He then defines the wali from the Qur'an as one who joins faith and piety, since the Qur'an so often pairs "those who believe" with "those who do righteous deeds", so that action must be added to faith; he explains steadfastness (istiqama) as the side of action. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:06:30} He quotes al-Fudayl ibn Iyad that the best deed is the most sincere and the most correct: sincere when done for God, correct when done according to the Sunna, and no deed is accepted without both. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:07:30}

He disagrees with Ibn al-Jawzi on the condition of beauty, holding that he wrote the opening of the reflection with the prophets in view rather than the friends of God, and cites [[person/julaybib|Julaybib]], who was not handsome, and Zahir, who was plain and whom the Prophet loved. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:23:00} He makes the friends of God's intense fear of a bad end one of their marks, citing the sayings of Abu Bakr and Umar on fear, the dream of [[person/abdullah-ibn-salam|Abd Allah ibn Salam]], and the death of [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]] fearing a bad end, and he says fear grows with faith rather than working against it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:30:30} Episode 7 in the archive repeats the recording of this same reflection. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep07 00:02:00}

## In the introduction of Hilyat al-Awliya
Abu Nu'aym opens his introduction to *Hilyat al-Awliya* (vol. 1, pp. 4–17) with what is related on harming God's friends: the sacred hadith "Whoever harms a friend of Mine has declared war on Me," in its fuller text: "No act of devotion is dearer to Me than performing what I have made obligatory upon him, and My servant does not cease to draw near to Me by supererogatory works until I love him, and when I love him I become his hearing with which he hears..." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 1/4-5}, the hadith "Whoever harms a friend of Mine has made himself My enemy" from Aisha, and the report of Umar and Mus'ab ibn Jabal at the grave: "A little showing-off is shirk, and whoever is hostile to the friends of God has challenged God to combat." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 1/5}

Then he says: "Know that the friends of God have manifest marks and public signs to which the wise and righteous incline, and by their station with God the prophets and martyrs envy them," transmitting hadiths for them, among them "Among God's servants are people who are neither prophets nor martyrs, yet the prophets and martyrs envy them on the Day of Resurrection for their place with God" — "a people who love one another for God's sake, with no kinship between them and no wealth they deal in between them." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 1/5}

He enumerates their attributes: those God feeds in His mercy and shelters in His safety when He casts them into His Paradise, with trials passing over them like the pieces of the dark night; the unassuming in food and dress, honoured at calamity and battle — in it al-Bara' ibn Malik's and Abu Hurayra's report of one who, were he to swear by God, would be answered; "By their certainty rocks split and by their oath seas burst" — in it the report of the afflicted man who came to himself reading a sura on the rock, and the report of al-Ala' ibn al-Hadrami and the crossing of the sea to Darin; and the forerunners of nations and centuries who by their sincerity are rained upon and aided, in it "every generation of my umma has forerunners," "the best of a nation in every generation are five hundred and the substitutes forty," and the hadith of the hearts (eight hundred on Adam's heart down to one on Israfil's). {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 1/5-9}

Among their attributes: they looked at the inward falsehood of the fleeting world and rejected it, and at the outward glitter and tinsel and set it aside; those shielded from the vileness of the world to the right of delusion, seeing their Beloved's handiwork through reflection and consideration; the enamored with Him and His love, treading His address and covenant — in it Musa's supplication "Tell me of the noblest of Your creatures toward You," and the answer "the one who hastens to My desire as the eagle's pinion hastens to its desire"; lamps of darkness and wells of guidance and attainment — in it "The most beloved of servants to God are the God-fearing who are hidden"; those who reach the rope, give bounty and judge with justice — in it "those who, when given their right, accept it, and when asked, give it, and judge for people as they judge for themselves"; those open in public and withdrawn in private — in it "a people who laugh openly at the joy of their Lord's mercy and weep secretly at fear of His severe punishment"; and those who hasten to rights without postponement and fulfil acts of worship without deficiency — in it "Among God's promptings are three" and "God has elect ones He houses in the loftiest of Gardens." {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 1/9-17}

Dhu al-Nun al-Misri describes the elite of creation: a people who made their kneecaps pillows for their foreheads and the earth a resting-place for their flanks, letting the Qur'an mingle with their flesh and blood, so they are fearful and wary, grieved and apprehensive — people rejoice and they sadden, people sleep and they keep vigil. {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 1/13-15}
