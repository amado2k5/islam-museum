## Overview
The wali in the creed is one of the friends of the Merciful; the Tahawiyya affirms 'all the believers are friends of the Merciful, the noblest to God the most obedient and most observant of His law', and 'we do not prefer any wali over any prophet' {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §§86,127}.

## In the sources
The creed affirms all believers are friends of the Merciful, the noblest the most obedient, and that no wali is preferred over any prophet {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §§86,127}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
In the reflection "On the friends of God" from [[work/sayd-al-khatir|Sayd al-Khatir]], [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] reads Ibn al-Jawzi's statement that God chooses for His friendship only a person perfect in form, handsome of face and balanced in stature; that some follow the path of renunciation and worship and others the path of knowledge and following the Sunna; and that God rarely joins both in one person. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:04:30} He then defines the wali from the Qur'an as one who joins faith and piety, since the Qur'an so often pairs "those who believe" with "those who do righteous deeds", so that action must be added to faith; he explains steadfastness (istiqama) as the side of action. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:06:30} He quotes al-Fudayl ibn Iyad that the best deed is the most sincere and the most correct: sincere when done for God, correct when done according to the Sunna, and no deed is accepted without both. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:07:30}

He disagrees with Ibn al-Jawzi on the condition of beauty, holding that he wrote the opening of the reflection with the prophets in view rather than the friends of God, and cites [[person/julaybib|Julaybib]], who was not handsome, and Zahir, who was plain and whom the Prophet loved. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:23:00} He makes the friends of God's intense fear of a bad end one of their marks, citing the sayings of Abu Bakr and Umar on fear, the dream of [[person/abdullah-ibn-salam|Abd Allah ibn Salam]], and the death of [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]] fearing a bad end, and he says fear grows with faith rather than working against it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep04 00:30:30} Episode 7 in the archive repeats the recording of this same reflection. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep07 00:02:00}
