## Summary
The venous artery in Ibn Sina is one of the two arteries growing first from the left cavity of the heart; it goes to the lung and divides in it, and unlike the other arteries it has a single coat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}.

## Work and structure
It reaches the lung and divides in it for inhaling the breeze and bringing the blood that feeds the lung from the heart, for the heart is the passage of the lung's food. It grows from the thinnest part of the heart, where the veins enter it, which is why it is called the venous artery {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}.

## Why one coat
It was made of one coat so as to be softer, smoother and more yielding to expansion and contraction, and more yielding to the seeping from it into the lung of fine, vaporous blood that suits the lung's substance. That blood has nearly finished ripening in the heart and needs no further ripening like the blood running in the vena cava, especially as its place is close to the heart, so the heart's warm ripening power reaches it easily. The organ in which it beats is a loose, delicate one, so there is no fear that the hardness of a wall would harm it with each beat, and it was spared the thickening that the arteries beside hard organs cannot do without {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}. Weighing its need for firmness against its need for smoothness, he finds the need for smoothness greater {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}.
