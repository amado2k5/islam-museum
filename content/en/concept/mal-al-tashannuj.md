## Killing by suffocation
Convulsion may grow strong until the neck twists and the teeth grind; everyone who dies of convulsion dies while his body is still hot, and it is among what kills by suffocation — it kills by suffocation because the breathing muscles convulse and their motion lapses; every convulsion following a wound is deadly and is among the signs of death in most cases {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.145}

## The bad and the hard
The hurtful convulsion is that arising from dryness; the hard bad one is what occurs in the burning fevers and the phrenitis that dries the nerve and muscle and roasts the brain, and what occurs in the chronic fevers that dries nerve, muscle and brain itself and consumes the innate moisture so it convulses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.144}

## Hippocrates' saying and the quartan
Among its treatments that occur by nature is the arising of an acute fever, and therefore Hippocrates said: for fever to occur after convulsion is better than for convulsion to occur after fever; the quartan benefits in it for the shake of its rigor and its much sweating, and whoever the quartan befalls is seldom befallen by convulsion, for it is a safeguard from it (this is Hippocrates' saying as transmitted in the book, not the museum's verdict) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.148} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.149}
