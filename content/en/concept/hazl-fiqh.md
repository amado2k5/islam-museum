## Summary
Hazl is jest, the opposite of seriousness; technically a word is used without intending its meaning, literal or figurative. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16525}

## Distinct notions
Hazl differs from play, banter, error and forced compliance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16525} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16526}

## Effect on capacity and choice
Jest does not affect capacity or the choice to perform the act, but it does affect the choice of the legal ruling; in contracts that can be cancelled, the cause is taken as valid while the ruling is not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16526} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16527}

## Contracts that can be cancelled
The Hanafis differ on a contract made when both parties agreed to proceed in jest: Abu Hanifa gives priority to the claim that the jest was dropped, while the two companions hold the contract void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16528} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16529} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16531}
The Shafi'is hold a jester's sale valid in the sounder view, and the Hanbalis void it in the prevailing view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16532}

## Contracts that cannot be cancelled
Most jurists hold that jest and seriousness are equal in divorce, manumission, return, oath and vow, relying on the saying 'three things, in jest or earnest, are binding'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16533} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16534} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16535}
In marriage, most jurists hold a jester's marriage valid; the Malikis hold it binding, and others do not bind it where proof of jest exists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16536} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16537}

## Khul', gifts and admissions
Abu Hanifa and the two companions differ on whether a divorce for compensation takes effect when jest is involved. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16538} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16539} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16540} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16541}
The Hanbalis hold a gift made in jest void, and the Hanafis hold a bequest void for jest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16542} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16543}
In admissions the Hanafis hold jest voids them, while the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold them binding. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16544} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16546}

## Belief
Most jurists hold that a Muslim who utters words of unbelief in jest becomes an apostate, and a non-Muslim who professes Islam in jest is treated as a Muslim by the Hanafis and Shafi'is. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16547} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16548}
Slander in jest is punished as slander by the Shafi'is; the Hanafis apply discretionary punishment to one who habitually jests with ugly words. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16548}
