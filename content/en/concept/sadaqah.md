## Overview
Sadaqah in legal usage is what is given seeking the reward; the jurists treat its rulings in the chapters of zakat and vows {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah}.

## In the sources
Fiqh al-Sunna details sadaqah in the zakat chapters and the rulings of vows and expiations {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah}; the encyclopedia treats its related terms {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam glosses sadaqah as charity, alms or a freely made offering. It says the word is sometimes used as a synonym for zakat, which it describes as the alms that every Muslim with means must pay, and that it can also mean voluntary charity beyond what is required, atonement for offences (giving [[passage/quran-2-271|2:271]] and [[passage/quran-9-104|9:104]] as Qur'anic examples) or a way to compensate for certain rituals left undone, such as cutting or shaving the hair after the pilgrimage. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2040}
