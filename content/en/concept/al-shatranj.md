## Overview
Chess is a game whose forms and origin [[person/al-masudi|al-Masudi]] describes in *Muruj al-dhahab*. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.678-681}

## Its six forms
Al-Masudi notes that all the instruments of chess come in six forms:
- The well-known square board of eight by eight, attributed to the ancient Indians.
- The oblong board, four by sixteen.
- The square board of ten by ten, in which there are two pieces called the *dabbabatan* (the tanks).
- The round board attributed to the Romans.
- The round astronomical board, whose rows are twelve in number, corresponding to the zodiac signs; he gives seven examples of it, corresponding to the number of the planets and the two luminaries.
- The newly devised *jawarihiyya* board, seven by eight.

{cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.678-681}

## Its inventor
Al-Masudi notes that the inventor of chess was just and capable in what he did, and the inventor of backgammon was compelled. It is also said that it was a red square of leather laid between two men known for their generosity, who discussed war and devised for it a likeness without shedding blood. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.678-681}
