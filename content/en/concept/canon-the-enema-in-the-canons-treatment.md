## Summary
Ibn Sina counts the enema an excellent treatment for driving superfluities out of the intestines and drawing them from the upper principal organs, but warns that sharp ones harm the liver and bring fever, that the best position is lying on the back and then on the painful side, that the best time is the cool of the air, and that the bath usually should not come before it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## Benefit and harm
The enema is an excellent treatment for driving superfluities out of the intestines and for drawing them from the principal upper organs, but the sharp kinds harm the liver and bring on fever. Enemas are used to drive out the remains left by evacuations {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## Position and time
He has described the form of the enema and how it is given in the chapter on colic. The best position for the one receiving it is perhaps lying on his back, then lying on the side of the pain, and the best time is when the air is cool, so that distress, disturbance and fainting are less {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## Bath and enema
The bath tends to stir the humours and scatter them, while the enema must draw the humours that are held, so it is usually not good to put the bath before the enema {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## Ulcer of the intestines
If someone has an ulcer in the intestines and needs an enema because of fever or another illness, and fears it will be retained, his seat and navel and the parts about them should be fomented with a warmed compress {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.
