## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the Book was sent down to judge between all people, not between believers alone. He holds that truth binds the judge even when the guilty party is a Muslim, that a Muslim is not favoured for being one, and that the judge does not defend the treacherous {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2605}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2607}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2608}.

## The coat of mail
He recounts how Tu'ma ibn Ubayriq of the Banu Zafar stole a coat of mail and left it with a Jew; when it was found with the Jew, the thief's kin asked the Prophet to rule against the Jew so that the Muslims would not be shamed {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2606}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2607}. He holds that this pressure comes from human instinct, and that the verse forbids the judge to become an advocate for the treacherous {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2608}. He reports that the Prophet ruled against Ibn Ubayriq in favour of the Jew {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2614}.

## The hadd on a Muslim
He holds that the hadd falls on a Muslim who errs, since his punishment is a witness that Islam does not favour anyone {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2607}. He recounts the hadith of the Makhzumite woman who stole: the Prophet refused an intercession for her and said that earlier peoples perished because they punished the weak and let the noble go {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2622}. The jurists' rules on judging between a Muslim and others are not yet set out from a named source.
