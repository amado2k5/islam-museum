## Summary
The "seveners'" step is [[person/ibn-mujahid|Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Musa ibn al-Abbas ibn Mujahid]] limiting his book to the readings of seven imams, one or two from each city, after which the number seven became common. This limiting took place in the early fourth century; its author, of Baghdad, died in 324 AH, a contemporary of a number of other compilers. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 44}

## Before Ibn Mujahid
Ibn al-Jazari lists those who preceded him in collecting the readings: Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam (d. 224 AH), who by Ibn al-Jazari's reckoning gathered twenty-five readers with the seven; Ahmad ibn Jubayr al-Kufi (d. 258 AH), who gathered the readings of five, one from each city; al-Qadi Ismail ibn Ishaq al-Maliki (d. 282 AH), twenty imams; Abu Jafar al-Tabari (d. 310 AH), more than twenty readings; and al-Dajuni, who added Abu Jafar; then came Ibn Mujahid, the first to limit himself to the seven. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 43} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 44}

## The objection
Ibn al-Jazari quotes al-Mahdawi that the cities' choosing the seven for the most part was an abridgement and selection that later men favoured, which the common people then made a binding duty, so that on hearing anything else they called it error or unbelief. He says the one who made them seven did what he should not have, confused the common people, and would have done better, in limiting, to fall short of seven or exceed it to remove the confusion. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 46} Al-Suyuti quotes Abu Shama that the notion that the seven readings now found are the ones intended in the hadith is against the consensus of all people of knowledge, and Ibn al-Arabi that these seven are not the only ones allowed, as with the readings of Abu Jafar, Shayba and al-A'mash. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 525}

## Makki and Abu Hayyan on the reasons for limiting
Al-Suyuti quotes Abu Hayyan that Ibn Mujahid's book and those who followed him contain only a little of the well-known readings: Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' had seventeen known transmitters, and the book limits itself to al-Yazidi, who in turn had ten, so why limit to al-Susi and al-Duri? {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 526} He quotes Makki that around the year two hundred people in Basra followed the reading of Abu Amr and Yaqub, in Kufa Hamza and Asim, in Syria Ibn Amir, in Mecca Ibn Kathir and in Medina Nafi'; by the year three hundred Ibn Mujahid put in al-Kisa'i and dropped Yaqub. Makki thinks the reason was that transmitters had grown many and so they limited, from what agrees with the codex script, to what is easy to memorize, and that the codices Uthman had sent were five in number, and that someone who did not know the matter's source took the seven ahruf to be the seven readings. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 526} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 527}

## Ibn Taymiyya's reply
Ibn al-Jazari quotes Ibn Taymiyya's reply: there is no dispute among reputable scholars that the seven ahruf are not the readings of the seven well-known readers; rather the first to gather them was Ibn Mujahid, so as to match the number of ahruf the Qur'an was revealed in, not from his belief or anyone's that the seven readings are the seven ahruf, or that these named men are the only ones one may read after. He also relays a leading reader's saying: had Ibn Mujahid not got to Hamza before me I would have put Yaqub al-Hadrami in his place. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 49} See [[question/are-the-seven-readings-the-seven-ahruf|Are the seven readings the seven ahruf?]] and [[concept/al-qiraat-al-ashr|the ten readings]].

## The works on which the seven and the ten rest
The research summary reports that Ibn Mujahid wrote Kitab al-Sab'a, the foundation for the choice of the seven; that Abu Amr al-Dani wrote al-Taysir; that al-Shatibi versified Hirz al-Amani; that Ibn al-Jazari wrote al-Nashr and its Tayyiba to establish the ten; and that al-Suyuti's al-Itqan arranges these topics in types. The works of Makki ibn Abi Talib and their commentaries are named as supporting {cite:research-summary-qiraat-dialects-quran-2026 on main works}. For Ibn al-Jazari, Ibn Mujahid gathered the seven and the remaining three equal them in authenticity, so restriction to the seven does not make the others irregular. The readers and their narrators are set out in [[concept/al-qiraat-al-ashr|The ten readings]]. See [[work/kitab-al-sabaa-ibn-mujahid|Kitab al-Sab'a]] and [[work/al-nashr-fi-al-qiraat-al-ashr|al-Nashr fi al-qira'at al-ashr]].
