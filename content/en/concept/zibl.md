## Nature and selection
Nature: dung differs with the kind of animal, and even with individuals of one kind, above all with people. Duck dung is not used because it is too hot; the dung of falcons, hawks and other birds of prey is seldom used, because it is excessive. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.467}
Temperament: no dung is cooling or moistening; pigeon dung is the hottest of the dungs in use, and poultry dung is less than that of grazing birds. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.467}

## Actions
Actions: wild goat dung, especially the mountain kind, is used on every flow of blood; the dung and dried dung of the ass, burnt and unburnt, is used on every flow of blood. Pigeon dung warms; with barley flour it dissolves. Burnt goat dung is finer and does not become hotter. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.467}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: sheep dung with vinegar on ant-like, nail-like and mulberry-like warts. Locust dung for kalaf and vitiligo; likewise the dung of the starling that feeds on rice; likewise the dung of the hudhun; and the warl improves the complexion. Goat dung, especially mountain dung, burnt, for the alopecia the book calls the fox's disease; likewise mouse dung is stronger. Pigeon dung is one of the complexion-improving remedies. Lizard dung clears kalaf. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.467}
Swellings and pustules: cow pats with vinegar on hot abscesses calm them. Goat dung and sheep dung with vinegar on burns from fire, with wax and rose oil; pigeon dung with honey, and linseed seed for the Persian burn-eschar and burns from fire. Goat dung for peeling; pigeon and bustard dung for the qawabi; the starling's dung fed on rice likewise. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.467}
Wounds and ulcers: dog dung from the bones, with honey, helps old ulcers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.467}
Joints: cow pats as a poultice on sciatica; goat dung, especially the mountain kind, with pig fat for gout and for sciatica; dried pig dung drunk with vinegar for weakness of the muscles; a cerate placed on twisted nerves and on all hardenings. Pigeon dung for joint pains; goat dung, tried on hardenings and swellings of the joints, especially mixed with vinegar, which is one of Galen's experiments, and with barley flour, which suits those whose flesh is harder and drier. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.468}
Head organs: donkey dung is smelled for a strong nosebleed, or its moisture is pressed into the nose so that it stops. Pigeon dung helps favus. Galen says that when grazing pigeon dung is used with the seed of al-harf for the headache called baida, and cow pats help the swellings behind the ear. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.468}
Eye organs: the dung of the monitor lizard, the dabb and the crocodile for whiteness of the eye; likewise pigeon and sparrow dung for whiteness; swallow dung is remarkable in this, and the author says he tried it with honey. Mouse dung is tried for ulcers of the cornea and the matter gathered under the cornea. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.468}
Chest organs: pig dung with water and wine for spitting of blood and pain in the side; dog dung fed on bones, given as a gargle for quinsy; children's dung too, which may sometimes spare the need for bloodletting, and a boy should be fed bread with lupin so that the stench lessens; cow pats for the lung in consumption and similar. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.468}
Digestion organs: goat dung, especially the mountain kind, drunk with certain aromatics for jaundice, is tried; it helps dropsy as a poultice and as a drink, and the poultice and anointing should be done in the sun. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.468}
Excretory organs: cow dung is burnt for fumigation of a swelling of the womb. Goat dung, especially the mountain kind, drunk with aromatics, brings the menses and causes expulsion, dissolves hardness of the spleen; its dried form is crushed, and it is given for bleeding of the womb, especially with frankincense, which is tried. The dung of the wolf is also used for colic without swelling. Vulture dung causes expulsion by fumigation. Mouse dung with frankincense in wine breaks up stones, and taken, it loosens the bellies of children. Pigeon dung helps the pain of colic when used as an enema. Dog dung fed on bones helps diarrhoea and ulcers of the intestines, as an enema or drunk in milk boiled with iron. Elephant dung, it is said, prevents conception. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.468}
Poisons: goat dung, especially the mountain kind, boiled with vinegar and wine, on the bite of vermin; with the witness of Galen it helps even the bites of vipers. Dry dung of a grazing donkey in wine is very good for scorpion stings. Hen dung is a tested remedy against the choking fungus. Goat dung has a drawing power that draws the venom of wasps. Cow dung in particular drives off bedbugs when it is fumigated. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.469}
