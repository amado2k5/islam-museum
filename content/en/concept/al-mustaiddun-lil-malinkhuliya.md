## Temperament and build
The illness arises less in the white and fat, and more in the al-adam al-zubb al-qudaf (as printed); it arises more in one whose heart is very hot and whose brain is moist, so that the heat of his heart generates black bile and the moisture of his brain receives what arises in his heart; among those prone to it, as printed, are 'al-lathigh al-ahdha' al-khifaf al-alsina' and 'al-taraf al-ashadd humrat al-wajh wa-al-adam al-zubb' (printed words, not interpreted), and especially, in their chests, those black of hair, thick of it, wide of vessels and thick of lips, because some of these are signs of heat in the heart and some are signs of moisture in the brain; they are often phlegmatic in outward appearance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.105}

## Sex, age and season
The illness comes more to men and is worse in women; it is frequent in the middle-aged and the old, rare in winter and frequent in summer and autumn, and it may flare often in spring too, because spring stirs the humours, mixing them with the blood, and at times it flares in cycles in which black bile is stirred and rises {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.105}

## What hastens it
The person prone to melancholia turns to it quickly when fear, grief or sleeplessness strikes him, or when a habitual flow of blood, or a black-bile vomit, or the like, is held back from him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.105}
