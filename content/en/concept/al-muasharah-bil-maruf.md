## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi separates affection (mawadda), a feeling of the heart, from kindness (ma'ruf), good conduct owed even without affection. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2080}

## Kindness is wider than affection
He says the command "live with them in kindness" is wider than affection, because kindness is given even when one dislikes someone, as one feeds a hungry person without any bond of love. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2081}

## Dislike and much good
He explains "it may be that you dislike a thing and God places in it much good" as meaning that a man may dislike one aspect of his wife while other aspects hold much good that he does not notice. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2082}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2083}

## The story of Umar
He recounts that a man came to Umar ibn al-Khattab complaining that he disliked his wife and wanted to divorce her. Umar reminded him that homes are not built on love alone and that affection grows over time. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2082}

## Good companionship in the wife's rights
The Sheikh counts good companionship as the first general right of the wife over her husband, and says that among its requirements the husband must not overlook her feelings. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:17:00}
He holds that a wife is not obliged to serve her husband's mother, and that she should treat her well. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:18:00}
He warns against a husband belittling his wife in front of her family. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:22:30}
He recounts that when Aisha, out of jealousy, broke a dish of food, the Prophet rebuked her gently. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:23:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh holds that spouses married on the Book and the Sunna, which contain a remarkable 'triangle': living together in kindness, keeping in kindness, or parting with grace if life together becomes impossible; he asks whether each of us bears with the other, is patient with him, absorbs his anger and leaves him until he calms down before discussing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:03:30} He cites God's words about the house of Zakariyya, that He set right his wife, explaining it by their hastening to good deeds, and quotes [[person/umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz|Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz]]: they set right what was between them and their Lord, and God set right what was between the wolf and the sheep. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:05:30} He urges each spouse to deal with the space of good in the other, not to remind one who has done good for twenty years of a few lapses, and holds that one spouse's bad character may be the other's way into Paradise through patience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep05 00:09:00} The details are under [[concept/fann-al-hiwar|the art of dialogue and listening]].
