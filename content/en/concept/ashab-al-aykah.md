## What the Qur'an says
The Qur'an names the Companions of the Thicket in four places. In [[passage/quran-26-176|al-Shu'ara' 176]] it says they denied the messengers, and goes on to say that Shu'ayb asked them whether they would not fear God. In [[passage/quran-15-78|al-Hijr 78]] it calls them wrongdoers, in [[passage/quran-38-13|Sad 13]] it counts them among the factions, and in [[passage/quran-50-14|Qaf 14]] it joins them to the deniers on whom the threat came due. Madyan, for its part, appears in [[passage/quran-7-85|al-A'raf 85]], [[passage/quran-11-84|Hud 84]] and elsewhere, and Shu'ayb is there called their brother, the brother of Madyan. Hence the question: is al-Aykah another name for Madyan, or a different nation?

## What "al-aykah" means
Al-Tabari defines al-aykah as a dense tangle of trees, any such tangle being an ayka for the Arabs, citing a line of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani; he relays Ibn 'Abbas that it is the thicket and the place where trees gather, and Qatada that they were people of a grove whose trees were mostly doum palm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 26:176} {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 15:78} The reading is also disputed, as al-Qurtubi reports: Nafi' and Abu Ja'far read Laykah in al-Shu'ara' and in Sad, whereas the reciters agree on al-Aykah in al-Hijr and Qaf; he cites al-Jawhari that Laykah and Aykah are like Bakka and Makka, a place name. He then quotes al-Nahhas that Abu 'Ubayd's claim that Laykah is the village and al-Aykah the country is not established and that the people of exegesis and language are against it. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 26:176} On al-Hijr, al-Qurtubi gives Abu 'Ubayda that al-Aykah and Laykah are their city, as Bakka is to Makka. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 15:78}

## Are they the people of Madyan?
- **That they are the people of Madyan:** al-Tabari reports Ibn 'Abbas, through Ibn Jurayj, that they are the people of Madyan and that al-aykah is the tangled trees, and he opens by saying that they are the people of Madyan "as reported"; and Ibn Zayd that God sent Shu'ayb to Madyan and to the people of the desert, who are the Companions of Laykah, the two words being one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 26:176} Ibn Kathir on al-Shu'ara' says they are the people of Madyan "on the sound view", that Shu'ayb was one of their own, and that the verse does not say "their brother" here because they are ascribed to worshipping the aykah, a tree. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 26:176} On al-A'raf he says madyan is used both for the tribe and for the town near Ma'an on the Hijaz road, and that they are the Companions of the Aykah. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 7:85}
- **That they are two nations:** al-Tabari reports Qatada on al-Hijr that Shu'ayb was sent to two nations, the people of Madyan and the Companions of the Aykah, who were punished in two different ways: the Cry seized Madyan, while the Aykah people suffered a heat that lasted seven days until God sent a cloud, they sheltered under it, and it burned them, the punishment of the day of the Shade. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 15:78} Al-Qurtubi relays Qatada through Ibn Wahb that they are two nations, and al-Dahhak's cloud story; he also relays on al-Furqan that Qatada counts the Companions of al-Rass and the Companions of the Aykah as two nations to whom Shu'ayb was sent. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 26:176} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}
- **Ibn Kathir's reply:** in Qisas al-Anbiya he calls weak the view of those among the exegetes, such as Qatada, who say they are another nation, and answers their two arguments. The first, that the text does not say "their brother" when it names the Aykah, he answers that the text ascribes them to worshipping the tree, which does not suit mentioning brotherhood, whereas once it ascribes them to the tribe it is right to call Shu'ayb their brother. The second, that their punishment is the day of the Shade while Madyan is said to have been seized by the quake or the Cry, he answers that if a different description of the punishment proved a different nation, then the quake and the Cry would be two further nations. He adds that God ascribes to the Aykah people the same cheating in measure and scales that he ascribes to Madyan, which shows one nation. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook on Shu'ayb and the Aykah} He also reports a hadith in Ibn 'Asakir's biography of Shu'ayb, from 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, raised, that the people of Madyan and the Companions of the Aykah are two nations; Ibn Kathir calls it strange, with narrators who have been criticised, and thinks it more likely 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr's own words from the two saddlebags of Israelite lore he got at the Yarmuk. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook on Shu'ayb and the Aykah}

## How they were destroyed
Qatada's and al-Dahhak's reports, in al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi, agree that fierce heat struck them and that they gathered beneath a cloud and were burned. In Ibn Kathir's Qisas al-Anbiya God stilled the wind from them for seven days, then a cloud shaded them, they gathered under it, and it was sent casting sparks and flames; the earth quaked and a cry came, so he joins the Shade, the quake and the Cry in one account. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 15:78} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 26:176} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook on Shu'ayb and the Aykah}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni view, al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir:** the Companions of the Aykah are the people of Madyan; al-aykah is the tangled trees they dwelt in or worshipped; the different descriptions of the punishment do not prove two nations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 26:176} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 26:176} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook on Shu'ayb and the Aykah}
- **Another Sunni view, Qatada:** they are two nations to whom Shu'ayb was sent, punished in two ways, as al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi relay. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 15:78} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 26:176}
:::

## What the text does not say
The Qur'an does not decide the relation between al-Aykah and Madyan; the above are the exegetes' judgments from the wording of the verses and the description of the punishment. It also gives neither the site of al-Aykah nor their time. I did not find a verified academic study of this particular question among the sources I read, so I attribute no scholarly view.
