## The signs
He looks whether a disease in the gum or its neighbourhood attends the toothache: a gum tumour, or pain on pressing the gum, suggests the cause is not in the tooth itself; no gum tumour — the cause is in the tooth or the nerve at its root; a tumour or corrosion felt in the tooth, or pain running its length, puts it in its substance; pain felt only in the depth, especially with spreading pain in the gums or jaw and a daras-like feeling, puts it in the nerve. Dryness is told by the tooth's wasting and looseness, wind by the moving stretching pain, the thick humour by settled pain without manifest heat or cold, the hot sanguine or bilious by quick hurt, pricking, colour change toward the humour and sharp heat to the touch; the humour's origin is known by the fullness found in brain or stomach. If the cause is in the gum, drawing avails nothing; in the tooth it ends the pain; in the nerve it may or may not, the pain going only when the matter finds wider room to disperse into {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.270}
## The treatments
If the pain is by a partner organ, cleansing that organ comes first — by venesection, by a purging of this kind, or by head-purging gargles when the cause is in the head; with a sensible gum tumour, venesection and purgation open, by strength and the known conditions, while the cooling things of this kind are held in the mouth strengthened by what he described, then graduation to the maturing resolvents, guarding against the strong ones leaking to the throat, and evacuation from the organ itself — leeches sent to the tooth roots, venesection of the vein beneath the tongue, or cupping under the chin with scarification (carried as the book's descriptions, not procedures). If the pain grows severe, what he named is pasted on the root, and at greater severity opium with rose oil is needed (the name carried on the book), maturing preferred wherever there is a way out; a simple bad temperament is treated by its contrary, dryness rubbed with a fatty thing of this kind, matter evacuated by its kind with repellers first, and wind treated by the named resolvents {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.270-271}
