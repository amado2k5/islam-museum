## Summary
The fixed stars are those whose positions do not change relative to one another; they all move together from east to west once each day, and al-Farghani adds to this a slow motion on the poles of the zodiac of one part in every hundred years, a full circuit in thirty-six thousand years {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 80}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 81}.

## Motion
Al-Farghani says the fixed stars are so called because all their motions from west to east are equal, so their figures and the distances between them remain fixed {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 80}. This follows Ptolemy's measurements, as he notes in chapter thirteen {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 81}.

## Number and magnitudes
Al-Farghani counted what could be measured of the stars: one thousand and twenty-two, divided into six magnitudes — fifteen stars in the first, forty-five in the second, two hundred and eight (OCR reading) in the third, four hundred and seventy-four in the fourth, two hundred and seventeen in the fifth, and sixty-three (OCR reading) in the sixth. Of these, three hundred and sixty are north of the zodiac, three hundred and forty-six within the zodiac's bounds, three hundred and sixteen south of it, and he also mentions a few cloudy and dim ones {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 55}.

## The greatest stars
In chapter nineteen al-Farghani names stars of the first magnitude, among them Qalb al-Asad (the Lion's Heart), Suhayl, al-Shi'ra al-'Abur, al-Simak al-A'zal, al-Simak al-Ramih, al-Nasr al-Waqi', al-Dabaran and Fam al-Hut al-Janubi {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 54}.

## Perspectives

## Pages 201 to 388: count, magnitude, and stars that never set
Al-Farghani records that the number of fixed stars that could be measured is one thousand and twenty-two, ordered in six classes by magnitude: fifteen of the first, forty-five of the second, two hundred and eight of the third, four hundred and seventy-four of the fourth, two hundred and seventeen of the fifth, and sixty-three of the sixth {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 206}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 207}. He says nine hidden and five cloudy ones do not enter this count visibly {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 207}.

The text gives the number of stars north of the zodiac as two hundred and sixty, on the zodiac three hundred and forty, and south of it three hundred and sixteen — figures that do not reach the total of one thousand and twenty-two, so the number is doubtful {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 207}.

Al-Farghani names fifteen stars of the first degree with their positions, among them al-Dabaran in Taurus, Capella in Auriga, Sirius and Canopus, Arcturus and Qalb al-Asad; their Arabic names are written in Latin letters in the edition {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 208}.

In the fourth climate three stars called Jady, al-Farqadan and Banat Na'sh never set, as do their southern counterparts which never rise {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 222}.
