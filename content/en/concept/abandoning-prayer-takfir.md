## Summary
In his chapter on Ali's legal rulings al-Sallabi gives a report from Ali about one who abandons prayer, then sets out the scholars' disagreement over declaring an unbeliever one who neglects prayer out of laziness while believing it obligatory {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 287}. In the Book of Faith of [[work/ibana-al-kubra-ibn-batta|al-Ibana al-Kubra]], [[person/ibn-batta-al-ukbari-ubayd-allah|Ibn Batta al-Ukbari]] gathers a chapter titled "The unbelief of one who abandons prayer and withholds zakat, and the permissibility of fighting and killing them if they do so". {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 669}

## Ali's saying
The book reports that Ali was asked about abandoning prayer and said, "Whoever does not pray is an unbeliever" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 287}; it also gives a saying of a narrator, whose name is garbled in the scan and needs checking, that the Prophet's Companions did not regard the abandonment of any deed as unbelief except prayer {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 287}. Ibn Batta transmits the same report from Ali through Ma'qil al-Khath'ami: a man asked him in al-Rahba about a woman who does not pray, and he said, "Whoever does not pray is an unbeliever." {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 680} The editor of al-Ibana, Rida Mu'ti, attributes the report "they did not regard the abandonment of any deed as unbelief except prayer" to Abu Hurayra in al-Hakim's Mustadrak. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 673}

## The disagreement
Al-Sallabi quotes al-Nawawi's distinction: whoever denies that prayer is obligatory is an unbeliever by the consensus of Muslims, while scholars differed over one who abandons it out of laziness while believing it obligatory {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 288}. Malik, al-Shafi'i and the majority held that he is a sinner who is asked to repent and, if he does not, is put to death by the sword as a legal penalty; a group of the early generations held him an unbeliever – among them a report from Ali, one of the two reports from Ahmad, and the view of Ibn al-Mubarak and Ishaq; Abu Hanifa and a group of Kufans held that he is neither an unbeliever nor put to death, but is disciplined and confined until he prays {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 288}. The editor of al-Ibana quotes the same words of al-Nawawi in his note on Jabir's hadith. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 670}

## What Ibn Batta transmits
Ibn Batta opens the chapter with Jabir's hadith, "Between a servant and shirk or unbelief there is nothing but abandoning prayer", through several chains; the editor notes the weakness of one chain and that it has sound chains, including Muslim's. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 669} He then relates how Umar, after he was stabbed and lost consciousness, was roused by the mention of prayer and said, "There is no share in Islam for one who abandons prayer", and prayed while his wound was gushing blood. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 671} He transmits Burayda's hadith, "Between us and them is abandoning prayer; whoever abandons it has disbelieved", his saying on a cloudy day, "Pray early, for whoever abandons prayer, his works are void", and Mujahid's question to Jabir about which deed distinguished unbelief from faith in the Prophet's time – "Prayer". {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 672} He relates that Abu Bakr sent Khalid ibn al-Walid with orders to fight people over the five pillars. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 675} Among the reports are Abu Dharr's hadith, "Whoever abandons prayer deliberately, God's protection is withdrawn from him"; Ibn Mas'ud's saying that guarding the prayers means keeping their times and that abandoning them is unbelief; and Abu al-Darda''s, "There is no faith for one who has no prayer". {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 678} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 679} He transmits from Ibn Mas'ud that one who withholds zakat is no Muslim, and from Ibn al-Mubarak a severe saying about one who says "I will pray the obligatory prayer tomorrow", and closes with the hadith of Abd Allah ibn Amr that whoever guards the prayer will have it as light, proof and salvation on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 681} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 682} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 683}

## Ibn Batta's comment
Ibn Batta says that these hadiths and reports from the Prophet, the Companions and the Successors all show that one who abandons prayer and denies the obligations is an unbeliever removed from the religion. He argues from the verse of al-Bayyina, which makes the upright religion sincere worship together with establishing prayer and giving zakat, and from the two verses of al-Tawba that make letting people go free and brotherhood in religion depend on repentance, prayer and zakat; for him this is the clearest proof that faith is word and deed and that prayer and zakat are part of faith. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 683} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 684}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (the majority, as al-Sallabi reports from al-Nawawi).** One who abandons prayer out of laziness while affirming its obligation is a sinner to be asked to repent, not an unbeliever. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 288}

**Sunni (a group of the early generations, Ibn Batta the Hanbali among them).** One who abandons prayer is an unbeliever outside the religion, as the hadiths and reports he gathered show; for him prayer is part of faith. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 288} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 683}

**Sunni (Abu Hanifa and the Kufans, as al-Sallabi reports).** He is neither an unbeliever nor put to death, but is disciplined and confined until he prays. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 288}
:::

## How the books treat it
Al-Sallabi sets out the disagreement without preferring either view in this place, and places what he reports from Ali among the rulings on prayer attributed to him {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 288}. Ibn Batta makes the chapter part of his reply to those who exclude deeds from what is called faith. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 684}

## Notes for researchers
- Al-Sallabi does not give the chain of Ali's report on the unbelief of one who abandons prayer; al-Ibana gives one through Ma'qil al-Khath'ami (p. 680).
- An authoritative statement of each school's position needs an independent legal reference for that school.
