## Summary
Ibn Sina explains that thrush is frequent in infants because the lining of the mouth and tongue is so soft, that the worst kind is the black, charcoal-like and deadly one and the safest the white and the red, and he lists drying and cleansing remedies from the lightest to the stronger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.214-215}.

## Cause and kinds
Thrush often befalls them, for the lining of their mouths and tongues is so soft it cannot bear touch, so how can it bear the scouring of the milk's wateriness, which harms them and brings thrush. The worst thrush is the black, charcoal-like, which is deadly, and the safest the white and the red {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.214}.

## The lighter remedies
They should be treated with the lighter of the thrush remedies given in the book on particulars. Violet alone may suffice, or ground carob alone or mixed with rose and a little saffron, or the juice of lettuce, nightshade and 'arfaj. If stronger is needed, ground liquorice root; bitter myrrh, gall-nut and frankincense bark ground very fine and mixed with honey may help the pustules of the gum and the thrush; the sour mulberry rob alone or unripe-grape rob may be enough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.214}.

## The stronger remedies
Washing with honey wine or honey water, followed by one of the dryers named, may also help. If something stronger is needed, six dirhams each of pomegranate rind, pomegranate flower and sumac, and two dirhams of dill, are pounded, sifted and sprinkled on {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.214-215}. The quantity of gall-nut in this recipe is garbled in the scanned text and is left out.
