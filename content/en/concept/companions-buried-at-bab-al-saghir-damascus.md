## Summary
This record gathers what the books read say about Companions buried in the Bab al-Saghir cemetery at Damascus, with the differing reports on each grave, as a companion to the record of the [[place/bab-al-saghir-cemetery|Bab al-Saghir cemetery]]. The books speak in several places of the 'graves of the Companions' within the cemetery and say who was buried at or beside them. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 3 p. 70} {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 39 p. 329} Named here are [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal ibn Rabah]], [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]], [[person/fadala-ibn-ubayd|Fadala ibn Ubayd]] and [[person/asma-bint-yazid|Asma' bint Yazid]].

## Bilal ibn Rabah
Al-Dhahabi says al-Waqidi held that Bilal was buried at Bab al-Saghir at some sixty years of age; Ali ibn Abd Allah al-Tamimi said at Bab Kaysan; Ibn Zabr said he died at Daraya and was buried at Bab Kaysan; others said he was buried at Daraya, and it is reported that he died at Aleppo, on the authority of Uthman ibn Kharrazadh from a shaykh of his. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 352} Ibn Kathir sets out the reports and says the first, burial at Bab al-Saghir, is the most correct, and God knows best. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 104} Ibn al-Athir quotes Muhammad ibn Sa'd, al-Waqidi's scribe, that he died at Damascus and was buried at Bab al-Saghir in 20 AH at some sixty years. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 131} The year of death is also disputed: 18 AH in the plague for Yahya ibn Bukayr, 20 AH for Ibn Ishaq and a group. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 352}
Al-Dhahabi relays from Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz that when Bilal was dying he said, 'Tomorrow we meet the beloved ones, Muhammad and his party'; his wife cried, 'Woe!' and he said, 'Joy!' {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1244}

## Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan
Al-Dhahabi relays from Abu Mushir that al-Dahhak ibn Qays al-Fihri prayed over Mu'awiya and that he was buried between Bab al-Jabiya and Bab al-Saghir, as far as the reporter had heard. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 7 p. 317} The editor of the book notes that the grave lies inside the Bab al-Saghir cemetery, is still known, close to the grave of the hafiz Ibn Asakir, and was renovated in recent years. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 7 p. 317}
Ibn Kathir records the dispute over the burial place: some say the governor's residence, the Khadra', others the Bab al-Saghir cemeteries, which is the majority view. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} He gives, as reported by more than one route, that Mu'awiya charged his son Yazid to shroud him in a garment of the Messenger of God that he had saved for this day, and to put the Prophet's hair and nail clippings he held in his mouth, nose, eyes and ears. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} He adds that, according to others, Yazid was absent and al-Dahhak ibn Qays prayed over him after the noon prayer in the mosque of Damascus. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512}
Al-Dhahabi relays from Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' that when death came to Mu'awiya he was asked whether he would not make a testament; he recited a line that there is no escape from death, then prayed that God overlook his stumbling, forgive his slip and pardon him through His forbearance, as he had no one else to hope in. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 7 p. 317}
Ibn Kathir relays from Ibn Asakir that Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Nawfali said that when Abd Allah ibn Ali entered Damascus he opened the graves of the Umayyads and found in Mu'awiya's grave only a black thread like dust. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 5 p. 427} The report does not say where the grave was.

## Fadala ibn Ubayd
Al-Dhahabi says Ibn Ma'in held that Fadala was buried at Bab al-Saghir, and that al-Mada'ini and others said he died in 53 AH. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 3025}

## Asma' bint Yazid (Umm Salama al-Ansariyya)
Al-Dhahabi says she lived in Damascus and that the grave of Umm Salama in the Bab al-Saghir cemetery is her grave, God willing. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 1090} In Tarikh al-Islam he repeats that the grave of Umm Salama is at Bab al-Saghir and that she is, God willing, this woman. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 8 p. 74} The attribution of the grave to her is therefore conjectural, in the form 'God willing'.

## The place of the 'graves of the Companions'
The books say that within the Bab al-Saghir cemetery is a place known as the graves of the Companions: Tarikh Dimashq says of a man who died in 505 AH that he was buried at Bab al-Saghir beside the graves of the Companions. {cite:tarikh-dimashq-ibn-asakir-fikr-1995 scan vol. 3 p. 70} Al-Dhahabi says a man who died in 533 AH was buried beside the graves of the Companions in the Bab al-Saghir cemetery. {cite:tarikh-islam-dhahabi vol. 39 p. 329} Ibn Kathir says Abu al-Qasim ibn Asakir, son of the hafiz, was buried over his father in the Bab al-Saghir cemeteries, east of the graves of the Companions, outside the enclosure. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 46} He says Kamal al-Din Mawdud al-Shaghuri was buried in the Bab al-Saghir cemeteries north of the graves of the martyrs. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 1 p. 81} So the books distinguish the 'graves of the Companions' from the 'graves of the martyrs' within the cemetery.

## Where the reports differ
The books differ on the place of Bilal's grave: Bab al-Saghir, Bab Kaysan, Daraya or Aleppo. {cite:siyar-alam-nubala scan vol. 1 p. 352} They differ on Mu'awiya's: Bab al-Saghir or the governor's residence. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 512} The pages I read give no comprehensive list of the Companions buried in the cemetery, so the above is what is named in them. I did not find in the scans of Tarikh Dimashq available to me the entries of these four people themselves, so I rely on the quotations from it in the other books.
