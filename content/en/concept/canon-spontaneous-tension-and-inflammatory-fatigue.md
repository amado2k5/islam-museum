## Summary
Ibn Sina makes the cause of spontaneous tension fatigue a fullness without a bad humour and treats it by thinning and cutting, and for the inflammatory kind orders venesection at once on the first day and then arranges three days, barley water, rubbing with a cool or moderate oil, then cooling dishes and river fish, with water withheld as far as possible {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.249-250}.

## The tension kind
The cause of tension fatigue here is fullness without a bad humour; its treatment in bodies of poor temperament is venesection and a thinning regimen, and in the body we are speaking of it is thinning and cutting alone. Some words after this are garbled in the printed text and are not taken {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.249}.

## The inflammatory kind and venesection
For the inflammatory kind the treatment is to venesect at once from the vessel that corresponds to the organ with most of the fatigue or where the fatigue first shows, and from the median vein if there is no difference between the organs. You may need to bleed him again on the second day, even the third; bleed on the first day as it shows, without delay, lest it become settled, and on the second and third days bleed him in the evening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.249}.

## The three days of food
His food on the first day should be barley water or plain wheat porridge if no fever arises, and if fever arises barley water alone. On the second day there is rubbing with a cool or moderate oil such as almond oil. On the third day dishes of lettuce, of the farʿiyya kind, of mallow and of sorrel, and river fish as isfidhbaj. In these days they are kept from drinking water as far as possible, but if their patience runs out on the third day and they have not digested their food, they are given honey water or thin white wine, or mixed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.249-250}.
