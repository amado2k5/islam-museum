## Summary
Ibn Sina describes what happens to one who leaves the stomach empty: sour belching, a bad mood, nausea, a bitter mouth, a loose belly, timidity, pain and stinging at the mouth of the stomach, scorching urine and cold limbs as bile pours into the stomach; this is commoner in bilious temperaments, who are managed with quick, divided feeding before the bath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## The signs
One who finds no digestion of his food meets sour belching, a bad mood, nausea, a bitter mouth and a loose belly, and also timidity, distress and pain and stinging at the stomach's mouth, and he imagines his bowels and viscera hang loose because the empty stomach clenches and contracts on itself {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## Urine, limbs and sleep
He passes scorching urine and burnt stool, and sometimes his limbs turn cold as bile pours into the stomach; this is commoner in bilious temperaments, and likewise in those bilious in the stomach alone, and his sleep is spoiled and restless {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## Management
Bodies whose stomachs gather much bile need food taken in divided portions, quickly, and before bathing; others should exercise, bathe and then eat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.
