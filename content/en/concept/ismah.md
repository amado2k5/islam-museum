## Summary
In the lexicon, ismah is preventing and protecting; the jurists also use it of the marriage bond {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11644}.

## Its three senses
The first is the protection God gives a person from sins. The second is the protection that makes a person and his property sacred, so that retaliation, compensation or damages are due on violating it. The third is the protection that makes violating it sinful without retaliation or compensation {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11644} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11645}.

## The protection of the messengers
The text states that the messengers are protected from sins after their mission; on their state before it, it records as the sound view that they were free of every defect {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11644} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11645}.

## How sacred protection is gained
A Muslim gains it by the testimony of faith; others gain it by a covenant of protection, such as a treaty or safe-conduct {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11645} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11646}.

## Ismah in marriage
The word is used figuratively for the marriage bond. It ends by annulment, as for apostasy or defect, or by divorce; the husband normally holds the power of divorce, and a wife holds it when he delegates it to her or she stipulated it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11646} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11647}.

## Where to look further
The prophets are treated under «prophets», divorce under «talaq», and delegation under «tafwid» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11647}.
