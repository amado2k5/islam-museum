## Its definition
One kind of ramad is a great swelling beyond bounds in size, in which the white mounts over the pupil, covers it and prevents closing — chemosis in Greek, known here as wardinj; it often befalls boys for the abundance of their matter and the weakness of their eyes, and it is not from hot matter alone but phlegmy and melancholic too {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.167}

## Its treatment
What of ramad has become wardinj is treated by evacuation, venesection and cupping ⁦(artery excision may be needed — its procedure is not carried)⁩; if from a hot swelling, after evacuation from every side including the head's veins and cupping, the repellents and the soft cold juices are used, and the outward poultices ⁦(their names are not carried)⁩, something of the narcotics being sometimes needed among them, and the ointments likewise ⁦(the tried salves and their animal parts are not carried)⁩; in wardinj it is preferred to busy oneself with the outward treatments and confine oneself to dripping milk into the eye for three days if the state and season bear it; the oculists have tried in wardinj, for the ulcerated pain, collyriums ⁦(their names are not carried — the book's report about the collyrium-makers)⁩; if phlegmy, evacuate with the laxatives and use the soft mucilages ⁦(their names are not carried)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.175}
