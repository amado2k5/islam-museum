## Summary
The Tablet in which the Pen wrote creation's decrees fifty thousand years before the heavens and earth; in it the Qur'an is preserved.

## In the texts
[[passage/quran-85-22|preserved Tablet]] · [[passage/quran-13-39|erases and confirms]]

## Note
God erases what He wills and confirms — some entries effaced, others established.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam renders the lawh mahfuz as a well-preserved tablet. It says the phrase, used in [[passage/quran-85-22|Qur'an 85:22]], refers according to traditional commentators to the original codex on which the Qur'an was written and which has always been in heaven. It links it with a second description of revelation's divine archetype, the mother of the book, and gives three Qur'anic references, one of them [[passage/quran-13-39|13:39]]. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1336}

This is a general link the Dictionary draws between two Qur'anic phrases and is reported here as it stands; what the Tablet contains and what is written in it rests on the sources above, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Kathir. The other two references are not repeated here with the numbers the Dictionary printed, and checking them against the Tanzil edition the museum uses is left to a later review.
