## Nature and selection
There are three kinds; it is like rice in strength, but rice is more nourishing; jawars is better in every state than dukhn, but more astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.430}. The editor's footnote calls it a kind of dukhn {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.430}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: cold and dry at the end of the second degree; some say hot in the first, and the first is more correct {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.430}. Actions: astringent and drying without sting; a poultice to ease pains; if not managed it produces bad blood; it nourishes less than other grains that are baked; its nourishment is small and sticky {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.430}.

## Uses by organ
Excretory organs: a poultice for colic, and it is a diuretic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.431}. Its substance and its bread are slow in the stomach; it is said that cooking it with milk, or with fine bran, improves its nourishment, especially with ghee or almond oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.431}.
