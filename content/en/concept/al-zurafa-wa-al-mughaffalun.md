## Definition
The wits and the fools are the heroes of the anecdotes in the Arab and Islamic tradition. The zarif is the man of the ready answer and of banter; the mughaffal or fool raises a laugh by his heedlessness; beside them stand the greedy man, the party-crasher and the miser. Ibn al-Jawzi wrote a book on each, both in the museum: [[work/akhbar-al-zuraf|Akhbar al-Ziraf wa-l-Mutamajinin]] and [[work/akhbar-al-humaqa|Akhbar al-Hamqa wa-l-Mughaffalin]].

## The best known
[[person/juha-abu-al-ghusn|Juha Abu al-Ghusn]] is proverbial for heedlessness, and the introduction to his anecdotes says he was a learned man given to jest in whose name people composed many anecdotes {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 4-6}. [[person/nasreddin-hoca|Nasreddin Hoca]] is the man of the anecdotes in Anatolia, to whom the Turks attribute the Juha stories {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 6-8}. [[person/ashab-ibn-jubayr|Ash'ab ibn Jubayr]] is the wit of Medina, proverbial for greed, whose reports al-Nuwayri tells {cite:nihayat-al-arab-nuwayri-vol4-scan p. 27-29}. Among the Companions is [[person/suwaybit-ibn-saad|Suwaybit ibn Sa'd]], and a report in al-Bukhari names Nu'ayman {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 6775}.

## How their reports are read
These reports are literature told for pleasure and reflection, and many are falsely attributed. The museum retells them in its own words with their sources, and does not judge the persons by what the anecdotes say.
