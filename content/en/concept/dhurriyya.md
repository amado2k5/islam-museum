## Definition
Dhurriyya in the Qur'an is the collective name for descendants — children and what branches from them — used by the servants in prayer ('my Lord, grant me from Yourself a goodly offspring; indeed You are the Hearer of supplication') and by God in praise of the prophets ('offspring, one of another; and God is Hearing and Knowing'), and set as a measure of trial: 'your wealth and your children are but a trial'. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Offspring between prayer and ruling
Offspring carries two faces in the Qur'an: a blessing asked for and praised — the prayers of Zakariyya and the wife of Imran, 'our Lord, grant us from among our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes' — and a charge to be led, for a child may be an enemy to his father on the last day if it turned him from God. Upon offspring stand the rules of inheritance: the male the share of two females, each parent a sixth, and the daughters their prescribed portions. {cite:tanzil-quran}

Offspring enters the Bunuwwa Browser as its own family: Qur'an, hadith, seerah and harvested-book passages that name the dhurriyya, the lineage and the progeny, and the collective names Bani Isra'il and Bani Adam where they stand as descent and kinship rather than bare inheritance labels. {cite:tanzil-quran}
