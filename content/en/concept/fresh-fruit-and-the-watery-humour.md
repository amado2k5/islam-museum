## Summary
Ibn Sina says fresh fruit such as apricot, mulberry, melon, peach and plum suits the non-exercising, bilious person in summer when eaten before the meal, though it is better to manage without it, since what fills the blood with wateriness seethes in the body and readies it for putrefaction; the watery humour may turn to ichor if it stays in the vessels, and exercise after fruit disperses it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.233-234}.

## Whom it suits
Fresh fruit suits only the non-exercising, bilious person in summer, eaten before the meal, such as apricot, mulberry, melon, peach and plum; but to manage with other things is preferable {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.233}.

## Its harm
Whatever fills the blood with wateriness seethes in the body as fruit juices seethe outside it, and though it may help for the moment it readies the blood for putrefaction; so too whatever fills it with a raw humour, like qiththa and cucumber, which is why those who eat much of these foods are liable to fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.234}.

## Ichor and exercise
The watery humour may turn to ichor if it does not disperse and stays in the vessels; those who exercise just as they take fruit disperse this wateriness and are less harmed. He adds that a raw or watery element in the blood prevents it from adhering to the body, and one who eats fruit should walk after it and then eat on top so that it slips along {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.234}.
