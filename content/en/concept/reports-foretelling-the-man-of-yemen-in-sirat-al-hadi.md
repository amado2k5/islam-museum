## Overview
After setting out the conditions of the imam, the preface of [[work/sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq|Sirat al-Hadi ila al-Haqq]] gives a series of reports and verses that mention the rising of a man of the Prophet's family in Yemen, and then concludes that he is [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]]. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.29} They are in the voice of the Zaydi narrators and are presented as attributed to them, without any verdict. See [[concept/imamate-conditions-in-the-preface-of-sirat-al-hadi]].

## The reports as attributed
The book gives a report attributed to Ali ibn Abi Talib about the signs of the age and the coming of "the man of Yemen" who takes the governors, with a command to help him because he is "a man of us, the family" who calls to the truth. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.29} It gives a report attributed to [[person/muhammad-al-baqir|Muhammad al-Baqir]] that the Yamani appears in Yemen when the Egyptians kill their amir, which the text dates to 280 AH, {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.29} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.30} and the editor's footnote says Khumarawayh ibn Ahmad ibn Tulun was killed at Damascus in 282 AH. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.30} It gives a report conveyed from Abu al-Abbas al-Ghuryani that the master of the affair is a Hasanid who appears in Yemen, his father's name being al-Husayn, which a marginal note in the manuscript takes to mean al-Hadi's father. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.30} It gives a report of an elder of the line of al-Abbas at Kufa, at the time of Yahya ibn Umar's rising, that the "Omani" would appear in Yemen; the editor notes the Sana'a manuscript has "Yamani", which is nearer the truth. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.30} And it gives a report said to be found in the books of Ubaydallah ibn al-Abbas about the Qa'im of the line of al-Hasan, who begins his march in Najd and goes on to Yemen, Tihama and Mecca, preceded by a man of the line of al-Abbas ibn Ali. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.30}

## Reckoning the time
The book gives a report attributed to Ali that there is affliction up to seventy and relief after it, which the narrators reckon from the Prophet's death, so that 280 AH is about 270 from the death. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.31} It gives another attributed to Ali about a trial after the year 280 in which a man of his kin bearing a prophet's name rises, whom a marginal note identifies as Yahya ibn Zakariyya, to be awaited in 284 AH. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.31} It then gives verses by a poet, saying that Yahya has a dominion that you will recognise, {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.31} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.32} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.33} and a marginal note in the manuscript says the verses need clarifying and their wording checking, while the editor corrects some readings from Maqatil al-Talibiyyin. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.32} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.33}

## Signs and qualities
The book relates that Abu al-Jarud asked Abu Jafar how the master of this affair could be recognised and was answered with a verse of sura al-Hajj, [[passage/quran-22-41|al-Hajj 22:41]], {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.33} a report attributed to the Prophet that there are just men in his family who drive false interpretation from the religion, and a saying attributed to Zayd ibn Ali that the imam of the family is the one trusted for his deeds, understanding and knowledge. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.33}

## The narrators' search among earlier risers
The narrators say they looked at those of the family who rose before and did not meet those they name: Ali, al-Hasan, al-Husayn, Zayd ibn Ali, Yahya ibn Zayd, Ibrahim ibn Abdullah, Muhammad ibn Abdullah al-Nafs al-Zakiyya, al-Husayn ibn Ali killed at Fakhkh, Muhammad ibn Ibrahim, and al-Qasim ibn Ibrahim, al-Hadi's grandfather. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.34} They then name one who rose in their own time, Yahya ibn Umar at Kufa, whom they call pious and sound in school though not complete in knowledge, and who was killed by an Abbasid commander, {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.34} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.35} and say that after him came groups at Kufa, in Khurasan, the Maghrib and the Yamama who had neither the knowledge nor the qualities of the just imams, so they did not stand with them and waited. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.35} See [[event/revolt-of-yahya-ibn-umar-al-talibi]]. This verdict on those who came before is the narrators' own.
