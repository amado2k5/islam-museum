## The rule and its limit
The Moon can be eclipsed only at opposition, when it stands inside the eclipse limits printed at the head of the conjunction and opposition pages. If its distance from either node, the head or the tail, is greater than the limit, there is no eclipse. If it is less, the eclipse is in proportion to the nearness to the node, and when the Moon is exactly on the node the eclipse is as full as it can be. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.150-151}

## The hour of opposition and the horizon
If the opposition falls at night, or near sunrise or sunset, the eclipse is wholly or partly hidden by the hour, and the text says the reader must then work out what can be seen. The limit is written in abjad letters that this reading takes as twelve degrees; the reading is not yet confirmed. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.150}
