## Summary
In the fourth chapter Ibn Sina describes how each carotid divides, at its end in the neck, into front and back parts, and how the larger back part weaves a net of vessels under the brain from which branches then rise to the ventricles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.85-86}.

## The two parts
Each carotid divides at its end in the neck into a front and a back part. The front has two: one goes inward to the tongue and the deep muscles of the lower jaw; one goes outward, rising before the ears to the temporal muscles and beyond them after leaving many branches to the crown of the head, where the tips of the right meet the tips of the left. The back has two parts: the smaller mostly rises backward and spreads in the muscles around the head joint, and some goes to the base of the back of the brain, entering a large hole at the lambdoid suture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.85}.

## The net
The larger enters in front of this hole, by the hole in the petrous bone, into the net, which is woven from it, vessels within vessels, layers upon layers, folds upon folds, so that none can be taken singly, and it spreads front, back, right and left; a pair then gathers again as at first, the membrane is pierced for it, and it rises into the brain, spreads in the thin membrane and then in the brain's substance toward its ventricles, and meets the mouths of the branches of the descending venous vessels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.85}.

## Why some rise and some descend
These rise and those descend because the venous vessels are conduits pouring out blood, whose vessel is best placed with its ends turned down; the arteries carry the fine, moving, rising spirit and need no inverted vessel, and if they had one it would drain too much of the accompanying blood and hinder the spirit's movement, which is easier upward. The net was spread under the brain so that arterial blood and spirit move back and forth in it and take on the brain's temperament after ripening, and then pass to the brain by degrees; it lies between the bone and the hard membrane {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.85-86}.
