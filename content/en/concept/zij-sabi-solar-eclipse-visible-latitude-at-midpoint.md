## Parallax in latitude
The ascendant is erected for the middle of the eclipse. The Moon's true latitude is found from the latitude motion corrected to that moment, and its parallax in latitude is found on the same figure. If the two lie on the same side they are added; if not, the smaller is subtracted from the larger and the side of the remainder is kept. The result is the visible latitude of the Moon at mid-eclipse. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.164-165}

## Comparison with the half-sum of the radii
If the visible latitude equals or exceeds the half-sum of the radii the Sun is not eclipsed; if it is less, the Sun is eclipsed, and the doubt lies only between the two limits. Once an eclipse is certain, the visible latitude is subtracted from the half-sum and the remainder is the part of the Sun's diameter that is covered. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.165-166}
