## Actions and properties
Its root draws from the hollow, dries, cleanses and washes; its oil serves in the states where jasmine oil serves, but is weaker {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Cosmetic uses
Its root draws out thorns and splinters, especially with rye flour and honey. Narcissus cleanses freckles and vitiligo, especially its root with vinegar, and the root helps alopecia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Swellings and pustules
Its root kneaded with honey and bitter vetch bursts abscesses that are hard to ripen, and a poultice of the root is used for swellings of the nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It dries wounds and sticks them strongly, even a cut tendon; powdered with honey it is used on fire burns, wounds of the nerve and deep ulcers; mixed with bitter vetch and honey it cleans the dirt of ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Joints
Its oil helps the nerve, and a poultice of the root is used for swellings and knots of the nerve and pains of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Head, ears and teeth
It opens obstructions of the brain and helps moist melancholic headache, as does its oil, which suits better; it gives headache to hot heads {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Chest and breathing
Its oil dissolves hard, cold swellings in the diaphragm when rubbed on the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its root, eaten as it is, provokes vomiting, and so does its first-pressed juice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Excretory organs
It helps pains of the womb and bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that drunk with honey water it causes the loss of foetuses, living and dead; the stated measure is not carried over here. Its oil opens the closure of the mouth of the womb and helps its pains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.
