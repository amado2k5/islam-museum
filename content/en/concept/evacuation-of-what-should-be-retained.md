## Summary
Ibn Sina says that the evacuation of what should be retained comes either from strength of the expelling power or weakness of the retaining power, or because the matter troubles by its heaviness through quantity, by distension through its wind, by stinging through its sharpness and pungency, or because the matter is thin and flows of itself, helped sometimes by wide passages {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.139}.

## What follows
When what ought to be retained is evacuated, coldness of temperament follows from the evacuation of the kindling matter on which the innate heat feeds, and sometimes heat, if what is evacuated is cold in temperament, like phlegm. Dryness always follows by itself, and every excessive evacuation is followed by cold and dryness in the substance of the organs, and sometimes by spasm and tetanus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.139}. Moderate retention and evacuation, coming when needed, are useful and keep the state of health {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.139}.
