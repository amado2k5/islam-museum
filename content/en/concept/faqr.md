## Summary
Poverty is the subject of the first half of the Book of Poverty and Renunciation, the fourth book of the quarter of saving things in [[work/ihya-ulum-al-din|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]]; the second half concerns [[concept/zuhd|renunciation]]. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7181} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7184} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7317} [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|Al-Ghazali]] defines it as "lacking what one needs"; lacking what one does not need is not called poverty. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7184}

## Absolute poverty and poverty of wealth
Al-Ghazali holds that everything other than God is poor in this sense, because it needs the continuation of its existence from God's bounty, and that there can be only one who is rich in existence. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7184} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7185} But here he does not mean absolute poverty, since the servant's needs are countless; he means poverty of wealth in particular. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7185}

At the start of this half he lists its contents: the reality of poverty; its merit in general; the merit of the content and satisfied poor; the superiority of the poor over the rich; the conduct of the poor man in his poverty and in accepting gifts; the prohibition of begging without necessity; the amount of wealth that makes begging forbidden; and the states of those who ask. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7184}

## The five states of the poor man, and the state above them
Al-Ghazali distinguishes five states in one who lacks wealth, and names each: {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7186}

1. **Renunciation (zuhd):** he would dislike wealth if it came to him, be troubled by it and flee from it, wary of its harm and distraction; he is the renunciant, and this is the highest. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7186}
2. **Contentment (rida):** he neither desires it enough to rejoice at receiving it nor dislikes it enough to be troubled; he is the content one. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7186}
3. **Satisfaction (qana'a):** he prefers having wealth to lacking it, but his desire is not strong enough to make him seek it; if it comes freely he takes it. He is the satisfied one. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7186} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7187}
4. **Covetousness (hirs):** he stops seeking only because he cannot, and would seek it if he found a way, or he is busy seeking it; he is the covetous one. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7187}
5. **Destitution (idtirar):** what he lacks is something he cannot do without, like the hungry man without bread or the naked man without clothing; he is the destitute. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7187}

Beyond these five he places a state higher than renunciation: that having wealth and lacking it are equal to him, so that he neither rejoices nor is troubled. He illustrates it with what he relates of [[person/aisha|Aisha]]: a hundred thousand dirhams came to her and she distributed them that same day; her maid said she might have bought a dirham's worth of meat for them to break their fast, and she replied, "Had you reminded me, I would have." {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7187} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7188} He calls one in this state "self-sufficient" (mustaghni) rather than "rich" (ghani), so that "the Rich" remains the name of the One who has absolute wealth, since this servant is free of need for money but not of need for God's continuing help. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7188} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7189}

## Which is better: poverty or wealth?
Al-Ghazali says people differed on this; he presents the views and then gives his own detailed answer: {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7240}

:::perspectives
- **Ahl al-Sunna: al-Junayd, al-Khawwas and most, as al-Ghazali reports.** They held poverty to be better; it is said that [[person/al-junayd-ibn-muhammad|al-Junayd]] invoked against Ibn 'Ata' for differing with him and a trial befell him. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7240}
- **Ahl al-Sunna: Ibn 'Ata' as al-Ghazali reports him.** He said the grateful rich man who fulfils its due is better than the patient poor man, citing the hadith of the poor who complained that the rich had outstripped them in charity, and arguing that wealth is an attribute of God. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7240} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7241} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7242}
- **Ahl al-Sunna: al-Ghazali (the author's view).** He answers the first argument with a fuller version of the report showing the greater reward of the poor; al-Iraqi says he did not find that version in this form, and that the known report with this meaning is Ibn Majah's, from Ibn Umar with a weak chain, that the believing poor enter paradise half a day, five hundred years, before the rich. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7242} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7243} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7244} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7248} He answers the second with a master's reply: if resembling an attribute of God were a merit, pride would be better than humility; the attributes of servanthood are the servant's merit. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7244} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7245} He then holds that both sides "clung to general statements open to interpretation"; this world is not to be shunned for itself but because it obstructs, and poverty is not sought for itself but because it removes the obstacle. Many rich men were not distracted by wealth, such as [[person/sulayman|Solomon]], [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] and [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], and many poor men were distracted by poverty. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7245} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7246} What distracts is love of the world. If two people free of love of money are imagined, the one who has what he needs is better, for the hungry man walks the path of death, not of knowledge; but taking most people, the poor man is further from danger, because the trial of ease is harder than the trial of hardship. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7246} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7247} Since the law addresses everyone and not the rare exception, it censured wealth and preferred poverty. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7249}
- **Not given here:** the academic view of Sufi poverty; not yet documented with a defined source.
:::

## For young visitors
Poverty in this book means having little money. Al-Ghazali says what matters is not having much or little, but that money should not fill a person's heart and turn it from God; he tells how Aisha gave away a great sum in a single day.
