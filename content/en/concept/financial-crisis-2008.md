## Summary
The crisis that began in the United States in the mortgage market, when banks expanded their risks in pursuit of gains and property prices then fell as borrowers defaulted {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 350}. It spread from one sector to another and from America to other countries, and governments resorted to injecting billions to rescue banks and corporations {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 350}. The author notes that the American economy would have collapsed without the bailout, citing a statement by Obama {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 350}.

## His Ten Causes
The author presents the causes from an Islamic perspective in ten points: riba, gambling and gharar, derivatives, securitization, the sale of debts, money creation, banking supervision, administrative corruption, excessive pursuit of private interests, and the role of scholars and the media {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 350}. ## Origin of the Paper
The final chapter of the book is a paper submitted to the Islamic Economics Research Center one week before the G20 summit held on 2008/11/15, according to the book {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 359}. It does not contain a statistical analysis of the crisis, but rather a general jurisprudential and economic reading.
