The child of fornication (walad al-zina) is the child a mother bears out of unlawful intercourse, not out of marriage. Lexically walad means any child, male or female, young or grown; the Hanafis define zina as a man's intercourse with a woman in the front passage outside ownership or its semblance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17929}

Unlike the child of li'an, whose lineage from the father was once established and then cut, the child of fornication never has lineage from the father. He is also, unlike the foundling, known as to his mother. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17929}

Religion: the Hanafis hold that a child follows the better of his two parents in religion, and the wording "parents" excludes the child of fornication. Ibn Abidin, citing the Shabli fatwas, notes Shafi'i disagreement about his Islam and prefers that he be judged Muslim, since the default is the natural disposition and caution favours religion. Ahmad held that the child of a Christian slave-woman by fornication is a Muslim, since no parent is left to raise him in another faith. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17930} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17931}

Call to prayer: the Hanafis and Malikis allow him to call the adhan, since its purpose, announcement, is served, but another is preferred, as such a child is usually ignorant and the call is a noble office. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17931}

Leading prayer: most jurists dislike it, with distinctions. The Hanafis dislike it where a more entitled person is present, but allow it if he takes the lead. The Malikis dislike appointing him as a regular imam. The Shafi'is prefer a more learned or more pious man. The Hanbalis allow it if his religion is sound, citing the hadith that the one most learned in the Book should lead, and a saying of A'isha that no one bears the burden of his parents. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17931} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17932}

Zakat, zakat al-fitr and 'aqiqa: the Hanafis forbid a father to give his zakat to his child of fornication, out of the closeness of the relation. The Shafi'i Shirwani holds that the zakat al-fitr for him falls on his mother. The Shafi'is say a mother who maintains the child may perform the 'aqiqa, without publicising the child's origin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17932}

Waqf: the Hanbalis hold that a waqf for orphans does not include the child of fornication, since an orphan's heart is broken by the loss of the father. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17932}

Marriage prohibition: all agree on permanent prohibition between the child of fornication and his mother. Between a fornicator and his daughter by that union, the majority (Hanafis, Malikis, Hanbalis, and a Shafi'i view) hold the prohibition applies as for other children, since she is his offspring in fact even though lineage is not established in law. The Shafi'i view, the preferred one for them, is that the prohibition does not apply and marriage is merely disliked; some say it is prohibited absolutely where the father is sure she is his. If the fornicator was insane at the time, lineage and prohibition are established. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17932} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17933} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17934}

A fornicator's marriage to the wife of his child by fornication (or the reverse) is disputed: the Hanafis and Hanbalis (in the school's view) and a Maliki view prohibit it under the general verses of prohibition; the Malikis in their preferred view, and al-Rahibani, prohibit nothing, since the child is ascribed to his mother and the wife is a stranger. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17934} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17935}

Collateral prohibitions: the prohibition covers the child's ascendants and descendants by the fornicator. Whether his uncles, aunts and brothers are also prohibited is disputed: al-Haskafi holds it applies generally, while Ibn Abidin, citing al-Bahr, denies the paternal uncle and maternal uncle of the fornicator are prohibited, since no lineage was established. The Malikis prohibit the ascendants and descendants; the Hanbalis add the fornicator's sister, granddaughter, nieces and aunts by the same union. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17935}

Equality in marriage: the Hanafis and Shafi'is count lineage in equality (kafa'a), the Malikis and one Hanbali view do not. Al-Buhuti reports that the child of fornication is said to be equal to a woman of lineage, though Ahmad disliked such unions because the woman and her guardians are harmed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17936}

Lineage: all agree on his lineage from his mother. The majority (Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis) deny lineage from the fornicator, citing the saying "the child belongs to the bed, and the adulterer gets the stone", and that without acknowledgement lineage is never established. Al-Hasan and Ibn Sirin hold he is linked to the fornicator if the hadd is applied to him and he inherits; Ibrahim links him if flogged or if he owns the woman; Ishaq links him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17936}

Foster-suckling from the milk of fornication: all agree that a child suckled by a mother who bore him by fornication is her foster child, and so prohibited to her. Whether he is prohibited to the fornicator is disputed: the Shafi'is, the preferred Hanafi view, and al-Khiraqi and Ibn Hamid among the Hanbalis deny it; the Malikis, another Hanafi view and Abu Bakr Abd al-Aziz among the Hanbalis allow prohibition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17936} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17937}

Inheritance: he inherits from his mother and her relatives, and they inherit from him, by fixed shares and agnation; his agnates are his mother's agnates. The majority deny inheritance from the fornicator and his kin, since lineage is cut. Two children of one mother by the same man, born before and after his marriage to her, are full siblings through the mother and inherit from each other. Al-Hasan and Ibn Sirin link him to the fornicator who is flogged, and make him heir. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17937}

Judgeship: the Malikis differ. Sahnun allows him to hold judgeship but not to judge hadd cases for fornication; al-Baji forbids it, since judgeship is a post of honour and purity, as with leading prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17937}

Testimony: most jurists (Hanafis, Shafi'is, Hanbalis) accept his testimony in fornication and other matters, by the general verses and because his parents' offence does not impair his probity. The Malikis and al-Layth accept it except in fornication and what attaches to it, such as slander and li'an, because he may be accused of wanting to share the mark of the fornicator's son. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17937} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17938}

Testimony for his fornicator father: the Hanafis and one Hanbali view reject it, since the father is his real progenitor, as shown by the prohibition between them. Another Hanbali view accepts testimony by a child for his father in fornication or suckling, since no maintenance or kinship duty binds them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17938}

Slander: whoever accuses him in person of fornication is liable to the hadd for slander, if the victim meets the conditions of chastity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17938}

Killing: the preferred Hanbali view is that a father is retaliated against for killing his child by fornication; another Hanbali view does not allow it, as the Hanafi statements imply, given the closeness of the relation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17938}
