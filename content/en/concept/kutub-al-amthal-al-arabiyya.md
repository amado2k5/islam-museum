## Summary
Scholars of Arabic gathered the Arabic proverbs in books of their own, and this page sets out four of them as a research summary supplied to the museum reports them, by author, time of composition and number of proverbs: Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam's Kitab al-Amthal, Abu Hilal al-Askari's Jamharat al-Amthal, al-Zamakhshari's al-Mustaqsa fi amthal al-arab, and al-Maydani's Majma al-Amthal. It then points to ten proverbs the summary tells with their stories, each of which has its own page {cite:arabictypinglab-arabic-proverbs} {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p16}.

## Abu Ubayd's Kitab al-Amthal
The summary notes in a side remark that the earliest foundational work of this kind is Kitab al-Amthal by Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam, who died in 224 AH, 838 CE, and that it contains 1,000 proverbs, 130 hadiths and 20 lines of verse. Its author has a record in the museum, [[person/abu-ubayd-al-qasim-ibn-sallam|Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam]], and the museum also holds a record [[work/al-amthal-al-saira|al-Amthal al-Sa'ira]] ascribed to him; the summary does not say that the book it names is this record, so the museum does not assert it {cite:arrafid-article-amthal-stories}.

## al-Askari's Jamharat al-Amthal
Abu Hilal al-Askari composed the Jamharat al-Amthal, finishing it about 395 AH, 1005 CE, and died about 400 AH, 1010 CE. It has, according to the summary, at least 2,000 main entries, and several hundred more in the commentary. The summary's references are a page of Arab News and an article in the University of Bisha journal for the humanities. The author has a record, [[person/abu-hilal-al-askari|Abu Hilal al-Askari]] {cite:arabnews-archive-216491} {cite:ubjh-journal-article-1117}.

## al-Zamakhshari's al-Mustaqsa
al-Mustaqsa fi amthal al-arab by Abu al-Qasim Mahmud al-Zamakhshari was completed in 499 AH, 1106 CE, and its author died in 538 AH, 1144 CE. It has, according to the summary, over 1,500 entries. The summary refers to its text on the Turath platform and to a PDF copy. The author's record is [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] {cite:turath-al-mustaqsa-6895} {cite:moswrat-al-mustaqsa-pdf-3410}.

## al-Maydani's Majma al-Amthal
Majma al-Amthal by Abu al-Fadl Ahmad al-Maydani belongs to the early sixth Hijri century, the twelfth CE, and its author died in 518 AH, 1124 CE. It has over 6,000 entries, so by the summary's counts it is the largest of the four. The museum holds two records for it: [[person/al-maydani|al-Maydani]] as author and [[work/majma-al-amthal|Majma al-Amthal]] as book. According to the summary the stories of the ten proverbs below are told in this book {cite:arabictypinglab-arabic-proverbs} {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p16}.

## What the order shows
The dates in the summary agree that Abu Ubayd's book is the oldest, that al-Askari's Jamhara was completed about 167 years after his death, and that al-Mustaqsa was completed in 499 AH, nineteen years before al-Maydani's death, so that the two lived in near the same generation. This is a calculation from the dates given, not a count the museum made of the proverbs. The numbers differ: 1,000 for Abu Ubayd, at least 2,000 for al-Askari, 1,500 for al-Zamakhshari, over 6,000 for al-Maydani; the summary does not say the counts were made in one way, so they are not compared as a firm comparison.

## The ten proverbs
[[concept/mathal-raja-a-bi-khuffay-hunayn|He returned with Hunayn's two boots]], [[concept/mathal-ala-ahliha-janat-baraqish|Against her own people Baraqish sinned]], [[concept/mathal-al-sayfa-dayyat-al-laban|In summer you wasted the milk]], [[concept/mathal-jaza-sinimmar|Sinimmar's reward]], [[concept/mathal-mawaid-urqub|The promises of Urqub]], [[concept/mathal-sabaqa-al-sayf-al-adhl|The sword preceded the blame]], [[concept/mathal-rubba-ramyatin-min-ghayri-ram|Many a shot is from one who is no archer]], [[concept/mathal-biyadi-la-biyadi-amr|By my own hand, not Amr's]], [[concept/mathal-atatka-bihain-rijlah|His own feet brought a doomed man to you]], [[concept/mathal-ata-al-abad-ala-lubad|Eternity has overtaken Lubad]]. For some of them the summary gives the place in these books by volume and page. The stories told of them are widely repeated reports in the books, whose happening the museum does not establish {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p16} {cite:arrafid-article-amthal-stories}.

## Basis
The page rests on a research summary supplied to the museum, and the museum did not open the pages named; the founder approved relying on it as it stands on 21 November 2026. All the names, dates and numbers here are attributed to the summary, and the museum has read none of the four books.
