## Definition
Ihmal is lexically leaving a thing neglected, or letting it go without management or use. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3663}

## Neglect and liability
- In trusts: neglect that leads to loss or destruction creates liability, since a trustee is liable only for excess or neglect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3663}
- A private hired worker is liable for neglect; a shared worker is liable in all cases by the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3663}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3664}
- Neglect by a skilled practitioner, such as a doctor, circumciser or teacher, makes him liable for what follows, as does a cutter who exceeds his mandate in retaliation or in the cutting for theft. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3664}

## Giving effect to speech
The rule is that speech should be given effect rather than left void, since void speech is idle. When the literal sense is impossible, a figurative sense is used; when neither is possible, the speech is left void, as in a claim that a living man has been killed by his heir, that a limb has been cut from one who stands, or that a child of known lineage is one's son. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3664}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3665}
- The impossibility may be of fact, of law, or by disuse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3664}
- Where the claim is contradicted by the senses, or what is like the senses, such as a claim of puberty in one too young, it is void, even if evidence is produced. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3665}
