## Overview
Muhammad al-Khidr Husayn opens with the linguistic sense of freedom — emancipation — then the intended one: a nation living in security, each person knowing his bounds and rights, neither trespassing nor deprived {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 44} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 45}. Freedom is a mean between license and deprivation, standing on four things: knowing one's rights and duties, an honor that bars aggression, submission to just laws, and a dignity that refuses falsehood {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 45} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 46}. Upbringing and education are its foundation; government is answerable for refining morals and enlightening minds before calling anyone to account.

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
In the eleventh episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] counts freedom of belief as the most important right Islam guaranteed to the human being, saying that the Prophet, though certain that he was right, did not take on guardianship over people's beliefs to change them. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:13:00} He cites the Prophet's stance with his uncle Abu Talib at the last moment, when he only urged him to say a word, and Abu Talib died on the creed of his fathers. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:13:30} He says that after the debate with the delegation of Najran and their refusal of the mutual invocation, the Prophet did not force them to believe but wrote them a covenant confirming them in their churches and their crosses, and that Umar ibn al-Khattab did the same when he took Jerusalem. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:16:00} Answering the claim that Islam spread by the sword, he says eight thousand soldiers entered Egypt when its people numbered four million, and asks how eight thousand could change the beliefs of four million without conviction; he sees the survival of Christians and Jews in Islamic civilization across the centuries as the clearest sign that there was no compulsion. These figures are his. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:18:30} He cites Gustave Le Bon's book on the civilization of the Arabs to the effect that had the Muslims treated minorities as the Crusaders, as he puts it, treated Muslims in al-Andalus, not one Christian or Jew would have remained. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep11 00:19:00}
