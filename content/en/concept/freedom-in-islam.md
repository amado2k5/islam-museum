## Overview
Muhammad al-Khidr Husayn opens with the linguistic sense of freedom — emancipation — then the intended one: a nation living in security, each person knowing his bounds and rights, neither trespassing nor deprived {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 44} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 45}. Freedom is a mean between license and deprivation, standing on four things: knowing one's rights and duties, an honor that bars aggression, submission to just laws, and a dignity that refuses falsehood {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 45} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 46}. Upbringing and education are its foundation; government is answerable for refining morals and enlightening minds before calling anyone to account.
