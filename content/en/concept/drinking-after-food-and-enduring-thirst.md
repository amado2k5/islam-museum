## Summary
Ibn Sina says much water should not be drunk over food, since it parts it from the stomach and quenches it; one waits until the food has gone down, judged by lightness in the upper belly, and sips a little cold water if thirst presses; enduring thirst and sleeping on it helps the cold and moist and harms the hot and bilious, and so does enduring hunger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.231-232}.

## Water over food
Much water is not drunk over food, for it comes between the food and the stomach and puts it out; one waits for the time the food takes to leave the stomach, judged by lightness of the upper belly, and if thirst presses, sips a little cold water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## Enduring thirst
Enduring thirst and sleeping on it benefits the cold and moist and harms the hot and bilious, as does enduring hunger, since in the bilious bile pours into their stomachs when they hold out against hunger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.231}.

## A spoiled appetite
The appetite for food may also be spoiled; then one drinks something light and unaltering that loosens the bowel, such as plum or a little shirkhisht, and eats when appetite returns {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.232}.
