## Summary
A juristic rule in Islam's laws of war and manumission; the Prophet declared it at the [[event/siege-of-taif|siege of Ta'if]] when his herald proclaimed freedom for any of the enemy's slaves who took refuge with the Muslim camp {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/128}.

## The historical origin
When the siege of Ta'if's fortress dragged on and Thaqif held out behind its walls, the Messenger of God's herald called: "Any slave who comes down to us and leaves the fortress is free" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/128}. Some dozen men lowered themselves from the fortress — among them [[person/abu-bakra-al-thaqafi|Abu Bakra Nufay' ibn al-Harith]], Wardan and Nafi' — and the Prophet freed them, entrusting each to a Muslim who would provide for him and teach him the Qur'an {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/128}.

When Ta'if's people later became Muslim, they asked the Prophet to return the men; he refused, saying: "No — those are God's freedmen", and it became a standing sunna with the imams of fiqh {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/128}.

## For young visitors
During the siege of Ta'if's fortress the Prophet announced that any slave who came out and joined the Muslims would gain freedom at once. Several slaves came out; he freed them and refused to return them to slavery even after their people's peace.
