## Summary
Ibn Sina counts the basilic as of great danger because the artery lies beneath it, and gives how it is known that the artery was struck, how its blood is stopped, and the dangers in cutting the artery and tying the limb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.294-295}.

## The danger
The basilic is of great danger because the artery lies beneath it, so take care in opening it, for when the artery is opened the blood does not cease, or ceases with difficulty. In some people two arteries flank the basilic; when one is marked he thinks himself safe and may strike the second, so you must learn of this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## Swelling and leaving the basilic
When the arm is bound, most often a swelling arises there, sometimes from the artery and sometimes from the basilic; the band must be loosed, the swelling rubbed gently away and the binding repeated, and if it returns it is repeated again. If that does not suffice, there is no blame on you if you leave the basilic and open the branch called the axillary, which is on the inner side of the forearm toward the lower part {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.294-295}.

## The lancet's path and the sign of error
The lower you go in opening the basilic toward the forearm the safer it is, and the lancet's path should be against the direction of the artery. The sign of error in the basilic and of striking the artery is that thin yellowish blood comes out leaping, and the swelling softens under the touch and sinks {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.295}.

## Stopping the blood of the artery
Act at once and stuff the mouth of the incision with some hare's fur together with some fine frankincense, dragon's blood, aloe and myrrh; put on the place some colcothar and vitriol, sprinkle cold water on it as far as possible, and bind it above the incision with a tight stopping band. When the blood stops, do not loosen the binding for three days, and after the three days take care again as far as possible and dress the region with astringents. The editor's footnote says colcothar is impure iron sulphate {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.295}.

## Cutting the artery and the danger of binding
Many people have their artery cut through, so that the vessel contracts and the flesh closes upon it and holds it. Many people have died of haemorrhage, and some have died because of binding the limb and the severe pain of the binding, which was meant to stop the artery's blood, until the limb went the way of death {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.295}.
