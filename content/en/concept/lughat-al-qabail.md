## Summary
Arabic in the Peninsula was not one uniform tongue; the tribes had their own ways of pronouncing and building words, which the books of language record. This page gathers what al-Suyuti transmits in al-Muzhir about the most eloquent tribes and about the tribes from whom the language was taken and those from whom it was not, the kinds of difference Ibn Faris lists in al-Sahibi, the words al-Suyuti says are Yemeni, and what is reported of the tongue of Quraysh in the Qur'an. Each statement is ascribed to its speaker.

## The most eloquent of the Arabs
In al-Muzhir, in the chapter on what was taken from the tribes' languages, al-Suyuti gives a saying of the Prophet: "I am the most eloquent of the Arabs, except that I am from Quraysh and grew up among Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr", and the page read shows no chain or grading for it. He quotes Abu Amr ibn al-Ala that the most eloquent Arabs are upper Hawazin and lower Tamim; Umar as saying that only the lads of Quraysh and Thaqif shall dictate our mushafs; and Uthman as saying the dictator should be of Hudhayl and the scribe of Thaqif. He quotes Tha'lab in his Amali that Quraysh stands above the 'an'ana of Tamim, the tal'ala of Bahra', the kaskasa of Rabi'a, the kashkasha of Hawazin and the 'ajrafiyya of Dabba {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.167}.

## The tribes from whom the language was taken
Al-Suyuti quotes the beginning of Abu Nasr al-Farabi's Kitab al-Alfaz wa-l-Huruf, as he gives it; al-Farabi's book itself was not read. Quraysh, he says, were the most discriminating of the Arabs in choosing the most eloquent words, the easiest to pronounce, the best to hear and the clearest in expressing what is in the soul. The tribes from whom Arabic was taken and by whom it was imitated are Qays, Tamim and Asad: "these are the ones from whom most of it was taken, and the greater part", and on them reliance was placed for rare words, inflection and morphology; then Hudhayl, some of Kinana and some of Tayyi', and nothing was taken from others {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.167}.

Al-Farabi then lists tribes not drawn on because of contact with other nations: Lakhm and Judham, neighbours of the Copts in Egypt; Quda'a, Ghassan and Iyad in Syria; Taghlib near the Greeks; Bakr, near the Copts and Persians; Abd al-Qays and the Azd of Oman, near India and Persia; the people of Yemen, near India and Abyssinia; Banu Hanifa in the Yamama and Thaqif in al-Ta'if, mixing with Yemeni traders; and the settled people of the Hijaz. He says that only the people of Basra and Kufa among the cities of the Arabs transmitted the language, recorded it in a book and made it a science and craft {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.168}.

## Kinds of difference in Ibn Faris
In al-Sahibi, [[person/ibn-faris|Ibn Faris]] has a chapter on the differences between the Arabs' languages and lists among them differences of vowels, such as the vowel on the prefix of the verb for seeking help, a fatha in Quraysh and a kasra in Asad and others according to al-Farra's report; vowel against sukun; substitution of letters; hamza against its softening; metathesis; deletion and addition; imala and tafkhim; masculine and feminine; assimilation; and inflection {cite:sahibi-baydun-1997 p.25} {cite:sahibi-baydun-1997 p.26}.

In the chapter on disliked dialects he names features ascribed to tribes: the 'an'ana of Tamim, which replaces hamza with 'ayn so that they say 'anna for anna; the kashkasha of Asad, which turns the feminine kaf into shin or adds shin after it; and the kaskasa of Rabi'a, which adds sin after the kaf {cite:sahibi-baydun-1997 p.29}.

## Yemeni words
In al-Itqan, al-Suyuti has a type on what occurs in other than the Hijazi language, and gives Yemeni words as examples. Among them are a word that Ikrima and Ibn Abbas explained as singing and called Yemeni; a word that al-Hasan said they did not know until a man of Yemen told them it means the canopy with the couch; two words that al-Dahhak called Yemeni; and an expression that Ikrima called a Yemeni use for marrying. Only the first page of this type was read {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.106}.

## The tongue of Quraysh in the Qur'an
At [[passage/quran-26-195|al-Shu'ara 195]], al-Baghawi reports from Ibn Abbas that the Qur'an came down in the tongue of Quraysh so that they understand what is in it {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 26:195}. In [[passage/bukhari-4987|al-Bukhari 4987]], [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] told the group copying the mushafs that if they and Zayd ibn Thabit differed on anything of the Qur'an they should write it in the tongue of Quraysh, for it came down in their tongue, and they did so {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. For the connection with the variety of readings see [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa|the seven ahruf]], and for the hearing among Quraysh [[concept/sama-quraysh-lil-quran|the hearing of the Qur'an by Quraysh]].

## What this page does not reach
Al-Farabi's book and Tha'lab's Amali were not read, only what al-Suyuti quotes from them. The lists of Yemeni words were not read in full here, nor the points of difference in al-Sahibi except those named. The page passes no judgement on any of the reports it transmits.
