## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat holds that the productive enterprise is the one blessed by the Islamic economy, whether stated explicitly in clear Qur'anic verses or drawn from their spirit and context, and it includes agricultural investment, livestock wealth, marine wealth, and whatever improves and develops these resources {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 89}.

## Beginning and Balance
The author states that forming any enterprise establishes new relationships among its elements, and that the success of the enterprise is proportional to the balance it achieves among these relationships, making the economy just and moral to that same degree {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 89}. He requires that an enterprise begin in the name of God, because resources do not run out with faith and sincerity in work, and because wealth belongs to God and the human being is entrusted with it as a steward {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 89}.

## The Six Characteristics
The author sets out six controls and characteristics for the Islamic economic enterprise, namely {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 90}:
- Consultation (shura) and direct democracy in decision-making, regardless of how important the decision is.
- Ending individual domination, including the employer's domination over workers, by lifting immunity from any individual and holding everyone accountable.
- Ending capitalist domination, and withdrawing power from the owners of capital.
- Optimal use of materials and resources, because bounty comes from God's grace and all people have a right to it on the condition of work and competence.
- Preventing the monopoly of certain resources, and placing everything at the disposal of those capable of working and producing.
- Ending the dominance of the material motive over individuals' feelings in production, and replacing it with a spiritual motive in which work is obedience to God's command to strive upon the earth.

## Forms of the Enterprise
The author mentions three forms: the individual enterprise, which is devoted to self-employment and in which family members may participate {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 90}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 91}; the cooperative enterprise, which is [[concept/al-jamiyya-al-taawuniyya|The Cooperative Society]] {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 91}; and the people's institution, which is [[concept/al-mashru-al-shabi|The People's Enterprise]] {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 93}.

## The Author's View
He holds that the individual enterprise is necessary in the Qur'anic economic method, especially in agriculture and small-scale production, provided it does not turn into domination by the individual or by capital {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 91}. This is his opinion, presented in the book as an assessment rather than the result of measurement.
