## Its definition and cause
Sabal is a veil occurring to the eye from swelling of its vessels visible on the surface of the conjunctiva and cornea, something weaving between them like smoke; its cause is the filling of those vessels, either from materials flowing to them by way of the outer membrane or by way of the inner membrane from the head's repletion and the eye's weakness. Sabal may bring itch, tearing, veiling and hurt from sunlight and lamplight so that sight weakens under both, for the eye is hurt and restless and what is imposed on it harms it; the sabala may occur to the eye making it smaller, the pupil's mass diminishing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.185}

## The signs
The sign of the sabal whose origin is the outer membrane is what we have repeatedly stated: fullness of the outer vessels, redness of the face, strong pulsation at the temples or fullness of the neck vessels; the signs of the other are what you know of the contrary, already made clear to you in the Canon {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.185}

## The treatment
With it all that the catarrh patient abandons toward the eye must be abandoned as stated; the evacuations and cleansings stated are used; oils and poultices on the head and snuff are avoided — it is also disliked in it, and I see no harm in using it if the head is clean ⁦(the speaker's word is the author's own view in the book)⁩. Galen permitted giving him a drink and putting him to sleep after it if he is clean with no matter in his body and head, and this seems fitting in the light sabal ⁦(Galen's permission transmitted as the book's report)⁩. The strong one cannot do without excision, and the best excision is to pass many threads under the vessels, and once completed draw them upward to lift the sabal, then excise with a sharp-pointed scissors an excision leaving nothing — for any remainder returns it to what it was or worse — then use the anti-adhesion management mentioned in the pterygium chapter; the best times for excision are spring and autumn, but after cleansing and evacuation lest the pain incline the superfluities toward the eye ⁦(the excision carried as the book's description, not as procedure; collyrium and drug names not carried)⁩.
