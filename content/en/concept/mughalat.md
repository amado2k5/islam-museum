## Summary
Mughalat means exaggeration: going beyond the proper measure in a thing. Its opposite is cheapness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14800}

## Excess in the dowry
Most jurists set no fixed upper limit on the dowry. Umar ibn al-Khattab once forbade raising the dowry above four hundred dirhams and then withdrew the ruling. The Malikis disliked a dowry that exceeds what is customary for such a woman, and most jurists regard a light dowry as recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14801} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14802}

## Excess in the shroud
The jurists agree that exaggeration in the shroud is disliked, following the hadith that forbids it. Ibn Abidin takes it to mean more than the customary shroud; a good shroud is one that is white, clean and thick, not an expensive one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14802} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14803}

## Excess in worship
A Muslim should be moderate in obedience to God, avoid both excess and neglect, and not burden himself beyond his strength. A small act done steadily is better than intense effort followed by giving up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14802} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14803}
