## Summary
Al-Battani offers a second way to the east-west line, and from it the north-south line, by knowing an altitude whose azimuth does not depart from the equinoctial rising or setting; when the Sun reaches that altitude the middle of the rod's shadow lies on the east or west point. The method works only while the Sun is in the northern signs, from the first of Aries to the end of Virgo. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.40-41}.

## The condition of the method
He says: if you wish to find the line between east and west in another way, and from it the line between north and south, it is by knowing the altitude whose azimuth has no deviation from the equinoctial rising or setting; and this is possible only when the Sun is in the northern signs, from the first of Aries to the end of Virgo, and no other. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.40}.

## The computation and the observation
The way to the altitude: know the Sun's place in those signs on the chosen day and its altitude at midday, take the chord of that altitude and the chord of its complement to ninety, then find the azimuth of the Sun's rising or setting for its place on the ecliptic at midday, which is always northern. Take the chord of that azimuth and multiply it by the chord of the altitude, and divide the product by the chord of the azimuth and the chord of the altitude's complement added together; the arc of the quotient is the altitude whose azimuth does not deviate. Then watch the Sun until its altitude equals it, mark on the circle the middle of the rod's shadow, a point which is the east or west of the equinox according to the hour, and quarter the circle on that point with two lines crossing at right angles. The wording of the multiplication and division is disordered in the scan, so this gives its order, not its text. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.40-41}.
