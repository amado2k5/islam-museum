## Summary
Maleness is the opposite of femaleness, and the jurists treat it as a condition in several rulings. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8474}
The Malikis require it for leading prayer, and the majority bar a woman from leading men; the Hanbalis and others allow a woman to lead women. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8474} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8475}
Maleness is a required condition for the obligation of Friday prayer and of holy war, so neither a woman nor an intersex person of uncertain sex is bound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8475} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8476}

## Governance
The jurists agree that maleness is a condition for the supreme imamate. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8477}
The majority require a male judge, while the Hanafis allow a woman to judge in cases without hadd penalties. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8477}

## Marriage
The majority require a male guardian, so a woman may not contract her own marriage, while the Hanafis allow a mature woman to contract her own marriage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8475} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8476}
