## Summary
Khata' is lexically the opposite of right; one who misses the way strays from it, and an archer who misses the target has not hit it; khati'a is a sin, the verb with intent being khatiya and without intent akhta'a. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7652}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Technically it is an act done without intent toward the object of the act: Kamal ibn al-Humam gives the example of rinsing the mouth that reaches a fasting man's throat, and shooting at game that hits a human. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Most jurists treat ghalat as equal to khata'; some Malikis distinguish that khata' is in the heart and ghalat in the tongue, yet ghalat takes khata's ruling; Abu Hilal al-'Askari distinguishes that ghalat is placing a thing out of its place and may be right in itself, while khata' is never right in any way. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653}
