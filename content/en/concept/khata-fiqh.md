## Summary
Khata' is lexically the opposite of right; one who misses the way strays from it, and an archer who misses the target has not hit it; khati'a is a sin, the verb with intent being khatiya and without intent akhta'a. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7652}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Technically it is an act done without intent toward the object of the act: Kamal ibn al-Humam gives the example of rinsing the mouth that reaches a fasting man's throat, and shooting at game that hits a human. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Most jurists treat ghalat as equal to khata'; some Malikis distinguish that khata' is in the heart and ghalat in the tongue, yet ghalat takes khata's ruling; Abu Hilal al-'Askari distinguishes that ghalat is placing a thing out of its place and may be right in itself, while khata' is never right in any way. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Most jurists use khata' for what slips from the tongue without intent, while the Malikis use ghalat for what concerns belief. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7654} Near terms are forgetfulness, inattention, distraction and heedlessness; compulsion (forcing another against his will, short of compulsion that removes choice); jest (speaking without meaning); and ignorance, simple or compound; the one in error is not responsible for what he erred in. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7654}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7655} Ignorance and error excuse in what is forbidden, not in what is commanded; they excuse nothing in the rights of persons, so the ignorant and mistaken pay for what they destroy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7655} The usulis differ on whether the act of one in error can be called lawful or unlawful: al-Asnawi holds that lawful and unlawful apply to accountable persons, so the error is not described; al-Shatibi holds that error and forgetfulness are in a zone of pardon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7655}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7656} Most jurists hold that error excuses some of God's rights but not all, citing the report "God has lifted from my community error and forgetfulness"; the rights of people are not lifted by error, so destroyed property and blood-money are owed, since that is a rule of ruling not of commanding. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7656}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7657} Al-Qurtubi distinguishes: the kind that is never lifted, such as fines, blood-money and the obligatory prayers; the kind always lifted, such as retaliation and uttering unbelief under duress; and the disputed kind, such as one who eats in Ramadan by forgetting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7657} The maxim "no weight is given to a clearly mistaken assumption" is held by the Hanafis and Shafiis; a case: one who thought the dawn was near and prayed it before the missed isha, then saw the time was long, must repeat the dawn prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7658}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7659} Shafii cases: one who thought himself pure, prayed, and then his impurity appeared, or who prayed behind an imam he thought Muslim who proved an unbeliever, and so on, mostly void. The Malikis use the maxim of clear manifestation; Ibn Rajab sets out rules on one who disposes of what he thinks he does not own. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7659}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7660} Error in purity: one who judges a vessel or cloth by guesswork and then finds it impure must repeat, by the Hanafis, Shafiis and one Maliki view; the Hanbalis do not apply the same rule. Error in the intention for wudu: by agreement of Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis the lesser intention can be overtaken; the Hanafis do not require intention for wudu or washing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7661}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7662} Error in the intention for the major washing and dry ablution: intending the washing for sexual impurity when it came from a wet dream is not harmful for the Shafiis and Hanafis; a menstruating woman intending only the major impurity raises a Maliki dispute. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7662}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7663} The entry continues in the following pages. On dry ablution: the Malikis and Ibn Maslama permit it, the Shafiis accept a mistaken intention for the minor state when one is in the major, and the Hanbalis require the purpose to be specified. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7664} One who had water in his baggage, forgot it, searched, found none, dry-ablutes and prays: valid for the Malikis, one Shafii view and the Hanbalis; the other Shafii view requires repetition for his neglect of the baggage. One who knew of the water but thought it was used up must repeat, by the Hanafis, Malikis, the sounder Shafii view and the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7664}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7665} In prayer intention: where the thing need not be specified, error does no harm, as with the place, the time and the number of units; where it must be specified, error harms, as with a noon prayer intended as an afternoon one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7665} On following an imam: error about his identity usually does no harm; intending to pray over one person and finding another invalidates the funeral prayer by most schools, and the Hanbalis differ on the sex of the dead. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7665}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7666} On ada' and qada' and the wrong day: the Malikis generally validate; the Hanafis and Shafiis invalidate except in certain cases; the Hanbalis validate qada' with a mistaken ada' intention when the error shows against belief. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7666}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7667} On prayer before the time: invalid by agreement, whether by design or error. The Shafiis require certain knowledge or a valid estimate; the Hanbalis accept a strong estimate, and repeat if the prayer proves early. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7667}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7668} On error in the qibla: the Hanafis accept ijtihad without repetition; the Malikis repeat within the time if clear signs were present and one faced the wrong way; the Shafiis have two views once error is certain; the Hanbalis require no repetition for the ijtihad, but the sighted at home and the blind without a guide repeat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7668} On error in recitation: the Hanafis distinguish errors of inflection, which do not invalidate unless they change the meaning badly, from errors of letters and words, and treat near-synonyms as harmless. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7668}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7669} On error in recitation as it affects the follower: the Malikis examine prayer behind a faulty reciter, some voiding it outright or by detail, and validating it where the reciter cannot learn; the Shafiis validate following one whose fault does not change the meaning, and void it where the opening chapter's sense is changed and he cannot correct it; the Hanbalis treat one who cannot recite the opening chapter, or whose faults change its meaning, as unfit to lead. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7670}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7671} Speech in prayer: the Hanafis void the prayer by speech done in forgetfulness, intent or error; others separate slight speech, which does not void, from much speech, which does. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7671} The imam\'s doubt: if two whom he trusts tell him of an error, he must go by them, by the three Imams; al-Shafii holds that if he judges their error likely he need not follow them; if he is certain of his own correctness, the Hanafis, Malikis, the sounder Shafii view and most Hanbalis do not follow the followers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7672} Error in the fear prayer: if the troops saw a dark shape and took it for an enemy, prayed the prayer of extreme fear, and it proved otherwise, the Hanafis, Hanbalis and one Shafii view require repetition; the Malikis do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7672}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7673} Zakat: if the fruit proves more than the estimate, the Malikis take zakat on the excess; if the owner claims the estimate was too high, the Hanbalis accept his word if the claim is plausible; the Shafiis distinguish by the size of the error. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7673}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7674} Error in the recipient of zakat: if it is given to one thought eligible who proves not, the Hanafis and one Shafii view hold it valid; Abu Yusuf, the Hanbalis and the sounder Shafii view, when the giver is the ruler, let the sum be recovered; the Hanbalis hold it invalid if given to an unbeliever or to one not eligible. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7674}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7675} Fasting: whoever gives a general intention for Ramadan, or errs in its description, has a valid fast by the Hanafis and one view of others, since the time is fixed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7675} One who breaks the fast by forgetfulness owes no expiation. Water entering by mistake while rinsing voids the fast and requires only a make-up by the Hanafis, Malikis and one Shafii view; the Hanbalis and another Shafii view hold it does not void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7676} Error about the time: one who ate on the belief that dawn had not broken, or broke the fast on the belief that the sun had set, owes a make-up but no expiation by most jurists; the Hanbalis require both make-up and expiation for intercourse, even by mistake. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7676}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7677} Hajj: if the pilgrims all erred and stood on the tenth, the Hanafis, Shafiis and Hanbalis hold it valid; the Malikis distinguish by how many erred; standing on the eighth in the belief it was the ninth is invalid for the Hanafis, Malikis and the sounder Shafii view, valid for the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7677}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7678} Erring in the place of standing and standing outside Arafat requires a make-up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7679} Killing game in the sacred precinct, or game by one in the state of ihram, is forbidden and carries a compensation whether done by intent or error, by agreement; the Hanafis and Malikis treat errors and intent alike for the prohibitions of ihram, while the Shafiis and Hanbalis distinguish destruction from enjoyment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7679}
