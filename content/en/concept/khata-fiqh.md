## Summary
Khata' is lexically the opposite of right; one who misses the way strays from it, and an archer who misses the target has not hit it; khati'a is a sin, the verb with intent being khatiya and without intent akhta'a. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7652}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Technically it is an act done without intent toward the object of the act: Kamal ibn al-Humam gives the example of rinsing the mouth that reaches a fasting man's throat, and shooting at game that hits a human. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Most jurists treat ghalat as equal to khata'; some Malikis distinguish that khata' is in the heart and ghalat in the tongue, yet ghalat takes khata's ruling; Abu Hilal al-'Askari distinguishes that ghalat is placing a thing out of its place and may be right in itself, while khata' is never right in any way. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7653} Most jurists use khata' for what slips from the tongue without intent, while the Malikis use ghalat for what concerns belief. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7654} Near terms are forgetfulness, inattention, distraction and heedlessness; compulsion (forcing another against his will, short of compulsion that removes choice); jest (speaking without meaning); and ignorance, simple or compound; the one in error is not responsible for what he erred in. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7654}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7655} Ignorance and error excuse in what is forbidden, not in what is commanded; they excuse nothing in the rights of persons, so the ignorant and mistaken pay for what they destroy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7655} The usulis differ on whether the act of one in error can be called lawful or unlawful: al-Asnawi holds that lawful and unlawful apply to accountable persons, so the error is not described; al-Shatibi holds that error and forgetfulness are in a zone of pardon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7655}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7656} Most jurists hold that error excuses some of God's rights but not all, citing the report "God has lifted from my community error and forgetfulness"; the rights of people are not lifted by error, so destroyed property and blood-money are owed, since that is a rule of ruling not of commanding. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7656}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7657} Al-Qurtubi distinguishes: the kind that is never lifted, such as fines, blood-money and the obligatory prayers; the kind always lifted, such as retaliation and uttering unbelief under duress; and the disputed kind, such as one who eats in Ramadan by forgetting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7657} The maxim "no weight is given to a clearly mistaken assumption" is held by the Hanafis and Shafiis; a case: one who thought the dawn was near and prayed it before the missed isha, then saw the time was long, must repeat the dawn prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7658}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7659} Shafii cases: one who thought himself pure, prayed, and then his impurity appeared, or who prayed behind an imam he thought Muslim who proved an unbeliever, and so on, mostly void. The Malikis use the maxim of clear manifestation; Ibn Rajab sets out rules on one who disposes of what he thinks he does not own. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7659}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7660} Error in purity: one who judges a vessel or cloth by guesswork and then finds it impure must repeat, by the Hanafis, Shafiis and one Maliki view; the Hanbalis do not apply the same rule. Error in the intention for wudu: by agreement of Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis the lesser intention can be overtaken; the Hanafis do not require intention for wudu or washing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7661}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7662} Error in the intention for the major washing and dry ablution: intending the washing for sexual impurity when it came from a wet dream is not harmful for the Shafiis and Hanafis; a menstruating woman intending only the major impurity raises a Maliki dispute. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7662}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7663} The entry continues in the following pages. On dry ablution: the Malikis and Ibn Maslama permit it, the Shafiis accept a mistaken intention for the minor state when one is in the major, and the Hanbalis require the purpose to be specified. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7664} One who had water in his baggage, forgot it, searched, found none, dry-ablutes and prays: valid for the Malikis, one Shafii view and the Hanbalis; the other Shafii view requires repetition for his neglect of the baggage. One who knew of the water but thought it was used up must repeat, by the Hanafis, Malikis, the sounder Shafii view and the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7664}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7665} In prayer intention: where the thing need not be specified, error does no harm, as with the place, the time and the number of units; where it must be specified, error harms, as with a noon prayer intended as an afternoon one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7665} On following an imam: error about his identity usually does no harm; intending to pray over one person and finding another invalidates the funeral prayer by most schools, and the Hanbalis differ on the sex of the dead. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7665}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7666} On ada' and qada' and the wrong day: the Malikis generally validate; the Hanafis and Shafiis invalidate except in certain cases; the Hanbalis validate qada' with a mistaken ada' intention when the error shows against belief. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7666}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7667} On prayer before the time: invalid by agreement, whether by design or error. The Shafiis require certain knowledge or a valid estimate; the Hanbalis accept a strong estimate, and repeat if the prayer proves early. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7667}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7668} On error in the qibla: the Hanafis accept ijtihad without repetition; the Malikis repeat within the time if clear signs were present and one faced the wrong way; the Shafiis have two views once error is certain; the Hanbalis require no repetition for the ijtihad, but the sighted at home and the blind without a guide repeat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7668} On error in recitation: the Hanafis distinguish errors of inflection, which do not invalidate unless they change the meaning badly, from errors of letters and words, and treat near-synonyms as harmless. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7668}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7669}
