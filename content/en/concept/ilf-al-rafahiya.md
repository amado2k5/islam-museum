## Summary
In his series "Al Fawzu Al Moubeen", Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy takes up what he calls habituation to luxury (ilf al-rafaha): children growing used to indulgence until it becomes second nature to them, which he criticises as a blight. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:09:30}
He mentions a United Nations statistic on celibacy and holds that true poverty is poverty of the heart, not poverty of wealth. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:10:00}
He then draws a comparison between a couple in a wealthy country and a couple in a country under pressure; the comparison carries a polemical charge whose wording is not reproduced here. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:10:30}

## The speaker's view on habituation to luxury
Sheikh Omar criticises what he calls habituation to luxury, the habit by which children grow accustomed to ease until it becomes their nature, and he regards it as a blight. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:09:30}
He holds that the righteous loved the created world as a field for worshipping God, and that this brought them close to success. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:09:30}

## A statistic on celibacy in wealthy countries
The Sheikh cites a United Nations statistic on celibacy and late marriage, and says its highest rates are in the wealthy countries of the Muslim world (needs_source: un-statistic-and-percent). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:10:00}
He gives a figure relating to Palestine (needs_source: un-statistic-and-percent). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:10:00}

## The speaker's view on true poverty
The Sheikh holds that true poverty is poverty of the heart, not poverty of wealth. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:10:00}

## The speaker's view on the comparison of women
The Sheikh compares a couple in a wealthy country with a couple in a country under pressure. He describes women in particular societies in a demeaning way, and attributes to men there a particular stance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:10:30}
This is a polemical passage in the speaker's discourse. It is recorded here as his view; the museum does not adopt it, and its wording is not quoted.
