## Definition
In the lexicon, ihram means to proclaim the hajj or the umra and to enter the state of sanctity. In jurists' usage it means, when unqualified, entering the hajj or the umra; the term is also used for entering prayer, as in «the takbir of ihram». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1777}

## Definitions by school
For the Hanafis it is entering certain prohibitions, valid only with intention together with a mention of God or the distinguishing act of driving the sacrificial animal. For the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis it is the intention to enter the prohibitions of hajj or umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1778} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1779}

## Ruling: pillar or condition
All scholars agree that ihram is an obligation of the rite. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis count it a pillar; the Hanafis count it a condition in one respect and a pillar in another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1778} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1779}

## Conditions and the talbiya
Its validity requires Islam and intention; the Hanafis add the talbiya or what stands in for it. The talbiya is the words «here I am, O God, here I am». It is a condition for Abu Hanifa and Muhammad, an obligation for the Malikis, and a sunna for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1779} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1781} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1782}

## What stands in for the talbiya
For the Hanafis, any speech that glorifies God stands in for it, and so does garlanding and driving the sacrificial animal with the intention to go along. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1783}

## Indefinite ihram and naming the rite
Entering ihram without naming the rite is valid by agreement, and it binds the person to all its rules. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis require the rite to be named before any act; the Hanafis and Malikis judge by the first act performed, circling the House or standing at Arafat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1783} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1784}

## Copying another's ihram and stipulation
Entering ihram in the same way as another person is valid, and according to the majority it takes the form of that person's ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1784} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1785} A stipulation made at the start («if a hinderer holds me back, my release is where you hold me») is valid for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis and allows release when hindered; the Hanafis and Malikis hold it invalid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1785}

## Joining one ihram to another
Joining the hajj to the umra takes several forms, depending on the timing and the status of the pilgrim. For the Hanafis, qiran is disliked for a Meccan; if he does it, it is lawful but he owes a make-up sacrifice. For a man from afar, joining the hajj to the umra before circling for it makes him a qarin; for a Meccan, the umra is abandoned and he owes a sacrifice for abandoning it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1786} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1787}
The Malikis and Hanbalis allow joining the hajj to an umra already begun, making the pilgrim a qarin; the Shafi'is forbid it once circling has begun and allow it before. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1787} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1788}
Adding an umra to a hajj already begun is not valid for the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, so no qiran results; the Hanafis and Malikis dislike it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1789}

## Two hajjs or two umras at once
If a pilgrim enters ihram for two hajjs or two umras, the majority hold that it takes effect with one and the other is void; Abu Hanifa holds that both take effect and one must be made up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1789}

## Forms of ihram: ifrad, qiran and tamattu'
Ihram divides by what the pilgrim intends to perform: ifrad, which is intending the hajj alone or the umra alone; qiran, which joins the two; and tamattu', which performs the umra first and the hajj after it in the same season. Each school defines these differently. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1790} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1791}

## Obligations of ihram
The obligations of ihram come down to two: that the ihram begins at the miqat, and that it is guarded against the prohibited acts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1790}

## Places and times of ihram (miqat)
A miqat is, in the lexicon, a set time or place; in law, the set places and times for a specific rite. Ihram has a time miqat and a place miqat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1790} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1791}

## Time miqat for the hajj
The three imams and Ahmad fix the time of ihram for the hajj as Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and the first ten days of Dhu al-Hijja; for Malik it runs to the end of Dhu al-Hijja. Part of this span is the time for beginning ihram, and part is the time for release. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1791} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1792}
The Hanafis and Hanbalis count the day of sacrifice among the months of hajj; the Shafi'is count only its night. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1792}
The Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis allow ihram for the hajj before its months, with dislike; the Shafi'is hold that it does not make a hajj and so is an umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1792} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1793}

## Time miqat for the umra
The umra may be performed at any time in the year, and it is recommended in Ramadan. The Hanafis hold it disliked as forbidden on the day of Arafa and four days after; for the Malikis, a pilgrim already in hajj cannot begin an umra until he has finished the hajj rites. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1793} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1794}

## Place miqat
People stand in four categories with regard to the places: the one from afar, whose home lies outside the miqat zone; the one of the miqat; the one of the sanctuary; and the Meccan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1794}
The places fixed for those coming from afar are agreed: Dhu al-Hulayfa for the people of Madina and those who pass it, al-Juhfa for Syria, Egypt and the Maghrib, and Qarn al-Manazil for the people of Najd. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1794}
