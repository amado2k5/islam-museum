## Definition
In the lexicon, ihram means to proclaim the hajj or the umra and to enter the state of sanctity. In jurists' usage it means, when unqualified, entering the hajj or the umra; the term is also used for entering prayer, as in «the takbir of ihram». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1777}

## Definitions by school
For the Hanafis it is entering certain prohibitions, valid only with intention together with a mention of God or the distinguishing act of driving the sacrificial animal. For the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis it is the intention to enter the prohibitions of hajj or umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1778} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1779}

## Ruling: pillar or condition
All scholars agree that ihram is an obligation of the rite. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis count it a pillar; the Hanafis count it a condition in one respect and a pillar in another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1778} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1779}

## Conditions and the talbiya
Its validity requires Islam and intention; the Hanafis add the talbiya or what stands in for it. The talbiya is the words «here I am, O God, here I am». It is a condition for Abu Hanifa and Muhammad, an obligation for the Malikis, and a sunna for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1779} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1781} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1782}

## What stands in for the talbiya
For the Hanafis, any speech that glorifies God stands in for it, and so does garlanding and driving the sacrificial animal with the intention to go along. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1783}

## Indefinite ihram and naming the rite
Entering ihram without naming the rite is valid by agreement, and it binds the person to all its rules. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis require the rite to be named before any act; the Hanafis and Malikis judge by the first act performed, circling the House or standing at Arafat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1783} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1784}

## Copying another's ihram and stipulation
Entering ihram in the same way as another person is valid, and according to the majority it takes the form of that person's ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1784} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1785} A stipulation made at the start («if a hinderer holds me back, my release is where you hold me») is valid for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis and allows release when hindered; the Hanafis and Malikis hold it invalid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1785}

## Joining one ihram to another
Joining the hajj to the umra takes several forms, depending on the timing and the status of the pilgrim. For the Hanafis, qiran is disliked for a Meccan; if he does it, it is lawful but he owes a make-up sacrifice. For a man from afar, joining the hajj to the umra before circling for it makes him a qarin; for a Meccan, the umra is abandoned and he owes a sacrifice for abandoning it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1786} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1787}
The Malikis and Hanbalis allow joining the hajj to an umra already begun, making the pilgrim a qarin; the Shafi'is forbid it once circling has begun and allow it before. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1787} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1788}
Adding an umra to a hajj already begun is not valid for the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, so no qiran results; the Hanafis and Malikis dislike it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1789}

## Two hajjs or two umras at once
If a pilgrim enters ihram for two hajjs or two umras, the majority hold that it takes effect with one and the other is void; Abu Hanifa holds that both take effect and one must be made up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1789}

## Forms of ihram: ifrad, qiran and tamattu'
Ihram divides by what the pilgrim intends to perform: ifrad, which is intending the hajj alone or the umra alone; qiran, which joins the two; and tamattu', which performs the umra first and the hajj after it in the same season. Each school defines these differently. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1790} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1791}

## Obligations of ihram
The obligations of ihram come down to two: that the ihram begins at the miqat, and that it is guarded against the prohibited acts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1790}

## Places and times of ihram (miqat)
A miqat is, in the lexicon, a set time or place; in law, the set places and times for a specific rite. Ihram has a time miqat and a place miqat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1790} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1791}

## Time miqat for the hajj
The three imams and Ahmad fix the time of ihram for the hajj as Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and the first ten days of Dhu al-Hijja; for Malik it runs to the end of Dhu al-Hijja. Part of this span is the time for beginning ihram, and part is the time for release. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1791} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1792}
The Hanafis and Hanbalis count the day of sacrifice among the months of hajj; the Shafi'is count only its night. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1792}
The Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis allow ihram for the hajj before its months, with dislike; the Shafi'is hold that it does not make a hajj and so is an umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1792} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1793}

## Time miqat for the umra
The umra may be performed at any time in the year, and it is recommended in Ramadan. The Hanafis hold it disliked as forbidden on the day of Arafa and four days after; for the Malikis, a pilgrim already in hajj cannot begin an umra until he has finished the hajj rites. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1793} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1794}

## Place miqat
People stand in four categories with regard to the places: the one from afar, whose home lies outside the miqat zone; the one of the miqat; the one of the sanctuary; and the Meccan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1794}
The places fixed for those coming from afar are agreed: Dhu al-Hulayfa for the people of Madina and those who pass it, al-Juhfa for Syria, Egypt and the Maghrib, and Qarn al-Manazil for the people of Najd. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1794}

## Rules on the miqats
The miqat for the people of Yemen is Yalamlam, and for Iraq and the rest of the East it is Dhat Irq; the miqats are fixed by the sunna and by consensus. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1795} Dhat Irq is disputed: al-Shafi'i and Malik hold that it was fixed by Umar's judgment, which the Companions accepted and so became consensus; the Hanafis, Hanbalis and most Shafi'is hold it was fixed by text. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1795} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1796}
Whoever passes a miqat intending one of the rites must enter ihram there; delaying it past the miqat is forbidden by agreement, and beginning at its far edge is best. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1796}
The place counts, not the village's name; entering ihram at the miqat or at its alignment suffices, and Umar's alignment for Dhat Irq made alignment the agreed basis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1796} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1797}
For someone passing two miqats, the Shafi'is and Hanbalis require ihram at the farther one; the Malikis allow the nearer; the Hanafis allow either and dislike delay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1797}
Entering ihram before the place miqat is lawful by agreement; the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis dislike it, and the Hanafis consider it better when the pilgrim is safe from breaking the rules. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1797} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1798}
Whoever passes the miqat without ihram intending a rite is sinful and must return and enter ihram there; if he does not return he owes a sacrifice, but one who does not return for an excuse such as fear of missing Arafa or severe sickness is not sinful. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1798}
The resident of the miqat zone takes his place as the miqat according to the Malikis, his village or encampment according to the Shafi'is and Hanbalis, and the whole zone according to the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1798}

## Miqat of the people of Mecca and the sanctuary
All schools agree that whoever lives in Mecca or the sanctuary enters ihram for the hajj from where he is, following the rule «whoever is nearer, from where he began». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1799} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1800}
In detail, the Hanafis require the resident of the sanctuary to enter ihram from the sanctuary, the mosque being better; the Malikis distinguish those who begin with hajj from those who begin with qiran. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1799} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1800}

## Umra miqat for those in Mecca
The umra miqat for those in Mecca is the lawful zone outside the sanctuary from any point. The majority prefer al-Ji'ranah; the Hanafis prefer al-Tan'im. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1800}

## Prohibitions of ihram
Some things lawful at other times are forbidden in ihram, to remind the pilgrim of his rite and to train him in simplicity and equality. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1800}
The core rule for a man's dress is that he may not cover all or part of the body with stitched or fitted cloth: no shirt, turban, trousers, hooded cloak or socks, and no garment touched by saffron or wars. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1800} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1801}
A qaba (a long coat) or similar: the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis forbid placing it on the shoulders; the Hanafis allow it without sewing the sleeves on. A man with no waist-wrap may wear trousers, with a sacrifice owed according to the Shafi'is and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1801} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1802}
A man with no sandals cuts the socks below the ankles and wears them, according to the majority; Ahmad in the accepted view differs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1802}

## Carrying a sword; covering the head
The Malikis and Hanbalis forbid wearing a sword without need; the Hanafis and Shafi'is allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1803}
Covering the head, or part of it, is forbidden by agreement; the Hanafis and Hanbalis define covering as what is meant as a cover by custom, and the Malikis and Shafi'is as what counts as a covering. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1803} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1804}
Placing a hand on the head is forbidden to the Malikis but not to the Hanafis or Hanbalis. Carrying something that is not a cover is not forbidden for the Hanafis unless it is meant as a cover. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1803} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1804}

## Shade
Shade from something fixed to a base, such as a tent roof, a house or a roofed car, is lawful by agreement; shade from something not fixed is lawful for the Hanafis, Shafi'is and in one Hanbali view, and forbidden by the Malikis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1804} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1805}

## Face covering and gloves
A man may not cover his face; this is the majority view, and the Shafi'is in the sounder view allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1805}
Gloves are forbidden to men by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1805} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1806}
A woman in ihram may not cover her face with a niqab, by agreement; the exception is need or fear of temptation, when she may let a cloth fall from above without it touching the face. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1806}
A woman in ihram may not wear gloves according to the Malikis and Hanbalis and the accepted Shafi'i view; the Hanafis allow covering the hands. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1806}

## Prohibitions on the pilgrim's body
The rule covers anything that perfumes the body, removes its untidiness, or ends the state of ihram's disorder. The root is the Qur'anic command in the chapter of the Cow (al-Baqara, aya 196); the verse is not quoted here. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1807}
These include shaving the head, removing hair from the body, cutting nails, oiling and scent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1807}

## Shaving and removing hair
The pilgrim may not shave his head or another's; trimming is the same, and so is removing a little hair, by plucking, burning or depilatory. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1807} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1808}
Shaving a lawful person is disputed: the Hanafis and one Maliki view forbid it, while another Maliki view and the Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1808}
Cutting nails is forbidden by agreement, and removing hair from the rest of the body is forbidden by analogy with the head. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1808}

## Oiling
The majority forbid oil even when unscented, because it adds comfort and adornment; the Shafi'is limit the ban to the head and beard, and the accepted Hanbali view allows it on the whole body, since a sacrifice needs a proof. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1808} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1809}

## Scent
Scent is defined differently by each school: for the Hanafis, what has a pleasant smell from which perfume is made; for the Shafi'is, what is meant mainly for its smell; for the Hanbalis, what is scented and used for smelling; the Malikis divide it into masculine and feminine kinds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1809} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1810}
The pilgrim may not use scent on his waist-wrap, cloak, bedding or sandals, nor perfume his body even for treatment, nor drink or eat pure scent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1810}
If scent is mixed into food, the ruling depends on whether it was cooked, how much scent there is, and whether its smell remains. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1810} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1811}
