## Nature
A herb that grows among the wheat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Temperament
It may be placed at the beginning of the first degree of heating and at the end of the second of drying {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Actions and properties
Subtle, cleansing and dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Cosmetics
It is smeared on bahaq with sulphur and benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Swellings and pustules
With flaxseed it dissolves swellings and scrofula, and with pigeon dung and flaxseed it bursts them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Wounds and ulcers
What grows of it with the wheat is smeared on ulcers and sprinkled on them and benefits, it is smeared on ringworm, and it is at times laid as a poultice on wounds with radish peel and benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Joints
It is cooked in the water of qaratin and laid as a poultice on sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Organs of the head
It intoxicates and dazes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that when used as a fumigation it helps conception, especially with barley meal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.672}
