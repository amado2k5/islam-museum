## Summary
In Shu'ayb's prayer in [[passage/quran-7-89|Al-A'raf 89]], he asks God to "open" between his people and himself in truth. [[person/rashid-rida|Rida]] holds that the opening here means judgement and the deciding between two parties, and that every opening between two parties carries the sense of judgement, from which victory derives. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.9}

## The report of Ibn Abbas
Rida relates a report from [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] that he did not understand the meaning of this prayer until he heard the daughter of Dhu Yazan say to her husband, "Come, let me open [a case] against you", and a Bedouin woman say to her husband, "Between me and you is the one who opens". Early exegetes, including Ibn al-Anbari and al-Bayhaqi, transmitted the report and understood "opening" as settling a dispute at law. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.9}

## What the language shows
Rida holds that the report shows linguistically that this sense of "opening" as judging is not Qurashi, and he mentions al-Suddi's view that it is Yemeni, while some limited it to Himyari. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.9}

## Opening in the Qur'an
Rida gives examples of "opening" in the sense of judgement in the Qur'an, including a verse in Surah Saba and Noah's prayer in Surah al-Shu'ara', and holds that what Shu'ayb meant by his prayer is exactly what Noah meant. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.9}
