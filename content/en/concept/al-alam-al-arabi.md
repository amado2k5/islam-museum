## Summary
This page sets out what the Encyclopaedia Britannica says of the League of Arab States and of the list of states where [[language/arabic|Arabic]] is official as it appears in the article on the Semitic languages. It ascribes every figure and date to its page, and it notes that the number of members that appears here is the sum of the names printed.

## The founding of the League
The encyclopedia says the Arab League, whose Arabic name it gives as Jami'at al-Duwal al-'Arabiyya, is a regional organization of Arab states in the Middle East and parts of Africa, formed in Cairo on 22 March 1945 as an outgrowth of Pan-Arabism. The founding states were Egypt, Syria, Lebanon, Iraq, Transjordan, now Jordan, Saudi Arabia and Yemen. It gives the aims of 1945 as strengthening and coordinating political, cultural, economic and social programmes and mediating disputes {cite:britannica-arab-league}. For the Arabs themselves see [[group/arabs|the Arabs]]; among the founders are [[place/saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], [[place/iraq|Iraq]] and [[place/syria|Syria]].

## Later members and suspension
The encyclopedia lists the later members with their years: Libya 1953; Sudan 1956; Tunisia and [[place/morocco|Morocco]] 1958; Kuwait 1961; Algeria 1962; Bahrain, Oman, Qatar and the [[place/united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] 1971; Mauritania 1973; Somalia 1974; the Palestine Liberation Organization 1976; Djibouti 1977; and Comoros 1993. It notes that when Yemen was divided, between 1967 and 1990, the two regimes were separately represented, and that the Palestine Liberation Organization was an observer in 1964 and a full member in 1976. It says Syria's participation was suspended in 2011 and reinstated in 2023, and that Libya's participation was suspended in 2011 {cite:britannica-arab-league}. The page names seven founding members and fifteen later ones, so the sum of the printed names is twenty-two; that addition is this page's own, and the encyclopedia does not print the number in what was read of it.

## Voting and agreements
Each member has one vote on the League Council, and decisions bind only the states that voted for them. The encyclopedia mentions an agreement on joint defence and economic cooperation of 13 April 1950, and the founding of the Arab League Educational, Cultural and Scientific Organization, known as ALECSO, in 1964 {cite:britannica-arab-league}.

## The states where Arabic is official
In its article on the Semitic languages the encyclopedia lists where Arabic is official, with its qualifications: Algeria with Tamazight; Bahrain; Chad with French; Djibouti with French; Egypt; Iraq with Kurdish; Jordan; Kuwait; Lebanon; Libya; Mauritania, where Fula, Soninke and Wolof are national languages; Morocco; Oman; the Palestinian Authority; Qatar; Saudi Arabia; Somalia with Somali; Sudan with English; Syria; Tunisia; the United Arab Emirates; and Yemen {cite:britannica-semitic-languages}. The two lists do not coincide: the language list has Chad, which the League article does not, and the League article has Comoros, which the language list does not; the two pages do not explain the difference.

## What this page does not reach
The League's charter and documents were not read, only the two encyclopedia articles, which are tertiary references; the League article shows no revision date. Membership developments after what the article states were not read, nor what particular states say of their official language in their constitutions. The museum does not estimate the number of Arabic speakers in these states; it gives the pages' figures in [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-al-alam-al-yawm|Arabic at the United Nations and UNESCO, and the speaker figures]].
