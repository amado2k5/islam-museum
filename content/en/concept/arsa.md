## Summary
In the lexicon, arsa is the courtyard of a house, an open wide space with no building on it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11549}. Jurists use it in a narrow sense, the open yard between houses, and a broad sense, any plot of land whether or not it lies between houses {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11549}.

## Related terms
Hareem is what surrounds a thing and serves its rights and uses; fina' is what extends from a thing and serves its purposes {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11549} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11550}.

## Sale and pre-emption
In a sale, a plot described as land, courtyard or open ground enters the sale with its building and trees {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11550}. Pre-emption over it is disputed: the Malikis do not allow it over the courtyard, while the Shafi'is and Hanbalis tie it to whether the plot can be divided {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11550} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11552}.

## Oaths and bequests
A person who swears not to enter a house that collapses into an open courtyard does not, according to the majority, breach the oath; the Hanafis distinguish between pointing to a particular house and describing it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11553}. Building on a courtyard that has been bequeathed counts as retraction for the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11553} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11554}.

## Where it is discussed
The courtyard is discussed in acknowledgement, hire, loan and partition {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11554}.
