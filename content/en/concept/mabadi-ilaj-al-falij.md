## Venesection and delay
In the five nerve diseases — numbness, convulsion, tremor, palsy and twitching — your venesection must aim at the back of the brain; do not hurry to the strong medicines at first but postpone to the fourth or the seventh day, or if the illness is strong to the fourteenth, and in this time keep to light things that soften, mature and loosen; clysters are acceptable in this time, then evacuate with the strong evacuants {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.139} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.140}

## Food, water and wine
Their food regimen: confine the palsied man at first onset to the like of barley-water and honey-water for two or three days; if the strength bears it, to the fourteenth; if not, feed him the light meats of birds, strive to keep him hungry on dry foods, then make him thirst a long thirst. Know that water is better for them than wine, for wine drives the matters into the nerves, and much of it may turn sour in their bodies into vinegar, and vinegar is the most harmful of things to the nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.140}

## Treatment by cause
What comes from a twist or compression is treated as defined in the chapter of twisting and compression; what from a fall or blow is hard to treat, yet in any case one looks whether it produced a swelling or drew matter, and each is treated as due; if the illness is the palsy arising from the nerve's relaxation, then after the shared regimen what is required is evacuation of its matter exactly as defined for evacuating the thin matters, no more and no less (the named medicines and their quantities are not reproduced) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.140}

## Caution with the strong plaster, and aftercare
If sensation is weak a strong plaster may bite without being felt and lead to severe lesion and ulceration, so beware of it and inspect the plaster's effect: if it reddens and puffs only skin-deep the effect is slight; if the reddening is lasting and the heat clear, stop. When the organ turns for the better, exercise it afterward, contracting and extending it, so full health returns; they also benefit from fever and from shouting and loud reading (the named ointments, plasters, snuffs, emetics and doses are not reproduced) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.141} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.142}
