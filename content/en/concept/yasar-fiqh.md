Yasar lexically means wealth and ease (the root of "he became well off"); its opposite is 'usr, hardship. The word also names the left side, the sister of the right. The technical sense does not depart from the lexical one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17991}

Related terms: ghina (wealth) is the wider term, and yasar and ghina stand in a general-particular relation. I'sar (straitened means) is the opposite of yasar: lacking money to spend, and technically being unable to meet maintenance or a debt from wealth or earnings; some say it is spending more than one earns. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17992}

First, yasar as wealth and ease.
(1) Seeking wealth: it is lawful to seek ease and earn by lawful means, on the verses urging the seeking of provision, and on the hadiths "every Muslim owes a charity" (Abu Musa's report) and "the best earning is a man's work with his hands" (Ibn 'Umar). The original rule is permission, and seeking wealth may be recommended or disliked. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17992} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17993}

(2) Wealth in equality (kafa'a) in marriage: the Hanafis, the school's Hanbalis and a Shafi'i view (preferred by al-Adhra'i) count it; the Malikis, the sounder Shafi'i view and a Hanbali narration do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17993}

(3) Effect on maintenance: the amount and kind of a wife's maintenance vary with the husband's means, on verses of surah al-Talaq and surah al-Baqara (verse 236). The jurists agree that a relative must maintain a relative only if he has a surplus beyond his own and his dependants' needs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17993}

(4) Effect on the expiations in order: manumitting a slave is required in expiations for zihar, for killing a believer and for breaking the fast of Ramadan by intercourse only for one who has the means: a slave, or the price of one, in excess of his and his dependants' needs for food, clothing, housing and necessary furnishings. If he lacks these, he moves to the other forms. The majority hold this. The Malikis hold that the one who makes a zihar who cannot free a slave at the time of payment fasts two months in succession, and that the one who can free a slave is not excused by need. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17994}

(5) The limit of means: for zakat it is owning a nisab beyond one's basic needs; the limit below which begging is not allowed, and the limit in kafa'a, are disputed (see the entries on begging and kafa'a). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17994}

(6) The husband's means for maintenance: the Hanafis, the Malikis and a Shafi'i view leave it to custom and the state of the person, whether he spends freely or not. The Shafi'is give several views. The school's view: the poor (the zakat "destitute") is the needy; one above him is in the middle, and one who, if asked to maintain a debtor, would be reduced to the poor is in the middle, and otherwise rich; this varies with cheapness or dearness, the family's size. A second view: the rich is one whose income exceeds his outgoings, and the needy the reverse, and the middle is equal. A third: the test is earnings. The Hanbalis: the rich is one able to maintain from wealth or earnings, the needy one who cannot from either, and the middle one who can pay part. Sahib al-Ri'aya: the zakat poor is needy, one above is middling, and otherwise rich. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17994} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17995}

(7) The limit of means for relatives' maintenance is disputed; see maintenance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17995}

The left in precedence: the rule is that what carries honour is begun with the right and what is its opposite with the left. The left foot enters a privy first and leaves it last; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis say the mention of the privy is only the usual case, and the same holds in the open field, on the analogy of a verse in surah al-Nisa on stepchildren. Cleaning after relief is with the left hand, on the report of Salman that the Prophet forbade cleaning with the right hand. The left is delayed where honour is meant: leaving the mosque, wudu', dressing, and the acts of the fitra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17996} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17997}

The limit of means for sacrifice and blood-money: the jurists differ on whether wealth is a condition of sacrifice and of the blood-money share of the 'aqila; see the entries on wealth and sacrifice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17996}
