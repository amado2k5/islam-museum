## Overview
In the chapter on the Islamic union, Shakib Arslan presents a plan he attributes to the Sanusi order in North Africa. This is what the author reports; it has not been checked here against its text, and the plan is not established in the sources of the Sanusiyya. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

## The stages of the plan as presented
Preparing the means for religious and moral reform; then opening the African lands; then the rest of the Muslim lands; and making the Islamic world one kingdom under one caliph. He notes that al-Sanusi held that political liberation from Western domination must be preceded by spiritual renewal and the refinement of the followers' morals. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

He mentions that the Ottoman government in Tripoli feared the influence of the order, so the sheikh moved it to Jaghbub. He mentions that the Sanusis' stance toward the Mahdist revolt in Sudan was refusal. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

A further source is needed: the writings of the Sanusiyya and studies of the order, and the report of the refusal to support the Mahdiyya rests on a single account.
