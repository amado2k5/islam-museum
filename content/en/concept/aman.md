## Definition
Aman in the lexical sense is the absence of an expected harm; its root is the calm of the soul and the lifting of fear. It names both a state and the instrument that grants it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3372}
In the technical sense it is the lifting of the lawful killing, enslavement and seizure of the property of an enemy in war, while he remains under Islamic rule. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3372}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3373}

## Related terms
- Truce (hudna): a contract with enemies to stop fighting for a time, for a price or without one; only the imam or his deputy may conclude it, while aman may be given by an individual Muslim. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3373}
- Poll-tax (jizya): protects lives, property and honour; only the imam may impose it; it is permanent and cannot be cancelled, while aman is a binding-less contract that can be annulled under conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3373}

## Ruling
The default is that giving or asking for aman is permitted; it is forbidden or disliked where it leads to harm or to neglect of a duty. Its effect is that the enemy is safe from killing, enslavement and seizure, and Muslims may not kill their men or enslave their women and children. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3373}

## How aman is given
Aman is made by any clear or implied word in any language, by writing, by a message, or by a sign that conveys it, since it is an intention that the giver shows in speech, writing or sign. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3373}

## Conditions
- The Malikis, Hanbalis and most Shafi'is require that no harm follow; al-Balqini adds that aman given by anyone but the imam needs a public interest; the Hanafis require an evident public interest, such as aman given when the Muslims are weak and the enemy strong. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3373}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3374}
- The giver: the imam may grant aman to all unbelievers, and an individual Muslim's aman is valid for a limited number according to most jurists; the Hanafis hold that one person may grant it to any number. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3374}
- Conditions of the giver: Islam, so an unbeliever's aman is not valid even if he fights with the Muslims; sanity, so a madman or a child who cannot think is excluded; adulthood, required by most jurists against al-Shaybani; and freedom from the enemy's control. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3374}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3375}
- Maleness is not required by most jurists, so a woman may give aman; Ibn al-Majishun of the Malikis disagreed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3375}

## Related places
The rules of aman are treated in the chapters on the conduct of war and jihad, and in the entry on the one who holds aman (mustamin). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3375}
