## Causes of the defect
Some of what must be said of it was said in the chapter of the tongue's relaxation; now we say: muteness and the other speech disorders may come from a disorder in the brain, the nerve-branch itself, or in the muscles themselves — that defect being convulsion, extension, hardening, relaxation, a short ligament, contraction after a healed wound, or a hard tumour. It is mostly from moisture, as you know, though it may be from dryness; and the disorder in speech may come from tumours and ulcers occurring in the tongue and its neighbourhood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.259}
## The accidental kinds and the larynx cause
It may occur after sirsam by the muscles' being driven from the brain into the nerves, and in the hot fevers from their intense drying — the tongue then wasted and convulsed, which is rare; these are among the accidental not-original disorders. The speech disorder may also be from a cause in the larynx's muscles when extended or relaxed: a man may find utterance hard at first because he strains his chest and larynx muscles in a forcing those muscles cannot bear until they rebel; if he dries at the speech's beginning he flows on afterwards. Such a man should not prepare for speech with a great breath and great moving of the chest but begin gently, for when he grows used to that, speech comes easy. The other ways had their treatments in their chapters; what comes after sirsam may greatly profit by bleeding the two veins beneath the tongue (the venesection site carried as the book names it) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.259} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.260}
