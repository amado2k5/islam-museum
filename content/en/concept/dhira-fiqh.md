## Summary
In lexicon dhira' is the arm from the elbow to the fingertips; the measuring cubit is also called dhira'. The jurists use both senses. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8413}
The majority require washing the arms up to the elbow in ablution, and a worshipper is disliked to rest his forearms on the ground in prostration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8414} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8415}

## Injury to the arm
Cutting the arm at the joint is retaliated for if deliberate and carries half a diya if accidental. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8415}
Breaking it short of the joint: the Hanafis, Shafi'is and some Hanbalis set a discretionary sum, while the Hanbalis set four camels for a broken forearm bone. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8415} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8416}

## The cubit as a measure
The jurists measure abundant and scant water by the cubit, fix the distance of a journey, and reckon the jarib, the qasaba and the qafiz by it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8416}
