## Nature and selection
A stone of several kinds, golden, silver, copper and iron, each kind resembling in colour the substance it is named for. The Persians call it the stone of roshna, the stone of light, for its benefit to sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Temperament
Hot in the second degree and dry in the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Actions and properties
It is astringent, warming, ripening, dissolving and cleansing; its force is strong, but unless it is ground fine its benefit does not show {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Cosmetic uses
Painted with vinegar on leukoderma, vitiligo and freckles; it dissolves humours held under the skin, and thins and curls the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Swellings and pustules
Mixed with ritiyanaj it helps hard swellings and dissolves them, and it enters dissolving ointments because of its ripening and dissolving force {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Wounds and ulcers
With ritiyanaj it knits ulcers, and with arsenic it removes excess flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Joints
It dissolves the pus-like matter that gathers in the parts of the muscle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Head, ears and teeth
It is said that when hung on a child's neck the child is not frightened {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.

## Eyes
It cleanses and strengthens the eye, burnt or unburnt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.563}.
