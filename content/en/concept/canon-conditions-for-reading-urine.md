## Summary
Ibn Sina requires that the urine taken for reading be the first of the morning, not held long, preceded by no drink, food or dye, and that it be taken whole in a wide flask and left to settle away from sun and wind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.183-184}.

## Conditions on the person
The urine should be the first that the person passes on rising, not held back for a long time and kept through the night; the person should not have drunk water or eaten food, nor taken a dye in food or drink, nor have done movements and tasks, nor have taken what drives out a humour, as one that drives out yellow bile or phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.183}.

## The six-hour rule, collecting and settling
So it is said that urine should not be looked at after six hours, for its signs weaken, its colour changes and its sediment dissolves and changes or thickens more; Ibn Sina adds that not even after an hour. The urine should be taken whole in a wide flask with none poured away, and judged not as it is passed but after it has settled in the flask, where neither sun nor wind reaches it to stir or freeze it, until the sediment separates and the reading is complete; it does not sink as it is passed, nor is it fully ripened, and it should not be passed into a flask not washed after the first urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.183-184}.
