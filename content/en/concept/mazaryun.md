## Nature and selection
A large euphorbia of two kinds: one with large thin leaves and the other with small thick leaves, which is the worse; the black is deadly. The best has many leaves like olive leaves and is finer, while the small curly-leaved one is bad, and its harm can be broken by dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the fourth degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Actions and properties
It cleanses, purifies and peels, and its pungency is severe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Cosmetic uses
All its kinds are used as an external paint for vitiligo, leukoderma and freckles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Wounds and ulcers
All its kinds are used with honey for ringworm and dirty ulcers, lifting off scabs because of its salty corrosive substance, and it dries scab {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Head, ears and teeth
A rinse of its decoction, especially of the black, calms toothache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Mazaryun harms the liver greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Excretory organs
It loosens water, especially when taken fresh at its flowering. It expels worms and gourd-seed worms, and its decoction helps severe difficulty of urine. Some said it also loosens black bile and phlegmatic humours. The weights and soaking preparations the book gives are not copied here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book also lists a soaked preparation of it, drunk for dropsy and for cleansing after childbirth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.

## Poisons and bites
Given with wine for bites of vermin; but it, especially the black, is deadly: mixed with barley flour and bound with water and oil it kills mice, dogs and pigs, and a small amount is fatal to people, killing by distress, vomiting and diarrhoea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555}.
