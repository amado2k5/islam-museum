## Overview
When the contradiction comes from a weak narrator, the stronger report is called the recognised (*ma'ruf*) and its counterpart the objectionable (*munkar*): so [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] defines it in [[work/nuzhat-al-nazar|*Nuzhat al-Nazar*]]. The editor 'Itr sums it up: the recognised is what a strong narrator transmits in contradiction of a weak one, and the objectionable what a weak narrator transmits in contradiction of a strong one. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 67}

## The example
Ibn Hajar's example is the report given by Ibn Abi Hatim through Hubayyib ibn Habib, brother of the Qur'an reciter Hamza ibn Habib al-Zayyat, from Abu Ishaq, from al-'Ayzar ibn Hurayth, from [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn 'Abbas]], from the Prophet: "Whoever keeps up the prayer, gives the alms, makes the pilgrimage, fasts and honours the guest will enter Paradise." Abu Hatim said: it is objectionable, because trustworthy narrators transmitted it from Abu Ishaq as Ibn 'Abbas's own words, and that is the recognised version. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 67-68} The editor adds that Hubayyib was very weak; Abu Zur'a considered him feeble and Ibn al-Mubarak abandoned him. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 68}

## The early usage
The editor notes that many early scholars applied the word *munkar* to any solitary report, even when its narrator was trustworthy. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 67}

## Objectionable and irregular
The objectionable and the [[concept/al-hadith-al-shadhdh|irregular]] report both require contradiction, but the irregular is the report of a trustworthy or truthful narrator and the objectionable that of a weak one. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 68}
