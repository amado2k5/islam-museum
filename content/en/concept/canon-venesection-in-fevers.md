## Summary
Ibn Sina sets out when bleeding is avoided in fevers and when it is allowed: he bars it in intensely inflaming fevers and at the start of non-acute ones, puts first in putrid fevers a look at the ten rules, the urine flask, the pulse and the physique, gives Galen's view that it is allowed even after the fourth day, and counts the sanguine fever as needing bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.291-292}.

## When it is avoided
Bleeding should be avoided in intensely inflaming fevers, and in all non-acute fevers at their start and on the days of the cycle, and it is lessened in fevers accompanied by convulsion even if the need for bleeding is present, because convulsion, once it comes, brings sleeplessness and much sweat and drops the strength, so a reserve of blood must remain for that. Likewise one bled for a fever not from putrefaction should be bled little, so that a reserve remains for the dissolving of the fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.

## Putrid fever not intensely inflaming
If the fever is not intensely inflaming and is putrid, look to the ten rules, then examine the urine flask: if the water is thick tending to red, the pulse great, the physique swollen, and the fever not hastening in its movement, bleed at a time when the stomach is empty of food. But if the water is thin or fiery, or the physique has been wasted since the start of the illness, beware of bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.

## Remissions and the rigor
If the fever has remissions, the bleeding should be in them, and the state of the rigor is considered: if the rigor is strong beware of bleeding. Watch the colour of the blood that comes out; if it is thin tending to white, stop at once, and guard in general against bringing on the patient one of two things, the stirring of the bilious humours or the stirring of the cold humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.291-292}.

## After the fourth day
When bleeding is due in a fever, no heed is paid to what is said, that there is no way to it after the fourth day; there is a way to it if it is due, even after forty. This is the view of Galen, though earlier and quicker is better when the signs are sound; if that was missed, at whatever time you find it due, bleed after heeding the ten matters {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## The sanguine fever
As for the sanguine fever, it must be evacuated by bleeding, not excessive at the start and excessive at ripening, and it often broke off in the course of the bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.
