## Overview
Wa'd: burying a female infant alive from fear of poverty or disgrace; the foulest practice of the Jahiliyya, which the Qur'an abolished and magnified as a sin.

## The story
Among the foulest and ugliest customs of the Jahiliyya people was wa'd: burying the daughter alive, whether from fear of poverty or dread that a tale of disgrace might come of her {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## In the Qur'an
When Islam came, the buried infant was asked for what sin she was killed: "And when the buried infant is asked for what sin she was killed" ([[passage/quran-81-8|al-Takwir 8–9]]); He forbade killing children from fear of poverty: "Do not kill your children for fear of want" ([[passage/quran-17-31|al-Isra' 31]]); and made killing them loss and straying from the religion: "Lost are those who killed their children in folly" ([[passage/quran-6-140|al-An'am 140]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
Some people long ago buried their little daughters alive from fear of poverty; Islam came forbidding that and making it among the greatest sins.
