## Meaning
Idhan (anointing) in the lexicon is smearing with oil or other grease; the wider term is ittila', which covers smearing with any substance, such as depilatory paste. The jurists keep to this lexical sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2001}
## General ruling
Anointing a person with perfume or other substances that contain no impurity is in general recommended, since it is a seemly adornment for a Muslim and one of the adornments that God has permitted. Reports urge it, including a saying about anointing at intervals (one anoints, lets the oil dry, and anoints again; some say every other day); al-Nawawi judged that saying weak and Ibn al-Salah found no source for it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2001}
It is especially recommended for the Friday prayer, the festival prayers and gatherings. The rule covers men, boys and slaves alike, but not women, who may not perfume or anoint themselves when they come to the Friday prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2001}
Some cases make anointing forbidden or disliked: the state of pilgrim ihram for hajj or umra, seclusion (i'tikaf), fasting, and a widow's mourning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2001}
## Anointing objects
Anointing things other than people, such as a rope, a wheel, a ship or a sandal, is permitted when they contain no impurity. For anointing with an impure substance, the jurists differ according to whether using an impure thing is permitted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2002}
## Where the topic is discussed
Anointing has many rulings, worked out in their own chapters: anointing by one in ihram in the chapter on hajj, by one in seclusion in the chapter on i'tikaf, by a fasting person in the chapter on fasting, by a mourning widow in the chapter on the waiting period, and anointing with an impure substance in the chapter on purity and impurity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2002}
