## Ease of falling in and coming out
In sum, boys fall easily into convulsion for the weakness of the powers of their brains and nerves and the weakness of their muscles, and they come out of it easily for the strength of their livers' and hearts' powers and because their humours are not defiant and very gross; therefore they recover quickly from the dry convulsion for the moisture of their temperament and food, while in the grown neither of the two is easy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.143}

## When it befalls them
Of the moist convulsion is what befalls boys for their moisture; it often befalls them in their acute fevers, when their bellies are bound, and in their sleeplessness and much crying they convulse also in their fevers though these be light; yet a bad convulsion may befall boys after the acute fevers, from which they seldom escape {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.143}

## Who passes seven
Whoever passes seven years does not convulse except for a very hard fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.143}
