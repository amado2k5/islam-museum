## The right angle to the ecliptic
At the middle of the eclipse the direction of the darkness falls on a right angle to the ecliptic, where the arc through the poles of the ecliptic and the Moon's place meets the horizon. The known longitude angle for the time of mid-eclipse is found and drawn from the rising or setting point of that moment on the side opposite the Moon's latitude, depending on whether the Moon is north or south. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.156-157}

## Closing note of the passage
The text says that when the Moon is in the east and its latitude is south the angle is drawn from the setting point, and when it is in the west from the rising point. It treats all these rules as lying on the same figure. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.157}
