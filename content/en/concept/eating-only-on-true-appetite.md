## Summary
Ibn Sina says the guardian of health eats only on appetite and does not hold off a real appetite once it stirs; a false appetite, like that of the drunk or the surfeited, is no guide, and enduring hunger fills the stomach with bad, watery humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## The rule
One should eat only on appetite and should not resist it when it stirs, provided it is not a false one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## False appetite
His examples of a false appetite are that of the drunk and of one who has surfeit. Resisting a true appetite is harmful, since enduring hunger fills the stomach with bad, watery humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.
