## The three passes
The corrected hours of conjunction are reckoned from midday. The parallax in longitude is then found for that hour on the given side, the ascendant and midheaven are erected, and the first parallax is multiplied by the Moon's hourly motion to give the first hours of parallax. The operation is repeated with the corrected hours until the second and third parallaxes agree; at that point the hours are those of mid-eclipse. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.161-163}

## The rules for adding and subtracting
The text fixes the additions and subtractions in small parts of an hour, a sixth, an eighth or a tenth as the case allows, and reverses the sign when the distance from the ascendant exceeds ninety degrees. The additions are never allowed to make a full hour. These are rounding rules, and the text refers to the Taun tables for their use. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.162-163}
