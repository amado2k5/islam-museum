## Summary
Al-Battani makes plain that the year he obtained by observation is less than the time Ptolemy gave by two parts and a fifth of a part, so that the Sun's motion he found exceeds the motion Ptolemy gave, both in the day and in the Egyptian year, by amounts the text states, and he ends with the words approximately, if God wills {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.68}.

## Checking the difference
The difference agrees with the earlier reckoning: Ptolemy's year is three hundred and sixty-five days and a quarter less a part of three hundred, which in the three hundred and sixty parts of the day is about eighty-eight parts and four fifths, while al-Battani's is eighty-six parts and thirty-six minutes; the difference is two parts and a fifth, as the text says. The passage is thus an instance of what al-Battani does in [[concept/zij-sabi-battani-corrects-ptolemy-by-observation|correcting the Almagest by observation]] {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.68}.
