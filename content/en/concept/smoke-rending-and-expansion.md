## What the verses say

Three verses are usually named together when the beginning of the heaven is asked about. [[passage/quran-41-11|Fussilat 11]] says that God turned to the heaven while it was smoke and told it and the earth to come willingly or unwillingly, and both answered in obedience. [[passage/quran-21-30|al-Anbiya 30]] draws the attention of those who disbelieve to the heavens and earth having been one joined thing that was then separated, and to every living thing having been made from water. [[passage/quran-51-47|al-Dhariyat 47]] says the heaven was built with strength and that God is its expander. All of them serve as arguments for the Creator's power; the Qur'an gives no measures or times in them.

## What early commentators made of the verse on rending

In his commentary on al-Anbiya 30, al-Tabari sets out what the interpreters said about the sense of the joined state and its splitting {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Tabari on 21:30}; the views fall into four groups.

First, that heaven and earth were stuck together and God separated them with air, the view of Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan and Qatada. Al-Tabari relays from Ibn Abbas that they were joined, so God raised the heaven and set down the earth, and Sa'id ibn Jubayr said something close, as Ibn Kathir reports {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Tabari on 21:30} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 21:30}.

Second, that the heaven was a single layer and God split it into seven heavens, and likewise the earth into seven earths: the view of Mujahid, Abu Salih and al-Suddi, with Mujahid adding that heaven and earth were not touching, and with al-Suddi placing it in two days, Thursday and Friday {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Tabari on 21:30}.

Third, that the heaven gave no rain and the earth no plants, and God split the one with rain and the other with vegetation: the view of Ikrima, Atiyya and Ibn Zayd. Ibn Kathir relays that a man asked Ibn Umar about the verse and Ibn Umar sent him to Ibn Abbas, who said the heavens were closed and did not rain and the earth was closed and did not grow, and when God made inhabitants for the earth He opened the one with rain and the other with plants; when the man reported back, Ibn Umar said he now knew Ibn Abbas had been given knowledge of the Qur'an {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 21:30}. Al-Tabari preferred this view because the verse is followed by the mention of the water from which every living thing is made {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Tabari on 21:30}, and al-Qurtubi calls it the one for which reflection is realised by sight and observation {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 21:30}.

Fourth, that the splitting is the separation of night from day, since night came first. Al-Tabari relays this from Ibn Abbas, and Ibn Kathir has from Ikrima that Ibn Abbas, asked which came first, replied that when heaven and earth were joined there was nothing but darkness between them {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Tabari on 21:30} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 21:30}. Al-Qurtubi also relays from [[person/kab-al-ahbar|Ka'b]] that God created the heavens and earth one upon another, then made a wind in their midst and opened them with it {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 21:30}.

## The smoke

Ibn Kathir explains the smoke in Fussilat 11 as the water vapour that rose when the earth was created, and relays from Mujahid from Ibn Abbas that God told the heavens to bring forth His sun, moon and stars and told the earth to split open its rivers and bring out its fruit; he says Ibn Jarir chose this {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 41:11}. Al-Qurtubi says the smoke came from the water's breathing out, as is related from Ibn Mas'ud and others in the commentary on al-Baqara, and that the command to come has two senses: that God spoke words, or that His power appeared to them and stood in the place of speech; most scholars, he says, held that God created speech in them and they spoke {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 41:11}.

## Building and expanding

On al-Dhariyat 47 Ibn Kathir says building the heaven means making it a high, guarded roof with strength, as Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatada and al-Thawri said, and that expanding means He widened its reaches and raised it without pillars {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 51:47}. Al-Tabari says the sense is that He has breadth in creating it and what He wills, and relays Ibn Zayd that He widened it {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Tabari on 51:47}. Al-Qurtubi gathers several readings: able, from Ibn Abbas and al-Hasan; having breadth; providers of ample sustenance through rain; or having set a space between the heaven and earth, and holds that al-Jawhari's sense of wealth and capacity covers them all {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 51:47}.

## Modern scientific readings

In contemporary Muslim discourse, above all in the literature of "scientific miracles", some read the smoke, the rending and the expanding as matching modern cosmological theories of the origin and expansion of the universe. That is a modern reading, centuries later than the classical commentary; only the commentators' material was read here, not the sources of this reading itself.

Nidhal Guessoum, a Muslim astrophysicist, published an article in Zygon in 2008 criticising the theory of the Qur'an's scientific miraculousness. He holds that a simple plan to adopt modern science within an Islamic revival turned into this theory, that it rests on a misunderstanding of what science is and a narrow view of the Qur'an, and he promotes a plurality of readings of the Qur'an, including ones informed by scientific knowledge, upholding Averroes's principle of "no possible conflict", which shows that some readings are consistent with a scientific theory rather than that the theory is found in the text {cite:guessoum-quran-science-zygon-2008}. This summary rests on the published abstract; the full text was not read, so no position on any particular verse is attributed to him.

:::perspectives
**Classical commentary.** The joined state is either heaven and earth stuck together and then separated by air, or one heaven split into seven, or the closed rain and soil opened by rain and plants; al-Tabari preferred rain and plants {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Tabari on 21:30} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 21:30}.

**An academic view of scientific-miracle claims in general.** Guessoum rejects the theory of scientific miraculousness while allowing that some readings may be consistent with a scientific theory without claiming it is in the text {cite:guessoum-quran-science-zygon-2008}.

**Modern scientific reading of these verses.** Found in the scientific-miracles literature and treated here as a modern interpretive claim, not as the view of the classical sources.
:::

## What the sources do not say

The commentators read here do not connect the rending or the smoke to any cosmological theory, and the verses give no dates or measures. The sources do not settle which reading is meant, and none was agreed on.
