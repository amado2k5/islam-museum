## Summary
Ibn Sina gathers the signs of fullness in general, then separates those of fullness of the vessels from those of fullness of the faculty, and notes the dreams that point to each {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.

## The signs in general
They are heaviness of the limbs, sluggishness in movement, redness of colour, swollen vessels, stretched skin, a full pulse, coloured and thick urine, poor appetite and dimness of sight. Some dreams point to heaviness, such as seeing oneself unable to move or rise, carrying a heavy load or unable to speak, whereas dreams of flying and swift movement show the humours to be thin and moderate in amount {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.

## Telling the two apart
Heaviness, sluggishness and poor appetite are shared by both kinds. But when fullness of the faculty is pure, the vessels are not greatly swollen, the skin not greatly stretched, the pulse not greatly full and large, the urine not very thick and the colour not very red; breakdown and weariness break out after exertion, and the dreams show itching, stinging, burning and foul smells. It also points to the dominant humour by that humour's own signs, and most often fullness of the faculty breeds disease before its signs are firmly established {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.
