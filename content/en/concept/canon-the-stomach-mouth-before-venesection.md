## Summary
Ibn Sina distinguishes three states of the mouth of the stomach in which bleeding is feared, excess of sensation, weakness, and readiness to receive bile, gives the sign of each and what each is fed with before he is bled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## The danger
One with keen sensation of the stomach's mouth, or weakness of it, or afflicted with the generating of bile in it, must be guarded against rashness in his bleeding, above all on an empty stomach. If these are bled without first tending the mouth of their stomach, great danger comes of it, and some of them may perish {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## The signs
The one with keen sensation of the stomach's mouth you know by his being hurt by swallowing stinging things; the one with weakness of the stomach's mouth you know by the weakness of his appetite and the pains of his stomach's mouth; and the one whose stomach's mouth takes in bile and generates much of it you know by his constant nausea, his vomiting bile at all times and the bitterness of his mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## Tending it before bleeding
The one with keen sensation and the one with weakness should be given morsels of clean bread dipped in a good-smelling sour rob. If the weakness is from a cold temperament, the morsels are dipped in such as sugar water with aromatics, or musked mint syrup, or musked storax, and then he is bled. As for the one in whom bile is generated, he should vomit after drinking much hot water with oxymel, then be given morsels to eat and rested a little, and then be bled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## Making up what was lost
The good blood that is lost must be made up by food, but it must be as little as possible, for the stomach is weak because of the bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.
