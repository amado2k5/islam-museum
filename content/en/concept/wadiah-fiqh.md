The deposit (wadi'a) is property placed with another to keep; the Hanbalis add: without return. Making someone a depositary is granting another custody of one's property; the Hanbalis add: as a gift. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17054} It is close to the loan of use, since the hand in both is a trust for some jurists, and to found property, since the finder's hand during the year is a trust; it differs from usurpation, which is seizing property by force and wrong. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17055} Its lawfulness rests on the sunna and on general consensus, and a report orders the return of trusts to their owner. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17056} On the ruling for some cases the text gives two opinions, prohibition and disapproval, and the printed preference is disapproval. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17058} On its nature there are two views: most jurists (Malikis, Hanbalis and, in the preferred view, Shafi'is) call it a restricted agency, since the depositary keeps but does not dispose; the Hanafis describe it as an express or implied grant to another of custody over one's property; some Shafi'is call it a mere permission, which makes the holder a trustee rather than a depositary. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17058}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17059} If the depositor withdraws, on the permission view the withdrawal is void and the deposit stands; on the contract view the deposit ends and the property stays in hand as a trust that must be returned once possible. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17059} Its three features: it is a contract either party may end at will, it is a trust contract rather than a guarantee, and it is gratuitous in origin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17059}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17060}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17061} The pillars are the formula, the two parties and the object according to most jurists; the Hanafis count an offer and an acceptance, which may be express, by allusion, or by act. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17062}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17063} The depositary may not charge for keeping unless agreed, customary, or a professional; the Hanbalis forbid stipulating a fee for the deposit itself. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17062} Capacity for the depositor is discernment according to the Hanafis and full adulthood, sanity and good judgement according to most jurists; a deposit made by a child or an insane person is not valid, and the depositary who takes it bears liability. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17064}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17066} On a discerning child there are four views; the first, held by most Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, is that a child cannot be a depositary at all, because the purpose is keeping and the child lacks the capacity for it, so a loss in his hands brings no liability. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17066}
