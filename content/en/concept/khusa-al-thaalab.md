## Nature
The book quotes Dioscorides that it is a plant whose leaf is spread on the face of the earth, green, like the leaf of the fine olive but thinner and longer, with branches about a span in length bearing a purple-coloured flower, and a soft root like the bulbus onion tending to length, which may be eaten boiled as the bulbus is eaten; it grows in stony and sandy places; there is a further kind that some call andriyas for its many benefits, its leaf resembling the leek leaf in length but broader, tender with a sticky moisture, its flower tending to purple and its root like the two testicles; and the herbage of both is rough and sweet {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.702-703}

## Temperament
Hot in the first degree and moist in it, its moisture superfluous {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Joints
It benefits spasm and the backward stretching and palsy greatly, and stands in the place of asqanqur {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Excretory organs
Its poultice opens fistulas, and drunk in a drink it holds the flowing of the belly, as some people claimed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it was said of this root that when a man eats the larger part, males are begotten, and that when women eat the smaller part, they bear females; and that it stirs desire and helps it, especially with a drink; a statement attributed to the book as passed on from earlier writers, without recommendation or endorsement {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}
