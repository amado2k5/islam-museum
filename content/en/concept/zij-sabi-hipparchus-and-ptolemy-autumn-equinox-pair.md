## Summary
Al-Battani presents Hipparchus's observation of the autumn equinox at Alexandria on the third of the five added days, then Ptolemy's, two hundred and eighty-five Egyptian years later, in the third year of Antoninus, on the ninth of Athyr about an hour after sunrise, and finds between the two in truth two hundred and eighty-five Egyptian years, seventy days, a quarter day and a part of twenty of a day {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.66}.

## The comparison with whole quarters
He then sets this against what should have gathered had the quarters been whole over these years, namely seventy-one days and a quarter, and finds the difference to be one day less a part of twenty, whose ratio to the two hundred and eighty-five years is as one day to three hundred years, so that the year becomes three hundred and sixty-five days and a quarter less a part of three hundred of a day {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.66}. The year numbers the text gives for Hipparchus's observation are disturbed in the printed text and are not given here; only what agrees with the rest of the reckoning is used.
