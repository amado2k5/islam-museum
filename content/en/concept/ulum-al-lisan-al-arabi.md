## Summary
In the forty-fifth chapter of [[work/al-muqaddima|the Muqaddima]], [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] presents the sciences of the Arabic tongue and limits them to four pillars, lexicon, grammar, rhetoric and literature, and puts grammar first because it shows the principles of meaning; he then tells how this science arose when the Arabs' faculty changed through mixing with non-Arabs. The page gives his words as they stand in the pages read and ascribes them to him.

## The four pillars
Ibn Khaldun says the pillars of the sciences of the Arabic tongue are four: lexicon, grammar, rhetoric and literature. He holds that knowing them is necessary for the people of the Shari'a, since the sources of all legal rulings are the Book and the Sunna, which are in the language of the Arabs, and were transmitted by Arab Companions and Successors, so whoever wants to understand these rulings needs the tongue in which they came down and were transmitted {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.753}.

## Why grammar comes first
Ibn Khaldun holds that the most important of these sciences is grammar, because by it the principles of intended meanings are shown through indication: the agent is known from the object and the subject from the predicate, and without it the root of conveying meaning would be unknown. He then compares it with lexicon, saying it would deserve priority, but most of the vocabulary stayed as it was, while inflection changed entirely and left no trace; so grammar is more important than lexicon {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.753}.

## How grammar arose
Ibn Khaldun describes the Arabs before grammar: their faculty in the tongue was the best of faculties, and vowels and particles pointed to the meaning. When Islam came and they left the Hijaz to seek the dominion that was in the hands of other nations, and mixed with the non-Arabs, that faculty changed through the faults that hearing took in, "for hearing is the father of the linguistic faculties". The scholars feared this faculty would be wholly corrupted and the Qur'an and hadith become closed to understanding, so they derived from the course of Arab speech rules for that faculty, such as that the agent is in the nominative and the object in the accusative, and called the change at the end of the word i'rab and its cause the 'amil. People then began to write on it {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.754}.

## Who wrote on it
He says the first to write on it was [[person/abu-al-aswad-al-duali|Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali]] of Banu Kinana, and it is said that this was at Ali's suggestion. People then wrote on it until [[person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi|al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi]] came in the days of al-Rashid, refined its chapters and completed them; [[person/sibawayh|Sibawayh]] took from him and completed its branches, and his famous book is [[work/kitab-sibawayh|al-Kitab]] {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.754}. [[person/abu-ali-al-farisi|Abu Ali al-Farisi]] and al-Zajjaj wrote short books for learners. Then the dispute arose between the Kufans and the Basrans, whose details are on the page cited {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.755}. For the origins see [[concept/ibtida-al-nahw|the beginning of grammar]] and [[concept/madrasat-al-nahw-al-basriyya-kufiyya|the Basran and Kufan schools]], and for the discipline itself [[concept/ilm-al-nahw|Arabic grammar]].

## The later books
Among the later books Ibn Khaldun names the Tashil of [[person/ibn-malik-al-jayyani|Ibn Malik]], the Mufassal of [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]], the Muqaddima of Ibn al-Hajib, and the Mughni of [[person/ibn-hisham-al-ansari|Ibn Hisham]], a book written in Cairo that reached the Maghrib {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.755}.

## The order of his argument
His course can be summarised in steps as he presents it: the sources of the Shari'a are Arabic, so the science of the tongue is needed; the pillars of that science are four, and the first is grammar because it tells meanings apart; and inflection is what changed in the tongue entirely, so it became a science that was written down. Then Abu al-Aswad began, al-Khalil completed the chapters, Sibawayh completed the branches, earlier scholars wrote short books for learners, and then came the books of the later authors. This summarises his wording and adds nothing to it {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.753} {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.755}.

## What this page does not reach
Of his discussion of the sciences of the tongue, only the forty-fifth chapter and the account of how grammar arose were read; what Ibn Khaldun writes on rhetoric and literature was not read. The details of the Kufan and Basran dispute on page 755 are not given here. Ibn Khaldun is read here as a historian relating how the science began, and his account was not set against the books of biographical classes. The other chapters of the Muqaddima that may touch on language were not read, nor other editions of the book. The books of the later authors he names were not read, nor those of al-Khalil and Sibawayh; only what Ibn Khaldun alone says about them was read.
