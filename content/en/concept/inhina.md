## Definition
Inhina' in the lexical sense is bending and turning from straightness; one says a man is bent by age when time has bent him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3461}
It is related to bowing (ruku'), prostration (sujud) and gesturing (iyma'), which are kinds of bending in prayer or indications by the head, hand or eye. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3461}

## Ruling
- Lawful bending: such as that in daily work. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3462}
- Obligatory bending: bowing in prayer, for one able to do it, to the point where the hands reach the knees of a man of medium height, by the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3462}
- Forbidden bending in honour of a person, an animal or an object. Bending before great men and rulers in honour is forbidden by agreement, since bending is only for God, and a hadith records a man asking whether he may bend to his friend on meeting him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3462}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3463}
- Bending as an imitation of unbelievers without honour is disliked, since it resembles the practice of the Magians. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3462}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3463}
- Bending under compulsion before a ruler is governed by the rules of compulsion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3463}
- A slight bending while standing does not spoil the standing for most jurists; the jurists differ on following a hunchback. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3463}
