## Summary
Hulwan means a gift; in the law it is the payment given to a soothsayer for his services, and it also names the sum a father takes from his daughter's dowry. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7274} The hadith forbids the price of a dog, the fee of a prostitute and the soothsayer's hulwan; the commentators read the last as the fee for his divining. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7274} Following al-Nawawi, the scholars agree the soothsayer's hulwan is forbidden, as an exchange for what is unlawful and as eating property wrongly. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7275} A father's taking of part of his daughter's dowry (hiba) divides the schools: the Hanafis and Hanbalis hold the condition binding and the dower valid; the Malikis distinguish a condition made at the contract, which belongs to the daughter, from one made later, which belongs to the father; the Shafiis hold the dower invalid and owed as the equivalent dower. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7275} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7276} Hulwan in the sense of dower is treated under the headword on dower, and the entry refers to related words such as ajal and jalaf. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7276} Ju'l differs from hulwan in being more specific, and hiba is a narrower sense of hulwan; rishwa (bribe) is lexically a giving. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7275}
