## Summary
Al-Battani gives as an example the line where the pole is 69 degrees 44 minutes high; 20 degrees 16 minutes remain of ninety, which is the Sun's declination when it is thirty parts from the summer solstice point on either side, that is from the first of Gemini to the first of Leo, so it circles the horizon for about two months without setting. Opposite, from the first of Sagittarius to the first of Aquarius, it stays hidden about two months, so the night lasts as long. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.28-29}.

## Checking the figures
The passage can be checked against the declination table: the first of Gemini stands at 60 degrees of the zodiac and the first of Leo at 120, each thirty parts from the summer solstice, and the declination at both is close to 20 degrees 16 minutes on his greatest declination. So the example agrees with his own table. The text states outright that what remains of the pole's altitude is 20 degrees 16 minutes; the two figures are clear in the scan, while the number of parts named is read from the signs the text lists. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.28}.

## A farther line: about four months
He then describes a still more northerly line whose distance from the equator is a figure the scan garbles beyond reading. On it, he says, the Sun does not set when its northern declination equals what the pole's altitude leaves of ninety, nor rise when it has the same declination in the south; it has that declination as it moves to the first of Taurus and the first of Virgo, so the day lasts about four months, and the night likewise between the first of Scorpio and the first of Pisces. The figures of this line are not reproduced here. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.29}.
