## Summary
In the eighteenth chapter Ibn Sina treats the causes that are neither necessary nor harmful: those that are not by their kind part of the nature and are not opposed to it. These are the things that meet the body other than the air, which is necessary, such as baths and kinds of rubbing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.139}.

## Two ways of acting
Things acting from outside by contact act in two ways: either by what is fine in them passing into the pores, or by the organs drawing them in through their pores, or by both together; or by a pure quality that alters the body without mixing, as with a plaster that cools or heats in act and a warm compress, or by a quality in potency that the innate heat stirs into act, or by specific property {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.139}.
