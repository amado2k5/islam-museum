## Summary
Ibn Sina describes the pulse of children, young men, men in middle age and the old, and explains each by power and need {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.176-177}.

## Children and young men
A child's pulse is softer because of moisture, weaker and more frequent, since the heat is strong and the power is not, for they are not yet complete. Measured against the size of their bodies it is great, because their instrument is very soft and their need severe, but against the pulse of the fully grown it is not great, only quicker and more frequent for need, since the smoky vapour gathers much in children from their great digesting and they need to expel it and to cool their innate heat. A young man's pulse is greater in size and not greater in speed, being much lower in it and in frequency and inclined to wide spacing; in early youth it is greater and in mid youth stronger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.176-177}.

## Middle age and old age
It was shown that the heat in children and young men is close to alike, so their need is close, but the power in young men is greater and reaches by greatness what spares speed and frequency. The mainstay in bringing about greatness is power; need is the summons and the instrument the helper. The pulse of men in middle age is smaller for weakness, less quick, and for want of need more widely spaced. That of the very old is small, widely spaced and slow, and may be soft from foreign moistures, not innate ones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.177}.
