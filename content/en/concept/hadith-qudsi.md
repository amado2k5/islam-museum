## Overview
A *hadith qudsi* ("sacred hadith") is a saying that the Prophet transmitted from his Lord. In the chains of transmission it is known by the formula "what he relates from his Lord." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.511-512}

## An example
An example is the hadith of Abu Dharr: "O My servants, I have forbidden injustice to Myself," reported by Muslim (2577). It is the first sacred hadith in the Forty of al-Nawawi. In the wording of al-Tirmidhi the formula "God the Exalted says" is explicit. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.511-512}

## Sayings about the hadith
Imam Ahmad said it is the most noble hadith for the people of Syria. When Abu Idris al-Khawlani narrated it, he would kneel on his knees; Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz relates this from Rabi'a ibn Yazid. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.511-512}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme In the Courts of the Hadith Qudsi
The first episode of [[work/fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah|Fi rihab al-hadith al-qudsi]] opens with the presenter saying that speech attributed to God is of three kinds: the Qur'an, the noblest of them, the books of the earlier prophets before they were altered, and the hadith qudsi; a hadith qudsi is what the Prophet transmitted attributing it to his Lord, whereas he does not attribute a prophetic hadith to his Lord. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:02:00}

[[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] then explains that what the Prophet conveyed from his Lord is of two parts: one by which God challenged the deniers, to prove their inability in the eloquence they excelled in, and another not coupled with the challenge and not recited as worship. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:05:00} He holds that a challenge is addressed only to the one who denies, that Abu Bakr and Ali believed without having heard anything of the Qur'an, and that the hadith qudsi came as a speech by which those who never denied are honoured. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:07:00} He says it is not read in the prayer nor recited as worship, and is known by the Prophet saying in it "God said". {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:07:30}

He argues that none of the masters of Arabic eloquence had three styles, while the Prophet's speech came in three: the style of the Qur'an, that of the hadith qudsi and that of the prophetic hadith. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:09:30} He sums up the difference by saying that the Qur'an is from God in wording and meaning, the hadith qudsi from God in meaning, and the prophetic hadith from the Prophet. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep01 00:09:30} In episode 23 he describes the chain of a hadith qudsi as "raised" (marfu') to God. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep23 00:19:00}
