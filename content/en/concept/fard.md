## Summary
The fard is what God has made obligatory upon His servants, and the plural is furud and fara'id. The word occurs in the Qur'an and the hadith in the forms "he made obligatory" (farada), "obligation" (farida), "he ordained" (iftarada) and "it was prescribed for you" (kutiba 'alaykum), and then in the technical usage of the scholars of legal theory it became the name of the highest degree of requirement. Under this section the browser gathers every text in the museum's sources that names an obligation or a farida, and it describes what is in them and issues no ruling on it.

## The fard and the wajib
In Irshad al-Fuhul, al-Shawkani says that the obligatory (wajib) is that for which the doer is praised and the one who leaves it is blamed, and that the majority treat the fard as its synonym {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.8}. The Hanafis distinguish between them according to the evidence, as recorded in the entry on [[concept/wajib-fiqh|the necessary (wajib)]]. Al-Ghazali makes the obligatory the first of the five divisions: that for which the address came requiring the act, joined to a warning of punishment for leaving it {cite:mustasfa-hafiz-v1 p.297}. The place of the fard among all the divisions is in the entry on [[concept/al-ahkam-al-taklifiyya|the taklifi rulings]].

## In the hadith
- The obligations are the dearest thing by which to draw near: in the sacred hadith, "My servant draws near to Me with nothing dearer to Me than what I have made obligatory upon him, and My servant continues to draw near to Me with supererogatory acts until I love him" [[passage/bukhari-6502|al-Bukhari 6502]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 6502}. The text places the obligations first and then [[concept/nawafil|the supererogatory acts]].
- The Bedouin's question: a Bedouin with dishevelled hair came and asked the Prophet what God had made obligatory upon him of prayer, fasting and alms. He answered him with the five prayers, the month of Ramadan and the alms, and said of each, "unless you do something voluntarily" [[passage/bukhari-api-1891|al-Bukhari 1891]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Bukhari 1891}. In its wording this hadith distinguishes between the obligatory and the voluntary.
- The hadith of the Forty: "God has made certain things obligatory, so do not neglect them; He has set limits, so do not transgress them; He has forbidden certain things, so do not violate them; and He has been silent about certain things as a mercy to you, not out of forgetfulness, so do not search for them" [[passage/arbaun-30|Forty Hadith of al-Nawawi 30]]. This text brings together four degrees in one passage: the obligation, the limit, the prohibition and what is left unsaid.
- The obligation of the pilgrimage: the Prophet addressed the people and said that God had made the pilgrimage obligatory upon them. A man asked, "Every year?" He was silent until the man had repeated it three times, and then he said, "Had I said yes, it would have become obligatory, and had it become obligatory you would not have been able to do it" [[passage/nasai-api-2619|al-Nasa'i 2619]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Nasai 2619}.

## Among the Companions
- The letter of Abu Bakr on the alms: Abu Bakr wrote to Anas, when he sent him to Bahrain, a letter that begins, "This is the obligation of the alms which the Messenger of God made obligatory upon the Muslims, and which God commanded His Messenger", and then he set out the minimum amounts for camels and sheep [[passage/bukhari-api-1454|al-Bukhari 1454]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Bukhari 1454}.
- 'Ali and the witr prayer: it is related of 'Ali that he said, "The witr is not a binding duty like your prescribed prayer, but the Messenger of God made it a sunna" [[passage/tirmidhi-api-453|al-Tirmidhi 453]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Tirmidhi 453}. In its wording there is a contrast between the prescribed and the sunna.
- 'Ubada ibn al-Samit: it reached him that a man in Syria, known as Abu Muhammad, said that the witr is obligatory. He said, "Abu Muhammad lied", and related the hadith, "Five prayers God has prescribed for His servants" [[passage/abudawud-api-1420|Abu Dawud 1420]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Abu Dawud 1420}. The disagreement over the ruling on the witr between the schools is in the entries on jurisprudence.

## What falls outside this section
"The fara'id" in the sense of the shares of inheritance and the science of them is not included here, nor is "he allotted to him" in the sense of a stipend or a dower, for these are other meanings of the word. As for the obligation upon every person, its section is [[concept/fard-ayn|the individual obligation (fard 'ayn)]], and the obligation upon the community has its section in [[concept/fard-kifaya|the communal obligation (fard kifaya)]].
