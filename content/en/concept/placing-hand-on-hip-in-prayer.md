## Summary
Takhassur is placing the hand on the side of the hip. In prayer most jurists hold it disliked as a matter of taste, and the Hanafis hold it disliked as unlawful. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4812} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4813}

## Definition
In Arabic, takhassur means placing the hand on the hip, and a makhsara is a staff or similar thing one leans on. A narration forbids praying in the manner of mukhtasir or mutakhassir; some explain it as praying with the hand on the side. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4812}
A saying that such posture in prayer is the rest of the people of the Fire is explained by Ibn Manzur as a rest the worshipper takes in this world, not rest in the Fire; al-Dhahabi graded the saying weak. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4812}

## Ruling in prayer
Most jurists hold takhassur in prayer disliked as a matter of taste. The Hanafis hold it disliked as unlawful, because it departs from the transmitted form of prayer and imitates the proud. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4812}
When the worshipper has a need, such as pain in his side or fatigue from night prayer, it is allowed to the extent of the need. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4813}
Sa'id ibn Ziyad reports that he placed his hand on his hip beside Ibn Umar, who said after prayer that this is the "spine" of prayer and that the Prophet forbade it. The book's footnote records al-Iraqi's grading of the narration as sound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4813}

## Outside prayer
Takhassur outside prayer is disliked as a matter of taste, in Hanafi texts, because it is the manner of the proud. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4813}
Leaning on a staff during prayer is treated under the entry on istinad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4813}

## Leaning on a staff in the Friday sermon
The Malikis recommend that the preacher lean on a staff during the Friday sermon, holding it in the right hand. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis count it a sunna of the sermon; the Shafi'is prefer the left hand holding it and the right hand on the edge of the pulpit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4814}
The Hanafis dislike leaning on a bow or staff during the sermon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4814}
The majority rely on the narration of al-Hakam ibn Hazn that the Prophet stood leaning on a sword, a bow or a staff; Ibn Hajar graded it fair in al-Talkhis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4814}
