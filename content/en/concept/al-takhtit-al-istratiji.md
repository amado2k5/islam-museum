## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan defines planning as a decision about the direction of the future, fixing three things: where we are today, where we want to reach, and how we get there; strategic planning begins at sector level and above and spans five years or more {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:07:30}.

## Versus operational planning
He explains that the dividing line between strategic and operational planning is the sector level: there is no strategic plan for a department or a section, and a strategic plan is never shorter than five years while an operational one is usually a year; the vocabulary differs too: vision, mission, values and long-range goals against annual priorities, budgets, policies and procedures {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:09:00}.
He adds that strategic planning is the planning apparatus's responsibility, while operational planning belongs to the department or section itself {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:10:00}.

## Methods of strategic planning
He says there are thirteen known scientific methods for strategic planning — among them scenario planning, PMSP, competing-for-the-future, SWOT and Pfeiffer's method — that 87 per cent of the world's plans are written with SWOT because people know nothing else, and that 70 per cent of strategic plans written with SWOT failed (needs_source: the two figures are the speaker's) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:36:30}.
He presents Pfeiffer's method from the book Applied Strategic Planning, adjusted by his own experience; its first step is 'planning to plan': forming a team of five to seven including people who understand the core business, the competitors, finance, information technology, human resources, marketing and planning {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:15:30}.

## His rules
Among his rules: the planning team's work never exceeds six months however large the organisation; a plan spans five years for a small organisation, ten for a medium one and fifteen or twenty for a large one — except IT companies, which never plan beyond three years because change is so fast {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:28:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:35:30}.
The decision-maker is not a member of the team; the team meets him after each step to lay out the alternatives {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:26:00}.
And the 'values review' — values meaning what the organisation holds valuable, not ethics — is done only for a new organisation or one whose leadership has changed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:41:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 01:50:00}.

## In "Secrets of Prophetic Leadership": forecasting and the emigration to Abyssinia
In episodes 22 and 23 of his programme [[work/asrar-al-qiyada-al-nabawiyya-program|"Secrets of Prophetic Leadership"]], [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|al-Suwaidan]] gives vision two competencies: analysing organisations, and forecasting, which is trained through strategic planning. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep22 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:02:30} Forecasting for him is planning on expectations built on past trends and present change; the unseen belongs to God, and these are human estimates. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:03:30} Its benefits are efficiency, flexibility, estimating needs, lowering risk and picturing the organisation's future. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:04:00} Its stages: set the goal; gather data on the past and present; list the possibilities and give each a probability; choose; decide. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:05:30}

He applies this to the [[event/first-migration-to-abyssinia|emigration to Abyssinia]], which he reads as a fallback plan at a time when Persia was closing in around Arabia, and he divides the course of the mission into stages: the Meccan stage; foundation, with Badr; the shift from defence to offence after the Trench; recognition, at Hudaybiyya; the conquest of Mecca; and going out beyond Arabia. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:47:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep23 00:49:30}

## In "The Academy for Preparing Leaders": models, Pfeiffer's steps and implementation
Al-Suwaidan returns to strategic planning in both filmed leaders' academies: two lessons in [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the first]] and a four-part lecture in [[work/leadership-development-academy-2-program|the second]]. He defines it as "a method for setting the organisation's long-range goals and how to reach them". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:03:00} In the second academy he puts it as a formula: "vision plus the route equals a plan". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:14:30} His rule is that strategic planning belongs above the level of a department and covers five years or more; below that, planning is operational. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:09:30} See [[concept/al-takhtit-al-tashghili|operational planning]]. He says studies found that organisations with strategic plans outperform those without by about thirty per cent. He rejects claims of a hundred per cent or more, and calls planning "a tool, not a magic wand". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:18:00} He makes the point with a story: [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] asked to see the famous sword of Amr ibn Ma'dikarib and found it ordinary, and Amr replied that it is not the sword but the arm. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:21:00}

Of more than thirteen models he presents four. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:09:00} SWOT, he says, is used for eighty-three per cent of the world's strategic plans, but only thirty per cent of those succeed, so he does not advise it for strategic planning. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:31:00} Scenario planning lists the main possibilities and their branches, then gives them percentages, which he calls the hardest step. He says he once worked on such a study while in America during the occupation of Kuwait. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:38:00} PMSP sets targets relative to the market rather than as fixed profits. It suits only fast-moving markets with early information. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:11:00} He notes that all thirteen theories of strategic planning are American. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep02 00:42:30} The model he prefers is Pfeiffer's, from the 1992 book "Applied Strategic Planning", which he says he summarised and adapted. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:14:00}

Pfeiffer's starting point, as he tells it, is the question why plans written by experts fail. The answer is that no one planned for the plan's success. Hence the first step, "planning to plan". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:17:00} Its rules:
- The team has five to seven members. It brings together planning, the core business, finance, marketing, human resources and information technology, but not a lawyer, who reviews from outside. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:24:00}
- The head of the organisation stays off the team but is consulted after each main step. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:34:30}
- Members are released full time. Otherwise, he says, planning gets "the leftovers of their time and the husks of their minds". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:38:00}
- They receive a single lump-sum reward. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:39:30}
- The plan is written in three to six months. Its length depends on the number of committed members: five years for fewer than a hundred, longer for larger bodies. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep03 00:44:00}
The later steps cover values, [[concept/al-ruya-wa-al-risala|vision and mission]], lines of business, [[concept/strategic-business-unit|strategic business units]], [[concept/kpi-muashirat|performance indicators]] and gap analysis.

On implementation he advises explaining the plan to everyone, not only to senior staff. He tells of a Gulf finance ministry where, he says, some assistant under-secretaries had never seen their own strategic plan. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 01:10:00} A new plan starts only when every unit has stopped working to the old one. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 01:12:30} Operational goals then come from two directions: a SWOT analysis of each unit, and the performance indicators set by the leadership. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 01:13:30} He closes on faith: planning is a required means, but reliance is on God, not on the plan. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 01:17:00}
