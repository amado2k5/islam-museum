## Definition
Insha' is lexically the beginning and raising of a thing. In the craft of writing it is anything that turns the writer's art into composing speech and arranging meanings; in rhetoric and usul it is one of the two kinds of speech, against khabar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3501}

## Khabar and insha'
A khabar is speech that admits truth or falsehood in itself; an insha' admits neither, because it has no external fact to match, and is so called because the meaning is created. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3501}

## Kinds
- Demanding insha': question, command, prohibition, wish and call. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3501}
- Non-demanding insha': praise, blame, the verbs of surprise, and oath. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3501}

## Contract words
The jurists and usulis differ on words of contract such as ‘I sold’ and ‘I bought’, on words of dissolution such as ‘I divorced’ and ‘I freed’, and on a judge's formulas. The dispute concerns words used to create the contract, not reports of an earlier one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3501}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3502}
- The Shafi'is hold these to be insha', because by custom they have passed from reporting to creating. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3502}
- The Hanafis hold them khabar, understood by implication, since the formula is set for reporting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3502}
- Al-Tahanawi preferred the Shafi'i view, as did the rhetoricians. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3502}
