## Summary
In the jurists' terms slaughter is the cutting of the throat or the lower neck; dhaka is the wider term, the means that makes the flesh of a land animal lawful to eat, by choice or by necessity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8379}
A valid slaughter needs an animal that is alive, a death that comes from the cut, an animal that is not game of the sanctuary, a slaughterer who is sane and a Muslim or a People of the Book, and a blade that cuts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8388} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8392} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8397} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8403}

## Kinds of dhaka
The chosen dhaka is slaughter (cutting the throat) for most animals, and the lower-neck cut for the camel in the majority view. The forced dhaka is a wound in any part of the body when the animal cannot be caught; this is called 'aqr. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8381} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8383} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8407} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8408}
The Malikis hold that animals without flowing blood need dhaka, while the majority do not require it for fish and locusts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8382}
A fetus taken from a slaughtered mother follows her dhaka, and the rule depends on whether it comes out alive or dead. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8410} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8411}

## Rules of slaughter
Dhaka keeps the animal's purity and makes its flesh lawful; the skin of a non-edible animal is clean by dhaka according to the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8382}
A person who omits the invocation when able and mindful makes the animal unlawful according to the majority; the Shafi'is and in one view the Hanbalis make the forgetful person's slaughter unlawful too, and the ignorant person is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8397} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8398} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8399}
The schools differ over the slaughter of a People of the Book, a Magian, an apostate and a child of mixed parents; the majority make the slaughter of the People of the Book lawful unless it was offered to other than God. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8392} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8393} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8394} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8395}
