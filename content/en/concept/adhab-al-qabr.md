## Summary
The questioning and punishment of the grave is one of the articles of belief to which [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] gives a chapter in the Book of the Sunna, "the questioning in the grave and the punishment of the grave", placed between the chapters on [[concept/hawd-basin-water|the Basin]] and [[concept/mizan|the Balance]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 74} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 76}

## The reports in the chapter
- **Al-Bara's short report**: [[person/al-bara-ibn-azib|al-Bara' ibn 'Azib]] relates that when the Muslim is questioned in the grave and testifies that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His Messenger, that is what God means in [[passage/quran-14-27|Ibrahim 27]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 74}
- **Anas's report**: the Prophet entered a palm grove of the Banu al-Najjar, heard a sound and was alarmed; told that the graves belonged to people who had died in the Jahiliyya, he said, "Seek refuge with God from the punishment of the Fire and from the trial of the Dajjal." He then described how, when the believer is laid in his grave, an angel comes and asks, "What did you worship? What did you say of this man?" He answers, "He is God's servant and Messenger," and is shown the place that would have been his in the Fire, exchanged for a house in Paradise; the unbeliever says, "I do not know; I said what people said," and is struck with an iron hammer and gives a cry heard by all creatures but men and jinn. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 74-75} In another version the dead man hears the tread of his companions' sandals as they leave, two angels come to him, and the hypocrite is named with the unbeliever. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 75}
- **Al-Bara's long report**: they went out with the Prophet to the funeral of a man of the Ansar; he sat by the grave before the niche was dug, and they sat round him "as if birds were on their heads"; he said twice or three times, "Seek refuge with God from the punishment of the grave." He then told how two angels sit the dead man up and ask, "Who is your Lord? What is your religion? Who is this man who was sent among you?"; the believer answers and says, "I read God's Book and believed and affirmed it," and a caller cries that My servant has spoken truly, so he is given bedding from Paradise and a door to it is opened; the unbeliever says, "Hah, hah, I do not know," is given bedding from the Fire, and his grave is narrowed upon him. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 75-76}

## From al-Khattabi's commentary
[[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|Al-Khattabi]] stops at the words *la darayta wa-la talayta* ("you neither knew nor…"), saying that the hadith transmitters say it this way and that it is an error; he quotes two explanations from Ibn Qutayba's Gharib al-Hadith: *wa-la atlayta*, a curse that his camels may have no young following them, or *wa-la i'talayta*, "you neither knew nor were able". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 75}

## Not covered here
The chapter records no disagreement on the question; the views of the theologians who denied the punishment of the grave, and the Sunni debate with them, are to be added from their sources.
