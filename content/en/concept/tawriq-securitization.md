## Summary
Securitization is the representation of assets by tradable securities, such as shares, so that their papers can be traded independently of the underlying asset {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 353}.

## Its Impact According to the Author
The author says that securitization helps increase trading volume, but it also facilitates speculation on prices without delivery or receipt, so the commodity remains in place and may become illusory or virtual {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 353} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 354}. He holds that the wave of excessive securitization helped separate the financial economy from the real economy, pushing it to very high valuations {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 354}.

## Its Origin
The author holds that the origin of securitization lay in the shares of capital companies or joint-stock companies: shares facilitate selling stakes without liquidating the company, yet they can be used as a tool for speculation until a share takes on a value other than its real value, which is an undivided share in the company's net assets {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 354}.
