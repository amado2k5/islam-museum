## Summary
Ibn Sina says that after a day of excess one should go hungry the next and sleep long in a temperate place, and if sleep does not come, walk a great deal, gently and without a break; he relays Rufus's praise of walking after the midday meal, and describes sleeping first on the right, then the left, then the right, with covering and a raised pillow as aids to digestion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.

## After excess
If a man overeats one day he should be hungry the next and sleep long in a place neither hot nor cold; if sleep does not help, he should walk much, gently, continuously, with no pause or rest, and drink a little unmixed drink {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.

## Rufus on walking
Ibn Sina quotes Rufus as saying that he praises this walking, especially after the midday meal, because it prepares for the good settling of supper {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.

## Sleeping posture
One sleeps on the right, or briefly on the right, then on the left, then again on the right; a covering and a raised pillow help digestion, and in general the limbs should lie sloping downward and not upward {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.
