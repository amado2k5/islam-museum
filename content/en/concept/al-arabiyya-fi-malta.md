## Summary
This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum says about Arabic in Malta: its legal status, the varieties spoken and the official figures, each as the summary gives it. Malta is a singular case in the summary: a national Semitic language, official in the European Union, descended from Siculo-Arabic and written in the Latin alphabet. The museum did not open the pages the summary names, and the founder approved using it as supplied on 21 November 2026. Where the summary says that no official source or figure was found, this page says exactly that and supplies no number of its own.

## What this page is
This page is an article on the subject "Arabic in Malta", in the world chapter of the Arabic browser, one of the pages that treat Arabic outside the states counted as Arab. It answers three questions: what is the legal status of Arabic there, which varieties of it are spoken, and what official figures have been published on speakers, if any. The museum keeps here to what it keeps to on all its pages: a describing voice, not a commanding one, every statement ascribed to its page, and no verdict on a disputed question. All the material comes from one research summary, so the page describes what the summary said and does not claim to have read the sources.

## Background and dates
The summary's sequence begins with the origin, Maltese descending from Siculo-Arabic, and passes to the modern record: the constitution of Malta, the entry of Maltese among the languages of the European Union on 1 May 2004, then the censuses of 2011 and 2021 and the skills survey of 2022. The detail of the passage from Siculo-Arabic to Maltese is treated in the page on Arabic in al-Andalus and Sicily, with the conquest of Sicily from 827, the Normans and the limit of 1198 [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-al-andalus-wa-siqilliyya|Arabic in al-Andalus and Sicily]].

## Legal status
According to the summary, Article 5 of the Constitution of Malta makes Maltese the national language, co-official with English, and the Maltese text prevails in legislative conflicts {cite:fra-constitution-of-malta}. Since 1 May 2004 Maltese has been an official and working language of the European Union, the only official Semitic language in it {cite:eur-lex-council-regulation-930-2004} {cite:ec-representation-malta-maltese-language}. These are the summary's statements about its pages, which the museum did not read, and the museum does not judge whether Maltese is a variety of Arabic or a language in its own right.

## Varieties spoken
The summary names Maltese (il-Malti), a standardised Semitic language descended from Siculo-Arabic, heavily influenced by Sicilian, Italian, French and English, written in the Latin alphabet, and including regional town and island dialects across Malta and Gozo {cite:ncml-national-council-for-the-maltese-language}. For the passage from Siculo-Arabic to Maltese see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-al-andalus-wa-siqilliyya|Arabic in al-Andalus and Sicily]]; for the place [[place/malta|Malta]], and for the older Maghrebi kind [[concept/lahjat-al-maghrib|The dialect of the Maghrib]].

## Official figures
The summary gives three figures from Malta's National Statistics Office. Malta Skills Survey 2022: 90.4 percent of residents aged 15 to 64 reported understanding Maltese {cite:nso-malta-skills-survey-2022}. Census 2021: Maltese is the predominant main language among Maltese nationals aged 5 and over, while 7.8 percent grew up speaking English and 1.2 percent another language {cite:nso-malta-census-2021-vol-3}. Census 2011: 357,692 residents aged 10 and over, or 94.7 percent, spoke Maltese at least at an average level {cite:wikipedia-languages-of-malta}. UNESCO classifies Maltese as non-endangered {cite:unesco-atlas-languages-in-danger-pf0000187026}. The last figure is relayed by the summary from Wikipedia and is ascribed to it.

## How the sources differ
The pages named for this record are of different kinds. Official texts and statistics: Constitution of Malta (law reference) {cite:fra-constitution-of-malta}, Council Regulation (EC) No 930/2004 (Maltese as an official language of the EU) {cite:eur-lex-council-regulation-930-2004}, Malta Skills Survey 2022, preliminary report {cite:nso-malta-skills-survey-2022} and Census of Population and Housing 2021, final report: health, education, employment and other characteristics (Vol. 3) {cite:nso-malta-census-2021-vol-3}; Reference, academic and press pages: UNESCO Atlas (unesdoc ark 48223/pf0000187026), as named in the research summary {cite:unesco-atlas-languages-in-danger-pf0000187026}, The Maltese language (European Commission Representation in Malta) {cite:ec-representation-malta-maltese-language} and National Council for the Maltese Language {cite:ncml-national-council-for-the-maltese-language}; Wikipedia pages: Languages of Malta {cite:wikipedia-languages-of-malta}. The official texts and statistics speak for the state or the statistical office; the reference, academic and press pages describe and interpret; Wikipedia supports only names, places and dates. Where the kinds differ, the museum reports each in its own voice without choosing between them, and it opened none of them.
