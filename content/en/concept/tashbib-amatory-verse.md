## Overview
Tashbib is the opening of a poem with amatory lines about women, the same as nasib and ghazal. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5178}

## Ruling
It is forbidden to write verse about a woman who is unlawful to the poet, or about a beardless boy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5178} There is no dispute that describing the lascivious physical or moral features of an unlawful non-mahram woman is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5178}

## One's wife or slave-girl
Verse about one's wife or slave-girl is lawful unless it describes her private parts or what must be concealed; if it does, the poet loses his manliness, and the act is forbidden or disliked, by a disputed view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5178}

## An unnamed woman
Verse about an unnamed woman is lawful unless it is obscene or sets a clue that identifies her; if it does, she is treated as named. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5178} Names such as Layla and Su'ad do not identify anyone, as shown by Ka'b ibn Zuhayr reciting his famous poem before the Messenger. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5178}

## A boy
Verse about a boy is forbidden if it says he is loved, even unnamed; some say an unnamed boy is treated like an unnamed woman. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5179}

## Composing and reciting
Composing in verse or prose is governed by the above; reciting or narrating such verse is lawful when it is not meant to urge the unlawful, for example as evidence or to learn eloquence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5179} The Hanafis limit the prohibition on verse about a woman to a living, identified woman; verse about a dead woman is not forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5179}
