## Definition
Milta is the lexical name for the samhaq, the thin layer over the skull between flesh and bone; in law most jurists take it to be the samhaq, or a wound through the flesh that comes close to the bone. The Malikis separate them: milta removes the flesh and nears the bone without touching it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14999}

## Related terms
For the Malikis the samhaq is the stripping of the skin from the flesh; so milta concerns the flesh and samhaq the skin. For most jurists the two are synonyms. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15000}

## Ruling
Most jurists hold that the milta carries no fixed blood money or fixed compensation, only a just assessment, whether it was intentional or not. The Malikis, a report from Muhammad, and a view among the Shafi'is hold that an intentional one requires retaliation, since its extent can be controlled. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15000}
