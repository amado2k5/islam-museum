## Summary
This record gathers what the books of hadith, biography and history say about the graves of the prophets: what is reported in the hadith, and what historians such as Ibn Kathir give of graves that are known and graves that are only attributed. Every identification of a grave here is attributed to the source that makes it, and the museum does not state any of them in its own voice as authentic; in many places the historian himself points out that there is no sound report for the identification.

## What the hadith establish
**Burial where the prophet dies.** The Sira of Ibn Hisham reports that the Muslims differed over where to bury the Prophet; one said they should bury him in his mosque, another among his Companions, and Abu Bakr said that he had heard the Prophet say that a prophet is not taken but is buried where he is taken; so the bed on which he died was lifted and a grave was dug beneath it. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 672} Ibn Kathir reports that when death came to Adam the angels took him, washed him, shrouded him, perfumed him, dug a niche grave for him and prayed over him, and then told his sons that this is their practice, in a report whose chain he says is sound as far as Ubayy ibn Ka'b. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73}

**The grave of Musa.** Al-Bukhari, in the chapter on Musa's death, reports from Abu Hurayra that the angel of death was sent to Musa and struck him, and that Musa asked God to bring him near the Holy Land, a stone's throw; the Prophet said: "Were I there I would show you his grave beside the road, under the red dune." {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 5} Ibn Hajar, in his commentary, says that Ibn Hibban claimed that Musa's grave is at Madyan between Medina and Bayt al-Maqdis, and that al-Diya' objected that the land of Madyan is not near Medina or Bayt al-Maqdis; he said it is widely known that a grave at Jericho with a red dune beside it is the grave of Musa, and Jericho is in the Holy Land. He quotes al-Suddi that when Musa's death drew near he walked with his servant Yusha' ibn Nun, and Wahb ibn Munabbih that angels saw to his burial and prayed over him and that he lived a hundred and twenty years. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 7} Ibn Kathir reads Musa's request as this: when his death came while he was with his people in the wilderness, he wished to draw near the land to which he had migrated and had urged his people toward. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531}

**The Prophet passing Musa praying in his grave.** Ibn Hajar quotes what Muslim reports from Anas, that the Prophet said: "I passed by Musa on the night I was taken on the night journey, at the red dune, standing praying in his grave." {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 11} Ibn Kathir gives the same hadith from Ahmad and Muslim. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531} In another place Ibn Hajar quotes al-Bayhaqi that what supports the saying that the prophets pray in their graves is what is established in Muslim of the Prophet passing Musa praying in his grave, and that Muslim also reports, from Abu Hurayra, that the Prophet saw a group of prophets standing in prayer. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 81} Ibn Hajar says the prophets are alive with God although in the form of the dead as regards the people of this world; a note in the margin of this edition comments that, if what is meant is a life in the barzakh, it differs from their worldly life and does not remove them from the description of death. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 1 p. 11}

**The warning against taking prophets' graves as places of worship.** Al-Bukhari reports from Aisha that in the illness in which he died the Prophet said: "God cursed the Jews and Christians; they took the graves of their prophets as places of worship", and Aisha said that but for this they would have made his grave prominent, except that she feared it would be taken as a place of worship. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1330} In the Muwatta, in a mursal report from Ata ibn Yasar, the Prophet prayed: "O God, do not make my grave an idol that is worshipped; God's anger is fierce against a people who took the graves of their prophets as places of worship." {cite:muwatta-malik 1.475} It also reports that Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz said that among the last things the Prophet said was that God fight the Jews and Christians who took the graves of their prophets as places of worship. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.470} See [[concept/grave-veneration|Veneration of graves]], which sets out Ibn al-Qayyim's view.

## What historians say of particular graves
**Ibrahim, Ishaq and Yaqub at [[place/al-khalil|Hebron]].** Ibn Kathir reports, from the People of the Book and others, that Ibrahim was buried in the cave he bought at Hebron from Ephron ibn Sakhr the Hittite, where Sara was buried; that Isma'il and Ishaq saw to his burial, that Ishaq was buried beside his father, and that Yusuf carried his father Yaqub, after his death in Egypt, to the same cave. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 237} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 290} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 333} He then says that his grave and the graves of his son Ishaq and his grandson Yaqub are in the "square" built by Sulayman son of Dawud in the town of Hebron, which is the town now known as al-Khalil, and that this is "received by continuous transmission, community after community and generation after generation" from the time of the Israelites to his own, that his grave is in the square; but as to fixing its place within it, he says there is "no sound report from the infallible", and he advises that the place be respected and not trodden in its parts, lest the grave of al-Khalil or of one of his prophet sons lie beneath it. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 238} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 239} The place has its own record, [[building/al-haram-al-ibrahimi|the Ibrahimi Sanctuary]].

**Yusuf.** Ibn Kathir reports that Yusuf, in what "they said", left instructions that he be carried with them when they left Egypt and be buried with his fathers, and that they embalmed him and put him in a coffin, where he stayed in Egypt until Musa took him out with him and buried him with his fathers. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 333} This is the account of the People of the Book, by Ibn Kathir's own wording in this passage, and it is ascribed to them. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 334}

**Adam.** Ibn Kathir says people differed about where Adam is buried: the well-known view is that he was buried by the mountain where he came down in India, and it is also said at Abu Qubays in Mecca; it is said that Nuh carried him and Hawwa in a coffin at the flood and buried them at Bayt al-Maqdis, as Ibn Jarir reports; and from some, that his head is at the mosque of Ibrahim and his feet at the rock of Bayt al-Maqdis. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 73}

**Nuh.** Ibn Jarir and al-Azraqi report, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Sabit or another Successor in a mursal report, that the grave of Nuh is in [[building/masjid-al-haram|the Sacred Mosque]], and Ibn Kathir says this is stronger and better established than what many later authors state, that it is in a town in the Biqa' known as "Karak Nuh". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 121} The museum also has a record of [[building/maqam-nabi-noah-hebron|the shrine of the Prophet Nuh at Hebron]].

**Hud.** Ibn Kathir says that it is related from Ali that he described the grave of Hud in Yemen, that others said it is in Damascus, where in the qibla wall of its mosque there is a place that some people claim is his grave, and he adds "God knows best". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 143}

**Harun.** The report of al-Suddi from Ibn Abbas and Ibn Mas'ud says that God told Musa to bring Harun to a mountain, where they found a tree, a built house and a bed; Harun lay on it and was taken, and then the house was lifted up, the tree went away and the bed was raised to heaven; this account names no grave, and Ibn Kathir says that in part of it there is "something objectionable and strange". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 531} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 532}

**Yahya.** Ibn Kathir quotes Ibn Asakir that the head of Yahya was seen brought out from beneath a corner of the qibla when the mosque of Damascus was built, the skin and hair unchanged. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 677} See [[event/zakariyya-prayer-and-killing-of-yahya|Zakariyya's prayer and the reports of the killing of Yahya]].

**Isa.** Ibn Kathir mentions a report in al-Tirmidhi that Isa will be buried with the Prophet in his house; al-Bukhari says of it "this hadith is not sound with me and is not corroborated", and Ibn Kathir finds the chain of what is reported from Aisha on this unsound. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 760} See [[event/isa-charge-and-parting-night-with-disciples|Isa's charge and counsel]].

**Dawud and Sulayman.** The books on which this record is built do not deal with their graves; for a shrine attributed to Dawud in Jerusalem see [[building/maqam-nabi-dawud|the shrine of the Prophet Dawud]], and the records [[event/death-of-dawud-and-inheritance-of-sulayman|Death of Dawud]] and [[event/death-of-sulayman-the-prophet|Death of Sulayman]].

## The grave of the Prophet Muhammad
The Sira of Ibn Hisham reports that the Prophet was buried in the middle of the night, the night of Wednesday, under the bed on which he had died, that those who went down into his grave were Ali ibn Abi Talib, al-Fadl and Qutham the sons of al-Abbas, and his freedman Shuqran, and that Shuqran put into the grave with him a velvet cloak he used to wear. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 672}

## Scope of this record
This record neither affirms nor denies the authenticity of any identification of a grave. It presents what each source says, attributed to it, together with the weakness, disagreement or strangeness the source itself points out.
