## Summary
Ibn Sina gives practical rules for one who finds the vein hard to open: not to press on with binding and cutting, the state of lean and fat bodies, hiding the pain, what is done when the branches show but not the vein, and washing and the compress {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## When the opening does not succeed
If one wished to open a vessel of the arm and it did not succeed, he should not persist with severe binding and repeated cutting but leave it a day or two; if necessity calls for a cut, it should be above the first cut and not below it. Severe binding brings swelling, and cooling the compress and wetting it with rose water or with cooled water is suitable and fitting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## The lean and the fat
In lean bodies tightening the band becomes a cause of the vessels being emptied and the blood held back from them; in bodies excessively fat the vein hardly shows with a loose band unless it is made tight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## Easing the pain
Some bleeders use skill to hide the pain: they lower the hand because of the tightness of the binding and leave it so for an hour, and some wipe the soft lancet's edge with oil. This, as he has said, lightens the pain and slows closing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## When the branches show but not the vein
If the vessels named do not show in the arm but their branches do, the hand is pressed along the branch by rubbing; if on lifting the rub the blood pours quickly into it and swells it, it is opened, and if not it is not opened {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## Washing and the compress
When washing is wanted, the skin is drawn so as to cover the cut, and washed, then returned to its place, the compress set right and bound. If fat inclines over the face of the cut it must be moved aside gently, and cutting it is not allowed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.
