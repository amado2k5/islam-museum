## Summary
Ibn Sina separates white in the sense of thin and transparent from true white, which is thick, and lists the kinds of true white, mucous, fatty, tallowy and semen-like, and what each shows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.187}.

## Transparent and true white
White urine may be understood in two senses: thin and transparent, or truly white. Transparency is in truth the absence of all colours, so white in the sense of transparent shows cold on the whole and is far from ripening, and if thick it shows phlegm. True white occurs only with thickness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.187}.

## Kinds of true white
One kind is mucous white and shows much raw phlegm; one is fatty white and shows the melting of fats; one is tallowy white and shows phlegm or suppurating sores in the urinary organs. One resembles semen and may be a crisis of phlegmatic swellings and of ailments from vitreous phlegm in the viscera; if it is no crisis but arises at the outset, it only warns of apoplexy or palsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.187}.
