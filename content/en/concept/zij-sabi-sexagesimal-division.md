## Summary
Al-Battani defines division as knowing how many multiples of the lesser the greater holds when the greater is counted by the lesser, and what part the lesser is of the greater; he sets down that degree divided by degree gives degree, that dividing a lower order by a higher gives the order which, multiplied by the divisor, returns the dividend, and that dividing a higher order by a lower requires extending the higher to the lower and then dividing, which yields degrees. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.15-16}.

## Dividing lower by higher
When a lower order is divided by a higher one, adjacent or not, the quotient falls in the order which, multiplied into the divisor's order, returns the order of the dividend. So seconds divided by minutes give minutes, and sixths divided by fourths give seconds. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.15}.

## Dividing higher by lower
When a higher order is divided by a lower, the way is to extend the higher order down to the lower and then divide by it, and the quotient is degrees. Minutes divided by sixths, for example, are first extended to sixths and divided by those sixths, giving degrees; and whenever an order is divided by its like, degrees come out. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.15}.

## Using the two tables for division
Al-Battani says that whoever wants what comes of dividing lower fractional orders by higher ones should look for the order he wants to divide in the table described before, run out beside it until it faces the higher order in the other table, and the order he reaches is what the division yields. For higher over lower, he extends the dividend to the lower order, looks in one of the tables for the order the extended quantity becomes, and the result is degrees. So the two multiplication tables serve two jobs, finding the order of a product and of a quotient. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.15-16}.
