## Children
Epilepsy strikes children often, on account of their moistures: it may appear in them as soon as they are born, or it may come after they are grown a little; if their regimen is treated it departs, otherwise it stays, and effort must be made to remove it from them before the teeth come in. Furthest of children from it is the one who gets ulcers and swellings in the region of his head and has running nostrils; for the brain has in its first constitution a moisture that is bound to break out — it may break out in the womb or after birth, and if it does not break out epilepsy is unavoidable {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.121} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.122}

## Youth and the elderly
Most epilepsy that strikes children may find its treatment light and departs at puberty, unless a bad temperament and neglect of treatment help it; epilepsy may also strike youths, and if it comes often after the age of twenty-five for a lesion in the brain — especially in its substance — it becomes fixed and does not leave, and the utmost that treatment can do for them is to soften its recurrence and slow its attacks; Hippocrates said that epilepsy stays with them until they die. As for the elderly, obstructive epilepsy seldom strikes them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.122}

## Temperaments and lands
Epilepsy befalls those moist by age, like infants and children, those moist by regimen, like people of indigestions, and those who live in lands of the south wind, because it fills the head and contracts the vessels less {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.122}
