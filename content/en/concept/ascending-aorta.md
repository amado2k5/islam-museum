## Summary
In the third chapter Ibn Sina says the ascending part of the aorta divides in two; the larger heads for the base of the neck, turns to the right and divides in three, among them the two carotids, and the smaller goes to the armpit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.85}.

## The larger part
It rises toward the gum, the word as printed here, probably meant as al-labba, the throat-hollow, as on p.87; it then turns to the right until it reaches the soft, mulberry-shaped flesh there, the thymus gland by the editor's note, and divides in three: two are the carotid arteries, rising right and left with the deep jugular veins; the third spreads in the breastbone, the first short ribs, the six upper vertebrae of the neck and the region of the collarbone until it reaches the head of the shoulder, then passes on to the organs of the arms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.85}.

## The smaller part
The smaller of the two parts of the ascending aorta goes toward the armpit and divides as the third of the larger part does {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.85}.
