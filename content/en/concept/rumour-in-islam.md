## Definition of Rumor

A rumor, as the book defines it, is a report that spreads without being verified, usually intended to leave a negative impact on people's minds and to sow turmoil and distrust among individuals and communities {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 10}. The author observes that nations vary in how readily they believe rumors: heedless nations lacking critical scrutiny of news are quicker to believe fabrications, whereas a nation that verifies its news has more wise people among it {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 10}.

## Who First Spread It

The book states that false rumors have existed since the dawn of humanity, spread by adversaries to weaken those they oppose or inflict defeat upon them, and suggests that the first to do so was Iblis when he tempted Adam {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 11}. In this regard, the book presents what Iblis said to Adam—that the tree was forbidden only out of dislike that the one who ate from it would become an angel or immortal—and treats Adam's belief of this claim as an example of the harm caused by believing falsehood without scrutiny {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 13, 15}.

## In the History of Messengers and Nations

The book traces the accusations leveled at the prophets by their peoples—such as misguidance, madness, folly, and corruption—and classifies these as rumors intended to turn people away from the truth and arising from envy of the messengers for what God had granted them {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 7–8}. It traces what was rumored about Noah, Hud, Salih, and Moses, as well as about the Prophet Muhammad, the Quran, and the Last Day, based on the verses that recount them {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 22–23, 33, 37, 70, 137, 163}.

In our time, the author believes rumors are even more harmful due to the speed of their spread, as modern means of communication have turned the world into a single city {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 8}.

## Psychological Warfare

The book asserts that rumor has become what is called psychological warfare in our day, and that many states have used it as a weapon to plant fear and defeatism in the hearts of their adversaries {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 184}. Among its examples are the Mongols under Genghis Khan, who sent merchant caravans to spread word that their army was unstoppable, and dispatched spies, informants, and terrifying letters to heads of state prior to invasion {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 185}.

The book recounts that Hulagu sent Sultan Qutuz a threatening letter, whereupon Qutuz executed its bearers and hung their heads, refusing to believe the rumor it contained aimed at Egyptian morale {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 185–186}. The book concludes that Qutuz's refusal to believe those rumors resulted in victory at [[event/ayn-jalut-658|Ain Jalut]], whereas believing them would have reversed the outcome of the battle {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 186–187}.

By contrast, the book presents—citing Muhammad Abdallah Inan—a rumor that spread in the Muslim army at [[event/balat-al-shuhada|the Battle of Tours (Balat al-Shuhada)]] that the spoils camp was about to fall into enemy hands, causing many horsemen to withdraw from fighting and disorder to break out in the ranks {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 187–188}.

## The Incident of the Slander (Hadithat al-Ifk)

The book presents the Incident of the Slander as one of the most heinous rumors, spread by the hypocrites—headed by Abdallah ibn Ubayy—against Aisha, Mother of the Believers; as the book recounts, Aisha fell ill in Medina for a month while people were talking about it {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 117}. It holds that the aim of spreading it was to harm the Prophet's standing and cast doubt on his prophethood, on the truthful daughter of Abu Bakr, and on Islam {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 122}. The Quran detailed the affair and commanded the believers to combat such fabrications {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 122}. (See [[event/incident-of-the-slander|The Incident of the Slander]] and [[passage/quran-24-11|Surah Al-Nur]].)

## Verifying News

The book holds that the most effective means of combating rumors are verifying the truth of what is said and heard, referring matters back to their reliable sources, asking scholars about what is unclear, concealing a rumor rather than repeating it, and maintaining good faith among members of society {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 9}.

The basis for this, in the author's view, is [[passage/quran-49-6|Surah Al-Hujurat, verse 6]], which commands verifying the report of a corrupt person lest one harm a people out of ignorance and come to regret what was done {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 189–191}. The book notes that Quranic commentators reported an occasion of revelation for it concerning the dispatch of Al-Walid ibn Uqbah to Banu al-Mustaliq to collect zakat: thinking they intended to attack him, he turned back and reported that they had refused zakat, though it later became clear that this was not the case {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 192}.

The book cites the hadith of Usamah ibn Zayd regarding the killing of one who said "There is no god but God," to show that people are to be judged by their outward profession until the contrary is proven, and that judgments based on rumors and conjecture will be held to account against the community {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 190–191}.

The book adds sayings attributed to the Prophet, to Ali ibn Abi Talib warning against haste in believing a talebearer, and to Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz on adjudication {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 194–195}. It holds that verification, deliberation, and clarifying matters before passing judgment protect a person from losing friends and multiplying enemies {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 195}.

## Note on Chains of Transmission and Grading

In these passages, the book does not mention a chain of transmission (isnad) or a grading for the sayings attributed to the Prophet, Ali, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz; therefore, no grading not stated by the book should be attributed to it, and these reports should be checked in their original sources before being used for a ruling {cite:al-ishaat-al-kadhiba-azhar p. 194–195}.
