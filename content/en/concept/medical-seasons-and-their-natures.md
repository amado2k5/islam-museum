## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that the seasons of the physicians differ from those of the astronomers: spring is the time that in temperate lands needs no notable warming or ventilation and its temperament is the moderate, summer is hot and dry, winter cold and moist, and autumn near moderate in heat but not in moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.114-117}.

## The limits of the seasons
The four seasons for the astronomers are the times of the sun's passage through quarter after quarter of the zodiac, beginning at the vernal point. For physicians spring is the time that in temperate lands needs no notable warming against cold or ventilation against heat, in which trees begin to grow, lasting from the vernal equinox, or a little before or after it, until the sun is in the middle of Taurus, and autumn is its counterpart, though in other lands spring may come early and autumn late. Summer is the whole hot time and winter the whole cold, so that spring and autumn are, for the discerning, each shorter than summer and winter. Spring is likely to be the time of flowers and the start of fruit, and autumn the time the leaves change colour and begin to fall {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.114}.

## Why summer is hot
The temperament of spring is the moderate, not hot and moist as is thought. The real cause of summer's heat is that the fall of the sun's rays is partly like the cone of an arrow and partly like the circumference or near it, and its force is strongest at the arrow's point, since the effect comes to it from all sides; in summer we stand at the point or near it, and in winter near the circumference, which is why light is brighter in summer though the distance from us to the sun near its apogee is greater. The ratio of near and far is shown in the astronomical part of the mathematical branch of philosophy, and the proof that heat grows with light in the natural part {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.114-115}.

## Summer, winter and autumn
Summer, though hot, is also dry, because moistures dissolve in it from the great heat, because the air's substance is rarefied and like the fiery nature, and because little dew and rain falls in it; winter is cold and moist for the opposite reasons. In autumn the heat has declined and the cold is not yet firm, so it is near moderate in heat and cold but not in moisture and dryness, since the sun has dried the air and the moistening causes have not yet come to balance its drying; cooling is not like moistening, for change to cold is easy and change to moisture is not {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.115}.

## Spring against autumn
Spring does not stay in winter's moisture as autumn stays in summer's dryness, for spring's moisture is moderated by heat in a time in which autumn's dryness is not moderated by cold. Moistening and drying here are like possession and its lack and not like two opposites: drying is the loss of moist substance and moistening the gain of it. By moist air he means air mixed with thick watery vapours, and by dry air air from which the watery vapours mixed in it have dispersed, or which has become akin to fire by rarefaction, or in which earthy smokes are mixed. In spring more dissolves than vaporises, since vaporising is done by heat with a little fine moisture on the surface of the air and by strong heat hidden in the earth; in winter the inside of the earth is very hot and the heat of the air little, so both causes of sublimation meet {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.115-116}.

## The noons of autumn
Whoever does not judge autumn very moderate in heat and cold is not far from the truth, for its noons are like summer, the autumn air being very dry and quite ready to take heat, while its nights and mornings are cold because of the sun's distance from the zenith and the quick yielding of fine rarefied air to what cools. Spring is nearer moderate in both qualities, its night not far from its day. If asked why an autumn night is colder than a spring night though its air is finer, the answer is that very rarefied air takes heat and cold faster, as very rarefied water that has been heated and then exposed to freezing sets faster than cold water, because the cooling passes through it; and that bodies do not feel spring's cold as they feel autumn's, because in spring they are moving from cold to heat and are used to cold, and in autumn the reverse, autumn heading toward winter and spring leaving it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.116-117}.

## Diseases of the seasons
The change of seasons stirs a certain kind of disease in each region, and the physician must learn it region by region so that his precautions and forward planning rest on it; a single day may also resemble some seasons and not others, warming and cooling in one day {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.117}.
