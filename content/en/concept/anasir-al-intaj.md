## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat presents the factors of production in the book as three material and spiritual entities that participate in production: land and labor are two primary factors, while capital and the organizer are two secondary factors {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 55}.

## The Spiritual Motive
Before discussing the material factors, the author places the spiritual motive for production. In his view, production stems from three motives: obeying the Creator's command to strive and work; strengthening the individual's position and raising his standard by satisfying his needs without begging anyone; and elevating the standing of the community and the dignity of the nation among nations {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 48}. This contrasts with the capitalist motive of profit and the communist motive of the power of the collective and the state {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 47}.

## Land
In his view, land abounds in raw materials and is the basic infrastructure of production, which Western colonialism sought out and plundered {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 49}. See also [[concept/al-mawarid-al-tabiiyya|Natural Resources]].

## Labor
The author considers human labor the principal factor in production, taking three forms: manual, such as work on the land, in construction, and in mines; intellectual, such as the work of the engineer, teacher, and doctor; and mechanical, which enters as an aid to both {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 52}. Productive efficiency has developed through specialization and continuous training on every new machine {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 53}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 54}. See [[concept/al-saay-fi-al-ard|Striving in the Land]].

## Capital and the Organizer
The author states that capital is necessary for production, but derives from labor and land, and that placing it at the head of the factors amounts to buying consciences and marks the beginning of capitalism and exploitation {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 55}. It is better for it to be treated as a partner in production rather than a primary factor {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 56}. The organizer is a human being who deals with the given inputs just as an engineer or a doctor does, and must not turn into an authority that commands and forbids as if he were the Lord {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 55}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 56}.

## The Author's View and Presentation of Modernity
The author presents this division as his view of the ranking, arguing that modern economics added factors to land and labor, and that this addition distorted the production process {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 55}. The text of modern economics on the factors of production is not presented here, so it needs to be supplemented.

## From the book of Zaydan Qa'dat: pp. 83–164 of the PDF file (Factors of Production in the Context of the Book)
Qa'dat holds that the production process requires three elements: the Creator, who provides the moral incentive for work; the human being, who deals with the elements of existence with justice and balance; and subjugated matter, ready to be shaped {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 113}. In his view, the factor of labor is divided into manual, intellectual, and technical work, and is evaluated so that each person receives in proportion to his work {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 101}.

## From the book Usul al-Iqtisad al-Islami: Part Two (pp. 101–200 of the PDF file)
The author divides the factors of production into independent ones—land, labor, and capital—and dependent ones—risk and time; neither of the dependent factors produces on its own, but rather joins labor, capital, or land, thereby increasing the share of the independent factor {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 103} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 116}.
