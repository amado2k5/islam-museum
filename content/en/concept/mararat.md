## Nature and selection
The strongest of the galls of quadrupeds is the ox gall, then the gazelle and bear, then the goat, then the sheep. The safest of the bird galls is that of the cock, the francolin and the partridge, and the other bird galls are stronger than those of quadrupeds. The very strong and stinging galls are those of the birds of prey, especially the large ones; the chosen colour is a natural yellow, while verdigris-green or lapis-blue is bad, and so is a bright red. The weakest gall is that of the pig. A method of keeping the gall, from Dioscorides, is not copied here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.560} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Temperament
All of them hot and dry in the fourth degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Actions and properties
All galls are hot and cleansing, and differ with the male and the female and with the state of thirst, hunger, satiety, rest and exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Cosmetic uses
The gall of the wild ass removes warts of the mulberry kind and helps as a paint on the marks of swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Swellings and pustules
It goes into ointments for erysipelas and prevents it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Gall mixed with natron, resin and Cimolian earth helps ulcerated scab; ox gall goes into ointments that guard wounds other than erysipelas and severe pains; the gall of the he-goat removes warty flesh; and the gall of the wolf is good for nerve wounds and in cold weather prevents the feared spasm and tetanus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Joints
The gall of the he-goat laid on elephantiasis and varicose veins helps, and so does that of the wild ass; the gall of the wolf prevents the spasm and tetanus that follow nerve wounds, especially from cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Head, ears and teeth
The gall of the he-goat and the ox is for fresh ulcers in the ears; vulture gall in oil is dropped into a heavy ear, and with the juice of Nabataean leek for ringing and hardness of hearing; ox gall with natron and Cimolian earth is for scurf, washing the head with it. It has been said that bear gall, licked, helps epilepsy, and tortoise gall is said to help malignant thrush in the mouths of children; snuffing it helps the epileptic, and all galls help the nostril and strongly open the obstructions of the ethmoid {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Eyes
All galls help dimness of sight. The gall of birds of prey, especially dried, helps the beginning of cataract and dispersion, and must only be used after the body and head are purified. The most useful for the eye is the gazelle among quadrupeds, the partridge among birds, and the shabbut among fish; goat gall, especially the mountain goat, helps the film {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Chest and breathing
Ox gall is rubbed on the palate with honey for quinsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561}.

## Excretory organs
Ox gall opens the mouths of the veins of piles. Every gall is a loosener, even pig gall if the navel is rubbed with it or it is used as a suppository. Ox gall with honey is a paint for ulcers of the anus, liniments for the womb and testicles are made from it, and it is laid on swellings of the scrotum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.561} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Poisons and bites
The gall of mountain goats is an antidote for one bitten, and so is ox gall {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.
