## Summary
Al-Battani gives two ways of finding the share of a degree in the difference of the day, that is, the gap between its rising in the place and its rising in the right sphere: the first by the chord of the right-sphere risings and the chord of half the excess of the longest day, the second by the chord of the place's latitude and the chord of the degree's declination. The result is then subtracted from the right-sphere risings if the declination is northern and added to them if it is southern. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.43}.

## The first and the second way
He says: to know how much of the equator rises with the given parts of the ecliptic in any country you want, take from the first of Aries to the degree you want, by the risings of the right sphere, and find the chord of that; multiply it by the chord of half the excess of the longest day in that country, divide by the radius, and take the arc of the result; the arc is the share, between the first of Aries and that degree, of the difference of the day in a quadrant of the circle. Another way: multiply the chord of the place's latitude by the chord of the degree's declination, divide by the chord of the complement of the latitude, multiply the result by the radius and divide by the chord of the complement of the degree's declination, then take the arc; the arc is the difference of the day in a quadrant of the sphere. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.43}.

## The direction of the declination, adding or subtracting
Then he says: once you know this share, by whichever way you like, look: if the degree's declination is northern, subtract the share that came out from the times of the risings between the first of Aries and that degree in the right sphere; if the degree's declination is southern, add the share to those risings. The risings, after the addition or subtraction, are the risings between the first of Aries and that degree in that country. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.43}.
