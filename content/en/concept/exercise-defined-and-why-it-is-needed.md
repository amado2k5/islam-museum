## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the second lesson of Fann 3, on the common regimen of adults in seventeen chapters, with chapter 1 on exercise in general. He defines it as a voluntary movement that forces large, frequent breathing, and puts it first in the keeping of health, before food and sleep {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.221}.

## Order and definition
Since the chief part of the regimen for keeping health is that a person exercise, then govern food, then sleep, he says exercise must come first. Exercise is a voluntary movement that forces large and frequent breathing, and whoever is granted its use in due measure and at its time needs no treatment for the material diseases or the diseases of temperament that follow them, provided the rest of the regimen is fitting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.221}.

## Why the body needs it
He explains that we are bound to need food, that no food turns wholly into nourishment in act but leaves a residue at every digestion, and that nature strives to expel it yet cannot do so fully alone, so a trace is left from each digestion and, repeated, gathers into a sizeable amount {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.221}.
