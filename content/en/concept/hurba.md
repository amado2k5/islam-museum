## Nature
The book gives it another name of Greek origin, left out here. It is a three-cornered seed like a spear, with a three-cornered leaf like that of another plant the book names in Greek, also left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.488}

## Temperament
The garden kind has little heat; the wild kind is hot in the second degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.488}

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: its fresh herb heals wounds. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.488}
Organs of digestion: its husk with vinegar on the spleen, and its dried leaf drunk, is said to cure the spleen. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.488}
Labelled listing, set aside for the founder's ruling: the book says it promotes flow, especially its leaf, and does not say whether urine or menses is meant. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.488}
