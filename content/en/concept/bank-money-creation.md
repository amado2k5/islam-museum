## Summary
In the author's view, commercial banks compete with the state in issuing money: the state issues base paper and metallic currency, while banks create credit bank money or deposit money {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}.

## Fractional Reserve
If banks kept deposits without lending them out, no bank money would be created and they would hold a full reserve, but in reality they hold a fractional reserve and lend out what exceeds it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}. The banking system can multiply deposits and bank money, lending out multiples of its capital and the deposits it has received {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}.

## The Danger in His View
If banks do not observe technical rules in granting credit and are not supervised by the central bank, the danger lies in excessive expansion in creating money and credit {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}. He observes that in recent years banks have turned toward encouraging consumption, credit cards, and personal loans, and that this, combined with lax oversight, was among the main causes of the crisis {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}.
