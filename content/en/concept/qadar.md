## Summary
Al-Tahawi says: God created the creatures by His knowledge, decreed decrees for them, set their terms; nothing was hidden from Him before He created them; He commanded them obedience and forbade them disobedience {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 10} {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 11}.

## Will and judgement
"Everything proceeds by His determination and will, and His will is done"; "the servants have no will except what He wills for them" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 11}. He guides whom He wills and leads astray whom He wills — guidance "by favour", leading astray "by justice" — and all move within His will between His favour and His justice {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 11}. Then he says everything proceeds by His will, knowledge, judgement and decree; His will overcomes all wills and His judgement overcomes all devices {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 27}.

## Knowledge and writing
God knew the number of those entering Paradise and the number entering the Fire in one count — "that number is neither increased nor decreased" — and likewise their deeds {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 16}; "each is eased to what he was created for, and deeds are by their endings" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 16}. He believes in "the Tablet and the Pen and all that has been inscribed in it", and says if all creatures joined to annul what God wrote they could not {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 18}.

## The prohibition on delving
He holds the root of the decree is "God's secret in His creation — no angel brought near nor prophet sent has perceived it"; delving into it is "the road to being forsaken and the stair to deprivation"; knowledge is two — a knowledge present among creatures and a knowledge absent — and faith stands only by accepting the present and abandoning the search for the absent {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 17} {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 18}. For the servant's act see [[concept/kasb|kasb and capacity]].

## In Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad
Ibn Qudama says God is "the Doer of what He wills — nothing is except by His will, and nothing departs His will"; He "created the creatures and their deeds, decreed their provisions and terms, guides whom He wills by His mercy and leads astray whom He wills by His wisdom" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 24}. Then: "We do not make God's judgement and decree a plea for abandoning His commands and avoiding His prohibitions"; God commanded and forbade only the able, compelled no one to disobedience nor forced him to abandon obedience {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 26}. He argues from a verse that "the servant has a deed and an acquisition, requited for its good with reward and its evil with punishment — occurring by God's decree and determination" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 27}.

## In Ibn Taymiyya's al-'Aqida al-Wasitiyya
Ibn Taymiyya says: "Belief in the decree is in two degrees, each containing two things" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 21}: the first, God's knowledge of what the creatures will do "by His eternal knowledge", then His writing the creatures' measures in the Preserved Tablet; the second, "God's operative will and encompassing power" — belief "that what God wills is, and what He does not will is not" and that there is no creator but Him {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 22}. Then: "The servants act in truth and God is the creator of their deeds"; "the servants have power over their deeds and have will, and God is their creator and the creator of their power and their will" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 22}.

## In al-Ash'ari's Maqalat al-Islamiyyin
In "the account of the sum of the saying of the people of hadith and sunna" in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin, Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari presents what the people of hadith and sunna hold, ending: "And in all we have mentioned of their saying we speak, and to it we go" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 350}. He relates there that they say: "There is no good or evil on earth except what God wills" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 345}; that they "affirmed there is no creator but God, that God creates the servants' evil deeds, that God creates the servants' deeds, and that the servants cannot create anything of them" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 346}. They say "good and evil are by God's judgement and decree", believing in His judgement and decree — its good and evil, sweet and bitter {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 346}; that God "granted the believers success to His obedience and forsook the unbelievers" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 346}; and that whoever dies dies by his term — and likewise whoever is killed {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 349}.

## What is not covered here
This entry presents the positions of the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya, Ibn Qudama's Lum'at al-I'tiqad, Ibn Taymiyya's al-Wasitiyya, and what al-Ash'ari related in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin of the people of hadith and sunna and declared his own — each attributed to its book. The sayings of the Maturidis and the remaining Sunni and non-Sunni schools from their own books have not yet been added; the entry needs specialist review before promotion.

## For Scholars
Al-Tahawi says: God created creation knowing, decreed their decrees, set their terms, nothing hidden from Him before He made them, and commands obedience while forbidding disobedience. 'All runs by His decree and will, and His will is done'; the servants have no will but what He wills for them. He guides whom He wills and leaves whom He wills — guidance 'in grace', misguidance 'in justice', all turning between His grace and justice; all runs by His will, knowledge, judgement and decree, His will defeating all wills and His judgement all devices. God knew the number entering Paradise and the number entering the Fire in one count 'neither increased nor decreased', and their deeds likewise; 'each is eased to what he was made for, deeds are by endings'. He believes in 'the Tablet and Pen and all inscribed'; were creation to gather to undo what God wrote they could not. The root of qadar is 'God's secret in His creation; no near angel nor sent prophet has seen it'; delving is 'a path to abandonment' — knowledge is of two kinds, present and lost, and faith holds by accepting the present and not seeking the lost. Ibn Qudama: 'the Doer of what He wills' and 'we do not take God's decree as our plea to leave His commands'. Ibn Taymiyya: two degrees — God's knowledge of what creation does and His writing it in the Tablet, then His effecting will and encompassing power — 'the servants act truly and God creates their acts'. Al-Ash'ari reports of the hadith people: 'nothing of good or evil befalls but what God wills' and 'God creates the servants' acts'.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy tells the tale of a king who lost a finger, whose minister said 'perhaps it is for the best' and was jailed for it; later the king was captured by idol-worshippers who meant to sacrifice him, but finding a finger missing they let him go, since an offering must be whole, and he understood that losing it had been good for him. He tells it to explain the maxim that the intelligent man looks at what God will do with him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep14 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep14 00:28:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep14 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep14 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep14 00:29:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh holds that a person has free choice in the deeds for which he is accountable, and is directed in what he is not accountable for, such as the time of his birth and death; he gives the example of the hand that can reach out with charity or with a weapon, and the tongue used for good or for abuse, and cites that no one forced Abu Lahab to disbelieve. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:04:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh holds that decrees do not run according to man's planning, giving the example of someone who wants mild weather all year although some fruits ripen only in heat, and says: 'plan not to plan, choose not to choose; your Lord creates what He wills and chooses'. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:05:30} He holds that people are guests in the house of the Generous, and a guest does not carry the worry of hospitality, giving the example of being invited to break the fast with a billionaire. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:06:30} He counts old age a relief rather than a hardship, since the mind matures and worship increases, digressing to criticise cosmetic surgery for a woman who wants to look thirty while her husband is seventy; this is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:05:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
## Caution does not avert decree
The Sheikh holds that caution does not avert decree and that the slain man dies at his appointed term, not by the killer's act; he relates an incident he says he saw with his own eyes more than forty years ago at a railway crossing before Giza station: a goods van stalled on the tracks as a train approached; people pulled the driver out and stood him well away; the train hit the van, one of its tyres flew into the air and came down on the head of the driver himself among the crowd, and he died (needs_source: his account of what he witnessed). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:04:00} He holds that whoever is given understanding in deprivation finds deprivation turned into a gift, giving Musa's mother and Ibrahim, who left his family in a barren valley, as examples. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:07:30}

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya'
In [[work/el-hadara-el-nabaouia|al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] explains the Prophet's answer on coitus interruptus, that there is no soul but has a book in God's knowledge, and the hadith that a child does not come from all the fluid and that when God wills to create something nothing prevents it. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:01:00} God knows, he says, before the creation of the heavens and the earth, who of Adam's children will be created, with their heredity, their role, their misery or happiness, their term and their provision, and the choice of the one sperm among millions that fertilises a given egg is made only by His will. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep27 00:07:30} He also takes the hadith that every soul has a book in God's knowledge to mean that the hereditary store in Adam's loins is distributed among humankind by God's knowledge until the Hour. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep22 00:01:30}
