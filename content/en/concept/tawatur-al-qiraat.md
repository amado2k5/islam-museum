## Summary
In the science of readings, mass transmission means that a reading is carried by a multitude whose collusion on a lie is inconceivable, from their like. Two questions branch from it: is mass transmission a condition for accepting a reading, and is everything reported from the seven readers mass-transmitted? {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 23} See [[question/is-tawatur-a-condition-of-a-valid-reading|Must a reading be mass-transmitted?]]

## What Ibn al-Jazari says
Ibn al-Jazari answers those who made mass transmission the condition of the pillar of transmission that once mass transmission is established the other two pillars are not needed: whatever is established as mass-transmitted from the Prophet must be accepted and held to be Qur'an, whether it agrees with the script or not; and if mass transmission were required for every variant, many variants established from the seven and others would fall away. He says he himself once leaned to that view before its flaw appeared to him. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 23}

## Abu Shama and al-Ja'bari
Ibn al-Jazari relays from Abu Shama that it had become common among later readers to say the seven readings are all mass-transmitted, meaning every single item reported from these imams; Abu Shama says he holds mass transmission for what all the paths agreed in reporting from them and all groups agreed on without objection, being widespread and spread abroad, and that at the least this is to be required where mass transmission is not complete in some. For al-Ja'bari the condition is one, soundness of transmission, and the other two follow from it. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 23}

## Ibn al-Hajib and manners of delivery
Ibn al-Jazari cites Ibn al-Hajib's statement that the seven are mass-transmitted except in what is of the nature of delivery, such as lengthening, imala and easing the hamza. Elsewhere he says he knows of none before Ibn al-Hajib who took delivery out of mass transmission, that the masters of legal theory such as al-Qadi Abu Bakr stated all of it to be mass-transmitted, and that this is correct 'because if the wording's mass transmission is established, so is that of its manner of delivery, since the wording stands only by it'. Al-Suyuti adds, quoting al-Zarkashi, that every kind of hamza easing is mass-transmitted. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 40} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 524}

## The hadith of the ahruf
Al-Suyuti says the hadith of the seven ahruf was narrated by twenty-one Companions and Abu Ubayd held it mass-transmitted. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 308} That is the mass transmission of a hadith, not of a particular reading; the question about the readings is which of their variants was mass-transmitted, which the two questions above take up. See [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa|the seven ahruf]].

## Note
These views are given to their holders as Ibn al-Jazari and al-Suyuti relate them; I did not read the works of al-Ja'bari, Ibn al-Hajib or Abu Shama themselves, so I attribute no more to them than is quoted.
