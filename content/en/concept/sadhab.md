## Nature
The book reports from Dioscorides a garden kind, a wild kind and a mountain kind; the mountain is sharper and more pungent than the garden and is not eaten in food, the one growing by a fig tree suits best {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.598}; the seed is what is used, ripening in autumn {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Selection
The garden rue that suits best is the one growing by a fig tree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree, the dried hot and dry in the third, and the dried wild hot and dry in the fourth, as is said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Actions and properties
Cutting, dissolving, greatly dispersing, cleansing the vessels, blistering and astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Cosmetics
With natron it is used on white vitiligo, warts and mulberry-like warts, it removes the smell of garlic and onion, and helps alopecia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Swellings and pustules
The wild kind, pounded and laid with salt on a limb, raises a hot swelling on it, and laid on scrofula of the throat and armpit it dissolves it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It is laid on ringworm, with vinegar and white lead on spreading sores and erysipelas, and it heals old ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Joints
It helps palsy, sciatica and pains of the joints, drunk and as a poultice with honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Organs of the head
A poultice of it with barley meal is for chronic headache; with vinegar it may be snuffed into the nose for nosebleed, which it stops; its warmed juice dropped in the ear cleans it, calms pain, tinnitus and ringing, and kills and brings out worms from the ear; and it is smeared on scalp ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Organs of the eye
It sharpens sight, especially its juice with the juice of fennel and honey, as a collyrium and in food; with barley meal it is laid on throbbing of the eye, and it helps weakness of sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Organs of the chest
A decoction of the fresh with dry dill is useful for chest pain and difficulty of breath, as Rufus attests, and it helps pains of the lung and side, cough and pain of the ribs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
A poultice of it with figs is for fleshy dropsy and wine-skin dropsy; it eases digestion, whets the appetite, strengthens the stomach, helps the spleen and calms phlegmatic hiccup {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}.

## Excretory organs
Its two kinds restrain the bowels, it calms griping, is given with oil as an enema for the pains of colic, is laid with honey on ulcers of the anus, is drunk for worms, and both kinds empty the body's residues by promoting urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}. With bay leaf it is laid on the testicles for their swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.600}.

## Fevers
Eating it and rubbing with its oil help shivering {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.600}.

## Poisons and bites
It resists poisons, especially taken with figs and walnuts, and eating much of the wild kind is deadly, as the book warns {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.600}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it dries and cuts the semen and removes desire for intercourse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.599}. It also lists that, kneaded with honey and applied to the woman's private part, it helps the pain that comes from suffocation of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.600}.
