## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi holds that dividing an inheritance spreads wealth "in a flowing way" among sons and daughters so that it circulates in society, unlike a forced dispersal that surprises children with deprivation. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2049}

## Effort and its fruit
He links a father's labour on the land with his children's right to its fruit, and says God placed in a father the tenderness that moves him to work for his children. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2049}

## Flowing and forced dispersal
He gives the example of a man who owns a hundred feddans and has sons and daughters: after his death the estate passes to them gradually, and each prepares for what he receives. A forced dispersal leaves them unprepared. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2050}

## Resentment
He warns that resentment must not appear in society, because when it does it undoes every good, and he says the rules of inheritance were set to preserve a dignified life for the weak and for women. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2051}
