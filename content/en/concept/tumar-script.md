## Summary
Al-Qalqashandi counts the tumar as the first of the pens used in the chancery. He explains that tumar means the full-size sheet, which in his day was called al-farkha, that the pen is named for writing on it, and that it is a majestic pen whose width the scribes reckon at twenty-four hairs of a pack-horse. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 53}

## In signatures and letters
He relates that caliphs wrote their signs in it in Umayyad days and after, and tells a report that 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, offered a tumar to write on, refused because it wasted paper from the Muslims' treasury; al-Qalqashandi thinks the pen is among the arrangements Mu'awiya ordered, and says the writing of the rulers of Egypt was settled in it from al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun to his own time. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 53} Quoting the Minhaj al-Isaba, he adds that its reed is cut from the pith of the green palm rib. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 53}

## The other pens measured against it
Al-Qalqashandi quotes, through the Minhaj al-Isaba, an account attributed to Ibn Muqla: Kufic writing has two poles for its fourteen manners, the tumar, which is wholly extended with nothing round in it, and ghubar al-hilya, which is wholly round with nothing straight, and 'very often the old Medinan mushafs were written' in the tumar; the other pens take different proportions of the straight and the round. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 52} Another group of scribes reckon the pens against the tumar by width: the thuluth is a third of it, eight hairs, the nisf twelve and the thuluthayn eighteen. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 52} Al-Nahhas says the finest large hand ended with al-Dahhak and Ishaq ibn Hammad, and al-Qalqashandi suggests he means the tumar or something close to it. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 16}

## The shortened tumar
Next comes mukhtasar al-tumar, used for the full Baghdadi sheet; its width falls between the thuluthayn and the full tumar, eighteen to twenty-four hairs, and the chancery scribes wrote in it the investiture charters issued for the caliphs and the letters to the great qa'ans among the kings of the East. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 59}
