## Its meaning for al-Tabari
Al-Tabari states that the people of interpretation are unanimous that "al-sirat al-mustaqim" is the clear road with no crookedness in it, and that this is its meaning in the language of all the Arabs {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 170}. He supports this with a line of Jarir ("the commander of the believers is upon a sirat", meaning a road of truth) and a line of Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 170}, then a rajaz verse, and says the evidence is more than can be counted {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}.

## Metaphor in Arabic usage
The Arabs borrow the word "sirat" for any saying or action described as straight or crooked, so that the straight is called straight and the crooked crooked {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}.

## The reading al-Tabari prefers
He holds that the best interpretation of "guide us to the straight path" is: grant us steadfastness upon what You have approved, and what You granted to those You blessed among Your servants, in word and deed. Whoever is granted what the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous were granted has been granted Islam, belief in the messengers, holding fast to the Book, acting on what God commanded and refraining from what He forbade, and following the way of the Prophet and the way of Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali. All of that is the straight path {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}.

## Differences among the interpreters
Al-Tabari says that the interpreters of the Qur'an differ over what the straight path means, and that the reading he chose includes the meanings of all of them {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}. The reported views themselves are later in his commentary and are not yet recorded in this entry.

## Its relation to the verse after it
On "the path of those You have blessed", al-Tabari explains that this phrase is a clarification of, and a substitution for, "the straight path": the straight path is the path of those upon whom God bestowed favour {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 170}. See also [[concept/al-fatiha-umm-al-kitab|al-Fatiha]] and [[concept/al-basmala|al-Basmala]].

## In al-Tabari's commentary: what the reports identify as the sirat
Al-Tabari sets out the reports that identify the sirat. From Ali, relayed to the Prophet, it is the Qur'an {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}; from Ibn Mas'ud, the Book of God {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 173}; from Ibn Abbas through Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak, the guiding way, the religion of God that has no crookedness {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 179}; from Jabir, Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd, Islam; from Nawwas ibn Sam'an, a hadith that God struck a parable of a straight path, the path being Islam {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 176}; from Abu al-'Aliya, the Prophet and his two companions after him, Abu Bakr and 'Umar {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 175}. The editor grades several of these chains as very weak, and treats the hadith of Nawwas as an abridgement of a longer report he holds sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 177}
Al-Tabari's preferred meaning includes all of these together: Islam, belief in the messengers, holding fast to the Book, acting on what God commanded, refraining from what He forbade, and following the Prophet's method. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}

## In al-Tabari's commentary: "ghayr al-maghdub" in grammar and sense
Al-Tabari sets out two ways to read "ghayr" in "ghayr al-maghdub 'alayhim": as an adjective of "those upon whom You have bestowed favour", or as a repetition of "sirat", and he holds the two close in meaning, since whoever God has favoured with guidance is safe from anger and from error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 181} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 182}
Following the Kufans, he holds that "wa-la al-dallin" is coordinated with a preceding negation, so "ghayr" carries the sense of negation and not of exception, and the meaning is: guide us to the path of those You have favoured, not of those who have incurred anger or who are astray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 184} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 191} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 192}
He cites [[passage/quran-4-69|al-Nisa' 69]] to identify those favoured as the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous, and holds that obedience is reached only through God's favour and grant of success. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 177} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 178} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 179}

## In al-Tabari's commentary: "nor of those who are astray"
Al-Tabari identifies "those who have incurred anger" as the Jews and "those who are astray" as the Christians, citing [[passage/quran-5-77|al-Ma'ida 77]] and reports from Adi ibn Hatim, Abu Hurayra and Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 189} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 193} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 194} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 195}
He answers the Qadariyya, who argued that because the Fatiha ascribes straying to the Christians and not God's causing them to stray, the servant strays of his own accord, by noting that Arabs attribute an act to the one who performs it even when its cause lies elsewhere, and by citing [[passage/quran-45-23|al-Jathiya 23]], where God is the one who misleads and guides. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 197}
