## Nature and selection
Azfar al-tib is pieces like fingernails, sweet-smelling and aromatic, used in fumigation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. The book reports Dioscorides as saying that they are of the kind of the tips of shells, taken from an island in the Indian sea where spikenard grows, and that there are a Qulzumi kind and a Babili kind, black and small, both with a good aromatic smell {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. The editor's footnote glosses them as a family of the large snails that have aromatic lids {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. The best leans to white and comes to al-Qulzum, the Yemen and Bahrain, while the Babili is black and very small; the perfumers said that the best is the sea kind, then the Meccan kind from Jedda, and some of it reaches 'Abbadan {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. The book says that it is treated, cleaned and perfumed, and the way this is done is not reproduced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}.

## Temperament and actions
It is hot and dry in the second degree, its dryness almost reaching the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. It refines {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}.

## Uses by organ
Its smoke helps epilepsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. When it is drunk with vinegar it moves the bowel, whichever kind it is {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that its incense rouses one who has suffocation of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation.
