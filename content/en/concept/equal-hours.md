## Summary
Equal hours are hours of always-equal length, counted by dividing the day with its night into twenty-four hours, each hour being fifteen degrees of the zodiac's course and a little more {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 88}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 87}.

## Day and night
With the astronomers the day begins at its night, from midday to the next midday, because the horizon's difference changes with the climes while the meridian is one in all climes {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 87}. The length of each day with its night is the zodiac's whole course, three hundred and sixty degrees, plus the sun's mean risings in a day and night {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 87}.

## Definition
The day arc is what the sphere turns from the sun's rising to its setting, and the night arc from its setting to its rising; the two are nearly parallel at the equinoctial {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 87}. Dividing the day arc by fifteen gives the number of equal hours of the day, and likewise of the night; what remains of the twenty-four is what the day's hours fall short of {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 86}.

- **The author (al-Farghani):** the equal hour is one twenty-fourth of the day's course with its night, so it does not vary between summer and winter {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 86}.
