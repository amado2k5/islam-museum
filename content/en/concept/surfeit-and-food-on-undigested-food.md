## Summary
Ibn Sina says the most harmful thing for the body is to add food to food not yet ripened and digested; surfeit from coarse foods brings joint and kidney pain, asthma and tight breath, and from light foods acute malignant fevers and acute bad swellings; yet sometimes one food is added to another so as to act like a remedy for it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.228-229}.

## The worst harm
The most harmful thing is to put food on top of food that has not ripened and been digested, and nothing is worse than surfeit, especially from bad foods {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## What surfeit brings
When surfeit comes from coarse foods it brings pain in the joints and kidneys, asthma and tightness of breath; when it comes from light foods it brings acute malignant fevers and acute bad swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.228-229}.

## Food as a remedy for food
At times one needs to add a food, or something like food, to a food so that it acts as its medicine: those who eat pungent and salty foods and, before digestion is complete, follow them with moist, bland foods set right the chyme of what they ate, and this regimen suffices them without exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.
