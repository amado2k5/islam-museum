## Water inside the skull
Watery moistures may gather inside and outside the skull; if inside the skull, with its place above the hard membrane, a heaviness within is felt and the closing of the eye becomes hard so that it cannot be done, the eye grows very moist, waters constantly and stares fixedly, and there is no remedy in the like of it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.86}

## Outer swellings and water outside the skull
Hot and cold swellings may come in the membranes outside the head, and there may come, especially to infants, a gathering of water in the head, which may come to adults too; these are watery moistures held between the skull and the skin or between the two outer membranes, and a sinking comes in that place of the head, with crying and wakefulness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.86} If the colour of the skin is as it was and it is raised, yielding to pressure and pushed back, it is water in the head; if the colour is changed and the touch differs, and there is firmness and resistance to being pushed, or a stinging and pain is felt, it is a swelling from outside the skull {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.86} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87}

## Hot swellings and the treatment
As for the hot swellings, their hot and cold are known by touch and colour and by what agrees with what reaches them, and in all of them a pain pressing on the skull is felt, and when you touch it you meet the pain; it is treated more lightly than sarsam, though in using strong means you are safe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87} Where the water is much and is pushed from outside to inside when overcome, it is not treated; for the little that is held between skin and skull he describes a surgical act and a local treatment, which are not reproduced here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87} He takes up likewise the sneezing of infants and says diarrhoea in this illness is bad; its remedies and what the nurse is given are not reproduced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.87}

## Birth (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Ibn Sina says this gathering of water comes to infants, for the most part, when the midwife errs and presses the head and splits it, opens the mouths of the vessels, and watery blood runs under the skin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.86}
