## Summary
Muhaqqaq is one of the [[concept/al-aqlam-al-sitta|six pens]], paired with [[concept/rayhan-script|rayhan]], its smaller variant. Maryam Ekhtiar notes that with [[concept/thuluth-script|thuluth]] it suited large Qur'ans. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 25} {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 26}

## The Met's example
The Met holds the colophon of the [[object/anonymous-baghdad-quran|"Anonymous Baghdad Qur'an"]], written in muhaqqaq in 707 AH/1307–8 CE by [[person/ahmad-al-suhrawardi|Ahmad ibn al-Suhrawardi al-Bakri]], pupil of [[person/yaqut-al-mustasimi|Yaqut al-Musta'simi]]; it is a large thirty-part Qur'an made at Baghdad in the Ilkhanid period. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 37}

## In Ibn al-Nadim and al-Qalqashandi
Ibn al-Nadim says people went on writing in the manner of the old script until the start of the Abbasid dynasty; when the Hashimites appeared, the mushafs were given these scripts, and a script called the Iraqi arose, which is the muhaqqaq, also called warraqi, and it kept growing and improving until the time of al-Ma'mun. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 24} He lists the muhaqqaq among the branches of the Riyasi pen. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 25} Al-Qalqashandi counts it among the eight pens of the chancery, says its writing was introduced for the tughras of the letters to the qa'ans, and that the 'way of the muhaqqaq' leans toward extension in its letters, where thuluth leans toward rounding. {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 52} {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 54} {cite:subh-al-asha-cairo-v3 p. 62}
