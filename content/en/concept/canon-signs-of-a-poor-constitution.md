## Summary
In the fifth chapter Ibn Sina describes the person whose constitution is not good, one whose organs do not share a like temperament, and ties the unevenness of build to poor understanding and intellect {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.

## Who he means
He means the person whose organs do not resemble one another in temperament; the principal organs pull against each other, one leaving balance towards one temperament and another towards its opposite. When the build is disproportionate it is poor even in understanding and mind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.

## Examples
He gives the man with a large belly, short fingers and round face, the one whose head is very large or very small, the one whose face is strongly mismatched, and the one with a round head and brow but a very long face, thick neck and dullness in the eyes, whom he counts among those furthest from good. This is Ibn Sina's judgement in this chapter, not the museum's {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.
