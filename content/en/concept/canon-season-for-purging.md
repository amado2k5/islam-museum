## Summary
Ibn Sina says the rising of Sirius, snow on the mountains and bitter cold are not the time for the drug, so it is taken in spring or autumn, a gentle one in spring and a strong one in autumn, that one needing a purgative in winter should watch for the south wind, and that nature should not be accustomed to a drug whenever it needs softening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.282}.

## The seasons
The time when Sirius rises, when snow falls on the mountains and in severe cold is not a time for the drug, so the drug is drunk in spring or autumn. Spring is a time that summer follows, so only a gentle drug is taken in it; autumn is a time that winter follows, so it can bear a strong one. Nature should not be accustomed to taking a drug whenever it needs softening, for that becomes a habit and brings its owner into a troublesome and unwholesome occupation. One who needs a purgative in winter should watch for the south wind, and for summer he said the reverse, with more detail given elsewhere. If a sick person needs a gentle purgative and it does not act, he is not stirred further but left {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.282}.
