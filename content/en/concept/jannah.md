## Overview
Jannah, in Sunni creed, is the abode of reward in the hereafter prepared by God for the believers; at the foundation of belief it is a created, already-existing realm — created before mankind, with people made for it, and whoever enters it does so by God's grace {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §106-107}.

## In the Tahawiyya creed
Article 106 of al-Aqida al-Tahawiyya states: "Paradise and the Fire are created; they never perish and never vanish," and continues: "God created Paradise and the Fire before creation and created people for them — whoever He wills among them to Paradise by His grace, and whoever He wills to the Fire by His justice" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §106-107}. Ibn Abi al-Izz's commentary elaborates these articles under belief in the hereafter {cite:sharh-tahawiyya-ibn-abi-al-izz}.

## In modern scholarship
Christian Lange's academic study presents paradise as a pair with the Fire whose imagery developed across the Qur'an, hadith and Sufi and philosophical literature — from gardens, rivers and chambers to spiritual and symbolic readings {cite:lange-paradise-hell-2016}.

## Paradise in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episodes 6 and 7)
The Sheikh says the believers' first joy in Paradise is the assurance that they will not be removed from it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:23:00} He says that in Paradise no one is pursued for debts, no bad news arrives, and nothing disturbs. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:27:00} He describes no scorching heat or bitter cold, no idle talk or sin, and angels entering to them from every gate. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:28:00}
He holds that there is no grief for the past and no fear for the future there. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:31:00} He says worldly feelings differ from those of the Hereafter, and gives examples from the stories of Noah's son and of Abraham and his father (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:33:00}
He relates a report on food and drink: a person eats in Paradise without hunger, since hunger is a pain that does not exist there, and sweat smells of musk (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:07:30} He explains that the food and drink of Paradise cost nothing, and that its people say words of glorification, and that the service there is without wage (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:10:00}
He says that bliss there is a gift and not a wage, and gives the example that ten years of worship count for nothing before grace (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:46:30}
He relates a report that the lowest person in Paradise has a kingdom like the kingdom of the world (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:41:00} He relates that the people of Paradise keep fixed ages, and that children who died young remain young (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:40:30}
He gives his view that the garden in which Adam was placed was not the eternal Paradise (see the other views below). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:36:00}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 1)

The Sheikh relates the hadith in which the Prophet says that no one enters Paradise by his deeds; the man asked, 'Not even you?', and he replied that not even he would, unless God alone enveloped him in His mercy {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:25:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 5)

The Sheikh affirms that entering Paradise is purely a grace from God, in which deeds have no part, and he cites the Prophet's saying that no one enters Paradise by his deeds {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:01:30}. He relates that the Prophet was asked, 'Not even you?', and answered that not even he would enter except through God's mercy; he adds that deeds apportion the ranks in Paradise, and that the emotions of the afterlife will differ from those of this world {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:02:00}. In his own comparison the Sheikh likens the levels of Paradise to the classes of air travel, from economy through business and first class to a private jet, and he holds that Paradise has ranks with God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:03:30}. He says that a father and mother may be at a higher rank in the highest Firdaus, and notes that the Prophet instructed people to ask God for the highest Firdaus {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:08:00}.

The Sheikh makes the point with an everyday comparison: a person feels no shame asking a billionaire or a central bank for a large sum, and so the servant asks his Lord for the highest Paradise {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:08:30}. He says that a family who gathered upon the Book and the Sunna in this world will not be denied that gathering by God in Paradise, and he cites a verse on joining believing offspring to their forebears {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:09:00}. He says that God does not bring the higher-ranked person down to the level of the lower, but raises the lower to the higher, and the Sheikh counts this among God's generosity {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:09:30}. He says that the love that existed between parents and children in this world is what God uses to reunite them in the afterlife, and that the emotions of this world differ from those of the people of Paradise {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:10:00}.

The Sheikh then turns to the question of relatives who did not believe alongside their prophets {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:12:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 6)

The Sheikh says that the first sip a person takes from the Kawthar pool removes malice, resentment and regret from him; he says people in this world live by wealth and glory, and calls this a miserable triangle {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:24:00}. He says that after drinking from the pool no envy remains, and then asks what there is in this world that does not trouble you {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:26:00}. The Sheikh lists everyday worries of this world, such as the demands of the household and the child's school, and says Paradise is free of them {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:26:30}. He contrasts someone who flips through channels and imagines a world where people live in peace with Paradise, which he stresses is a reality and not a dream {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:27:30}.

He says that worldly sentiment does not exist in Paradise, and he asks about the case of Ibrahim and his father Azar, and of the Prophet with Abu Lahab and Abu Talib; he notes that Durra, daughter of Abu Lahab, became Muslim and was taunted over her mother Umm Jamil {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:33:30}. He says there is no idle talk in Paradise, nor sin, gossip, plotting or conflict, unlike this world which is all conflict; he relates that Uthman was asked when rest comes and answered that it is when you see your left foot beside your right foot inside the threshold of Paradise {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:34:30}. He relates that an old woman asked the Prophet to be his companion in Paradise; he told her there are no old women in Paradise, she wept, and he reminded her that the people of Paradise are all thirty-three years old (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:36:30}. He says the people of Paradise will know one another and chat together in happiness, and then the Sheikh begins a joke about a preacher in an Arab country who is giving a sermon about the houris {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:37:00}.

The Sheikh is of the view that many household quarrels with the in-laws arise because the circle of peace is missing, and he says Paradise offers peace and a full cup {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:40:00}. He says that a racehorse trained for speed runs between the start and the end of one of the gates of Paradise for forty years (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:41:30}. He describes an invitation card written in letters of light, saying that God tells His servant that He has missed him and asks him to visit, and he says all this bliss is for an hour's patience (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:42:30}. The Sheikh takes up the question of an everlasting reward for finite deeds, saying it is the height of God's grace, because God knows from a servant's intention that, had he lived forever, he would have worshipped forever, and so He grants him bliss forever {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:47:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:47:30}.

:::perspectives
- **Sunni view (not yet sourced)**: The Sunni position on this question has not yet been sourced.
- **The speaker's view (opinion)**: He holds that God knows the sincerity of a servant's intention, and that the believer who, had he lived his whole life, would have loved goodness, treated his wife well, not consumed other people's rights and eaten only what is lawful, is rewarded everlastingly in keeping with the sincerity of his intention {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:47:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 7)

The Sheikh says the sensory delights mentioned in the Qur'an can only be understood by worldly measures, and he cites the Prophet's saying that this world shares only names with the next, adding that if the realities of the afterlife were described exactly as they are, we would not understand them {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:02:00}. He gives an illustration: in 1957, he says, people in the countryside and the desert did not believe that the dog Laika and then Gagarin had gone up in a spacecraft and took it for madness, to show that people reject what exceeds their experience (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:03:30}. He says the Prophet used to speak to people in terms they could understand, and mentions that the Qur'an describes the fruit of Paradise as resembling the fruit of this world {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:04:00}. He says hunger is a pain, and that Paradise has no such feeling, so a person eats without hunger and feels no indigestion {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:06:30}.

The Sheikh holds that the next creation is prepared for this marvellous food, and he approaches it through the example of astronauts for whom special meals are prepared {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:08:00}. He says an astronaut takes a capsule every four hours and is satisfied, and asks: if that is so under the laws of this world, what of what God has promised? (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:08:30}. He is of the view that Paradise differs entirely in its terms from the mechanics of this world, and that food and drink drain a large part of a household's budget {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:09:00}. He says that what a person spends on food over twenty-five years, if gathered, would make him a billionaire, and that a person's money comes only through toil, whereas in Paradise there is no toil (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:09:30}.

He contrasts this world, where a driver, a cook or a servant costs money, with the servants of Paradise, who he says come at no cost {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:10:30}. He says the lowest person in Paradise has a kingdom a thousand years in extent, and adds that God knows best, perhaps it is a thousand light years (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:11:30}. He says that Adam dwelt in Paradise, and that the true believer among the children of Adam returns to the home he came from, the dwelling his father was in {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:38:30}. He cites Ibn al-Qayyim's well-known poem, which calls toward the gardens of Eden as the believer's first dwelling, and then moves on to the scene of Jibril and the angels standing in ranks {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:39:00}.

## In Ahmad Farid's lessons 'The Boat'
In [[work/the-boat-ahmad-farid|'The Boat']] [[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] calls paradise the great fruit of faith and good works, a home God created by His hand for those He loves; its soil, he says, is musk and saffron and its roof is the Throne of the Merciful, and he recalls the sacred hadith of what no eye has seen, no ear has heard and no heart has imagined. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep08 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep08 00:04:30} In the last lessons he calls the pious the rightful heirs of paradise and contrasts a worldly win, a car or a flat, and a stay in a five-star hotel or a beach chalet, with the triumph and 'good return' promised to them. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep20 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep20 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep20 00:03:00} Paradise, he says, is brought near to the pious to welcome them, they are driven to it in groups, and he ends with the hadith that whoever asks God for paradise should ask for al-Firdaws, its middle and highest part, whose roof is the Throne. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep21 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep21 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep21 00:04:00}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the first episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] opens his series by describing Paradise itself before turning to those who were promised it, and he says that they, men and women, number in the hundreds {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:02:00}. Among its names he lists the Abode of Peace, the Abode of Bliss, the Abode of Eternity, the Seat of Truth, the Footing of Truth and the Gardens of Eden {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:02:30}. He cites the hadith qudsi in which God tells that He has prepared for His righteous servants a bliss beyond anything eyes have seen, ears have heard or hearts have imagined; he says al-Bukhari and Muslim both relate it, and that the Prophet linked it to a verse about delights kept hidden for the believers to gladden their eyes {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:04:30}. In his view its bliss surpasses imagination and dreams "billions of billions of times" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:05:00}.

He describes its gates as eight, of pure gold, each with a ring of red ruby, and jokes with his listeners that this is no 18-, 21- or 24-carat gold {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:06:00}. He attributes to the two Sahihs that among its eight gates is one called al-Rayyan, entered only by those who fast {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:06:30}, that the span between the two leaves of a gate is a forty-year journey, and that a day will come when it is crowded with people {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:07:00}. He relates from Sahih Muslim, through [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], that on the Day of Resurrection the Prophet will come to the gate of Paradise and ask for it to be opened; the keeper asks who he is and, hearing his name, tells him he was ordered to open it for no one before him {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:08:00}. Through [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], and attributing it to the two Sahihs, he gives the description of the first group to enter Paradise: faces like the moon on the night it is full, those after them like the brightest of stars, nothing impure leaving their bodies, combs of gold and sweat of musk, wives from among the [[concept/hur-al-ayn|hur al-'in]], hearts as the heart of one man, in the form of [[person/adam|Adam]], sixty cubits tall {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:09:00}. He says that at its gates are two springs: the people of Paradise drink from one and the rancour leaves their hearts, and bathe in the other and their faces shine; he attributes this to [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] and links it to the verse about removing rancour from their breasts {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:10:30}. A herald, he says, announces to them health, life, youth and bliss that never end {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:11:30}.

He describes its trees: trunks of gold, leaves like the ears of elephants, fruit that grows back whenever one is picked, softer than butter and sweeter than honey, and branches that bend down to the hand {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:13:00}. He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari, through Anas, that in Paradise is a tree in whose shade a rider travels a hundred years without crossing it, and he names it Tuba {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:13:30}; and he attributes to al-Tirmidhi, graded hasan sahih as he says, that every tree in Paradise has a trunk of gold {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:14:30}. He lists its rivers, [[concept/al-kawthar|al-Kawthar]], a river of milk, a river of wine, a river of pure honey and a river of water that never turns stale, and among its springs Salsabil, Tasnim, ginger and camphor {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:14:30}. He describes al-Kawthar as whiter than milk, colder than snow and sweeter than honey, with domes of pearl along its banks and mud of musk, and links it to [[passage/quran-108-1|Surat al-Kawthar]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:15:30}; and he says the wine of Paradise brings no headache and takes away no one's mind, being only a delight to those who drink it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:15:30}.

He says the food of its people is the flesh of fowl and every kind of fruit, that the branch bends down to the mouth of whoever desires it, and he glosses one tree that the verse names as bananas layered one upon another {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:17:30}. He attributes to al-Tirmidhi and Ahmad that the Prophet, asked about how Paradise is built, described a brick of silver and a brick of gold, mortar of musk, pebbles of pearl and ruby and soil of saffron, and said that whoever enters it never despairs or dies, his clothes never wear out and his youth never fades {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:19:00}. Its people's garments, he says, are of silk and kinds of brocade, and their ornaments bracelets of gold and pearl {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:19:30}. He relates from Ahmad's Musnad, through [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn Umar]], that a Bedouin asked the Prophet whether the garments of Paradise are created or woven; some of those present laughed, the Prophet rebuked them for laughing at an unlearned man asking one who knows, and then told him that the fruits of Paradise split open to yield them {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:20:30}. Their servants, he says, are youths who never age, like scattered pearls {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:21:30}. He describes the women of Paradise as purified of every impurity and blemish, virgins, loving towards their husbands and all of one age, and he glosses the verse's word for them as skill in words of love to the husband {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:21:30}. He says that were one of them to look upon this world her light would outshine the sun and the moon, were her veil to fall it would fill the world with fragrance, were she to spit into the salt sea it would turn sweet, and that they sing to their husbands {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:22:30}.

Among its joys, he holds, is the company of the prophets: the believer sits with [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]], with [[person/ibrahim|Abraham]] the Friend and [[person/musa|Moses]] who spoke with God, and with [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:23:00}. He states that its greatest bliss is looking upon the face of God, citing the verse that describes radiant faces looking towards their Lord (see [[concept/ruyat-allah|the vision of God in the hereafter]]) {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:23:30}. He relates from Sahih Muslim that a herald tells the people of Paradise of an appointment with God; they recount the favours He has given them, then a light shines out, their Lord greets them and tells them it is the Day of Increase, they ask to see His face and the veils are lifted {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:23:30}. He ends the episode with the teaching that a person is gathered with those he loves, hoping his listeners will be gathered with those promised Paradise through their love of them {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep01 00:25:30}.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On [[passage/quran-2-25|verse 25]], al-Tabari explains the gardens as orchards, meaning their trees and fruit rather than the ground, and reports from Masruq that Paradise's date palms are heaped with fruit from root to branch, the fruit like large jars, with water flowing without a channel. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 384} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 385}
