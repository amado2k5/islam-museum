## Nature
The nut of this tree is finer than the pistachio, with a thin shell, brittle and red, and it splits into a long, white, oily and pleasant kernel. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.490}
The large kind is from the pine called sus; the small kind is a three-cornered nut with a harder shell and a sharper kernel, with pungency and astringency. The small kind is nearer a medicine than food. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.490}

## Temperament
The large kind is like the moderate, leaning to heat, and adds moisture; the small kind is hot and dry in the second degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.490}

## Actions
It ripens, softens, dissolves and stings, especially when fresh; the sting goes if it is soaked in water, and then its softening is complete. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.490}

## Uses by organ
Joints: the large kind helps slackness and weakness of the body when eaten, and dries the corrupt humours in it. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.490}
Organs of the chest: the large and small kinds help putrid moisture, pus, bleeding and cough of the lung, especially with a sweet drink to clean lung pus. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.490}
Adornment: it fattens. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.490}
