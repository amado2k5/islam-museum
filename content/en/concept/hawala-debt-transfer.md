## Summary
Hawala lexically is transfer; technically it is the transfer of a debt from one liability to another by offer and acceptance between two of three parties: the creditor, the debtor and the one who undertakes payment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7336} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7337} The transferor is the debtor, the transferee the creditor, the transferee-debtor the one who undertakes payment, and the debt is the object. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7337} Hawala differs from surety in that it releases the transferor's liability, and from release in that it moves a right rather than dropping it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7337} It is lawful by the saying 'delay by a rich man is wrong, and when one of you is referred to a solvent man let him follow the reference', by consensus and by analogy with surety. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7337} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7338} On the command to accept, Ibn al-Humam takes it as permission, and a creditor known for good payment may be followed as recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7339} On the nature of the contract, the Hanafis, Hanbalis and the sounder Shafii view treat hawala as an exception to the sale of debt for debt; others call it a sale; Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim deny that it runs against analogy, and the Hanbalis treat it as an act of accommodation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7339} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7340} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7341} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7342} Hawala is divided into restricted and unrestricted: restricted when tied to a debt the transferor is owed by the transferee, or to a deposit or a secured object in the transferee's hands; unrestricted when tied to nothing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7343} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7344} The unrestricted form is either immediate or deferred, and a deferred one may be fixed by stipulation or by the terms of the original debt, with some rules borrowed from surety. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7345} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7346} Most jurists hold that hawala cannot be made on a specific object; they require the transferred sum to be a debt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7347} Pillars are five on the majority view (formula, transferor, transferee, transferee-debtor, the debt), while the Hanafis make the formula alone the pillar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7347} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7348} Any words conveying the sense are valid; the Malikis require the word hawala in Khalil's view, the Shafiis hesitate over the word sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7349} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7350} The transferor's consent is required by the Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis, and the Hanafis require it in one report; the transferee's consent is not required by the majority but is by the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7350} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7351} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7352} If the parties dispute whether the act was a hawala or an agency to collect, the Hanafis and Shafiis have set rules about who is believed, depending on the words used. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7352} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7353} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7354} When the transferor claims the act was agency for collection and the transferee denies it, the jurists give rules on who is believed and on the money received, whether it is still held, consumed or lost. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7355} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7356} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7357} The Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis have the hawala concluded by the transferor's offer and the transferee's acceptance in the session of knowledge; the Hanafis require offer and acceptance in one session. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7357} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7358} A timed hawala, or one deferred to an unknown term, is invalid, while a deferred debt with a known term is valid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7359} The transferor must be sane and competent; some jurists allow a man under insolvency decree to make one under two conditions; the majority require the transferor to be a debtor to the transferee. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7359} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7360} The transferee must be sane; the Hanafis also require the transferee-debtor to be adult and able to pay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7360} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7361} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7362} The article continues beyond p.7362.
