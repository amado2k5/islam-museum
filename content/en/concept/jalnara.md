## Nature and selection
It is the flower of the wild pomegranate, Persian or Egyptian, and may be red, white or rose-coloured; its juice is like the juice of the goat's beard in nature {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}. Bulus says its power is like that of pomegranate fat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}. Its substitutes are oak galls or pomegranate caps {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: cold at the end of the first degree and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}. Actions: astringent and stops every flow; it produces black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.

## Uses by organ
Adornment: good for bleeding gums {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.
Wounds and ulcers: heals wounds and ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.
Joints: made into a plaster for the neck {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.
Head: strengthens loose teeth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.
Chest: stops spitting of blood greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.
Excretory organs: binds, and helps intestinal ulcers and the flow and bleeding of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423} [book listing].
