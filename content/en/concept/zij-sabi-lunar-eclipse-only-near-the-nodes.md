## Summary
At the start of chapter forty-three al-Battani holds that a lunar eclipse can happen at opposition only when the mean position of the Earth, that is the Earth's shadow, lies within the eclipse limits drawn in the pages of the months of conjunctions and oppositions. If the Moon exceeds those limits or falls short of them, no eclipse can happen. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.146}.

## The nodes and their nearness
He says the eclipse is complete when the Moon is at the head or the tail of the node; if its distance from either node is more than a certain number of degrees ahead or behind, no eclipse can happen, and if less it happens in proportion to nearness. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.146}.
