## Summary
Dhamm is the opposite of praise; in the rulings it is harm done to another by speech, such as slander, abuse and insult, and it carries the hadd and discretionary penalties. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8478}
Blaming God or His Messenger is among the gravest injuries and amounts to disbelief, and cruel speech against believing men and women is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8479}

## Mockery and nicknames
The law forbids mockery, sneering, and calling people by insulting nicknames. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8479} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8480}
The jurists allow a nickname that describes a trait the person cannot help and does not resent, such as lame or hunchbacked. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8480}

## Blaming innovators, sins and unbelievers
Blaming innovators and innovations is sought in the law. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8480} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8481}
The Qur'an blames sins, disbelievers and hypocrites; the idols of unbelievers may not be cursed if that leads them to curse God. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8481}
