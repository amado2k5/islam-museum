## Summary
In the lexicon, asaba are a man's sons and his paternal relatives, who inherit from him. As a term they are heirs with no fixed share agreed upon, who take what remains after the sharers take theirs, or the whole estate if there are no sharers {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11639} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11640}.

## Against sharers and distant kin
The sharers (ashab al-furud) have a fixed share in the Book of God; the distant kin (dhawu al-arham) are relatives with neither a fixed share nor agnatic standing {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11640}.

## Kinds of agnate heir
The agnate in his own right, such as a son; the agnate through another, such as daughters with their brothers; and the agnate with another, such as sisters with daughters. They take what the fixed shares leave, and the whole estate if alone {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11641}.

## Other rules
Agnates take priority in washing and praying over the dead, in the guardianship of marriage, and in custody when no woman entitled to it is available; they share the blood money for manslaughter with the paternal group, the Shafi'is excluding the ascendant and descendant {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11640} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11641}.
