## Overview
The humours are treated in the fourth lesson of the Canon's first book, in two chapters — the first on the humours' nature and kinds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.28}. For Ibn Sina the sound humoral moistures fall into four genera: blood (the best), phlegm, yellow bile and black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.28}; among the corrupt phlegms by taste: the salty, the sour, the astringent and the stale {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.30}. The second chapter shows how the humours are generated — temperate heat makes blood, excessive heat yellow bile, cold phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.33} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.35}.
