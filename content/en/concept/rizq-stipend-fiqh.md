## Summary
Rizq in the lexicon is provision, given in this world or the next; in the jurists' usage it is what is fixed from the treasury by need and sufficiency, monthly or daily, and it is distinguished from 'ata' which is set yearly by a person's service and merit in religion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8725} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8726} Judges, agents and governors may be paid from the treasury; al-Qarafi holds that such pay may be cut, reduced, increased or changed as the public interest requires. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8726} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8727} The imam should keep a register of the paid fighters, give each what he needs, put the Quraysh first, and enter no child, madman, woman or weak man unfit for war; he pays once a year and may pay monthly, and gives the dead man's wife and young children enough. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8728} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8729} Those the imam supports are three groups: the needy; those who uphold the pillars of religion, such as judges, rulers and muftis, who must be relieved so that they can devote themselves to it; and those given a share of the treasury for their standing, such as the Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8729} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8730}
