# Secularism and Islam — conflict or concord

## The episode and the votes
The fifth 'Al-Wasatiyya' episode posed the question of secularism and Islam: necessary conflict or possible concord? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:01:00} The studio youth voted: 43% could define secularism, 57% could not; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:06:00} on its spread in the Arab world: 24% weak, 42% medium; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:27:00} on whether it can meet Islam: 65% judged it impossible, a third found a formula possible {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:45:30}.

## The definition — a spectrum, not a single bar
Definitions graded across the panel: for Ramadan, 'using scientific reason to manage life at both its private and public levels' — science may even serve religion within a frame; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:08:30} for Muhammad Saad, citing Western dictionaries: not adopting religion as the reference for education and morals (Oxford), working for this world not the next (Britannica); {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:09:30} for Omar al-Shobaki: separating religion from the public sphere, born in conflict with the Church's authority, not religion itself — so it cannot be counted inherently atheist; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:12:00} for Ammar Ali Hassan two levels: partial (religion separated from politics and economics) and total (from all of life); {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:13:00} and for Muhammad Jamal Hishma the founding definition — transfer of authority from religious to civil institutions — plus the Turkish AKP's own written definition: religious freedom, civil authority, pluralism {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:14:30}.

## Three models — France, the Anglo-Saxon, Turkey
Al-Shobaki detailed that secularism is not one shape: the French (laïcité) is harshest — it separates public institutions from religion completely, down to the cross on a school blackboard, and the state neither funds religious institutions nor exempts them from tax since the 1905 law; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:16:30} the Anglo-Saxon is softer — permits personal religious display (the hijab in a state school) but bars state adoption of religious content; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:18:00} and early Turkey was severest — the mosque imam removed turban and robe outside his mosque {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:20:00}. The only common thread across all models: separating religion from politics — and even that was never fully achieved at their extreme; the West never managed to remove religion altogether {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 00:20:30}.

## The closing question — tolerant secular or despotic religious rule
The panelists rejected the host's binary: 'the question is loaded' — what is wanted is an Islamic rule that accepts the other and affirms freedom and pluralism; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:14:00} one said ordinary people want a system providing food, clothing and medicine before elite debates; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:15:00} another said despotism is rejected whatever its dress; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:17:30} one chose a secular rule that respects religions over a religious rule imposing one opinion {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:16:00}.

## The host's conclusion — centrist Islam dispenses with it
Al-Suwaidan closed: what all the models share — electing the ruler and the people's power to remove him, respecting freedom — 'is a deeply authentic part of Islam'; Islam was distorted by intellectual claims and historical applications, while centrist Islam comfortably absorbs what the West needed secularism for — 'centrist Islam dispenses with secularism' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep05 01:20:00}.
