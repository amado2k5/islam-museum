## Summary
Al-Battani watches the rod's shadow early in the day, long and shortening until it reaches the circumference, and marks that spot; then watches it after noon, lengthening until it reaches the circumference again, and marks the second spot. He bisects the arc between the marks and joins the midpoint to the centre by a line carried to the other side of the circle: this is the meridian line for good. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.38-39}.

## The two marks of the shadow tip
He says: watch the shadow early in the day while it is long; it keeps shortening until it reaches the circle's circumference and is about to enter, and there you mark a point on the circumference as the tip of the shadow. Then follow the shadow until it passes the middle of the day and begins to lengthen, and when it reaches the circumference and is about to leave, mark the tip's place on the circumference with a second point. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.38}.

## Bisecting the arc and drawing the line
Then he says: divide the arc between the two points into two halves and mark a point at the middle of the arc; from this point draw a straight line passing through the circle's centre to the opposite side of the circumference. The way to do it is to lay the edge of a ruler on the arc's midpoint and on the centre and draw a line through both to the circumference on the other side. Whenever the shadow of the rod at the centre falls on this line, it is the time of midday, whether the shadow is long or short; and this line is the direction between south and north. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.39}.
