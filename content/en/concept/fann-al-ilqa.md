## Summary
"The art of public speaking" (fann al-ilqa') is the skill that [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] teaches in two lessons of [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the Leaders' Academy]], right after [[concept/fann-al-talif|the art of authorship]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:02:00} He says he normally gives this course over five days, five hours a day, and that the academy has only two hours for it. For more detail he points to his book on public speaking and to its video album, which he advises hearing four or five times. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:03:00} The rules, models and percentages below are his teaching and his figures. Where he relies on studies he does not name them.

## The outstanding speaker
He opens with the qualities of the outstanding speaker. They include knowledge, and preparation, which he repeats three times, with respect for the audience. He adds language, a clear goal, and confidence without arrogance. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:09:00} He says the young men of his Ruwwad centre once listed eighteen differences between the arrogant and the self-confident. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:09:30} Further qualities are truthfulness, awareness of the audience, good listening, and being an example of what one teaches. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:14:30}

## The "five Ps" model
He calls his framework "al-Suwaidan's model of public speaking". He says he tried to make the initials of its parts spell a word and could not, so it became five words beginning with P: planning, preparation, pattern, presentation and performance evaluation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:18:00} Planning means knowing the audience and the venue. He advises visiting the hall at least two hours early. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:21:30} It also means knowing one's goals: the general goal, the specific goal the audience wants, and any hidden goal. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:26:00} One should never train for money. He says he once turned down six well-paid courses for an oil company because he had not refreshed the subject. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:23:00}

Every speech, he says, falls into one of three kinds: informative, persuasive, or for an occasion. Persuasion works through the speaker's credibility, emotion and logic. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:34:00} For ordering the body of a talk he offers several patterns: by number or letter, cause and effect, problem and solution, importance, place and time. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:35:30} Statistics should be recent. He quips that a figure from 1997 is already ten years old. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:44:30} For timing he allows about two minutes per slide, one idea per slide, and blocks of about fifteen minutes. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:47:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep05 00:48:00}

## Slides, fear and the shape of a talk
A speech should not be read, he says, except where the words carry legal weight. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:01:30} He gives rules for slides:
- the screen can be read from a distance of eight times its height;
- avoid red text, which he says four per cent of people cannot see;
- at most five lines of five words, better three by three;
- one idea per slide;
- always have a backup ready.
{cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:09:00} He calls fear of speaking natural, saying 71 per cent of people are terrified of it, and gives practical remedies for trembling, sweating and eye contact. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:14:30}

The introduction must win attention within forty seconds, because "speaking is like take-off". It should not begin with a joke. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:19:00} The conclusion can end with a request, an example, a repeated line, a quotation, a summary, or a story. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:24:30} He says studies put the share of content in a speaker's influence at about seven per cent. Voice takes most of the rest, together with movement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:27:00}

## Voice, pauses and body
He warns against four bad voices and against verbal tics, and advises varying speed and pitch. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:33:30} He also advises against imitating a famous preacher. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:33:00} Humour is prepared in advance and never aimed at people's bodies, disabilities or nationalities. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:35:30} Colloquial speech is acceptable if it is understood, but simple Arabic should be the base. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:36:30} He quotes a scholar of speaking: "speaking is the art of pauses". A short pause comes before and after the key word, and silence is better than "aah". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:37:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:39:00} He speaks next of dress, then of the hands. They should be free, with natural gestures and no finger pointed at the audience. He says he read in the Sira that the Prophet pointed with his whole hand. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:40:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:47:30} The eyes rest on one listener for three to four seconds before moving on. Walking is better than standing still, and standing better than sitting. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:52:00}

He says he himself could not speak at all at first. The first time was at the end of secondary school, when a teacher made him read the morning address on the school radio. Afterwards he took courses in it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep06 00:50:00}
