## Summary
In the lexicon, uzla is a noun from i'tizal, avoiding a thing with the body or the heart. As a technical term it is withdrawal from mixing with people by seclusion and cutting off {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11590}.

## Solitude
Khalwa is a person being alone with himself, and it differs from uzla {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11590}.

## The ruling on withdrawal
Withdrawal is better in times of trial and corruption, unless one can remove the trial, in which case one must strive to remove it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11590}. Outside such times, most jurists held that mixing with people while safe from their harm is better, while some preferred withdrawal for its safety {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11591}. The ruling is said to vary with the person {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11592}.

## Benefits and harms of withdrawal
Its benefits include devotion and freedom from the sins that company often brings {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11594}. Its harm is the loss of what is gained from the help of others {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11594}.

## Etiquette of the one who withdraws
Writers on spirituality give etiquette for the one who withdraws: he should wish others to be safe from his evil rather than seek his own safety from theirs, leave blameworthy traits, and be with people in good and apart from them in evil {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11592} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11593}.
