## Summary
Ibn Sina warns the bather against drinking anything actually cold on leaving the bath or in it, since the pores are open and the cold rushes to the principal organs; against very hot water, for fear of consumption and wasting; against leaving abruptly, uncovering the head or exposing the body to cold; he advises leaving wrapped in winter, and that those in the fever's heat or with a solution of continuity beware the bath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.

## Cold drinks and very hot ones
He should avoid drinking anything actually cold on leaving the bath or in it, for the pores are open and the cold soon rushes into the substance of the principal organs and spoils them. He should also avoid anything very hot, especially water, for if he takes it there is fear that it will quickly reach the principal organs and bring consumption and wasting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.

## Leaving
He should avoid leaving the bath abruptly, uncovering the head afterward and exposing the body to cold; in winter he should leave wrapped in his clothes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.

## Who should beware the bath
Those who are feverish in the heat of the fever, or have a solution of continuity, should beware the bath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.
