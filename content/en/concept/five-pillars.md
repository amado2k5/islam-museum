## Summary
"The five pillars of Islam" are the deeds upon which Islam was built in the Prophet's hadith: "Islam is built on five: the testimony that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger, establishing the prayer, giving the zakat, the pilgrimage, and fasting Ramadan" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}.

## The pillars in detail
- [[concept/shahada|The two testimonies]]: testifying that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}.
- [[practice/salah|The prayer]]: establishing the prayer {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8} — five prayers in the day and night, as in the hadith of the Najdi man {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 46}.
- [[practice/zakat|The zakat]]: giving the zakat {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}.
- [[practice/hajj|The hajj]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}.
- [[practice/sawm-ramadan|Fasting Ramadan]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}.

The order of the pillars differs between accounts: in this account the hajj comes before the fast, while in Gabriel's hadith the Prophet mentions prayer, zakat and fasting when defining [[concept/islam-din|Islam]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}.

## The hadith's source
Al-Bukhari recorded this hadith in the Book of Faith; the editor notes that Muslim also recorded it in the chapter "the pillars of Islam and its great supports" as number 16 {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}. The editor explains "supports" as being to the building in the place of pillars — it does not stand without them {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}.
