## Summary
Kitab al-Ru'ya is a hadith chapter in the two Sahihs, the Muwatta and the Sunan, gathering what is reported of the prophets' and Companions' dreams and their rulings {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:muwatta-malik} {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}.

## Where it sits in the books
The two Sahihs hold a whole book called Kitab al-Ru'ya, placed among the chapters on the merits of the Ansar and on righteousness and kinship; it holds the hadiths that the righteous dream is from God and the bad dream from Satan, and what was reported of interpreting them and of the Prophet being consulted on them {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}. The Muwatta has chapters in its Jami', I'tikaf and Funerals books that touch on dreams, including the chapter on what came of dreams and their beginning {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997}. Al-Tirmidhi has chapters on dreams and what pertains to them {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}.

## What the chapter gathers
It gathers three kinds:

- **Reports of dreams**: the Prophet's dreams of drinking milk, of Umar's palace in Paradise and of the Kaaba and Laylat al-Qadr, and the Companions' dreams of Ibn Zayd's adhan, A'isha's moons, Ibn Umar's silk brocade and Khalid's nightmares {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997}.
- **Rulings on dreams**: that the righteous dream is one forty-sixth of prophethood, that the mubashshirat remain after it, and the seeking of refuge from the bad dream with the left-hand blowing {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.545} {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.546} {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.547}.
- **Related fiqh**: ihtilam and its ghusl, and the woman's ghusl when she sees in sleep what a man sees {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.95} {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.96}.

## The reported dreams
Among the best known in the chapter are [[passage/bukhari-3681|the Prophet's dream of milk]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}, [[passage/bukhari-3680|the dream of Umar's palace in Paradise]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}, [[passage/muslim-2479|Ibn Umar's dream of silk brocade]] {cite:sahih-muslim}, [[passage/muwatta-1-172|Ibn Zayd's dream of the adhan]] and [[passage/muwatta-2-623|A'isha's dream of three moons]] and [[passage/muwatta-31-2666|the dream of the Kaaba and the Messiah]] {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997}, and [[passage/muwatta-5-894|the dream of Laylat al-Qadr]] {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.429}.

## From the chapter to the books of interpretation
Since the chapter is the greatest of what has reached us on dreams from the hadith, standalone books were written on interpretation, the best known being [[work/tafsir-al-ahlam-ibn-sirin|Tafsir al-Ahlam]] attributed to [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Ibn Sirin]]; the museum reviews its modern discussion in [[concept/tafsir-al-ahlam-al-yawm|dream interpretation today]].

## Perspectives
- **The hadith scholars:** this chapter is among the most comprehensive in the two Sahihs on the subject of dreams; from it is taken that the true dream is from God and has a lawful interpretation among the people of knowledge.
- **The jurists:** from this chapter are derived the rulings on ihtilam (the dream emission) and ghusl and what relates to dreams in fiqh {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.
