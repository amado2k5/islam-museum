## Summary
Ibn Sina lists what the bleeder should have with him, lancets, a ball, a vomiting tool, hare's fur and musk, to meet faintness, and says when the approach of faintness is not minded, and the cause of faintness at the first bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.290-296}.

## Faintness at the first bleeding
Faintness comes at the first bleeding from the surprise of what is unaccustomed, and vomiting beforehand prevents it, and so does vomiting at the time it occurs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.290}.

## The tools
They said: the bleeder should have with him many lancets, with a hair edge and without; the one with the hair edge is better for slippery vessels such as the jugular; and he should have a ball of wool-silk and silk, a vomiting tool of wood or feathers, hare's fur, the medicine of aloe and frankincense, a musk pouch, the musk medicine and musk tablets {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## Treating the faint
So that if faintness comes, which is one of the things feared in bleeding and whose victim may not do well, he hastens to put the ball in his mouth, make him vomit with the tool, let him smell the pouch and give him a little of the musk medicine or its tablets to drink, and his strength revives; and if a burst of blood occurs, he hastens to stuff it with hare's fur and the frankincense medicine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## When it is not minded
How seldom faintness comes while the blood is still on its way out; most of it comes after stopping, unless the bleeding is excessive. Yet the approach of faintness is not minded in continuous fevers, the beginnings of apoplexy, quinsies, thick great deadly swellings and severe pains, and this is done only when the strength is strong {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.
