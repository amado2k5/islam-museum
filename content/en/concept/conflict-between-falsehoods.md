## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that there is one truth. A battle between truth and falsehood is short, while a battle between two falsehoods lasts because neither side has a better claim to God's help {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1062} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1063}.

## Truth is one
He says that what might be called two rival truths is not so: truth is one, and a battle is either between truth and falsehood or between two falsehoods {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1062}.

## The third party
He notes that the verse on two groups of believers fighting (49:9) orders others to reconcile them, then to fight the aggressor, then to act with fairness. He holds that this reconciling party is missing from the wars of his age, so they last {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1063}.

## What remains
He holds that God leaves falsehood's partisans to fight among themselves, so that elements of good remain in the world for someone to notice. He takes this as God's mercy toward the worlds {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1064}.
