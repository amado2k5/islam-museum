## Summary
Ridh in the lexicon is a helper or supporter; in the usage of jihad he is one who stands in for the fighters or waits at a post and fights when they withdraw. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8689} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8690} The jurists agree that the helper and the direct fighter are alike in the right to share the booty, and differ on giving one more than the other. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8690} A helper may be disciplined in cases of discretionary penalty; he has no hadd penalty for fornication, drinking or false accusation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8691} In banditry the majority treat the helper like the direct perpetrator, while the Shafi'is hold no hadd for one who only helped. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8691} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8692} In retaliation the majority do not punish the helper, while the Malikis punish those who agreed in advance to the killing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8692} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8693}
