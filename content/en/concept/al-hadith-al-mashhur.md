## Summary
The mashhur is the second class of report by number of chains for [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] and the first kind of solitary report: one with a limited number of chains above two; hadith scholars call it mashhur, "well-known", for its clarity. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 42} Every [[concept/al-hadith-al-mutawatir|mutawatir]] is mashhur but not the reverse: a report with many chains that fails to yield knowledge is only mashhur. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 39}

## Mashhur and mustafid
Ibn Hajar says the mashhur is what a group of leading jurists call mustafid, "widespread", from *fada* (water overflowing); some distinguish the two, the mustafid being equal in number at beginning and end and the mashhur broader; others distinguish them in a way that does not belong to this discipline. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 42-43} The editor, [[person/nur-al-din-itr|Nur al-Din 'Itr]], counts the discussion of the mustafid among the topics of legal theory that Ibn Hajar brought into his book. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 19}

## Well-known on people's tongues
"Mashhur" is also used for what is current on people's tongues, including reports with a single chain or more, and even with no chain at all. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 43} The editor distinguishes what is well known among common people, grammarians, jurists and men of letters, and names al-'Ajluni's Kashf al-Khafa' as the fullest collection of such sayings. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 43}

## Its standing
The mashhur is a solitary report, and solitary reports include both accepted and rejected; its standing, like that of the 'aziz, depends on whether the conditions of acceptance are met — it may be sound, good or weak. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 43} {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 46} Yet Ibn Hajar counts among reports supported by indications, which yield inferential knowledge, the mashhur that has distinct chains free of weak narrators and hidden defects, a view he attributes to Abu Mansur al-Baghdadi and Abu Bakr ibn Furak. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 50} Such knowledge, he adds, comes only to one deeply versed in hadith, who knows the narrators and the hidden defects. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 49}
