## Summary
Sha'rawi explains the verse ([[passage/quran-3-79|Al Imran 79]]) which asks people to be rabbaniyyun by teaching and studying the scripture. He reads its corrective turn as answering those who would make people worship someone other than God after he has been given the scripture, wisdom and prophethood {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1563}.

## The meaning of rabbani
Sha'rawi says the root r-b-b carries the senses of upbringing, guardianship and care, as in the word for Lord and the word rabbani. Belonging to the Lord means that what a trainer takes from the scripture is ascribed to God and to no one else, so he is rabbani in what he receives {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1564}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1565}.

## Teaching and study
Sha'rawi distinguishes teaching, meaning receiving the text of the law, from study, meaning the intellectual understanding of the text through mutual exchange. He compares it to threshing wheat so that the useful grain separates from the chaff. Some texts are clear and some admit more than one meaning, so the rabbani needs both {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1565}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1566}.

## Honouring the Prophet without worship
Sha'rawi holds that the one whom God has given scripture, wisdom and prophethood would not tell people to worship him, nor order them to take angels and prophets as lords. He notes that some Muslims wanted to honour the Prophet by prostrating to him, and that the Prophet made clear that prostration is due to God alone; agreeing would have taken them out of Islam {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1566}.
