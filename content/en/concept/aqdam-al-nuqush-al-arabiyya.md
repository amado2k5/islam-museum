## Summary
This page sets six inscriptions in the order of their dates, from the Nabataeo-Arabic text at al-Hijr of 267 CE to the Dome of the Rock inscription of 72 AH. They are the oldest dated writings in the Arabic script and its ancestors that a research summary supplied to the museum presents. Each has its own record; this entry is limited to the order and the comparison, and the museum does not claim that the list covers every inscription ever found {cite:britannica-arabic-alphabet}.

## The six inscriptions in order
1. [[object/raqosh-epitaph-jsnab-17|The Raqosh epitaph, JSNab 17]]: June or July 267 CE, on a tomb at [[place/al-hijr|al-Hijr]], in a mixture of Nabataean Aramaic and Old Arabic in the Nabataeo-Arabic script; Ka'bu son of Hrtt builds the tomb for his mother {cite:diconab-inscription-196} {cite:wikipedia-jsnab-17-en}.

2. [[object/namara-inscription|The Namara inscription]]: 7 December 328 CE, a five-line basalt lintel in Old Arabic in the Nabataean script, the epitaph of Imru' al-Qays ibn 'Amr, king of the Arabs, held in the Louvre as AO 4083 {cite:louvre-collections-namara-ao-4083} {cite:wikipedia-namara-inscription-en}.

3. [[object/zabad-inscription|The Zabad inscription]]: 512 CE or 24 September 511 CE, the lintel of the shrine of Saint Sergios in Greek, Syriac and Old Arabic, in Brussels {cite:oxford-cult-of-saints-e01817-zabad} {cite:wikipedia-zabad-inscription-en}.

4. [[object/jabal-usays-inscription|The Jabal Usays inscription]]: 528 or 529 CE, or 532 or 533 CE on an alternative reading, four lines in Old Arabic, Ruqaym son of Ma'arrif the Awsite as frontier guard for "al-Harith the king" {cite:wikipedia-jebel-usays-inscription-en}.

5. [[object/harran-inscription|The Harran inscription]]: 567 or 568 CE, in Greek and Old Arabic, the phylarch Sharahil son of Zalim building a shrine of Saint John {cite:wikipedia-harran-inscription-en}.

6. [[object/dome-of-the-rock-inscription|The Dome of the Rock inscription]]: 72 AH, 691 to 692 CE, a mosaic band about 240 metres long in early Kufic with Qur'anic passages, pious formulas and a dedicatory text {cite:britannica-dome-of-the-rock} {cite:wikipedia-dome-of-the-rock-en} {cite:smarthistory-dome-of-the-rock}.

## What the order shows
Arabic appears first mixed with Aramaic at Raqosh in the third century, then as Old Arabic in Nabataean letters at Namara in the fourth, then beside Greek and Syriac at Zabad in 511 or 512 CE, then alone at Usays in 528 or 529 CE, then beside Greek at Harran in 567 or 568 CE, and last in early Kufic on a great building in Jerusalem in 72 AH. The find-places move from tombs, shrines and a frontier stone to a major religious building. The gap between neighbouring inscriptions is roughly sixty years from Raqosh to Namara, nearly two centuries from Namara to Zabad, some seventeen years from Zabad to Usays, some forty from Usays to Harran, and about 124 from Harran to the Dome. This is a description of the dates as the summary gives them, not the museum's judgement on how the script developed.

## What is still uncertain
The summary does not always give one date: Zabad falls between 511 and 512 CE, Usays between 528/529 and 532/533 CE, and Raqosh between June and July. Every conversion from a local calendar, such as the era of the Province of Arabia, the era of Bostra and the Seleucid era, is derived and is the work of the sources the summary reports; the museum has not recomputed any. The phylarchs and "al-Harith the king" are explained as the summary gives them.

## Basis
This entry rests on a research summary supplied to the museum. The museum did not open the pages it cites, and the founder approved using the summary as it stands on 21 November 2026, so every fact here is the summary's report of those pages, not something the museum checked. The reservations and alternative readings are kept as they came. The Encyclopaedia Britannica places the rise of the Arabic alphabet in the 4th century CE, descended from the Nabataean, with its origins and early history vague {cite:britannica-arabic-alphabet}.
