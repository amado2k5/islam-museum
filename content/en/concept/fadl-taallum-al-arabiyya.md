## Summary
This page gathers what is reported about learning Arabic from the Companions and Successors, and the arguments of two major scholars, [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] in al-Risala and [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] in Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim. Each report is given with its narrator and the verdict of the modern editors, because some are graded sound by one editor and broken by another, and some are quoted with no chain at all. The page describes what was said and issues no ruling.

## Reports from the Companions and what is attributed to Umar
In the Musannaf, in the chapter "What came on the inflection of the Qur'an", Ibn Abi Shayba reports from Ubayy ibn Ka'b that Arabic should be learned as the memorising of the Qur'an is learned. The editor says the men of its chain are trustworthy {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 16 p.390}. This report is attributed to Ubayy, not to Umar.

In the same chapter there is a saying of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] by way of Abu Mu'awiya, from Asim, from Muriq: "Learn lahn and the shares of inheritance, for it is part of your religion." The editor's footnote on this report reads "sahih" {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 16 p.392}. The same saying, with the Sunna added, has a report in a later place in the Musannaf {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 17 p.245}.

In another report a narrator says he asked al-Hasan al-Basri, who answered: has it not reached you what Umar wrote, that you should learn Arabic, good expression and understanding of religion? The editor judges this report broken, because al-Hasan did not hear from Umar {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 16 p.391}.

Al-Suyuti, in Jam' al-Jawami', quotes Abu Muslim al-Basri as saying that Umar said learning Arabic firms the intellect and adds to manliness, and ascribes it to al-Bayhaqi in the Shu'ab and al-Khatib in al-Jami'. The editor of the Shu'ab says its chain has people he does not know. On the same page Ata ibn Abi Rabah reports that Umar heard a man speaking Persian during the tawaf, took him by the upper arms and told him to look for a way to Arabic {cite:jam-al-jawami-azhar-2005 14 p.753}.

## Umar's letter to Abu Musa
Ibn Abi Shayba reports that Umar wrote to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari: "Now then, gain understanding of the Sunna, gain understanding of Arabic and give the Qur'an its i'rab, for it is Arabic." The chain runs Isa ibn Yunus, Thawr, Umar ibn Zayd {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 14 p.226}. In another place in the Musannaf there is an addition telling them to adopt the ways of Ma'add, for they are Ma'addis {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 16 p.389}.

Modern editors differ on the grading. The editor of the Musannaf calls it unknown and broken, since Umar ibn Zayd is weak and does not narrate from Umar {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 14 p.226} {cite:musannaf-ibn-abi-shayba-shathri-2015 16 p.389}. Al-Bukhari, in al-Tarikh al-Kabir, says that Umar's letter to Abu Musa is mursal {cite:tarikh-kabir-bukhari-hyderabad 6 p.157}. Ibn Taymiyya gives the report with Ibn Abi Shayba's own chain, and the editor of the Iqtida' says he found no mention of Umar ibn Zayd except in al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim, and that Isa ibn Yunus is trustworthy and reliable {cite:iqtida-aql-1999 1 p.527}. We also read a footnote by the editor of al-Ba'li's abridgement of the Iqtida' saying the report is in the Musannaf with a sound chain; the editors' judgements therefore conflict.

## Al-Hasan al-Basri
In Ibn Wahb's Tafsir, in the chapter "On Arabic in the Qur'an", Hammad ibn Zayd reports from Ubayda ibn Zayd al-Numayri: I heard al-Hasan say, "Foreignness destroyed them; they interpret the Qur'an other than its interpretation" {cite:tafsir-ibn-wahb-muranyi-2003 3 p.44}. By "foreignness" ('ujma) is meant the foreignness of tongue; the editors say no more on the page read.

Al-Shatibi in al-I'tisam quotes this saying and another of al-Hasan, given in answer to a question about learning Arabic: a man reads a verse, cannot find its sense, and perishes. The editor says the chain of the second saying is sound, and that the saying on foreignness is in al-Bukhari by way of this al-Numayri, who is of unknown status, with no one found who declared him trustworthy, so the report is weak because of him {cite:itisam-shatibi-ibn-al-jawzi 2 p.52}.

## Al-Shafi'i in al-Risala
In the chapter of the fifth "bayan", al-Shafi'i takes up the claim that the Qur'an contains non-Arabic words, and argues from verses including [[passage/quran-14-4|Ibrahim 4]] and [[passage/quran-41-44|Fussilat 44]] that its tongue is purely Arabic {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.45} {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.47}. He then says: "Every Muslim must learn of the tongue of the Arabs what his effort reaches," so that he may bear witness to God's oneness and the mission, recite the Book of God, and say the takbir, tasbih and tashahhud {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.48}. Whatever more he learns is better for him, like learning the prayer, and in it he is a follower, not one followed {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.49}.

## Ibn Taymiyya in Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim
Ibn Taymiyya holds that for a city, a household, a market or a government office to make another language its habit is disliked, since Arabic is the emblem of Islam and the tongue of the Qur'an and the habit resembles the non-Arabs. He recalls that the early Muslims accustomed the people of the lands they settled to Arabic until it prevailed, and that later people grew lax, took to Persian, and Arabic was abandoned by many {cite:iqtida-aql-1999 1 p.526}.

He then gives his argument: "Know that habit in a language has a strong and plain effect on the intellect, character and religion." He adds that Arabic itself is part of the religion and knowing it is obligatory, since understanding the Book and the Sunna is an obligation that cannot be fulfilled without it, and what an obligation cannot be completed without is itself obligatory; part of it is fard 'ayn and part fard kifaya. He supports this by Umar's letter to Abu Musa {cite:iqtida-aql-1999 1 p.527}.

He next gives a saying that orders learning Arabic because it is part of the religion, and learning the fara'id because they are part of the religion, introducing it as in another report from Umar, with no chain. He explains that fiqh of Arabic is the way to understand the sayings of the religion, and fiqh of the Sunna is understanding its deeds. The editor's footnotes on the page give only variants between manuscripts and do not grade the report {cite:iqtida-aql-1999 1 p.528}.

## A famous report that does not stand
A report circulates widely that orders love of the Arabs for three reasons: that the Prophet is Arab, the Qur'an is Arabic and the speech of the people of Paradise is Arabic. Al-Hakim gives it in al-Mustadrak from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet, and its chain includes al-Ala ibn Amr al-Hanafi and Yahya ibn Burayd al-Ash'ari. The editors of the Dar al-Risala edition say its chain is utterly weak, and quote Abu Hatim al-Razi: "this is a lie of a hadith", and al-Uqayli, that it is rejected with no basis {cite:mustadrak-risala-2018 7 p.692}.

Al-Hakim held otherwise and graded it sound, as Ibn al-Mulaqqin records; his abridgement answers that Ahmad and others weakened Yahya and that he is not a mainstay {cite:mukhtasar-istidrak-ibn-al-mulaqqin 5 p.2470}. Al-Fattani reports that al-Dhahabi replied to al-Hakim that it is weak, neither sound nor fabricated {cite:tadhkirat-al-mawduat-fattani p.112}. Al-Albani grades it fabricated {cite:daif-al-jami-albani p.26}. For these graders the report ranges from weak to rejected, so this page does not rely on it for the merit of Arabic.

## What this page does not reach
The wording "Learn Arabic, for it is part of your religion" was not found with a full chain in the pages read; Ibn Taymiyya gives it without one. The judgement of the editor of al-Ba'li's abridgement that Umar's letter has a sound chain was read in a book that is not among this page's sources, so the page does not rest on it. Ibn al-Jawzi's books on fabricated reports were not read, nor al-Dhahabi's Talkhis itself; his judgements are read as others quote them.
