## Summary
A hasana is every good deed that draws near to God; its plural is al-hasanat. The Hasanat Browser gathers every occurrence of this word family in the Qur'an and the harvested corpus, by a mechanical scan of the texts: whenever newly harvested books mention the word, the passage appears on its own, without manual tagging.

## In the Qur'an
The verses record that the good deeds are written, multiplied and able to efface evil deeds: "God does not wrong a single atom's weight; if it be a good deed He multiplies it and gives from Himself a great reward" [[passage/quran-4-40]]; "whoever brings a good deed will have ten like it" [[passage/quran-6-160]] [[passage/quran-27-89]] [[passage/quran-28-84]]; "the good deeds efface the evil deeds" [[passage/quran-11-114]]; "those are they whose evil deeds God will change into good deeds" [[passage/quran-25-70]] when the servant repents and does righteous work; "We changed evil to good" [[passage/quran-7-95]], and "changing evil for good" [[passage/quran-27-11]]. The good loan has its own verses: "who lends God a good loan" [[passage/quran-2-245]] [[passage/quran-57-11]] [[passage/quran-64-17]] [[passage/quran-73-20]], including "lend God a good loan and He will multiply it for them" [[passage/quran-57-18]]. And al-husna is the reward of ihsan: "he shall have the reward of the best" [[passage/quran-18-88]], "for those who do good, the best and more" [[passage/quran-10-26]], "He will reward those who do good with the best" [[passage/quran-53-31]], and "he believed in the best reward" [[passage/quran-92-6]].

## In the hadith
The hadith qudsi "God has written down the good deeds and the evil deeds" gathers the matter {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 7501} {cite:sahih-muslim 0131}, with its recensions in Sahih Muslim {cite:sahih-muslim 0128} {cite:sahih-muslim 0128b} {cite:sahih-muslim 0129}: whoever intends a good deed and does not do it has a good deed written for him, and whoever does it has it written up to seven hundredfold. Ibn Majah generalizes the multiplication: "every deed of the son of Adam is multiplied — the good deed tenfold up to seven hundredfold" except fasting, which is for God {cite:sunan-ibn-majah 1638} {cite:sunan-ibn-majah 3823}. Muslim records that whoever initiates a good practice in Islam has its reward and the reward of whoever does it {cite:sahih-muslim 1017}. The good deeds appear in the reward of particular acts: "whoever kills a gecko with the first blow ... has a good deed" {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 5263} {cite:sunan-al-nasai 2215}, and "follow the evil deed with the good deed and it erases it" {cite:arbaun-nawawi 18}. In the hadith qudsi the angels say "My Lord, that is Your servant who intends an evil deed" when the servant has intended either evil or good {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}.

## Two senses of hasana in usage
The word carries two senses in the Qur'an, and this record gathers both: the good deed — as in "whoever brings a good deed" and the good-evil pair in the chapter of reward — and the good fortune or relief that descends in this world, as in "if good befalls them they say it is from Allah" [[passage/quran-4-78]] and "our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the hereafter" [[passage/quran-2-201]], the counterpart of evil in worldly affairs rather than in reward. "Qard hasan" is the fixed collocation for the loan to God [[passage/quran-2-245]]. "Al-asma' al-husna" (the most beautiful names) is a different formula belonging to the divine names, not to the chapter of good deeds, and the browser does not admit it.

## What stays out
Bare "hasan" and "al-hasan" stay out because they are personal names and the hadith grade "hadith hasan"; so does the adverb or adjective "hasanan"; and "hasana" stays out when it is a feminine adjective on a noun ("an excellent example", "the goodly exhortation", "a goodly intercession") rather than a noun for a deed. Verified exclusions are frozen in the detector's exclusion list.
