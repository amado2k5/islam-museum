## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds that muraawada is gently drawing a creature out of its natural state. He illustrates it with the Arab who coaxes a camel to drink, and holds that it is the Prophet's manner in commanding and advising. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:31:30}

## The meaning of muraawada
The Sheikh explains muraawada as gently drawing a creature out of its natural state, and holds that this is how the one who commands or advises should act. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:31:00}

## The camel
The Sheikh describes an Arab stirring water and whistling for a camel, so that the camel, absorbed in the sound and the movement, drinks more than it usually would; this, he says, is the root of muraawada. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:31:00}

## Gentleness in commanding
The Sheikh holds that the nation is commanded to enjoin what is right, and that the manner required in doing so is gentleness. His example is a parent who coaxes a small child to take medicine rather than forcing him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:30:00}
He holds that the Prophet's conduct was to be gentle in what one commands. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:31:30}

## Heavy advice
The Sheikh holds that advice is heavy for the listener because it runs against people's desires, so it should not be thrown at them like a mountain but offered gently. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:31:30}
