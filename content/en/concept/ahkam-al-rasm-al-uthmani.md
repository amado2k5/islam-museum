## Summary
The Arabic rule is that a word is written with the letters of its spelling, with regard to starting with it and stopping on it; the grammarians laid down principles for this, but the script of the Imam codex departed from them in some letters. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2199} Scholars of the rasm, foremost al-Dani in al-Muqni', therefore gathered where the codex departs from the spelling rule and followed it word by word, and Abu al-Abbas al-Marrakushi wrote 'Unwan al-Dalil fi Marsum Khatt al-Tanzil' to explain what differs from the rules of writing. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2196}

## May it be changed?
Al-Suyuti relays from Ashhab that Malik was asked whether the codex should be written in the spelling people have since introduced, and said no, only in the first writing; al-Dani adds that no scholar of the community differs with him. Asked about letters in the Qur'an such as the waw and alif, whether they should be changed in the codex if found so, he said no. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2199} He relays from Ahmad ibn Hanbal that it is forbidden to depart from the writing of Uthman's codex in a waw, ya', alif or anything else, and from al-Bayhaqi in Shu'ab al-Iman that whoever writes a codex should keep to the spelling in which they wrote, because they were more learned, truer in heart and tongue and more trustworthy, and we should not suppose ourselves able to correct them. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2200} For the dispute over the grounds see [[question/may-the-mushaf-be-written-in-imlai-spelling|May the codex be written in modern spelling?]]

## The six rules
Al-Suyuti says the matter of the rasm is confined to omission, addition, hamza, substitution, joining and separating, and whatever has two readings and is written to one of them. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2200}
**Omission.** The alif is dropped from the vocative particle, the demonstrative ha', and the attached plural pronoun in certain words; from demonstratives; from the names of God, al-Rahman and 'subhan' in places; after lam in words; from proper names longer than three letters; from the non-final dual; from sound masculine and feminine plurals; from broken plurals on the mafa'il pattern and the like; and from numbers, each with exceptions counted place by place. The ya' is dropped from defective nouns with tanwin in the nominative and genitive, from a vocative noun annexed to the ya', and from the first-person ya' in a number of verbs; the waw is dropped when another waw meets it, and the lam when assimilated into another lam. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2201} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2202} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2207} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2209}
**Addition.** For instance an alif after the waw of the plural at the end of a noun or verb, and after a hamza written as waw. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2213}
Then the third and fourth rules on hamza and substitution, the fifth on joining and separating, the sixth on what has two readings and is written to one of them, then a branch on what was written to agree with an irregular reading. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2229}

## Maghribis and Easterners
The footnotes to the edition show that al-Dani and Abu Dawud differed in many places: the Maghribis, following al-Dani, chose omission in some, while the Easterners, following Abu Dawud, kept the alif; in the dual, for example, the Maghribis dropped its alif and the Easterners kept it. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2203} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2204} So the rasm is not one absolute form but a followed practice fixed along two respected schools.

## Explanations of omission
Al-Suyuti quotes al-Marrakushi's explanation for dropping the waw in four verbs: that it alerts to how swiftly the act occurs, how easy it is for the doer and how fully the one acted on takes it. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2212} Ibn Khaldun thinks much of such explanation has no basis but sheer arbitrariness, and that the departure of the Companions' spelling from the analogies of the writers' craft is because they were not perfected in the craft of writing; see [[question/is-the-rasm-tawqifi|Is the rasm tawqifi?]] {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 418}

## What I did not list
The books of rasm set out the rules of omission and addition by naming Qur'anic words place by place, which does not suit here since the museum does not copy the Qur'an's wording; I kept to the chapters and schools, and whoever wants the places should consult the Itqan and al-Muqni'.
