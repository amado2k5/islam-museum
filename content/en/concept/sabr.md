## Overview
Patience under harm and persecution for the sake of the call, and what steadies believers in hardship. *Al-Rahiq al-Makhtum* counts among the causes of the Companions' steadfastness faith in God, the leadership of the Prophet, a sense of responsibility, faith in the Hereafter, the Qur'an, and the good news of success. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.118}

## The causes of patience and steadfastness among the Muslims (al-Rahiq al-Makhtum, pp. 118–122)
The book mentions causes of the Muslims' steadfastness under persecution: faith in God and true knowledge of Him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.118}; then the leadership of the Prophet and the perfection of his character, including what was related of his beauty and perfection {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.118}; then the sense of responsibility from which no one turns aside {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.120}; then faith in the Hereafter, and that this world is not worth the wing of a gnat {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.121}; then the Qur'an, which steadied them and answered the obstinate {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.121}; and then the good news of success, that Islam aims to remove the Jahiliyya {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.122}.

## Notes for researchers
- These causes are the author's reflection on the life of the Companions, and the museum presents them as the book sets them out.

## For young visitors
The first Muslims bore harm patiently because they believed in God, loved the Prophet, and knew that the Hereafter is better.

## Patience in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episode 5)
The Sheikh says patience is the first stage of the first half of faith, and that the believer's first trial is patience under affliction. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:20:00} He holds that the believer should be glad he is still steadfast, and that trials are in wealth or in health. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:20:30}
He says patience is not innate but acquired by practice. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:49:30} He holds that patience drives away anxiety and treats the feeling of weakness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:50:00}
He holds that whoever is tried and is patient is raised, and that trials refine the believer as fire refines gold, so the impurities rise and are removed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:30:30} He tells an illustration of oil and water, attributed to Ibn al-Jawzi, which could not be checked in the recording (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:31:00}
He says that visiting the sick lightens the trial, since many people are afflicted. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:25:30} He relates that whoever says the words of return at a calamity receives blessings from his Lord (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:29:30}
He tells of al-Ahnaf ibn Qays, whose nephew killed his son: he kept silent, then forgave, then paid the blood money from his own wealth, which he presents as patience with anger (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:50:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:51:30}
He offers a practical exercise when angry: seek refuge in God, write the angry letter and keep it, then reread it after a day; he holds that this cools anger (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:54:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:55:00}

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen

He tells of a man whose ship was wrecked on a deserted island, where he lit a fire that was later followed by a passing boat that rescued him; he concludes that a person should never despair (needs_source: unnamed-parable) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:14:00}.

When affliction comes, he suggests three positions: that it is a test and a sign of nearness to God, that the alternative to patience is despair, and that the prophets are the most tried of people {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:02:00}. He calls for remembering those who suffer greater trials, such as illness, divorce, undutiful children and homelessness (needs_source: figure-unclear) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:05:00}. He quotes sociologists (needs_source: unnamed-saying) that one struck from behind advances, and urges the believer to be like a tree that is pelted with stones and returns fruit {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:12:00}.

He describes children mocking a boy who prays at school, and holds that the sincere believer bears this patiently [[passage/quran-83-29|Qur'an 83:29]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:08:00}. He holds that patience with a matter makes the heart sound {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:26:00}. He sees Hajar's patience and trust in God as good upbringing: she never spoke ill of her son's father, which is a lesson for mothers and wives after separation {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:38:30}. He sets two options before her: patience and trust, since it is God's command, or argument, if it was Ibrahim's own judgement (needs_source: third-question-garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:40:00}.

He uses the image of oil that rises above water only after bearing pressure, and comments on the pressures of work {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep10 00:39:00}. A means of patience, he says, is to ask oneself what the Prophet would have done before answering one's wife; he counts bearing harm among the meanings of the expansion of the breast {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep11 00:45:00}.

He holds that faith has two halves, patience and gratitude, and that the work of a teacher, doctor, worker or manager cannot be right without patience {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:34:30}. Patience helps one control feelings and tongue: the patient person owns his tongue before he speaks {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:35:00}. He attributes divorce to lack of patience, citing a rate of about sixty-five percent in some Muslim countries (needs_source: divorce-rate-unsourced) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:35:30}.

He suggests to the traffic police that reckless young drivers be sent to graze sheep in the desert for a month or two, and cites a saying attributed to Ali on patience {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:36:30}. He describes a village custom of smearing a mother's breast with bitter aloe juice to wean an infant, and explains the plant's name as meaning one that endures dryness (needs_source: folk-etymology) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:37:30} and cites God's command to the Prophet to pray and endure in it [[passage/quran-20-132|Qur'an 20:132]]. He holds that steady patience becomes habit and character, and cites God's address to believers to be steadfast [[passage/quran-3-200|Qur'an 3:200]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:38:30}.

He says that restraining anger is patience, and refers to a man asked about his origin who answered that the matter is one of patience (needs_source: garbled-story) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep14 00:18:00}. He recounts a test in which strong men each pressed another's finger, and only the impatient turned red; he holds that true courage is patience at the start of provocation, not winning by force (needs_source: demonstration-unverified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep14 00:18:30}. He recounts a young man who insults a man who receives the insults patiently, and holds that forbearance and generosity are signs of good manners (needs_source: anecdote-garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep14 00:19:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)
The Sheikh illustrates how worries differ with a student who complains that he wants a job, then a marriage, then a house {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:25:30}. He quotes the words of a wronged man, who says that if his enemies imprison him his prison is seclusion with God, if they kill him his death is martyrdom, and if they exile him his exile is travel {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:51:00}. The Sheikh remarks that the man's enemies can do no more than imprison, kill or exile him, and that imprisonment is then seclusion and killing martyrdom {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:51:30}. He then asks whether the person killed dies by the killer's act or by the term written for him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:51:30}. He holds that a husband's patience with his wife may bring him into Paradise, as may hers with him, and he calls for thanks to God for a good wife {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:04:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 3)
The Sheikh asks whether calamity is, on the face of it, good or bad, and says the verse shows that nothing befalls us except what God has written for us {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:16:30}. He concludes that calamity is therefore in a person's favour since God wrote it for him, and cites the hadith that the believer's affair is entirely good: if blessing touches him he gives thanks, and if harm touches him he is patient {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:17:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 5)
The Sheikh says the first level of the first half of faith, which is the patience by which a believer enters Paradise, is patience in affliction {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:19:30}. He holds that the key to Paradise after the oneness of God is patience and gratitude, and that when affliction strikes the afflicted person should hand his affair over to God and say the words of return to Him [[passage/quran-2-156|Al-Baqara 156]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:29:00}. He offers what he calls a faith prescription: enter by the door of patience, for he takes it that behind the door of patience lies the door of Paradise {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:36:00}. He urges learning patience beginning with spouses, since some complain that a wife talks too much and others that a husband says too little {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:36:30}. He describes types of spouses, noting that some wives magnify small matters {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:37:30}. He describes an easygoing husband who leaves his wife to manage the economic and social affairs, such as visits, congratulations and condolences, and jokes that she is the director of public relations and the minister of foreign affairs and of the interior {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:38:00}. He advises newlyweds who complain after two years to be patient for three or four more, until each understands the other's personality {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:38:30}. He holds that the first stage of patience is with a wife who is meticulous and loves precision, and that the husband should learn patience with her in that {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:39:00}. He jokes that precision is not his own habit, and suggests that anyone whose wife talks a great deal should try patience with her first {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:39:30}. He holds that homes and societies crack only when patience dissolves, and that once it is gone people begin to harm one another {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:41:00}. He calls for tolerance toward a curious colleague, taking in his questions rather than trying to raise him in one's own way {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:41:30}. He comments on the verse about repelling evil with what is better, saying that the person with whom you have enmity becomes through it like a close friend, and that only the patient attain this quality [[passage/quran-41-34|Fussilat 34]] [[passage/quran-41-35|Fussilat 35]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:46:30}. He notes that the verse after it commands seeking refuge with God from the prompting of Satan [[passage/quran-41-36|Fussilat 36]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:46:30}. He calls for doing good to a nephew, a cousin, a neighbour and a colleague for God's sake alone, when one counts everything one does as done for God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:47:30}. He relates that Socrates had a foul-mouthed and short-tempered wife (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:47:30}. He relates that Socrates advised a young man to marry, telling him that if his wife was good he would be happy all his life, and otherwise he would become a philosopher (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:48:00}. He calls for patience with the traits of a spouse that do not change, and tells of a well-known humorous poet in Egypt who said he had learnt humour from his wife's bad temper toward him, fleeing from her and composing a funny poem (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:48:30}. He urges starting today with the first of the keys to Paradise, which is learning patience with a husband, a wife, children, neighbours, colleagues and relatives, and says that patience is a faculty {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:49:00}. He cites the command to pray and to command one's family to pray [[passage/quran-20-132|Ta-Ha 132]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:49:00}. He tells of an obeyed chief who sat with his knees drawn up among his followers when someone whispered to him that his nephew had killed his son; he finished what he was saying, the killer was brought in bound so that he would not escape, and the chief ordered him freed (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:51:00}. He says the chief paid the blood-money for his son out of his own property, and the case was closed (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:52:00}. He gives a practical tip: if your brother or friend angers you, do not write an angry message and send it at once, but write it somewhere else and look it over first {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:54:30}.

## The speaker's view: beautiful patience
He says that beautiful patience is patience without complaint to people, and that complaint is made to God alone. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:19:00}
He says that the Qur'an speaks of beautiful forgiveness and beautiful parting, and that beautiful patience means not complaining to people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:32:00}
He distinguishes patience from gratitude: gratitude to God means submitting to His commands, such as prayer, while beautiful patience is patience without complaint. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:05:30}

## The speaker's view: patience as steadiness on the road
He likens patience to a steady mount that does not stumble and carries a person to Paradise, and he says contentment and thanks come with it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:21:30}
He says that the hasty person who drives his mount too hard arrives at no destination, whereas patience steadies the nerves and lets a person solve his problems step by step. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:06:30}
He holds that impatience restores nothing: if a person loses wealth, health or standing, fretting gains him nothing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:02:00}

## The speaker's view: patience in trial and with God
He holds that a person should know that God loves the one He tests, and cites the Qur'anic praise of Ayyub for his patience (the verse is not cited by number in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:02:00}
He cites a hadith that the prophets are tested most, then the righteous, and that trial is in proportion to a person's faith, so a strong believer is tested more. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:08:00}
He says that one should be patient in obedience to God, with God and for God, and that this must never come at God's expense. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:15:30}
He says that patience with the envious is required, since their envy consumes them as fire does. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:36:30}

## The speaker's view: the ruler, the subjects and children
He says that the patience of the ruled follows the patience of the ruler, and that the reward for patience after a loss is greater than the loss itself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:14:00}
He holds that children should be taught patience through the examples of earlier generations, who had less and endured more. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:44:30}

## Why the great were patient
The Sheikh answers the question whether the patient are of 'a special make': all humans are a handful of earth and a breath of God's spirit; he likens the believer to gold melted in fire until it becomes twenty-four carat, the dross rising and the pure remaining. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:02:30} He says people are like soils: some are clay that never forgets a kindness, some sand that drinks the water and gives nothing; and he speaks of two 'bags' in memory: good people put others' kindness in the bag without holes and their wrongs in the one with holes, while most people do the reverse, so that after forty years a husband says he never saw any good from his wife. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:04:30}
He lists the reasons for their patience as he took them from the scholars: their trust in God's good choice, their seeing the 'gifts that arrive' with affliction, like someone who began to pray, put on the hijab, paid zakat or gave up wine after a calamity, their knowledge that God has wisdom and knowledge, and that patience yields contentment. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:10:00} He gives the example of a man who loves meat and is forbidden it by his doctor because of gout, seeing in it that the share of meat written for him has run out. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep14 00:09:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme al-Munjiyat
Omar Abdelkafy counts patience among al-munjiyat and says the sweetness of its fruit eases the bitterness of pain. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:01:30} He says patience is mentioned in the Qur'an in sixteen ways, among them the command to it, God's special companionship with the patient which includes protection, victory and support, reward without measure, good tidings, their being the people of firm resolve, and that leadership in religion is reached through patience and certainty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:11:30} He divides it into patience in obedience, in keeping from sin and under trial, {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:22:00} and then into patience by God, for God and with God, explaining patience with God as the servant turning with God's command wherever it turns. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep09 00:16:30} In the tenth episode he says patience has three degrees. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep10 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep10 00:01:30}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme In the Courts of the Hadith Qudsi
Episode 26 of [[work/fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah|Fi rihab al-hadith al-qudsi]] explains the hadith qudsi on one whom God tries with the loss of his sight and who is patient, whom He compensates with the Garden. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:00:00} The presenter explains how the eye works, distinguishes "looking" (nazar) with the eye from "seeing" (basar) with the brain, and reads [[passage/quran-7-198|al-A'raf 198]] in this light. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:14:30}

[[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] says that each limb has a task, but sight is the mistress of the limbs and the first guide of life's movement, which is why the hadith speaks of the eyes as what a person holds dearest. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:20:00} He holds that one who has lost his sight should know that the One who took it is his Lord, who will not abandon him, and that people are moved to serve him. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:21:00} He contrasts a blind person who is proud against God's decree and refuses help, whom people leave, with one content with his lot, whom everyone hurries to serve. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:21:30} He says a person is between two states: given a blessing, he gives thanks, since no one has a right over God; struck by harm, he is patient. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:24:00} The presenter mentions a report in al-Tabarani and al-Bayhaqi that patience is half of faith. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep26 00:26:00} (needs_source: no grading is given.)
