## Ripening
Ibn Sina says that if the matter is phlegmatic, the mother remedies we use in ripening it are those with thinning, cutting and dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.28} The grades of these remedies must rise in proportion to the matter: if it is great in amount and strong in quality we make the hot remedies strong, unless boiling of the matters is feared, which is when they are very many and we fear that if they are heated their volume will grow and cause a painful stretching or swelling; there we must begin by evacuating some of it and then go on to ripen the rest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.30}

The more correct course in ripening soft, raw humours is for the treatment and the dressing to be with remedies of moderate heating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.30} If the matter is small in amount or weak in quality we limit ourselves to the mild ones of the first grade, and if middling, to the middling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.30}

If the matter is black-bilious we do not limit ourselves to these remedies; its ripening needs softening and moistening, followed by ripeners of mild dissolving, and the better course is to join the softening and moistening ones with the hot, cutting, dissolving ones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.30} As for a hot matter, its ripening gathers its consistence, and opens and cuts besides, and these are the moistening coolers that have cleansing and washing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.30}

Recipes, quantities, the names of the veins and the sites of venesection and cupping, and methods of application are not reproduced here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.28-31}

## Drawing off, and the cleansing particular to the head
He says we counter the matter by evacuation, by drawing to the opposite side, and by both, and drawing to the opposite side is drawing to the hand and the leg, helped by rubbing them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.32} Among what is used in this matter is the exercise in which the head is kept still and the body does not move, only the lower parts moving alone, and it comes after evacuation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.32} Rubbing and binding the limbs is of this kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.32} The head alone may be treated with light exercise such as rubbing, pressing, even combing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.32}

What joins both managements is enemas, suppositories, diuretics and sweat-inducers according to the matter and the strength {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.32} He says there are purgatives that suit thin bilious humours and others that suit thick and black-bilious humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.32}

As for the cleansers particular to the head, one is gargles; if the humours are purely bilious, gargling is not used to cleanse them, for fear that they will descend to the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.34} He mentions sneezing preparations and nasal drops, some meant for cooling and moistening, some for dissolving, some for strengthening, and says that a strong dissolving drop is used in stages {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.36} If the origin of the matter and the vapours is the stomach, the nature of the humour in the stomach is considered and it is evacuated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.36}

Recipes, quantities, the names of the veins and the sites of venesection and cupping, and methods of application are not reproduced here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.28-36}
