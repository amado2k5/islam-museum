## Summary
Hisba (the office of enjoining good and forbidding wrong) is, for most jurists, enjoining good when it is seen to be neglected and forbidding wrong when it is seen to be done. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7036} It differs from judgeship in that the muhtasib deals with acknowledged rights and not with denial; it adds the power to act on wrong without a plaintiff, and judgeship calls for patience while hisba calls for awe. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7037} It is distinct from fatwa, from testimony, and from the redress of grievances (mazalim), which ranks above it, and hisba comes after judgeship. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7038} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7042} Hisba is a duty in general: when it concerns an obligation or a prohibition it is obligatory for the able, and when it concerns a recommended or disliked matter it is recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7041} It is an individual duty of the ruler, and a communal duty for others as far as they are able and safe. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7042} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7043} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7053} The conditions of the muhtasib: Islam, legal capacity, knowledge, justice (which the majority require of the office-holder but not of the volunteer), capacity, and maleness for many. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7047} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7048} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7049} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7050} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7051} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7052} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7054} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7055} The office-holder is paid from the public treasury. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7056} The etiquette: refusing gifts, touring the markets, gentleness and patience, and concealing faults where possible. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7057} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7058} Good is what is known to be right; wrong is what the law forbids, including the disliked forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7059} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7063} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7064} Good concerns God's rights, such as the Friday prayer, congregational prayer and the call to prayer, and concerns human rights, such as the public water supply and the rights of debtors. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7059} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7060} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7061} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7062} A wrong that the muhtasib may act on must be forbidden and visible to him without spying. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7064} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7065} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7068} Concealing the faults of those who slipped is preferred by most jurists except where God's penalties apply, and the innovators of misleading heresies may be confronted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7065} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7066} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7067} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7068}


## Who may act, the grades of action, and the liability of the muhtasib
A person may enjoin good on children, on parents, a pupil on his teacher, a wife on her husband, and subjects on rulers, with gentleness and no more than need requires. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7075} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7076} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7077} Acting on non-Muslims is for evident wrongs they are not allowed to commit, while they may display their religion within what they agreed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7077} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7078} The grades of action are: reminder, exhortation, rebuke, action with the hand, punishment, and reference to the ruler. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7078} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7079} The muhtasib may impose discretionary punishment for any offence that has no fixed penalty or expiation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7080} If an overreach causes loss, the Hanafis and Hanbalis hold him free of liability, while the Malikis and Shafi'is hold him liable for what exceeds the need. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7080} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7081} Whether the blood-money falls on the kin or the public treasury is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7082}
