## Nature and selection
Nature: well known. Ways of making it include treating copper with vinegar dregs; sprinkling its filings with vinegar and burying them in dew; inverting a copper vessel over one with vinegar until it turns green and scraping it off; or mixing it with ammonium salts and burying it in dew. It is also taken from what forms on rock and in copper mines, and a finer kind is made by further processes the book describes, whose details are left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Choice: the best is the mineral kind; the strongest is that made from tuwbal and rusakhtaj; the vinegar kind is softer than the ammonia kind. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Temperament: hot and dry to the fourth degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.465}

## Actions
Actions: a sharp cleanser that eats both hard and soft flesh; the cerate tempers it, making it drying without stinging. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.465}

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: it stops running ulcers and heals with cerate; it cleans dirty ulcers; with mastic and natron it treats ulcerating scabies, baras (a white skin complaint) and white patches. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.465}
Head organs: verdigris made with ammonia, alum and vinegar, pounded and blown into the nose, helps stench of the nose and bad sores there; water is held in the mouth so that the powder does not reach the throat. Iron rust with vinegar firms the gums, and so does copper rust. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.465}
Eye organs: it helps thick and hard eyelids, clears the eye, enters remedies for eye ulcers, and greatly draws tears; when used in kohl, it is wise to foment the eye with a sponge dipped in hot water. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.465}
Excretory organs: it enters remedies for piles, and wicks made of it and of al-ashq are placed in piles. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.465}
