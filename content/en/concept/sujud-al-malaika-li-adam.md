## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the angels' prostration to Adam was obedience to God's command and not worship of Adam {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.254}, and that when Iblis refused he answered the command with a refusal, which was disbelief {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.256} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.257}. See [[passage/quran-2-34|al-Baqara 34]] and [[passage/quran-18-50|al-Kahf 50]].

## Worship as obedience
He holds that worship is obedience to God's commands and avoidance of His prohibitions {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.254}. He uses the pilgrimage: one kisses a stone and stones another stone, both following a command, so nothing is sacred except God's command and method {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.254}.

## Who prostrated
He holds that those who prostrated were the angels who had a task toward humanity, such as the recorders and guardians, and that the command did not include the exalted angels who carry the Throne and guard the heavens {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.255}. He says Iblis was of the jinn, who have choice and may obey or disobey {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.256}.

## Pride and disbelief
He holds that Iblis's disobedience was at the top, since he answered the command and thought himself better than Adam, and that his pride led him from one disobedience to another {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.257}. He cites a report that calls Iblis "the peacock of the angels", and says that vanity was his door {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.257}.
