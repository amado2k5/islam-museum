## Summary
In the lexicon, ard means showing and disclosing, and the jurists use it in that sense {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11555}.

## Offering Islam to a spouse who has not converted
When one of two pagan or Magian spouses converts, or a People-of-the-Book man married to a pagan or Magian woman converts before consummation, the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that the separation is immediate and is an annulment, not a divorce {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11555}. After consummation, the Shafi'is and, in one report, Ahmad wait for the waiting period to end; if the other spouse converts before then, the marriage stands {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11555}. The Hanafis offer Islam to the spouse who has not converted and separate the couple if that spouse refuses, with distinctions for People of the Book {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11556}.

## A woman offering herself to a righteous man
A woman may make known her desire for a man for his goodness, knowledge or a quality of piety, and there is no shame in it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11557}.

## A guardian offering his ward to people of goodness
A man may offer his daughter or another ward to someone whose goodness he trusts, even if that man is already married {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11557}. The jurists cite Umar's offer of Hafsa to Uthman and then to Abu Bakr after she was widowed, before the Prophet married her {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11558}.
