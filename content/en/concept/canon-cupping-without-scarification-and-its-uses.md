## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that cupping without scarification serves several ends: drawing matter away from where it moves, bringing a deep swelling out, moving it, warming the organ, dissolving its winds, returning it to its place and easing pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.301}.

## Drawing matter away
Cupping without scarification is sometimes used to draw matter away from the direction in which it moves, as when it is set on the breast to stop the flow of menstrual blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.301}.

## The swelling and the organ
It may be meant to bring out a deep swelling so that treatment can reach it, or to move the swelling to a lesser organ nearby, or to warm the organ, draw blood to it and dissolve its winds, or to return it to the natural place it has left, as in hernia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.301}.

## Easing pain
It may be used to ease pain, as when it is set on the navel for severe colic, winds of the belly and the pains of the womb that come when the menses stir, especially in young women {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.301}.
