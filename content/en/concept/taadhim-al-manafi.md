## Summary
In the author's view, the principle of maximizing utility is seen not only in production and consumption, but also in redistribution {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 343}. He cites the hadith of the one dirham and the hundred thousand, commenting that the marginal utility of a dirham to a poor person is greater than to a rich person {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 343}.

## What He Quoted from al-Izz
The author quotes from al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam the saying of al-Shafi'i that "great wealth" has no fixed measure because it varies with people's aspirations {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 343}. Al-Izz states that the ruler must prioritize necessities over needs for everyone, treat them equally by prioritizing the poorest and most urgently needy among them, and that equality does not lie in the amounts paid, but in paying each person what meets their need {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 343}.

## Measuring Expenditures by Needs
The author quotes al-Izz as saying that measuring expenditures by needs—despite their variation—is justice and equality, because the supreme goal is meeting needs rather than the amounts that reach people {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 344}. He gives the example of a loaf of bread divided between two children, where it is split so as to satisfy each one's hunger in proportion to the other's {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 344}.
