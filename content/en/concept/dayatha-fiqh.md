## Summary
In the lexicon dayatha is a lack of jealousy over one's household and kin. The dayyuth is the man without such jealousy; jurists add the qurtuban and the qarnan as equivalents. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8304}
Dayatha is a grave sin, and a report counts the dayyuth among those who will not enter Paradise. When it is widespread it may be counted as corruption in the land. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8304} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8305}

## Related terms
Qiyada is the procuring of illicit relations between a man and a woman; it is the act of the procurer, as dayatha is the act of the dayyuth, and the two are close in meaning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8304}

## Rules that depend on it
The jurists hold that dayatha is among the things that bring about or accompany divorce, with a difference over whether it makes divorce obligatory or recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8305}
One who insults another by calling him dayyuth is discretionarily punished and not hadd-punished, because he has harmed him by attaching a stain to him, and analogy has no place in the hadd penalties. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8305}
The Shafi'is and Hanbalis count dayatha among the things that remove a witness's probity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8305}
