## Overview
*Asabiyya* is the bond of descent and blood around which the members of a tribe gather, so that they support one another. *Al-Rahiq al-Makhtum* states that it was the foundation of the social order among the Arabs of the Jahiliyya, for whom they lived and died. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.45} Ibn Khaldun later made it the key to understanding the rise and fall of states. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun}

## Asabiyya in pre-Islamic society according to al-Rahiq al-Makhtum
The book states that the people of the Jahiliyya lived and died for tribal solidarity, and that the foundation of the social order was blood kinship and the womb. They followed the proverb "help your brother, whether he is an oppressor or oppressed", in its literal sense, without the qualification Islam introduced, that helping the oppressor means restraining him from oppression. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.45} It notes that the tribe was a miniature government, whose political existence rested on the unity of solidarity and on mutual interests in protecting the land and repelling aggression. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32} See [[concept/social-condition-jahiliyya|the social condition]] and [[concept/pre-islamic-political-condition|the political condition]].

## What is not recorded here
The book does not elaborate Ibn Khaldun's theory of *asabiyya*, nor the development of the term after Islam. These need other sources.

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
In the seventh episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] distinguishes a person's love for his own people, which he considers permitted, from blameworthy tribalism, citing a hadith in which the Prophet was asked whether it is tribalism for a man to love his people, the answer being, in meaning, that tribalism is helping one's people in wrongdoing. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:11:30} He says Islam fights tribal loyalty only when it conflicts with religion, as when a Muslim puts his tribe above justice and supports the wrongdoer, or breaks some part of the Sharia. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:12:30} He gives examples of useful tribal grouping: the army of the conquest of Mecca, which entered in tribal units, and the arrangement of armies by tribe at al-Qadisiyya, Tustar and Jalula, though not, he notes, at Yarmuk. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:16:00} And of harmful tribalism: the Muslim defeat at Balat al-Shuhada, which he puts down to a quarrel between Berbers and Arabs during the battle, and the fall of the Ottoman caliphate, which he says the English and French helped bring about by stoking national feeling between Arabs and Turks; this is his reading of these events. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep07 00:21:00}
