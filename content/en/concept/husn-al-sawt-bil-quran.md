## The texts
Al-Bukhari has from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said: "he is not of us who does not yataghanna the Qur'an" (Sahih al-Bukhari 7527), and some narrators added "meaning, aloud" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. Muslim has from Abu Hurayra: "God has not listened to anything as He listens to a prophet yataghanna the Qur'an aloud" (Sahih Muslim 792) {cite:sahih-muslim}. Al-Nasa'i has from al-Bara' ibn 'Azib that the Prophet said: "adorn the Qur'an with your voices" (Sunan al-Nasa'i 1015-1016), and in the wording of the people of the Sunan "for the beautiful voice increases the Qur'an in beauty" {cite:sunan-al-nasai}. Al-Bukhari has from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari that the Prophet said to him: "O Abu Musa, you have been given a mizmar of the mazamir of the house of David" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5048) {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}, and al-Nasa'i has from Abu Hurayra and 'A'isha that the Prophet heard Abu Musa's recitation and said it (Sunan al-Nasa'i 1019-1020) {cite:sunan-al-nasai}; in Muslim's long narration Abu Musa told the Prophet: "had I known, I would have adorned it for you with adornment" {cite:sahih-muslim}.

## The explanations
The scholars read yataghanna two ways: some carry it on beautifying and softening the voice in recitation and reading aloud with humility; others — among them Ibn 'Abbas in what is reported — on being content with the Qur'an against everything else, and a group explained it as sufficiency in the Qur'an against falsehood and amusement; the jurists record both {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}. Ibn al-Qayyim reports in Zad al-Ma'ad that the salaf recited the Qur'an with tahzin and tatrib, beautifying their voices with tenderness, longing and delight at turns — a thing rooted in natures {cite:islamqa-169799}, and Ibn Qudama reports the scholars' agreement that recitation with tahzin, tartil and beautifying is recommended {cite:islamqa-1377}.

## The limit of beautifying
The people of performance agree that beautifying the voice is sunnah inside tajwid, and that taghani is not drawing the voice out until it adds to or takes from the letters and the mudud — what takes a letter beyond its measure leaves tajwid for lahn. This is the door adjoining the disagreement over reciting with melodies and maqamat {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3} {cite:islamqa-1377}.
