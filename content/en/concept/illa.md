## Summary
In the lexicon, illa means an illness, and also a cause. In legal theory it is an apparent and regular attribute that the Lawgiver attached to a ruling and set as its sign {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11793}.

## Distinctions
The illa is not the wisdom (hikma) of a ruling, which is the purpose the ruling serves. It differs from a sabab (cause without its own effect), from a condition, and from a preventer {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11793} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11794}.

## Conditions
The illa must be an apparent, regular and extendable attribute, not confined to the original case, and not one that the Lawgiver disregarded {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11794} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11795}.

## How it is established
It is established by explicit text such as a word of causation, by allusion and indication, by consensus, and by deduction, including the purification of the ground of ruling and the examination and division of attributes {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11795} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11797}.
