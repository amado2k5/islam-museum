## Overview
Qiyas in usul usage is extending a root case's ruling to a branch case for a shared cause; al-Shafi'i counts it among the proofs with the Book, the Sunna and ijma' {cite:risala-shafii}.

## In the sources
Al-Shafi'i devotes chapters of the Risala to qiyas, defining it and its conditions, and holding that knowledge proceeds by the Book, the Sunna, ijma' and qiyas {cite:risala-shafii}.

## Qiyas in al-Shawkani's Irshad al-Fuhul

[[person/al-shawkani|Al-Shawkani]] devotes the fifth aim of [[work/irshad-al-fuhul|Irshad al-Fuhul]] to analogy. Linguistically, he says, it is measuring one thing against the pattern of another and making them equal, which is why a measuring vessel is called a miqyas. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.200} For its technical definition he gives [[person/al-baqillani|al-Baqillani]]'s — applying one known case to another in affirming or denying a ruling for both by something they share — then others, including [[person/abu-al-husayn-al-basri|Abu al-Husayn al-Basri]]'s, and himself prefers: deriving for what is unmentioned the like of the ruling of what is mentioned, by something shared between them. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.200} Its pillars are four: the root case, the branch case, the cause and the ruling. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.206}

On its authority he reports agreement that analogy is valid in worldly matters such as medicines and foods, and in analogies made by the Prophet himself, the dispute concerning legal analogy: most Companions, Followers, jurists and theologians held it to be a source of the law. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.201} He says the first to declare its denial openly was [[person/al-nazzam|al-Nazzam]], followed by some Mu'tazilis and then by [[person/dawud-al-zahiri|Dawud al-Zahiri]] in legal rulings, and he quotes [[person/ibn-hazm|Ibn Hazm]] that the Zahiris rejected analogy altogether. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.202}

He then examines the proofs offered for it: the verse "take heed" ([[passage/quran-59-2|al-Hashr 2]]) does not, he argues, point to legal analogy; the verse on compensation for game ([[passage/quran-5-95|al-Ma'ida 95]]), which al-Shafi'i cited in the Risala, only requires a like animal as judged by two upright men; and the verse on those who derive the matter ([[passage/quran-4-83|al-Nisa' 83]]), even if it covered analogy, would be limited to analogy whose cause is stated in a text. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.202} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.203} He concludes that the analogy to be followed is that whose cause is stated in a text, that in which any difference is excluded with certainty, and the a fortiori kind, and he notes that the deniers of analogy themselves count this kind as part of what the text indicates, so that the dispute over it is verbal. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.205} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.206}

## Qiyas in Ibn Rushd's Bidayat al-Mujtahid
In the introduction to [[work/bidayat-al-mujtahid|Bidayat al-Mujtahid]], [[person/ibn-rushd|Ibn Rushd]] presents analogy as the majority's way to what the Lawgiver left unaddressed, and reports that the Zahiris said analogy in the law is void and what the Lawgiver left unaddressed has no ruling; he holds that reason testifies for it, since cases among people are unlimited while texts, acts and approvals are limited, and the unlimited cannot be matched by the limited. {cite:bidaya-bakri-bayt-al-afkar p.17} He defines legal analogy as extending a ruling the law establishes for one thing to a thing left unaddressed, because of its resemblance to the first or a shared cause; hence it has two kinds, analogy of resemblance and analogy of cause. {cite:bidaya-bakri-bayt-al-afkar p.18} He distinguishes it from "a specific term meaning the general": attaching the unaddressed to the stated by way of what the wording points to is not analogy but a form of verbal indication, and he says jurists very often confuse the two. {cite:bidaya-hallaq-v1 p.25} His examples of analogy are treating the wine-drinker like the slanderer in the fixed penalty, and the dower like the minimum value for amputation; he counts attaching usurious goods to staple, measured or edible foods as a specific term meaning the general, and holds that the Zahiris should dispute the first kind but not the second, which belongs to transmitted indication. {cite:bidaya-hallaq-v1 p.25}
