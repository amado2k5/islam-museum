## Nature and temperament
Nature: chickpea comes in many kinds: white, red, black and kursunni; there is also a wild kind that is sharper, more bitter and hotter, and it acts like the cultivated kind in strength, but the cultivated food is better than the wild. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.481}
Temperament: the white is hot and dry in the first degree; the black is stronger. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}

## Actions
Both open and soften; it cuts, and it nourishes more strongly than bean, and is more sticky; none of its kinds nourishes the lung better, and its moist form creates more superfluities than the dry. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it clears freckles and improves colour, as a poultice and as food. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
Swellings: helps hot and hard swellings, all swellings, and those in the glands. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
Wounds: its oil helps herpes, and its flour helps malignant, cancerous and itching ulcers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
Joints: helps back pain. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
Organs of the head: helps moist pustules of the head; its steep helps toothache; it helps hot and hard gum swellings and swellings under the ears. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
Organs of the chest: clears the voice and nourishes the lung better than anything else, so it is made into a broth. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
Organs of digestion: its decoction helps dropsy and jaundice, and opens the obstructions of the liver and spleen, especially the kursunni and black kinds; it should be eaten not at the start nor at the end of the meal but in its middle. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
Excretory organs: the black kind's decoction breaks up stone in the bladder and kidneys with almond oil, radish and celery; it is bad for ulcers of the bladder and increases sexual power greatly; its steep erects strongly when drunk fasting; the whole thing loosens the bowels. Labelled listing on the womb, set aside for the founder's ruling: the book says it expels the whole foetus. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.482}
