## Summary
The chahar bagh is a garden divided into four quarters by crossing water channels, lined with walkways, platforms, basins, fountains and fruit trees, and enclosed by walls and gateways {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.4}.

## From Iran to India
Rezavi links the chahar bagh to Timurid practice. He notes that Babur laid out several around Agra, including the Bagh-i Fath near Fatehpur Sikri, with an Iranian-inspired pavilion at its centre {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.5}.

## In Mughal architecture
The garden became the setting for Mughal tombs such as Humayun's Tomb in Delhi and the tombs of Akbar and Jahangir. Its organising axes were later carried into town planning at Fatehpur Sikri and Shahjahanabad {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.4} {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.6}. The author reads this use as carrying the Iranian image of paradise into architecture {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.6}.
