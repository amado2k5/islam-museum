## The shell and the hole
Know that the ear is an organ created for hearing: it was given a coiled shell to hold all sound and keep its resonance, and a hole entering the stony bone, coiled and curved so that its winding lengthens the air's path inward over a short depth — had the hole been driven straight the distance would be short; it was so managed to lengthen the path lest over-hot and over-cold surprise its inside, reaching it gradually instead {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.217}
## The cavity and the nerve
The ear's hole leads to a cavity holding still air, its inner surface paved with fibres of the seventh nerve arriving from the fifth pair of the brain's nerves — hardened, extra-hardened, lest it be weak and too impressed by the air's striking and manner. When the sound wave arrives there, hearing perceives it. This nerve is to the states of hearing as the icy humour is to the states of sight; the rest of the ear's organs are as the coats and humours surrounding the icy humour, created for it to serve, protect or aid it; and the meatus is as the uveal aperture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.217}
## The wisdom of its cartilage and its place
The ear was created cartilaginous: were it flesh or membrane it would not keep its hollowed, winding shape; were it bony it would be hurt and hurt at every knock — so it was made cartilage, keeping shape with ease of bending. It was placed on the sides, for the front was fitter for sight and was occupied by the eye; and it was set under the hairline in man so as not to lie under the cover of hair or clothing. This organ's pains are deadly, and its diseases often bring hard fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.217}
