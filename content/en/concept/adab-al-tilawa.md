## Beginning with the isti'adha and the basmala
The Qur'an commands at the outset: "when you recite the Qur'an, seek refuge in God from the accursed Satan" (al-Nahl 98), and the reciters open with the isti'adha then the basmala, except at the head of Bara'a, as the manuals of performance record {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## The reciter's state
The books of etiquette name for the reciter purity, covering, facing the qibla, humility and pondering, and reciting unhurried in tartil rather than hadr — and God praised tilawa done as it deserves in "they recite it as it deserves to be recited" (al-Baqara 121) {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}. In the hadith of al-Bara' ibn 'Azib he heard the Prophet recite at 'isha' in one of the rak'as and draw out his voice, so that he heard no one with a finer voice — the hadith is in al-Bukhari and Muslim {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}.

## Listening, silence and the khatm
God says: "and when the Qur'an is recited, listen to it and be silent, that you may receive mercy" (al-A'raf 204). The commentators explain insat as silence for hearing the recitation — and on it rests the well-known disagreement over reciting behind the imam {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}. The early generations completed the Qur'an often in Ramadan, and people gathered for the du'a of the khatm; the books of reports tell of one of them that he gathered his family at the completion and said: mercy descends at the completion of the Qur'an {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.
