## Turning and looking
This dawar may come to a person because he has turned round on himself, so the vapours and spirits in him turn, as a cup holding water is turned for a while and then stops, while what is in it goes on turning for a while; when the pneuma turns, it seems to the person that things are turning; it may also come from looking at things that turn until that sensed form is fixed in the soul; the weaker the body, the stronger this effect in it, as in the sick {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.113} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.114}

## Bodily causes in the brain
Dawar may come from bodily causes present in the substance of the brain, arising in it from vapours moving in its vessels and in the nerve, or from humours of every kind confined in it, which vaporise at the slightest movement or heat; or from an abundance of vapours confined in it, risen to it from other places and settled there from an earlier acute illness or a cold illness; or from a bad temperament changing suddenly, from which follows an agitated movement in the pneuma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.114}

## A blow
It may come from an outside mover of the pneuma, such as one striking the head or breaking the skull so as to press the brain and the still pneuma, and various turning, wave-like movements follow, as happens in water when a weight falls on it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.114}

## Vapours rising from other organs
It may come from vapours rising to the brain while they rise, though not generated in its substance, and their rising is either in the passages of the nerve, from the stomach, by way of the stomach, the gall bladder, the bladder and the diaphragm when illnesses strike them or the humours in them move, most of it from the stomach; or in the veins and arteries, deep and outer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.114}

## Matter and sharing
The matter of the vapour may be bile or phlegm, and phlegmatic dawar is like epilepsy; the sharing that brings on sadar and dawar is often not for a matter that arrives but for a harm by a quality that reaches the brain, like what comes at emptiness and hunger to some people, especially one who cannot bear hunger, because the mouth of his stomach is harmed and the brain shares in it; dawar and sadar may come by way of a crisis, and recurring dawar, especially in the old, warns of apoplexy, as does dawar that comes after a lasting numbness of an organ; an incidental headache may resolve dawar and dawar may resolve a headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.115}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
In this chapter Ibn Sina names the womb with the stomach, the gall bladder, the bladder and the diaphragm among the organs from which the vapour rises, and says that most of it is from the stomach and, after it, from the womb that receives residues {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.114}
