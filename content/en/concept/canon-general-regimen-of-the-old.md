## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the third teaching, on the regimen of the old, in six chapters, with a general statement: the old are to be moistened and warmed together by longer sleep and more time in bed than the young, and by foods, baths and drinks, with their urine kept flowing and phlegm expelled; moderate rubbing with oil is of great benefit, then riding or walking {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.251}.

## The principle
Ibn Sina makes the whole of the regimen of the old a matter of using what moistens and warms together: longer sleep and more time in bed than the young, foods, baths and drinks, keeping the urine flowing and expelling phlegm, and keeping the bowels soft {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.251}.

## Rubbing and perfume
Moderate rubbing with oil, moderate in amount and in kind, benefits them greatly, followed by riding, or by walking for those too weak to ride. For the weak, the rubbing is repeated and doubled. They should often take care to use perfume, especially a moderately warm one, and be anointed with oil after sleep, which rouses the vital power, and then walk or ride {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.251}.
