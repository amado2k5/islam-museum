## Summary
Ibn Sina says black stool shows, like black urine, intense burning, the maturing of a black-bile illness, a dye taken in, or a drink that purges black bile; green shows the quenching of the innate heat; then he turns to its form, timing and the best stool. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.199}.

## Black and green
Black stool shows what black urine shows: intense burning, the maturing of a black-bile illness, taking a dye, or drinking a purge of black bile; the first is the bad one. Pure black bile is deadly in most cases when it comes out, whereas the black chyme often appears. Green stool shows the quenching of the innate heat and dullness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.199}.

## Form, timing and the best stool
Swollen stool like cow dung shows wind. If it comes quickly and before the habit it is a bad sign of much bile and weak retentive power; if slow, of weak digestion, cold bowels and much moisture. The best stool is gathered, uniform in its parts, as thick as honey, easy to pass, not biting, yellowish, not very foul, without rumbling or froth, at the usual time and about the amount eaten. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.199}.

## Evenness of stool and the particular book
Not every evenness or smoothness of stool is praised, for they may come from complete maturation or from uniform burning and melting, in which case they are among the worst signs. Stool of moderate consistency tending thin is praised if without rumbling and wind and not passed in little bits. The particular book gives fuller explanation of stool and urine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.199}.
