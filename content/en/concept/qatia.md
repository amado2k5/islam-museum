## Overview
*Qati'a* (allotment) in Basra and al-Sawad is land that the sultan or the governor allots to a man, who revives it and brings it into cultivation. This is what the report in *Futuh al-buldan* states. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Examples
Ziyad ibn Abi Sufyan used to allot a man an allotment and leave him for two years; if he cultivated it, well; if not, he took it back from him. He used to say that only what has been cultivated is carried out. Ziyad was the first to uproot the *'uthm* (a thorny plant) in Basra, and he allotted land to people in the villages whose inhabitants had died. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Some governors' allotments reached thousands of *jurib* (units of land): the allotment of Hilal ibn Ahwaz was eight thousand *jurib*, and that of Abd Allah ibn Umayr eight thousand. The surveyors of al-Mansur measured the allotment of the family of Abu Bakra, which was a hundred *jurib*, and found it to be a thousand. They allowed them to keep a hundred in their hands and took back the rest. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
