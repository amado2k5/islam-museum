## Course and outlook
The substance of the brain may also swell with it, by sharing or by transfer, which is very bad and kills in the fourth, and whoever passes it escapes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76} It seems not to be resolved except by sweat or nosebleed, and often the membrane and the vessels that leave the head swell until the shu'un almost open with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77} Confusion of mind made of weeping and laughing, hour after hour, is bad, and so is it when it is a transfer from dhat al-ri'a, for that shows the intensity of the humour's heat, and likewise if it passes to the unreal kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77}

## Signs of warning
If the heaviness lasts in the regions of the head and the lung, then convulsion comes with verdigris-coloured vomit, which the editor's footnote glosses as of the colour of verdigris, the patient dies within the hour, and his longest respite is a day or two if the power is strong {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77} When the birsam patient stares fixedly and vomits red bile repeatedly, if he is weak he dies the same day, and if strong after two days {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77} No one with swelling in the regions of the brain whose urine was watery has been seen to escape {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77}

## Praised signs and transfers
The most hopeful of the kinds of qaranitus is that the patient recalls what he raved after his fever eases, and when piles come upon them (the footnote glosses the printed hamuridus as piles) it is a praised sign {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77} Qaranitus often resolves through the piles when they flow; it may cool and pass into lethargus, or the patient may be free of it and fall into consumption (diqq) or madness; the unreal often passes into the real, and old men seldom escape it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77}
