## Nature and selection
The gum of tarthuth, sometimes called gold glue, because papers and quires are gilded with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.357}. Hot at the end of the second degree and dry in the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}.

## Temperament and actions
Its resolving and drying are strong, and its burning is not strong; it opens so far that blood flows from the mouths of the veins, and it has softening and drawing power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: applied and poulticed with vinegar and natron, it helps scrofula, hard swellings and cysts. Wounds and ulcers: it helps bad wounds, eats away diseased flesh and grows good flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Joints: taken with honey or barley water, it helps sciatica, the flank and the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Eyes: it softens roughness of the eyelids and mange, clears the whiteness of the cornea and helps the humours of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Chest: licked with honey or barley water, it helps asthma and laboured breathing, cleanses the sores of the diaphragm and helps suffocation from phlegm and black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Stomach: taken by mouth it helps hard spleen and liver and dropsy, and applied with vinegar it does too {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Excretory organs: it brings out urine so freely that blood is passed, kills tapeworm and purges. The book states that it expels a foetus, alive or dead, and brings on menses; this is the book's listing, not a recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Poisons: taken in a drink with bitter theriac, it is a counter to a poison the book calls taamaun {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Substitute: the wax-scrapings of the bee's cell {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}.
