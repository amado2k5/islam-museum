## Definition
Ilgha' is annulment in the lexical sense. Fariq is the agent of "to separate one thing from another". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3325} For the usulis, ilgha' al-fariq is the showing that a difference between root and branch has no effect on the ruling in analogy, so that the ruling holds for what they share. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3325}

## Examples
- Extending the manumission of part of a slave to the whole, as in the hadith of the two Sahihs about a man who frees his share of a slave, to a slave-girl: the difference is sex, which has no effect on manumission. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3325}
- Extending the penalty for false accusation of married men to that for married women: womanhood is a difference that has been annulled. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3325}

## Related terms
- Tanqih al-manat: a text that gives a cause by a description is read as general by removing the particular description through reasoning; the Hanafis call it istidlal. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3326}
- Sabr wa taqsim: listing the descriptions in the root and removing those unfit as causes, so that the remainder is the cause. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3326}
- The difference: in ilgha' al-fariq the annulled description is in the branch; in tanqih al-manat it is in the root. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3326}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3327}

## Ruling
Usulis differ on whether it is a path to the cause; some list it among them, and Ibn Qudama reports the disagreement over calling the extension of the unmentioned to the mentioned analogy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3326}

## Related places
It is treated under the cause in analogy, in the division of analogy into clear and hidden, and into analogy by cause, by indication, and in the sense of the root. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3326}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3327}
