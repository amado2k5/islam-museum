## Summary
Ibn Sina says very thick urine mostly shows lack of ripening and less often the ripening of thick humours, distinguishes what is passed at once from what is passed little by little, and explains why both thinness and thickness show lack of ripening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.189-190}.

## What thickness shows
Very thick urine shows in most cases lack of ripening, and in the fewest the ripening of humours thick in consistency, and it comes at the end of humoral fevers or at the bursting of swellings. The safest thick urine in fevers is that of which much is emptied at once; that which is emptied little by little shows many humours or weak strength. If thin urine turns thick in acute diseases and no relief follows, it shows melting away {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.189}.

## Thinness, thickness and lack of ripening
For a healthy person who keeps passing thick urine and feels pain about the head and a breaking in the limbs, it warns of fever. Thinness and thickness alike show lack of ripening because ripening is followed by moderation of consistency: the ripening of the thick is to be digested toward thinness, and of the thin to be cooked toward warmth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.189-190}.
