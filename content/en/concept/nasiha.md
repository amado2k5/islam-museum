## Overview
*Nasiha* (sincere counsel) is the wish for good for the one counselled, and doing good for him in intention and in deed. In the hadith of Tamim al-Dari it is said that "religion is sincere counsel": to God, to His Book, to His Messenger, to the leaders of the Muslims, and to their common folk. {cite:sahih-muslim}

## Its meaning according to Ibn Rajab
[[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]] relates from al-Khattabi that the root of *nasiha* in the language is sincerity. For God it means soundness of belief in His oneness and sincerity of intention in worshipping Him; for His Book, belief in it and acting by what it contains; for His Messenger, affirming his prophethood and giving obedience to him in what he commanded and forbade; and for the common Muslims, guiding them to their interests. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.193-204}

He relates from Ibn al-Salah a detailed account of the counsel due to each of these. He notes that counsel appears in the pledge of Jarir ibn Abd Allah: "on establishing prayer, giving the alms, and counsel to every Muslim." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.193-204}

## Its standing
Ibn Rajab relates from Abu Dawud that it is one of the hadiths on which jurisprudence turns, and from Muhammad ibn Aslam al-Tusi that it is one of the quarters of religion. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.193-204}

## Sincere advice in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episode 1)
The Sheikh recalls that religion is sincere advice, and lists those to whom it is owed: to God, to His messenger, to the leaders of the Muslims and to their common folk (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:01:30}
