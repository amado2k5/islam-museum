## Summary
Wadi Muhassir is the valley between Mina and Muzdalifa that belongs to neither; it is named for Abraha's elephant, which was exhausted there. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16598}

## Hastening through it
Pilgrims are recommended to hasten for about a stone's throw when they reach it after leaving Muzdalifa, following the Prophet, who moved his camel gently at its bed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16598} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16599}

## Prayer on passing it
On passing it, the passer-by is recommended to say what Umar ibn al-Khattab used to say. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16599}

## Standing there
Most jurists hold that Wadi Muhassir is neither Mina nor Muzdalifa, and that standing there does not suffice, on the words: 'the whole of Muzdalifa is a place of standing; leave the bed of Muhassir'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16599} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16600}
Some Shafi'is hold it part of Mina, citing the report of al-Fadl ibn Abbas. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16600}
