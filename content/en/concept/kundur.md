## Nature
Kundur is found in the lands the Greeks knew by a city of the same name, and in a country called al-Murbat, a sea port from which many ships bring it to traders; it also grows in India. Hunayn says the best comes from the Greek lands and is called the male, or sataunis; it is white, hard, does not break quickly, and when broken what is inside sticks when touched. Dioscorides adds a second kind called amumitus, white, whose rubbed scent is that of mastic. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}

## Adulteration and recognition
Kundur is adulterated with pine resin and gum arabic. The test is that gum arabic does not catch fire, pine resin smokes, and kundur burns; the adulterated kind can also be recognised by its smell. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}

## Temperament
Its shell is drying in the second degree and slightly colder than kundur. The kundur itself is hot in the second degree and drying in the first; its shell is drying about the third degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.516}

## Properties
Its drying force is not strong and its astringency is slight; the drying belongs to its shell. It ripens, does not sting the flesh, and stops bleeding. Taken in excess it burns the blood, and its smoke is more drying and astringent. Some say the red is clearer than the white, and that the fine powder is weaker than kundur itself. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.516}

## Cosmetic use, wounds and ulcers
With honey it removes the dark mark called dahas, and its shells are good for the traces that sores leave. It heals new wounds very well, stops malignant ulcers from spreading, and is used on the ulcers of the wedge-shaped kind (harfiya), on chilblains, and on burn ulcers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.516}

## Swellings and pustules
With chimolia (cimolia earth) and rose oil it helps hot swellings of the breast, and it enters the resolving poultices for swellings of the viscera. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.516}

## Organs of the head and eye
Organs of the head and eye: it is said to strengthen the mind, to clear scurf with natron, to ease a painful ear, and, with pitch, oil or milk and applied as a plaster, to help a cracked ear and stop haemorrhage from the membrane of the nose. It heals and fills eye ulcers, ripens a chronic swelling in the eye, its smoke helps hot swellings, and it checks watery discharge from the eye. The book counts it among the great remedies for a chronic red film over the eye, and says it helps eye cancer. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.516}

## Chest and digestion
Mixed with chimolia and rose oil it helps the hot swellings that come on the breast after childbirth, and it enters the remedies for the windpipe. It stops vomiting; its shell strengthens and tightens the stomach, heats the stomach more strongly, helps digestion, and suits a slack stomach best. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.516}

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling): the book says it stops bleeding from the womb and the anus, helps dysentery, and stops malignant ulcers of the anus from spreading. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.516} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}

## Fevers and poisons
It helps phlegmatic fevers. Taken in excess with wine it kills, and likewise with vinegar. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}
