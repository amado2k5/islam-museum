## Summary
Ibn Sina says the organs of nourishment are exercised along with the rest of the body, that sight is exercised by studying fine things and gently looking at high places, that hearing is exercised by listening for faint sounds and rarely for loud ones, and that each organ has its own exercise which he will give in the account of keeping each organ healthy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.223-224}.

## Sight
Sight is exercised by looking closely at fine things and, by degrees at times, by looking gently at heights; the eye has its own exercise in seeing the fine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.223-224}.

## Hearing
Hearing is exercised by listening for faint sounds and, rarely, by hearing great ones. The exercise of the organs of nourishment follows that of the rest of the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.223-224}.

## Each organ its own exercise
Each organ has an exercise of its own, he says, which he will give under the keeping of each organ's health when he takes up the book on particular matters; the throat has its exercise in raising the voice by degrees, and the tooth and ear likewise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.224}.
