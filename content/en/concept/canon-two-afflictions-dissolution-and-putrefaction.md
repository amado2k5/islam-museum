## Summary
Ibn Sina holds that bodies are exposed to two kinds of affliction, each with a cause from within and a cause from without: the gradual dissolving of the moisture we are made from, and its putrefaction, corruption and change from fitness to supply life, which in the end also ends in dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.200-201}.

## The two kinds
One affliction is the dissolving of the moisture from which we were created, which happens gradually. The other is the putrefaction of that moisture, its corruption and its change from being fit to supply life. Putrefaction first spoils the moisture and alters the state in which it suits our bodies, and in the last stage it dissolves because of the rot; rot first works on the moisture, then dissolves it and leaves a dry, ashen residue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.200-201}.

## Distinct from other harms
These two afflictions are apart from those that come from other causes, such as freezing cold, poisons, the kinds of fatal rupture of continuity and the other diseases. But the two named are the ones most worth weighing in preserving health, and each arises from external causes and from internal ones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.
