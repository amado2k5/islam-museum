## Summary
In chapter 18 Ibn Sina traces the causes of swelling to the side of the matter, which is fullness from the six things already named, and to the condition of the organ, namely the force of the expelling organ and the weakness of the receiving one and its readiness to take the superfluity, and notes that a fracture, bone and even a tooth may swell {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.147}.

## The matter and the organ
These causes are partly from the matter and partly from the condition of the organ. Those from the matter are fullness from the six things mentioned. Those from the organ's condition are the strength of the organ that expels and the weakness of the organ that receives and its readiness to take the superfluity, either by the nature of its substance, made for this, like skin, or by its looseness, like the soft flesh in the three bends behind the ear at the neck, the armpit and the arnaba {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.147}.

## Further causes
Also the width of the limb that leads to it and narrowness of the limb that leads from it, or its low position, or its smallness, so that it is too narrow for the nourishing matter that reaches it; or its weakness to digest its food through some defect; or a blow that confines matter in it; or its lacking the dissolving of what dissolves from it by exercise; or an excessive heat in it which draws. That heat is either natural, as in flesh, or acquired, produced by pain, violent movement or some warming thing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.147}.

## Fracture, bone and tooth
A fracture produces swelling through any of these causes, such as bruising, pressure on the organ and the stretching by which it is set. Bone itself, even a tooth, may swell, because it takes growth from food and takes wetness and putrefaction, and so takes swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.147}.
