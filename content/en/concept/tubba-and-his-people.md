## What the Qur'an says
In [[passage/quran-44-37|al-Dukhan 37]] the Qur'an asks the Quraysh whether they are better than the people of Tubba' and those before them, and says God destroyed them because they were criminals. In [[passage/quran-50-14|Qaf 14]] it comes back to the people of Tubba' among those who denied the messengers, so that the threat came due. The text does not name Tubba' himself, nor say what his people did or how they perished; it holds them up as an example. Al-Qurtubi reads the question as asking who had the greater prosperity, wealth, might and protection, the point being that the addressees are no better than those destroyed. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 44:37} {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 44:37} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 50:14}

## A title or a person
Al-Qurtubi says Tubba' is not one man but the kings of Yemen, whose rulers were called the tubba's, a title like the caliph, Kisra or Qaysar. He quotes Abu 'Ubayda that each was called tubba' because he follows his predecessor, and al-Suhayli that it is the title of whoever ruled Yemen with al-Shihr and Hadramawt; among them he lists al-Harith al-Ra'ish, Abraha Dhu al-Manar, 'Amr Dhu al-Adh'ar, Shammar, to whom Samarqand is attributed, and Ifriqis ibn Qays. He then judges that the verses most likely mean one of them whom the Arabs knew by the name better than the others. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 44:37} Ibn Kathir says the same: Himyar, who are Saba', called each king who ruled over them a tubba', as one says Kisra, Qaysar, Pharaoh or the Negus. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 44:37} In al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya the Arabs are said to call whoever ruled Yemen with al-Shihr and Hadramawt tubba'. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya on the genealogy of Saba' and Himyar}

## Tubba' in the commentaries
- **Al-Tabari:** reports Mujahid and Qatada that he was the Himyarite; Qatada that he marched with armies until he built al-Hira, then came to Samarqand and destroyed it; Ka'b that his description is that of a righteous man, God blaming his people and not him; 'A'isha that she used to say do not revile Tubba', for he was a righteous man; and Sa'id ibn Jubayr that he clothed the House and forbade reviling him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 44:37}
- **Al-Qurtubi:** judges that he is Abu Karib, who clothed the House after meaning to attack it, and who raided Medina meaning to ruin it, then turned back when told it was the refuge of a prophet named Ahmad. He gives disagreement about his status: Ibn 'Abbas says he was a prophet; Ka'b that he was a king whose people were soothsayers; al-Kalbi names him Abu Karib As'ad ibn Malikikarib. He mentions verses testifying to Ahmad and a letter Tubba' is said to have written declaring his belief, found in Ibn Ishaq's report and others, and a grave found at San'a with two women in it, said to be Tubba's daughters, reported by al-Zajjaj, Ibn Abi al-Dunya and al-Zamakhshari. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 44:37}
- **Ibn Kathir:** notes that Himyar called its king tubba', and that it happened that one of their tubba's left Yemen and reached Samarqand; he passed Medina and held back after two Jewish rabbis advised him, and at Mecca he honoured the Ka'ba and clothed it, then called his people to Judaism, which he says was the religion of Moses and on guidance before the Messiah. He suggests, God knows best, that he was first an unbeliever and then believed, and says Ibn 'Asakir mixed in some accounts this Tubba' with another who came long after, since the people of the Tubba' of the Qur'an believed at his hands but returned after his death to idols and fire, and God punished them. He calls him the middle Tubba', named As'ad Abu Karib ibn Malikikarib, and reports that they say he reigned 326 years and died some 700 years before the mission, whereas al-Qurtubi reports a thousand years between his death and the mission; both figures are reported, not established. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 44:37} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 44:37}

## In the sira
Ibn Hisham, from Ibn Ishaq, says Tuban As'ad Abu Karib was the one who came to Medina, took two rabbis of the Jews to Yemen, and built up and clothed the Sacred House; his reign was before that of Rabi'a ibn Nasr. He meant to ruin Medina, and the Ansar claim that they fought him by day and entertained him by night, until two rabbis of Banu Qurayza told him it was the refuge of a prophet who would come from Quraysh in the last days, so he turned back and followed their religion. He and his people had been idol-worshippers; Himyar took him to judgement by a fire in Yemen, from which the two rabbis came out unharmed, and Himyar then followed their religion. Ibn Hisham remarks that a poem containing a line on Tubba's anger at two tribes of Jews is fabricated and he does not include it. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira on the account of Tubban As'ad}

## The hadith on Tubba'
Two Prophetic reports mention him, and scholars graded them differently.

1. **The Prophet said he did not know whether Tubba' was accursed**, from Abu Hurayra, through 'Abd al-Razzaq, in Abu Dawud (4674) and al-Hakim. Al-Hakim called it sound on the two Shaykhs' condition, al-Dhahabi agreed, and al-Albani said "it is as they said". Al-Daraqutni, as Ibn 'Asakir quotes him, said 'Abd al-Razzaq was alone in it; al-Hanna'i called it strange and said Hisham ibn Yusuf's mursal version through al-Zuhri is the sounder, and al-Bayhaqi mentions something similar from al-Bukhari. Al-Albani answers that either the connected chain is preferable, since two reliable men agree on it, or Ibn Abi Dhi'b had both. He also reports Ibn 'Asakir's remark that the Prophet's doubt came before the matter was made clear to him and he was then told Tubba' had become Muslim. {cite:silsila-sahiha-albani-afia-201703 hadith 2217}
2. **"Do not revile Tubba', for he had become Muslim,"** transmitted by Ahmad from Sahl ibn Sa'd through Ibn Lahi'a, and by al-Tabarani from Ibn 'Abbas. Al-Albani calls both chains weak, because of Ibn Lahi'a and his teacher Abu Zur'a 'Amr ibn Jabir in the first and Ahmad ibn Abi Bazza in the second, but gathers supports: 'A'isha's statement, not raised, which al-Hakim graded sound and al-Dhahabi agreed; Wahb ibn Munabbih's mursal report, which he calls a good mursal support; and concludes that together they reach the grade of hasan at the very least. Ibn Kathir gives the report and adds 'Ata's statement that the Prophet forbade reviling him. {cite:silsila-sahiha-albani-afia-201703 hadith 2423} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 44:37}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni view, al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir:** Tubba' is either a title of the Himyarite kings or one of them known to the Arabs; the Qur'an blames his people and not him, and he was most probably a believer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 44:37} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 44:37} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 44:37}
- **Historians' critical view, Ibn Khaldun:** the reports that the tubba's raided the lands of the Turks and Tibet are weak because of the distance, the need for provisions and fodder, and the Persian and Roman peoples in the way; he reads Ibn Ishaq's "the last Tubba' marched east" as meaning Iraq and Persia, and says the tubba's only fought the Persians on the Iraqi frontier. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun on the errors of historians}
- **Hadith scholars:** the grade differs between the two reports: the first was graded sound by al-Hakim, al-Dhahabi and al-Albani and criticised for being mursal by al-Hanna'i; al-Albani calls the chains of the second weak but raises it to hasan by supports. {cite:silsila-sahiha-albani-afia-201703 hadith 2423}
:::

## What the text does not say
The Qur'an does not say whether Tubba' believed, which Tubba' is meant, or how his people were punished; everything about his belief, journeys and dates comes from the commentators and the chroniclers. I found no verified source for the names of the tubba's and the number of years they ruled. I did not read a recent academic study of the tubba's, so I attribute no scholarly view.
