## Digits and stay
The Moon's diameter is divided into twelve digits. The number of digits eclipsed is found by subtracting the Moon's latitude from the half-sum of the radii, multiplying the difference by twelve and dividing by the corrected diameter. If the remainder of the half-sum exceeds the Moon's diameter, the eclipse has a stay, that is a time in which the Moon lies wholly in the shadow; the minutes of this stay are what is left. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.151-152}

## The sequence of the steps
The result is multiplied by itself and subtracted from the square of the half-radii sum; the square root of what remains gives the minutes of fall and stay together, which are then divided by the Moon's hourly motion to give hours. The text then gives a second route: the remainder of the half-sum, whether smaller or larger than the diameter, is multiplied by twelve and divided by the diameter to give the digits. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.151-153}
