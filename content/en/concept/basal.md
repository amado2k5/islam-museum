## Nature and selection
A well-known vegetable. Along with its cutting pungency it has bitterness and astringency. The longer kind eaten is sharper, the red is sharper than the white, the dry is sharper than the fresh, and the raw is sharper than the roasted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot in the third degree, with a superfluous moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}. It thins and cuts, especially when eaten, and with its astringency it has strong polishing and opening power. It causes wind, and draws the blood outward so that it reddens the skin. Cooked onion gives little nourishment worth counting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}. Eaten onion alone helps against harm from water and against what its smell removes when its residue is thrown away {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: it reddens the face; its seed removes white patches and, rubbed on a site of alopecia, helps greatly; with salt it removes warts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}. Wounds and ulcers: its water helps dirty ulcers, and with chicken fat helps chafing of the shoe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}. Head: when its juice is sniffed it cleanses the head; drops in the ear for the residue of the head, ringing and pus in the ears; it causes headache, and too much of it causes stupor, harms the mind by producing bad humours, and increases saliva {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.390}. Eyes: the juice of the eaten onion helps watering of the eye and sharpens sight, and its juice mixed with honey is used as a kohl for white of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.391}. Chest: onion water with honey helps cough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.391}. Food organs: the wild kind is hard to digest, one kind causes vomiting, and the eaten kind, because of its bitterness, strengthens the stomach. Twice-cooked onion gives much food and thirst, and helps jaundice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.391}. Excretory organs: it opens the mouths of piles; all kinds of onion stir desire, and onion water promotes menses and loosens the bowels. The book's statements on menses and desire are recorded as written and not as advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.391}. Poisons: it helps the bite of a mad dog when its water is poured on the bite with salt and rue, and the eaten onion removes the harm of the wind of poisons {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.391}.
