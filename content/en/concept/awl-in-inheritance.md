## Summary
'Awl (proportional reduction) occurs when the shares of the heirs exceed one whole, so a reduction is applied proportionally to the Qur'anic sharers so that none of them is excluded {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 335}. The example the author gives is a husband and two full sisters: the husband receives one-half and the two sisters two-thirds, making a total of seven-sixths, so the estate is divided into seven parts {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 335}.

## Its Effect on the Dispersal of the Estate
The author argues that 'awl increases the dispersal of the estate because it reduces some heirs' shares to make room for others {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 335}. He explains that the disagreement among legal schools over al-radd (return of residue) and its prohibition affects whether the estate is concentrated or dispersed {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 335}.

## The View of Ibn 'Abbas
The author notes that some of the early authorities, such as Ibn 'Abbas and those who agreed with him, do not accept 'awl {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 336}. He states that both positions effectively involve a reduction: the first applies the reduction to all heirs, and the second to some of them {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 336}.
