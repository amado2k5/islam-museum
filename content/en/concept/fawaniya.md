## Nature
It is ud al-salib, of which there is a male and a female; the male has white roots, thick as fingers, astringent in taste, and the female has many branches of root and stems {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Temperament
Hot, but not intense {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Actions and properties
It has drying and astringency together with dissolving, opening, attenuating, cutting and cleansing, and when chewed for a while a sharpness inclined to astringency appears in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Cosmetics
It cleanses the black marks in the skin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Joints
Beneficial in gout {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Organs of the head
It benefits epilepsy, even when hung on the person, and its hanging has been tried and found to prevent, so that when it was removed the epilepsy returned. The Jew said that fumigation with its fruit benefits the mad and the epileptic and cures them, and likewise its fruit drunk with jalanjabin benefits greatly. Ibn Sina remarks that this may be a kind of Roman peony, since what reaches us from India has no great effect in this matter; and its seed drunk with wine benefits nightmare {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It restrains the bowels when cooked in astringent drinks and stops the matters flowing to the stomach; its seed strengthens the stomach and calms its pains and stinging, and its root benefits jaundice and opens the obstructions of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Excretory organs
Drinking it also promotes urine; its root benefits pain of the kidneys and bladder, and its decoction in wine restrains the bowels and promotes urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that drunk with wine and with diuretics it stirs the menses, that its seed with wine benefits suffocation of the womb and stops a flow of blood, and that its root is given to the woman in childbed and cleanses her of the superfluities of childbirth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.632}
