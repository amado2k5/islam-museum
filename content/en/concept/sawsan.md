## Nature
The book reports from Dioscorides a plant whose leaf is like that of kasiqun but broader and larger, with a stem bearing a drooping flower of colours that resemble one another, white, yellow, purple and the colour of the sky, and for this variety of colours it is likened to the rainbow; its roots are hard, knotted and of good scent, and when pulled up they should be dried in the shade and stored. Another kind is white and bitter and weaker than the first, the irsa is the root of this sawsan, and the garden kind holds a delicate earthy part that took on bitterness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Temperament
The white garden kind known as sawsan azad is hot and dry to the second degree, and the wild irsa is stronger in warming and drying {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Actions and properties
It cleanses and dries with moderation, its root cleanses more, its oil is subtler because its flower is subtler, and it is astringent as well, with relief for pains and putrefactions, its power warming and thinning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Cosmetics
It helps freckles and spots, especially the root, and washing the face with it cleans and polishes it and removes its tightness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Swellings and pustules
A poultice of the pounded leaf and seed with wine is said to help erysipelas greatly, and likewise raw phlegmatic swellings, ulcerating scab, scabs of burns and ringworm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It fills ulcers with good flesh, the root helps scalds from hot water, and so does the leaf, and it heals over; it is best used with rose oil, the juice of the irsa is for chronic ulcers and wounds, and the garden kind is the best of drugs for a scald from hot water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Joints
It is good for severance of a nerve and for those with spasm in the nerve, benefiting them greatly, and helps sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Organs of the head
A decoction of the root is used as a mouth rinse for toothache, especially of the wild kind; it brings sleep, its oil suits scalp ulcers and dandruff, dropped in the ear it calms ringing, with vinegar and rose oil it is a poultice that helps headache, and smeared on the nose it removes the soft moisture that appears on the outside of it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Organs of the chest
The root helps orthopnoea, especially the irsa, suits cough, and thins the moistures of the chest that are hard to clear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It helps the spleen, but is bad for the stomach, especially its oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Excretory organs
It helps colic, with henbane seed and wheat flour the cooked root calms hot swellings of the testicles, it suits those with bilious ileus, the oil of irsa opens the mouths of piles, and so does the root of sawsan in any form, and taken with vinegar it helps those who discharge fluid with intercourse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Fevers
It helps cold and shivering {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Poisons and bites
It helps against the stings of vermin, especially the scorpion, the plant, its juice and its drink, and its oil is an antidote to henbane, coriander and mushrooms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists its oil as opening, dissolving and softening the hardness of the womb, drunk and rubbed on, says it has no equal in diseases of the womb, that it expels the foetus, that with wine it provokes the menses, and that the boiled root and its water, used as a fomentation by women, helps pains of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.591}.
