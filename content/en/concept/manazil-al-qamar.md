## Summary
The lunar mansions are twenty-eight mansions of stars which the moon traverses in its month; al-Farghani lists them by their Arabic names in chapter twenty, because most people know them by those names {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 53}.

## Names and order
The mansions begin with al-Sharatayn at the head of Aries, and run: al-Batayn, al-Thurayya, al-Dabaran, al-Haq'a, al-Han'a, al-Dhara', al-Nathra, al-Tarf, al-Jabha, al-Zubara, al-Sarfah, al-'Awwa', al-Simak al-A'zal, al-Ghafr, al-Zubana, al-Iklil, Qalb al-'Aqrab, al-Shawla, al-Nu'ayyim, al-Balda, Sa'd al-Dhabih, Sa'd Bul', Sa'd al-Su'ud, Sa'd al-Akhbiyya, Fargh al-Dalw al-Muqaddam, Fargh al-Dalw al-Mu'akhkhar and al-Hut {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 51}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 52}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 53}.

## Description of their stars
Al-Farghani describes each mansion by its stars' number and form: al-Sharatayn are two separated stars at the head of Aries, al-Thurayya seven small stars gathered together, al-Dabaran a red star ringed by small ones called the Qala'is, and al-Haq'a three scattered stars at the head of Gemini {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 53}. Al-Iklil has three fixed rounded stars, and al-Hut northern stars following al-Sharatayn {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 52}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 51}.

## Mansions and risings
Al-Farghani notes that a mansion rises in the east at dawn while its counterpart sets in the west, so the mansions are counted by their rising from the sun's rays, and each mansion rises one night while its counterpart sets {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 36}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 38}.
