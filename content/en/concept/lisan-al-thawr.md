## Nature and selection
An herb with broad leaves like marjoram (maru), rough to the touch, its woody stems like the legs of locusts, its colour between green and yellow. The Khurasani kind should be used, with thick leaves on whose face are dots that are the bases of thorns or down. What is found in these countries and used by physicians is mostly a kind of maru, which is not lisan al-thawr and does not give its benefit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.541}.

## Temperament
Near the moderate in heat, tending to slight heat, and at the end of the first degree in moisture; the dry is less moist. Al-Khuzi said it is cold and moist at the end of the second degree, which is far from the truth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.541}.

## Actions and properties
The strength of its burnt ash removes thrush of children and quiets burning of the mouth, and the herb itself does the same, though weaker {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Chest and breathing
It gladdens and strengthens the heart and is good for dread and palpitation in wine, and for black-bile ailments. Some give it, with Armenian bole, to one with hot palpitation. It helps cough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.
