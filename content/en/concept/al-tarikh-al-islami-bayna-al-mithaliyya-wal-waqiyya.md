## Summary
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] devoted an episode of [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|Al-Wasatiyya]] to "Islamic history between idealism and realism". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:02:30} Opening it, he said history is neither time nor a chain of successive events but the movement of people in time and place, made by human beings with their convictions and abilities; since human beings are imperfect by nature, this shows as imperfection in history. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:01:00} He held that written history, even what great scholars wrote, contains what is authentic and sound alongside intrusions that do not fit the truth, and that we study history to build a future that learns from its mistakes and corrects them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:01:30} He said he wanted to acknowledge the mistakes of past politicians, soldiers and even some scholars, while taking pride in the great achievements and the civilisation, and asked: is all our history spotless, as some portray it, or all dark apart from early Islam, as others claim? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:02:30} The subject touches the entry [[concept/ilm-al-tarikh|the science of history]].

## First poll: conflict or civilisation-building
The presenter asked whether most periods of Islamic history were conflict and greed: 22% agreed and 78% disagreed, and he said he had expected a different result. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:05:00} These are the votes of that episode's studio audience. A young woman in the audience saw a balance between conflict and peace, and a young man held that had it been all conflict no great Islamic civilisation would have arisen, and that the fault lies with the writers of history, who passed on the conflicts and left out much of the civilisation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:06:30}

## "History is creed": al-Dughaym's view and the debate around it
The presenter introduced Mahmud al-Sayyid al-Dughaym as a fellow of the Centre of Islamic Studies at the University of London, a doctor from the University of Salford in legal theory and history, and a member of the Union of Arab Historians, and said he had heard from him before the show that he considers history a creed and Islamic history ideal. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:07:30} Al-Dughaym explained that Islamic history is what concerns Islam and Muslims, that a Muslim historian is one committed to Islam, and that one who follows whims he does not count among Muslim historians. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:09:00} Asked whether he places all non-Sunni groups among "the people of whims", he said yes, naming the Shu'ubis among others. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:09:30} He said the first to begin this luminous history was the Prophet Muhammad and those who followed his path. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:10:00}

When the presenter distinguished history from the writing of history, al-Dughaym held that writing embodies the reality of history, and that the method of [[concept/jarh-wa-tadil|al-jarh wa-l-ta'dil]] used to assess hadith transmitters can be applied to historians. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:11:00} As an example of false reports he named [[work/al-aghani-abu-l-faraj|al-Aghani]] of [[person/abu-l-faraj-al-isfahani|Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani]], which he said orientalists treat as a main source though to him it is full of lies; when the presenter objected that it is a book of literature, not history, al-Dughaym answered that literature is one of history's vessels. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:11:30} He acknowledged that books of exegesis and history contain [[concept/israiliyyat|Isra'iliyyat]], flagged by Ibn Kathir and others, that the historian's task is to examine reports from every angle and compare them, and that what contradicts the Sunni position is set aside. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:13:00} He said sound Islamic history is sacred to him because it is religious history and an embodiment of the message, and that he is ready to defend it in every field. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:14:30}

Abdallah al-Khabbas replied that Islamic history cannot be ideal or rise to the level of creed, since creed is taken from the Qur'an and Sunna alone, while history was written by men, some of whom had partisan or sectarian leanings and wrote about their opponents with distortion. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:15:30} He asked al-Dughaym about [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj]]; al-Dughaym prayed for mercy on him, and al-Khabbas objected that al-Hajjaj killed [[person/said-ibn-jubayr|Sa'id ibn Jubayr]] and bombarded [[person/abdullah-ibn-al-zubayr|Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr]] and crucified him by the Ka'ba. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:16:30} Al-Dughaym said he had not called the killing of Sa'id ibn Jubayr right, only pointed to a merit of al-Hajjaj, the conquest of Sind and the Punjab, recounting that he sent the army without consulting the caliph when he heard that Muslim women had been captured on the pilgrim road and had called on him for help. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:19:00} This is his account.

## Second poll: did historians write neutrally?
The presenter asked whether the great historians were swayed by their doctrinal and political leanings and were not neutral: 62% said yes and 38% that they wrote neutrally, a result he said surprised him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:22:00}

Dr Nama held that the great scholars wrote history in an unprecedented way, narrating events with [[concept/isnad|chains of transmission]] as in hadith; citing the maxim "whoever gives a chain has referred you", she said the historian passes on the report with its chain, even from a liar, and leaves the reader the tools to judge, which she called "the height of fairness". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:24:00} She said [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]], on the killing of al-Husayn ibn Ali, gave both the reports that support the Shi'a and those against them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:24:00} The presenter said that when he read these reports he was influenced even by the false ones, because the historian did not tell him they were false but said "[[person/abu-mikhnaf-lut-ibn-yahya|Abu Mikhnaf]] said". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:24:30} He contrasted [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Imam Ahmad]], who he said selected his Musnad, more than thirty thousand hadiths, from seven hundred thousand, with al-Tabari, who did not select and did not call his book "the sound history". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:25:30} These are his figures.

Salah al-Din al-Jourchi, a Tunisian researcher and journalist, held that a historian, even a praying Muslim, is a human being living in a period riven by conflicts and interests who cannot fully step outside it, and so selects reports according to his influences, relations and ties. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:26:30} He said the historian tries to come as close to objectivity as he can, and does not merely pass on reports but discusses them, links them to events and interprets them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:27:30} He described Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a as "a political party" with its own outlook and position, which is his view, and said that for this reason the writing of history cannot be given a sacred character. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:28:00} He recalled reading [[person/sayyid-qutb|Sayyid Qutb]]'s [[work/maalim-fi-al-tariq|Milestones]] in the early days of the Islamic movement, called its chapter "a unique Qur'anic generation" "brilliant but booby-trapped", and held that the Companions' generation excelled in many things but was a human generation, and that stripping it of its human and historical frame leads to errors in activism, politics and culture. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:29:30}

## Islamic history and the history of Muslims
The presenter introduced Bassam as a former minister, member of parliament and university professor of sharia. Bassam said calling history a creed is unacceptable by any measure, and distinguished "Islamic history", meaning the stories of the prophets in the Qur'an, what is related of the Prophet and his campaigns, and what is in the hadith, from "the history of Muslims" after that, which is neither creed nor sacred. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:31:00} He held that in studying history no one should be judged to paradise or hell, only the act assessed, and that he rejects turning a blind eye to history's mistakes and hiding them from the young, since history is studied for its lessons. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:32:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:32:30}

The presenter asked him about the method reported from Imam Ahmad and others of refraining from the disputes among the Companions: is that not a method of looking away, meant to avoid speaking wrongly of the Companions? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:33:00} Bassam answered that this differs from studying an event and checking whether it is sound, said Islamic history has not been verified as Imam Ahmad's Musnad was, and noted that [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|al-Albani]] used to say "tala'a al-badru 'alayna" is not established. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:33:30} He said he does not accept the approach of those who curse the figures of history, and gave as an example of human error even among the Rightly Guided Caliphs that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] refused to build a fleet, fearing for the Muslims because he did not know the sea, while [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman ibn Affan]], who knew travel, built it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:35:00} This is his account.

Al-Dughaym refused to count Umar's position as a mistake and objected to what he called boldness against the Companions by readers of the orientalists. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:35:30} He held that the campaign against [[person/harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]], who raided one year and made the pilgrimage the next, arose, as he put it, because he ended the Barmakids and humbled Nikephoros, emperor of the Byzantines, so that the [[concept/shuubiyya|Shu'ubis]], the Byzantines and their followers turned on him; he noted that Abu Yusuf, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan and Malik lived in his time. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:36:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:36:30} He mentioned a shrine to Abu Lu'lu'a, the killer of Umar ibn al-Khattab, visited today; the presenter confirmed it exists and said it was not the episode's subject. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:37:30} Al-Dughaym clarified that he had not said history is like faith in the Qur'an and Sunna, but that a committed historian who reports every account is neutral, and that he holds sacred what is sound, not mistakes or false reports. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:37:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:39:00}

## Reason in criticising reports
Dr Nama said that if Muslims failed to verify history, its writers are not to blame, and that the parts of history found in the hadith collections are the ones that have been verified. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:40:30} The presenter said the books contain reports from "a man I met at the mosque door" whose identity is unknown, that we have the right to doubt such reports and pause over them until history is verified as hadith was, and to say a writer may have been swayed by political, doctrinal or sectarian leanings, something done by the people of whims and by some Sunnis too; Nama held that the last point can only be said after verification. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:42:30}

The presenter said some reports can be judged by reason without verification, citing the biographical report that a certain worshipper prayed a thousand rak'as each night: if the night in northern Iraq is at most ten hours, that is a hundred rak'as an hour, a rak'a and a half a minute, and he said: "this is a marathon, not worship". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:45:30} He said the books are full of exaggerations and implausible numbers, while affirming his respect for the great scholars. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:44:30}

Muhammad Ijmi'an held that history was written after events had happened, and that applying the standard of hadith criticism would drop many reports from al-Tabari's history, first among them the reports of the ridda that he narrated from Sayf ibn Umar and that all later writers took from him, since hadith critics do not accept Sayf's reports. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:45:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:46:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:47:30} He noted that al-Tabari says in his preface that he passed the reports on as they reached him, and that [[person/al-baladhuri|al-Baladhuri]], who lived under about seven Abbasid caliphs, in his reading supported the authorities, left out many events and did not give the opposition's view. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:49:00}

Al-Jourchi said we look in history for two things: the truth, and the laws that governed events and their development; hence the importance of [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]], who laid down in his [[work/al-muqaddima|Muqaddima]] theoretical principles of historical development that let him criticise reports and reassemble events, something still lacking today. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:49:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:50:30} He held that our history, though a history of civilisation, is also a history of continuous revolts between central power and its opponents at home and on the frontiers. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:50:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:51:00}

## The presenter's experience: civilisational history
The presenter said he had written a complete history of Palestine and of al-Andalus, and had finished a not-yet-published book on the whole history of Islam. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:51:00} He said a reader of our history books comes away with a political and military history full of conflict, strife and conspiracies, though Muslims built a civilisation, led humanity and taught Europe how to leave its dark ages, and asked why civilisational history was not written at the same length and in the same sequence. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:52:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:52:30} He said he tried in his book to write political and military history together with the history of civilisation, which scholars appeared, when, and what they added, giving among his examples that Muslims brought lemons to Europe, taught Europeans that tomatoes are not poisonous, and from al-Andalus taught them to bathe, alongside astronomy, medicine, engineering and algebra. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:53:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:53:30} These are his examples.

## The orientalists
Bassam said the [[concept/orientalism|orientalists]] slipped poison into history, trying to belittle the great and magnify the small; the presenter explained to the audience that they are non-Muslim researchers, mostly European, who wrote with precision and a method that looks scientific, some of it carrying attacks on Islamic history. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:53:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:54:00} Bassam said Abdallah Laroui wrote in his book on the Arabs and historical thought that the Muslims went out in search of food and drink, noted that al-Siba'i wrote "Min Rawa'i' Hadaratina" on endowments and more, and asked those orientalists who say Islam spread by the sword how it reached Indonesia and Africa. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:55:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:55:30} He said a reader of [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]]'s [[work/tarikh-al-khulafa|History of the Caliphs]] finds that most caliphs were assassinated, cited [[person/muhammad-qutb|Muhammad Qutb]] on caliphs who spent public money without account, in contrast to Abu Bakr, who was given only what he had earned from his work, and urged the young not to fear criticising history with sound intent: what was good and established we take pride in, and of what was negative we say he erred. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:55:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:56:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:56:30}

The presenter said some Arab Christian writers wrote novels on Islamic history, such as [[person/jurji-zaydan|Jurji Zaydan]], whom he read at fifteen before studying sharia and was influenced by, and that a reader of his novel on the conquest of Egypt comes away thinking the Muslims conquered Egypt because Amr ibn al-As loved a woman; he said history is not taken from romantic novels. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:57:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:58:30} He noted that major orientalists wrote detailed histories that became references for Arab writers such as al-Aqqad. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:58:30}

## Third poll: orientalists and scientific method
The presenter asked whether orientalists wrote Islamic history with a more scientific method than Muslim historians: 45% said yes and 55% no, and he said he had expected most people to be against them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 00:59:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:01:30} He said he had read their books and lived in the West, and saw no comparison in method: the orientalist mentions nothing without stating where he took it from and how he analysed it, "while our people go by emotions and feelings". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:02:00}

Dr Nama disagreed: the chain of transmission in historical reports is a distinction the West acknowledged before the East did, and the orientalists' method is scientific on the surface but not underneath; she cited what [[person/ignaz-goldziher|Goldziher]] wrote about al-Zuhri and the Dome of the Rock, what some wrote about [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], and their selection of reports and sources that serve their purpose. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:03:30}

Al-Dughaym said most of us have not read what our historians wrote, citing that [[work/tarikh-al-rusul-wa-al-muluk|al-Tabari's History]] relates under the year 27 AH that Uthman ordered the conquest of al-Andalus and an army entered it without settling, and that the conquest of Sind began under Uthman before al-Hajjaj. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:05:30} He said German orientalists edited al-Tabari's history in the nineteenth century and later Arabic printings reproduced their text, as with Ibn al-Athir's [[work/al-kamil-fi-al-tarikh|al-Kamil]], that he does not trust a text edited by a non-Muslim, and that this is a failing of Muslims. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:07:00} He said the book al-Imama wa-l-Siyasa is falsely attributed to [[person/ibn-qutayba|Ibn Qutayba]], in his view. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:07:30} He recounted comparing manuscripts of the "Ta'wilat al-Qur'an" attributed to [[person/ibn-arabi-muhyi-al-din|Muhyi al-Din ibn Arabi]] in the Ragip Pasha and Süleymaniye libraries in Istanbul and finding it identical, letter for letter, to the work of Abd al-Razzaq al-Kashani, so that in his view its attribution to Ibn Arabi has stained his reputation for centuries. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:08:30} This is his account and has not been checked here.

Al-Dughaym said the last seven years of al-Suyuti's life fell in the reign of [[person/qansuh-al-ghawri|Qansuh al-Ghawri]], who, he said, leaned to Shi'ism and allied with Shah Isma'il the Safavid against Sultan Selim I, so that Shi'i hadiths entered what al-Suyuti wrote in that period, citing al-Durr al-Manthur on [[passage/quran-2-223|al-Baqara 223]]; this is his claim and has not been verified here. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:09:30} He said half of Islamic history is Ottoman, that whoever writes Ottoman history from Safavid, English, Russian or French sources writes "with a spiteful spirit", and advised anyone who does not know Ottoman Turkish to stay away from that history. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:10:30}

Abdallah al-Khabbas, a professor of modern literature, said there is a difference between the akhbari, the compiler of reports, and the historian: the historian has a view of what he reports, while the compiler passes reports on without intervening, which is not neutrality. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:12:30} He challenged the measure of sectarian belonging with the example of [[person/al-mutanabbi|al-Mutanabbi]] and his ties to the courts of [[person/sayf-al-dawla|Sayf al-Dawla]] and [[person/kafur-al-ikhshidi|Kafur al-Ikhshidi]], asking what heroism, sanctity or idealism lies down that road. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:13:00}

## Al-Jourchi's summing-up
Al-Jourchi summed up the discussion in five points: Islamic history needs critical reviews, so it is not sacred; no report is excluded but each is verified, however much we disagree with its narrators; history is a science and science means methods, so modern methods should serve in rewriting it; what is needed is a civilisational history of the community, not a history of ruling families; and religious faith must be distinguished from historical argument, so that the writing of history is not given an ideological cast. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:14:30} The presenter said these were the points he had meant to make. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:14:30}

## The presenter's closing (his view)
The presenter said the episode's aim was not to confuse the young but to let them know that the history in the books is not pure: it carries whims and political and sectarian loyalties, and literature and love poetry sometimes entered it and distorted the image of some caliphs such as Harun al-Rashid, so history should not be taken from literary books such as al-Aghani. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:15:30} He held that history must be rewritten by verifying the chains of transmission, that orientalists wrote with a scientific method that is not neutral, and that he has seen hardly any book, by orientalists or others, written neutrally. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:16:30} He said great effort went into purifying creed and verifying hadith, and much into verifying the Prophet's biography, but little into verifying history and recasting it for the community, and he placed this task on scholars and historians. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:17:00}

He compared the movement of history to the stock market, with rises and falls but a general trend that can be upward: whoever takes only the low points says our history is black, and whoever takes only the high points says it is spotless. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:18:00} He said Muslims spent about a thousand years rising, with black spots in it, and about four hundred years declining, with white spots in it, that what they gave humanity no other nation has given, and he urged the young to take pride in their history. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep39 01:18:30}
