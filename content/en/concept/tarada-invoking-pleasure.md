## Summary
Tarada is asking for pleasure, a formula in which one invokes God's pleasure upon the person named. Its rule depends on who is named. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4967} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4968}

## Definition and its difference from tarahhum
Rida is the opposite of anger, and tarada for a person is a formula that invokes God's pleasure upon him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4967}
Tarahhum invokes mercy and tarada invokes pleasure; both are invocations. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4967}

## The ruling by the person named
- Those whose prophethood is disputed, such as Dhu al-Qarnayn, Luqman and Dhu al-Kifl: invoking pleasure upon them is recommended. Al-Nawawi preferred the formula of pleasure over that of blessing, since their rank is not that of prophets and their prophethood is not established. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4967}
- The Companions: invoking pleasure upon them is recommended by agreement, since they strove for God's pleasure and did what pleases Him. When a Companion's father was also a Companion, the formula names both; when the grandfather is one too, all three are named. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4967} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4968}
- Others: the author of Umdat al-Abrar holds that it is allowed for the early shaykhs and scholars, on the general words of the verse of surah al-Bayyina (98:7-8) about believers. Scholars have long begun their writings with this formula. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4968}

## Keeping the formula
One should keep writing the formula for the Companions and the early scholars and the righteous, and not tire of repeating it. A listener who hears the Companions named is recommended to invoke pleasure upon them; the detail is under the entry on the sermon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4968}
