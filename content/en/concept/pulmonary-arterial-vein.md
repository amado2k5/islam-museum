## Summary
The arterial vein in Ibn Sina is the vessel that parts from the hollow vein at the heart as three vessels to the lung, made with two coats like the arteries, which is why it has this name {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.89}.

## Its structure
Facing the heart the hollow vein leaves three vessels going from it to the lung, standing out at the origin of the arteries near the left and bending in the right cavity toward the lung. It was made with two coats, like the arteries, and so is called the arterial vein {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.89}.

## The two benefits
The first benefit is that what seeps from it is blood extremely thin and akin to the lung's substance, since this blood is newly from the heart and has not ripened in it as far as the blood poured into the venous artery. The second is that the blood ripens in it a little further {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.89}.
