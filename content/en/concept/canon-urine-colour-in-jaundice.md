## Summary
Ibn Sina says that in jaundice urine that grows redder, toward black, and stains cloth is the safer when abundant, while white or faintly red urine with the jaundice persisting raises fear of dropsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.185}.

## Red and white
In jaundice, the redder the urine, up to black, staining a cloth with a dye that does not wash out, and the more of it there is, the safer. If the urine is white, or only faintly red, while the jaundice remains as it was, dropsy is feared. Hunger is among the things that greatly increase and sharpen the dye of urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.185}.
