## Summary
Ajal in the lexicon is the term of a thing, the time when it falls due. The participle ajil (deferred) is the opposite of 'ajil (immediate). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1654}

## Uses in the Qur'an
The word ajal is used of the end of life, of the fixed term for paying a debt, and of a period or time generally. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1654}

## Deferral in contracts
Most jurists allow a power of attorney to be dated to a future time, and a salam to fix its object to a known time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1656}

## Deferral in writing of freedom, blood-money and rent
Staggered payment in a writing of freedom is a kind of term, and blood-money for quasi-intentional and accidental killing is owed by the kin in three yearly instalments. A deferred rent runs to its term; a staggered rent follows what the parties agreed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1657}

## Term for expediting
This is the time mentioned in a contract to bring its effects forward; the jurists treat it in hire. If the term of a lease is fixed, the jurists do not allow fixing the work as well; Abu Hanifa, al-Shafi'i and the Hanbalis agree. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1657}

## Divisions by source
A term is shar'i (fixed by the law), qada'i (fixed by a judge) or ittifaqi (fixed by agreement). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1658}

## Shar'i term: pregnancy
The shortest and longest periods of pregnancy were derived from the Qur'an. A report says 'Umar meant to stone a woman who bore a child after six months, and 'Ali stopped him; jurists then derived that the least gestation is six months, from the two verses on suckling and weaning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1658}

## Shar'i term: truce and the lost property
A truce with the polytheists may be as long as the interest of Muslims requires; al-Shafi'i and one Hanbali view limit it to ten years. The term for announcing a lost thing is fixed by law, from the report of Zayd ibn Khalid al-Juhani: know its tie and its container, announce it for a year. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1659}

## Shar'i term: impotence and vow of abstention
A husband's impotence is given a year, which the report from 'Umar, 'Ali and Ibn Mas'ud is said to fix, since the four seasons pass in it. A man who swears not to approach his wife is given four months by the text of the Qur'an. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1660}

## Shar'i term: suckling
The jurists differ on the period of suckling. The majority take two years from the report 'there is no suckling except within the two years'; Abu Hanifa takes thirty months from the verse on bearing and weaning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1661}

## Agreed term: option of stipulation
Most jurists allow an option by stipulation; Abu Hanifa, Zufar and al-Shafi'i limit it to three days or less, while Abu Yusuf, Muhammad and the Hanbalis allow a known term however long, on the report about Hibban ibn Munqidh and the reasoning that deferral is needed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1662}

## Shar'i term: the courses
Among the Shafi'is and Hanbalis the least menstrual period is a day and night and the most fifteen days. The Hanafis take three days as the least and ten as the most; a view of Abu Yusuf takes two days and the greater part of a third. The Malikis set no least period in time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1663}

## Age of menopause and the period of menstruation
Jurists differ on fixing the age at which menstruation ends. Some do not fix an age; others fix fifty years, and a report from 'A'isha says a woman who reaches fifty has left the limit of menstruation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1664}

## Postnatal bleeding
A woman after childbirth leaves prayer for forty days unless she sees purity earlier. This is the view of the majority, including the Hanafis, Hanbalis, al-Thawri and Ishaq, relying on a report from Umm Salama. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1665}

## Duration of wiping over socks and the distance of travel
The duration of wiping over socks is fixed at three days and nights for a traveller and one day and night for a resident, while the Malikis do not fix a time for it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1666} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1667} The Hanafis define the journey that changes the rules as three days and nights by camel or on foot; Abu Yusuf puts it at two days and most of a third. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1668}

## Judicial term
The judicial term is the term that a judge sets for the parties to attend or for a proof to be produced. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1668}

## Stipulating delay of delivery of a thing
Jurists differ on whether a sale may stipulate that delivery of the thing sold is deferred for a known period. The Malikis and Hanbalis, and a weaker Shafi'i view, allow it, on the general command to fulfil contracts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1669}

## Term for the capital of salam
Most jurists require the capital of a salam to be paid before the parties separate; deferring it turns the matter into a debt sold for a debt. The Malikis allow the payment to be deferred for up to three days. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1671}

## Price after rescission, and loans
Rescission restores the original position according to the majority, so a deferred price stipulated in it is void while the rescission stands; the Malikis treat rescission as a sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1672} The return of a loan is due at once in the majority view; a stipulated deferral is void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1672} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1673}

## Term for blood-money
Blood-money for intentional killing is due at once from the killer's property, according to the majority. The Hanafis distinguish blood-money agreed by settlement, which is immediate, from blood-money owed when retaliation falls by a doubt, which is paid over three years. For quasi-intentional killing it is paid over three years by the kin, according to the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1673}

## Counting the term
The month is taken as thirty days by agreement when the crescents cannot be used. For a term of three months, the schools differ: some count two months by the moon and one by thirty days. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1684} A term begins from the time the parties set, and from the contract if they set none. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1684}

## Terms by feasts and doubtful terms
A term may be set to a known feast such as the Feast of Fitr or Adha. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1684} Where a term may mean one of two things, it is taken in its customary sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1684}

## A term that is unknown but close
The Hanafis do not allow a sale to an unknown term even when the uncertainty is small, as at harvest or threshing; if the buyer cancels the term before it falls due, the sale becomes lawful according to Abu Hanifa, Abu Yusuf and Muhammad, but not according to Zufar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1685} The Shafi'is hold the contract void, since the term must be known and fixed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1685} The Hanbalis hold that the term is void and the sale stands. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1685} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1686}

## A term that cannot be fixed
The jurists agree that a term may not be set to an event whose time cannot be known or fixed, such as a traveller's return, rain or a wind, since the uncertainty varies widely and leads to dispute over delivery. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1686} They rely on the report of Barira, which voids a condition but not the contract. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1687}

## Sale above the day price for deferred payment
Most jurists allow selling a thing for more than its price that day for deferred payment, relying on the general permission of sale, 'God has made sale lawful'. Those who forbid it take the Jahiliyya usury as a loan with an extra sum in return for delay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1688} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1689}

## Reduction in return for early payment
Jurists forbid taking a return for the term, so a debtor may not be given a discount for paying early, Abu Thawr and al-Nakha'i allow a reduction for early payment in general, while the Hanafis and Hanbalis allow a master to reduce a mukatabah price for early payment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1689} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1690}

## Disputes about the term
A dispute between the parties may concern the term itself, its length, when it falls due or when it has passed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1690}
