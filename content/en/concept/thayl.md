## Nature
A known plant with knotted branches creeping on the ground, from which roots strike into the earth, sweet in taste, with broad leaves sharp at the tips, stiff like the leaves of the small reed, and cattle and all beasts graze it; the book quotes Dioscorides on two further kinds, one useful in the art of medicine that kills livestock when they eat it, especially the one growing in the land of Babylon by the roads, the other growing in the land of Orsus with leaf like ivy, a white fragrant flower, a small fruit and white soft sweet stinking roots, whose juice, root decoction and seed are used in remedies; and a third kind grows at Qaliqala, which a beast eating it fresh is quickly sated by and which swells cattle if they eat much of it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}

## Temperament
Cold and dry in the first degree, especially its fresh root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}

## Actions and properties
Its power is astringent and there is a sting in it, and its juice prevents the flowing of matter to the viscera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}

## Wounds and ulcers
It benefits bad fresh wounds, closing them with flesh as a poultice laid on them, especially its root, and it heals {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}

## Organs of the head
It prevents all catarrhs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}

## Ocular organs
The book names its juice, cooked with a drink and honey, with myrrh, frankincense and aloes, as entering a good remedy for the eye, and another composition of it with myrrh, pepper and frankincense; the proportions are left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its seed and root cut vomiting and prevent flow to the stomach, and its seed on the whole is good for the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}

## Excretory organs
Its seed as an electuary promotes urine and breaks up stones through its dryness with bitterness, and so does its root, and their decoction benefits ulcers of the bladder; drinking its decoction is good for colic, difficulty of urine and ulcers that occur in the bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.697}
