## Nature
Honey is a hidden dew that falls on flowers and on other things, and the bees gather it; it is a vapour that rises and ripens in the air, then changes and thickens in the night and falls as honey. Honey also falls ready-made on the mountains of Qasran, and it differs according to the tree or stone on which it falls; the visible part of it is gathered by people and the hidden part by the bees, and the author thinks the working of the bees has an effect on it, the bees gathering it to feed on it and to store it. There is a pungent, poisonous kind of honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Selection
The best honey is that of true sweetness, good scent, inclined to pungency and to redness, firm and not thin, sticky so that it does not break off; the best is the spring honey, then the summer, and the winter is bad, as is said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Temperament
Bee honey is hot and dry in the second degree; the honey of sugar candy and of cane is hot in the first degree, not dry, and may be moist in the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Actions and properties
Its power is cleansing, opening the mouths of the vessels and dissolving moistures; it draws moistures from the depth of the body and keeps putrefaction and corruption from the flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Cosmetics
Smearing with it keeps off lice and nits and kills them; with costus it is a liniment for freckles, especially long-standing ones, and with salt for the marks of the aubergine-coloured blow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Wounds and ulcers
It cleanses dirty, deep ulcers; what is cooked until it thickens joins fresh wounds, and smeared with dill it cures ringworm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Organs of the head
Andarani salt is mixed with it and it is dropped warm into the ear, cleansing it and its ulcers, drying them and strengthening hearing; and smelling the pungent poisonous kind takes away the wits, so what of eating it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Ocular organs
Honey clears darkness of sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Organs of the chest
Rubbing the palate with it and gargling cure quinsy and benefit the tonsils {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.619}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Honey water strengthens the stomach and stimulates appetite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Excretory organs
Cane honey softens the bowels and sugar-candy honey does not; honey with its froth not removed causes wind and loosens the bowels, and if the froth is removed that lessens; the cooked does not stir the bowels but may even restrain it in the phlegmatic, and nourishes much; the cooked with water promotes urine more. The author says that honey and its water, if able to carry the food through, restrain, but if they meet movement and little readiness of the food to pass, they release the pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Poisons and bites
Honey drunk warmed with rose oil benefits the bites of vermin and the drinking of opium, and licking it is a remedy for the bite of a rabid dog and for eating the deadly mushroom; the cooked is useful against poisons, and he who vomits with it is freed. The pungent honey whose smell makes one sneeze brings on a sudden loss of wits and a cold sweat, and its remedy is to eat salted fish, to drink hydromel and to vomit with it. This is a warning about the poisonous kind, not advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}
