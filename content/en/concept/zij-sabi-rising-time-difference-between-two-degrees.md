## The straight and the clime risings
To find the interval in straight-sphere time between two degrees, the distance of the earlier degree from a cusp is found first, and then the next degree. If both lie in the eastern half of the sphere, the interval between them is taken in straight-sphere risings and again in the risings of the limited clime. If the two figures agree the interval is the distance between the two degrees; if they differ, the difference is taken, its sixth found, which is the share of one hour of difference, and this is multiplied by the hours of the earlier degree from the chosen cusp. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.204-206}

## The western half and the general rule
The calculation is repeated if the earlier and the following degrees both lie in the western half, multiplying the seasonal hours of the following degree by the hours of the earlier degree from the nadir and subtracting from the risings. The text says the reverse is also known, that is, which degree is separated from the earlier one by a given interval of straight-sphere time. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.205-206}
