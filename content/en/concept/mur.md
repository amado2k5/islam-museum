## Nature and selection
A gum, some pure and some mixed and adulterated. The best leans toward white and red, is not mixed with the wood of its tree, and has a good scent. It is sometimes adulterated with a deadly spurge and then becomes deadly; this spurge is called barfasis, a deadly tree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.565}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.565}.

## Actions and properties
Opening and dissolving winds, with astringency, sticking and softening; its smoke serves for what it serves for but dries more; it is fine and does not sting, and it enters the great remedies for its many uses. It prevents putrefaction so that it keeps a dead body and guards it from change and stench, and it dries raw superfluities. The kind brought from Iqlitiya is stronger in warming, ripening and softening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.565}.

## Cosmetic uses
Mixed with myrtle oil and ladanum it helps strengthen and thicken the hair; it cleanses the marks of ulcers; held in the mouth it sweetens the breath and removes foul breath; smeared with wine on the armpits it removes their odour; smeared with honey and salikha on warts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.565}.

## Swellings and pustules
Helps phlegmatic swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It heals and covers bare bones, is used with vinegar on ringworm, and cures putrid wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Joints
Smeared with the flesh of the shell on injured cartilages such as the ear and others {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Galen said: the smell of myrrh gives a headache to the healthy, let alone to epileptics. It is among the drugs, especially with thafsia, opium and jundbadastar, that help a bruised ear. Used as a rinse with wine and oil it greatly firms and strengthens the teeth, prevents their decay, firms the gums and removes their humidity; sprinkled on ulcers of the head it dries them; used with jundbadastar, mamitha and opium for painful ulcers of the ear and for pus; the nostrils are smeared with it for chronic catarrh, which it restrains; and it is snuffed to purify the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Eyes
It cleanses the marks of ulcers in the eye and fills their ulcers, or cleanses the whiteness; it helps roughness of the eyelids, and sometimes dissolves the water at the start of its descent if it is thin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Chest and breathing
Good for chronic moist cough, for cold, difficulty of breathing, orthopnoea and pains of the side, and it clears the voice, all through its gentle cleansing without roughening; it is held under the tongue and its water swallowed for roughness of the throat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Pure myrrh helps slackness of the stomach, yellow water and bloating of the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Excretory organs
It expels worms and gourd-seed worms through its bitterness, and is drunk for ulcers of the intestines, scraping and diarrhoea, in the measure the book gives, which is not copied here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
It brings on the menses, especially as an enema with rue water, wormwood water or lupin water; it expels foetuses and softens the closing of the mouth of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Poisons and bites
Given with wine for scorpion stings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.

## Fevers
The book says that with pepper at the start of the shivering fit it prevents it; the quantity named is not copied {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.566}.
