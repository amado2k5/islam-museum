## Summary
Chapter 16 treats the hours of the day elapsed by measuring the Sun: al-Battani finds the Sun's midday altitude and half the day arc for that day, then measures the altitude at any time, and uses the returned chords: he multiplies the chord of the measured altitude by the returned chord of half the day arc, divides by the chord of the midday altitude, subtracts the result from the returned chord, and finds the returned arc of the remainder, which is the arc that remains until midday or has passed since it. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.48-49}.

## The altitude and the returned arc
He says: if you want to know how many hours of the day have gone by measuring the Sun, find the Sun's midday altitude on that day, then half the day arc that day, then measure the Sun's altitude by an instrument, whose name is damaged in the scan, or by the shadow. Once you know the altitude at any time of day, find the returned chord of half the day arc in the way laid down at the beginning of the book in the chapter on finding returned chords from arcs. Then take the chord of the Sun's altitude at the time of measurement and multiply it by the returned chord of half the day arc, divide by the chord of the midday altitude, subtract the result of the division from the returned chord of half the day arc, and find the returned arc of the remainder as laid down for converting returned chords to arcs. See [[concept/zij-sabi-returned-chords-the-versed-sine|the returned chord]] for the term. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.48-49}.

## What has turned of the sphere, in seasonal and equal hours
Then he says: when the returned arc results, keep it; if the measurement was before midday, subtract that arc from half the day arc; if after midday, add the returned arc to half the day arc; half the day arc after the addition or subtraction is what has turned of the sphere since the Sun rose until the time of measurement. Divide it by the times of the hours of the day, meaning of that day, taken by the Sun's degree, and the result is how many seasonal hours of the day have passed; if you divide it by fifteen they are equal hours. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.49}.
