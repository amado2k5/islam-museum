## Summary
The larynx is a cartilaginous organ made as an instrument of the voice, built of three cartilages, with a lam-shaped bone before it from which the fibres of its muscles arise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.65-66}.

## Its cartilages
The first can be felt in front of the throat under the chin and is called the thyroid, the shield-like, since it is hollow within and convex behind like a shield. The second lies at the back near the neck and is called the nameless one. The third is bowed over both, called the pitcher-like or arytenoid; it joins the nameless cartilage by a double joint of two hollows into which two processes enter. By the thyroid drawing near the nameless cartilage or away from it the larynx widens and narrows, and by the arytenoid bending over the thyroid or standing off it the larynx closes and opens {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.65-66}.

## The hyoid bone and the muscles
Before the larynx lies a triangular bone called the lam-shaped bone after the Greek letter; its use is to be a support from which the fibres of the laryngeal muscles arise. The larynx needs muscles that draw the thyroid to the nameless cartilage, muscles that draw the arytenoid and close it, and muscles that draw it away and open the larynx. One pair arises from the hyoid and comes to the front of the thyroid; contracting, it pushes the arytenoid forward and up so the larynx widens. There are also narrowing muscles and closing muscles that arise inside the larynx and resist the muscles of the chest and diaphragm in holding the breath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.66}.
